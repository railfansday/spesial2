Our 2026 Whole Home Makes Stunning Storage a Self-Care Essential

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Our homes aren’t just bursting with beautiful design moments. They are also filled with our stuff. As closets brim with clothes, kitchens fill with appliances, and vanities overflow with products, it's undeniable—our homes are only as beautiful as our storage solutions allow them to be.

Bathroom vanities are getting larger. In fact, according to a 2026 survey by Houzz, 21 percent of bathroom projects are ditching the standard 60-inch vanity for 72-inch models, expanding floor plans and storage potential with them.

Clever storage makes spaces livable, transcending aesthetics to make our homes more functional. After all, what use are gorgeous countertops if their glossy facades are too crowded to appreciate? While storage is more a necessity than an accessory, smart solutions can balance being both practical and pretty.

This year’s Whole Home is a master class in curating stunning interiors, with designs boasting the next big color, pattern, and texture trends. No room is overlooked, especially baths, where Americans are spending more and more time unwinding, recharging, and investing in self-care. Designer Anissa Zajac, founder and principal designer at the Indianapolis-based studio, House Seven Design, brought elevated flair to the evolving space with whimsical wallpaper, mixed-metal hardware, and a his-and-hers vanity boasting expansive counter space, leaving visual breathing room between bold design details and the products that define our daily routines.

In addition to designing vast countertops, Zajac focused on texture, opting to place fluted cabinet doors alongside classic wainscoted drawers to add tactile and visual interest to traditionally flat designs. The fluted faces conceal even more storage space, so you can keep your more intimate, personal backstock, or less frequently used products neatly hidden behind spa-like flair.

So, the next time you feel the urge to declutter, remember that when it comes to storage, we’re talking function and form.
