Ex-St. John’s star RJ Luis Jr. debuts for LSU amid eligibility legal battle

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Former St. John's star RJ Luis Jr. is officially back in college basketball.

Luis, who is at the center of an ongoing saga surrounding eligibility in college sports, played his first game for LSU on Wednesday night in the Tigers' 79-67 win over UL-Monroe in Baton Rouge, La., entering eight minutes into the game.

He recorded 11 points on 3-of-9 shooting and nine rebounds in 15 minutes in his debut with the program.

Tigers head coach Will Wade declined to address the situation regarding the saga, but said Luis — the 2025 Big East Player of the Year with St. John's — had not practiced with the team before Wednesday.

"I'll be happy to answer any questions about how he played," Wade said after LSU's win. "But I'm going to refer anything in terms of his eligibility and rostering to the university statement yesterday. But everybody we played tonight is eligible."

Tigers guard RJ Luis Jr., who scored eight points off the bench in his debut with the team, shoots over a Louisiana Monroe defender during LSU's 79-67 win on Nov. 18, 2026 in Baton Rouge, La. AP Photo/Ella Hall

Wade also said he was happy with Luis' performance.

"He played well for not having practiced in a while," Wade said. "He can shoot it. You saw some physicality and him being able to play above the rim. He can guard. He's a physical defender.

"He's going to continue to get better," Wade added. "I don't think there's any question about him as a basketball player."

After entering the game with 12:05 left in the first half, Luis grabbed a rebound and later drew a loud roar from the crowd when he hit a 3-pointer.

Wade said he noticed the crowd's reaction to Luis and appreciated it.

"I'm always happy when any of our players get recognized," the coach said.

Wade declined to discuss the reasoning that went into placing Luis on the roster this week and playing him, other than to note, "he's eligible and he played."

LSU also did not make Luis available for interviews after the game.

Luis' appearance marked the latest twist in a legal battle and opposition from the SEC in the 2024-25 Big East Player of the Year's return to college basketball.

Luis had declared for the NBA draft in 2025, but went undrafted and subsequently signed a two-way contract with the Jazz and was later traded to the Celtics. He never played in an NBA regular-season game.

He committed to LSU earlier this year, which set off the legal battle over his eligibility between the conference and the LSU men's basketball program.

Luis had been granted eligibility via an injunction by a Louisiana judge in August, but the SEC had publicly emphasized rules that prohibited member schools from including any player who had signed a professional contract on their roster shortly after the court ruling.

Additionally, the conference said it had the power to levy any penalties against schools that broke those rules.

Among the penalties included a half-season suspension for the head coach and the revocation of the school's voting privileges on SEC matters.

Attorneys for Luis argued that the conference had enacted the rule after Luis was granted the injunction and that it shouldn't be applied to him retroactively, while the conference said the rule had been on the books.

The legal case took another turn when a judge prohibited the NCAA, SEC or SEC commissioner Greg Sankey from punishing LSU for putting Luis on its roster, and SEC officials said Monday they would adhere to the court's ruling.

LSU president Wade Rousse said in a statement released Tuesday that the university would comply with the judge's ruling.

"LSU is committed to compliance with NCAA and SEC rules, but it is equally committed to obeying the orders of Louisiana's courts," the statement said.
