Personal Investments • Re: Taxable brokerage vs After-Tax 401k (with pseudo mega backdoor Roth), and an ESPP question

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Because after-tax 401(k) contributions are non-taxable basis when rolled into a Roth IRA, that converted basis isn't actually subject to the 5-year penalty clock or locked up until 59.5 (only the taxable portion of a conversion has a 5-year penalty wait). You can generally access that rolled-over after tax basis tax and penalty free anytime, which gives you even more flexibility for your 5-year timeline.
As I explained in an earlier post, you can't withdraw the nontaxable portion until after you've withdrawn the taxable portion and paid the 10% penalty on that. It may not be a large penalty or a big deal.

I failed to mention that your plan may allow you to send your after-tax contribution amount ($30k in the earlier example) to a Roth IRA, and send the earnings ($2k in that example) to a traditional IRA. In that case no taxable earnings will have been converted to Roth, and so the entire $30k can be withdrawn tax and penalty free at any time as described above.

But... if you also do Backdoor Roth contributions, then moving pretax funds to a traditional IRA can interfere with that, so at that point it may be simpler/cleaner to just convert the entire $32k and pay a bit of tax.

If you can completely avoid any earnings while the funds are parked in the after-tax 401k that would simplify things a bit, but isn't it better to have earnings that you'll be taxed on than to have no earnings at all?
