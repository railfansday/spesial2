Ryan Fitzpatrick says criticism of Broncos QB Bo Nix 'crossed a line'

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Editor's note: Follow live coverage of the Denver Broncos vs. Pittsburgh Steelers Black Friday game here.

The day after Thanksgiving game came with a side of crow for Amazon Prime Video NFL analyst Ryan Fitzpatrick.

Prime Video host Charissa Thompson sat down with Denver Broncos quarterback Bo Nix prior to the Nov. 27 Black Friday game between the Broncos and Pittsburgh Steelers. After the piece aired, Thompson reminded Fitzpatrick that he was critical of Nix after Denver's Oct. 15 victory over the Seattle Seahawks on "Thursday Night Football."

"I know you two have squashed it," Thompson said. "You got on the phone. You talked, so that's over."

Nix was in his sixth game back after fracturing his ankle in a January divisional-round playoff game and completed 17 of 27 passes for 107 yards. He ran four times for 2 yards. He regularly passed up passes down the field and instead threw the ball out of bounds or took a sack. It was the fourth week in a row the Denver offense had totaled less than 300 yards; Nix averaged 4.0 yards per attempt.

The 2024 first-round pick has certainly played better since then. He's thrown 6 touchdowns in the last four contests and has taken one sack per game in that span.

The Broncos are 6-0 since head coach Sean Payton took over play calling duties from offensive coordinator Davis Webb.

"Going back to that, I did have some harsh critiques of him. I felt like – it did feel like it crossed a line into being personal and so that's why I reached out to him, and just said 'Look, I'm sorry, it wasn't a personal attack.' You see, he looks like the nicest person in the world there with his family," Fitzpatrick said, alluding to the Thompson interview.

It was quite the different tone from what he said after the win over the Seahawks. Fitzpatrick's postgame rant went viral and was striking since it was a former NFL quarterback being particularly harsh on one of his own brethren.

"It was really frustrating to watch," Fitzpatrick said. "Bo Nix is not playing competitively, it's almost disrespectful to the quarterback position how he's playing football.

"First of all, the guy doesn't want to throw the ball," Fitzpatrick continued, bemoaning Nix's decision-making on the aborted Denver drive late in the fourth quarter that gave Seattle the opportunity to tie the game and force overtime.

On Friday, Fitzpatrick was more complimentary.

"There has been an uptick in the explosive stuff … part of it is, he avoids the catastrophic plays," Fitzpatrick said.

The former Buffalo Bills quarterback said third-and-long and three-and-outs are common scenarios with Nix at quarterback.

"But in the end, when he has to make the plays, he's just shown over and over again that he has the ability," Fitzpatrick said. "There's some frustration there, but at the same time it's been productive."
