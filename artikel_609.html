Hollywood’s Most Notable Deaths of 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Dolly Parton, Patrick Stewart, Catherine O’Hara, Tim Curry, James Van Der Beek, Robert Duvall, Eric Dane and James Tolkan are among the entertainment legends the industry lost this year.

The entertainment industry is already mourning the loss of several Hollywood legends this year, including actors, singers, performers, creatives and executives.

The Hollywood Reporter is highlighting some of the most well-known figures who have died in 2026, from Dolly Parton, Patrick Stewart, Catherine O’Hara and Tim Curry to James Van Der Beek, Robert Duvall, Eric Dane and James Tolkan.

Below are more of this year’s most notable deaths within Hollywood, and check out THR‘s obituaries page for other 2026 industry deaths, as well as last year’s entertainment deaths here.

May 11, 1932 – Jan. 19, 2026Valentino, the legendary Italian fashion designer who seamlessly blended opulence and high style to create clothes that delighted generations of high-profile women, died Jan. 19 at his Rome residence, his foundation announced. He was 93. Read his obituary.

March 4, 1954 – Jan. 30, 2026Catherine O’Hara, a gifted comedic actress and two-time Emmy winner known for her roles in Home Alone, Schitt’s Creek and The Studio, died Jan. 30 at her home in Los Angeles after a brief illness, her reps at CAA confirmed. She was 71. Read her full obituary here.

March 8, 1977 – Feb. 11, 2026James Van Der Beek, the charismatic actor who starred as the sensitive and insecure Dawson Leery on The WB teen melodrama Dawson’s Creek, lost his battle with colorectal cancer on Feb. 11. He was 48. Full obituary here.

Jan. 5, 1931 – Feb. 15, 2026Robert Duvall, the steely-eyed actor whose performances in the first two Godfather films, Apocalypse Now, The Great Santini, Lonesome Dove and The Apostle made him one of the finest actors of any generation, died on Feb. 15. He was 95. Read his full obituary.

Nov. 9, 1972 – Feb. 19, 2026Eric Dane, the hunky actor who found fame and the nickname “McSteamy” for his eight-year run as the plastic surgeon Mark Sloan on the ABC drama Grey’s Anatomy, lost his battle with ALS. He was 53. Full obituary here.

March 10, 1940 – March 19, 2026Chuck Norris, the martial arts champion and karate school teacher who jumped fist- and feetfirst into stardom with 1980s action movies like Missing in Action and the long-running CBS drama Walker, Texas Ranger, died on March 19. He was 86. Read his obituary.

April 12, 1971 – March 20, 2026Nicholas Brendon, the actor best known for playing lovable underdog Xander Harris on all seven seasons of the TV series Buffy the Vampire Slayer, died on March 20. He was 54. Full obituary.

June 20, 1931 – March 26, 2026James Tolkan, the character actor who expressed a disdain for “slackers” in the Back to the Future trilogy and portrayed Tom Cruise’s no-nonsense commanding officer in Top Gun, died on March 26. He was 94. Read his full obituary here.

Nov. 19, 1938 – May 6, 2026Ted Turner, the media visionary who forever altered the news business by founding CNN and helped introduce Americans to pay TV by creating cable channels like TNT, Turner Classic Movies and Cartoon Network, died on May 6. He was 87. Read his obituary.

June 17, 1956 – May 30, 2026Kelly Curtis, an actress, documentarian and daughter of Tony Curtis and Janet Leigh, died on May 30, her younger sister, Jamie Lee Curtis, announced. She was 69. Read her full obituary.

April 13, 1951 – June 2, 2026Peabo Bryson, the two-time Grammy-winning R&B artist memorable for his work on the hit Disney songs “Beauty and the Beast” and “A Whole New World,” died on June 2. He was 75. Full obituary.

June 29, 1993 – June 14, 2026Oliver Tree, the singer, director and record producer known for his hit songs “Life Goes On” and “Miss You,” with Robin Schulz, died on June 14 in a helicopter crash in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. He was 32. Full obituary here.

April 4, 1932 – June 22, 2026Clive Davis, the hands-on hitmaker with a “golden ear” who brought Janis Joplin, Whitney Houston and Bruce Springsteen to the world and revitalized the careers of Carlos Santana, Rod Stewart and Aretha Franklin, died on June 22. He was 94. Read his obituary.

Sept. 14, 1947 – July 13, 2026Sam Neill, the amiable and adventurous leading man who emerged from New Zealand to make his mark in such films as Jurassic Park, The Piano, Dead Calm, In the Mouth of Madness and so much more, died on July 13. He was 78. Full obituary here.

Oct. 5, 1941 – July 29, 2026Arlene Smith, an original member of The Chantels who wrote and sang the lead on the R&B girl group’s classic 1957 hit, “Maybe,” died on July 29. She was 84. Read her obituary.

Jan. 13, 1932 – Aug. 3, 2026Jon Cypher, who portrayed Prince Charming opposite Julie Andrews in the first live TV production of Cinderella and Chief of Police Fletcher Daniels on all seven seasons of Hill Street Blues, died on Aug. 3. He was 94. Read his obituary.

Aug. 21, 1989 – Aug. 16, 2026Hayden Panettiere, known for her roles on shows such as Heroes and Nashville, died on Aug. 16, her father said in a statement. She was 36. It was later announced that she died from the toxic effects of fentanyl as well as prescription drugs. Read her full obituary here.

Oct. 15, 1932 – Aug. 18, 2026Bill Rasmussen, a onetime sportscaster for a Massachusetts TV station who borrowed $9,000 on his credit card on the way to launching the sports behemoth ESPN, died on Aug. 18. He was 93. Full obituary here.

Dec. 22, 1928 – Aug. 20, 2026Bunny Levine, an actress who appeared in Gilmore Girls, You Don’t Mess With the Zohan, Thelma and numerous other TV shows and films, died on Aug. 20. She was 97. Read her obituary.

Jan. 19, 1944 – Aug. 22, 2026Shelley Fabares, the wholesome daughter on The Donna Reed Show who went on to star opposite Elvis Presley in three movies and as Craig T. Nelson’s independent wife on Coach, died on Aug. 22. She was 82. Full obituary here.

Jan. 19, 1946 – Aug. 25, 2026Dolly Parton, the irrepressible singer-songwriter, actress and entrepreneur who penned chart-toppers such as “9 to 5” and turned her namesake Tennessee theme park Dollywood into one of the world’s top amusement destinations, died Aug. 25 after a “brief battle with cancer.” She was 80. Read her full obituary here.

April 19, 1946 – Aug. 25, 2026Tim Curry, the offbeat British actor who specialized in playing eccentrics like the seductive, cross-dressing scientist Dr. Frank-N-Furter in The Rocky Horror Picture Show and such fiendish villains as Pennywise the Clown from Stephen King’s It, died on Aug. 25. He was 80. Full obituary here.

July 28, 1941 – Aug. 26, 2026Peter Cullen, the Canadian voice actor who starred as the heroic Optimus Prime in a convoy of Transformers films, TV shows and video games and as the gloomy Eeyore in dozens of Winnie the Pooh projects, died on Aug. 26. He was 85. Read his obituary.

March 25, 1934 – Sept. 2, 2026Gloria Steinem, the writer, activist and icon of the feminist movement, died on Sept. 2. She was 92. Full obituary.

March 24, 1940 – Sept. 14, 2026Bob Mackie, the nine-time Emmy-winning costume designer known as the “Sultan of Sequins” for the spectacular, over-the-top outfits he created for the likes of Cher, Diana Ross, Mitzi Gaynor and Carol Burnett, died on Sept. 13. He was 87. Read his obituary here.

July 2, 1999 – Sept. 20, 2026Presley Gerber, the son of Cindy Crawford and Rande Gerber, died at a rehab facility on Sept. 20. He was 27. Full obituary.

July 4, 1924 – Oct. 6, 2026Eva Marie Saint, the ethereal beauty who won the best supporting actress Oscar for her work in her first motion picture, On the Waterfront, then dazzled audiences as a double agent in North by Northwest, died on Oct. 6. She was 102. Read her obituary here.

Aug. 21, 1992 – Oct. 12, 2026R.J. Mitte, the actor and disability rights advocate who portrayed Walter White Jr., the son of Bryan Cranston’s character, on Breaking Bad, died on Oct. 12. He was 34. Read his obituary.

Dec. 7, 1979 – Oct. 25, 2026Eric Bauza, the Emmy-winning voice actor who was the modern voice of Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck and other Looney Tunes characters, died on Oct. 25. He was 46. Full obituary.

July 13, 1940 – Oct. 28, 2026Patrick Stewart, the sophisticated actor who brought a Shakespearean touch to Star Trek and X-Men as he made an otherworldly transformation from British stage veteran to sci-fi icon, died on Oct. 28. He was 86. Read his obituary here.

Sept. 25, 1952 – Nov. 4, 2026Toukie Smith, a top model in the 1970s and an actress who appeared as Eva Rawley on the Marla Gibbs-starring NBC sitcom 227, died on Nov. 4. She was 74. Full obituary here.

June 2, 1941 – Nov. 10, 2026Stacy Keach, the two-fisted actor who starred as the hard-boiled private eye Mike Hammer on television and won acclaim — but never superstardom — for his work in Fat City, The New Centurions and many other films, died on Nov. 10. He was 85. Read his obituary.

Feb. 16, 1961 – Nov. 23, 2026Andy Taylor, Duran Duran‘s guitarist during the band’s commercial peak in the 1980s as well as a founding member of supergroup The Power Station, died on Nov. 23. He was 65. Full obituary here.
