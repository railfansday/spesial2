Adani Expects ‘One or Two’ Foreign Airlines to Pause Mumbai Flights Rather Than Move Airports

Adani says a few airline exits would barely dent passenger numbers, but that’s not the only measure of a new airport’s success. If well-known long-haul carriers refuse to fly to Mumbai, Navi Mumbai’s international network will be mostly Indian airlines, which limits its appeal to global travelers.

Some international airlines may temporarily suspend their Mumbai services rather than shift operations to Navi Mumbai International Airport, said Arun Bansal, CEO of Adani Airport Holdings.

“There might be one or two airlines that decide not to operate from Mumbai for a couple of years, but that will have almost no impact on the overall passenger count,” Bansal said during Adani Enterprises’ second-quarter earnings call last week.

He added that the airport is close to wrapping up discussions with most international airlines, with only a few dialogues still to be resolved.

The proposed shift is part of a larger operational change at Mumbai’s Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (CSMIA), which Adani also operates.

Adani Airport Holdings expects a handful of international airlines to pause Mumbai service rather than relocate to Navi Mumbai International Airport, and CEO Arun Bansal said the loss would barely register in overall passenger numbers. The shift is driven by the April 2027 redevelopment of Terminal 1 at Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport, which will push domestic flights into Terminal 2 and squeeze out some international carriers that Adani wants to move 45 kilometers away to Navi Mumbai. Foreign carriers have asked for more time, and Air France-KLM said in September that splitting its Mumbai operations "is not an option." Indian carriers are moving first: Akasa Air shifts its Mumbai–Jeddah flights in December, IndiGo plans to build to 12 international destinations between February and July 2027, and Air India Group has announced seven international routes. IndiGo CEO Willie Walsh said Navi Mumbai matters to the airline's long-haul ambitions and that it will operate at both airports, but that its timing will follow its own network plans rather than the operator's requirements. Adani is targeting full use of Navi Mumbai's 20 million passenger initial capacity by the end of the next financial year, with Terminal 2 construction due to start this month.
