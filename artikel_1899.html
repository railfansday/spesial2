Liverpool defender Kostas Tsimikas was superb as Greece beat Serbia 1-0 in the Nations League on Monday.

Giorgos Masouras' second-half goal eventually proved to be the difference, in a result that will have been bittersweet.

Just days ago, Greece had looked set to finish in the top two of group A and automatically qualify for the knockout stages, but they lost their goal difference in Friday's 7-0 defeat at the hands of the Netherlands and ultimately finished third, level on points with the teams above them.

Tsimikas featured in that game and will have been glad for some vengeance, earning a clean sheet against Serbia on Monday.

The left-back has struggled for minutes at Liverpool this season, making just nine appearances across all competitions and only four in the Premier League.

But Milos Kerkez has been far from exceptional for Andoni Iraola, who has been posed a question by Tsimikas' impressive display on Friday.

Tsimikas has never been the first choice at Liverpool, although it was always clear that he had quality.

His greatest error was likely costing just £11.5million, with the £40m-rated Kerkez favoured for his age.

The Hungary international has continued to disappoint, however, and displays like the one Tsimikas provided on Monday could finally see change.

The 30-year-old created three chances from left-back and completed three dribbles - more than anybody else.

He put seven crosses into the box, took four touches in the opposition area and completed 91 per cent of his passes.

That is not to mention his defensive work, which was superb. Tsimikas made two tackles, a block, four clearances and six recoveries.

He won six duels on the night, winning the joint-most ground duels of anybody on the pitch.

Football Insider revealed earlier this month that Liverpool are exploring their options for a new left-back.

No talks have taken place with Tsimikas over a new deal, with his current terms set to expire in 2027.

A display like Monday's may well see them think twice about that decision, however.

Kerkez is yet to justify the money spent on him in 2025, and Tsimikas surely deserves at least an opportunity.
