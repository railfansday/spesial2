Pair a receipt scanning app that pays per receipt, Fetch for gift cards or Receipt Hog for PayPal cash, with one that pays per featured product, Ibotta or Checkout 51, and use both on every grocery trip. Expect a few dollars a month from your receipts, since one with no bonus earns cents.

Fetch pays at least 25 points for any paper receipt and values 1,000 points at roughly $1 in gift cards, so a receipt with no bonus works out to about 2.5 cents. Most of its rewards start at 3,000 points, and your first has to be $10 or more.

Every app here pays on the receipts you already bring home. Some pay a little for each receipt, and others pay cash back only for what their offers feature, mostly specific products. In return, each app shares data on what you buy with brands or other business partners. Amazon also pays up to $10 a month in Amazon credit for other stores’ receipts, though space on its shopper panel is limited. Other savings need no receipt photo at all, such as card-linked offers you add to the card you pay with and your store app’s digital coupons.

Receipts from restaurants, gas stations, and malls earn its 25-point minimum too, though three receipts a week with no bonus come to about 325 points a month, roughly 33 cents by our math.

At Walmart, CVS, and Dollar General, a linked store account used at checkout pays the offers you added before shopping with nothing to snap, so those receipts go only into your per-receipt app.

Its gas offers pay only if no other cash back program pays on the same fill-up, so give gas receipts to Checkout 51 alone and grocery receipts to it and your per-receipt app.

Receipt apps pay small amounts, so what you earn depends on snapping every receipt, week after week, into apps that fit how you shop. Start with one that pays per receipt in the form you’d rather be paid, gift cards or PayPal cash, and pair it with an app whose featured brands you already buy.
