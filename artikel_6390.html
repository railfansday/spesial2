Papa Johns Wants Your Honest Opinion—And It's Offering Free Pizza In Return

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

They're asking for the good, the bad, and everything in between.

Opening a pizza box can bring out the critic in anyone. But whatever your reaction, whether it's about a pale crust or toppings piled onto one side, Papa Johns wants to hear about it—and your opinion could be worth a whole other pizza.

The chain has launched a two-week promotion called "Snap a Pizza, Get a Free Pizza," inviting customers to snap a photo of their orders and tell the company what they really think. You can praise it, complain about it, or land somewhere in the middle. Even a less-than-glowing review is welcome.

Before you start snapping pictures of every slice in sight, though, there's some fine print worth reading.

To participate, you'll need to spend at least $15 on an order placed through the Papa Johns website or app. You'll also need a Papa Rewards account with text messages enabled, since that's how the chain will contact you about submitting your review.

Papa Johns wants an unedited photo of your pizza, along with your thoughts on everything from the level of cheese and how it's cooked to the overall experience. Once the photo is approved, you'll receive an offer for a large one-topping pizza in your Rewards account.

That word "free" does come with a qualification. Redeeming your reward requires spending at least $5 on your next order, meaning you'll have to open your wallet twice. Still, if you've ever had strong opinions about the way your pizza arrived, you might as well get something out of sharing them.

Of course, Papa Johns is getting something out of this arrangement, too. All those pictures and reviews give the chain a closer look at what customers are actually receiving.

"We see the pizza pics, the unboxings and the hot takes online every day, and we know our customers aren't shy about sharing what they think," said Chris Lyn-Sue, Papa Johns' global chief marketing officer, in a statement.The promotion is part of Papa Johns' recently announced "Unbox Better" initiative, which focuses on things like food quality and the chain’s general service. It's also not the only fast food chain inviting customers to speak their minds. Earlier this year, Burger King went so far as to encourage customers to call or text its president directly with their feedback.

Papa Johns is offering a slightly different incentive, although I suspect a free pizza might convince a few more people to participate.
