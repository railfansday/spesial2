HBO's 8-Part Thriller Masterpiece Is Aging Like Fine Wine

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Certain franchise TV shows suffer from longevity for a variety of reasons, but an eight-episode thriller on HBO is the complete opposite. The ever-increasing investment in shared universes has sometimes led to disappointing end products, especially those that rely far too much on tying to wider narratives or character arcs. For instance, several MCU TV shows have had this issue, be it Echo's reliance on characters from Netflix's Marvel shows or She-Hulk's attempts to explore the franchise in a meta way.

Still, there are others that age well. HBO, in particular, is a studio that has several examples of this; The recent Lanterns, despite the DC Universe's future now being in doubt, is one of HBO's best sci-fi shows and is a self-contained story that doesn't require wider viewing. This is just one example of HBO's ability to produce TV shows that work in their own right, despite being part of a wider universe. However, even Lanterns could age poorly, given James Gunn's recent exit from DC Studios.

Conversely, one HBO show that is aging like fine wine is an eight-part story that still exists within a wider continuity, but operates as a concise, well-written, utterly compelling thriller with its own narrative themes and weight. That show is The Penguin, 2024's spin-off of Matt Reeves' The Batman. The Penguin exists within Reeves' Batman Epic Crime Saga, but it works on its own, meaning it doesn't rely on The Batman, its upcoming sequel, or any other entries in the franchise.

This alone renders The Penguin a show that will age well, as it could be watched 10 years from now with no prior viewing and still work as an excellent, character-driven thriller. Beyond that, though, The Penguin will avoid the aforementioned issue with some shared universe entries, as it doesn't need the wider franchise to succeed. With all of the uncertainty surrounding DC at the moment, shows tied to the wider narrative like Lanterns or, in an even more extreme case, Peacemaker, may see lingering threads fizzle out. As its own entity, however, The Penguin will age well with or without the wider DC franchise.

The future of DC being so unsettled raises questions about The Batman - Part III, while Part II remains plagued with production issues. The latter recently paused production due to Matt Reeves' personal issues, with Steven Soderbergh taking over to finish filming. This indicates The Batman - Part II will still be released in February 2028, but the impending shake-up at DC could mean the planned third installment will not happen, especially with Reeves facing so many issues making the second movie.

When looking at The Penguin, it will not matter whether The Batman franchise continues after Part II. The tight, eight-episode narrative wraps everything up perfectly, with only the subtle hints and Easter eggs to the events of The Batman being woven into it effortlessly, without any need to know the context. Moreover, The Batman - Part II is not expected to feature the character of Oz Cobb all that extensively, meaning The Penguin will only continue to age well, no matter the fate of its wider franchise.

All of this just proves that The Penguin nailed how to be part of a shared universe, only as its own spin-off. As alluded to, some franchises fail to find this balance, with even some of Marvel's better-rated shows, like Ms. Marvel, being essential viewing before The Marvels or, presumably, the upcoming Avengers: Secret Wars. The Penguin, on the other hand, feels like its own thing. Yes, it has connections to The Batman, but the central character arcs and wider narrative are not directly impacted by the movie, nor does it set up any sequels with lingering plot threads.

The Penguin can still tie into future stories, should they so wish to be constructed as a sequel of sorts. Still, it remains the case that this will be a decision of different filmmakers, as opposed to The Penguin's creative team actively writing the show to tie it to future stories. Instead, The Penguin was constructed to be its own entity, with its own unique story, characters, and eight-episode narrative.
