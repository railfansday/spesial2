Ampace Presents PU100 Fire Test Results with Substantial Margins Against UL 9540A Sixth Edition Criteria

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

LAS VEGAS, Nov. 18, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Ampace today presented the results of its PU100 large-scale fire test (LSFT) under UL 9540A, 6th Edition, and received a UL certificate at RE+ 2026. The results demonstrated control of fire propagation, substantial margins below the assessed temperature limits, and no observed re-ignition over the 24 hours following self-extinguishment, providing measurable evidence to support battery safety assessments for AI data centers.

"Safety is a core principle of our product design. For data center operators, the critical question is how effectively a battery system can limit the consequences of a failure," said Dr. Drew Feng, Chief Safety & Reliability Officer of Ampace. "Through large-scale fire testing, we turn that question into measurable evidenceâwhere propagation stops, how much heat reaches surrounding structures, and whether re-ignition occurs. These findings help customers make informed decisions about system selection, installation design and recovery planning."

PU100 test results demonstrate safety performance beyond the assessed criteria

The PU100 results demonstrate how propagation control, limited thermal exposure, compact-layout testing and post-fire stability can inform safety assessments for data center installations:

Contained impact: no thermal runaway in adjacent modules. Despite simultaneous heating of 50 percent of the cells in the initiating module, thermal runaway did not propagate to adjacent modules, which recorded normal post-test voltage readings of 53.2 V. The adjacent cabinet retained mechanical integrity and remained operational during post-test inspection. These findings support fault-isolation assessments and recovery planning.

Limited thermal exposure: measurable margins below the assessed limits. Wall temperature rise remained below 80Â°C against a 97Â°C limit, while ceiling steel-beam temperature stayed below 250Â°C against a 538Â°C limitâmargins of more than 17Â°C and 288Â°C, respectively. These measurements help project teams assess thermal exposure to surrounding walls and structural steel.

Evidence for compact deployment: no fire spread to adjacent or target cabinets. The test involved 10 cabinets at 100 percent state of charge, with zero side-by-side clearance, 20 mm back-to-back spacing and 850 mm face-to-face spacing. No fire spread to adjacent or target cabinets was observed, providing evidence to inform space planning and engineering assessments for compact installations.

Post-fire stability: self-extinguishment followed by 24 hours without observed re-ignition. With sprinklers armed but not activated, the fire self-extinguished after 43 minutes, with no re-ignition observed over the following 24 hours. No flaming debris escaped the cabinet, exceeding the assessed criterion of no flaming debris beyond 1.5 m. Together, these observations provide evidence for assessing post-fire risks and the potential impact on surrounding equipment.

The findings apply to the tested configuration and conditions and support project-specific engineering assessments.

The pursuit of ultimate safety is central to Ampace's product philosophy, extending beyond UPS batteries across its wider portfolio. Since its establishment in 2021, Ampace has supplied battery packs and cells at scale across diverse applications, with zero recorded safety incidents according to company records. The PU100 results further demonstrate this commitment through rigorous testing and measurable evidence, helping customers assess battery safety and make informed decisions about their power infrastructure.
