Star Trek In 2027 Will Get Its Biggest Boost In A Decade Thanks To Skydance (No, Really)

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Skydance merging Paramount+ and HBO Max will be good news for existing Star Trek content and potentially gives Gene Roddenberry's 60-year-old franchise its biggest boost in the decade since Star Trek: Discovery premiered. Now, it's understandable if Trekkers are skeptical. Read any room Star Trek fans occupy, and there's a general uneasiness, at the least, about Star Trek's future under Skydance.

After all, it was the looming purchase of Paramount Global by Skydance back in 2023 that sparked the gradual cancellation of every Star Trek TV series on Paramount+ executive produced by Alex Kurtzman. While other factors contributed as well to Star Trek's diminishment, such as the cost of Kurtzman's Star Trek shows relative to their streaming numbers, Star Trek's new owners and their still undefined new direction for the venerable franchise led to the slate being wiped clean of original Star Trek shows on Paramount+.

In the eyes of fans, Star Trek is "going away" or is "over" after 2027, when Star Trek: Starfleet Academy and Star Trek: Strange New Worlds premiere their final seasons, with no new Star Trek in production. The doomsaying also has to do with fans' distrust of Skydance CEO David Ellison's right-leaning politics, which Trekkers fear will overrule the progressive vision Gene Roddenberry's Star Trek is built upon.

Whatever happens to Star Trek in terms of creative after 2027, something good will come out of Skydance's plans for HBO Max and Paramount+.

Skydance will bundle Paramount+ and HBO Max in early 2027, offering consumers both streamers for one price. Ellison called the bundle “our first step towards unifying the two services," in a conference call to Wall Street. Combining HBO Max and Paramount+ will take time, Ellison admits, but the ultimate goal is for one streaming service to host franchises like Game of Thrones, DC, Harry Potter, Landman, Dutton Ranch... and Star Trek.

The future of Star Trek is of paramount concern to fans. Roddenberry Entertainment COO Trevor Roth speaks to what's next for the franchise.

Star Trek's greatest heights in terms of viewership and pop culture relevance were in the 1990s, when audiences could visit the final frontier on free TV in syndication or on UPN. For well over the last decade, Star Trek has been trapped behind streaming paywalls, although Pluto TV offers classic Star Trek series and occasional episodes of Paramount+'s Star Trek shows to stream for free. With Paramount+ and HBO Max bundled en route to full integration, Star Trek has a chance to grow its audience in ways it couldn't before.

New and classic Star Trek series being offered alongside HBO's most prestigious series like The Pitt, House of the Dragon, and The White Lotus also raises the franchise's profile. Despite the high quality of Alex Kurtzman's series in terms of production values and visual effects, and casting Oscar-caliber A-list stars like Holly Hunter, Paul Giamatti, and Michelle Yeoh, Star Trek is still seen as a niche sci-fi franchise that has trouble enticing general audiences.

Simply making Star Trek available to HBO Max's audience makes it easier and more enticing to sample the final frontier and fall in love with Star Trek's rich universe of unforgettable characters and thought-provoking stories. Being exposed to HBO Max's viewers is potentially the biggest boost existing Star Trek will get to create new fans.

Skydance considers Star Trek a crown jewel IP, although the studio's top focus seems to be relaunching the Star Trek movie franchise before creating more TV shows. It's also likely that a new executive producer will replace Alex Kurtzman to oversee Star Trek in line with Skydance's vision for the franchise. How fans will feel about Skydance's Star Trek remains to be seen. Still, many are bracing for the worst.

Looking Star Trek pre- and post-Skydance, even if audiences reject the next iteration of the final frontier, the greater availability of 1966-2027 Star Trek will allow viewers to flock to the old shows and movies as part of their subscription to whatever the combined HBO Max and Paramount+ streamer will be called.

Paramount+ and HBO Max merging is beneficial to the Star Trek TV shows and movies fans already know and love. If the audience for Star Trek can grow with Skydance's bundle becoming a unified streaming service, more people discovering Captain Kirk, Captain Picard, Mr. Spock, Captain Pike, Data, Saru, and Captain Burnham is a good thing.
