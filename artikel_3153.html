La-Z-Boy (LZB +3.76%) reported second quarter results featuring sales growth in the Retail and Wholesale segments. Management attributed top-line performance to store acquisitions and new store openings while operating margins reflected supply chain investments and onetime warranty adjustments. The company is executing a multiyear distribution and home delivery transformation and manufacturing plant consolidations aimed at improving long-term cost structures.

Melinda Whittington: Thanks, Mark. Good morning, everyone. Yesterday, following the close of market, we reported solid October-ended second quarter results. We delivered continued strong sales growth in Retail and returned to growth in Wholesale, excluding the impact of the wholesale casegoods divestiture, even as the broader environment remained choppy. We continue to benefit from expansion of our retail business through new and acquired stores and strong in-store execution and from growth in our wholesale business through strategic partnerships. Additionally, we continue to advance our major initiatives and remain on track to realize these benefits over time. This quarter provides further proof in our strategy to grow retail and invest to position our company for long-term value creation.

Highlights for our second quarter included Retail written sales increasing 11%. Retail delivered sales increasing 14%, led by acquisitions and new stores with delivered same-store sales roughly flat. We added 3 company-owned stores during the quarter, including 1 new store and 2 acquired, bringing our total to 237 company-owned stores or 62% of the total store network. We also signed an agreement to acquire one additional independent La-Z-Boy store. Wholesale delivered sales increased 3%, excluding the impact of the casegoods divestiture, led by growth in our core North America La-Z-Boy wholesale business, which has now grown 5 of the last 7 quarters.

Bringing it all together, total company delivered consolidated sales increased 4%, excluding the impact of the wholesale casegoods divestiture. Adjusted operating margin was 5%. Cash flow continued to be strong. We returned $32 million to shareholders through share repurchases and dividends, triple the prior year second quarter amount, and we announced a 12% dividend increase, marking our sixth consecutive year of double-digit dividend increases. I'm pleased with the quarter's strong top line performance and solid execution while balancing the impact of multiple strategic investments in process. Our focus on bringing innovative, high-quality handcrafted products to market and delivering an inspiring end-to-end consumer experience is strengthening connections with existing consumers and introducing La-Z-Boy to new audiences.

I am confident in our ability to continue to grow our business even in this dynamic environment. Turning to written trends. Second quarter total written sales for our company-owned retail segment increased 11% versus last year's second quarter, driven by acquired and new stores. Written same-store sales, which exclude the benefit of new and acquired stores, declined slightly for the quarter. We were pleased with positive same-store sales trends for the combined August and September period, highlighting the strength of our Labor Day execution. However, this momentum was offset by the particularly soft October trends seen across many consumer businesses.

While consumer trends continue to be quite choppy, we have seen positive momentum in early November as we enter the key holiday sales season. Our Joybird business continues to experience the most significant volatility with written sales decreasing 30% in the quarter as we rightsize marketing spend levels. We continue to take decisive action to improve the resiliency and cost structure of this business, including transitioning Joybird production into our established U.S. plant network by the end of our third quarter. While early, we also continue to test additional go-to-market adjustments to enable sustainable profit accretive growth, including dedicated Joybird branded space in a small number of La-Z-Boy stores as well as continued adjustments to our marketing approach.

As we prepare to celebrate La-Z-Boy's 100th anniversary in March, we are strengthening our century-long heritage of comfort, quality, customization and quick speed to market. We remain agile and focused on leveraging our competitive advantages to drive growth for the next 100 years. Even as the macroeconomic backdrop remains challenging, we believe the strength of our iconic brand, retail operations and vertically integrated supply chain enables us to navigate the near-term, driving sales growth and margin accretion while investing for the long-term sustainable growth. I want to take a moment to highlight progress on our strategic investments.

In Retail, during the second quarter, we added 3 company-owned stores, including 1 new store in La Crosse, Wisconsin, and 2 acquired stores in New Orleans, bringing our total to 237 company-owned stores, which is nearly double our company-owned store count just 10 years ago and bringing corporate ownership up to 62% of the total La-Z-Boy store network of 380 stores. This Retail expansion gives us greater control of the end-to-end consumer experience where we can inspire and delight more shoppers. We continue to see an opportunity to expand to roughly 450 locations across North America at a rate of approximately 10 new stores annually, most of which will be company-owned.

Independent dealer acquisitions remain another important growth lever as these transactions are immediately sales and profit accretive and often offer additional growth opportunities in underpenetrated markets. During the quarter, we signed an agreement to acquire another one additional independent La-Z-Boy store in Madison, Wisconsin, that is expected to close in December. Over the last year, we have acquired 3 independent dealers and 20 independent stores, and we see continued opportunity across our nearly 40 independent dealers and over 140 independent stores still in our network. Our digital transformation continues to advance as an important enabler to expand La-Z-Boy brand reach, enhancing how consumers discover, explore and engage with the brand online.

This is creating a stronger connection between online inspiration and in-store experience. We recently launched redesigned product detail pages across key upholstery categories, making it easier for consumers to explore our products, personalize their selections and move seamlessly through the purchase journey. The new experience includes enhanced 3D product imagery, higher definition fabric and cover visualizations, simpler navigation and customization and more prominent add-to-cart functionality. Importantly, since launching the new product pages, add-to-cart rates have continued to improve, providing another proof point that these investments are strengthening consumer engagement and helping create a more connected digital experience.

We're encouraged by progress in our digital transformation with meaningful improvements in traffic, conversion and sales online and our more connected experience to also drive growth into our retail stores. We're also thoughtfully advancing our use of AI across the enterprise with a focus on enhancing the consumer experience and driving enhanced analysis and efficiencies. We are advancing work to ensure product and brand discoverability as consumers' search behavior evolves and building capabilities around our data and internal processes to make the omnichannel journey more intuitive, seamless and efficient. While technology is enhancing how we serve our consumers, our people remain at the heart of the La-Z-Boy experience.

We are proud that La-Z-Boy was once again named to Forbes 2027 Best Customer Service list for the fifth consecutive year. This award recognizes our team's passion for delivering the transformational power of comfort and creating an experience commensurate with our iconic brand from online and in-store to in-home and after care. To expand a little more on our journey with the consumer, we continue to strengthen the La-Z-Boy brand through consumer-led marketing and innovation, using deeper insights to better understand how consumers live, shop and engage with our brand. Those insights are helping shape our product assortment across styles, features, price points and life stages.

We continue to see a more bifurcated consumer in the marketplace, and we have optimized our product assortment, pricing and messaging to attract both higher-end design consumers as well as those consumers looking for a value-focused entrance into our brand lineup. Recent introductions include our expanded power lift assortment with enhanced comfort and functionality; AudioLuxe, our premium audio furniture line featuring sound by Klipsch, which is now available in select stores; and Comfort Essentials, an opening price point offering within our stationary assortment.

Together, these innovations create clear opening price point and trade-up options, extend La-Z-Boy into more rooms and homes and allow us to reach a broader audience while remaining rooted in the comfort, quality and customization that has defined our brand for generations. In the Wholesale segment, we continue to drive growth in our core North American La-Z-Boy Wholesale business, targeting a broader consumer reach. We remain focused on growing with compatible strategic partners that value the comfort and quality we deliver to consumers and the traffic that La-Z-Boy can drive to their stores. We ended the quarter with over 1,400 La-Z-Boy Comfort Studio and branded space locations, each with dedicated space for La-Z-Boy branded products.

Our final strategic pillar, strengthening our foundational capabilities continues to advance with our multiyear distribution and home delivery transformation and our manufacturing plant consolidation. Our vertically integrated model with over 95% of upholstered furniture produced domestically is a key competitive advantage, allowing us to deliver customized products within 4 to 6 weeks while limiting our relative exposure to trade policy volatility. During the second quarter, we completed the planned exit of one of our facilities, and we have accelerated our plans to consolidate our Joybird manufacturing plant into our U.S. network by the end of our third quarter.

Separately, our distribution and home delivery transformation, now in year 2 of a 4-year project, will optimize our footprint from 15 distribution centers to 3 centralized hubs, enabling 20% less mileage traveled and 30% less warehouse square footage and doubling our delivery radius to consumers. The remaining 2 of our centralized hubs will open in Missouri and Tennessee by the end of our fiscal year, and we will complete our transition into these new sites throughout our fiscal '28.

These significant supply chain investments will create an even stronger company by expanding the value we offer to consumers through greater reach and speed of delivery, supporting our communities through jobs and localized investment and driving value for shareholders by improving our cost structure and working capital productivity. We remain focused on creating our own momentum, controlling what we can control and driving value for all our stakeholders. While the timing of an industry recovery remains uncertain, we are well positioned to continue to grow regardless of the external environment and further accelerate that growth when housing trends improve. Diluted earnings per share totaled $0.45 on a GAAP basis and adjusted diluted EPS was $0.53. As I move to the segment discussion, my comments from here will focus on our adjusted reporting unless specifically stated otherwise. Starting with the Retail segment.

For the second quarter, delivered sales increased 14% to $252 million, driven by growth from acquired and new stores with delivered same-store sales roughly flat versus a year ago. Retail adjusted operating margin was 9.7% versus 10.7% last year, driven by a shift in product mix, along with investment in targeted promotional activity. For our Wholesale segment, delivered sales decreased 2% to $362 million versus last year on a reported basis and increased 3% adjusting for the impact of the casegoods divestiture. Sales were led by strong growth in our core North America La-Z-Boy Wholesale business.

Adjusted operating margin for the Wholesale segment was 5.9% in the second quarter versus 8.1% last year due to strategic investments in our supply chain, including plant consolidations and distribution transformation, both in progress and a 150 basis point impact from a onetime change in our dealer warranty arrangements, which benefited last year's second quarter, partially offset by favorable net tariff impact, including refunds and pricing actions net of tariff costs. For Joybird, reported in Corporate and Other, delivered sales were $25 million, down 29% on lower delivered sales volume and reduced marketing investment against a challenged consumer backdrop.

The Corporate and Other adjusted operating loss increased versus the prior year, primarily due to expense deleverage on lower Joybird delivered sales as we continue to work to exit the stand-alone plant and minimize fixed costs. Moving on to our consolidated adjusted gross margin and SG&A performance for fiscal 2027 second quarter. Consolidated adjusted gross margin for the entire company increased 50 basis points versus the prior year second quarter. The increase in gross margin was primarily driven by the shift in consolidated mix towards our Retail segment, which has a higher gross margin rate than our Wholesale segment and favorable net tariff impact, including refunds and pricing actions net of tariff costs.

This was partially offset by strategic investments in our supply chain, including the plant consolidations and distribution transformation, both in progress. Adjusted SG&A as a percent of sales for the quarter increased by 260 basis points compared with last year, also due to the shift in consolidated mix towards our Retail segment, which carries a higher fixed cost structure relative to Wholesale and 110 basis points impact from a onetime change in our dealer warranty arrangements, which benefited last year's second quarter. Our effective income tax rate on a GAAP basis for the quarter was 26% versus 26.7% for the prior year period.

We continue to expect a normalized effective income tax rate for the full year in the range of 26% to 27%. Our balance sheet remains strong with $247 million in cash and no externally funded debt. Our capital allocation strategy remains consistent to reinvest 50% of operating cash flow back into the business and return 50% to shareholders in share repurchases and dividends.
