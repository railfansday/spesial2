LIV Golf Shutting Down After Last-Ditch Financing Plan Fails

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Five years after it began its quest to upend a genteel sport with billions in Saudi Arabian money and brash style, LIV Golf is liquidating its operations.

The would-be rival to the PGA Tour, which was championed by major winner Phil Mickelson and run until 2025 by another former player of note, Greg Norman, was left scrambling when the Saudi Public Investment Fund pulled out last year. BC Partners Credit, after promising $300 million for a reboot dubbed “LIV 2.0,” reversed course after seeing the difficulty the golf league was having recruiting players.

Executives who were leading the effort to shepherd LIV through Chapter 11 bankruptcy told a U.S. Bankruptcy Court judge in New Jersey on Thursday that they would pivot toward a full liquidation of the league. The Saudi Public Investment Fund’s decision to back away from LIV, which had been a major component in the Mideast nation’s effort to promote itself on the global stage, proved to be an insurmountable hurdle. The league regularly drew controversy due to its Saudi backing, drawing protests at a Donald Trump-owned tournament site in New Jersey from families of 9/11 victims outraged by what they saw as the legitimizing of a state sponsor of the terrorist attacks.

Jon Rahm, one of the PGA veterans who accepted large paydays in order to go exclusive with LIV, has recently expressed a willingness to face penalties in order to return. Bryson DeChambeau, perhaps the most ardent promoter of LIV among current major championship contenders, faces an uncertain future after the dissolution of the league. The reluctance of Rahm, DeChambeau and others to sign onto LIV 2.0 sealed its fate.

LIV got its name from the Roman numerals for 54, which is the number of holes its three-day tournaments encompassed. Traditional PGA and USGA events span four days and 72 holes. Along with that departure from golf custom, LIV also allowed players to wear shorts and organized them into Formula 1-style teams with names like the Crushers. On the courses, loud music blared across every hole as players teed off in shotgun starts as opposed to the usual pattern of everyone beginning on Hole No. 1. It was all part of an intentional bid to disrupt the country-club feel and hushed tones of the PGA.

The Saudi billions did enable LIV to offer certain players nine-figure fees to defect, and also put up massive tournament purses. While the overall effort didn’t ultimately succeed, it did force the PGA to update its strategy and philosophy with regard to prize money and other aspects of its relationship with players.
