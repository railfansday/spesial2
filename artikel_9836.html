Converting WebP to JPG inside a Chrome extension: OffscreenCanvas in an MV3 offscreen document

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Chrome, Edge and most sites now hand you WebP or AVIF when you right-click an image and pick "Save image as". Renaming photo.webp to photo.jpg changes the label and nothing else: the bytes are still WebP, and the next print shop, marketplace upload form or old image editor rejects it.

I make PixLasso, a Chrome and Edge extension that saves every image on a page into one ZIP. The feature people asked for first was a right-click entry, "Save image as JPG", that converts on your computer and never uploads anything. This post is about how that conversion runs inside a Manifest V3 extension, and the three things that bit me.

So the work runs in an offscreen document (chrome.offscreen.createDocument with the BLOBS reason). It is a hidden page the worker opens on demand. The worker fetches nothing itself: it sends the job over chrome.runtime.sendMessage, the offscreen page fetches, decodes, converts, zips, creates a blob URL and asks the worker to start the download.

Canvas limits. Chrome refuses a canvas over 32,767 px on a side or 268,435,456 px in total. A 20,000 × 15,000 scan from a museum site is under the side limit and over the area limit, and getContext quietly returns a context that draws nothing. Trust the bytes, not the name. A URL ending in .jpg served with Content-Type: image/webp is common on CDNs, and the other way round happens too. The first bytes decide: RIFF....WEBP is WebP, \xFF\xD8\xFF is JPEG, \x89PNG is PNG, and AVIF is an ISO BMFF box with ftyp at offset 4. Sniff those, then decide whether a conversion is needed at all. If the source is already the wanted format, keep the original bytes: re-encoding a JPEG as JPEG only loses quality.

Messages sent before the offscreen document is ready are lost. createDocument resolves before the page's script has registered its onMessage listener. The offscreen page sends an offscreen-ready message as its last line, and the worker holds jobs until it has seen that message. Without the handshake, the first job after a cold start can disappear, which is the worst kind of bug to reproduce.

Two smaller ones: run jobs one at a time in the offscreen page so a 500-image batch does not hold 500 decoded bitmaps at once, and revoke each blob URL after chrome.downloads reports the download complete, with a 30-minute timer as a safety net in case the worker restarts and never sends the revoke.
