How to Match Your Resume to a Job Description With AI (Free Keyword Check)

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Posted on Nov 7 • Originally published at all-done.xyz

Disclosure: I'm the maker of All Done (all-done.xyz), a site of free online tools — no signup, no cost. This is a cross-post of the full guide on our blog, adapted for DEV.

Tailoring a resume to each job description is the single highest-leverage habit in a job hunt — and the one most people skip, because doing it by hand takes an hour per posting. The free AI workflow compresses it to about ten minutes: build once on an ATS-safe foundation, generate the summary, score the match, and close the gaps.

Each step uses a free tool with no signup, and each step feeds the next. Here's the loop that turns one generic resume into a matched application for every posting.

Step one is the foundation: a resume built in the ATS Resume Builder, where the single-column templates, standard headings, and text-based formatting mean parsers extract your information cleanly. A resume the software can't read can't match anything — formatting comes before keywords.

Step two sharpens the top of the page. The builder's "Write with AI" generates the professional summary from the job title alone — 2–4 sentences tuned to the role — plus experience bullets to match. This is also the fastest section to re-tune per posting, since the summary carries the most keyword weight in the least space.

Step three is the reality check. Paste your resume and the job description into the AI Resume Optimizer: it extracts the posting's keywords, cross-references your resume, and shows the match as a percentage with the missing terms listed. That missing-terms list is your edit list — concrete, specific, and per-posting.

Step four is closing the gaps, and this is where honesty is the strategy. Mirror the posting's phrasing for experience you genuinely have: "helped customers" becomes "customer success" if that's what you did. Add skills you truly hold but hadn't named. What you must never do is stuff keywords you can't defend — recruiters spot it in seconds, and a match score is worthless if the interview exposes it.

The whole loop runs in your browser with nothing uploaded and no account anywhere in the chain — your resume text stays on your device through the build, the summary generation, and the keyword check. And because nothing is metered, the workflow scales to every application: each posting gets its own ten-minute pass.

That's the compounding advantage. One tailored application beats five generic ones, and a free, unlimited loop is what makes "tailor every application" practical advice instead of aspirational advice.
