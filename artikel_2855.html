5 Bills to watch vs. Dolphins

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Buffalo Bills are looking to extend their current winning streak to six games this week as they take on the Miami Dolphins, who are looking to avoid extending their own three-game losing streak. In 17 matchups with No. 17 at the helm against Miami, the Bills are 14-3. While the Dolphins did win the last time these two teams met, scoring a decisive 30-13 victory over Buffalo last November, much has changed since then.

For starters, neither team's head coach from that game is with the club, as former Buffalo head coach Sean McDermott is unemployed, and former Dolphins head coach Mike McDaniel is the offensive coordinator for the 1-8 Los Angeles Chargers. Miami has torn the team down to the studs, as quarterback Tua Tagovailoa, running back De'Von Achane, and wideouts Jaylen Waddle and Tyreek Hill are either no longer with the club or injured. Buffalo, meanwhile, has started the Joe Brady era by winning eight of their new head coach's first nine games.

How can the Bills avoid dropping what should be a winnable game against a rebuilding divisional foe? It would help if their top players performed well, but with some injuries to navigate, the Bills will have some new faces stepping up this week. Here are five Bills to watch against Miami on Sunday._____________________________________________________________________________

Allen has long dominated the Dolphins, posting video-game numbers against Miami for the whole of his career. Allen has completed 66.6% of his passes for 4,608 passing yards, 45 passing touchdowns, and 11 interceptions. On the ground, he's run for 723 yards and five scores on 101 carries against Miami. Remember, this is across 17 games. Allen traditionally does great things against the Dolphins, and I'd expect that he'll continue that trend this Sunday. He might not run for the kinds of yards that he did in years' past against Miami, especially with a game coming up on Thursday night, but he will definitely be a central figure in the action. Look for the Bills to give Allen some quick-hitters early to establish a rhythm before hitting some big throws on play-action as the game progresses.

Dalton Kincaid is doubtful to play thanks to thumb and ankle injuries, and while it's entirely possible that he suits up, he'll likely be limited even if he does play. That means that Knox may have a chance to step up against a Miami defense that has not defended opposing tight ends well this season. George Kittle, Travis Kelce, Mason Taylor, T.J. Hockenson, Mike Gesicki, and others have either had big games or scored plenty of points—in real and fantasy football—against Miami. Knox isn't elite, but he is a reliable target for Josh Allen as his longest-tenured pass-catcher. If Kincaid is able to go, I expect he'll break off a big play or two, but if he can't, expect Knox to have his best day of the season.

Groot is the kind of guy who can just wreck anything the Dolphins have going for them on offense. Against second-year man Kyle McCord, who will be making his second start, Rousseau presents a problem for multiple reasons. First, his length gives him the ability to block passing lanes that would otherwise be open. Second, he is shifty enough and strong enough on the edge to make McCord uncomfortable regardless of the situation. Third, the Dolphins are very weak at tackle, and having someone like Rousseau out there with Ed Oliver, Deone Walker, Bradley Chubb, T.J. Sanders, and T.J. Parker doesn't bode well for a struggling offensive line. Austin Jackson, Miami's starter at right tackle, is ranked No. 71 out of 72 qualifying tackles at Pro Football Focus. He's No. 69 in pass block grade and No. 65 in run block grade. Rousseau should be excited to see Jackson across from him this week.

The rookie is likely to start this week with veteran Dorian Williams out due to a calf injury he suffered in last week's win over the New York Jets. That's a big loss for Buffalo, as Williams has been very good of late. However, the team is high on Elarms-Orr, and he has flashed some big-hitting ability early in his career. He's appeared on just 21% of Buffalo's defensive snaps this season, totaling 18 tackles, but he alreayd has two fumble recoveries to his name, as well. He's struggled a bit in pass coverage, so look for Miami to try to manipulate him through motion and play-action early. Will the Bills call veteran Shaq Thompson up from the practice squad to supplement the linebacker group this week? And, if they do, will Thompson eat into Alarms-Orr's playing time? We'll find out on Sunday, but look for No. 52 to make some big hits this week.

The second-year man has looked totally lost this season, as a rookie year that flashed some potential has given way to a second season where he has lost not only a shot at a starting gig, but really any shot at playing many meaningful snaps. Rookie Davison Igbinosun has far outplayed Hairston this season, but with Christian Benford out this week, Hairston has a chance to build back some trust with the coaching staff. Miami's passing game isn't exactly elite—it's not even league-average, as the Dolphins rank No. 31 in attempts, No. 29 in passing yards, and No. 29 in passing touchdowns—so this is a good time for Hairston to make a play or two. I think the ship has sailed, at least for now, where we look at Hairston as someone who should be playing when all of Buffalo's corners are healthy; however, it would be nice for him to have a week where he looks like someone who could play in a big spot if needed. That's a low bar to clear, but one he's consistently knocked over throughout this season. Veteran Tre'Davious White may see time as a practice squad elevation, too. If Hairston can't see snaps and play effectively this week, then it might be time for a bigger conversation about the former Kentucky product.
