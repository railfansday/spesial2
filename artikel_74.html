Exiled leader Sheikh Hasina plans to return to Bangladesh on Dec. 16

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

After two years of exile in India, ousted Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina has set a return date to Bangladesh. She was sentenced to death in absentia last year over a brutal crackdown that left hundreds dead.

Ousted Bangladeshi Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina plans to return to her country on December 16 after two years of exile in India, according to her spokesperson.

That's despite the Bangladeshi government saying she can only return from the neighboring nation through extradition.

"Hasina is a convicted criminal; a fugitive terrorist," Humaiun Kobir, Bangladesh's minister of state for ⁠foreign affairs, ​told Reuters.

"She can only return home by extradition treaty; in handcuffs, and under ​strict surveillance."

Hasina, however, urged current Bangladeshi Prime Minister Tarique Rahman to arrange her return on the national airline.

"Since Sheikh Hasina was flown to India on a Bangladesh aircraft on August 5th, 2024, she now calls upon the Government to arrange for ‌her air transport from Delhi back to Dhaka," the spokesperson, Syed Mozammel Ali, said in a ‌statement.

"Having ​exiled her contrary to the Constitution under such circumstances, it is now the responsibility of the government to make possible her return to Bangladesh on Victory Day on December 16th."

December 16 is the anniversary of the end of Bangladesh's 1971 liberation war with Pakistan.

Hasina, having been sentenced in absentia by a Bangladeshi court last year, faces the death penalty on returning home.

She was ousted by angry protesters in 2024 amid a deadly government crackdown that killed around 1,400 people, according to the UN.

Hasina rejected the verdict and has said she is ready to fight the case once she is back. She has also denied ordering the crackdown.

Earlier this year, Hasina had vowed to return to Bangladesh while speaking at a correspondents' event in New Delhi via live audio feed.

"I know that they may put me in jail, or they may kill me, or anything may happen, but still I have to go back," she said. "Fear cannot decide my duty to the people," she had said then.

This, however, is the first time she has set a date, setting the stage for potential legal disputes.

India did not immediately comment on the set return date.

In April, the Foreign Ministry said it was examining Bangladesh's request to ​extradite her and that it wanted ‌to "engage constructively with the new government and further strengthen bilateral ties."
