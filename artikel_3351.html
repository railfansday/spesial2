10 Funny Movies To Watch With Your Family This Thanksgiving

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Thanksgiving is a time of great football, food, and family. Whether you're gathering with blood relatives or a chosen family, the spirited holiday offers a chance to slow down and reflect on all the things, big and small, to be thankful for. There's nothing better than stuffing yourself with moist turkey, melt-in-your-mouth mashed potatoes, green bean casserole, and freshly baked dinner rolls before camping in front of the TV for a nap (or two) and long-overdue family time with favorite comedies and family-friendly movies.

There are countless holiday movies and TV, including Thanksgiving-themed episodes of The Simpsons, to watch to put you in the mood for gratitude and celebration. Thanksgiving is the perfect time to take time off from work, reconnect with loved ones, and laugh a little.

Millennials will undoubtedly remember Robin Williams' bonkers performance in Les Mayfield's sci-fi comedy Flubber. Through developing a new energy source, Medfield College professor Philip Brainard (Williams) invents a sentient green goo that escapes before flying through the neighborhood. Brainard must recapture it, prove its worth to the college board, and steal back his fiancé, college president Sara Jean Reynolds (Marcia Gay Harden), from his arch nemesis. Slapstick gags and raucous humor characterize this family-friendly gem that has never gotten its proper due.

Williams always dove headfirst into his roles, particularly when it came to comedy. He committed to the bit and delivered knockout performances. As Brainard, he manages to find a tremendous heart pounding at the center of the movie's themes of love and thankfulness.

Director Gurinder Chadha takes cues from National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation, but makes the story a tasty Thanksgiving treat. The family gathers for a tremendous feast, but there's naturally plenty of hijinks from various oddball relatives that would give the Griswold family a run for their money. The cast is as star-studded as they come and includes Kyra Sedgwick, Julianna Margulies, and Alfre Woodard.

The hilarious ensemble piece celebrates dysfunctional families (we all have one) and how to find even the smallest things for which to be thankful. What's Cooking? also shines a spotlight on different kinds of families and relationships. It's not only about blood relatives, but a chosen family comes into view, as well.

The Barry Sonnenfeld-directed and starring comedy, Addams Family Values serves as a sequel to 1991's The Addams Family, but it can be viewed on its own without any prior knowledge of the original. For not-yet-inducted family members, Addams Family Values supplies all the macabre, yet good-natured, humor you could want, including an epic scene of Wednesday (Christina Ricci) lighting the set of a camp play on fire.

Joan Cussack joins the cast as Fester's (Christopher Lloyd) new gold-digging wife Debbie Jellinsky, a black widow with a thirst for killing her husbands. In true Addams Family fashion, Values normalizes the strange with smartly written dialogue and funny bone-tickling physicality. It's a guaranteed laugh fest.

Rewatching A Charlie Brown Thanksgiving has been a Thanksgiving tradition for many families for decades - and for good reason. But for those who haven't experienced it yet, Charlie Brown and his friends' antics deliver both humor and thoughtfulness. It's about honoring the holiday through a melodramatic story about Charlie Brown throwing a feast. Naturally, the shenanigans make it a welcome addition to anyone's festivities. And oh yes, the physical football gag is pure cinema.

A Charlie Brown Thanksgiving reminds us to be thankful for the life we've been given, the people who make it worth living, and how we can find a silver lining when things go wrong. Charlie Brown might get exasperated with his friends, but he loves them and wouldn't trade them for the world.

Decidedly not for kids, Donald Petrie's Grumpy Old Men gears its witty, sometimes vulgar, humor for older audiences. Max (Walter Matthau) and John (Jack Lemmon) hate each other, and they've been fighting since 1938. As they've grown older, they've learned to cherish their friendship, oddly enough. They hold grudges, that's to be sure, and will do anything for a good dose of payback. But when things go sideways, health-wise, it becomes clear that they really do care deeply about one another.

Beneath the humor, Grumpy Old Men contains powerful messages about living every day like one's last, cherishing friendships, letting go, and bracing for the future. Matthau and Lemmon expertly offer up performances that capture the humor and heart of the situation. It's also about allowing others into your world when it's the most difficult mountain to climb.

Robin Williams' turn as Alan Parrish in Joe Johnston's Jumanji never gets old. As a child, he discovers a wooden boardgame buried on the site of his father's factory. He takes it home and begins to play, inviting his crush Sarah Whittle (Laura Bell Bundy) to join him. Well, things end horribly with Alan being sucked into the game for decades. Sarah grows into a woman (now played by Bonnie Hunt) afraid of the world and in therapy.

Two kids, Judy (Kiersten Dunst) and Peter (Bradley Pierce), move into the old Parrish house and find the game. They unwittingly unleash giant killer mosquitoes, mischievous monkeys, and a flesh-hungry lion. Oh, and they also free Alan from the game, along with hunter Van Pelt (Jonathan Hyde), who is on a mission to kill him. Dated CGI aside, Jumanji makes you grateful for the cards you've been dealt in life because it could all be worse. What's more, it's one of the best family-friendly movies of the 1990s.

Planes, Trains, and Automobiles stars John Candy and Steve Martin at the top of their game. When Neal (Martin) and Del (Candy) travel home for Thanksgiving, they get a rude awakening when a snowstorm hits the Midwest that prevents them from landing in Chicago. As such, they end up stranded in Wichita, Kansas, and then travel by train and, you guessed it, automobile just to get home to their families. Wackiness leads to an unlikely friendship that makes the film a holiday essential.

A surprising emotional core emerges by the last scene, boosting the already great film into the stratosphere. Planes, Trains, and Automobiles isn't only about the humor but the real heart.

The Muppets doesn't get talked about enough. The comedy musical features all the usual suspects: Kermit the Frog, Miss Piggy, Fozzie the Bear, Animal, and countless others. After parting ways, everyone went off to live their own lives. Some stepped further into the spotlight, while muppets like Kermit retreated into the shadows. Their once-cherished studio has fallen into a derelict state and is being sold to Tex Richman (Chris Cooper), who plans to demolish the property and drill for oil.

When Gary (Jason Segel) takes his girlfriend Mary (Amy Adams) and muppet brother Walter (Peter Linz) to Hollywood, they make a stop at the rundown Muppets studio only to see how tragic it is now. Walter plots to get the band back together in order to raise $10 million in order to save the property from utter destruction. The Muppets is equal parts road trip movie and flashy musical that knows exactly how to tug on the heartstrings, delivering tearful moments aplenty. Of course, it's also characterized by a signature style and humor that make any day truly joyful.

A loose remake of the 1950 movie of the same name, Shawn Levy's Cheaper by the Dozen examines big families, and there's no better time than Thanksgiving to revisit or watch it for the very first time. Steve Martin and Bonnie Hunt play Tom and Kate Baker, heads of a household to a whopping total of 12 children. When Kate launches her book tour, Tom is left to parent all 12 kids without any guidance or know-how on taking care of so many at once. It's a hilarious premise that more than delivers the laughs.

For all big families out there, Cheaper by the Dozen no doubt resonates with its situational humor, particularly when it comes to siblings fighting and a father that can't handle the heat in the kitchen. Once again, Martin knocks it out of the park with his performance, and the cast, which includes pop star Hilary Duff, rises as one of the best comedic ensembles ever.

Jimmy Hayward's 2013 animated movie Free Birds resets Thanksgiving Day as not Turkey Day but Pizza Day. When a smart turkey named Reggie (Owen Wilson) gets selected as the president's pardoned turkey, he enjoys a lavish life of late-night TV and all the pizza he can eat. But a rogue turkey enlists him, or rather kidnaps him, for a top secret mission, and Reggie goes back in time to stop turkeys from being on the menu for Thanksgiving.

Free Birds stars a smorgasbord of big-name talent, including Amy Poehler, Woody Harrelson, Keith David, George Takei, and Dan Fogler, and makes dozens of wisecracking jokes about the game Angry Birds. At the end of the day, who doesn't love a bunch of animated turkeys running around trying to save all turkeys from being eaten on Thanksgiving?
