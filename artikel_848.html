Genge eager for England to build on victory in All Blacks rematch

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

England thrashed New Zealand 37-12 at the Allianz Stadium on Saturday and will face each other again on Saturday, having both finished second in their respective Nations Championship groups; England also beat New Zealand last November

Ellis Genge called on England to back up their record victory over New Zealand as they go in search of an unprecedented hat-trick against the All Blacks.

The rivals face an immediate rematch of England's 37-12 procession at Allianz Stadium that meant they each finished second in their Nations Championship pools, setting up the battle for third place on Saturday.

Following on from last November's 33-19 triumph, it was only the second time in the fixture's 121-year existence that England had registered consecutive wins - and Steve Borthwick's men are aiming to carve out their own piece of history.

"Try and emulate it, do the same thing again," Genge said. "It's as simple as it sounds. I don't think you can take anything away from New Zealand. They're not any less of a beast next weekend than what the challenge was this week.

"New Zealand tends to be one team that catches you off guard, but what shocks you is that they're actually quite direct.

"You think they're all traditionally very good at moving the ball, but they're very punchy and we got that right against them."

England delivered the best result of Borthwick's four years in charge by flattening the All Blacks to extend their winning run to five Tests and issue a warning that they will be genuine contenders at next year's World Cup.

But amid the euphoria that has greeted confirmation of their post-Six Nations revival, Genge will keep perspective - recalling the criticism their followed their 45-21 drubbing at Ellis Park in the Nations Championship opener.

"Sport's fickle in that sense," Genge said. "We were the worst team in the world against South Africa when we got hammered at their gaff about five months ago.

"Then we hammered Fiji because of a red card apparently, then scraped past Argentina and then now we're the best thing since sliced bread. It's one of them.

"Sometimes the outside noise can creep in. Sometimes it can build you up and that's a long way down to the bottom.

"You've got to try and just keep your head on your shoulders and just build results. We do as best as we can at the job to keep everyone's head on their shoulders, especially with a young squad.

"Some of us miserable old boys are a bit easier, but the boys get so excited to play and be a part of it, which is brilliant. You've got to prepare them for that as well."

Seb Atkinson was the last of England's five tryscorers at Allianz Stadium and he marked the crowning moment of a stellar individual performance by sticking out his tongue and making a throat-slitting gesture.

The celebration has been mistaken online as a reference to the All Blacks' haka, but Atkinson revealed it was born out of his interest in gladiatorial combat and is deciding whether to make it his calling card.

"I've had a bit of stick already on that, so we'll see whether it stays," Atkinson said. "It's a gladiator celebration. I used to have a big interest in Roman history so it was bit of a gladiatorial theme from when they used to do it in the Colosseum.

"I enjoyed learning about that when I was growing up and I trialled it with Gloucester and then I felt like I had to give it a bit extra today [Saturday]."

Atkinson is expected to feature again for England against New Zealand next week and hopes the hosts can take a leaf out of South Africa's book by showing the hunger to evolve like the back-to-back world champions.

"Any professional team, any sporting team, always wants to strive to get better because a team that gets caught still is in trouble," he added. "That's what the South Africans have done really well over the last period.

"Their game has been transformed. We'll be focusing on ourselves, but with an eye just to keep getting better and see what we can do."
