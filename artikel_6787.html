Why Does A Crypto Bro On ‘The Altruists’ Have The Most Mature Erotic Fan Fiction Take?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Broken clocks are right twice a day about *checks notes* Dramione.

What sets The Altruists apart from other limited series about millennial scammers in the 2010s is a love story between two like-minded individuals who just *got* each other’s quirks, habits, and Harry Potter fandom. Sure, the relationship between Sam Bankman-Fried (Anthony Boyle) and Caroline Ellison (Julia Garner) was a contributing factor to their corporate failure and fraudulent activities. But it’s compelling. And, in a time where geeky pursuits are increasingly under the microscope, The Altruists presents the most mature and romantic view of erotic fan fiction that we’ve seen in a while.

Early on in The Altruists, Sam discovers that Caroline reads and writes explicit Draco/Hermione fan fiction. (Because television is a visual medium Caroline also prints her story out, which is odd. Not a bad idea, though, given recent events.) He laughs nervously at first, but then reacts positively. “This is hot,” he insists. “You know what you like. You know how to express yourself. You know what turns you on. And you can talk about it. I think you’re incredible. I think you’re so cool.”

Holy nuance, Batman! Why does a 23-year-old MIT grad have a less judgmental take on erotica than half of the think pieces I’ve read over the last 2 to 3 years? Yes, exploring fantasies and kinks and learning how to express them in a safe environment are some of the many positives about fan fiction and/or romance as a genre. This opinion is surprisingly mature for a guy who wants to turn finance into a video game, dresses like Mark Zuckerberg’s kid brother, communicates via memes, and almost exclusively hires his childhood friends. Granted, in the moment he wants to impress Caroline, and The Altruists is not a documentary. But it goes to show that, when it comes to nerdy things, you really do have to ask a nerd.

The real Ellison is a well-documented Harry Potter fan and a LARPer. According to the NY Post, she wrote a regency romance novel while awaiting sentencing. Fortune’s profile of Ellison includes her original fantasy writing. Gawker went ham on Ellison’s Tumblr and found a link to Gossip Girl fan fiction. You can skim an archive of an archive of those Tumblr posts and see references to Lore Olympus, the fic “Harry Potter and the Methods of Rationality,” posts comparing herself to Hermione, and more... but no real evidence that she posted to FF.net or AO3 herself.

The Altruists is fiction land. Not every character shares Sam’s tolerant views, sadly. When Caroline’s coworkers find the story, cheekily titled “A Ride On Draco’s Nimbus,” they make fun of her. Anticipating that she’ll no longer be taken seriously at pitch meetings, she quits. This frees her up to start working with Sam, who again assures her that he doesn’t find her writing fan fiction embarrassing. Later, one of the only other female employees at FTX mocks Caroline behind her back.

But in The Altruists finale, when the press tries to expose Caroline’s “controversial” writing, Sam shuts them down in a televised interview.

I’m just going to say it: that line is so romantic. It’s such an ingenious choice to have a public defense of writing fan fiction serve as Sam’s final declaration of love on The Altruists. He says it direct to camera, as if he knows Caroline is watching. That’s how he expresses to her, and the world, that he still cares about her. You know what else is hot? Having a moral line you refuse to cross, no matter what happens.

Also, in the words of terminally online millennials everywhere, he’s right and he should say it! As the fan-fiction-to-novel-to-screen adaptation pipeline (the After series, The Love Hypothesis, Game Changers AKA Kip and Scott’s book from Heated Rivalry, etc.) pushes the form into the mainstream, outsider efforts to understand fan fiction’s pull unfortunately tend to further “other” it. Even calling something a guilty pleasure still implies that it’s something one should feel shame about. When headlines about fan fiction open with “Why Are You So Into This” and “Celebrities Reveal How They Really Feel” the clear negative connotation hangs above the most positive arguments. What if we didn’t do that?

Throughout the series, The Altruists will occasionally show Caroline self-inserting as Hermione through the fic that she writes. It’s not framed in a way that makes her look especially silly or childish. It just tells the audience how she’s feeling about Draco Sam at any given time, simple as that. It’s a creative outlet! Maybe we should, and I can’t believe I’m saying this, be more like Sam Bankman-Fried*.

Unfortunately, I’m not even sure we can prove Dramione is Ellison’s Harry Potter OTP IRL. Pity, because Boyle originated the role of Draco’s bookish son Scorpius Malfoy in the play Harry Potter and the Cursed Child. What better meta silver lining could a convicted felon fangirl ask for? She needs it, too, because I don’t think the real Bankman-Fried defended her like that either. Bummer.

*A fictional version of Sam Bankman-Fried, written by Graham Moore and portrayed by Boyle in The Altruists, a work of fiction. Don’t get it twisted!
