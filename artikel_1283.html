World football’s biggest stadium boasts an enormous capacity of 114,000 seats - but a new ground currently under construction will soon claim top spot by surpassing it.

The Rungrado 1st of May Stadium in North Korea is top of the world’s biggest stadiums list, as things stand. Built in 1989, the unique-looking stadium in the secretive nation initially boasted a staggering 150,000 capacity, before being reduced to 114k in 2014.

It’s one of only four stadiums that currently boast a capacity north of 100,000 seats, along with the Michigan Stadium (107k), the Ohio Stadium (102k) and the Melbourne Cricket Ground (100k).

Barcelona’s Spotify Camp Nou is expected to have a final capacity of 105,000 once completed, moving it above the latter two stadiums, while Manchester United have secured the land to build a new 100,000-seater stadium that will replace Old Trafford in the early 2030s.

However, the Hassan II Stadium in Morocco, which is being built ahead of the 2030 World Cup, will surpass the Rungrado 1st of May Stadium with a planned capacity of 1115,000 seats.

But the Hassan II Stadium will only be in second spot by the time it opens in late 2029. That’s because another stadium, the VinFast - Bronze Drum Stadium is set to open in July 2027 with a breathtaking capacity of 135,000 - making it the world’s biggest stadium by some distance.

Construction of the stadium in Hanoi, Vietnam, only began nine months ago but is already taking shape, as recent video footage shows.

The stadium is named and modelled on a traditional Vietnamese bronze drum and is being built at the centre of the broader Olympic Sports City development, which is poised to cover around 22,200 acres on the outskirts of Hanoi, according to The Mirror.

It’s understood the total cost of the project is an eye-watering $38 billion (£28.8 billion), and that construction teams are working non-stop shifts in order to meet a series of demanding deadlines.

The reinforced concrete structure has a deadline of December 2026. Installation of the dome system, electrical systems and finishing work will then begin ahead of the planned July 2027 opening.

The original date for the stadium’s opening was August 2028, but the project is currently ahead of schedule. The retractable roof is now set to be in place by May 2027.

The bronze drum is considered a symbol of ancient Vietnamese culture, representing "the spirit of community, strength, and longevity," according to the project's architects.

It’s hoped that having the world’s largest stadium will boost Vietnam’s chances of hosting a huge global sporting event like the World Cup or the Olympic Games.
