The Roadmap Ahead

The S&P 500 Index is topping. Gold and silver are bottoming. It's a process. In our view, both sides of the trade have never been more attractive in terms of valuation and timing.

The S&P 500 finally experienced a significant correction last week. The volatility index (VIX) spiked to almost 40 last Friday. With the S&P 500 down more than 8% that week and volatility so high, we booked significant short-term profits on our S&P 500 put portfolio in our macro and long/short funds on Friday. With President Trump reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO of Nvidia on Monday and the massive short-covering rally in big tech that followed, we began layering those puts back in and became fully positioned on the short side again yesterday. We have had several days this month when these three funds have been up while the S&P 500 has been down.

In bear markets, put portfolios need to be tactically traded. There is no guarantee we will be successful on the short side going forward, but we have high conviction that there is much more to play out on the downside for the stock market at large. We have been minimizing the risk of shorting the market through put options while preserving the asymmetric reward-to-risk upside potential to a further downturn in the S&P 500 in these three funds. These are lessons learned by Crescat's founder and CEO in managing hedge funds through the 2000s tech bust and the 2008 Global Financial Crisis.

Meanwhile, our activist metals mining stock portfolio has experienced a significant pullback in line with the precious metals markets. We believe the precious metals complex is in deep oversold territory, on par with other major V-bottoms historically, not unlike in the depths of the 2008 GFC and the 2020 Covid crash.

There are three notable historical precedents for our primary macro investment thesis today: the deflationary Great Depression, the stagflationary bear market of 1973-74, and the bursting of the technology bubble in 2000. In each of these periods, the premier gold mining stocks appreciated substantially while the broader equity market suffered major declines. While not all gold mining companies were successful in these periods and risk remains, we believe the following three macro charts highlight the parallels we find ourselves in today and provide the roadmap for what lies ahead.

In the deflationary Great Depression, Homestake Mining (HM) was the largest gold mining company in the US and dramatically outperformed the Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA). From the DJIA's high in September 1929 to its low in July 1932, it lost 89% of its value, yet HM's stock price was up 48%. By February 1936, HM shares had gained 580% while the DJIA was still down 59% from its top.

During the stagflationary stock market slide of 1973 and 1974, the S&P 500 fell 48% from its high in January 1973 to its low in October 1974, a period over which the Barronâs Gold Mining Index (BGMI) rose 193%. Notably, the BGMI began rising almost two months before the S&P 500 peaked and continued rising during the ensuing bear market. From its low in November 1972 to its intermediate high in August 1974, the BGMI was up 376%, while the S&P 500 was down 34% over the same period.

The early innings of the 2000 tech bust marked the end of a two-decade bear market for precious metals mining stocks. From its high in October 1980 to its low in November 2000, the BGMI fell 82% while the S&P 500 gained 985% over the same period.

The worst of that bear market for mining stocks occurred from 1996 to 2000, when technology stocks were booming, potentially not unlike today. As the technology bubble burst, a new secular bull market for precious metals mining stocks began. From November 2000 to March 2008, the Philadelphia Gold and Silver Index (XAU) gained 393% while the Nasdaq Composite declined 27%.

Over the last seven years, we have curated our activist metals portfolio with the help of geologic experts. It includes many of what we believe are the world's most exciting new, large precious and critical metals discoveries. Our conviction in these companies has not changed, and we expect the biggest upside yet in our portfolio is still ahead of us.

We derive a price target for gold of $20,000 per troy ounce based on two independent macro models. The first looks at the trend of global M2 money supply compared to the above-ground gold stock as shown in the chart below. With the precious metal now under accumulation by global central banks, we think this chart is highly relevant. Extending the trendline points to a price target for gold of $20,000 in approximately four years, though we suspect global M2 could accelerate given the current fiscal imbalances and geopolitical climate to shorten this timeline.

Our second approach to the target price for gold uses a gold-to-S&P 500 ratio model and assumes a run-of-the-mill US stock market decline of 50% and an ensuing dollar devaluation. Starting from historic large cap equity valuations today and record fiscal imbalances, we believe such a path could once again lie ahead. Each of the prior peaks in this ratio was catalyzed by a stock market crash from historic large cap equity valuations and a substantial dollar devaluation. A 50% lower S&P 500, combined with a 5.25 gold-to-S&P 500 multiple, which is well below its 1980 peak of 7.58, though slightly above its 1933 peak of 4.76, also gets us to our $20,000 price target for gold.

A market top may have already happened or could be forming very soon. The time window from the peak of the stock market to the ensuing peak in the gold price was only 4.3 years from September 1929 to January 1934. It took 7 years from January 1973 to January 1980.

Our activist metals portfolio remains the largest long investment theme across our five hedge funds. As shown below, we currently have US$412 million in exposure across 61 positions, including 30 companies with bona fide discoveries using the Newmont criteria (3 or more 100 gram-meter intercepts on a gold equivalent basis). Most of our companies are public, but we have 13 private companies. We have ownership stakes exceeding 5% on a partially diluted basis in 45 companies, with median firmwide partially diluted ownership of 9.1%.

We expect our portfolio companies to have 102 drills operating over the next 12 months, including substantially more greenfield drilling than all the major mining producers combined. Collectively, based on our geologic estimates, our companies have identified 256 million target gold-equivalent ounces of precious and critical metals in viable US-friendly mining jurisdictions around the world. In our strong view, these include many of the new tier-1 metal discoveries and deposits that the major mining companies will need to acquire in order to extend their reserves. The major mining companies have under-invested in new resource development for the past 15 years. As a result, the industry is facing a supply cliff. We believe Crescat's portfolio companies will contribute substantially to the new supply that the world needs.

The activist metals portfolio subset consists of firmwide holdings across all Crescat funds and SMA accounts in the mining industry where Crescat strives to help companies build economic metal resources through exploration and drilling. For some companies, Crescat provides capital and geologic guidance to help companies build resources across Crescat's activist portfolio.

Crescat target resource estimates are based on internal modeling and geologic estimates, and include various assumptions based on analysis of geology, geophysics, geochemistry, historic drill assays, and metallurgical recovery data received to date. Target resource estimates are discounted based on drilling progress to date, an assessment of the management and technical team's strengths and weaknesses affecting their ability to advance the project, and environmental, local community, and government permitting risk factors. Estimates are displayed on a gold equivalent basis based on current price-to-gold ratios for silver, copper, and other metals if the primary metal is other than gold. Further drilling, assaying, resource modeling, and engineering studies will be required to determine whether Crescat's target resource estimates can be reasonably expected to be achieved. Crescat's target resource estimates are updated monthly across the entire portfolio.

The number of active drills includes the number of drills currently in operation doing exploration and/or infill drilling or expected to be deployed over the next twelve months based on each company's drilling plans and Crescat's assessment of the company's ability to finance and execute those plans.

The S&P 500 is still valued near record all-time highs across a variety of measures, including Shiller CAPE ratio, market cap-to-GDP, EV-to-sales, EV-to-free cash flow, and price-to-book. Don't overthink it! In our strong view, as the reflexive circular financing that drove AI capex bubble goes into reverse and drives the AI bust, it's extremely positive for our short S&P 500 and long precious and critical metals mining thesis. That's because we believe the Fed will cut interest rates and embark on its biggest inflationary money printing yet in 2027. By stark contrast, the Fed funds futures market today is still pricing in two-and-a-half more Fed rate hikes (and implicitly zero QE) over the next year. Crescat's founder and CEO thinks this is the biggest mismatch in Fed expectations versus plausible reality that he has ever seen. It is a rare setup that we believe will prove explosive to the upside for gold prices in the months and years ahead.

Across Crescat's five funds, we have built a portfolio of what we believe to be some of the biggest and best new major gold, silver, and copper discoveries in safe US-friendly mining jurisdictions around the world. We believe our activist metals portfolio is deeply undervalued. Our small-cap, high-growth, exploration-focused companies have been flying under the radar of the general investing public. Many of our small-cap companies have already made large-cap discoveries, in terms of the potential future mineable metal value they have identified through drilling. Based on the estimates of our geologic expert, Bill Pearson, PhD, we believe the intrinsic value of our portfolio companies is worth many multiples of where the market currently values them.

At Crescat, we have built an activist metals portfolio that we believe has outstanding upside relative to gold for the current macro environment. Our metals portfolio propelled us to five of the top sixteen performing hedge funds in the world in 2025, according to the Preqin database1. We are determined to do something like that again in 2027 and beyond. The pullback in precious metals in 2026 is way overdone, in our strong opinion. It presents an excellent opportunity to deploy capital into Crescat's funds now. Yes, the precious and critical metals markets are volatile, but the potential upside for the macro environment ahead more than justifies the risk, in our strong view.

The opportunities to rotate out of mega cap tech and into undervalued new growth sectors of the market are not just limited to precious and critical metals miners. We see extraordinary opportunity in biotechnology as well, which we view as part of the defensive health care sector. As a reminder, the Crescat Biotech Fund is launching on January 1, 2027. We believe we are in the early stages of a new biotech investment cycle following a prolonged post-pandemic correction. The sector offers attractive valuations, accelerating biomedical innovation, and a looming patent expiration cliff facing big pharma that we believe will drive M&A activity.

Our investment focus is on late-stage clinical and emerging commercial companies developing transformational therapies, with an emphasis on identifiable value-creating catalysts such as Phase II and III clinical trial results, FDA approvals, commercial launches, strategic partnerships, and pharmaceutical mergers and acquisitions.
