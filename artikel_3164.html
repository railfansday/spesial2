Microdrama Revenues Hit $20B, But Profits Outside China Still Weak – MPA Report

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The global microdrama business is booming, but outside of China, where ByteDance rules the roost, profits haven’t yet caught up with revenues.

That’s according to a Media Partners Asia (MPA) report, which reveals revenues on a global basis are estimated to rise 24% year-on-year to hit $20B in 2026. That will hit $32B by 2031, a growth rate of 9.8% per year, but profitability is still thin.

The MPA’s second annual Micro-Drama Economy report notes that outside of China, where microdramas first emerged, every 56 cents in a dollar are spent on marketing and another 18 cents goes to app stores and payment providers, which is significantly reducing earnings. Excluding China, the microdrama biz is worth $4.87B, with the U.S. the largest at $1.71B, but has fallen 2% year-over-year and will be overtaken by Asia Pacific (excluding China) by 2029. The overall revenue figure will grow to $11B in 2031. Watch on Deadline

The MPA noted the two biggest microdrama groups take revenues of more than $1B a year, but within that DramaBox recorded a net margin of 3.2% in 2025 and ReelShort lost $12M, though it made an $8M profit the first quarter of this year.

DramaBox and GoodShort, which announced a merger at the end of October, along with ReelShort, Kunlun and NetShort collectively account for 65% of revenues outside of China, where the real action is right now. All four apps are Chinese-owned or backed. India is providing the fastest growth, with revenues tripling this year, as local platforms Kuku TV and Story TV boomed and JioStar debuted in the space in April with JioHotstar service Tadka. India is also the largest Asian market outside China with $322M.

MPA’s assessment is that the microdrama business outside of China is not yet settled on a model, but TikTok is predicted to be a dominant force, as it is “positioned on every side” – selling apps’ advertising, carrying their series and, through its free app PineDrama, competing with them.

TikTok’s parent, ByteDance is applying the model that shaped the Chinese market – using free, advertising-funded apps to gain audiences first and monetizing later – and MPA predicts it will gain “significant scale in both the U.S.” and Southeast Asia by 2031. Of course, ByteDance was forced to sell a majority of its shares in TikTok in the U.S., but does retain a minority stake.

YouTube and Meta are not expected to make a major impression on the microdrama space, while locally the likes of Vigloo in Korea and Fuji TV’s Fod Short in Japan are competing with Chinese-backed apps.

China accounts for 76% of total microdrama revenues ($15.2B), following 90% growth in 2025. This will slow to 6.7% in 2031 as viewing plateaus. The MPA doesn’t estimate profits in China as none of the leading players such as top trio ByteDance, Tencent and Kuaishou single microdrama out in their financial reporting.

Advertising on free services took over paid viewing last year, and this year accounts for 69% of revenues, That will grow to 79% in 2031. ByteDance, which operates Hongguo and Douyin, earning 59% of China’s revenues. ByteDance, Tencent and Kuaishou collectively account for 82% of the market.

MPA calculates revenue as what viewers, advertisers and brands pay before app-store and payment fees; and outside China it covers dedicated apps and their web stores, not general social feeds. In China, MPA’s estimates exclude producers’ spending on AI models and platform investment, which are both included in the National Radio and Television Administration’s RMB100B estimate for 2025.

“Demand for micro drama is established in every major market, but outside China this is still an emerging business, and it has yet to show that it can earn a durable profit,” said Vivek Couto, CEO and Executive Director of MPA.

“The leading apps spend more than half of their revenue to acquire viewers, and most of those viewers do not pay. Consolidation has begun, and China has already moved to free, advertising-funded viewing. The companies that endure will be those that retain their viewers, bill them directly and invest in titles worth returning to.”

The MPA report raised a further notable asides: new title listings on apps outside China rose from about 100,000 in 2025 to 440,000 in 2026, and 75% were made with AI. This counts series that are made in different languages more than once. MPA’s research shows an AI-made title costs “about a tenth of a series filmed in Los Angeles,” it added.

“AI has made a title cheap enough to test almost any idea, and catalogues have filled accordingly,” said Myat Pan Phyu (May), Lead Analyst, Micro Drama and Vertical Video for MPA. “Viewing has moved far less: live action still dominates the leading titles on most of the platforms we track.

“What decides a platform’s economics is how many of its titles earn back the marketing spent on them. That favours companies with established IP, stars and audiences of their own, which is why local broadcasters, studios and telecom operators matter to the next phase.”
