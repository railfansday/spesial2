How to Pull Off Coffee Nails, From Creamy Latte Shades to Deep Espresso

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The cozy brown manicure trend works as a glossy neutral, a velvet cat-eye, or a caramel French tip.

There’s a lot to love about coffee, and even if you’re not a fan of the drink, the color itself, with a rich, deep brown, serves as the perfect neutral to pair with your favorite light or dark shade. On nails, it works seamlessly across skin tones. And don’t think that coffee brown is only reserved for cooler winter months. Nail experts share how to wear the shade as a base, accent, or main character color this fall and beyond.

Max Estrada, a professional nail artist and founder and creative director of eNailCouture based in Tucson, Arizona, calls the coffee shade “latte lacquer” to describe its range in the rich brown color family. “It’s essentially the beauty equivalent of walking into a gorgeous Italian café and being presented with an entire menu of indulgent coffee creations,” he says. “Think creamy cappuccino, warm caramel, rich mocha, toasted hazelnut, and deep espresso.” It’s a beautiful spectrum of warm, sophisticated neutrals inspired by different shades and textures of coffee.

Estrada notes that the trend ties into the gourmand fragrance movement. “We’ve fallen in love with perfumes that smell like vanilla, caramel, chocolate, and coffee, and now we’re bringing that same delicious, comforting luxury to our fingertips,” Estrada shares. According to Estrada, what separates coffee from other brown polish shades is its depth and richness.

When choosing your version of a coffee manicure, Viktoriia Voloshyna, international judge, award-winning nail educator, and founder of Tory Pro Nails Studio in Beverly Hills, suggests thinking of a coffee menu, from light to dark:Milk foam: a soft ivory with a gentle glowFlat white: a warm, creamy beigeLatte: a soft satin beigeCaramel: a rich, warm brownEspresso: a deep coffee brown or a dark chocolate brown

“All these shades mix beautifully on one hand. You can go from light to dark, like a gradient from milk to espresso, or do milky nails with one espresso accent nail,” says Voloshyna.

If very light shades aren’t your norm, Estrada recommends starting with a warm caramel latte, meaning creamy beige, toasted almond, or buttery caramel shades. For something more dramatic, he suggests rich mocha, dark chocolate, and espresso tones, particularly those with warm undertones that complement the natural complexion of the hands. “Personally, I think the secret is choosing colors with beautiful depth rather than flat, lifeless browns,” says Estrada.

To create the depth and dimension Estrada and Voloshyna refer to, mix a few coffee shades into a French manicure, a blended ombré, or a magnetic cat-eye finish. “One technique I particularly love is creating a beautiful caramel or chocolate French manicure and then layering a sheer milky nude over the entire design,” says Estrada. “It softens the contrast and creates this gorgeous creamy, diffused effect, almost like watching milk swirl into a freshly poured espresso.”

Although the darkness of coffee lacquer feels more seasonally appropriate in fall and winter, Voloshyna says it’s easily adaptable in spring and summer, too. “In spring and summer, I would go lighter and more sheer, like an iced latte: milky, translucent shades with a glossy finish. In fall and winter, it’s all about deeper mocha and espresso, and you can add cat-eye or a little shimmer for the holidays,” says Voloshyna.

For the lighter looks, Estrada also recommends a delicate caramel French tip or blended ombré finishes that combine the lighter latte shades.

In winter, Estrada goes more extravagant with deep mocha shades accented with gold chrome, espresso with a velvet magnetic finish, or rich chocolate with delicate metallic embellishments. “It’s the same beautiful color family, but with an entirely different personality depending on the season,” he adds.

The range of a coffee manicure works on different nail shapes, too. “A beautifully tapered, elongated almond nail in a rich mocha shade is incredibly sophisticated and almost sensual. It reminds me of the elegant, exaggerated fashion silhouettes of the 1990s. For something more architectural, I love a dramatic, boxy square nail with a caramel French manicure. It’s wonderfully nostalgic and fashionable,” explains Estrada.

Shorter nails can certainly wear the trend too, especially in a single warm cappuccino shade—but Estrada says longer extensions leave room to experiment with more elaborate gradients, sculpted details, and multidimensional finishes.

If you do go dark on longer nails, though, Voloshyna says to keep the nails thin, especially if you’re painting over a gel or acrylic base. “Coffee shades, especially the dark ones, make thick nails look heavy,” she notes.

The finish you choose changes everything, so choose wisely. “A beautiful high-gloss caramel manicure resembles liquid toffee or freshly poured espresso. It's rich, polished, and incredibly luxurious. But a matte finish creates an entirely different personality. Deep chocolate and mocha shades suddenly resemble suede, velvet, or the finest cashmere,” describes Estrada. Feel free to combine textures, too. Try a matte espresso nail with an ultra-glossy French tip or a chocolate magnetic cat-eye to create the look of crushed velvet.

Another technique Estrada uses to create dimension is layering a sheer milky color over glitter. Begin with a champagne, gold, or caramel glitter, then apply a translucent milky nude over the top. “Instead of having that very sharp, obvious sparkle, the glitter becomes beautifully diffused beneath the creamy layer,” he says. Think precious jewelry viewed through frosted glass. “The sparkle is still there, but it has this gorgeous soft-focus quality. It doesn’t actually make the manicure matte, but it softens the visual intensity of the glitter, and you can choose a glossy or matte topcoat depending on the final look you want.”

With coffee, Voloshyna prefers a glossy finish to keep the color rich and creamy, like fresh coffee. Matte topcoats are reserved for mocha and espresso manis to give a soft, velvety look. “In winter, a brown cat-eye adds depth and makes the color look like melted chocolate,” she concludes.
