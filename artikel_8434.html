I would just try to see the forest through the trees: you are achieving tax deferred diversification in exchange for more granular control over when you can sell individual holdings. I don't know your reasons for doing a 351 exchange in the first place, but presumably one reason involves decreasing concentration risk from individual stocks. While it's true that you are at the mercy of the market gods to determine what all your holdings will trade for on end of market 11/19, look at it this way: if you didn't do the exchange and were able to sell "at the top" for any given stock, you'd lose at least 30% of your gains to capital gains tax anyway (so your after-tax "sell" price is probably something like 25% lower).
Thank you. Yes, the 351 strategy makes a ton of sense for my small amount of stock I'm selling each year, and being able to choose which tax lots sell. You get equivalent tax lots and can choose which ones to sell. However if you are not optimizing your taxes very precisely it might make more sense to sell a stock when it's really high instead of letting it sell on a random date for the 351 exchange.
You are not "selling" anything with a 351 exchange (as that would be a taxable event). Your cost basis of the stock lots you choose for the exchange will transfer over exactly in kind to the AAUD shares, so you are left with equivalent "gains" in AAUD, except that now you have a diversified index. So in theory one might want to transfer the shares with the lowest cost basis / highest gains, as you could theoretically hold AAUD shares forever and never sell since it's a total market index.
