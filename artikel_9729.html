Can AI Make Online Forms Less Painful? Here's What We're Building.

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Online forms haven't changed much in years.

Whether it's customer feedback, student surveys, client onboarding, or recruitment, the process usually looks the same.

Create a form. Add fields. Share a link. Hope people complete it.

But what if people could simply explain what they wanted to say, and software handled the structure?

That's the idea behind Talking Links, a new product under development at Digiwares.

"What was the most confusing part of today's lesson?"

Instead of navigating multiple form fields, a student could respond by voice or text:

"I understood the first example, but I got confused when we started working with fractions with different denominators."

That's useful information, but collecting dozens of responses like this creates another problem: organising them.

The respondent gets a simpler way to answer, while the creator receives structured information that is easier to review.

A teacher, business owner, recruiter, or researcher creates a link for collecting responses.

Respondents open the link and answer by voice or text. No account required.

The system processes responses and organises the extracted information in a central dashboard, grouped under the original link.

The goal isn't to replace what the respondent said. It's to make the useful information easier to identify and work with.

The accompanying GIF illustrates the concept.

The interesting part isn't creating links or storing responses.

It's turning unpredictable human language into reliable structured data.

People don't communicate like database schemas.

They provide incomplete information, mix different topics, change direction halfway through a sentence, or speak in different languages.

That introduces several engineering challenges:

These are some of the areas we're focusing on during testing.

It's tempting to build every feature users might eventually need.

Integrations, automated follow-ups, advanced analytics, templates, and complex workflows are all possibilities.

But we're deliberately keeping the initial version focused on three capabilities:

Create a link → Share it → Collect structured responses.

The priority is making the core experience reliable before expanding the product.

The Talking Links MVP is built and undergoing testing.

We're validating the end-to-end workflow, investigating technical issues, and refining the experience before wider availability.

We've also shared the concept with a small number of people internationally, including in Thailand and Vietnam. Their initial reactions have been encouraging.

But positive reactions aren't the same as real-world product validation.
