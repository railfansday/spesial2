David Moyes got the better of Matthias Jaissle to pile pressure on the Newcastle United manager at St James' Park.

Thierno Barry scored the only goal on Monday, 2 November, a result which saw Everton shoot up to seventh in the Premier League table.

The Magpies, on the other hand, have been left floundering in 13th place, with Jaissle furious with his players' failure to follow his game plan.

Jacob Ramsey's last-gasp red card just rubbed salt into the wounds in the North East, sparking questions over Jaissle's future at the club already.

Moyes had been facing similar questions due to The Friedkin Group's situation at the Hill Dickinson Stadium, but the Toffees have done incredibly well so far.

Now, a day after the game, Football Insider has looked at five moments that fans may have missed during the 90 minutes.

Chasing the game at 1-0 down, Jaissle sent on new instructions with Fabian Schar, hoping to secure a very late equaliser against the Toffees.

🚨 𝗡𝗘𝗪: Newcastle United players were seen throwing away a piece of paper containing tactical instructions from their manager shortly after receiving it during the match. pic.twitter.com/N6hC7NzZW5

The defender instantly ditched the piece of paper with the instructions on, with television cameras able to zoom in and see the new plans.

Several players ended up in John Brooks' notebook, but they weren't the only ones, as Moyes also saw his name taken by the referee.

Barry appeared to be taken down inside the penalty area in the second half, an incident which injured the striker, but no spot-kick was given.

It's safe to say that Moyes was absolutely furious, with his reaction catching the referee's attention.

Lewis Hall instantly became a villain amongst the fanbase when trying to pick up an injured Harrison Armstrong.

An awful, awful challenge by Ramsey left the midfielder in a heap, but with Newcastle wanting to find an equaliser, Hall took matters into his own hands.

How he avoided a yellow card, well, only the referee can explain that.

Some fans couldn't help but notice that Jaissle's substitutions were very similar to what Eddie Howe used to do at St James' Park.

Rather than go more attacking and chase a goal, Jaissle frustrated many by going like-for-like for his changes, and it was just more of what Howe used to do.

It took just 22 minutes for James Tarkowski to wind up Joelinton after the two clashed in the goal mouth during the Carabao Cup game mid-week.

The two players seemed to be laughing at the situation, but deep down, there's clearly a resentment between the pair.
