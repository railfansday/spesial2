Betting Transactions in Bank Statements: How Lenders Identify Gaming and Gambling Debits After the 2025 Ban

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Betting transactions in a bank statement are debits to, and credits from, gambling or online money gaming platforms, often disguised as payments to unrelated merchants or individuals. Since India banned online money games in 2025, these flows have moved to offshore apps and mule accounts, so lenders must identify them through patterns rather than merchant names.

Before August 2025, a credit analyst could often spot gaming spend by name: a regulated real-money gaming app appeared clearly in UPI narrations. That visibility has largely gone.

The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 prohibited online money games, and domestic platforms withdrew their money-game offerings. Demand did not disappear. It moved to offshore platforms that collect money through personal accounts, payment gateway merchants with unrelated business names, and rotating UPI handles.

This guide explains the legal position, why betting debits still show up, how to identify them in a statement, and what underwriting and AML policy options lenders have.

The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 passed the Lok Sabha on 20 August 2025 and the Rajya Sabha on 21 August 2025, and received presidential assent on 22 August 2025. MeitY released draft rules in October 2025, and the rules were notified in April 2026.

Section 7's broad wording means institutions that facilitate such flows, even unknowingly, face regulatory risk. Suspicious patterns should go through the institution's STR process under PMLA.

A borrower earning Rs 80,000 a month shows 45 UPI debits of Rs 1,000 to Rs 5,000 to rotating individual payees over two months, clustered on evenings with cricket matches, plus occasional credits from similar payees. Flagged outflows equal 30% of income. The lender declines under its threshold policy.

A business loan is disbursed and, within five days, a large share moves to a wallet and then to multiple merchants with generic names. Post-disbursal monitoring flags possible diversion and triggers a review.

A newly onboarded "digital services" merchant receives thousands of round-value UPI payments late at night from many unrelated payers. The PA suspends settlement pending investigation.

The 2025 ban removed regulated real-money gaming from Indian payment rails, but it did not remove betting transactions from bank statements. It made them harder to see. Flows now hide behind personal accounts, generic merchant names and rotating UPI handles, the same infrastructure used for mule activity and transaction laundering.

Lenders that structure statements, flag behaviour and not just names, and apply a clear policy will protect both credit quality and their compliance position under section 7. Treat it as part of standard underwriting rather than an occasional manual check.
