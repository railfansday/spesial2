Bessent blames Lutnick's Commerce for not blacklisting Nvidia's chip partner

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the Commerce Department controls the decision to put Nvidia customer Megaspeed on a U.S. export blacklist or not

Photo Illustration by Matteo Della Torre/NurPhoto via Getty Images

The downfall of Nvidia $NVDA CEO Jensen Huang is reverberating in the Trump administration.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Friday that the Commerce Department controls the decision to put Nvidia customer Megaspeed on a U.S. export blacklist or not. Huang resigned Thursday following revelations of extramarital affairs, including with a Megaspeed executive that U.S. officials believe to be a spy for China.

Appearing Friday on CNBC, Bessent said his department and others have favored doing so for months. But he said that the Commerce Department, led by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, "has the pen," adding that "you will have to ask them about their internal processes." He said the Treasury, State and Defense departments, and the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative, all supported adding Megaspeed to the entity list.

"I am frustrated when President Trump made it very clear that China should not receive any Nvidia chips above H100s," Bessent said. "Any leakage is very frustrating," he said, because it undermines American firms as they compete on artificial intelligence and benefits Beijing. He also said there is "a lot of leakage in the system" and that the government is working to close it.

Bessent pushed back on Lutnick in the Situation Room last week, questioning why Commerce was hesitant to put Megaspeed on the blacklist, according to CNN. Asked about the exchange, Bessent said, "We don't repeat conversations in the Situation Room." Commerce spokeswoman Kristen Eichamer told CNBC on Wednesday that the two had "no such conversation." Commerce has so far declined to list Megaspeed among the firms on its entity list.

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO on Thursday after acknowledging an affair with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom U.S. intelligence officials suspect of spying for China. (The two are not related.) Bessent said the focus should be on whether Nvidia knew its client and worked to prevent sales to pass-through shell companies. “I think it’s important that anyone at the company there be held accountable for that,” he said.

Some advisers to President Donald Trump are now weighing Lutnick's future, according to CNN. The White House said Thursday that Trump "maintains complete confidence in the secretary."

Sen. Tim Scott, a South Carolina Republican, said Thursday that the Senate Banking Committee has asked both Jensen Huang and Lutnick to testify next month. Eichamer, speaking to CNBC on Friday, said Lutnick has agreed to testify of his own accord.

House committees have requested a private session with Jensen Huang on Nov. 18. House Foreign Affairs Chairman Brian Mast, a Florida Republican, has requested that Jeff Kessler, Commerce’s under secretary for industry and security, appear at a Dec. 3 hearing on chip smuggling and export control enforcement.
