Ex-refs chief blasts Craig Pawson over Michael Kayode incident in Brentford vs Everton

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Craig Pawson failed to act appropriately during Brentford's 4-0 victory against Everton at the Gtech Community Stadium.

That is according to former PGMOL chief and ex-FIFA referee, Keith Hackett, who exclusively told Football Insider that the man in the middle relied too heavily on VAR as a "lifebelt".

Keane Lewis-Potter dragged a first-half brace, with Vitaly Janelt and Fabio Carvalho piling on the misery after the Toffees were sent down to 10 men.

Following a VAR review, James Tarkowski was given his marching orders for a studs-up challenge on Yehor Yarmoliuk in the 40th minute.

It was a very simple decision for Pawson after a second look at the incident, but fans were left wanting more from the official.

Just before the hour mark, Michael Kayode flew into a challenge on Jack Grealish, landing a big blow on the winger's shin, sparking angry calls for a red card.

VAR didn't feel compelled to intervene after checking the incident, though, which meant that the 22-year-old full-back avoided punishment altogether.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Hackett hit out at Pawson, who had missed a simple caution.

"Reckless is when a player acts with a disregard for the danger to, or consequences for, an opponent and must be cautioned," he said.

"This, clearly, was a challenge that fulfilled the above criteria for a yellow card. In any game, whatever has gone on before, this was a challenge that needed to be acted on.

"VAR, of course, cannot be the lifebelt that comes to the rescue of Craig Pawson, and when he saw the Tarkowski challenge, he had to upgrade his yellow card.

"The referee should have been more decisive and pulled a yellow card out of his pocket!"

There is no doubt that Grealish was left in a lot of pain, and the calls for the game's second red card were completely understandable.

It was a high challenge, and with some force behind it. But fans were going to call for absolutely everything to get their team back onto a level playing field.

As commentators pointed out, though, the incident would have hurt a lot less had Grealish had properly-sized shin pads on.

It was evident just how tiny they were, and how very little of his shin they actually covered. It's understandable why players do it, but it opens them up to more injuries.
