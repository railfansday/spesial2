What Happened to Stephanie Gonzalez? Mom Allegedly Killed in Birthday Ambush

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

A Texas mother of four was fatally shot along a Dallas freeway on her 35th birthday — and investigators now say two of her own relatives allegedly orchestrated the ambush after she told her husband she wanted a divorce.

Stephanie Gonzalez was driving home from work on November 15, 2026, when she was shot and killed on the C.F. Hawn Freeway, according to the Dallas Police Department. One week later, her brother-in-law, Juan Gonzalez-Orduna, was arrested and charged with murder. Authorities say his son, her nephew Jose Gonzalez, was allegedly directly involved in the killing.

Stephanie was reportedly about 10 minutes from home when the attack occurred around 9:20 p.m., police said. Officers responding to the scene found her inside her vehicle, which had been struck by several bullets. She was transported to a nearby hospital, where she later died from her injuries.

The Dallas Police Department initially said in a November 16 statement that “the motives and circumstances surrounding this offense are still under investigation.” Authorities did not immediately name any suspects in the days following the shooting.

Stephanie’s cousin Vivian Britton told NBC DFW that the family had struggled to accept the timing of the killing. “It was her birthday last night. And the way she was attacked was somewhat personal, I believe,” Britton said. Speaking to Fox 4 News, Britton added: “She was leaving work. She was maybe 10 minutes away from here, and she was on her way home to see her babies.”

“It’s just tough seeing it’s so senseless. It doesn’t make sense. But we don’t believe in coincidences,” Britton continued. “The way she was attacked was somewhat personal, I believe.”

From the earliest hours of the investigation, Stephanie’s loved ones were convinced the shooting was no random act of violence. Her workplace was only a 30-minute drive from her home, and she had been en route to see her four children — including a baby just a few months old — when she was killed.

A coworker who identified herself as Emily spoke to CBS News about the loss. “I’m confused, I’m hurt and I’m worried about her … her babies at home,” she said. “I know how much she loved them.”

Emily also described their friendship inside the office. “I would just go into her office and start talking about random things,” she recalled. “She would always tell me about her farm, about her chickens … and you can always hear her laughing or making other people laugh.”

“She just wanted to make sure that everybody was OK before she was OK. And that … I think that’s what I’ll take with me, going anywhere else,” Emily added. “You know, I will tell everybody, ‘Well, your day could be worse, you know, just keep your head up.’ And try to make them smile how she made me smile.”

The investigation took a turn on November 22 when the Dallas Police Department announced that Gonzalez-Orduna had been booked on a murder charge in connection with Stephanie’s death.

Gonzalez-Orduna was detained at the Lew Sterrett Jail, where records showed he is also detained on an immigration hold over his alleged illegal immigration status. It wasn’t immediately clear whether he had an attorney who could comment on his behalf.

Dallas police have not publicly specified a motive, and the department declined to share further details when reached by Us via email on November 23.

Details that emerged in an arrest warrant affidavit obtained by Fox 4 News describe what occurred in the hours leading up to the shooting.

Friends told detectives that Stephanie had allegedly been involved in an affair with a coworker and wanted to leave her husband, per the affidavit. The couple reportedly argued after she mentioned wanting a divorce, just hours before she was killed on November 15.

That evening, Stephanie called 911 to report she had been involved in a minor car crash on the C.F. Hawn Freeway. The dispatcher on the line heard her scream — followed by the sound of gunfire — according to the affidavit.

Based on the 911 recording, traffic camera footage and a witness interview, detectives identified Gonzalez-Orduna and his son as the alleged attackers. Investigators say the pair deliberately crashed into Stephanie’s car to force her to pull over, then ambushed her on the freeway.

According to the affidavit, Juan is suspected of driving the vehicle — which belonged to a relative and had its license plates removed — while his son is suspected of opening fire on Stephanie, killing her.

Authorities are reportedly continuing to search for Gonzalez-Orduna’s son, Jose Gonzalez.

Fox 4 News reported that Stephanie’s husband is not suspected of being involved in the killing. The couple had been married for more than two years at the time of her death, per her obituary.

Stephanie’s obituary described her as “a loving daughter, wife, mother, sister, aunt, and friend whose warm spirit touched the hearts of everyone who knew her.” She is survived by her mother, brother, sister and nephew, in addition to her four children and husband, Luis Enriquez Gonzalez.

The tribute paid special attention to her love for her children and for the animals on her farm. “Whether she was spending time with family, enjoying music, or tending to her farm, she embraced life with warmth and appreciation,” the obituary reads. Of her marriage to Luis, it added: “Together they shared a life built on care, companionship, and mutual support.”

A GoFundMe launched by Stephanie’s cousin Julissa Clemente is raising money for funeral expenses and ongoing support for her children. “Stephanie was loving, caring, hardworking, and above all a great mother,” Clemente wrote on the page. “She leaves behind her husband and four beautiful children, who are now facing an unimaginable loss without her.”

“Our family is heartbroken by this loss, and it is hard to put into words how deeply Stephanie will be missed,” Clemente added.

Dallas police are asking anyone with information related to Stephanie’s death to contact Detective R. Kramer at 214-671-3608. The investigation remains ongoing.
