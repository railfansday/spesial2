Three Premier League talking points

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Mauricio Pochettino suffered a torrid return to the Premier League as Manchester United's new manager watched his side thrashed 4-0 at Liverpool.

Xabi Alonso's problems mounted as Chelsea were humbled by Leeds, while Manchester City spluttered again.

AFP Sport looks at the key talking points from this weekend's action:

Mauricio Pochettino has waited a decade to be handed the Manchester United job, but he could not have made a worse start to his reign.

The Argentine's decision to give Matthijs de Ligt his first Premier League start for nearly a year alongside Lenny Yoro in central defence spectacularly backfired in a brutal demolition at arch rivals Liverpool.

Yoro was sent off after just eight minutes after a De Ligt error allowed Alexander Isak to race clear on goal.

Within five minutes the 10 men were 2-0 down as Dominik Szoboszlai fired home the resulting free-kick and Florian Wirtz turned home a cross from Rio Ngumoha.

Pochettino tried to limit the damage with the introduction of Harry Maguire after just 22 minutes, having dropping the England international in favour of De Ligt from the starting line-up.

Ngumoha and Victor Munoz added further goals but only wayward Liverpool finishing prevented a repeat of the 7-0 thrashing United suffered at Anfield three years ago.

Pochettino's painful debut defeat keeps United second bottom of the Premier League table.

More tellingly, it gives Pochettino an early taste of the huge task he faces to turn around the fortunes of the fallen English giants.

Chelsea midfielder Romeo Lavia urged his team-mates to stick together after their humiliating 4-1 defeat against Leeds on Saturday.

Leeds ended their 27-year wait for a Premier League win at Stamford Bridge in resounding style, sparking jeers from angry Chelsea fans at the final whistle.

It was a third defeat in four league matches for Xabi Alonso's team who are marooned in mid-table and face a fight to climb back into the race to qualify for the Champions League.

"Everyone is very angry and disappointed. At home this is never the way you want to finish a game. We let them back in the game when we shouldn't have," Lavia said.

"This is something we will analyse and get better at. With these types of results, it feels like the end of the world, but we are not going to ring the bell now to say it's a panic moment."

Calling for a reaction in Friday's trip to struggling Nottingham Forest, Lavia added: "This hurts. It needs to hurt so it provokes a reaction, and this is definitely going to help us for the next game."

Enzo Maresca challenged Manchester City to show more defensive resilience after their dismal run extended with a controversial 1-1 draw against Fulham on Saturday.

City needed a second-half equaliser from Elliot Anderson at the Etihad Stadium to avoid a fourth successive Premier League defeat after Rodrigo Muniz's early opener.

City striker Erling Haaland also had a goal contentiously disallowed for kicking the ball out of the grasp of Fulham keeper Bernd Leno.

Maresca is becoming increasingly concerned with third-placed City's issues at the back, leading to 14 goals conceded in 11 league games.

"For sure we are conceding too many goals. We need to avoid that," he said.

"Sometimes you need to win games 1-0, you need to keep clean-sheets and we are not able to do that.

"We concede almost 80 percent of our goals in the first 20 minutes, so there is something that we need to improve."
