Eagles vs. Cowboys Thanksgiving: The good, the bad, and the ugly

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Nick Sirianni had to be redirected away from the stands more than a few times by the Eagles' media relations team at AT&T Stadium. He tried to high five everyone wearing midnight green Thursday afternoon. The Eagles were dead two times, three times, 10 times, it seemed, and they managed to stage the largest Thanksgiving Day comeback in NFL history, overcoming a 21-point deficit (31–10) to beat the Dallas Cowboys, 39-38, in one of the most improbable victories games in Eagles' memory.

It sets the Eagles up to now become NFC East contenders, considered where the Eagles were two weeks ago.

The Eagles never gave up. They were banged up, without Cooper DeJean, Dallas Goedert, relying on Ty Robinson's blocked punt, and pivotal nine-yard sack that turned the ball over and set up Jake Elliott's game-winning 59-yard field goal with 2 seconds left.

The pickup from the Cowboys' practice squad hauled the Eagles from a 31-10 deficit to complete 21 of 31 passes for 331 yards and three touchdowns being named MVP of the game.

DeVonta Smith was amazing in a career-high game, catching a career-high 11 passes for a career-high 200 yards on 14 targets.

It is a signature victory in Sirianni's six years.

The Eagles are now 4-7 with the lowly Arizona Cardinals and Indianapolis Colts next, two teams with a combined 6-16 record, which could bring the Eagles to 6-7 record with Seattle coming to Lincoln Financial Field on Dec. 19.

The Eagles are still at the bottom of the NFC East, but in a position to push the 6-3-1 New York Giants, and the 4-5-1 Washington Commanders.

The Eagles' 20 fourth-quarter points exceeded the 17-points per game the Eagles were averaging this season.

The Eagles continued playing hard for Sirianni.

He still made dubious choices, like the decision to go for a two-point conversion after Milton's 15-yard TD run with 9:09 left to play, and the Eagles trailing 38-30.

The Eagles were down at one point 31-10 with 3:35 left in the third quarter, then proceeded to score on four of the next five drives.

There was a lot of good, some bad and some ugly in the Eagles' 39-38 win over the Dallas Cowboys.

Joe Milton exploding on national TV in completing 21 of 31 passes for 331 yards and three touchdowns. He just had a habit of coming up in clutch situations to make big plays. He probably made himself a pile of money from some NFL team, too, whether the Eagles are in his future or somewhere else. Wherever he goes, no Eagles' fan will forget what he did on Thanksgiving Day 2026 against the Dallas Cowboys, bringing a dead team back to life.

Ty Robinson's blocked punt and Kenny Moore's 3-yard scoop and score suddenly made what looked like a blowout into a game. Robinson came slicing through the line and dove at Dallas punter Bryan Anger to catch the punt with his right hand, and Moore ran after the loose ball and gathered it in for a three-yard score. Robinson later came up with a huge nine-yard sack at the Eagles' 48 with 55 seconds left, setting up the Elliott game-winner.

Jake Elliott coming through big again. He was 2-for-2 on a 48- and 59-yard game-winning field goal. This came after Elliott went 4-for-4 the previous week against Pittsburgh with field goals of 41, 29, 53 and 55 yards. Elliott is 13 of 14 on field goal attempts, and 21 of 21 on extra points—including 5-for-5 on field goals attempts of 50 yards or more. Sirianni is going to have to think more of Elliott kicking field goals instead of trying to go for it on fourth down. What sometimes gets lost is Elliott is a two-time Super Bowl champion who has made huge field goals in huge games.

DeVonta Smith seemed to be the only weapon the Eagles had, and despite added attention, he caught a game-high 11 passes for a game-high 200 yards on 14 targets, including 50- and 45-yard touchdown catches. It was Smith's 45-yard TD catch with 15 seconds left in the third quarter that continued to make the game intriguing pulling the Eagles within 31-17.

Milton's 50-yard rainbow to DeVonta Smith for the Eagles' only touchdown in the first half. Milton was about the only thing the Eagles had going in the first half. Otherwise, it was bad.

Two of Milton's first three passes converted third downs. They were the first two good signs the Eagles might be able to move the ball against the NFL's 31st defense. Milton's first completion was a nine-yard completion to Dallas Goedert on a second-and-eight to the Eagles' 17. Three plays later. Milton connected with Smith for a 16-yard reception to the Eagles' 35. Of course, the Eagles blew it by going for it on fourth-and-one at the Dallas 37.

Money on third down. Milton completed eight of his first 11 passes, three of which came on third down. On the Eagles' first drive, he hit Smith for 16 yards on a third-and-8, and on third drive, he hit Makai Lemon for 20-yard pass on a third-and-12 to the Dallas 34. It led to Elliott's 48-yard field goal.

Byron Young with nine tackles from the defensive tackle position. He had five solo tackles, assisting on four.

Jeremiah Trotter Jr. continues to lead the defense. Every time the linebacker out of St, Joe's Prep and Clemson has started this season, he has led the Eagles in tackles. Thanksgiving against Dallas was no different, with a game-high 12 tackles, seven solo, with a sack and two tackles for loss. Trotter is now second on the Eagles in tackles with 64 tackles, although has a team-high 41 solo tackles.

Cobie Durant's interception with 7:05 to play seemed to seal the victory for the Cowboys after the Eagles' fourth stop of the game. Brandon Aubrey missed a gimme 40-yard field goal attempt bailed out the Eagles.

The Eagles losing Cooper DeJean, Saquon Barkley and Dallas Goedert to injury in the first quarter. Not that it mattered.

Cornerback Quinyon Mitchell flagged with pass interference on the very first play of the game. The 17-yard call took the ball from the Dallas 23 to the Cowboys' 40 and jumpstarted one of the NFL's most potent offenses. Mitchell was called for two pass interferences penalties for a total of 46 yards. It was not a good game for Mitchell, who was later burned for a 39-yard completion on a second-and-16 right after the Eagles' blocked punt score. The quick euphoria the Eagles had after the blocked punt was quickly zapped on a Dak Prescott 14-yard touchdown scramble with 10:11 to play.

The Eagles first half. Everyone may have given up by then and began eating their Thanksgiving Day dinner. The Cowboys scored on four of their first five drives. They had outgained the Eagles, 245 to 169, had 17 first downs to the Eagles' eight, and the Eagles committed penalties for 71 yards, including Quinyon Mitchell's second interference call for 29 yards, and Riq Woolen's 10-yard interference call leading to the Cowboys' last touchdown in the first half. The Cowboys converted 6 of 7 third downs to the Eagles' 2 of 5.

With 5:09 left in the first half, the Cowboys scored on three of their four possessions, had amassed 208 total yards of offense, averaging 8.3 yards a play, 12.9 yards a pass and 5.3 yards a rush. Zak Prescott was 6 of 10 for 129 yards and a touchdown.

Dak Prescott's 31-yard touchdown pass to Kavontae Turpin for a Cowboys' 14-0 lead with 36 seconds left in the first quarter. It looked like the route was on.

The Cowboys' second drive. This time,the Cowboys needed just five plays to score. But they came up with a big third-down play again, this time, on a third-and-16, when Prescott hit George Pickens for a 36-yard completion to the Eagles' 33. Riq Woolen made the tackle, and earned a benching again, but the Eagles were so depleted, they had to keep the problem on the field.

Sirianni opting to go for it on fourth-and-one at the Cowboys' 36, passing on a 53-yard field attempt by Elliott.

Will Shipley caught jawing on the sideline with Nick Sirianni in the first quarter, no doubt about playing time.

The Cowboys' first drive of the game. It was an 11-play, 77-yard drive that chewed up 6 minutes and 11 seconds off the clock. Of the Cowboys' 11 plays, they only faced three third downs—converting all of them. The true backbreaker was Prescott's 28-yard completion to CeeDee Lamb right down the middle of the field to the Eagles' 7. Three plays later, Javonte Williams scored from a yard out for a 7-0 Dallas lead with 8:50 to play in the first quarter. Of the Cowboys' first 11 plays, they threw it only three times, while Williams gashed them for 35 yards. It was the ghost of the previous Dallas game, when Williams sliced his way through the Eagles for 155 yards rushing, averaging 6.2 yards a carry. In the first game, the Cowboys ran 65 plays against the Eagles' defense for zero tackles for a loss. Dallas pounded the Eagles for 416 total yards, averaging 6.4 yards a game, with 204 yards on the ground, averaging 5.4 yards a carry. The Cowboys' first drive seemed to give loud warnings for another long day for the Eagles' defense.

There is a reason why Jihaad Campbell has struggled, it's because he gets lost in coverage. The linebacker once again managed to lose CeeDee Lamb on a third-and-18 on the Cowboys' first drive to the Eagles' 7. Campbell thought he was reading Prescott's eyes while Lamb ran right past him. It led to the first score of the game.
