Jürgen Klopp has an unexpected connection to West Ham’s January plans

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Despite a change in the dugout, West Ham’s woes in the Championship have continued.

Scott Parker is yet to taste victory since taking over from Nuno Espírito Santo, with a 1-1 draw at home to bottom of the table Preston North End last weekend typifying their early-season struggles.

The Hammers now sit 10th in the second tier standings after 16 league games, with it difficult to see when and how the East London outfit are going to get back on an upward trajectory.

One way could be to address certain positions in the January transfer window, but yet again, there are so many games to come in a congested winter period, so it’s important that from somewhere, West Ham find a bit of magic to keep themselves in the promotion mix.

A position West Ham have really struggled with so far this season is the lack of a clinical frontman, with none of Taty Castellanos, Joel Piroe or Pablo contributing enough on a consistent basis.

Meanwhile, one forward they let go of in the summer, Niclas Füllkrug, is enjoying such a fruitful spell back in Germany on loan at Werder Bremen, so much so he’s earned recognition from national team manager Jürgen Klopp.

Such a rise back to form may give the Hammers an interesting decision to make in January as to whether they get the forward back in the fold or sell him for a sizeable fee at the turn of the new year.

Niclas Füllkrug arrived at West Ham with quite the reputation.

After netting 15 and assisting 10 in all competitions for Borussia Dortmund in the 2023-24 campaign, he followed that up with a couple of goals for Germany in Euro 2024, peaking the Hammers’ interest.

West Ham thought they were bringing such goalscoring prowess to England following the tournament, spending £27 million to sign the then-31-year-old, but his time at the London Stadium was nothing short of disastrous.

Füllkrug’s first season at the club was marred by injuries, with the German starting just six times and scoring three goals in the process.

West Ham are determined to keep Konstantinos Mavropanos even if there is interest in the centre-back in the January window.

Last year, he made just eight league appearances before being deemed surplus to requirements and loaned out to AC Milan, where he scored just once in under 650 minutes of football.

It wasn't exactly a spell which warranted a return to the West Ham fold ahead of a Championship return, and the 33-year-old was loaned back to Werder Bremen during the summer, with it expected that he'd see out the final two years of his Hammers contract elsewhere.

Many connected with West Ham will be amazed at the turnaround Niclas Füllkrug has made on his return to Germany, demonstrating himself to be one of the most lethal finishers in the Bundesliga this season.

Füllkrug has found his form back in familar surroundings, with his last campaign with Bremen in 2022-23 resulting in a 16-goal return,

But the 33-year-old is well on his way to surpassing that number, netting 10 goals in as many appearances so far in the German top-flight.

Füllkrug is currently the top scorer in the division, one ahead of Bayer Leverkusen’s Patrik Schick and six ahead of Ballon D’Or winner Harry Kane in an unprecedented revival.

With Niclas Füllkrug banging in goals for fun and his confidence sky-high, there was no surprise to see him raise eyebrows on the national front.

And at the start of November, a report by German outlet Kicker revealed that the Werder Bremen frontman had been recalled to the national team by Jürgen Klopp for their Nations League fixtures against Serbia and the Netherlands.

Klopp was very complimentary of Füllkrug’s form too, saying: "Good form and scoring goals are never a bad thing.

"Fülle has been performing and scoring all kinds of goals, almost all of them spectacular. He has an incredible influence on Werder Bremen, not just through his goals, but also through the way he carries himself."

Such impressive words from Klopp are testament to the impact Füllkrug has had on his return to Germany, and it leaves West Ham with a big decision to make as the January transfer window ticks down.

Füllkrug remains contracted to the Hammers as he’s only on a season-long loan at Bremen, but Werder’s managing director, Clemens Fritz, has publicly expressed interest in signing the striker permanently, although he stressed that any agreement must involve all three parties of Bremen, West Ham and the player.

The West Ham faithful will be bemoaning the fact Niclas Füllkrug failed to show much goalscoring ability at the London Stadium, but then returning to Germany has him placed as one of the league’s top performing players.

And while their association with the German frontman on the pitch has been a complete waste of time, there could be some light at the end of the tunnel as to how much money they can get for him.

Despite having just two years left on his contract, Werder Bremen clearly value Füllkrug highly given the impact he’s made on his return, and with their managing director already showing his interest in making the Weserstadion Füllkrug's home again on a permanent basis, it’s evidence enough that the German outfit would be willing to pay quite a lot of money to keep hold of the Bundesliga’s top scorer.

Werder Bremen are believed to not have an option to buy in the original loan deal, so the ball is very much in West Ham’s court when it comes to negotiating terms.

The Hammers must stand firm when it comes to a price, with the club having to hold out to get as much back on the £27m deal they signed off to bring him to the London Stadium in the first place.

Praying on Werder Bremen’s desperation, such a lump of cash could have a significant say on their January transfer plans, with much of that money being used to bring in a new striker that could help turn the Hammers’ fortunes around and still have them in contention for a place back in the Premier League.

So while much of Füllkrug's stint at West Ham has been filled with bitter disappointment, it could end up having the perfect ending and playing a huge part in Scott Parker’s men achieving their promotion dreams in May.
