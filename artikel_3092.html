(COCH -4.69%) reported significant progress in the pivotal clinical trial for its Acclaim fully implanted cochlear implant, with the full 56-patient cohort reaching the six-month follow-up milestone. Management stated that the company is utilizing a modular Premarket Approval submission strategy to allow for rolling FDA review while clinical data collection continues through early 2027. The company is transitioning toward commercial preparedness, planning for headcount growth and a twofold expansion of its manufacturing footprint in Minnesota. Management indicated that the current adult cochlear implant market is substantially underpenetrated, and the company intends to capture market share through its differentiated invisible device design that eliminates the need for external head-worn components.

The reality is that our journey to this point spans more than 3 decades and over $350 million of invested capital. Rome, as they say, was not built in a day. Envoy Medical was founded in 2000 with a clear goal to create a fully implanted device that works with the natural ear. The founding principles were simple. Avoid microphones and externally worn components at all costs. And use the body's natural hearing pathway to capture sound. The benefits of a fully implanted hearing device were compelling. Nothing to wear on or behind your ear. The ability to hear all day and all night.

No expensive external hardware to lose or break, No discomfort or annoyance from wearing something on the side of your head all day. The potential for better sound localization, fewer limits on everyday and, of course,, nothing for others to see. That was the dream, and Envoy Medical set out to make it a reality. Our solution was a middle ear sensor that leverages the natural hearing pathway No microphone on or behind the ear. No microphone buried under the skin. Instead, the sensor picks up vibrations of the middle ear and uses them as the signal. Much as the natural ear does. We first validated our sensor technology in the Esteem fully implanted active middle ear implant.

The Esteem fully implanted hearing device is the first and currently the only of its kind to receive FDA approval. Roughly 1 thousand Esteem devices have been implanted. Some more than 2 decades ago in early clinical trials. We believe no company has more experience with fully implanted hearing devices than Envoy Medical. Unfortunately, for patients with hearing loss and the professionals who serve them, CMS classified the Esteem as a hearing aid. That classification made it ineligible for Medicare coverage, and in practice, limited patients' access to the technology. We continue to believe that fully implanted active middle ear implants are hearing prosthetics, not hearing aids, and should be covered by public and private payers.

If that policy changes, we believe the Esteem would be well positioned to benefit. In 2015, we shifted our focus on an opportunity that had been calling for our attention for 2 decades, fully implanted, invisible cochlear implants. Cochlear implants have been commercially available in the United States since the 1.98 thousands and are well established as a solution for severe to profound hearing loss. Yet only a small fraction of adult candidates, roughly 5%, have pursued 1. We believe the significant reason for this low penetration is that most people do not want to live with or be limited by an external component worn on the side of their head.

We quickly got to work with no preconceived notions holding us back, our team was able to examine different design choices from the ground up. After carefully weighing the options and trade offs, we selected a design built to support long-term interest of the patient. A modular implanted system with 4 main components. These are our sensor, a processor connected, to the electrode array, a large capacity battery placed in the chest, and a power lead connecting the battery to the processor. We built the system ourselves using our own designs and intellectual property, which we believe makes it a more valuable independent technology. That work became our investigational Acclaim fully implanted cochlear implant or FCI.

We are extremely proud of the Acclaim and believe its design offers several key advantages. No required head-worn externals, The Acclaim FCI is designed so that no sound processor or charger needs to be worn on the head. Middle ear sensor. The Acclaim FCI uses our middle ear sensor to pick up sound through the natural hearing pathway. There is no external microphone, and no microphone under the skin. Flexibility. Because the Acclaim FCI uses the ear itself to pick up sound, it is designed to allow patients to keep using ear-worn devices such as popular earbuds or even hearing aids.

As artificial intelligence drives rapid innovation in ear-worn consumer electronics, we believe an Acclaim patients will be positioned to take advantage of those new devices. No magnet. There is no magnet anywhere in the Acclaim system. Because nothing needs to be held in place on the head, a magnet is not required. We believe this may offer advantages for patients who eventually need MRI or other imaging of the head. Currently, the investigational Acclaim FCI has not received MR conditional labeling, but we are hopeful that this will change once testing is submitted and approved. Large capacity rechargeable battery. Perhaps our most intentional design choice was placing a large capacity rechargeable battery in the chest rather than in the head.

In general, a larger battery generally means more time between charges. The Acclaim FCI is designed to run for several days, uninterrupted, on a single charge. Replaceable battery. All rechargeable batteries eventually lose capacity. The Acclaim FCI was designed so that when that happens, the battery can be replaced through a straightforward procedure without explanting the entire device. The Acclaim FCI received breakthrough device designation from the FDA in 2019. The first patients were implanted in an early feasibility study at Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Minnesota in October 2022. In 2024, we advanced into our pivotal study. Which the FDA requested we conduct in 2 stages.

Stage 1 with 10 participants followed by stage 2 with an additional 46 participants for a total cohort of 56. Stage 1 was fully enrolled in April 2025. After 3 months of follow-up, we submitted the data to the FDA and requested expansion into stage 2. In October 2025, the FDA authorized us to expand the study into stage 2. Enrollment in stage 2 moved quickly. The trial was fully enrolled in March 2026. All 56 participants have now passed their 6-month follow-up milestone. We anticipate that all 56 will complete their 12-month follow-up visit by April 2027, at which point data collection for the trial will be complete.

In July, we reported encouraging 12-month results from 10 participants in stage 1. The mean of 53.2%, compared with a pre implantation baseline of 15.2%. Representing a 38 percentage point improvement. While data in the full cohort needs to be collected and analyzed prior to drawing any conclusions, we are encouraged by these results and look forward to seeing the final data. We are pursuing a modular premarket approval or PMA, submission strategy. Rather than submitting 1 large PMA application, we plan to submit 4 modules over time. On 06/30/2026, we submitted our first module. And on 10/12/2026, after quarter end, we submitted our second.

With 2 of 4 modules now with the FDA, the agency can review these sections while clinical data collection continues. We continue to target submission of the remaining 2 modules, including the final clinical module in the first half of 27. The market opportunity reinforces why we believe this work matters. And why we are so energized about Envoy Medical's future. We currently think about the cochlear implant market in 3 buckets. The first bucket is the current market. In The United States, cochlear implants represent roughly $1 billion in annual revenue. Exact numbers are difficult to pinpoint, but we estimate that roughly 20 thousand to 25 thousand cochlear implants are implanted in US adults each year.

The second bucket is the expanded market. Today's market is substantially underpenetrated. We estimate that approximately 2.8 million in the United States could be candidates for a cochlear implant. Yet only about 150 thousand adults within that candidate pool have received 1. That represents only about 5% penetration of the estimated addressable population. If a new technology that patients want were able to increase that penetration, we believe the market could grow significantly. The third bucket is existing cochlear implant recipients.
