Arista Networks vs. Axon Enterprise: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Arista's customer concentration and Axon's cash flow quirks each carry hidden trade-offs that could shape which stock earns the nod.

By Seena Hassouna – Updated Nov 21, 2026 at 10:01AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Arista Networks ›

Choosing between Arista Networks (ANET -11.59%) and Axon Enterprise (AXON -5.16%) requires balancing high-speed networking prowess against public safety innovation for your long-term portfolio. Both companies are currently leading their respective markets.

Arista Networks builds the high-speed data center switches that power modern cloud and artificial intelligence systems. Axon Enterprise provides the hardware and software backbone for modern policing, including TASER devices and digital evidence management. Both companies dominate their respective markets, making them frequent candidates for growth-oriented investors.

Arista Networks provides high-performance networking platforms that manage data traffic in massive data centers and cloud environments. It focuses on high-speed switching for cloud titans and specialized providers building out artificial intelligence clusters. Its latest reports indicate that two specific customers accounted for 16% and 26% of revenue, meaning customer concentration adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $11.9 billion as demand for high-speed infrastructure accelerated. This represents a robust revenue growth rate of approximately 40.7% over the prior year. Net income for the same period was close to $4.8 billion, showcasing the scalability of its software-driven model and a net margin of roughly 40%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, Arista Networks reported a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.0x, which compares total debt to the amount of equity held by shareholders. The current ratio of approximately 3.0x indicates a strong ability to pay short-term debts with short-term assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly $4.3 billion, a high figure among tech stocks today.

Axon Enterprise is a dominant provider of public safety technology, ranging from TASER energy devices to body-worn cameras. Its integrated ecosystem includes digital evidence management software that enables law enforcement agencies to organize and securely share data. No single customer accounted for more than 10% of total sales, according to its latest annual report, indicating a diversified revenue base.

During the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue climbed to nearly $3.4 billion as more agencies transitioned to cloud-based records. This reflected revenue growth of approximately 33.6% compared to the prior year. The company recorded net income of roughly $258.5 million, supported by a net margin of close to 7.6% as it invests in international expansion.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, Axon Enterprise had a debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 0.6x, representing total debt relative to shareholders' equity. The current ratio of approximately 2.5x shows the company can comfortably cover short-term debts with its short-term assets. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 300.1% of operating cash flow, meaning reported cash generation is heavily inflated by this non-cash add-back.

Arista Networks competes against established rivals such as Cisco and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE -10.07%). The company also faces competition from Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) and relies on Broadcom (AVGO -12.19%) as its sole source for switching chips. Geopolitical tensions and trade restrictions regarding China also pose risks to its supply chain and volatile revenue patterns.

Axon Enterprise depends on law enforcement agencies adopting its products, and demand can be affected by negative publicity. The company faces significant product liability risks from the use of its conducted energy devices. Rapid technological change and supply chain dependence for TASER 10 components also present operational complexities as the business expands internationally.

Axon Enterprise appears more attractively priced based on its P/S ratio, which measures market value against sales over the past twelve months. Both companies trade at similar premiums relative to their Forward P/E ratios, which compare stock prices to future earnings estimates.

Arista gets my vote for 2027, and the reason comes down to what each forward multiple is paying for. The two stocks trade at nearly identical forward earnings multiples, so the usual shortcut says they cost the same. They don't. Arista already turns a large share of every sales dollar into profit, so its multiple rests on earnings the company mostly produces today. Axon's multiple rests on profit that still has to grow into the price, and its much lower price-to-sales ratio mostly reflects thin margins, not a bargain. If Axon's margins expand as more agencies move to cloud software, the stock can work. If they stall, that multiple has nowhere to hide. Arista carries more near-term risk, and this week's AI sell-off showed it. Two customers account for a large chunk of revenue, Broadcom supplies its switching chips, and Nvidia competes with it, so any wobble in data center spending hits the stock fast. Axon's demand comes from slow-moving police budgets and a customer base where no one dominates. That stability cushions Axon during a sell-off, but it doesn't make it a stronger long-term business. Investors who share the long-term case for Arista should treat a drop like this as a test of their time horizon, not a verdict on the business, and size the position so a further decline doesn't force a sale. Axon fits only investors with a long runway who can wait for margins to catch up, and the cheaper sales multiple shouldn't talk anyone into rushing.
