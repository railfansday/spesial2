The Outlook- W12 (NE-LAC, WAS-ARI, SEA-SF, LV-CLE, CAR-TB)

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Hey, folks! We're back with our third and final block of games for the Week 12 NFL slate, and we're covering all three late games, Sunday Night Football and Monday Night Football!

We've compiled the groupings of games here to make it easier to find the games you want:

Block 1: GB-LAR, CHI-DET, PHI-DAL, KC-BUF, DEN-PIT

Block 2: NO-CIN, TEN-JAC, NYG-IND, NYJ-MIA, ATL-MIN, BAL-HOU

Block 3: NE-LAC, WAS-ARI, SEA-SF, LV-CLE, CAR-TB

"The Outlook" will give you the details, stats, analysis, recommendations and everything else you need to make informed fantasy football decisions. Every fantasy-relevant player on each team will be profiled in this article. We want everyone reading this to bring home championships in their fantasy leagues!

I'm incredibly excited to bring this article to all of you this season, and I hope you enjoy the ride. If you have any questions, feel free to find me on Bluesky (@ktompkinsii.bsky.social) or tag me in a post with any lineup questions. I'll do my best to answer them for you.

All game and implied point totals listed were compiled via DraftKings Sportsbook on Thursday for NE-LAC, WAS-ARI, SEA-SF, LV-CLE and CAR-TB.

Implied Point Total: Patriots 19, Chargers 16.5

With no Drake Maye, possibly for the rest of the season, Stevenson will be needed in a massive way to help move the ball. He's coming off a poor Week 10 in which the Lions blew out the Patriots and Stevenson touched the ball only seven times for five yards. The Chargers are a solid matchup for Stevenson, but with A.J. Brown ruled out and backup quarterback Tommy DeVito slated to start, defenses may key in on Stevenson and take their chances with DeVito throwing the ball.

Fantasy Advice: Volume is on Stevenson's side, but efficiency hasn't been there WITH Maye. What will it look like without him? Stevenson is a high-end flex option based solely on volume, with the hope that he'll mosey on into the end zone.

The Chargers have made it a point of emphasis to give Hampton more of the running game instead of splitting carries with Kimani Vidal and Keaton Mitchell. After a Week 10 in which Hampton saw 65% of the team's rushing attempts, Hampton carried the ball only 14 times to Vidal's seven, but he had his most efficient game of the season with 6.4 yards per carry. Hampton feels like a real running back these days, which puts him back on the fantasy radar here against the Patriots. Justin Herbert was unable to clear concussion protocol and was ruled out on Friday, so that's a massive hurdle that trickles down to the running game. A Chargers offense, believe it or not, is incredibly different with Herbert at quarterback than with DJ Uiagalelei or Jake Browning.

Fantasy Advice: Hampton is worth flex consideration since he is consolidating most of the rushing role for the Chargers now, but not having Herbert will still likely impact his efficiency greatly.

Implied Point Total: Commanders 24.5, Cardinals 17

With the return of Croskey-Merritt last week, the hope was that the backfield would get less messy. Rachaad White was dealing with a heel injury and appeared on the injury report heading into Week 11. He played 22% of the snaps but didn't touch the ball. Austin Ekeler touched the ball nine times for 44 yards. At the very least, JCM returned to a solid role with 77 total yards and a touchdown on 41% of the snaps to lead the backfield. White getting healthier may take away some of Ekeler's opportunities, but it will probably also take away some of Croskey-Merritt's receiving work (2-23). It's not the best part of the backfield split, which is why we're not prioritizing this backfield as one to target for fantasy football.

Fantasy Advice: JCM ideally slots into fantasy lineups as a flex option where you're looking to get around 15 or so touches and a touchdown out of him. Teams attack the Cardinals through the air and not as much on the ground, so it wouldn't shock me if we didn't see a lot of rushing unless the Commanders have a positive game script and are salting the game away.

Is it possible that more than one Commanders receiver is relevant in fantasy football? In THIS economy? It may be true, but we'll have to get some confirmation here. Stefon Diggs has been a stalwart for fantasy managers as a WR3 or flex play all season, but we may have seen a breakout performance from rookie Antonio Williams in Week 11. Williams put up career highs with 11 targets and a 9-122-0 line on 95% routes. He's been running most of the routes anyway, but Jayden Daniels really looked for him against the Bengals over and over again. The Commanders have needed a consistent receiver threat for a long time, preferably somebody under the age of 30. We could have that with Williams. Before anointing him, however, we must see more flashes of that in the coming weeks.

Fantasy Advice: Plug Williams into lineups as a flex play as he tries to build his fantasy value for the playoffs and the road ahead. The matchup against the Cardinals is as pristine as it gets for a receiver.

I haven't understood the hype and fervor for Okonkwo for a while now, but I am here to report that Okonkwo has scored 10 or more fantasy points in four of his last six games. He is the TE13 in points per game since Week 8. That's perfectly fine for a streaming tight end, and hey, maybe he'll score a touchdown this season!

Luckily for Okonkwo, Arizona has been awful at defending tight ends, most recently allowing 25.4 fantasy points (TE1 overall last week) to Travis Kelce. ESPN's adjusted ranks have the Cardinals as the second-easiest matchup in the league for the position. The ride hasn't been the smoothest when it comes to trusting a non-Diggs Commander in fantasy lineups this season, but Okonkwo feels a bit better in this specific matchup.

Fantasy Advice: If you're playing the matchups, you could do much worse than Okonkwo this week against a porous Cardinals defense.

McBride keeps doing McBride things, with a slight tweak. Yes, he had 12 of the Cardinals' 28 targets against the Chiefs with a 9-54 receiving line. However, the aDOT has lessened considerably with Carson Beck and Josh Johnson at quarterback, with a 4.2-yard and 3.0-yard aDOT, respectively, over the last two weeks. The receptions are great, but the upside has scaled back to the point where he's become a bit of a reception merchant. He obviously draws incredible volume, but defenses can make him their sole focus and let the quarterbacks try their hand with the receiver room to make plays. To be clear: you're still starting him in the vast majority of leagues, but that startability declines when you get into half-PPR and standard leagues.

Fantasy Advice: Keep McBride in your lineups against the Commanders, as they, like the Cardinals, are horrible at limiting fantasy tight end scoring.

Implied Point Total: 49ers 23.25, Seahawks 20.25

Must-Start Players: WR Jaxon Smith-Njigba

Seattle's backfield came out of Week 10 with a much clearer pecking order than anybody expected a month ago. In his third game back from an ACL tear, Zach Charbonnet played 72% of the snaps, ran 68% of the routes and handled 20 carries for 94 yards against the Raiders. Jadarian Price was left with 27% of the snaps, 28% of the routes and 26% of the team's rushing attempts. Charbonnet's early-down and third-down roles have both grown each week since returning. As far as the role and split go, it's pretty nice. Of the potential rookie risers over the second half of the season, it feels like Price has the steepest climb toward paying off his middle-round ADP.

San Francisco's run defense is fifth in efficiency and first in explosive run rate allowed, and ESPN's adjusted matchup ranks have the 49ers as the third-toughest defense for running backs in Week 12. It's not great, but this game is going to be a grinder for both teams. Head coach Mike Macdonald likely trusts Charbonnet more in these spots than Price, so we'll skew that way.

Fantasy Advice: Charbonnet is a volume-driven low-end RB2 who is in a brutal matchup against the 49ers. Price is a handcuff and bench stash who has no business being in lineups until the split moves back in his direction.

Must-Start Players: QB Brock Purdy, RB Christian McCaffrey, TE George Kittle

If you had some reservations about how Evans would fare in his first game back with an Achilles injury, they can officially be squashed. Evans may be a bit more managed with his workload, running a route on 57% of Brock Purdy's dropbacks, but he still saw a 29% target share, catching six of his seven targets for 109 yards and a touchdown to pace a team where nobody else had more than four targets. Evans put up a 7-113-1 stat line when these division rivals met in Week 5, so there shouldn't be too much worry here as long as Evans' Achilles doesn't give him any issues.

Fantasy Advice: Lock Evans in as a high-end WR2 against the Seahawks, as he should carry the volume and the big plays for the 49ers at home. Be advised that he was listed as questionable on the final Week 12 injury report, so have a backup plan in mind.

Samuel has settled into a role that is more about touches than targets, and his two games since returning from the hamstring injury show it. In Week 11, he caught three balls for 20 yards and added 27 yards on two carries against the Vikings. His Week 10 performance was built around a 70-yard touchdown run on top of a 3-31-1 receiving line. The usage is what you'd expect from a gadget player, but with everybody healthy for the most part in the San Francisco offense, Samuel will have to maximize the touches he gets moving forward. The offense is well-equipped for just that, luckily.

Fantasy Advice: Samuel is a solid flex play this week against Seattle, but there's some volatility here with his role.

Implied Point Total: Browns 22, Raiders 17.5

Must-Start Players: TE Brock Bowers (if active)

It was quite the circuitous route to get back there, but Ashton Jeanty may have returned to bell-cow status. He played 84% of the snaps compared to Mike Washington Jr.'s 22%, with a route share of 72%, and has been the RB16 in points per game since Week 9. Last week brought a 46-yard run and a 68-yard receiving touchdown, as he caught five of eight targets for 90 yards. I mean, sure, the receiving work drives most of the value, but that's not a bad thing! On a team that was missing Bowers in Week 11, somebody had to propel the Raiders on offense, and Jeanty (along with Jack Bech's 8-88-1) certainly did the trick.

Fantasy Advice: Cleveland has allowed the highest rate of explosive runs in the league, along with a high missed-tackle rate over its last five games. We'll take that with Jeanty as a low-end RB2 with some solid upside here. That upside increases if Bowers remains out.

The Browns' offense seemed to find a pulse in Week 10 against Houston, where Watson threw for 312 yards on 25-of-40 passing, ran for 49 yards and scored on the ground in a comeback from 17 points down. That was a 22.4-point fantasy day, good for QB9 on the week. Over his last three games, he's averaging 314.7 passing yards and 23.3 rushing yards per game. Strictly speaking from a fantasy football perspective, Watson has been a useful low-end fantasy asset. Coming off a bye week and now facing a leaky Raiders defense, Watson could return QB1 value this week against a team that has allowed 22 fantasy points per game to quarterbacks this season.

Fantasy Advice: If you roster Watson, he's definitely worth starting this week against the Raiders.

The Raiders allow the third-most fantasy points per game to opposing backfields, and that benefits Quinshon Judkins more than Dylan Sampson. Judkins, the hammer of the duo, out-touched Sampson 13-to-8 in Week 11, rushed for 38 yards with a touchdown and added 33 yards on three catches. Sampson was on the field a bit more and took on more of a receiving role, though both backs notched six targets. The Raiders allow the fewest targets per game and second-fewest receptions per game to running backs, so Sampson's fantasy viability could be a little more in question.

Fantasy Advice: I'm more apt to start Judkins over Sampson, but Judkins is an RB2 for me, with the rushing component being a far better way to attack the Raiders than Sampson's skill set. That said, Sampson is a low-end flex with some upside if he can compile receptions and buck Vegas' season-long trend.

Early in the season, KC Concepcion was the quieter producer of the pair of rookie receivers that includes Denzel Boston, but he has averaged almost seven targets per game over the last four contests. Additionally, the Raiders have allowed the third-most yards per target and the second-most fantasy points per opportunity to slot receivers. This is a good opportunity to put together a quality game on a national showcase the Browns don't usually have. Boston is the bigger question, as he didn't practice to start the week with what is listed as a quad injury after barely practicing the week before the bye and playing without much effectiveness. Boston and Concepcion both have 27 targets over the last four games and have consolidated targets among the wide receivers, so Boston being limited or out could really juice up Concepcion.

Fantasy Advice: Both Concepcion and Boston are solid flex plays against the Raiders on Sunday night, but keep an eye on Boston's status over the weekend (he was listed as questionable on the final injury report). Boston being out would pump Concepcion up to a startable WR3 option.

Fannin led the Browns with nine targets in Week 10, ahead of Concepcion (eight) and Sampson (six), and he continues to be a focal point of the passing game. ESPN's adjusted ranks have Las Vegas as the eighth-easiest defense for tight ends, which bodes well for Fannin to continue the target-earning binge he's been on over his last three games (23 targets). Let's hope that continues post-bye. He was limited at practice with a groin issue on Wednesday but practiced in full on Thursday, so he should be good to go for Sunday night.

Fantasy Advice: Tight ends have been wild this season from an injury standpoint, and somebody who has the potential for target earning of this magnitude should be rostered in every league and started in the vast majority. Get Fannin into lineups.

Implied Point Total: Buccaneers 23.25, Panthers 20.25

Must-Start Players: RB Chuba Hubbard, WR Tetairoa McMillan

Anytime you see "Bryce Young" and "Buccaneers" in the same sentence, it's hard not to think back to Week 7. Young scored just 5.42 fantasy points in that meeting, taking five sacks, throwing two interceptions and losing a fumble. Young was QB24 that week against this exact pass defense, and he's been middling ever since, averaging slightly more than 12 fantasy points per game over the last five weeks. Young is also doing this without offensive linemen Taylor Moton and Ikem Ekwonu. It's tough out here, but Young just hasn't had the time or the line stability to make the plays he made earlier in the season.

Fantasy Advice: The Bucs have been rough on quarterbacks, and Young has felt that firsthand, so Young is a low-end streaming option here against Tampa.

Egbuka leads the Buccaneers with 33 receptions and 296 receiving yards, and he's been flat-out horrific this season, with just 0.88 yards per route run over his last two games and a 1.05 YPRR across the season in one of the worst passing games in the NFL. Carolina is a soft 26th against wide receivers in ESPN's adjusted ranks, with the second-highest CPOE allowed over its last five games. The Bucs are home favorites, but trusting Egbuka at this stage of the season feels hard to get around to.

Fantasy Advice: It's Egbuka's last stand. He's a low, low-end flex play and nothing more. He's droppable if you need to reinforce the receiver position for the playoff stretch run.
