‘War’ Stars Sienna Miller and Dominic West Tussle Over Who Won the Divorce Drama: ‘Gwyneth Paltrow Ran So That the Rest of Us Could Walk’

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

SPOILER ALERT: This article contains major spoilers for “War,” which had its season finale on Nov. 19 on HBO and HBO Max in the U.S., and drops on Nov. 20 on Sky and NOW in the U.K.

The climactic season finale of “War”—  the Sky and HBO drama series starring Sienna Miller and Dominic West as warring spouses — sees an unexpected denouement just as the couple’s increasingly bitter divorce is about to be finalized. With their lawyers still battling it out, the couple bury the hatchet and hammer out their own, more equitable deal. And with that, they walk off into the sunset.

Written and created by by George Kay (“Hijack”), the series is set to return for a second season with a new case — and new clients — to occupy the show’s fictional law firms, Taylor & Byrne and Cathcarts. In Season 2, Marisa Tomei, Kelly Macdonald and David Harewood join the cast as the litigants in a high-stakes defamation case.

While Miller and West won’t reprise their roles as actress Carla Duvall and tech billionaire Morgan Henderson, neither seems quite ready to leave their characters behind. In a chat with Variety, both actors repeatedly slipped back into their roles, cheerily arguing over Morgan’s “flirting” with Carla’s barrister (Celia Imrie), Carla’s dalliance with Johnny Warren (Archie Renaux) and who really won their explosive — extremely expensive — divorce battle.

Sienna Miller: It was the last thing we ever shot, so that was lovely. And I can’t really remember: A part of me probably wanted something really dramatic and cataclysmic to happen. But at the same time, I defer to the brilliance of George Kay knowing what an audience wants, because he’s written the show that people are devouring. I think the sensationalist in me wanted handbags at dawn [British idiom meaning “a dramatic showdown”], but I also in retrospect think it’s quite lovely that they had that moment. Dom?

Dominic West: “Handbags at dawn” — that would have been great. I’d have loved handbags at dawn.

Miller: I think George told us that essentially, this is what’s going to happen: The lawyers screw everything up, and [Carla and Morgan] got there in a much more simple way. So we had an idea — not at the beginning of shooting, but as we were going on — we’re like, “How does it end? What happens?!” You know, nagging like a bunch of kids. We had an inkling that that was what it was, but, again, I was 35 weeks pregnant, so the whole thing is a blur. That’s why I’m hoping Dom can jump in.

West: I remember my first thoughts were “Fucking hell, what a waste of £10 million” or whatever they spent on the lawyers when they just make it up between themselves. But actually, I think that was the point George was trying to make, and I quite like the idea that they had to go through this terrible process of lawyers and courtroom drama and going to war with each other basically in order to realize actually what they meant to each other. And how they could amicably come to an arrangement where both felt that they’d got what they deserved, and that their relationship had been quantified in a good way, in a just way.

But I think that’s the thing that really hit me was “What a fucking waste of £10 million.” But then, if you’re a billionaire, I suppose you do waste £10 million quite happily just to work out how much you like your ex-wife or ex-husband.

Miller: I do think there’s something very zeitgeist-y — and I’m not sure how he’s done this, George — but the timing of this show is so interesting. I read an article, and it was talking about “Just normalize divorce.” That the process of divorce has become so acrimonious, hellish. The children suffer. It should be something that can be amicable. Obviously, Gwyneth Paltrow ran so that the rest of us could walk. But there is a conscious uncoupling that can be lovely and better for everyone, and it left it left me feeling that it probably should be the goal to avoid conflict and remember why you love this person and how to end it in the nicest way.

West: No, we didn’t. But [Kay] didn’t tell us straight away, did he? I don’t think I knew that it was going to end so amicably.

Miller: Well, I think the audience might feel that I did because you couldn’t remember the fucking poem, could you?

West: Well, I suppose you won in that you got money for ideas and running a business that you had nothing to do with. So in that way, I suppose you were a winner.

Miller: I hope that, because [Kay] has left it amicable at the end that Dom and I can pop back.

West: What, a spinoff with Sienna and Johnny? No way!

West: I haven’t watched it all yet. I’ve only just got Sky, but I’m getting to it. I can’t wait. I watched the first episode six times, and I haven’t managed to see the rest. But I’m on it.

Miller: One of the cast texted me something very funny, which was “I hope you can come back, but Carla’s such a crazy bitch, you never know she might do something else to get in there.”

West: She’s bound to have more legal trouble.

Miller: Well, judging by your nefarious behavior in Episode 1, I think it’s coming for you too. Now it’s a hit, we really want to be invited back.

Miller: Sorry, we haven’t spoken in a while!

West: It may have ended amicably on set, but it hasn’t really ended amicably between Sienna and I in terms of the characters.

West: It was really noticeable in court: When I was talking Sienna was sitting right there, and so we’re very close to each other, we can look at each other in the eye. And it was quite thrilling to be putting the boot in, or trying to make war with someone, when you’re sitting right there. I was getting into the moment, and that was quite thrilling, to be so confrontational and to be having a go at Carla, with her being right there. The second thing was being cross-examined by Celia Imrie was quite flirty. In fact, very flirty. She was really flirty, Celia.

Miller: You say that about everyone, Dom. I think it might be you.

Miller: One thing I particularly love about when Dom answers a question about “War” is he always says “to be at war with each other.” You always drop the name, which I appreciate. I just had to say.

West: Yeah, no, good. But anyway, let me finish. So yeah, [Imrie] was quite flirty, but that was her strategy and it was in the writing. But Celia really has got such a twinkle that I loved being cross-examined by her.

Miller: Well, I have been cross-examined [in real life] a few times, I think. I’m pretty sure I have been. Archie Panjabi [who plays Morgan’s barrister] was amazing and quite stern, and I had horrible flu that day, so again, an unhelpful, blurry response. I feel like Carla felt so in-the-right in herself that I wasn’t particularly rattled. It was an intimidating environment and it was a long day, but I quite enjoyed it because I felt like I had the upper hand. Dom would disagree.

Miller: I mean, I definitely have experienced opening the door and being faced with what Carla is faced with. Not anymore, thank God. But I only read the first script when I signed on. So you’re taking a gamble. I mean, I knew she was an actress and I knew she was famous, so I assumed there might be some of that, but I didn’t really — I don’t think I want to play an actress again. Unless it’s Carla coming back in “War.”

Miller: Not particularly, no, because I didn’t know where it would all go or the spins that it would take. And no, it’s not particularly interesting for me to relive those moments or those years, but I do have an innate knowledge of them, and I suppose that’s interesting for people to see. I feel like I’m so different to Carla herself that maybe her response was an interesting thing to explore.

West: Yeah, I mean, I suppose I did have hesitations about it, but in practice, in doing it, what was quite thrilling was the thought of a billionaire getting the sort of scrutiny that the British press normally reserves for young actresses. I found that quite a thrill.
