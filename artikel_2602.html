'Just keep coming to work': Texas A&amp;M players turn focus to Oklahoma

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

This season hasn't been what the Texas A&M football program or its fans expected, but the Aggies have a major opportunity to salvage the 2026 campaign, starting with a showdown against an old Big 12 rival on Saturday.

Texas A&M's biggest struggles this year have been on offense, ranking No. 13 in yards and points per game in the SEC. Inconsistent quarterback play from Marcel Reed has led to those marks, with Reed ranking No. 14 in completion percentage (55.7%) and quarterback rating (108.79) while tying for third-most interceptions in the league.

Despite that, third-year head coach Mike Elko continued to protect Reed, placing all the blame on himself and the offense as a whole for being completely inept. To defeat Oklahoma and No. 7 Texas to close out the year, Texas A&M must figure things out offensively, and fast, as both the Sooners and Longhorns boast top-five defensive units in the conference.

Here are quotes from senior safety Tawfiq Byard and sophomore tight end Kiotti Armstrong ahead of Texas A&M's road trip to Norman:

"I think the defense played pretty well these past two games, a couple of games that we played since Alabama. You know, just playing more together, playing more free, playing more together, just trying to focus on what we can do to help this team win and just keep stacking," Byard said.

"We've just got to do our job. I know it's kind of repetitive. We say it every week. You probably hear it every week, but it's really just simply just doing your job and making the crucial plays when we need it," Byard said.

We can't get caught up on who's doing what on offense, if they're scoring or not. Because if you just think about it, if we're so worried about what the offense is doing, we're never going to be able to do what we want to do as a defense," Byard said.

"They're a lot different now with coach Witten coaching the offense. They're a matchup-driven team. They try to get matchups depending on the guy. They've got a good tight end, good receiver, a good quarterback. They try to get their guys the ball in many ways they can. Explosive, kind of up-and-down offense," Byard said.

"(I'm) more comfortable as I got more reps, just getting back in the flow of things, getting back to what I do. You know, just like I said, just being more comfortable, being more vocal, you know, just going out there and cutting loose," Byard said.

"I feel like I've been progressing pretty well, just staying honed into the plans. The guys are making it real easy to lock into the playbook, understand why we're doing stuff. And coaches make it real easy for me to go out there and play for them," Armstrong said.

"Coming from high school, I played more receiver-based. In-line blocking was kind of new to me. I wouldn't say I was scared of it, but doing it against high-caliber players, I just wasn't ready at the time. I feel like I've taken a big step in doing that now," Armstrong said.

"Just honing into playbooks and we do as many details as we can fix going into it. Obviously, we're not going away. There's a lot of stuff you can try to point the finger at, but it's really just simple details," Armstrong said.

"Just keep coming to work every day. Honestly, it just takes one big play and momentum swings. Obviously, we haven't got the play we wanted yet or the game we wanted yet, but just keep coming to work. Nothing goes how you expect it or how you want it to go all the time, so you've got to keep showing up," Armstrong said.

"A lot of fronts. Obviously, the defense coordinator is a great defense coordinator for a long time in this game. We've got to hone in and stay detailed to the many different things they can do and just keep playing the game with the hard work," Armstrong said.
