Mass Killing in Poland Becomes Cause for a Political Fight

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The government blames the far right for inspiring the gunman, while the opposition accuses the government of lax security.

Reporting from Warsaw, Krakow and Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski, Poland

The shooting had barely stopped in Krakow’s medieval central square on Saturday when the head of a right-wing Polish television station who was in the area by chance appeared on camera to accuse Poland’s centrist government of unforgivable laxity that had left Poles unprotected.

In reality, the gunman had been swiftly confronted by two police officers, who were both wounded in an ensuing exchange of fire, but TV Republika broadcast a live report by its editor in chief, Tomasz Sakiewicz, claiming that “there was a clear absence of police,” with only “a single officer in nonstandard uniform.”

A passerby who heard the stand-up report, screamed at the broadcaster: “How can you spout such nonsense? What garbage!”

The shouting across Poland’s deep political divide has only grown louder since.

Politicians and commentators from all sides, seeking to score political points and rally their supporters ahead of an election next year, have jumped on the mass shooting on Saturday in Krakow’s Old Town market square.

Ignoring the political din, thousands of people crowded into the sprawling square on Monday evening for a “march of silence” organized by the city’s liberal-leaning mayor to mourn those killed two days earlier. A trumpeter played “The Sound of Silence.”

Poland’s centrist prime minister, Donald Tusk, and its nationalist president, Karol Nawrocki, briefly put aside their many differences to attend.
