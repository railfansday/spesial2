Hitting it big in the stock market doesn't have to mean getting in early on the next Nvidia (NVDA -3.30%) or Apple (AAPL -0.92%). Of course, that would be appreciated, but it's far from required to make good money in the market. Countless investors have built wealth by leaning on broad ETFs and trusting the power of time and consistency.

One ETF that can be a great addition to your portfolio is the Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF (VUG -2.21%). It has been a consistent market beater, and based on its historical returns, $500 monthly investments could grow to over $784,100 in 25 years.

VUG is the best of both worlds. On one side, its growth focus offers strong upside. On the other hand, because it holds only large-cap growth stocks, it offers a bit more stability than smaller growth stocks typically do.

VUG is tech-heavy (72.1%) because that's where much of the market's growth has been over the past couple of decades, but it also has decent non-tech exposure that provides some hedge. Just outside VUG's top 10 holdings are industry leaders like Visa (V -3.12%) and Costco (COST -0.25%), which have performed well for investors over the years.

Some have worried that major tech valuations have become inflated amid the current AI boom, increasing the chance of a pullback or correction. While those concerns may be warranted, they don't diminish the tech sector's growth potential or the companies leading the way for VUG.

Plenty of tech trends -- whether it's AI, cloud computing, or cybersecurity -- are on the earlier end of what many expect them to become. Investing in VUG is about capitalizing on growth while having a little crutch from other industries.

How much you ultimately make will depend on time and returns (which we can't predict), but the bigger takeaway is how powerful time can be in investing. In each case, you would've contributed much less than your ending investment.

VUG has a lot of overlap at the top with popular indexes like the S&P 500 (^GSPC -2.02%) and Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -2.25%), so if you're investing in an S&P 500 ETF or Nasdaq-100 ETF, double-check that you're not too concentrated in VUG's top holdings.

Apple, Nvidia, and Microsoft are great companies, no doubt, but part of the reason for investing in ETFs is diversification. There's no need to override that by overexposing to the same handful of stocks.
