Why HP Inc. Stock Jumped Nearly 15% Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The PC and printer giant beat fourth-quarter expectations and sidestepped the margin panic that crushed corporate cousin Hewlett-Packard Enterprise yesterday.

By Anders Bylund – Nov 25, 2026 at 1:06PM EST

HP Inc. (HPQ +11.49%) is the best performer in the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.25%) index today. On a gloomy morning when the leading index fell as much as 3.2%, the printing-oriented half of former tech titan Hewlett-Packard traded 14.7% higher at 11:25 a.m. ET.

The stock isn't only reversing Wednesday's general market mood. Corporate cousin Hewlett-Packard Enterprise (HPE +1.57%) posted similar results on Monday, and that stock fell 16.9% on Tuesday.

HP Inc. smashed Wall Street's expectations in Q4 2026. Net revenue rose 9.3% year-over-year to $16.0 billion. Adjusted earnings fell 10.8% to $0.83 per share. The average analyst would have settled for earnings near $0.75 per share on top-line revenue in the neighborhood of $15.4 billion. Looking ahead, management expects the next fiscal year's earnings per share to hold steady around $3.30. The Street consensus currently points to $3.17 for that metric.

This adds up to the bullish market reaction you'd expect after a robust financial report.

So why didn't Wall Street treat HP Inc. as harshly as it judged HPE's similar figures? It comes down to profit-margin math, customer clout, and a healthy dose of artificial intelligence (AI) bubble jitters.

Both cousins are wrestling with the same industrywide memory shortage, which pushed DRAM and storage up to roughly 40% of HP Inc.'s PC component costs last quarter. In fact, the personal systems division's operating margin took a beating, dipping to a razor-thin 3% as consumer demand cooled.
