Blackburn Rovers must heed Swansea City warning with Andri Gudjohnsen transfer

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Andri Gudjohnsen is having one of the best seasons a striker could have, scoring every other shot he takes for Blackburn Rovers in the Championship and netting consistently for Iceland, too.

No doubt there'll be interest in the 24-year-old, whether it comes in January or in the summer, if he continues in this vein of form. Then, questions will need to be asked about whether this can be sustainable, and if Blackburn would be wise to cash in.

Gudjohnsen has been incredible so far this season. He has 10 goals from 20 shots, the most clinical rate in Europe, and has recently netted four in two Nations League games for his country.

There haven't been any concrete links with interested clubs, but he's got plenty of admirers across EFL fanbases, whether that be West Brom or Birmingham City, and it's only a matter of time before some top-flight sides officially feel the same.

Blackburn may feel inclined to keep him, though, as he could possibly help them return to the Premier League, but this level of goalscoring mirrors something from last season in South Wales, and a decision made by Swansea City shouldn't be repeated.

It's often the case that foreign strikers need a year to properly settle into their new surroundings in the Championship.

Gudjohnsen, who struggled with injury and only netted seven last season, is proving that theory, and last year, Zan Vipotnik did the same at Swansea. The Slovenian only scored seven in 42 in his debut campaign after arriving from Bordeaux, but exploded into life last year.

Vipotnik scored 23 times in 44 league appearances for the Swans last term, winning the Golden Boot at a canter. However, it wasn't a case of him scoring easy chances; he massively overperformed his underlying numbers.

The 24-year-old's non-penalty xG last season was 9.48. He scored 18 of his goals from open play, outperforming his underlying numbers by nearly double. That's a mark of a good finisher, but questions were rightly being asked about how sustainable that could be.

This past summer, Vipotnik was the subject of interest, with newly-promoted Premier League side Hull City having a £10 million bid rejected. The 24-year-old had signed a new contract through until 2030 with the Swans, who evidently saw him as a piece in their puzzle to get to the top flight themselves.

Granted, at the time, £10 million for someone who had scored 23 times the year before did seem a little low, and Swansea were probably right to try and hold out for more.

Three months into the current season, though, the South Wales side may be regretting this decision slightly.

Vipotnik hasn't been the first choice for Swansea under Vítor Matos this year, with a resurgent Adam Idah leading the line in the main, and Ross Stewart coming in for starts in a few games before the international break.

The 24-year-old has scored three goals, but two of them have been penalties. His non-penalty xG is 2.00, so, importantly, he's not showing the clinical nature which led him to the Golden Boot last season.

Now, as the January window approaches, you'll be hard-pressed to find a side willing to spend the £10 million that Hull were offering this summer for Vipotnik. If anything, he may not have any interest at all.

If Vipotnik's numbers last season led some data-led thinkers to question whether he could maintain it, they must be going crazy looking at Gudjohnsen's.

The Icelandic international has been brilliant at getting into the right positions and being lethal when the opportunities come, but his overall expected goals tally is 3.94. His overperformance of 6.06 is by far the largest in the EFL.

Vipotnik's 23 goals came from 13.42 xG and 87 shots last season. That's an average of one goal every 3.78 shots — 1.78 more than Gudjohnsen's goal every other attempt so far this year.

Not only will it be a question that will be asked as the campaign goes on, whether Gudjohnsen can keep this up, but it'll be something that needs to be considered in future seasons, especially as and when transfer interest grows, and Blackburn will be weighing up whether to sell.

Ultimately, Blackburn hold all the cards regarding a January sale of Gudjohnsen. The Icelandic international signed a new "long-term" contract just last May, meaning they won't be under any pressure to sell.

There's potential that interested clubs will be monitoring his situation, but may not go in strong with a mid-season bid due primarily to his underlying numbers. After all, this could be an extended purple patch of clinical finishing for the 24-year-old.

If Gudjohnsen continues in this vein for Rovers, though, and ends the campaign pushing between 20 and 25 league goals at Ewood Park, there'll be much more expectation that bidders will come in, especially if Blackburn remain in the Championship.

His heritage as a third-generation star and son of Eidur Gudjohnsen could mean that clubs will start at a little more than what they did for Vipotnik this past summer, but even then, Blackburn could feel inclined to keep him as they strive for a top-flight return themselves.

But after such a prolific campaign, striking while the iron is hot and selling for eight figures could be the best thing they do.
