Burnham considering cuts to national insurance to create more youth jobs

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

PM hints he is open to suggestion in Milburn report to lower NICs contributions paid by companies for under-25s

UK firms: what is stopping you hiring young people?

Andy Burnham has said he would consider cuts to national insurance for young people “very carefully”.

The prime minister also believes his proposed changes to welfare are a “very different proposition” from those of his predecessor, Keir Starmer, and the former chancellor Rachel Reeves in the last government.

On Tuesday, a report suggested national insurance contributions (NICs) paid by firms for under-25s should be gradually lowered, starting with retail and hospitality companies, to help create more jobs.

The independent report, by the former Labour health secretary Alan Milburn, also proposed doubling the threshold at which businesses start paying NICs for 21- to 24-year-olds from £5,000 to £10,000.

Asked if he believed the last government was wrong to increase NICs, Burnham told LBC: “I think it’s something you’ve got to consider very carefully going forward. Obviously, I’ve come in [and] inherited the situation.

“I think the last government was right to offer support to employers to take on young people. So, that was one of the things that they did through the youth guarantee.

“I will want to look at all of the evidence in relation to it, but also the affordability of making any change to national insurance. There is an exemption for young people up to 21, so there is some [movement] that’s been done in that area.

“But I can’t give you a firm commitment today because obviously these matters are matters for the budget more broadly.”

Currently, businesses do not pay employer NICs for workers younger than 21 nor for apprentices under 25 unless they earn above a threshold of £50,270. For non-apprentices aged 21-24, employers pay 15% on annual earnings above £5,000.

Official figures show there are nearly 1 million 16- to 24-year-olds not in employment, education or training (Neet) in the UK.

Later, Burnham was challenged by Kemi Badenoch about NICs at prime minister’s questions, with the Conservative leader saying Milburn’s plans would not create jobs without parallel plans to make it cheaper and simpler to employ young people.

She said: “They destroy jobs, they kill businesses, and then they offer the businesses a little money to give young people work placements. It makes no sense at all.”

Burnham accused Badenoch of only seeking cuts to benefits, without wanting to offer support to jobless young people.

He said: “We will not accept [Badenoch] time and again coming to this house saying that we are going to bring down the welfare bill, but failing to support our young people.

“[The Conservatives] slashed youth services when they were in government. They cut the education maintenance allowance. They trebled tuition fees. They took hope from a generation – we will give it back to them.”

On the backbench rebellion to the last proposed reforms to welfare, Burnham told LBC: “Well, it is a very different proposition. First and foremost, it’s about support for young people, opportunity for young people.

“It’s not about an arbitrary number for cuts within the system. You’ve got Conservatives and Reform who are going that way. It’s the crude cuts approach, and it just doesn’t work. It’s why they left office with a million Neets, and we’re going to have to do something fundamentally different.

“I think Alan Milburn has laid out what that looks like. It is also about a very different way of providing support, so not the old top-down tick-box system of the past, but a much more localised approach.”
