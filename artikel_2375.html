Spain deliver footballing lesson to send France packing

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The great Spanish football revolution transformed the world game in the first decade of the 21st century, and in reaching a second World Cup final, Spain delivered a lesson in Dallas in how to play with deadly effectiveness.

This 2026 France team of stars were, in terms of football ringcraft, handed their passports and boarding cards and waved off at the gate by a Spain team in complete control. The reigning European champions are an unpretentious side, with just the single star in Lamine Yamal, but even he has no choice but to work as part of this cohesive, intelligent team.

Neither Yamal nor any of his team-mates look set to win the 2026 Golden Boot, but on Sunday just outside New York they may take home the only trophy that really matters.

This semi-final was bossed by a midfield triumvirate of Rodri, Dani Olmo and Fabián Ruiz, who did what all the best modern Spanish midfields do to their opposition: spirit away possession, and slowly drain hope. For France, so often the pitch was first too small, and then in an instant much too big. Such is the simple effectiveness of those intense passing triangles that confound the press and preface a switch of play, a new idea, or a ball into a man in space.

The first question from the French media after the game for Didier Deschamps was how he felt about being given such a lesson – and it was hard to argue with that. The marvellous Luis de La Fuente, a man whom France’s 1998 World Cup-winning captain would have breezed past without recognising five years ago, took Deschamps to places where, tactically, he had no answer.

Not for the first time either: in this summer and the previous two, De La Fuente has beaten Deschamps. Two years ago in the semi-final of Euro 2024 and then again in the Nations League semi-final last summer. This was the worst of the three for Deschamps, who will step down after four World Cups in charge of France with more questions about what might have been than that single triumph in 2018.

Mikel Oyarzabal, the 29-year-old Real Sociedad striker, dispatched a penalty won by Yamal in the first half. The Tottenham Hotspur full-back Pedro Porro added the second after half-time with the kind of forward foraging that would have drawn an approving smile from Ange Postecoglou, the man for whom Porro once did that regularly.

There were more Real Madrid players in France shirts than there were playing for Spain, but the club’s new acquisition, Marc Cucurella, was superb again. So too the man formerly known as French, Aymeric Laporte, and others such as Alex Baena and Oyarzabal. None has the profile of France’s biggest names.

To borrow a local analogy, Kylian Mbappé, Ousmane Dembélé and Michael Olise were corralled into the paddock and the gate closed behind them. Deschamps spoke later about his team failing to reach their best levels. He asked rhetorical questions about the referee Iván Barton, from El Salvador, suggesting he had made mistakes. There was a suspicion of handball from Yamal for his penalty award. But in reality the France coach had no definitive answers – he seemed not to know how he had lost this game.

Ultimately, Deschamps placed his faith in the pace and finishing of some of the best attackers in the world, and when they failed to click there was no alternative. Adrien Rabiot was booked early and substituted at half-time. William Saliba became the latest Arsenal player to be injured at this tournament, coming off in the first half.

To counter Mbappé’s pace on a couple of occasions, the Spain goalkeeper Unai Simon took up positions radically far outside his own area to play as a sweeper. Simon was rarely obliged to make a big save and there were even fewer last-ditch challenges from Spanish defenders. Instead, there was an unswerving belief from this Spain team that the game could be controlled and shaped, and France directed away from the areas that made them dangerous.

In the Fifa VIP seats, four greats of Spain’s 2010 World Cup win – Iker Casillas, Sergio Ramos, Xavi Hernandez and Carles Puyol – looked on approvingly. Their side, dominant over two Euros and one World Cup, was different. Built from the great Real Madrid and Barcelona teams of the era, while this Spain side have reached the World Cup final on Sunday without anything like that big-two star power.

“We did it with discipline, organisation, sacrifice,” De La Fuente said. “We did very well at predicting the game’s phases and that’s what we did best as Spanish players. We can interpret games and how to deal with the different phases. It’s the fruit of our work in the academies – and all the coaches. We really need to value what they are doing at that level.”

He was right, of course, as the head of a football nation that develops and refines such a high volume of players capable of operating in such harmony. Certainly, they go into Sunday’s final with an extra day’s rest and surely now favourites against either Argentina or England. “You could see this tie as like a final,” De La Fuente said, “and you could say the same for England and Argentina. I don’t prefer one over the other.”

Two years ago in Berlin, De La Fuente’s Spain did much the same to Gareth Southgate’s England in the Euro 2024 final as they did on this occasion. Then, an evening of Spanish control gave way to a gradual realisation that England could not land a punch until it was too late.

The same was the case this time for France. They conceded a penalty after 20 minutes when Lucas Digne swung at a loose ball without checking that Yamal was about to beat him to it. Similar to the penalty Harry Kane conceded against Mexico in the last-16 tie, it was clumsy, but Yamal anticipated the contact. The ball struck his shoulder and was not given as a handball. Later in the second half, Yamal raced past Digne and finished, only to be fractionally offside at the start of his run.

As France struggled to make the connections that are so crucial to them – chiefly Olise and Mbappé – at the other end Spain’s passing was exemplary. There was one lightning sequence, in the 35th minute, when Baena won possession and Rodri, Yamal and Olmo combined to make a chance for Ruiz. It all happened too fast for France.

In the moments before the second goal, Olmo moved through yards of space where the French midfield should have been. Seconds later Porro passed the ball urgently to the feet of Olmo and slipped the return past Mike Maignan. Deschamps tried to change with Désiré Doué and Rayan Cherki, but it was too late. France were in the trap, and there was no escape.

Whether you use your eyes or the underlying data, France and Spain look like the two best teams at this World Cup but only one of them will play in Sunday’s final in New Jersey.

France are the tournament’s top goalscorers with 16 goals in six matches, while European champions Spain have the best defence.

France have certainly played as a team at this tournament, but their great strength is a luxurious level of individual quality across a front four of Desire Doue, Michael Olise, Ousmane Dembele and Kylian Mbappe. Spain rely more on their collective patterns of play and a distinctive style which is very much their own.

Most opponents at this World Cup have sat off France and hoped to limit the damage, but Spain will not approach the game in that way which should make this an absorbing contest. Spain will want to hide the ball from France’s best players and ask questions of a defence which has been relatively untroubled.

However, the more possession Spain have, the higher up the pitch their centre-backs Aymeric Laporte and Pau Curbasi will be, which could leave them vulnerable to a fleet-footed France attack in transition.

If Spain are to hurt France, Lamine Yamal against either Theo Hernandez or Lucas Digne looks the most likely avenue. Left-back is the one position in France’s team in which they do not have an elite option.

Spain have been utterly dominant for long stretches of their matches, but have not converted that control into goals in the manner of France, and that could well be the difference. Mbappe is currently the tournament’s joint top scorer on eight goals, level with Lionel Messi and one behind Argentine in the all-time list of World Cup top scorers.

Messi, and England, will be watching this closely and it ought to be a cracker in Dallas.
