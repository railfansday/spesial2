After Further Review: New York Jets-Los Angeles Chargers

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

After each game, we'll be sharing a comprehensive reivew that breaks down some of the controversial decisions from the officiating crew in the game. You may disagree in some cases, and that's fine…

Chargers 6-50 (leading to one Jets first down)Jets 4-18 (leading to two Chargers first downs)

Note: This does not include penalties that are declined or off-set.

Plays where the call was obvious, uncontroversial or not visible on broadcast footage

Olu Fashanu holding on a third down incompletion in the red zone. A very clear grab by Fashanu, who pulled back on the inside of Khalil Mack's shoulder pad as he went by him to prevent him from getting to Geno Smith.

Delay of game. Smith got frustrated here as he was unable to get everyone lined up in time and opted to just take the penalty rather than waste a timeout or rush to get the play off.

Jake Slaughter ineligible man downfield to negate a five-yard screen pass on 2nd-and-6. Clearly at least two yards downfield as the ball was being thrown, as his timing was a bit off. You only get a yard of leeway.

Delay of game on a punt. This didn't appear to be deliberate but they had plenty of time to get it off.

Jalyn Armour-Davis was flagged for going out of bounds as a gunner on a punt. He went out without being forced by a Jets player but did make the effort to get back in the field of play which is why it was five and not 15 yards.

Donte Jackson illegal contact to negate a Chargers sack. There was a slight grab on Adonai Mitchell as he made his route break, but this was a little soft.

Ladd McConkey offensive offside and Kayode Awosika offensive holding on a red zone incompletion. McConkey was definitely on the ball, so that was clear. The hold was arguably just as clear as Awosika had a handful of Darrell Jackson's jersey around the shoulder pad area and pulled his momentum away from pursuing the quarterback.

Cole Strange unsportsmanlike penalty after an extra point. The footage was not shown on the broadcast so it's unclear what he did, but it damaged the Chargers' late game strategy.

Roughing the passer on David Bailey to negate a third down red zone stop. This has already been discussed at length online. Of course it was very soft and not the kind of play the law is designed to prevent, and reminds us of many worse hits on Jets quarterbacks in recent history that went uncalled. Clearly Bailey didn't mean to hit Justin Herbert in the face mask as he was trying to deflect a pass, but his hand came down, made contact with Herbert's arm on his follow through and then hit Herbert's face mask. It wasn't deliberate, but by the letter of the law even though it was inadvertent, it was contact to the head/neck area and it was forcible because his hand was swiping downwards. He will probably end up still having to pay the fine on this one even if they appeal it. In isolation, this is probably hard to argue based on the rules as they are right now. This flag meant the Jets fell just 15 seconds short of a penalty-free first half.

Andrew Beck kickoff short of the landing zone to open the second half. This gave the Chargers the ball at their 40 and is probably one of the worst kickoffs you'll ever see, but that's part of the price you pay by having the extra tackler out there. He drilled it low, didn't seem to make clean contact and it bounced almost 15 yards short of the landing zone at the 34. This would have been even funnier if the Jets had been penalty-free at the half.

Azareye'h Thomas illegal hands to the face to negate a third down, red zone sack. This is the third time Thomas has been called for this in 2026, in exactly the same situation, and all of them have been soft. This one might actually have been the most justifiable of the three called, as he once again tried to jam with two hands to the chest in press coverage, but one hand slipped upwards into the side of the receiver's helmet. On the others, the receiver lowered his head to initiate the contact, but this wasn't the case here.

Kimani Vidal personal foul after a play. Vidal clearly threw a punch/shove at Malachi Moore's chest at the end of a scuffle. Herbert had been marked short of the marker on his 2nd-and-9 run, so this penalty would have made it 3rd-and-16. However, the spot was reviewed and the first down granted, which made it 1st-and-10 even though the ball was still moved back 15 yards.

Cam Hart defensive holding away from the ball on Sterling Shepard's first touchdown catch. Hart was covering Mitchell and there did appear to be a slight grab as Mitchell broke back towards the middle. Closer to the ball, Tony Jefferson grabbed Mason Taylor in almost identical fashion, but this was not flagged.

Logan Taylor ineligible man downfield on an incomplete pass which Herbert basically just shoveled out of bounds to avoid a loss of yardage. It was a surprise to see this called. Taylor had essentially stopped playing and was slowly jogging to a stop. Had Herbert not kind of double-clutched the toss out of bounds, he wouldn't even have been a yard downfield on the release and as it was it was only a yard and a half.

Here were some of the other notable missed calls, replay situations and controversial moments:

On the play mentioned above where Herbert was ruled to have picked up the first down after a review it was very close as to whether his foot came down inbounds or out of bounds, but this doesn't actually matter because the ball was level with the line to gain as it came down anyway, and then he crossed the sideline at that moment.

On Shepard's first touchdown he made a low diving catch and landed with the ball on about the half-yard line, but did have immediate control before then and the ball was across the plane of the goal line at the moment of control, so it was correctly ruled a touchdown.

McConkey had a catch ruled as an incompletion after Jamien Sherwood knocked the ball loose and it went out of bounds at the marker. The first official called it a catch and fumble but he was overruled by the second official. However, after a challenge, they reverted to the original call, which was correct as he made a football move in turning upfield and extending the ball.

On the fourth down play where D'Angelo Ponds bumped the running back towards the sideline, but he stayed inbounds and fell forwards for the first down, it probably didn't matter because when Ponds initially hit him, the ball was right at the marker, so he likely gets forward progress and a first down either way.

A flag was thrown and then picked up after a late on-field scuffle which wasn't shown on the broadcast footage. This stopped the clock which then meant there was a 10-second runoff, although that didn't particularly benefit or inconvenience either team on this occasion.

There was one ridiculous play where Joe Tippmann had his helmet ripped off by Teair Tart, but nothing was called.

On Omarion Hampton's longest run of the day, Tre Harris got away with a block in the back on Kingsley Enagbare.

Smith smartly threw the ball at Jeremy Ruckert's feet in the pocket to avoid a sack or intentional grounding call.

Taylor's rub route helped free Shepard up for his second touchdown as the defender hesitated. Contrast this with what happened last week when the defender just ran right into Taylor and got a flag from it.

Finally, Hall's knee was down with the ball short of the marker on second down when he picked up a late first down on the drive where the Jets ran out the clock. Any review would have been upstairs anyway because it was the last two minutes but the Chargers might actually have preferred a first down to 3rd-and-inches, because the latter could have given the Jets a chance to ice the game immediately.

Let us know what we missed – or misinterpreted – in the comments…
