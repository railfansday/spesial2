Aidan Hutchinson’s season-ending ACL tear is a nightmare for the Lions

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Detroit's worst fears were confirmed Monday afternoon when it was announced Aidan Hutchinson officially tore his ACL while celebrating a sack against the Patriots in Germany, and will miss the rest of the season. It's a mammoth blow to a team already wracked with injuries on the defensive side of the ball, and could become the straw that breaks the back of this team's chances to win the NFC North in 2026.

Hutchinson joined a small but infamous group of players who have suffered a serious injury while celebrating. It's easy to take a 10,000 foot view and say "it was dumb to jump around like that," but it was a freak injury — one of those random things that happen in football. There's no one really anyone's fault here, unless you believe in fate.

"Fate" is perhaps the most apt term to use when it comes to the Hutchinson injury, because this could well be fatal when it comes to the Lions' chances in 2026. This is still an incredibly good football team, sitting at 7-2 and very much in the picture to not just make the playoffs, but potentially win the NFC North — however, losing a player of Hutchinson's caliber really isn't something this Lions team can afford, especially as this season gets more difficult in the race to the finish.

It's far too easy to look at Hutchinson's raw stats, see six sacks, and assume "that can be replaced." The truth is, Hutchinson has been the best 4-3 EDGE rusher in the NFL this season by a considerable margin. While finishing sacks might not be his biggest strength, generating pressure is — even though he's routinely doubled off the edge.

In nine games, Hutchinson has generated 52 pressures and a league-high 34 hurries. A wrecking ball, his ability to drop his pad level while maintaining speed and power is second to none. This has made him incredibly difficult to mitigate with chip blocks, meaning opponents need to dedicate a lineman with additional help to stop him from closing distance.

Playing 347 pass-rush snaps, Hutchinson has generated pressure on 14.9% of downs, while also having a staggering 27.1% pass-rush win rate. To put this in context: Last season, Myles Garrett had a win rate of 29% en route to breaking the NFL all-time sack record.

That isn't something that can be easily replaced, especially by Derrick Moore, who will take Hutchinson's role in the lineup now. Moore is a serviceable rotational pass rusher, but his pressure rating is 10% this season, and his win rate is substantially lower. There is no question the pass rush will take a massive step backwards, while also altering how the Lions can approach their defense.

An underrated element to Hutchinson's game is his ability to seal the edge against the run. While his raw stop numbers aren't eye-popping, they shouldn't be — largely because teams simply don't run the ball to his side when he's on the field. This impact is profound, and lacking that element will hurt even more with Jack Campbell out of the lineup, who so often filled the role of the clean up hitter on the Detroit defense. Hutch would seal off one side of the field, Campbell would make the stops in the middle or to the alternate side. Now all of that is gone.

Five more wins would have put the Lions at 12-5 on the season. However, several of these wins become a lot more interesting in light of the Hutchinson injury. The Buccaneers defense is one of the best in the NFL, and a unit that legitimately has the talent to disrupt Jared Goff enough to do damage. Now they can establish their running game with less opposition, knowing they no longer have to worry about Hutchinson off the edge.

There's no question Atlanta is a broken team on the verge of retooling once more, but they still have Bijan Robinson. Who are we kidding? This and the Titans game are still easy wins for the Lions.

That leaves the Giants and Packers, both of which feel like toss-up games now. The Giants have the edge pressure to get to Goff, the Packers will be able to generate more explosive plays with Jordan Love having more time in the pocket. It's a bad combo for the Lions in that game too.

These are all very small margins, but small margins are all you get in the NFC North this season. It's an absolute murderer's row, and nobody can really afford to drop games down the stretch when four teams are all competing for three playoff spots. Moreover, Hutchinson absense will put more pressure on the rest of the defense, which is already reeling from injuries this season. It's unclear if anyone has the ability to step up and make noise, which is concerning.

Rumors of Detroit's demise are silly … right now. We don't truly know how profound the drop in play will be, though if this team comes out flat (or heck, loses) to the Buccaneers — then we can sound the alarm. There's just no question that this is the worst defensive injury the team could face, and it comes at the worst possible time for their playoff push. Especially considering it's less than a week after the close of the NFL trade deadline.
