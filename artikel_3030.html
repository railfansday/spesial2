Black Friday Stock Deals: 7 Bargains Our Analysts Are Watching Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

A lower price tag doesn’t make a stock a bargain by default. As Warren Buffett’s quote reminds us, the key word is “quality” — some stocks are marked down for a reason.

By The Premium Team – Nov 27, 2026 at 1:55PM EST

10 stocks we like better than GE Vernova ›

It's Black Friday, and millions of shoppers will hit stores and websites for the biggest retail day of the year. Meanwhile, Wall Street is quietly running a clearance sale of its own.

Warren Buffett famously loves a good sale. "Whether we're talking about socks or stocks, I like buying quality merchandise when it is marked down," he once wrote.

The recent market sell-off has left plenty of high-quality businesses sitting on the clearance rack. We asked our analysts to name one stock they think is on sale right now, and why. Here's what they picked (as a reminder, these are personal views and not official Motley Fool recommendations, so be sure to do your own research before buying).

GE Vernova's (GEV +2.84%) business has rarely looked stronger, but its stock tells a different story. Artificial intelligence (AI) data centers require massive amounts of power, and the high-voltage transformers and gas turbines needed to deliver it are in short supply.

That demand pushed GE Vernova's backlog to $197 billion in the third quarter of fiscal year 2026, providing multi-year revenue visibility. Until a few months ago, the company didn't expect backlog to hit $200 billion before 2028. Its turbine factory slots are getting sold out through 2032, and it has struck a deal to acquire Trench Group for $9 billion, vertically integrating to secure critical grid parts.

GE Vernova ended Q3 with nearly $17 billion in cash, generated $5.4 billion in free cash flow, and doubled its stock buyback plan to $20 billion.

With the stock down nearly 32% over the past six months, I'd say this is one AI stock on sale you can't go wrong with.

Alphabet (GOOG +0.54%) (GOOGL +0.68%) stock has reached bargain-basement levels, trading around 21x forward earnings estimates, as investors worry about the company's hefty investment in the AI infrastructure build-out.

That said, Alphabet's spending helps serve existing demand, and the cloud backlog of $561 billion suggests a huge wave of revenue growth lies ahead. On top of this, Google Search's longtime search market dominance should support ongoing ad revenue growth. Still, Alphabet's main risk is lasting pressure on free cash flow if the AI spending deepens and drags on.

Despite enduring a rocky ride, Amazon (AMZN +3.96%) investors continue to prosper, with the stock outpacing the broader S&P 500 so far this year. It's easy to see why.

E-commerce sales climbed nearly 12% year over year this far in 2026, while its industry-leading cloud revenue from Amazon Web Services (AWS) jumped 35% -- thanks to strong enterprise adoption of the company's artificial intelligence services. The company has become the world's third-largest digital advertiser, with ad sales up 20%.

Wall Street expects Amazon to grow its top line by nearly 16% annually over the next five years. Yet for all that growth, Amazon is remarkably cheap, selling for just 21 times earnings, well below its average three-year multiple of 39. That's a price worthy of Black Friday.

As of Nov. 19, Nu Holdings (NU +4.45%) is down 29% in 2026. Most of that drop came from one painful Brazilian election week. But political noise tends to be temporary, and this too shall pass.

I bought my first shares on Oct. 26, right after the stock fell 17% in a day on progressive-leaning election results.

Meanwhile, the stock trades at 11 times forward earnings while the business grew sales 37% last quarter. That's a wide-open buying window, and I climbed through it with an open wallet.

Nu makes over $1 billion a quarter in bottom-line profit with a return on equity above 31%. It signed up 3.7 million new customers in Q3 alone. The company also just secured a banking license in Brazil with no extra capital strings attached. And there's the U.S. launch, with Visa alum Rob Livingston as the California-based CFO. Nu is no longer a one-country story (or even stricly a Latin American business), and innovation outlasts election cycles.

Lula gets another four years, and Brazilian borrowers are paying late more often. Brazilian politics and late-stage credit quality could keep Nu's stock bumpy for a while.

Quarterly revenue at Anthropic (ANT +10.98%) has grown twelvefold in a year to $17 billion. Wow. More remarkably, existing customers are spending 9X what they did a year earlier. Claude is evolving from a software subscription into a fundamental business resource. And Anthropic is approaching operating breakeven. Most of its reported net loss stems from non-cash accounting charges. With shares below their IPO price, investors are buying extraordinary enterprise customer demand at a discount to the long-term opportunity. This is a B2B juggernaut.

KLA (KLAC +4.96%) dominates the market for finding microscopic defects in semiconductor manufacturing. The more complex chips become, the more financially damaging each defect becomes. That makes KLA increasingly indispensable. Quarterly revenue rose 29%, while free cash flow reached $1.34 billion. Its growing population of installed machines also generates recurring service revenue. KLA benefits not merely from greater chip production but from the rising difficulty of making each chip. And 20-year CEO Richard Wallace, an unsung hero, is one of the best-performing leaders in technology.

BillionToOne (BLLN +2.38%) uses proprietary genetic technology to detect incredibly small amounts of DNA associated with pregnancy and cancer. Quarterly testing volume increased 25%, average selling prices rose 18%, and cancer revenue nearly doubled. Gross margins reached 71%, alongside positive operating profits and free cash flow. The December launch of a blood-only test for detecting residual cancer expands BLLN's opportunity. Unlike many young diagnostics companies, BillionToOne has demonstrated that scientific innovation can finance further scientific innovation. I expect this company to deliver top-line growth and rising returns on capital for a long time to come!I believe all three of these stocks will beat the market over the next five years and yet I also believe all three will be more volatile than the market.

A lower price tag doesn't make a stock a bargain by default. As Buffett's quote reminds us, the key word is "quality" -- some stocks are marked down for a reason.

So before you buy, ask two questions: Why did the price drop, and would you still want to own this business at full price? If the answer is yes, the discount is just a bonus. If the answer is yes, the discount is a bonus.
