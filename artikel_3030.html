Birchtech (BCHT) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Partnership shifts and a $78 million judgment shape the company's next phase.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Birchtech Corp. (BCHT +6.36%) reported a shift in revenue composition as product sales growth partially offset the absence of prior year licensing fees. Management stated that the air quality segment serves as a capital source to fund the expansion of the water treatment platform, which targets contaminants like PFAS and PFOS. The company transitioned its carbon strategy from a potential acquisition to a partnership model to establish market presence with lower capital requirements. Management indicated that ongoing patent litigation resulted in a $78 million judgment, with interest continuing to accrue during the appeal process. The company is prioritizing the conversion of licensed utilities into recurring supply customers while advancing its carbon rejuvenation facility plans.

Third quarter product revenues, which are primarily derived from our sorbent product sales, increased 15.7% year-over-year to approximately $5.5 million. That growth was driven by the mix of plants running and increased demand for coal-fired generation, supported by more extreme weather conditions and higher natural gas prices. The third quarter has historically been one of our strongest periods for mercury emissions control, as summer cooling demand drives higher coal-fired generation, and with it, greater sorbent consumption at the plants that we serve. Our SEA platform remains a cornerstone of the company's air business. The SEA technology is used in approximately 80% of U.S. coal-fired power plants that use sorbent technologies.

And with all IPR challenges concluded, our commercial focus is squarely on deepening those relationships. As incumbent supply arrangements conclude at utilities that we've already licensed under Birchtech, we're now positioned our sorbent formulations and the applied expertise that comes with them for adoption into their day-to-day operations. The demand backdrop remains constructive. Electricity demand from data centers and AI continues to rise, and coal-fired generation continues to play an important role in grid reliability. We expect this environment to support a growing pipeline of supply conversations with utilities over the coming years. As we close out 2026 and look ahead to 2027, the air division's road map remains centered on 3 objectives.

One, convert unlicensed users of our technology into licensees and ultimately, long-term supply customers. Two, grow reoccurring activated carbon sales across our roster of licensed utilities, and also settle our last major case with a Midwest defendant. And three, direct the cash flow generated by this mature, high-margin business towards scaling up our water platform, where we see the greatest long-term return potential. In short, the air business continues to serve as our self-funding engine, generating both the capital and the market credibility that allow us to invest in water while continuing to deliver value to our shareholders. Now let me turn to the water, where our focus this quarter was on building the commercial infrastructure required to scale.

Our Design Centers remain the foundation of our data-first approach to water treatment. We continued to expand the capabilities of our Analytical Design Center, which now supports evaluation of a broader range of treatment media using utilities' own water, helping smaller systems identify the most cost-effective path to compliance before they commit their capital. Through our collaboration with national engineering firm, Civil & Environmental Consultants, RSSCT testing performed at our Analytical Design Center, is available to hundreds of water utility clients that they serve across the country. And we continue to support multiple national engineering firms with a combination of RSSCT analytics and carbon reactivation work on research projects with government entities and larger municipalities.

Every one of these engagements produce performance data that helps the utility select the right treatment approach and positions Birchtech as a natural partner when that utility moves to full scale. On the product side, our SEA-IX nuclear-grade ion exchange resin line, which we launched in March, continues to target an estimated $200-plus million addressable market, spanning nuclear power, coal-fired utilities and municipal water treatment. And we have since expanded that line with resins designed to remove boron and heavy metals. Alongside granular activated carbon, ion exchange resins are recognized by the U.S.

EPA as a best available technology for many contaminants, including PFAS, which allows us to match the right media to each customer's water chemistry rather than forcing a one-size-fits-all solution. Our water treatment solutions business continues to collaborate with engineering firms and utilities on integrated projects that may include end-to-end media change-out services, vessel replacements and shorter-term pilot testing, primarily in wastewater applications for water utilities as well as coal and nuclear power plants. Our largest long-term opportunity remains with our Carbon Rejuvenation technology. Our improved thermal reactivation process has demonstrated that rejuvenated granular activated carbon performs comparable to virgin carbon for PFAS removal, which could mean meaningful lower life cycle media costs for utilities while reducing that waste.

As part of our efforts to establish commercial-scale reactivation capabilities, we also evaluated potential acquisition opportunities. In September, we announced a proposed acquisition under a nonbinding term agreement. Following further evaluation, however, we decided not to proceed with the acquisition and terminated the term sheet. Separately, we recently entered into an agreement with an industry partner for the supply of granular activated carbon, which we believe will provide a faster, more capital-efficient route into the water treatment market than the proposed acquisition.

Under the agreement, we expect to begin supplying high-grade granular activated carbon to water treatment customers in early 2027, starting at 2,000 tons per year without requiring any capital expenditure on our part to establish this initial supply capability for the water market. We expect this agreement to generate meaningful granular activated carbon supply revenues beginning in '27 while supporting the water market with a high-grade product and the capacity to scale with demand. We plan to expand our sales team as the supply ramps up, given that the total supply, once fully up to capacity, is approximately 10,000 tons per year.

At the same time, we continue to advance our Carbon Rejuvenation strategy, including the development of our first reactivation facility. And developing our own facility, built around our proprietary process, allows us to align that investment with the growth of the water treatment market and scale capacity as the demand grows. Beyond that facility, our strategy contemplates additional reactivation capacity developed in partnership with water utilities. We are now in discussions with prospective water utilities, those partners and the engineering firms that serve them.

Data generated at our Design Centers is being used to define the permitting requirements, capital expenditure parameters and projected operating costs for our planned reactivation capacity, and we look forward to sharing more as these efforts progress. The regulatory backdrop continues to enforce the need for affordable solutions. The federal drinking water limits of 4 parts per trillion for PFOA and PFOS remain in place. And while the EPA has proposed allowing eligible systems up to 2 additional years to reach compliance, the underlying obligation is not going away.

And under that proposal, systems with PFOA or PFOS levels at or above 12 parts per trillion that request the extension would still need to implement interim treatment measures for the meantime. For many utilities, particularly smaller and rural systems, the question is no longer whether to treat, but how to do so affordably. That is precisely the problem our water platform is designed to solve. That focus on smaller systems is also why we were pleased to welcome Charlie Cooper as Business Development Manager on October 1.

Charlie brings 21 years of hands-on water treatment experience, from industrial mine water discharge to municipal drinking water, including 5 years overseeing the West Virginia Rural Water Association's Emerging Contaminants Program, where he helped systems across the state prepare for new testing, treatment and regulatory demands. With nearly 50,000 community water systems nationwide, many of them small utilities with limited internal resources, Charlie's experience will help us engage these systems earlier and connect them with practical, cost-effective solutions, including the potential savings of Carbon Rejuvenation. From here, our water strategy is about conversion and scale, turning testing engagements into reoccurring service and product revenue, advancing our carbon rejuvenation capabilities and continuing to broaden our product line.

Complementary to established air business, our clean water technologies provide a second substantial revenue stream that we expect to grow meaningfully over the next several years.
