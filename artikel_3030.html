Nvidia Soared 35% on Jensen Huang's Return. Is It Too Late to Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The stock is still down with room to run.

By John Bromels – Nov 23, 2026 at 3:31PM EST

If you've been following the fast-moving Jensen Huang drama at Nvidia (NVDA +31.48%), you probably have a severe case of whiplash this morning. Nvidia announced today that its board unanimously voted to reinstate the former CEO less than two weeks after his resignation.

In response, Nvidia's shares jumped as much as 35% intraday after falling 47.5% from their early October highs.

Given the size of the jump, is it too late to buy Nvidia stock? I don't think so. Here's why.

The Jensen Huang soap opera -- which includes reports of foreign spies, lurid affairs, and shocking reversals of fortune -- is hard to look away from. Indeed, it's easily the most exciting drama to hit the stock market in years. But it's the kind of drama that doesn't tend to impact long-term results.

No, it's never a good thing when your CEO's name is plastered all over the news as having had an affair with a potential foreign spy, but unless there are ongoing national security implications for the company, the business should keep chugging along regardless.

And chug along it has. Nvidia's Q3 revenue hit $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year (YOY), and up 20% from the prior quarter. Meanwhile, data center revenue totaled $107.6 billion, up 110% year over year. Gross margins stayed close to 75%. Net income more than doubled YOY to $65.3 billion, while management guided for about $130 billion in Q4 revenue (which would be a 91% year-over-year increase).

That Q4 projection, it's worth noting, was made by CFO Colette Kress after Huang's resignation and prior to his reinstatement. It seems unlikely that Huang's return would result in lower projections. If anything, the sense that Nvidia has put its drama behind it might only improve its outlook.

But is the drama actually in Nvidia's rearview mirror?

Various figures within the Trump administration, including the president himself, publicly backed Huang prior to today's surprise reinstatement.

Afterward, Trump took credit for the reinstatement, telling CNBC that he had spoken to Nvidia's board and given them "assurances at least with everything we know, and we know a lot," reiterating that, in his view, Huang "did nothing wrong."

If Nvidia's board was concerned that national security risks or consequences tied to Huang's personal behavior or business activities could blow back on the company as a whole, it's unlikely they would have unanimously approved his return.

Last week, the board created a national security and export control oversight committee, with an independent investigation ongoing, so investors can't quite regard the matter as fully closed.

Nvidia's growth comes from a product lineup, supply chain, and customer relationships built by thousands of people over many years. There was never a good reason to expect that growth to flag without Huang aboard. Still, his return should soothe a nervous market.

Even after the 35% post-reinstatement surge, Nvidia's stock is still down about 11% for the year, despite quarter after quarter of strong results. That gap suggests the market has been pricing in more worry than the fundamentals warrant. In my view, there's still plenty of room for shares to run.
