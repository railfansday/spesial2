Small Businesses: AI Should Enable Team Collaboration, Not Siloed Work Gains

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Shawn Chang is General Manager of ASUS North America, where he drives consumer and commercial business strategy in United States and Canada.

In a small business, AI deployment usually follows a similar pattern. Employers will share an initiative to start implementing a new AI tool, and employees will start experimenting with prompts, seeing how they can implement this new tool into their operations. After a few weeks, a pattern emerges: Managers are using AI to summarize reports; marketing teams are using it to brainstorm campaign ideas; and directors are using it to draft presentations. These are great use cases and have had an overwhelmingly positive impact on operations.

But take a step back, and you’ll see that these AI gains are working in isolation. Each employee is using AI differently, rarely in sync with their co-workers without adding up to real business performance gains. Using AI to draft an email, enabling an employee to work faster, does not necessarily equate to a team working faster as it is often the handoff of projects between people that are the slowest.

AI works better when employees understand it as a collaboration tool. Instead of using it as a tool for individual tasks, AI should be a tool for managing workflows through teams and people. For example, AI can prepare first drafts that can be reviewed and refined, or it can surface insights to inform decisions or handle routine coordination. While as of now most businesses see the benefits and success of personal AI integration, the next step is to see how to enable AI collaboration among team members.

McKinsey also reports similar sentiment in their “Agents, robots and us: Skill partnerships in the age of AI” report. In fact, they estimate that this collaborative implementation of AI into human centered workflows could unlock as much as $2.9 trillion in economic value by 2030. They report that using AI technology can redesign how work is connected between people and allow both AI and people to thrive in what they can do best. Employees can deploy their skills that require human connection and trust (things like judgment, strategy, creativity and relational skills), while AI can support tasks such as research, drafting and monitoring. In this context, neither replaces the other. Rather, each makes the other more effective and efficient.

This idea is especially important for small and medium-sized businesses (SMBs). We found that SMBs are eager to deploy AI, with around 60% of small and medium-sized businesses surveyed as part of our “Future of SMB report - Harnessing the Potential of AI PCs” (email required) saying that they are ready to adopt AI, being optimistic about its potential. The same report, which also surveyed U.S.-based SMBs, also reported that only 38% have seen benefits so far. Again, part of the reason, data shows, is that much of today’s AI use is still individual. A 2026 global survey by SAS found that more than one-third of SMBs are still exploring AI in isolated, unconnected ways. When each employee experiments alone, the gains stay with that employee instead of reaching the team.

The good news is that SMBs are in a unique position to start implementing this change. As compared to large enterprises, SMBs work in small, close-knit teams that often can deploy new technology and workflows in a matter of weeks, while enterprises often need years to deploy a new system across thousands of employees and multiple levels of management. For a small team, AI collaboration could quickly translate into increased efficiency and revenue.

To get started on implementing AI as a collaboration tool, SMBs can begin by assessing their workflows and identifying one where AI can deliver visible and immediate value. First, map where work and projects pass between people, then determine where human judgment should remain and where AI can step in. As this is being deployed over time, measure the results to see what is successful and apply it to your other teams. Implementing this does not require a large-scale transformation; rather, use small projects and bite-sized increments to see what is successful.

The shift every small business should be thinking about right now is how their teams can better collaborate using AI. The real payoff of AI will be from reframing it from a personal productivity tool into a team capability tool. In the age where AI integration is already underway, it will be the businesses that add the collaboration element in their workflows, people and infrastructure that will be best positioned for success.
