Tanggal: 2026-11-06

My 2 cents (probably worth half that, or less):

The election was bad news for data centers, with dozens of direct local votes halting them and their biggest supporters now voted out. This is bad news for semiconductors used to power all the new centers. SOX was down 12.2% today, second largest drop ever, a substantial chunk of the overall index declines (big Tesla and SpaceX declines driven by the election also contributed). But I think this will be good for everyone else in the near future. Some of the trillions earmarked for data centers will now likely be freed up for other purposes, lowering rates and making money cheaper for companies generally. Bonds rallied today. Even the big AI companies will come out ahead as they can scale back their chip/data center purchases (they will still be high) which has been sucking up a big percentage of their large profits. Tl;dr, the trend that drove down semis (and the indexes) today will be a net positive for profits and the markets overall in the near and middle term. Of course, I could be wrong . . .
No need to worry…. It is nothing more than the illusion of choice… It’s all a show for the masses to serve as a feel good distraction. They WILL be built.
I would agree with that. The locations might change, the cost to build may go up, and projects may take a little longer because developers will need to provide better incentives and address more local concerns.

For example, OpenAI just announced that for future compute campuses it owns and operates, it will stop using NDAs with public officials except for narrow protections, pay the full cost of the electricity and infrastructure needed to serve its facilities, and give local communities more input into development plans. OpenAI also reiterated commitments to cover the full cost of electricity and infrastructure for its campuses and to help protect local utility ratepayers.

So I would not assume that opposition means the data centers will not be built. It may simply mean that companies have to work harder, pay more, and offer better terms to the communities where they want to build them.
