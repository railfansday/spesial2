Why Brandon Williams' return should boost the Warriors' struggling offense

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Why Brandon Williams' return should boost the Warriors' struggling offense originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SAN FRANCISCO – After five consecutive games in which the Warriors' offense too often slowed to a drip that kept them well below typical NBA scoring, help is on the way.

It's not coming from Stephen Curry. Not yet.

But Brandon Williams, the quickest man on the roster, is expected to return Wednesday night after missing six games with a mild left hamstring strain. His presence and ability to attack almost any defense immediately intensifies the offense.

"Ball pressure really hurt us, kind of put us on our heels," coach Steve Kerr said Monday, referring Golden State's recent 0-5 road trip. "You can't do that with Brandon. He'll just blow right by you. He'll be a big help, and him going out at the same time as Steph was a big blow and another reason for kind of this offensive disruption that we've had."

After averaging 91.8 points over the last six games, maybe now Golden State can remind scoreboards around the league that Stephen Curry's absence did not spur any intent to recreate the low-scoring offenses so prevalent in the 1990s.

(Example: Only three teams – the Bulls, Jazz and Suns – averaged more than 101 points in 1996-97).

This season, the Hornets (99.9 points per game) are the only team averaging fewer than 106 points. Eleven teams average more than 115 points.

Coming off a miserable road trip, the Warriors returned to Chase Center on Sunday and were able to grind out an 88-87 victory over Boston. The win was a tonic, but the offensive output, including 40-percent shooting from the field, including 31 percent from distance, is not a sustainable winning formula in today's NBA.

"No matter what the score is," Kerr said, "it's fun to see when a group that's struggling really summons a great effort collectively and fights and competes and plays through all the mistakes and tough calls and flurries everything else. I took a lot of joy in that game to see how our guys responded from what was a tough trip."

Yet the Warriors know they need to bump up their scoring. Even during their 88-point game against the Celtics, there were signs of improvement.

Charles Bassey made his third consecutive start, producing 13 points and nine rebounds; he will remain in the starting lineup. Inserting Bassey sends Draymond Green to power forward, where his proficient shooting from the 3-point corners – he made three triples in the first six minutes against Boston – are another source of scoring.

In addition, Kerr has indicated rookie forward Yaxel Lendeborg will spend more time posting up. As he should; he leads the league points per possession on post ups.

Williams, however, brings a missing dimension. He's Golden State's fourth-leading scorer, averaging 10.4 points per game. He provides a boost off the bench if Curry is healthy and is a crucial element with him on the sidelines.

He's the only player on the active roster who can consistently blast through traffic in the paint and find success.
