Bills vs. Chiefs, Week 12 preview: 5 questions with Arrowhead Pride

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Five questions is back this week, Rumblers! All eyes are on the Buffalo Bills and Kansas City Chiefs when they play, which means there's always room to chat about the greatest current NFL rivalry. That it's happening on Thanksgiving night is gravy.

If you've been a Bills fan long enough, you already know the mystique and magic that surrounds this head-to-head series between quarterbacks Josh Allen and Patrick Mahomes. But there's so much more to these games than that alone.

To get us ready for Bills-Chiefs I spoke with Maurice Elston, who writes in-depth analysis, film study breakdowns, and more for Arrowhead Pride. Here, we dive a bit further into who and what the Chiefs click in 2026.

Below, Maurice and I tackle everything from the biggest differences between this year's team and previous rosters, plus a look at Kansas City's greatest strength and most glaring weakness. We also dive into what wide receiver Josh Downs has brought to the team since joining the team at the trade deadline.

Then, Maurice explains what played into the Chiefs' pair of losses compared to their dominant wins, and he closes with a look at what has to go right if Kansas City is to win at Highmark Stadium.

Read on below for some fantastic insight from Maurice into a team you can't ever learn enough about!

1. Catch us up with this season's Chiefs, if you can. What's different about them as compared to previous seasons?

Maurice Elston: The biggest difference is that the Chiefs finally have an efficient running game—and a coaching staff willing to lean into it and stick with it. Kenneth Walker III has given Kansas City the dynamic running back it has lacked for much of the Patrick Mahomes era. Now, with Walker injured, fifth-round rookie Emmett Johnson has demonstrated that the success of the running game can continue without him.

That has made the Chiefs a much more balanced offense. They can operate from under center, use play action and stay committed to the run instead of placing the entire game on Mahomes' shoulders. Mahomes is still capable of taking over when necessary, but he no longer has to perform heroics every week for Kansas City to win. That balance has been the defining difference between this team and the Chiefs offenses of recent seasons.

2. At 8-2, what do you see as the Chiefs' greatest strength, and their most glaring weakness — and how might either be impacted vs. Bills?

Maurice Elston: Kansas City's greatest strength is its balance. The Chiefs can run the football effectively and build their play-action passing game from it, which is something they haven't consistently had during the Mahomes era. Even without Walker, Johnson has rushed for 206 yards over the last two games and shown that Kansas City doesn't have to abandon its offensive identity.

The most glaring weakness is the lack of continuity along the offensive line. The Chiefs still haven't played a game with the original starting five they envisioned entering training camp. Injuries and lineup changes have forced players into unfamiliar roles throughout the season.

That will be important against Buffalo, especially with right tackle Jaylon Moore ruled out. The Bills know Kansas City will want to establish Johnson and protect Mahomes with play action. If Buffalo can stop the run without committing extra defenders and then attack the Chiefs' backup tackles, that could disrupt the balance that has made Kansas City so successful.

3. What has WR Josh Downs brought to/done for the offense since KC traded for him?

Maurice Elston: If the Chiefs were missing anything at wide receiver, it was someone who could consistently create separation against man and press coverage. Downs has brought them a polished route runner who understands how to defeat leverage, set defenders up with his releases and find openings in the middle of a defense.

The production has already been encouraging. In his first two games with Kansas City, Downs has eight receptions for 112 yards and a touchdown, with six of those catches resulting in first downs. That is significant production considering how little time he has had to learn Andy Reid's offense.

His presence also helps the other receivers because defenses now have another player they must respect opposite Travis Kelce. The more comfortable Downs becomes with the playbook, the more Kansas City can expand his role. He gives the Chiefs a separation skill set that has largely been missing since Tyreek Hill left Kansas City.

4. KC's two losses came against playoff/SB teams in the Broncos and Seahawks. What's the real story behind those defeats — especially only putting up a single field goal against Seattle — as compared to the way they've played in eight wins?

Maurice Elston: The two losses were different. Kansas City moved the ball and scored 24 points against Denver, but pressure and offensive-line breakdowns hurt the Chiefs in critical situations. Seattle was a complete offensive collapse.

The Seahawks committed to stopping Walker, challenged Kansas City's receivers in man coverage and generated pressure without having to compromise their coverage. Kansas City finished with only 213 yards, while Mahomes completed just six of 13 passes for 44 yards and an interception when pressured. When the receivers couldn't separate and the offensive line couldn't hold up in obvious passing situations, Mahomes became unsettled and the entire offense lost its rhythm.

Since those losses, Downs has given the Chiefs another answer against man coverage, Kahlil Benson has gained valuable experience at left tackle and Mahomes has looked increasingly comfortable in the pocket. Those are positive developments, but the real test begins now. Kansas City's upcoming stretch against contending teams will show whether those problems have truly been corrected or were merely hidden during the eight victories.

5. What do you see as the biggest key to success for the Chiefs, if they accomplish their main goal Thanksgiving night?

Maurice Elston: As dynamic as James Cook has been, the biggest key is containing Josh Allen and eliminating the explosive plays he creates outside the structure of Buffalo's offense.

Many of Allen's most damaging plays happen when a defense appears to have him stopped. A pass rusher loses containment, Allen escapes the pocket and suddenly turns a potential sack into a long run or a completion downfield. Kansas City's defensive line must maintain disciplined rush lanes instead of becoming so focused on sacks that it gives Allen an escape route.

When the Chiefs have an opportunity to bring him down, they must finish the play. Keeping Allen in the pocket, forcing him to operate on schedule and preventing those backbreaking second-reaction plays will be the biggest key to Kansas City leaving Buffalo with a Thanksgiving night victory.
