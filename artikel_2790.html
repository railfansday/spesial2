Closer, but not enough for Rebels to avoid loss in Players Era 8

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

LAS VEGAS — Well, at least it was more competitive.Pretty, it wasn't.But whether you get blown out by 25 points or fall by 15, a loss is a loss. And for UNLV, it was another frustrating night at Michelob Ultra Arena in the Players Era 8 basketball event.Yes, the Runnin' Rebels weren't embarrassed by Auburn. But despite their own shortcomings the Tigers managed to do enough things right to win 86-71 and send Josh Pastner's team to its second straight loss after they started the season 3-0.UNLV will try to avoid being swept in the Players Era for a second consecutive year when it faces Rutgers at 5 p.m. Thursday at MUA. Last year, the Scarlet Knights defeated the Rebels 80-65.In the championship game, Houston, which knocked off No. 1 Florida 86-76, will face Kansas, which rallied to get by West Virginia 86-82 to set up an all-Big 12 title matchup."Our guys fought and competed," Pastner said. "But we don't usually see this kind of size in the Mountain West. It's a different situation in the Players Era."The Tigers, who are big up front, including 7-0, 280-pound Narcisse Ngoy, who was drafted by the Atlanta Hawks in the second round of last year's NBA Draft after playing professionally in France, gave UNLV trouble inside. Bukky Oboye, who is 7-1 and played at Santa Clara before transferring, really caused the foul-plagued Rebels problems, particularly in the second half as he went to town down low and would finish with 14 points.So while Kansas' speed did UNLV in Tuesday, Auburn's size was their undoing Wednesday. Even a lousy night at the free-throw line couldn't deter the Tigers. They were a miserable 7 of 16 from the stripe, including an 0-for-4 performance from Ngoy, which on most nights would ultimately lead to their undoing.Not this night, however. Not when their opponent shot the ball poorly from anywhere on the court.The Rebels were 39 percent from the floor and made just 5 of 17 3-point attempts. Somehow, they outrebounded Auburn 39-38.Still, it was another double-digit loss. And what has to hurt for UNLV was it was well within striking range in the second half, having pulled within three, 47-44, with plenty of time to come all the way back.Instead, Auburn went inside to Oboye, got some outside shooting from Adam Olsen to balance things out as the Vancouver-area native had five made 3-pointers and would finish with 15 points. It was a nice complement to guard Tahaad Pettiford, who despite foul trouble led Auburn with 22 points."We had a chance in the second half," Pastner said, referring to getting within three. "They finished with some lobs, we had a couple of misses, turnovers and they were able to extend it again. But credit to Auburn, their bigs did a good job."Tyrin Jones and Cam Miles led UNLV with 16 points apiece but it wasn't near enough. Sebastian Mack struggled, as the team's leading scorer managed to make just 3 of 12 shots and finished with nine points in 33 minutes.Miles said it's on him to make sure his team sustains its offense when it falls behind."As the point guard, I need to get my guys the ball in the right spots," he said. "I need to make the right plays."Perhaps. But missing open looks and bricking free throws along with surrendering layups and dunks will get you beat every time.So once again, UNLV will look to regroup in less than 24 hours, try and find its game over 40 minutes against a Rutgers team whose coach, Steve Pikiell, is battling cancer and is on a leave of absence from the program, and which took Notre Dame to overtime before falling 110-100 Wednesday."It's not a problem of competing," Pastner said. "We had chances (vs. Auburn). We just didn't capitalize on them."
