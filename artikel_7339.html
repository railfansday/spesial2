A Playbook for Marketers on Agentic Commerce

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

This post was created in partnership with Incubeta

As consumers integrate AI into their everyday lives, brands are looking for ways to not just be mentioned by platforms like ChatGPT and Gemini, but be recommended.

During an ADWEEK House Commerce Summit fireside chat co-hosted with Incubeta, Lauren Johnson, deputy editor of retail media and commerce at ADWEEK, sat down with Amy Crowther, CEO, Americas, at Incubeta, to discuss how AI is changing marketing and how brands can capitalize on agentic commerce.

Crowther began by sharing best practices for brands that want to be ranked prominently by AI agents. She stressed the importance of having a consistent language across every digital touchpoint, like a brand’s website, a YouTube channel, or even an FAQ page.

“It’s not the sexiest project,” Crowther said, “but it entails going in and overhauling that content, looking at where there are gaps, like an old FAQ page. AI will pick up everything that exists. And so that audit stage to begin with is really critical.”

Similarly, consistent brand storytelling and messaging across content is key. Crowther pointed out that having a large volume of content that can connect with different consumer demographics feeds the LLMs and generative AI models. She cited YouTube as an important source.

“YouTube is a big one because people forget about the advertising side of the channel,” Crowther added, sharing that some brands push their video content out on YouTube, but once they’re live on the channel, the videos become an afterthought. “

“YouTube is the predominant place that feeds the engine,” she explained. “So that’s your first place to start.”

Crowther went on to list social media sites like Instagram and Pinterest as the next areas to focus on for brand consistency when doing an AI audit.

Crowther also works with creatives and has seen the power of AI when designing campaigns. She shared an experience with a travel luggage brand that debated whether campaigns should feature just the product or include people. According to Crowther, this discussion went on for years.

“We ran the creative through our intelligence agents, and the minute a face comes onto the screen, all the focus goes off the product,” Crowther recalled. “We tied that back to the brand’s media campaigns over the last two years, and there was a downward trajectory whenever that happened. Product wins—don’t put a face in the ad.”

Crowther emphasized the value of the “feedback loop” AI creates within creative workflows, allowing marketers to adjust campaigns while they’re running. She said this changes the traditional approach to campaign planning and execution.

“If I can leave one thing with you, it’s to get away from this idea of ‘we build the plan, then we execute, and then we ask, did it work?’ I challenge that completely,” Crowther shared.

She encouraged marketers instead to “build the engine, get the momentum going, and then iterate. That has to be the new way—with media, creative data, and your own infrastructure being connected; it’s the only way to unlock incrementality.”
