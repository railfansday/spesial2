‘Rosemary’s Baby’ Meets ‘The Fly’ in a New Body Horror Thriller Officially Out Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

Nora Pacetti figures her daughter is teething. It would explain the screaming, and by bath time, Olivia seems happy enough. There’s still the matter of what looks like a splinter in her arm, though Nora can’t think where a baby would have picked one up. Her mother, Giulia, gets a good look while she’s toweling off her granddaughter and asks Nora to run downstairs for her reading glasses. Of all the things a grandmother might find on a changing table, an incoming feather takes some explaining.

Giulia is usually the person Nora can count on to have whatever a child might need. She carries an ice pack in her purse in case somebody else’s kid takes a fall, and she also spends enough time watching conspiracy videos on YouTube that Nora would like a second opinion on the feather. Her husband is happy to oblige. He wants to take Olivia to a specialist, and under almost any other circumstances, Nora would probably be the one making the appointment. This time, she’s afraid of what might happen once somebody else gets a look at her daughter.

Christina Henry’s Pinfeathers is out now from Berkley. Released November 17, the 320-page thriller is the 20th book from the author of The Place Where They Buried Your Heart. The book has already drawn comparisons to both Rosemary’s Baby and David Cronenberg’s The Fly, with the feathers accounting for the Cronenberg half. The Rosemary’s Baby bit comes into play when people outside the family begin asking questions about Olivia, and Nora has to figure out who told them enough to ask.

Four franchises, four completely different ways of getting underneath your skin. This is the one built out of your particular fear, why it works so well on you, and what it would leave behind.

Your nightmare does not wait outside the door. It waits for you to close your eyes, which you are going to have to do eventually, because nobody has ever won that particular argument.

Your nightmare is not really the doll. It is the face of the person you tell, and the way they decide, very kindly, that you have imagined all of it.

Your nightmare has your number, knows your name, and has almost certainly been inside your house before. The worst part is that you already know them.

Your nightmare is not really about you. It is about the people asleep down the hall, and the feeling that whatever is here is not interested in leaving.

Henry told the Chicago Sun-Times that the image of a baby sprouting feathers came to her before she knew the story. She begins her books in longhand and works out what those early images mean as she writes. In this case, that process brought her to a new mother in a condo on Chicago’s North Side. Henry described the fear behind Nora’s story as “the parental anxiety around illness, difficulty, things that you don’t expect when you become a parent.”

Readers can get a look at the scene that started it all in an excerpt published by The Nerd Daily, which shows the three generations of Pacetti women in the middle of an otherwise ordinary visit. Giulia shows up with food, kisses her daughter hello, and nudges her out of the way so she can have a turn with the baby. By the time she notices Olivia’s arm, Henry has already established who usually takes charge in this family.

Pinfeathers is available now in hardcover, ebook, and audiobook.
