Meanwhile in the other game in A4, it’s a half-time score of Denmark 2-1 Norway. Pierre-Emile Højbjerg and Rasmus Højlund put the hosts two up in short order in Copenhagen, but Erling Haaland scored his 67th goal for Norway on his 61st appearance to reduce the arrears. As things stand, this group will end Portugal, Denmark, Norway, Wales.

… nothing occurs. Turns out we did have enough added time for a goal or two, though. Both teams came so close. This is great.

45 min +3: … and now Wilson comes again, cutting in from the right and curling towards the bottom left. Costa is forced to turn around for a corner. From which …

45 min +2: So here’s the first half in microcosm. Rodon is skinned by Dalot down the left. Ramos meets the cross with a diving header that Davies bravely blocks with his trouser arrangement. The ball breaks to Wilson in the centre circle. Wilson spots Costa out of his box and goes for goal. His long-range rake beats Costa, but just clears the crossbar and lands on the top of the net! So close to an all-timer!

45 min +1: Portugal pass it around patiently and professionally, the spoilsports.

45 min: There will be two additional minutes. Plenty of time for another goal each.

Trincão dribbles purposefully down the left and crosses long. The ball sails over Dasilva, and Neto can’t miss when the ball drops to him, a couple of yards out. Bing bong.

42 min: Portugal should have gone 2-1 up; instead they’re now 2-1 down. This is fun.

Neto dribbles in from the right. He tees up Neves on the penalty spot. Neves shoots. Blocked. Ward claims. Wales counter immediately, and Thomas is sent racing into space down the inside-left channel. He enters the box, steps across the line of Neto so the Portugal man can’t make a tackle or intercept, opens his body, and steers into the bottom right! Bing bong!

39 min: Fernandes breaks into the box down the left and whips a low cross through the Wales six-yard box. Ramos hasn’t made the run, so Fernandes has his say. To be fair to Ramos, he was in danger of being caught offside in the build-up, so he’s got an excuse for having checked his forward movement.

37 min: Neto scuttles in from the right wing, reaches the edge of the Wales box, and rolls a weak curler into the arms of Ward. It’s all Portugal in terms of possession, but the hosts are holding their shape.

35 min: So having made the point about the Atari-game pace, the intensity drops markedly.

33 min: Davies nicks the ball off Fernandes, then immediately wins another 50-50 with Neves, who catches him late on the top of his boot. Neves should be booked, but the referee plays it cool. Don’t do it now and pay later.

31 min: … and now Thomas barrels down the left. He could shape a curler around Costa, but cuts back to tee up Wilson instead. A selfless act, but Wilson smashes his shot straight at Costa, who parries well. Wild end-to-end antics. The ball’s like a pixel in a game of Pong. Someone once wrote a song about that …

29 min: Brooks embarks on a power romp down the right. He’s got options, but takes a heavy touch. Portugal counter, Trincão advancing down the left. Trincão looks for Ramos in the middle, but Davies intercepts.

27 min: There’s another game going on in Group A4. “Hei!” begins Wil Lee-Wright. “While there might not be much to play for in the Wales-Portugal match, the Denmark Norway game is a match up for the ages. Norway need to win to leapfrog their neighbours, but they’ve not won a competitive game in the Danish capital in 100 years and despite a strong start, they’ve somehow conspired to find themselves 2-0 down after just 15 mins. Wil, from a despondent sports bar in Gjøvik, Norway.”

25 min: Roberts takes a quick throw from inside his own half to release James on the scamper down the right. He’s got team-mates in the middle, but his cross doesn’t beat the first man, so a corner will have to do. And from that, hit long from the right, Rodon slaps a poor header wide left from six yards. That’s another big chance spurned. In a parallel universe, it’s now 2-2.

23 min: That should have been 1-2, and though the flag went up for offside, I’m not sure Trincão actually was. VAR might have reversed that decision had Trincão scored, as he surely should have. Let’s leave it be, for Trincão’s sake, there’s nothing to be gained from it.

21 min: Neto glides in from the right, and curls a delicious cross towards Trincão, coming from the left to meet the ball six yards out. Trincão has to score, but leans back and volleys a quickly rising shot off the top of the crossbar and into the stand. What a miss!

19 min: Wales snaffle the ball and launch a fast counter. Wilson is close to barging free down the middle, only for Dalot to step in, just in time.

17 min: Cancelo is sent romping into acres down the right. His low cross isn’t all that, and Sheehan clears to great cheers. Sheehan now in moral credit.

16 min: Cancelo loops a long pass down the inside-right channel and nearly releases Neto. Ward comes to the edge of his box to claim. There are a few more goals in this.

14 min: VAR has a long check for an offside in the build-up … but all’s fine. That’s some reaction from Wales, and especially Sheehan, who has bounced back from his mistake with involvement in a goal.

Oh yes! Yes they can! Sheehan sends Wilson free down the left. Wilson shoots. Costa parries. The ball pings back to Colwill, who rolls a pass towards Brooks on the penalty spot. Brooks sweeps into the bottom right, and that’s the perfect response!

12 min: What a way to mark your 99th appearance. Can Wales respond?

Er, scrub that. Sheehan, playing out from the back, is robbed by João Neves, who shuttles the ball infield for Fernandes, on the edge of the D. Fernandes whips a shot towards the top-left. Ward can’t reach it. What a finish, though from the Welsh point of view, that’s a self-inflicted wound.

10 min: Nothing much going on at the moment.

8 min: Portugal stay patient, then suddenly go agricultural. A big hoof down the middle nearly releases Neto. Last man Dasilva does well to stand his ground and bat the ball away. “Some people make news even when they are not there - maybe more when they are not there,” begins krishnamoorthy v. “I want Portugal to win a few games resoundingly so that the team can discard the millstone and move on.”

6 min: Now it’s Ward’s turn to clank a poor pass straight to an opponent. Neto tries to release Cancelo into the Wales box with a wedge down the right, but gets it all wrong. Ward claims and breathes out again.

4 min: António Silva strides out of defence and scuffs a strange pass straight to James. He’s fortunate that James doesn’t seem to know what to do with the ball, even though he’s got a bit of time down the inside-right channel and Wilson in the middle. The ball’s handed straight back.

2 min: The atmosphere in the Cardiff City Stadium is more Fun than Excitement right now. Not a whole lot going on, and not that much riding on it either.

Wales get the ball rolling. James hares after a long pass down the left but can’t get past Dalot. A positive start, though.

The teams are out! Wales in red, Portugal in second-choice white and green. Bruno Fernandes stands in line for the Portuguese national anthem for the 99th time. Once the day is done, he’ll be just the 135 caps shy of Cristiano Ronaldo’s record. We’ll be off in a minute!

Bellamy’s on the Beeb now, where he’s heaping praise on Portugal. “A good team … really good … in that team there’s seven European Cups between them … so they’re strong … talent … if you look at Vitinha and Neves, they’re probably my two favourite players at this present moment … we have to put the shackles on them … the best form of defence is to attack.”

Craig Bellamy speaks to S4C. “[Portugal] can score four or five as well [as Denmark] …on the day they were quite unlucky … the opportunities they created … they didn’t convert them … we’re aware of their threat … at times they will be open as well … even when we’re suffering, there’s always an opportunity to exploit the space … no doubt we can impose our game … now we want the results … tonight that’s the aim … can we make them suffer? … it’s a tough test … but an exciting one as well.”

Portugal make four changes after that 4-1 defeat at home to Denmark. Rúben Dias is suspended so António Silva comes into the defence. João Neves, João Cancelo and Francisco Trincão step up; João Félix, Alberto Costa and Rúben Neves sit down. The Club Brugge winger Carlos Forbs is also on the bench, sporting Portugal’s fancy new post-Cristiano continental-style No 7.

Wales make six changes to their starting line-up following the 3-1 defeat in Norway. Two are enforced: Ethan Ampadu is suspended while Neco Williams is injured. In come Danny Ward, David Brooks, Connor Roberts, Rubin Colwill, Sorba Thomas and Josh Sheehan. Karl Darlow, Liam Cullen, Lewis Koumas and Brennan Johnson drop to the bench.

Wales: Ward, Roberts, Rodon, B Davies, Dasilva, Wilson, Sheehan, Brooks, D James, R Colwill, Thomas.

Portugal: Costa, Dalot, Silva, Veiga, Cancelo, Vitinha, Fernandes, Neto, Trincao, Ramos, Neves.

Can Wales bounce back after their spirited defeat in Norway? Will Portugal prove a point after their humbling by Denmark, the first misstep in the post-Him world? Kick-off is at 7.45pm. It’s on!
