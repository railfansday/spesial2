Three immediate Crystal Palace changes Thomas Frank should make including new formation

Thomas Frank has been announced as Crystal Palace's next manager.

The former Brentford and Tottenham boss has come into Selhurst Park with plenty to do, and much to turn around.

The Dane is taking over the Eagles when they are sitting at the bottom of the Premier League, with the goal undoubtedly to climb out of those relegation positions.

As well as continuing with their journey into the latter stages of the Europa League.

Frank will have plenty of decisions to make, with the potential for new chances to emerge for the fringe players in Palace's squad,

With Frank likely to make plenty of changes, these three need to be first on his list of priorities.

Frank is a manager who has used three-at-the-back in the past, and it is one of the common formations he uses.

However, he is also fond of using the 4-2-3-1 as well.

At Brentford and Tottenham, he often swapped between the two, but at Crystal Palace, their lack of central defensive options makes it tougher to sustain it.

In the squad, there are only four fit natural centre-backs, with Jaydee Canvot, Chris Richards, Axel Disasi and Honest Ahanor.

The latter also barely featured this season, with three appearances.

With three centre-backs needed each game, and European football this season, it is not ideal for 75 per cent of your options to be used up each matchday.

Making a change to a four-man defence makes a lot of sense.

Speaking of Crystal Palace's defence, with one centre-back dropping out, Frank will have a decision to make.

And it should be Disasi dropping out of the team.

As per FotMob, he has been on the poorer side in winning duels, coming in at the 32nd percentile with 54.1 per cent for total duels won.

While for aerial duels, he comes in the 30th percentile, with just a 53.8 per cent win rate.

A partnership of Richards and Canvot may ultimately be the best option for the Eagles, with the American having a 63.2 per cent success rate in duels, and the Frenchman with 60 per cent.

Daichi Kamada has been having a good season for Crystal Palace, even if the Eagles have not.

The Japanese midfielder has one goal and three assists in 10 Premier League games, and his influence has been clear.

Kamada may be better played centrally, rather than in those wide number 10 positions he has often been deployed in.

Kamada ranks in the 93rd percentile for line-breaking passes with 84 this season, showing he can consistently play passes that break the opponent's structure.

And doing so is much easier from a central role.
