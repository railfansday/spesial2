The 10 longest bridges in the world

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Seven of the world's 10 longest bridges stand in China, and a Louisiana causeway is the lone entry outside Asia.

Most drivers cross a bridge without a second thought. Road bridges fold into the daily landscape, lifting traffic dozens of meters above rivers and valleys. A handful of engineering projects pierce that indifference with their scale and with the technical demands their builders had to meet. Height offers one way to stop a viewer. France's Millau Viaduct climbs to 341 meters. In China, the Beipanjiang bridge hangs 565 meters above the Beipan River. Each turns the plain act of getting from one bank to the other into a spectacle.

Length can impress as much as altitude, and it tests a structure in a different way. A tall bridge commands one point on the map. A long one claims a whole stretch of it, carrying its deck over kilometer after kilometer. Italy supplies a useful yardstick. The country's longest viaduct, at Coltano on the A12 motorway, runs 9.6 kilometers. The runner-up, an A19 motorway viaduct over the Fichera stream, covers seven kilometers, 2.6 fewer than Coltano. Both distances give readers a baseline for judging the reach of the crossings that follow.

We Build Value, Webuild's digital magazine, ranked the world's 10 longest bridges in an article dated May 17, 2021. The magazine ordered the list on a single measure, how far each bridge extends from end to end, and gave no other criteria. Asia dominates the field. Nine of the 10 stand on that continent, seven of them in China, with one each in Taiwan and Thailand. The 10th sits in the U.S. Europe, home to the Millau Viaduct and Italy's top two, places no entry among the 10. The group serves several modes of travel, from high-speed rail lines to roads to an urban subway. The list captures the picture as of spring 2021, and new construction can reorder such a lineup. Each entry below draws on the magazine's measurements, locations, and completion dates.

The Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge runs 164,800 meters, a distance no other bridge on Earth matches. Its path cuts through Jiangsu province in China, linking Shanghai and Nanjing. High-speed trains on the Beijing-Shanghai line travel along its deck. The record holder doubles as working rail infrastructure. It occupies part of the Yangtze River delta. It stretches more than four times as far as the Wuhan Metro Bridge, the shortest entry among the world's 10 longest.

Canals, rivers, lakes, and rice fields crowd the land beneath the route, and builders needed every one of those meters to clear them. Soft ground near the Yangtze River created a second engineering problem. Designers answered with a viaduct broken into smaller segments, with arches and pillars bearing each one to prevent collapse. The result resembles a chain of modest structures joined end to end, each sized for ground too weak to carry a single massive one.

Nine kilometers of the crossing pass above the waters of Yangcheng lake. The lake section makes up about 5.5% of the total, leaving roughly 94.5% over farmland and smaller waterways. Piers carry the deck across the lake. Some portions rise high enough for naval vessels to slip beneath. Flexible diagonal cables lend extra support, making the bridge cable-stayed as well. Over Yangcheng lake, the bridge had to serve trains above and shipping below.

Construction began in 2006 and wrapped up in 2010, a four-year effort that employed more than 10,000 workers. The project cost $8.5 billion in total. Per meter, the price comes to about $51,600. Divide the length by the workforce and each person accounts for less than 17 meters of bridge. Crews averaged about 41 kilometers of finished bridge a year over that period. At that rate, the crews added the equivalent of Italy's Coltano viaduct roughly every three months.

Taiwan's Changhua-Koahsiung Viaduct stretches 157,317 meters, enough to make it the second-longest bridge in the world and leave it 7,483 meters behind China's Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge. Across two structures that each exceed 150 kilometers, a gap of about seven kilometers counts as a narrow margin. The viaduct covers more than 95% of the Chinese leader's distance. China's Cangde Grand Bridge, in third, sits 41,417 meters behind the Taiwanese structure, more than five times the gap at the top. The two front-runners form a class of their own, well clear of the remaining eight.

Of the other two entries outside China, neither comes within 100 kilometers of the viaduct. Thailand's Bang Na Expressway, the closer of the pair, trails it by 103,317 meters. The Taiwan crossing extends more than 16 times as far as Italy's Coltano viaduct, the longest in that country.

High-speed trains run over the viaduct, a key segment of the Taiwan High-Speed Rail line. Vertical bearings and transverse shear keys support the box girder spans along that railway. Rail service ties the viaduct to its neighbors on the list. Danyang-Kunshan and Cangde, the entries on either side of it, both carry China's Beijing-Shanghai high-speed line. The world's three longest bridges all serve rail traffic.

Builders completed the viaduct in 2007, three years before crews finished the Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge in 2010. Weinan Weihe, done in 2008, arrived later but runs 77,585 meters shorter. The Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge, completed a decade after the viaduct in 2017, measures 101,600 meters less. From 2007 until 2010, the viaduct stood as the longest finished structure among these 10.

The geography of the area between Baguazhang and Zuoying forced the viaduct's builders to meet strict anti-seismic criteria. Taiwan's builders applied the standard across a structure reaching beyond 150 kilometers. The viaduct shows that a bridge held to demanding earthquake standards can approach the world record in length.

The Cangde Grand Bridge lifts 115,900 meters of high-speed track off the ground on 3,092 pillars, a reach that earns it third place among the world's longest bridges. Each support carries an average of about 37 meters of deck. Italy's Coltano viaduct would fit inside the Chinese structure more than 12 times.

The pillar count shows how a crossing of this scale takes shape. Workers set more than 3,000 supports into the soil along the route, each one a separate point where the deck meets the earth. The bridge owes its extent to repetition, with one modest span following another across the full distance.

The viaduct belongs to the Beijing-Shanghai high-speed line, the same corridor that carries the Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge. Crews finished both structures in 2010. Within a single year, one rail route gained two of the world's three biggest bridges. The line holds an outsized share of the planet's elevated rail. Work on the Tianjin Grand Bridge ended in 2010 too. Every bridge in the top four except Taiwan's Changhua-Koahsiung Viaduct, which came earlier, reached completion that year.

Cangde's standing hinges on an uneven pair of gaps. It falls 48,900 meters short of the Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge and 41,417 meters short of the Changhua-Koahsiung Viaduct. Below it, the Tianjin Grand Bridge trails by 2,200 meters. The cushion beneath Cangde amounts to less than one-twentieth of the deficit separating it from first place.

In distance terms, Cangde has more in common with the next Chinese viaduct down the list than with the two giants above it. The top pair form a class of their own, and Cangde heads the tier that follows.

The Tianjin Grand Bridge stretches 113,700 meters between Langfang and Qingxian in China, a distance that secures fourth place among the world's longest bridges. A mere 2,200 meters separate it from the Cangde Grand Bridge, the crossing one spot higher. The Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge, at the head of the list, outstrips it by 51,100 meters. Below the Tianjin span, the gap yawns wider. The bridge closes out a group of four giants that pulls clear of the remaining six.

The Chinese rail bridge runs more than twice the length of Thailand's Bang Na Expressway, which held the global record until 2004. It measures close to three times the length of the first of the two parallel bridges that make up Louisiana's Lake Pontchartrain Causeway. It covers more than double the length of the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge, which opened in 2018. Work on the Tianjin Grand Bridge started two years after the Bang Na Expressway lost its crown. Ten years after the Bang Na Expressway's 2000 completion, China had built a railway crossing reaching beyond 113 kilometers.

The Tianjin Grand Bridge serves China's railway network. SlashGear reports that the bridge carries the Beijing-Shanghai High-Speed Railway on an elevated structure. Box girders weighing several hundred tons apiece went into the build, according to SlashGear. The raised track crosses dry ground in some sections and bodies of water in others.

Construction ran four years, from 2006 to 2010, and the bridge's formal inauguration followed in 2011. The Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge, the top entry of all, kept the identical timeline, breaking ground in 2006 and wrapping up in 2010. Builders finished the Cangde Grand Bridge in the same year. Crews finished the Beijing Grand Bridge, farther down the list, in 2010 and opened it the following year, matching the Tianjin sequence. China completed three of the four bridges at the top of this list in a single year, 2010, the Tianjin Grand Bridge among them.

The Weinan Weihe Grand Bridge runs 79,732 meters, trailing the fourth-place Tianjin Grand Bridge by 33,968 meters. One other step in the list opens a wider gulf, the 41,417-meter drop between the second and third entries. The four structures above Weinan Weihe form a separate tier, each longer than 110 kilometers. The Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge covers more than twice its distance.

Weinan Weihe is the list's sole entry on China's Zhengzhou-Xi'an High-Speed Railway. Danyang-Kunshan, Cangde, and the Beijing Grand Bridge all serve the Beijing-Shanghai high-speed line. Crews finished the bridge in 2008, two years ahead of the three longer mainland viaducts, each of which reached completion in 2010. Taiwan's Changhua-Koahsiung Viaduct, done in 2007, stands as the lone crossing among the four longer ones to beat it to the finish. The Beijing Grand Bridge shares the 2010 date, leaving Weinan Weihe the earliest completed of the five mainland Chinese rail viaducts on the list. The Zhengzhou-Xi'an route had its long crossing in place while work on the Beijing-Shanghai corridor's three viaducts continued.

The viaduct threads water and dry land alike. Trains on the line clear the Wei River and many of the streams that feed it. Roads and farmland lie beneath the deck. A single structure lifts the railway over each of those obstacles.

Weinan Weihe outruns Italy's longest viaduct, at Coltano, more than eight times over. The 10th-place Wuhan Metro Bridge, the list's shortest entry, covers less than half the ground Weinan Weihe spans.

The sixth-place Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge falls 24,015 meters short of Weinan Weihe, a gap 9,953 meters narrower than the one separating Weinan Weihe from Tianjin. In length, the fifth-place viaduct sits nearer the bridge below it than the one above. Weinan Weihe belongs at the head of the list's lower six, the longest of the crossings whose reach runs to double-digit kilometers.

The Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge runs 55,717 meters across the Lingdingyang canal, connecting Hong Kong, Zhuhai, and Macao in the Pearl River delta. Its outline echoes the dragon of Chinese tradition. The crossing edges past Thailand's Bang Na Expressway, the next entry down, by 1,717 meters. Vehicles move along a divided deck with three lanes in each direction, according to ScienceDirect. The bridge's reach extends beyond the three cities in its name. The link forms a vital piece of the main highway system joining the large urban areas around Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Hong Kong, Macao, and Zhuhai.

Close to seven kilometers of the route dips underwater as a tunnel, about one-eighth of the total length, leaving more than 48 kilometers outside it. The tunnel follows an immersed design and passes beneath the Pearl River Estuary's primary shipping channels near Hong Kong International Airport. Artificial islands tie this submerged section to the bridge segments. Cable-stayed spans lift the roadway over other navigation channels so sea-going vessels can pass. Between the tunnel and those raised spans, the layout keeps the estuary's principal shipping routes clear for ocean traffic.

Builders poured 400,000 metric tons of steel into the project, about seven tons for each meter it covers. Work lasted eight years and finished in 2017, a year before the bridge opened in 2018. No other entry among the 10 opened later. An unofficial figure puts the full bill at about $7 billion. Spread across the whole distance, the sum comes to about $126 million per kilometer.

Harsh weather and a conservation zone surround the islands and the tunnel. Those islands and the tunnel face the Ling Ding Channel, where typhoons are frequent and convection and monsoon conditions turn severe. The Chinese white dolphin's core protected area lies nearby. The parts that keep ships moving sit where storm exposure and dolphin habitat meet.

Thailand's Bang Na Expressway stretches 54 kilometers, and apart from a single river crossing, its whole course rides above dry ground. The Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge outruns it by about 1.7 kilometers. So slim a gap separates a sea link from a route that keeps to the land, two opposite engineering problems that produced near-twin lengths.

One river marks the only point where the expressway clears water. Everywhere else, its deck passes over solid earth from end to end. Its reach comes from covering terrain, a trait that distinguishes it from entries earning their places over lakes, canals, or open sea. Builders of those bridges answered to the water in their path. Bang Na shows that dry land alone can call for a bridge on a record-setting scale.

Louis Berger drew up the design in 1994, and crews finished the work in 2000. Six years separated the first plans from the completed structure. Berger is also the only designer the ranking names for any of the 10 bridges. The finish date sets Bang Na among the elders of the group. Only the Lake Pontchartrain Causeway in Louisiana predates it, which makes the Thai expressway the oldest Asian bridge on the list.

Bang Na held the title of world's longest bridge from 2000 until 2004, a four-year reign. Six structures surpass it, and each reached completion between 2007 and 2017, years after that reign ended. Their arrival pushed the former titleholder down to seventh. The expressway lost ground without losing a meter. The Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge, today's record holder, runs about three times as far, a margin that turns the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge's edge into a rounding error. Bang Na outreaches the Beijing Grand Bridge, the next entry, by more than 5.8 kilometers. A structure finished before eight of the other nine has kept its footing in the top seven through a decade of longer construction.

The Beijing Grand Bridge carries the Beijing-Shanghai high-speed line along a 48,153-meter railway viaduct, joining the Danyang-Kunshan, Cangde, and Tianjin grand bridges as the fourth entry from that route among the world's 10 longest.

Its route partners dwarf it. The Cangde Grand Bridge exceeds it by 67,747 meters, and the Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge runs about 3.4 times its length. Yet the junior member of the quartet would swallow Italy's record-holding Coltano viaduct about five times over.

The Beijing viaduct's nearest rivals come from outside the rail corridor. Thailand's Bang Na Expressway adds 5,847 meters to its span, and the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge adds 7,564. Those two crossings sit within a few kilometers of the Beijing viaduct, a tight cluster beside the 65,547-meter gulf that separates it from Tianjin, its closest partner on the line.

The Beijing Grand Bridge holds a clear margin over the two entries below it. It surpasses the Wuhan Metro Bridge by 10,365 meters and the longer of the Lake Pontchartrain Causeway's two parallel spans by 9,738 meters. Its lead over the Louisiana causeway outstrips its deficit to Bang Na, which places the viaduct closer to the crossings above it than to those beneath.

Combined, the Danyang-Kunshan, Cangde, Tianjin, and Beijing viaducts lift 442,553 meters of track off the ground, more than 2.8 times the reach of Taiwan's Changhua-Koahsiung Viaduct. The Beijing span supplies the smallest slice of that total, and it completes the set of four bridges that gives the Beijing-Shanghai high-speed corridor four places on a list of 10.

Crews finished the Beijing Grand Bridge in 2010, and its inauguration followed in 2011. The Danyang-Kunshan, Cangde, and Tianjin grand bridges reached completion in 2010 as well, and Tianjin opened to service in 2011 alongside the Beijing viaduct. One year delivered four of the world's 10 longest bridges, more than any other completion date on the list. The four 2010 finishers match the corridor's four entries exactly, tying the list's most productive year to a single Chinese railway.

The Lake Pontchartrain Causeway carries traffic 38,400 meters over a Louisiana lake on its original span, a reach that holds ninth place and edges the 10th-place entry by 612 meters.

The causeway predates every other structure on the list by decades. Crews finished its first span in 1956, 44 years before Thailand's Bang Na Expressway, the next-oldest entry, reached completion in 2000. Builders completed the remaining eight between 2004 and 2017, most of them for high-speed rail lines. A 1950s road link keeps pace with projects finished nearly half a century or more after it.

Mandeville and Metairie sit at opposite ends of the crossing. Between those two communities, the causeway covers Lake Pontchartrain in its entirety. The Bang Na Expressway rides over dry ground everywhere except a single river. The causeway inverts that pattern, with water beneath the deck from one shore to the other.

Two parallel bridges make up the causeway. A second span joined the first in 1969, 13 years after the original, and measures 15 meters longer at 38,415 meters. The near match shows the newer structure tracing the same path across the lake. The 15-meter bonus still leaves the 1969 bridge 9,738 meters behind the eighth-place Beijing Grand Bridge.

Vehicles split by direction across the pair. Southbound drivers heading for Metairie use the 1969 span, which leaves northbound travel toward Mandeville on the 1956 structure. The arrangement turns two independent bridges into one divided route.

A pivoting section on the northbound bridge turns to let large boats pass. The movable piece lets the causeway serve motorists on its deck and vessels on the lake below. Boats big enough to need that opening show Lake Pontchartrain working as a navigable waterway, and the causeway has to accommodate both.

Combined, the two spans lay 76,815 meters of roadway across Lake Pontchartrain, more than any single bridge from sixth place down covers on its own.

The Wuhan Metro Bridge runs 37,788 meters through Wuhan, China, and carries every meter of Line 1 on the city's subway. One unbroken elevated structure holds the entire route, earning the bridge the title of the world's longest continuous viaduct. Nine entries on the list outdistance it. The title rewards an uninterrupted span, and the Wuhan structure supplies one between the two termini of its line. A trip on Line 1 never leaves the bridge.

Line 1 opened in 2004 and made Wuhan the fifth Chinese city with a subway. The city's first metro route ran its full course on a raised deck. Wuhan's riders entered the subway era above street level. Four Chinese cities had opened subways before Wuhan, and the fifth arrived with its debut route lifted into the air. Among the 10, no other bridge hosts a subway.

Wuhan's 2004 opening makes this bridge the oldest of the seven Chinese structures on the list. The other six reached completion between 2008 and 2017. Five of those six carry railways between cities. Wuhan's structure serves trips that begin and end inside one metropolis. Only Thailand's Bang Na Expressway and Louisiana's Lake Pontchartrain Causeway predate it, and Taiwan's Changhua-Koahsiung Viaduct followed three years later.

The Wuhan crossing falls 627 meters short of the newer Lake Pontchartrain span and 612 meters short of the original one. No neighboring pair among the 10 sits closer together than the Louisiana causeway and the Wuhan bridge. The causeway splits its traffic across two parallel bridges. Wuhan's viaduct keeps its whole line on one continuous structure. It covers less than a quarter of the distance of the Danyang-Kunshan Grand Bridge. An urban subway line, opened before any of China's high-speed rail viaducts on the list, reaches far enough to stand among the world's 10 longest bridges.
