India news: Gandhi vows to step up pressure on Modi at protest rally

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Rahul Gandhi said the removal of election chief Gyanesh Kumar will be the first step to getting the country on the right track. Follow DW for more.

Here is a roundup of the biggest headlines from India on Monday, November 2.

This is all we got for you today. We will be back with more news from India tomorrow.

Bollywood star Amitabh Bachchan made his regular weekly appearance on Sunday outside his Mumbai home, but this time from behind a bullet proof glass with heavy security, following a bomb threat.

Bachchan was seen waving to his fans with his son Abhishek Bachchan standing beside him.

The tightened security comes after a threatening email was reported to Mumbai police last week.

Reportedly, the email was sent to a school in the Bandra-Kurla Complex area in Mumbai.

The email allegedly threatened to target Bachchan's house with a bomb and named October 31 and November 27 as possible dates for attacks.

The police has launched an investigation and it is still to be ascertained whether the email specifically mentioned Bachchan's residence.

After cyclone Arnab, India is bracing for cyclone Murasu which is moving along the coast of the southeastern state of Andhra Pradesh, packing winds ranging between 100 and 120 kilometers per hour (75 miles per hour).

According to the Indian Meteorological Department (IMD), Murasu is expected to make landfall on Tuesday.

Thousands of people have been evacuated along India's eastern coast. Schools and colleges have been closed as heavy rainfall is expected.

Meanwhile, the IMD has issued an orange alert for six districts and a yellow alert for five districts in the neighboring state of Telangana. An orange alert means heavy rainfall of 11-20 cm.

IMD has also warned of the possibility of Murasu intensifying further into a very severe cyclonic storm before reaching the Andhra Pradesh coastline.

A local youth organization, protesting against the dam construction in northeastern state of Arunachal Pradesh's Siang district, announced a 36-hour long shutdown, local newspaper the Assam Tribune reported on Monday.

The protests over the proposed Siang Upper Multipurpose Project (SUMP), an 11,000–11,200 MW mega-dam planned on the Siang River, have intensified in recent day.

Protesters burnt tires while bus services and other public transport facilities remained shut in the district, according to the report.

The state wing of Indian National Congress has demanded the scrapping of the proposed dam project.

The party said, "Different groups but one voice; different organisations but one collective resolve – Stop SUMP, Save Siang."

Locals are demanding the release of protesters arrested for opposing the dam construction, deployment of security forces and suspension of ongoing surveys for the proposed mega hydropower project on the Siang river.

The dam, envisioned as India's biggest hydropower project, has been facing opposition from sections of the indigenous Adi community over concerns about displacement, loss of agricultural land, identity and culture.

With pollution levels soaring as winter is arriving across northern India, authorities collected fines amounting to nearly €64,500 on the first day of the anti-pollution drive on Sunday.

Nearly 15,000 vehicles were checked by authorities, while more than 1,500 vehicles were sent back to Delhi's borders and more than 500 tickets were issued for failing to comply with the Pollution Under Control Certificate (PUCC) norms.

The Delhi government has imposed several restrictions as the time when pollution levels peak in Delhi approaches.

The authorities in Delhi have imposed a ban on non-Delhi vehicles that do not meet emission standards from November 1, 2026, to January 31, 2027.

Vehicles running on compressed natural gas (CNG), electric vehicles (EVs) and those involved in essential services were exempted from the restrictions, according to a report by Indian news channel NDTV.

According to the report, 300 construction sites were inspected, of which 32 were reported to be violating dust control norms.

Leader of the opposition Indian National Congress (INC) party, Rahul Gandhi, dialed up the pressure against Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Home Minister Amit Shah as thousands joined the INDIA bloc rally held at Ramlila Maidan in Delhi, demanding India's election chief Gyanesh Kumar's resignation.

Addressing the crowd, Gandhi said, "Narendra Modi ji and Amit Shah ji, understand this: after Gyanesh Kumar, we will stand against you and don’t forget that for the vote chori (vote theft) you have done, there will be legal action against you and Narendra Modi."

Gandhi said the resignation of Kumar as the Chief Election Commission would be "the first step to getting the country on the right track."

He alleged that "without Gyanesh Kumar, Narendra Modi wouldn’t have become PM and Amit Shah wouldn’t be home minister."

The rally follows a series of protests in more than a month against Kumar and the controversial mass voter roll revision, which removed more than 130 million names from voter lists.

Opposition parties have alleged the exercise disproportionately affected Muslims and other minorities.

The rally was attended by several opposition leaders including senior INC leaders Sonia Gandhi, Mallikarjun Kharge along with leaders of regional parties such as Mamata Banerjee, Akhilesh Yadav, Mehbooba Mufti, and Tejashwi Yadav.
