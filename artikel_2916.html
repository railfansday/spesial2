Spurs eager to kick off NBA Cup group play against Sacramento Kings

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

SACRAMENTO - Spurs coach Mitch Johnson is a big fan of the NBA Cup, and it's not just because his team reached the championship game of the in-season tournament last year.

The Spurs fell to the New York Knicks 124-113 in Las Vegas last December after upsetting the Los Angeles Lakers and the Oklahoma City Thunder in the single-elimination knockout round. It was an impressive run for a young team, a confidence boost that gave it some of the juice needed to go on to to win 62 games before a stunning postseason that ended with a rematch against the Knicks in the NBA Finals.

"It was beneficial in the sense our group hadn't had any experience together, let alone added visibility or importance in terms of what people would put on a game," said Johnson, who notched career win No. 100 with Wednesday night's 112-111 victory over Dallas.

To reach Vegas, the Spurs had to win games on the road against the Denver Nuggets and the Los Angeles Lakers.

"Our guys got excited about it," Johnson said.

That excitement is exactly what the NBA hoped the Cup would generate when the league launched it in 2023.

"I think the two things in recent history that weren't quite sure how they would unfold were the NBA Cup and the NBA play-in," Johnson said. "And when you look at the product, it's hard to say that it hasn't been an absolute success. So tip of the cap to the league for putting that together and organizing it in a way that has, I think, added an element that fans have really enjoyed and seemed to add some more excitement and another layer to the league."

The surging Spurs are hoping for more excitement this season as they compete against the Lakers, the Golden State Warriors, the Portland Trail Blazers and the Sacramento Kings in West Group C after all 30 teams were divided into six groups of five across the Eastern and Western conferences

The Spurs, who are 6-2 after winning their sixth in a row, begin group play against the Kings (1-7) Friday night at Golden 1 Center.

"It definitely brings some excitement," Spurs forward Keldon Johnson said of tourney. "I'm looking forward to it. It was great for us last year. As a team, we're looking forward to it. Everybody's on the same page and locked into that."

That includes one of the stars of last year's run to Vegas.

"It's a different feel. Honestly, I like the Cup games," said forward Devin Vassell, who scored a season-high 35 points in the 139-136 come-from-behind victory over the Nuggets that advanced the Spurs to their quarterfinal matchup in Los Angeles. "It feels like you're playing for something bigger, kind of a little bit, and I'm excited. We've all been talking about it. We feel like we want to get our 'get-back' from last year."

San Antonio Spurs guard Devin Vassell, right, goes up for a basket as Denver Nuggets forward Spencer Jones defends in the first half of an NBA Cup basketball game Friday, Nov. 28, 2025, in Denver. (AP Photo/David Zalubowski) (David Zalubowski/Associated Press)

In a bit of foreshadowing, the Spurs blew an 11-point third quarter lead to the Knicks in Vegas. Six months later in the Finals, they lost in five games to New York after squandering double-digit leads in each of their four losses, including blowing a 29-point advangage in the 107-106 loss in Game 4 at Madison Square Garden.

"People say the Cup isn't that important, but if (winning it) translates to (winning the Finals), you never know," Vassell said. "They're really good games. The crowds be crazy in those games. It's a good atmosphere."

The Spurs' other group-play games will be against the Warriors on Nov. 13 at the Frost Bank Center, the Trail Blazers on Nov. 25 in Portland, and the Lakers on Nov. 27 at the Frost.

The Cup quarterfinals are set for (Dec. 4-5) and the semifinals (Dec. 8 and/or Dec. 9) will take place in NBA team markets before the tournament concludes with the championship Dec. 11 at Hinkle Fieldhouse in Indianapolis.

Eight teams advance to the knockout stage - each group winner and two wild cards. The wild cards are the teams from each conference that finish with the best group play record among second-place finishers.

All NBA Cup games except for the championship game will count toward teams' NBA regular season records. The 22 teams that don't reach the knockout rounds will play two regular season games on the off days of the knockout round, Dec. 6-7 and Dec. 9-10. The four quarterfinal losers will be matched up with each other for a regular season game in one of those windows to round out their 82-game schedule.

Every player on teams that make the final eight earns $53,093. If they make it to the final four, it's $106,817 per player. The title/game loser collects $212,373 per player. Players on the championship team pocket $530,933.

"We have our eye on the prize, for sure," Vassell said.
