Las Vegas Grand Prix 2026 Guide: Schedule, How to Watch and F1 Standings

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Las Vegas Grand Prix 2026 arrives with both championships already decided and a starting grid already being rearranged by five penalties. Here is everything you need for the rest of the weekend on the Strip: session times in Eastern and Pacific, how to watch in the US and abroad, what happened in Thursday's rain-hit practice, and where the drivers' and constructors' standings sit with three races left.

Round: 21 of the 2026 season, the first of the final three races before Qatar and Abu Dhabi

Circuit: Las Vegas Strip Circuit, 6.201 km (3.853 miles), running past Caesars Palace, the Bellagio and the Venetian

Race distance: 50 laps or 120 minutes, whichever comes first

Format: Traditional weekend, with three practice sessions, qualifying and the Grand Prix. There is no Sprint

Race start: Saturday, Nov. 21, at 11 p.m. ET (8 p.m. local)

Previous winners: Max Verstappen (2023, 2025) and George Russell (2024). Those are the two drivers now fighting for second in the championship

Las Vegas runs on Pacific time, so East Coast fans are looking at late nights all weekend. The race is on Saturday night in North America and early Sunday morning in Europe. All times below come from the official F1 timetable and are subject to change.

In the UK, qualifying is at 4 a.m. GMT on Saturday and the race starts at 4 a.m. GMT on Sunday, Nov. 22. F1 Academy also closes its season in Las Vegas, with its reverse-grid race on Friday at 9:15 p.m. ET and its feature race on Saturday at 7:20 p.m. ET.

United States: Apple TV is F1's US broadcaster in 2026, and Apple has made the Las Vegas Grand Prix free to watch with no subscription. Its free offer covers practice, qualifying and the race, live and on demand. Open the Apple TV app or go to tv.apple.com, find the Formula 1 section and pick the session. For other race weekends, Apple TV costs $12.99 per month.

Canada: On TSN, practice streams only on TSN Premium through TSN.ca and the TSN app. Qualifying and the race are on TSN1 and TSN5 as well as streaming. French-language coverage is on RDS.

United Kingdom: Sky Sports F1 carries every session live. Remember the eight-hour time difference: the race starts at 4 a.m. on Sunday.

Everywhere else: In countries where it is available, F1 TV Pro streams every session live, along with onboard cameras for every car and live timing. Check F1's broadcaster list for your country.

Thursday gave teams very little dry running to learn from. Rain on the Strip meant first practice produced no timed laps; drivers only completed installation runs. Second practice started on full wets and finished on intermediates. George Russell set the pace with a late 1:47.272 for Mercedes, 0.071 seconds clear of Alpine's Pierre Gasly. Charles Leclerc was third for Ferrari.

World champion Kimi Antonelli finished eighth, and Max Verstappen was only 13th after spending much of the cold session in the garage. Friday is forecast to stay dry, so the one hour of FP3 tonight is effectively the only dry preparation anyone gets before qualifying.

Oscar Piastri drops 10 places on the Las Vegas grid after taking a new engine. File photo from the 2025 Japanese Grand Prix, not from Las Vegas. Photo: Liauzh / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Tonight's qualifying order will not be Saturday's starting order. Power unit changes have already handed out these grid penalties:

Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes): Five-place grid drop for a new internal combustion engine. He is also running Mercedes' upgraded turbo, which Russell is not using

Oscar Piastri (McLaren): 10-place grid drop for a new engine

Oliver Bearman (Haas): Starts from the back after taking a batch of new power unit parts

Carlos Sainz (Williams) and Lance Stroll (Aston Martin): Both also face grid drops for component changes

Antonelli and Piastri both drive cars that can reach Q3, so drivers who qualify just outside the top 10 could move forward before the lights go out. For more, see our Las Vegas notebook on the penalties and the fight for second.

Antonelli clinched the 2026 drivers' title at the Mexican Grand Prix, so the fight now is for second. After Lando Norris won in São Paulo, just 33 points separate second-placed Verstappen from Norris in sixth.

Verstappen is the driver in form. He has won three of the last five races and brings an eight-race podium streak to Las Vegas, a track where he has won twice. Russell trails him by 17 points and leads Hamilton by just one. With 75 points left for any one driver over the final three races, second place is still wide open.

Kimi Antonelli has already won the 2026 drivers' title and comes to Las Vegas with nine wins. File photo from the 2025 Japanese Grand Prix, not from Las Vegas. Photo: Liauzh / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Mercedes sealed the constructors' championship in São Paulo, its ninth title overall and its first since 2021. Ferrari leads Red Bull by 95 points in the race for second, and McLaren is 17 points behind Red Bull.

Standings source: Crash.net, after the São Paulo Grand Prix.

Expect it to be cold. Skies are expected to clear after Thursday's rain, but the race-night forecast is in the mid-40s Fahrenheit (about 7°C). That makes getting the tyres up to temperature, especially on the restart after a safety car, one of the main challenges on the Strip.

The season ends with back-to-back races: the Qatar Grand Prix on Sunday, Nov. 29, and the season finale in Abu Dhabi on Sunday, Dec. 6.
