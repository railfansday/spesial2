Billionaire David Tepper of Appaloosa Management Has 65% of His Portfolio Invested in These 7 Artificial Intelligence (AI) Stocks. Here Are the 3 I’d Buy Hand Over Fist Right Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

David Tepper is going all in on AI. Some of his picks look like long-term winners.

By Prosper Junior Bakiny – Nov 27, 2026 at 3:00AM EST

Wall Street is split on whether the artificial intelligence (AI) industry will continue growing rapidly. Some fear that leading AI stocks will experience massive pullbacks as infrastructure spending slows. Others believe we may still be in the early innings of the AI revolution, and the market has bright prospects.

Billionaire David Tepper, the billionaire founder and president of Appaloosa Management, is probably closer to the second group. How do we know that? His hedge fund's public equity portfolio is full of leading AI stocks. Among the fund's top nine largest holdings, seven are significantly exposed to the AI industry.

These seven companies are Micron Technology (MU -4.53%), Amazon (AMZN -3.12%), Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -1.96%), Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%), Nvidia (NVDA -3.91%), Meta Platforms (META +3.40%), and Alphabet (GOOG -2.02%) (GOOGL -2.13%).

Together, these AI stocks account for about 65% of Appaloosa Management's public equity portfolio, as of the end of the third quarter. That's a fund that's bullish on the future of AI and could deliver outstanding returns over the long run, provided the industry continues to grow -- but it is likely to be volatile, given ongoing fears among many investors about the industry's future.

That said, several of these stocks look attractive, in my view, and the three with the most promising outlooks are Amazon, Alphabet, and Nvidia. Here is why.

Amazon is both a great pick to ride the AI tailwind and a decent hedge in case the industry's growth slows considerably. As a leader in cloud computing, it offers a large portfolio of cloud-based AI services that have helped boost sales within its Amazon Web Services (AWS) division, which is by far its most profitable business. In the third quarter, Amazon's net sales were $201.9 billion, an increase of 12% year over year.

AWS sales came in at $46 billion, 39% higher than the year-ago period. Amazon partly credited demand for AI products for AWS' fastest year-over-year revenue growth in nearly five years. But Amazon offers plenty of services beyond AI, and, as Andy Jassy has pointed out, the overwhelming majority of IT spending still occurs on-premises.

Much of that should migrate to the cloud over the long run, providing a massive tailwind to the company. Further, Amazon remains a leader in e-commerce and operates a fast-growing, high-margin digital advertising business. Even if the AI market slows, the company boasts attractive opportunities across its business, making it a top long-term pick.

Alphabet also has a booming cloud business that continues to improve thanks to AI. In the third quarter, the company's revenue increased by 25% year over year to $127.6 billion. Cloud sales soared 103% year over year to $30.7 billion. Alphabet's cloud revenue growth has accelerated in recent quarters, and its strength in AI offerings has a lot to do with it.

The company is doubling down. Alphabet's free cash flow has turned negative in recent quarters as it continues to invest significant sums to fund its AI ambitions. What if that doesn't pay off? In my view, Alphabet will be fine either way.

The company still generates most of its sales from ads, and it boasts a wide moat from switching costs across its famous Google search engine and its YouTube video-sharing platform. Alphabet's advertising empire should continue to post solid sales growth, while the company remains well-positioned to benefit from the growing cloud computing market over the long run.

Lastly, Alphabet has other opportunities that could pay off too, such as Waymo, its self-driving car business.

Nvidia is more of a pure-play AI stock than Amazon and Alphabet, but it remains a top long-term pick for at least three reasons. First, it has evolved from a GPU (graphics processing unit) leader -- a market where it holds an overwhelming lead -- to offer a full-stack AI platform that includes hardware such as GPUs and CPUs (central processing units), software, cloud services, and more.

Second, although Nvidia lost its iconic CEO, Jensen Huang, for a little over a week, he was recently reinstated after the company's board voted unanimously to do so. Huang's alleged extramarital affairs, including with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset, have created problems for the AI leader, but Huang is worth the trouble and can probably help the company navigate the future of the AI industry better than anyone else could.

Third, Nvidia's shares look dirt cheap after its recent troubles. The stock is trading at just 9.3x forward earnings, which, in my view, makes it a steal at current levels.
