Nike's Turnaround Is Taking Longer Than Expected. These 3 Stocks May Be Better Buys.

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Should investors show patience with Nike or turn to one of these alternatives?

Business turnarounds capture investors' attention because they offer a chance to buy a beaten-down stock. That means it's an opportunity to own a value stock that will produce outsize gains.

However, it's a risky investment, particularly among retail and consumer goods companies, where turnarounds have proven notoriously difficult.

Nike's (NKE +0.71%) management has been making the attempt. This once-heralded brand has fallen on hard times. Should investors buy shares of Nike, or instead consider one of the following three companies?

Nike sold popular products (particularly sneakers), athletic endorsements, and juggernaut sales. Then, product innovation slipped, the company faced intense competition, and sales dropped.

Two years ago, Nike brought in former company executive Elliott Hill as CEO. His plan was to concentrate once again on sports, focus on product innovation, and rebuild relationships with wholesalers.

However, sales have continued sliding. Nike's fiscal first-quarter revenue fell 5% year over year, excluding foreign-currency translations. While its gross margin expanded 0.6 percentage points to 42.8%, that was due to lower costs. I believe the only way to sustain margin expansion is high product demand and strong sales growth. The period ended on Aug. 31.

Management doesn't expect results to improve anytime soon, either. It projects a high-single-digit percentage drop in revenue for the year.

With continuing weak revenue, I'd pass on Nike's shares. That's despite the stock falling 40.4% this year through Nov. 13, and trading at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 18 compared to the 10-year median of 33.

Consumers clamored for Lululemon Athletica's (LULU -0.18%) products, notably leggings and workout clothing, and revenue and the stock skyrocketed. However, when you rely on fashionable merchandise, you risk missteps that cause consumers to turn elsewhere. That's happened with Lululemon as people looked elsewhere, competition intensified, and product innovation lagged.

It hasn't regained momentum. Lululemon's fiscal second-quarter revenue (ended Aug. 2) fell 5% in constant dollars, and same-store sales (comps) dropped 10%. Its gross margin expanded by 2 percentage points, but this is misleading. After removing tariff refunds, Lululemon's gross margin actually contracted by 3.6 percentage points.

Management anticipates weak revenue for the second half of the year. Its guidance calls for a 5% to 7% decline. Lululemon's shares have dropped a whopping 49.2% this year. The P/E ratio has fallen to 9, well below the 10-year median of 41.

However, this has the makings of a value trap, given the difficulty the company has had in winning back customers and its persistent revenue declines. I also wouldn't buy Lululemon stock.

On Holding (ONON -2.59%) primarily sells footwear. Formed in 2010, this relatively young company has become a major competitor to Nike.

The company's innovative products have clearly resonated with consumers. On Holding has grown its sales at a rapid clip. That includes 17.8% year-over-year growth in the third quarter, and management expects sales to increase by more than 20% this year.

Despite a challenging economic environment, On hasn't been forced to discount its products. In fact, the company's gross margin, excluding tariff refunds, expanded by 3.2 percentage points to 66.9%.

Still, in the face of stiff competition and a promotional environment, investors have been concerned that growth may slow and its margin will contract. They've sent the shares down 20.7% this year.

However, the price drop has created a much better valuation. The P/E ratio has fallen from over 50 to 20 this year. On's stock has a five-year median P/E of 87.

It's enticing to buy shares of a fast-growing company at a discounted valuation. I'd approach the situation with caution, since popular brands can lose their edge when innovation slows or fails to meet consumer expectations. It's difficult to predict when, or if, that might happen.

Deckers Outdoor (DECK -3.87%) sells footwear and apparel. It has two primary brands: Hoka (primarily athletic footwear) and Ugg (boots).

These have produced steady sales gains. Still, Deckers' fiscal second-quarter sales growth slowed to 2.8% year over year. Hoka and Ugg's sales increased 6.8% and 3.5%, respectively. Its gross margin expanded by 4.3 percentage points to 60.5%, but management said this was primarily due to tariff refunds. The results were for the quarter ended on Sept. 30.

Nonetheless, these results came at a time when consumers have been challenged by high prices, including for gas. That's squeezed discretionary spending.

The market has reacted to the recent results, sending the shares down 16.3% this year.

That's helped drive the P/E ratio down from 15 to 12. Deckers' stock, with a 10-year median P/E multiple of 23, is also attractive from an historical viewpoint.

With two powerful brands that have seen sales temporarily hurt due to outside factors, this seems like a good opportunity to buy Deckers' shares at a discounted valuation.
