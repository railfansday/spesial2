Here are 4 ways OKC Thunder can snap out of current 3-game losing streak

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

For two entire regular seasons, the Oklahoma City Thunder never experienced a three-game losing streaks. Now, they've experienced two of those over the last month. Injuries have finally done a number on the once-hopeful title favorite. A month into the Post-D.C.C. (Dub, Chet, Caso) world, they're clinging onto pure survival.

For the first time since the Thunder experienced one of the darkest days in their franchise history, their postseason spot is starting to look a little murky to forecast. Once a foregone conclusion, OKC enters Thanksgiving in 11th place in the Western Conference standings.

The Thunder started the year an admirable 8-2. Since then, they've lost seven of nine games. The latest painted a similar picture to most of their defeats. They kept it close, but no dice, in an NBA Cup loss to the Minnesota Timberwolves.

So, now what? As surreal as it sounds, the Thunder still have 63 regular-season matchups to go. Here are four quick fixes to their current three-game losing streak:

When the Thunder first experienced their season-altering injuries to Jalen Williams, Chet Holmgren and Cason Wallace, most penciled Mitchell in as a benefactor. With three of their four best players out, he was pushed to the front of the line. His temporary promotion includes him going from a Sixth Man to OKC's second-best scorer. So far, it's been a wild ride.

Before he dropped 25 points over the daunting Timberwolves, Mitchell had struggled in his last handful of contests. He went on a seven-game streak failing to crack 20 points and on poor efficinecy. That obviously needs to change. Out of pure necessity, too. He hasn't taken full advantage of the opportunity in front of him, per se, but has a couple of more months to do so.

Sometimes, the biggest solutions are the simplest ones. Even through their dire health situation, the Thunder have remained stubborn on Caruso's role. It's been mostly bench-exclusive. Sans one start. Run of the mill for his three seasons in OKC. In that time, he's started four whopping games total. Quite the juxtaposition from what his role was on the Chicago Bulls.

But considering the Thunder have habitually fallen behind with slow first quarters, can they afford to keep Caruso in his bench role? I just don't think so. He's a basketball impact deity. There's a reason why all of the advanced metrics absolutely love him. He has an eye-popping plus-33 point swing in his on-off splits. I get wanting to reserve that tsunami number to keep your second unit afloat, but that's irrelevant if you're always behind to start games.

On Thanksgiving, Thunder fans have very little to be thankful for. A rare exception to that? Isaiah Hartenstein's play. In a funny twist of fate, he's had picture-perfect health. He's the sole Opening Night starter who has played in every game. The 28-year-old couldn't crack 60 games in either of his first two regular-season years in OKC. Considering how depleted their frontcourt room is, he's been a godsend for that group. He's averaged 8.4 points, 10.8 points and 3.9 assists in his scaled-up role.

As awesome as Hartenstein has been, the Thunder badly need some of their other centers to get healthy. Obviously, Holmgren is still a little away. He should be back around New Year's from his small torn patellar tendon. Anything from Thomas Sorber should be treated as found money. Especially since he had a third knee surgery recently. But Aday Mara and Jaylin Williams? Those two should be reinforcements added soon. They just need another healthy big man. Amazing how one of the deepest center rooms has completely fallen apart.

After a couple of close losses to the Indiana Pacers and Timberwolves, nothing is a given for the Thunder. That said, these next three opponents should be padded wins. They will travel on the road to face the Milwaukee Bucks on Saturday, Nov. 28; then at the Brooklyn Nets on Monday, Nov. 30; and return to OKC to host the Nets on Wednesday, Dec. 2.

Those three are gimme wins. The Bucks are the worst team in the league. The Nets aren't too far behind. I know the Thunder have slowly lost a ton of trust in banking on wins — especially with their current duct-taped group — but there's zero reason why those three should be cruises. In that scenario, OKC could go from 10-9 to 13-9. Which obviously helps with their postseason ambitions. I think the Thunder can still be a top-four seed, but you have to be more open to the play-in tournament than initially thought.
