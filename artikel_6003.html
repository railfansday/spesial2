Alaska Airlines Boeing 737 Collides With Catering Truck At JFK, Wing Damage Halts Flight

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

An Alaska Airlines Boeing 737-800 has been temporarily grounded in New York after colliding with a catering vehicle ahead of a flight to San Francisco International (SFO). The jet, which bears the registration N548AS, was set to depart from John F Kennedy Airport (JFK) on Monday evening as Alaska Airlines flight AS41, but, now, its return to San Francisco has been pushed back to later on Tuesday morning.

A video uploaded to social media by one of the passengers showed the aftermath of the collision, which saw the wing of the 737 come into contact with a truck. Such was the extent of the damage that the flight ended up having to be canceled. However, the fact that N548US is set to re-enter service just a day later suggests that it was less terminal than a similar incident that befell a United Airlines A320 this year.

Alaska Airlines flight AS41 is a daily scheduled service from New York to San Francisco. It is one of the carrier's longest transcontinental flights, with a block time of six hours and 41 minutes between its planned departure from JFK at 5:25 PM and its scheduled arrival into the Bay Area at 9:06 PM (all times local). The iteration of this flight on Monday, November 23, however, ended up having to be canceled.

This, as seen in the clip above, was due to the jet having been hit by a catering truck on the ground at JFK ahead of its departure. The Aviation Safety Network reported that "the ventral strake of the left-hand split scimitar winglet" bore the brunt of the damage, which was severe enough to take the jet out of service. Passengers were then told that the damaged Boeing 737 would return to the gate to deplane.

Simple Flying has reached out to Alaska Airlines for further information on this incident. We will update our coverage upon receiving a statement from the carrier.

In aviation, nothing is more important than safety, and so the cancelation of the flight and the temporary grounding of N548AS represent a logical course of action. However, early indications seem to suggest that the damage can be repaired easily enough, given that, according to flight tracking data, the jet is set to re-enter service later on Tuesday. At the time of writing, it was rostered to operate flight AS33.

This is another scheduled Alaska Airlines flight from New York to San Francisco, which departs at 11:30 AM and arrives at 3:06 PM, six hours and 36 minutes later. For the passengers impacted by Monday evening's cancelation of flight AS41, Alaska Airlines also appears to have scheduled an extra flight to San Francisco on Tuesday morning, numbered as AS9230, which is scheduled to depart JFK at 9:00 AM.

According to fleet data from ch-aviation, N548AS is one of 59 examples of the Boeing 737-800 that Alaska Airlines currently has at its disposal. These narrowbody twinjets are 18.7 years old on average, although N548AS is one of the more senior examples, having first flown 21.45 years ago. It was delivered to Alaska Airlines on July 1, 2005, and it currently seats 161 passengers across two classes.

Thankfully, there were no passengers in the aircraft.

With N548AS set to re-enter service later on Tuesday morning, less than a day after it was initially hit by the catering truck on the ground at New York JFK Airport, Alaska Airlines seems to have resolved the issue relatively quickly. However, such incidents can end up being more terminal for older aircraft, such as N470UA, a United Airlines Airbus A320-200 that was hit by a truck in Washington in May 2026.
