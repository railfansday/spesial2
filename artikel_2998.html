Micron vs. Sandisk: Which AI Memory Stock Is the Better Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Both of these AI memory stocks still have triple-digit returns in 2026 despite selloffs since this summer.

By Dave Kovaleski – Nov 24, 2026 at 3:05PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Micron Technology ›

It has been a wild ride in 2026 for two leading memory chip stocks, Micron (MU -11.01%) and Sandisk (SNDK -11.13%).

Both are among Wall Street’s best performers year to date, but their paths to those returns have been extremely volatile.

At its 2026 closing high of $1,213 per share on June 25, Micron was up about 326% year to date. At its split-adjusted closing peak of $291 per share on June 22, Sandisk was up 870%.

Since then, Sandisk has lost 68% of its value, falling to $91 per share. But it's still up 209% from $30 per share at the start of the year. Micron had fallen 51% to $594 per share on Nov. 20, but bounced back on Nov. 23, climbing to $717 per share. It's still up 147% from $285 per share at the start of the year.

So, if you’ve owned shares since before this summer, you're still probably happy with the returns, despite the wild swings. But heading into 2027, which of the two is the better buy?

Both Sandisk and Micron have benefited from the current memory and storage supercycle, which was brought on by the fast advance of artificial intelligence computing. Demand for AI memory and storage has been magnified by the massive amount of AI infrastructure being built by hyperscalers, neoclouds, and others, and, in turn, chipmakers such as Nvidia, Intel, and Advanced Micro Devices.

The demand for chips that can provide quickly accessible memory and storage for the data AI requires and creates has boomed as a result. Because memory chips are essentially a commodity, the companies that manufacture them try to avoid overbuilding their production capacity. In the wake of the rapid surge in demand, they now can't keep up. This has given Micron, Sandisk, and their peers incredible pricing power and huge backlogs of orders.

While Micron and Sandisk have products that do directly compete with each other, their respective cash cow products are in separate areas. Micron's main product is high bandwidth memory (HBM) chips for AI data centers that can provide fast memory for AI processing. It is mostly sold out of HBM through 2027. Sandisk's main products are NAND and solid-state storage drives, which provide long-term data storage for AI computing.

Both memory giants also have long-term contracts with major hyperscalers and customers with commitments through at least 2030.

Sandisk just underwent an 8-for-1 stock split on Nov. 20, which brought its previously lofty share price down to a more accessible $91 per share.

Though both of these stocks have dropped so sharply over the past few months, that was mostly due to factors beyond their business performance. Many investors took profits when the stocks were higher. They were also brought down by broader concerns about whether the enormous levels of spending on AI infrastructure will deliver the return on investment that the bulls promise. The downtrend was likely exacerbated by the U.S. midterm elections, in which voters in many locales made it clear that they want stricter regulatory and government controls around data center development. This has led to concerns that hyperscalers might negotiate down or out of their long-term chip deals.

The Jensen Huang Nvidia situation did not help AI stocks either, although he was just reinstated as CEO on Monday, so we'll see where that leads.

The fact remains that both Sandisk and Micron are vital suppliers in the AI boom, and even if there are more controls on where data centers can go and a greater level of concern for their environmental impact, that won't eliminate the need for AI processing and storage.

Also, both of these stocks are dirt cheap, made even cheaper by the recent sell-off. This is a testament to their massive earnings power. Micron is trading at just 3.4 times 1-year forward earnings. Sandisk is even cheaper with a 1-year forward P/E of 2.5. And both have minuscule 1-year forward price/earnings-to-growth ratios of about 0.05.

I own shares of Micron and will add more at this low valuation, but Sandisk is also a screaming buy.
