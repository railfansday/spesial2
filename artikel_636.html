Actor Awards 2027 Preview: Breaking Down the Top Film and TV Contenders

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The awards season acting races are taking shape, and nothing will test them like the Actor Awards, the ceremony formerly known as the SAG Awards. There is a real possibility the season is barreling toward another edition of #OscarsSoWhite, and this is the stage where the nominating committee can exert the most influence. In TV, a similar problem remains after an Emmys full of white winners. With first-time contenders, overdue veterans and potential breakout performances crowding every category, nothing is locked. Andrew Scott has three Actor Award nominations from his TV work, but none for film — Focus Features’ “Elsinore” could finally give SAG voters a chance to catch up with what theater audiences have known for years. John Malkovich, who has two Actor Awards nominations — the latest one for ensemble cast in 2000 — is close behind for Martin McDonagh’s “Wild Horse Nine.” Matt Damon, carrying Christopher Nolan’s “The Odyssey,” has seven Actor prize nominations but is looking for that first win. Ryan Gosling’s everyman science teacher turned hero in “Project Hail Mary” has emerged as one of the race’s most beloved performances and could hand the six-time nominee another bid. John Turturro rounds out the category for the indie drama “The Only Living Pickpocket in New York.”The veteran actor has six Actor Award noms, but only one for a feature film. PedroPascal (“Behemoth!”) and newcomer Anthony Ippolito (“I Play Rocky”) are potential entrants, along with Jaafar Jackson (“Michael”) and Sebastian Stan (“Fjord”).

Julianne Moore is back in the conversation more than a decade after winning for “Still Alice.” Her performance in Jesse Eisenberg’s “The Debut” anchors a film that is also gaining traction in other categories across all awards, including Oscar. This combination could make it difficult to unseat her as the favorite.

The season’s breakout is Inde Navarrette, whose work in the horror film “Obsession” has turned a festival discovery into a serious top-five contender. Olivia Wilde is also in the mix for “The Invite,” which she also directed, giving her awards season narrative a boost. Voters have rarely been inclusive of non-English-language performances, which leaves Sandra Hüller from “Rose” — and others like Virginie Efira (“All of a Sudden”) — vulnerable to a snub.

The nominating committee loves Emily Blunt, which could carry her into the fold for Steven Spielberg’s “Disclosure Day.” She’s landed noms for her wide range of work, including “The Girl on the Train,” “Mary Poppins Returns,” “A Quiet Place” and “Oppenheimer.” She even won for the latter two, making her one of only two performers to turn a SAG film-acting win into nothing at the Academy, the other being Idris Elba for “Beasts of No Nation.”

Zendaya has two possible entries — “Dune: Part Three” and “The Drama” — testing her ability to avoid a vote split.

Andrew Garfield leads for his portrayal of OpenAI chief Sam Altman in Luca Guadagnino’s “Artificial.” A three-time nominee playing one of the decade’s most scrutinized figures makes for a compelling awards combination.

John Leguizamo could earn his first nomination for “The Odyssey,” delivering a performance that’s the moral center of Nolan’s epic. Paul Giamatti (“The Debut”) and Sam Rockwell (“Wild Horse Nine”) are familiar faces the guild knows well. The last slot could go to Guitarricadelafuente, whose feature film debut in Netflix’s Spanish-language “La Bola Negra” has made him one of the season’s most intriguing newcomers, but again, non-English-language performances struggle here. Still, Netflix got the “Emilia Pérez” stars there, with a win for Zoe Saldaña. Colman Domingo’s Joe Jackson in “Michael” could earn him a fifth nomination after back-to-back bids for “Rustin” and “Sing Sing.”

Anne Hathaway is at the forefront with her Penelope in “The Odyssey,” while Samantha Morton also gives the epic another supporting contender. Penélope Cruz is playing double duty this year, most likely to be recognized for “The Invite,” though also has a credible opportunity in “La Bola Negra.”

The latest news is that Amanda Seyfried is not eligible for SAG attention for her work in Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s documentary “You Can See Everything.” That could leave a spot open for Olivia Colman (“Elsinore”), Sandra Hüller (“Project Hail Mary”), Kate O’Flynn (“Tender Loving Care”) or Gemma Chan (“Josephine”).

In the TV race, it’s likely that audiences will see a familiar cast taking the stage with Noah Wyle and his “The Pitt” crew, who took the prize last year. The show could go up some of the same players — “The Diplomat” and “Landman” would be repeat noms from last year — as well as others like “Paradise,” “The Gilded Age” and “Slow Horses.” As for a new edition eligible for the first time this season, “Furious” could make a big splash at the Actor Awards with excellent performances from both the lead and supporting casts.

In the comedy race, another Emmy repeat may be in place with Apple TV taking this award twice in a row — last year for “The Studio,” this year could see “Widow’s Bay,” which dominated the Emmys. “Hacks” will also be a huge contender for one last time, as will the usual suspects “Abbott Elementary,” “Only Murders in the Building,” “Shrinking” and possibly “The Bear.” That said, Apple also has “Margo’s Got Money Troubles” and the return of “Ted Lasso,” both of which could steal away spots.

In comedy, Matthew Rhys is the early predicted nominee after his “Widow’s Bay” success, but Jason Sudeikis re-enters the race in a category he’s won twice for “Ted Lasso.” Plus, never count out Harrison Ford, one of the town’s most beloved actors, for “Shrinking.” In drama, it’s tough to imagine Wyle not taking it once again, but with supporting and lead together, some of his co-stars could go against him. Additions to this year’s race could be Kyle Chandler and Aaron Pierre for “Lanterns.” And in limited, much like at the Emmys, expect multiple nods for “DTF St. Louis,” meaning David Harbour and Jason Bateman up against each other.

Jean Smart is a top contender for the last time in this category for “Hacks,” but unlike the Emmys, she hasn’t always taken the trophy. “Widow’s Bay’s” Kate O’Flynn is solidly in the race (and won the supporting Emmy), as is Lisa Kudrow, a two-time SAG winner returning with “The Comeback.” Two-time nominee Hannah Waddingham is back for “Ted Lasso.” The drama race could see a plethora of actresses from “The Pitt” in the race, as well as Keri Russell (“The Diplomat”), Carrie Coon (“The Gilded Age”), Zendaya (“Euphoria”) and Emmy Rossum for the only freshman show, “Furious.” In the limited/movie category, both Linda Cardellini (“DTF St. Louis”) and Sally Field (“Remarkably Bright Creatures”) took home Emmys, but keep an eye on Florence Pugh with her impressive role in “East of Eden” and Meghann Fahy who shone in “The Good Daughter.”
