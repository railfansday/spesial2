InTest Corp (INTT) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Record backlog and rising semiconductor demand, but lead times are stretching.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

InTest Corporation (INTT +9.27%) reported record quarterly orders and backlog driven by a surge in back-end semiconductor demand. Management stated that semiconductor customers are increasing capacity for analog, mixed-signal, and high-power devices, shifting constraints from wafer production to packaging and testing. The company is implementing an operational plan to transition from separate business units to a coordinated platform to capture cost synergies and commercial opportunities. Financial guidance for the fourth quarter assumes a sequential revenue range of $34 million to $36 million with a shift in product mix impacting margins.

Richard Rogoff: Thanks, Duncan. Before we open the call to questions, I want to walk you through how electronification connects our end markets, how it is shaping our R&D and our new product priorities and how it is creating new opportunities for InTest. Electronification is making customer testing more complex and essential, which is increasing demand for our solutions. Our portfolio is positioned well to meet these changing needs. In Semi, our advanced manipulators and high-powered chillers address customers' growing power and thermal and testing requirements. The same trend is reshaping our Flying Probe business.

These systems were originally built to check circuit board continuity and perform basic probing, but now they must meet more demanding test requirements as customers add electronics to their products. Battery testing is a clear example as it requires longer, more complex routines than flying probe systems have traditionally run. We are expanding these systems from single-purpose testers into broader test platform. Each new application adds test content per system, increasing the value for both the customers and to InTest. Our functional test systems are following a similar path. First developed for Auto/EV, they now also serve other end markets. Our teams are also working more closely to win customers.

As an example, in Q3, an industrial customer for an in-circuit test system placed a separate order for one of our functional test systems. For European markets where new regulations are restricting usage of certain refrigerants, we will launch a first-of-a-kind new product next week that conforms to the new legislation that we believe equals or exceeds the performance of its predecessor. Collaboration like this helps us grow revenue, but that's only half the job. The other half is making sure more of that growth reaches the bottom line consistently and through the cycle. On my first call as CEO, I stated I would develop an operational plan for InTest and share it with you.

On our Q2 call, I outlined where I was focused and that work is well underway. Over the past 2 quarters, I have examined the business through 5 lenses: our product portfolio, the levers that drive profitability, including product cost, mix and supply chain, how efficiently we deploy our operating expenses, how we optimize our manufacturing footprint and how we can share R&D more effectively across our divisions. Over that time, I have visited our facilities, met with our teams and worked with my direct reports to develop detailed actions. InTest has grown through acquisitions, and our business have largely been run and managed separately.

InTest's next phase is to operate our business as a coordinated platform, capturing commercial opportunities, cost synergies and the sharing of ideas.
