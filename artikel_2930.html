3 Energy Stocks to Buy Before 2026 Ends That Could Help You Make a Fortune

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

These energy companies are positioned for long-term expansion.

By Jeff Siegel – Nov 19, 2026 at 2:05AM EST

10 stocks we like better than GE Vernova ›

GE Vernova (GEV -0.99%), NextEra Energy (NEE +0.43%), and Williams Companies (WMB -0.34%) are three energy stocks worth your consideration before 2026 ends. All three are benefiting from growing electricity demand, particularly from artificial intelligence, data centers, and expanding industrial activity. But each approaches that opportunity differently.

GE Vernova manufactures power generation and electrical equipment. NextEra develops renewable energy projects and operates one of America's largest utilities. Williams owns natural gas infrastructure that helps supply power plants and industrial customers.

Indeed, no stock can guarantee extraordinary returns, but recent financial results and long-term growth prospects offer compelling reasons to own them.

Image source: Image source: Getty Images.

GE Vernova has become one of the biggest beneficiaries of the growing demand for electricity infrastructure. The company manufactures natural gas turbines, electrical grid equipment, and wind turbines. And as utilities and technology companies scramble to secure additional power, demand for much of its equipment continues to accelerate.

In Q3, revenue reached $12.6 billion, increasing 27% year over year. Net income totaled $1.3 billion, while adjusted EBITDA reached $1.9 billion. And the company's order backlog is particularly impressive.

GE Vernova ended September with approximately $197 billion in backlog, an increase of $20 billion from the previous quarter. Its gas turbine business is especially strong. The company reported a 122-gigawatt gas power equipment backlog and slot reservations, with expectations of reaching at least 130 gigawatts by year-end.

Meanwhile, its Electrification segment generated $3.9 billion in quarterly revenue, up 51%. Management subsequently raised its full-year revenue guidance to between $46.5 billion and $47.5 billion, with projected free cash flow of $16 billion to $17 billion.

NextEra Energy offers a different way to profit from increasing electricity consumption. Through Florida Power & Light, the company operates one of the largest regulated utilities in the U.S., and its NextEra Energy Resources business develops and operates renewable energy projects, battery storage systems, and other energy infrastructure.

Third-quarter adjusted earnings reached $2.59 billion, or $1.24 per share, compared with $1.13 per share a year earlier. That's approximately 9.7% earnings-per-share growth. The renewable energy business is also expanding.

During the quarter, NextEra Energy Resources added 3.7 gigawatts of renewable energy and storage projects to its backlog, bringing the total to approximately 36.7 gigawatts. Battery storage accounted for 2.4 gigawatts of those new additions.

NextEra also expects adjusted earnings per share to grow at a compound annual rate of at least 8% through 2032 and is targeting the same growth rate from 2032 through 2035. And unlike many high-growth energy companies, NextEra pays a dividend.

The combination of renewable energy development, regulated utility earnings, and shareholder distributions could make NextEra attractive if you're seeking long-term compounding rather than dramatic short-term price swings.

There are risks, though, including higher financing costs, regulatory decisions, and the substantial capital required to develop new infrastructure. That said, NextEra still offers exposure to renewable energy without depending entirely on the success of an emerging technology.

Williams Companies doesn't manufacture turbines or develop solar farms. It owns natural gas pipelines, processing facilities, and related infrastructure. That business could become increasingly valuable as natural gas consumption rises alongside electricity demand.

Williams reported third-quarter adjusted EBITDA of approximately $2.04 billion, up 6% year over year. Operating cash flow increased 5% to $1.51 billion, while adjusted earnings per share rose 10% to $0.54. The company also completed its acquisition of Momentum Midstream, expanding its presence in the Haynesville region, a major natural gas-producing area supplying Gulf Coast markets.

Management now expects adjusted EBITDA growth of more than 11% annually through 2030. Williams also pays a dividend, giving you a source of income while the company pursues additional growth.

Of course, pipeline development faces regulatory hurdles, construction costs, and potential delays. Williams also carries significant debt, making financing costs worth monitoring. But unlike companies whose fortunes depend primarily on natural gas prices, Williams earns substantial revenue from transporting and handling the commodity. That is a significant strategic advantage.

I'd give GE Vernova the edge for aggressive growth investors, provided its valuation leaves room for reasonable returns. Its enormous backlog and accelerating equipment demand provide considerable earnings visibility.

NextEra Energy would be my choice if you're looking for renewable energy exposure, dividend income, and relatively predictable utility earnings. And Williams offers a compelling combination of natural gas infrastructure growth and income.

Ultimately, these companies aren't likely to make anyone rich overnight. But their expanding businesses could help investors build substantial wealth over the next decade. And with electricity demand expected to remain a major investment theme well beyond 2026, all three deserve consideration before the year ends.
