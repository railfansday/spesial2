Trump Just Disclosed Tesla and SpaceX Stock Purchases. Here's What His Portfolio Moves Reveal

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The president's latest financial disclosure includes purchases of Tesla and SpaceX, but the stocks he sold tell a more interesting story.

By Jonathan Ponciano – Nov 19, 2026 at 3:09PM EST

President Donald Trump's latest stock transactions are out, and yes, Tesla and SpaceX made the list.

On Thursday, the Office of Government Ethics made public a 25-page transaction report covering hundreds of transactions from September. It shows purchases of Tesla (TSLA -2.51%) and SpaceX (SPCX -4.82%) stock, along with sales of household names like McDonald's (MCD -0.80%) and Home Depot (HD -0.13%).

So how have the moves panned out, and what do they say about the billionaire president's portfolio? So far, the results are mixed: Several of the stocks Trump sold have outperformed the Tesla and SpaceX shares he bought, and the filing seems to indicate a shift toward dividends and bonds rather than a big bet on Elon Musk.

Reports like this one list each trade as a dollar range rather than an exact amount. Trump's four Tesla purchases, made between Sept. 1 and Sept. 10, total between $117,004 and $330,000. His two SpaceX stock purchases add up to $16,002 to $65,000. He also bought $100,001 to $250,000 in SpaceX bonds that pay 5.35% interest and mature in 2031.

The president also had much bigger moves in the filing. On Sept. 23 alone, Trump sold shares of McDonald's, Home Depot, Comcast (CMCSA -1.96%) and Fidelity National Information Services (FIS -3.69%) worth $1,000,001 to $5 million apiece. He also sold Nike (NKE -5.74%) and Constellation Brands (STZ -1.57%) stock worth $500,001 to $1 million each. The single largest transaction in the report, made the same day, was a purchase of $5,000,001 to $25 million of the Vanguard Dividend Appreciation ETF (VIG -1.74%).

Here's how each has done since the day of the transaction, based on that day's closing price and Thursday afternoon trading, according to Nasdaq data. The filing doesn't give the exact prices Trump paid or received.

Four of the six big stocks Trump sold have climbed 10% or more since then. But the FIS sale dodged a 25% drop, marking the best call in the batch. In other words, it's a mixed record, like most investors', and of course, these are just short-term movements. Long-term the picture can look vastly different.

There are three big problems with treating this filing as a stock-picking guide.

The first is timing. These transactions happened in September and became public about eight weeks later. Anyone who copied the McDonald's and Home Depot sales on Thursday would be selling after those stocks had already rallied.

The second is size. Because transactions are reported in ranges, a $1,001 Tesla purchase and a $15,000 one look identical. It's hard to tell a big bet from routine housekeeping.

The third is motive. The filing doesn't say who chose these transactions or why. Many of them involve funds and municipal bonds, which make the account look more like a rebalancing portfolio than a series of handpicked bets.

Tesla and SpaceX are two of the most talked-about tech stocks on the market, and a presidential purchase makes a fun headline. But the biggest message in Trump's filing is a lesson in diversification: a large move into a dividend fund, trims to individual stocks, and lots of bonds.

If you like Tesla or SpaceX, buy them because you believe in the businesses and can hold through the swings, not because they showed up on a government disclosure form.
