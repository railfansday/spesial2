The $120K on a $5.8M portfolio is a no brainer on the topic’s question of whether you have enough and can stop. Your task is picking a cashflow process for the years when you are at age 51-59.5, and if other extracurricular financial activities such as Roth conversions are desired in early retirement. ACA premium tax credit could be another goal, so there is some work here for you to figure out.

I estimate all of your taxable fund distribution add up to $46,946 annually ($24,760 being tax-exempt). This means in year one you will have to draw down on the taxable accounts for either the $75K number or the $120K number.

Regardless of how much income is realized, you have the benefit of knowing your state tax is always at 3.07%. I am in CA where the brackets are progressive. As far as deciding on your VPALX that distributes the annual $24,760, you should decide on what activities you want in the years before 59.5 as they lower your AGI which counts against Roth conversions by filling up ordinary income space. I kept my CA municipal funds, but you will have a decision to make depending on if you want more OI space for Roth conversions, or to take the loss to offset gains for ACA PTCs. Congratulations!
