Fani Willis asks to rejoin case against Trump over $16 million fee

ATLANTA (CN) â Fulton County District Attorney Fani Willis asked the same court that disqualified her from the case against President Donald Trump to let her rejoin to fight nearly $17 million sought in legal fees on Tuesday.

Because the Georgia Court of Appeals previously determined that Willis’ office was “wholly disqualified” due to a conflict of interest, the Fulton County judge overseeing the case denied her a seat at the table.

In March, Fulton County Superior Court Judge Scott McAfee ruled that Willis cannot return to defend her past charging decisions, as the appeals court determined her continued involvement would undermine public confidence in the integrity of the proceedings.

But Willis argued to a Georgia Court of Appeals panel that this matter is distinct and her intervention is necessary. She claims barring her office from the litigation violates “fundamental notions of due process” by denying her an opportunity to be heard or to challenge the reasonableness of the claimed attorney fees before they are taken from her budget.

“Your briefing suggests that you are entitled to some process being the district attorney, but I think we’ve held repeatedly that government is not entitled to due process,” Judge J. Wade Padgett said.

Senior Assistant District Attorney Donald Wakeford said the interests of Fulton County taxpayers are imperiled, as those funds are expected to be allocated towards the district attorney’s prosecution of crimes.

He also said the Georgia law at issue “has created a mess.”

The new law, known as Senate Bill 244, awards reasonable attorneyâs fees and costs to a criminal defendant if a motion to disqualify the prosecuting attorney for misconduct succeeds and results in the dismissal of the case.

It was passed in May 2025, after Willis was removed from the case. The law also provides a financial lifeline to the wrongfully convicted by guaranteeing individuals “$75,000 for every year of wrongful imprisonment.”

Wakeford emphasized that Judge McAfee noted in his ruling that the statute does not expressly address whether the disqualified office may still play any role in the proceedings.

“Why is the district attorney’s interests not adequately represented by Fulton County when they are gonna fund it anyways?” Judge Kenneth Hodges asked.

In McAfee’s order, the judge held that Fulton County, as a corporate entity, could participate in the awards fight as it provides the “overwhelming source of funding” for the district attorney’s office and has a unique interest in preventing its shuttering as the sole prosecuting authority for all felonious conduct that occurs within its borders.

He also concluded that the county will bring a different perspective than the state with its knowledge of budgetary issues, local government and the structural interplay between it and the district attorneyâs office.

Trump and the 13 other defendants named in the sprawling racketeering case brought by Willis in August 2023 claim the Fulton County DA’s interests are already represented in the matter.

They argued that the executive director of the Prosecuting Attorneysâ Council, Peter Skandalakis, who appointed himself district attorney pro tempore after Willis’ removal, represents the state of Georgia, which includes Fulton County.

Skandalakis ultimately dropped all charges last year, citing the interests of justice and a sitting president’s legal immunity.

He, too, has challenged the $16.8 million in legal fees sought from the defendants, as well as the constitutionality of the newly enacted law forcing counties to foot the bill.

Trump alone is asking the court for more than $6.2 million back, claiming he paid over $1.5 million just to lead attorney Steve Sadow.

The three-judge state appeals panel includes Judge Elizabeth Gobeil. They did not signal when they will release a decision.

McAfee will then have to consider arguments and navigate uncharted procedural waters to decide whether the millions of dollars in requested fees, spanning 14 different defendants, is reasonable.
