Fiona the hippo under veterinary care after eating hat at Cincinnati zoo

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Knit hat caught in ceiling fan and flung into enclosure eaten by Fiona, who is being closely monitored by zoo staff

The Cincinnati zoo says Fiona, its beloved hippo who became a global celebrity after her premature birth nearly a decade ago, is undergoing veterinary care after swallowing a knit hat that got into her enclosure.

Zoo veterinarians and staff are monitoring Fiona and working to help her pass the hat, the zoo said in a statement Monday.

“Her care team is cautiously optimistic that she will pass it and return to her usual self,” the zoo said. The zoo said she received fluids and an enema Sunday to help move things through her system.

The cap got into Fiona’s habitat after it was tossed into the air by a visitor and caught by a ceiling fan that flung it through a small opening above the area’s viewing windows, said zoo spokesperson Michelle Curley.

“We probably couldn’t make [it] happen again if we tried,” zoo spokesperson Michelle Curley told Axios in a statement, emphasizing it was an accident. Fiona will be off the exhibit until she is in better health.

Fiona was born six weeks premature in January 2017, and her fight to survive grabbed international attention.

She initially could not stand on her own and weighed just 29lbs (13.2kg) at birth, 25lbs (11.3kg) less than the lowest recorded birth weight for her species.

Fans followed her progress online, and she became so popular that books were written about her. She also inspired a line of beer, all sorts of clothing and an ice cream flavor.

One couple even got engaged in front of her exhibit, with Fiona getting into the picture while underwater in the background.

Other zoos contacted Fiona, showing concern for her health. Timothy, the hippo at the San Antonio zoo sent well wishes via Instagram. “I know you’ve got some amazing people looking after you. We’re sending lots of love from Texas to the most beautiful hippo in the world. Hope you’re feeling more like yourself soon!”

The zoo said in a social media post it will share more information as they get it, also reminding visitors to be careful when near habitats and to secure belongings.

“If something falls into a habitat, please notify a zoo staff member immediately,” the zoo said.
