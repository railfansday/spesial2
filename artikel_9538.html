How we stopped LLMs from writing fake Armenian: a deterministic gate for AI-translated lessons

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

I'm building Samevia from Stepanavan, a small town in northern Armenia. It teaches whole fields of knowledge, from mathematics to psychology, as short lessons in your own language, on the web and on Android. It is free to start and works offline.

Today it has 460 lessons across 16 fields. 429 are published in English and 269 in Armenian. Every one of them was drafted and translated by LLMs. This post is about the part that made that possible without shipping garbage: a small, boring, deterministic gate.

Armenian has its own alphabet. When we started translating lessons with smaller models, many outputs looked like translations to anyone who doesn't read Armenian, and were unusable to anyone who does:

An LLM judge did not catch these reliably. A regex did.

Every translated leaf (a heading, a paragraph, a quiz option) goes through the same checks before it is accepted:

If a check fails, the leaf is not "fixed up". It is sent to the next provider in the chain. Free-tier models go first; a paid model is only a backstop at the end. That ordering is why the product can be free.

We log every translation attempt with its outcome (ok, still_invalid, script_leak, error) and the model that produced it, and alert when the hourly success rate drops below 80%. That alert is how we noticed, yesterday, that two models in the chain had started producing mostly invalid Armenian, while others were fine. Without per-model outcomes it would have looked like "translation is flaky".
