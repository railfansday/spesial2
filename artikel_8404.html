You have done well, but it seems 35% of your portfolio is MSFT. That is a very high concentration. Of course, that concentration doubled your investment in it over the last 3 years (but basically kept about even the last year).

You have about $5000K in your portfolio now (but that is before you count the tax liability). It could easily drop to $4000K or less before you can unwind the MSFT concentration.

At $4000K the 4% rule says you have $160K/year to spend and keep up with inflation and probably not run out of money in 30 years (most likely you would end up with about the same amount as a legacy, but definitely not guaranteed).

Since you are possibly/likely going to live more than 30 years, you should plan for longer (35, 40, 50?).

You should convert most (possibly all) your cash to what you want your AA to look like (VTI, probably is best). Convert some of the IRA to cash if you like the safety of cash.

Start selling MSFT now. Sell the lots that have the highest basis that are LTCG. Sell it a hundred shares at a time to make it easier. It is hard to know what is a good price, because that changes daily. A 100 shares a $10/share change is only $1K out of a $50K sale. The 52 week range has been something like 350-550. That is about $200K for only 1K shares. You likely have over 3K shares.

You can probably keep your Amazon shares if you still believe in them. They are a small enough part of your portfolio, you don't have to rush to diversify them.

There are some here who would say diversify now, you are crazy for taking that risk. They are not exactly wrong. Just look at what happened to NVDA over the last 6 months. It is down 30%. It might recover quickly or it might take a decade. The good news is the people who didn't diversify will either get a TLH opportunity or pay lower tax when they sell (but end up with less money than if they had just sold six months ago, when everyone said it was just going to keep going up).

You absolutely need to get clarity on all your expenses including taxes and healthcare and a plan for LTC. This will determine how close to being ready financially you are.

If emotionally you are ready to quit, you can make the finances work (you just might not really like what it takes to do that).

I did the one more year thing for several years because it made sense to me. I don't regret doing the math many different ways to really get comfortable with the fact that I had enough. I didn't want to make sacrifices in spending, so I kept working long enough to ensure I wouldn't need to do that.
