‘Much Ado About Nothing’ Broadway Review: Tom Hiddleston, Hayley Atwell & Jamie Lloyd Throw An Irresistible Party Of Shakespeare’s Great Comedy

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Outside of ticker tape parades and New Year’s Eve in Times Square, you might be hard-pressed to think of a better, more joyous use of floating paper than the confetti that piles up nightly on the stage of Broadway‘s Winter Garden Theatre these days. We’re talking blizzards of the pink stuff, so deep that the man known for playing that Marvel trickster Loki can all but bury his superhero frame in the drifts.

The new, infectious Much Ado About Nothing, Jamie Lloyd‘s inventive and joyous revival of the Shakespeare comedy opening tonight at the Winter Garden, turns those old falling drifts of thin paper into a special effect that, for sheer exuberance, more than holds its own against the cutting-edge projections and awesome digital flimflammery delighting audiences at any number of Broadway houses this season. Of course, even a confetti squall such as this wouldn’t impress a lick if the rest of Lloyd’s production couldn’t match its energy, and fortunately for us all this Much Ado About Nothing never lacks for giddy enchantment, from the Tony-worthy performances of stars Tom Hiddleston and Hayley Atwell to the irresistible choreography of Fabian Aloise that will likely leave audiences of this straight play convinced they’ve just witnessed one of the best new musicals on Broadway this season. Hiddleston, with his charm level cranked, and Atwell, best known to moviegoers for her Agent Carter of Marvel fame and here matching her fellow superhero beat for beat, play Benedick and Beatrice, two of Shakespeare’s most delightful characters, Loki-level tricksters who, as another character notes, engage in a “merry war” of plotting, planning and scheming in the name of love (though neither would admit to that motive, at least early on).

Lloyd, whose typically stark theatrical approach if on full view here but utilized to an effect wholly different than the shadows-and-light desolation of his A Doll’s House (starring Jessica Chastain) or Betrayal (which also paired him with Hiddleston), fashions this foray into Shakespeare as a 1990s-era dance club – at one point, the entire cast dons oversized, mascot-style headpieces (a chick, an octopus, a dog, a robot and more) suggesting so many ecstasy-fueled club kids packed into the Limelight.

The plot, of course, is the usual Shakespearean farce full of disguised identities, battles of the sexes, intricate schemes and star-crossed romances both helped and hindered by busybodies, eavesdroppers and nefarious in-laws. (In Shakespeare’s day, the word “nothing” carried a double meaning: In addition to our understood definition, “nothing” in its ye olde pronunciation carried a near-pun on the word “noting,” or gossip).

While productions of Shakespeare have long been interpreted with any number of contemporary spins and modern touches – such are the stock in trade of New York’s Shakespeare in the Park – Lloyd has come up with an approach that feels entirely fresh: Hiddleston, Atwell, the wonderful Mason Alexander Park and the rest of the cast move and sing to such jubilant dance and pop hits as Taylor Dayne’s “Tell It to My Heart,” C & C Music Factory’s “Gonna Make You Sweat (Everybody Dance Now),” Deee-Lite’s “Groove Is in the Heart,” and All-4-One’s “I Swear.” I knew we were in for a treat when Hiddleston’s Benedick and his boy crew made an entrance dancing to the Beastie Boys’ booming “Fight for Your Right (To Party).”

If period drama set to a more-or-less contemporary soundtrack doesn’t sound all that innovative – Six and Moulin Rouge! would like a word – Lloyd and his crack creative team (and his cast, of course) see to it that we pretty much forget all other contenders. With that ever-present pink confetti, a giant inflatable heart, character-accentuating modern dress (Soutra Gilmour did both the scenic design and the party-wear costume designs) and Jon Clark’s gorgeous lighting combine to create a dazzling experience. Like any dance club worth its cover charge, the sound design (by Ben and Max Ringham) is practically a showstopper in itself, with music thumping when it needs to thump and, when we need to hear dialogue, dropping to the sort of low-level thrum you might hear on the curb outside a packed club.

Hiddleston, whose last team-up with Lloyd was the austere Betrayal, is a revelation here, adding some incredibly hot dance moves to his overflowing bag of tricks. And he’s very, very funny (watch how he ever so deadpan blows a bit of stray confetti from his lips), playing off his movie star appeal with every sidelong glance and knowing grin seemingly directed to each person in the audience (he’s not above a quick show of gratitude when his seated admirers whoop their appreciation, as if his Benedick is welcoming everyone into his scheming maneuvers).

Atwell is every bit his equal, with Beatrice’s fiery, feminist mettle rendered as the true heart and brains of this tale. Both actor and role are this productions not-so-secret weapons – and yes, Lloyd and his cast are entirely in on the Marvel appeal going on here, so much so that Hiddleston-as-Loki and Atwell-as-Carter make brief appearances in the form of standees that had the audience at the reviewed performance all but levitating.

As good as they are – and they are – newcomer (to American audiences anyway) Mason Alexander Park as the sharp-tongued Margaret nearly walks off with the show, never more so than when, mic in hand, she/they performs “Tell It To My Heart” as if placing a dance-pop diva in 16th Century Milan is no more strange than wooing your heart’s desire.

Indeed, there’s no weak link in this cast, with James Phoon’s callow young Claudio swoon-worthy when he needs to be, infuriating when he’s a jerk; Tim Steed’s meddlesome Don John a hissable villain; and Gerald Kyd’s Don Pedro – that fair-weather father to the slandered Hero (a winning Mara Huf) – an appropriately garrulous, welcoming host (until he isn’t).

The grand appeal of this production is evident in just how much Hiddleston, Atwell and their compatriots seem to be enjoying themselves, and why not? So is everyone within the clarion-call-to-groove of those thumping, galvanizing dance beats.

Title: Much Ado About NothingVenue: Broadway’s Winter Garden TheatreWritten By: Willliam ShakespeareDirected By: Jamie LloydCast: Tom Hiddleston, Hayley Atwell, Mara Huf, Mika Onyx Johnson, Gerald Kyd, Forbes Masson, Phillip Olagoke, Mason Alexander Park, James Phoon, Tim SteedRunning Time: 2 hr 20 min (including intermission)
