'Football, bloody hell!' - How Man Utd bounced back from a woeful season to win their first Champions League title under Sir Alex Ferguson

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Scottish manager almost quit the Old Trafford club just a year before he made history with the likes of Yorke, Sheringham, Beckham, Keane and Schmeichel

"I can't believe it. I can't believe it." Alex Ferguson is rarely lost for words, but on May 26, 1999, the Scottish manager was left dumbstruck by Manchester United's staggering late comeback to win the Champions League final.

As the European decider against Bayern Munich at Camp Nou entered the 90th minute, United were on the verge of a heartbreaking defeat. Bayern were 1-0 up and standing firm in a close-fought match against the champions of England.

Then, in stoppage time the Red Devils got a corner. With nothing to lose and everything to gain, goalkeeper Peter Schmeichel ran up from his box to get in the mix.

"Can you f*cking believe him?" an aghast Ferguson asked assistant Steve McClaren as the Dane galloped up the field.

When David Beckham's corner flew deep into the box, chaos ensued. Bayern's failed clearance went straight to Ryan Giggs, whose poor shot found its way to substitute Teddy Sheringham and with what he called "a scruffy scuff" off of his sock, the forward knocked in the equaliser.

A drained and exhausted Bayern kicked believing extra time was inevitable, but 30 seconds later United had yet another corner. Beckham's cross found Sheringham who diverted it across goal for Ole Gunnar Solskjaer to finish in the 93rd minute.

With just seconds remaining, Bayern had no chance of even equalising let alone restoring their advantage. For the first time since 1968, United were European champions and had completed a legendary Treble.

Yet that glorious moment in Barcelona - and the long spell of dominance that followed - almost never happened. Less than 12 months prior, the Old Trafford board came perilously close to pulling the trapdoor on Ferguson's reign.

A dismal season almost saw Ferguson sacked just before their iconic 1998-99 campaign

A catastrophic end to the 1997-98 campaign saw United blow a massive lead at the top of the Premier League to lose the title to Arsenal, finishing just one point behind the Londoners. United and Ferguson were in such disarray after the trophyless season that the manager almost quit.

A feud with chairman Roland Smith and chief executive Martin Edwards ensued as the latter feared that the Scot's recent rise to celebrity status had extinguished the fire within and that his passion for horse racing had become too much of a distraction.

"It happened so quickly. I felt Alex had taken his eye off the ball the previous season – he was reported to be at race meetings, although he'd likely argue it was the break from football he needed," Edwards said to FourFourTwo.

"I thought maybe we shouldn't have lost the title, and we hadn't won a trophy. We'd been winning trophies consistently, so it was to try to get him back on track."

Smith and Martins expressed their disappointment directly to Ferguson and, as most would expect of the fiery Govanite, he went ballistic.

"He praised my capacities as a manager but said my success at Old Trafford had made me a celebrity. I was not focusing on the job as much as I had done," Ferguson wrote in his autobiography.

"My new found interest in racehorses had obviously not gone down well.

"I could hardly believe what I was hearing but let them make their points before asking them: 'Do you want me to call it a day?'

"They both said 'no, no' quickly, insisting they were only trying to offer me advice."

"He was furious, saying: 'If that's what you think of me then get stuffed... I am resigning'. And off he went," Edwards told The Daily Mail. "I can't say I was particularly concerned as I thought he would come back."

Edwards accepted the resignation but was confident Ferguson would soon back down. By the end of the day, the manager had retracted his threat.

"Happily, I was right," said Edwards. "He had obviously had a word with somebody. It could have been his solicitor who would have told him that if he resigned he wouldn't be entitled to any money. But I don't know, I am speculating.

"But there is no point in worrying. If you feel it's the right thing to do - and I know it was - then you have to take the consequences. He was going to respond one way or the other and I would have been hugely unpopular had he walked."

It wasn't the first time United and Ferguson had come close to splitting up. In 1990, after four years in charge, fans were screaming for him to be sacked. Edwards resisted the pressure and Ferguson delivered the FA Cup that year, his first trophy with the Red Devils.

The following season, they won the Cup Winners' Cup, beating Johan Cruyff's Barcelona in the final, and League Cup runners-up, going on to win that competition and the European Super Cup in the 1991-92 campaign. United won their first Premier League title under Ferguson the season after that, then in 1994 a League Cup final defeat to Aston Villa deprived them of a clean sweep of domestic trophies. Two years later, making a mockery of Alan Hansen's famous "you can't win anything with kids" comment, they claimed another league and cup double.

By the time Ferguson had exploded at Smith and Edwards, the ex-Aberdeen manager had become bulletproof at Old Trafford.

After the animosity died down, the Red Devils' rejuvenation began with three significant signings. Towering defender Jaap Stam was signed from PSV for around £11 million, Dwight Yorke made the £13m move after a prolific period with Aston Villa and midfielder Jesper Blomqvist arrived from Parma. The trio added further strength to a side consisting of Andy Cole, Beckham, Roy Keane, Paul Scholes and Gary Neville, among others, but the early signs were troubling.

After Arsenal humiliated them in the Charity Shield, United drew their first three games and slumped to 13th in the Premier League.

United were dealt difficult opponents as they kicked off their European campaign

United had brightened up from their dim domestic start by the time the Champions League kicked off, but few gave them a chance in a difficult group containing Barcelona, Bayern and Brondby.

Barcelona had the tactical swagger of Louis van Gaal, who brought in a few European conquerors from his famous Ajax team - Patrick Kluivert, the De Boer brothers and Michael Reiziger - to team up with Rivaldo, Luis Figo and Pep Guardiola. Bayern, marshalled by Ottmar Hitzfeld, brought pragmatism in the shape of Stefan Effenberg, Lothar Matthaus, Markus Babbel and Oliver Khan, among others. Even Danish side Brondby couldn't be written off as group whipping boys after they beat Bayern 2-1 in the first group stage game.

Ferguson's side enjoyed a brilliant start at Old Trafford against Barca, racing to a two-goal lead within 25 minutes through Giggs and Scholes, but the visitors pulled level in the second half before Beckham restored the Red Devils' advantage. Then, Luis Enrique stepped up for his side's second penalty of the night to salvage a point.

Despite conceding early on against Bayern, the English side put in another solid display, with Yorke and Scholes turning the game around before Giovane Elber's late strike denied them the win.

Against Brondby, though, the Red Devils showed their class. A 6-2 victory in Copenhagen was followed by a 5-0 trouncing in Manchester, while Bayern beat Barca both times to keep the English team in a strong position in the group.

Back then, finishing second in the group did not guarantee a spot in the next round - only the two best-ranked runners-up would go through, so the stakes were high heading into the last rounds at Camp Nou and Old Trafford.

In a similar fashion to their earlier meeting, United and Barca played out a 3-3 draw in the Catalan capital. This time, Sonny Anderson opened the scoring for the hosts immediately after kick-off. While Yorke and Cole soon had United leading, Rivaldo pulled Van Gaal's men level again before Yorke made it 3-2. Once more, Rivaldo struck to share the spoils.

Two weeks later, Keane gave Ferguson's team a first-half lead against the Munich side, but Hasan Salihamidzic earned a point for the visitors.

Unbeaten and with 10 points from the six matches, they finished a point behind Bayern but were able to scrape through as one of the top runners-up alongside Real Madrid.

While Bayern went on to meet domestic rivals Kaiserslautern in the next round, United were paired with an Inter team that carried Roberto Baggio, Youri Djorkaeff and Gianluca Pagliuca as well as Argentine duo Javier Zanetti and Diego Simeone.

It was a Beckham-Yorke masterclass in the first leg, with the midfielder setting up the forward for both goals in a 2-0 win before a draw in Manchester to set up a semi-final showdown with Juventus.

An "unbelievable" showdown against Juventus sent Fergie's men on to the decider

It was the third year in a row in which United had locked horns with Juventus. The Bianconeri had emerged victorious in the previous two occasions, scraping 1-0 wins each time. After Antonio Conte broke the deadlock 25 minutes into the first leg at Old Trafford and the stubborn Turin side continued to dominate, it looked like the pattern was to repeat itself.

"Juventus were the benchmark," Neville admitted years later. "We measured ourselves against them and I still look back on the team of Alessandro del Piero, Zinedine Zidane, Alen Boksic and Didier Deschamps as the best I ever faced. They had everything that I would love to have in my team."

Overpowering Carlo Ancelotti's team was no easy feat, but a 92nd minute strike from Giggs ensured United were still level ahead of the conclusion in Turin.

Juventus were the dominant force in the early stages of a feisty affair. A short one-two from a corner saw Zidane whip the ball towards the back post for Filippo Inzaghi to poke in. Five minutes later, the poacher received a pass as he ran into the United box and leathered it just as Jaap Stam stuck a foot in, sending it bouncing high over Schmeichel.

United began to claw their way back into the game, with Yorke combining well with Cole before sending a shot sailing over the bar. But it was midfielder and captain Keane who gave them reason to believe as he flew in at the front post to head Beckham's cross from a corner beyond Peruzzi. Ten minutes later, Neville's long pass to Beckham was headed down to Cole, who whipped it into the box and Yorke beat Ciro Ferrara to it with a fine diving header.

The teams exchanged chances over the next hour as they battled for the winning goal, with Inzaghi getting into great positions and seeing a goal disallowed for offside while Denis Irwin hit the post and then the side-netting with his follow up effort.

Then, in the 83rd minute, Yorke pounced on a woeful Juventus clearance and slipped between two defenders to go one-on-one with Peruzzi. He skipped past the goalkeeper but was brought down. Before a penalty could be awarded, Cole swooped in to tap into the empty net.

"Unbelievable, just unbelievable. To win the game how we won the game – phenomenal," Cole said after the 4-3 aggregate win.

"It's a proud, proud moment for me," Ferguson said. "My players were absolutely fantastic, absolutely magnificent. I thought the first 45 minutes was the best in my time as a manager… We gave them a start, didn't we?! But we recovered well, the players kept their composure, and I think they deserve to be there."

The game dealt two significant blows, however, as both Keane, who was phenomenal in that game in Turin, and Scholes received yellow cards and were banned from the final.

"It's a tragedy for them, I don't know whether we can appeal or what, but it would be tragic for them to miss the final. I feel for them… It's a fantastic night for me. This is the level we want to play at," the manager said.

An unbelievable ending to the final saw the Red Devils complete their treble at Bayern's expense

Like United, Bayern were still in contention to win a treble of trophies heading into the Champions League final at Camp Nou. They had already wrapped up the Bundesliga crown and were through to the DFB-Pokal final.

Their English opponents, though, were on a rampage. As the month of May began, they had lost their stranglehold on first place in the Premier League but regained it with two games to go thanks to Yorke's strike against Middlesbrough. A 0-0 draw with Blackburn on the penultimate day of the campaign, however, saw the title go down to the final showdown.

Tottenham took a shock lead at Old Trafford through Les Ferdinand, but Beckham struck the equaliser just before half-time and Cole delivered early in the second half. The visitors put up a good fight in a nerve-wracking finale, but Ferguson's men saw out the win.

Just like the previous year, it came down to one point, but this time United were ahead of Arsenal.

Six days later, goals from Teddy Sheringham and Scholes clinched the FA Cup against Newcastle at Wembley. With another domestic double under their belts, only the European crown stood between Ferguson's team and immortality. But the coach faced a selection headache on the way to Barcelona. With Keane and Scholes out, the Scot pushed Beckham into a central role alongside Nicky Butt, moved Giggs to the right wing and played Blomqvist on the left. Across the tunnel, Hitzfeld's Bayern stood ready, anchored by the veteran Matthaus sweeping behind defense.

It was a disastrous opening for United. Just six minutes in, Ronny Johnsen fouled Carsten Jancker outside the area, and Mario Basler curled a low free-kick past Peter Schmeichel into the bottom corner. For the next 80 minutes, Bayern suffocated United's improvised midfield and hit them on the counter. Mehmet Scholl chipped Schmeichel only to see the ball bounce off the post and Jancker saw bicycle kick smack the bar. With time running out, Ferguson made the key substitutions, bringing Sheringham on for Blomqvist in the 67th minute and Ole Gunnar Solskjaer for Dwight Yorke in the 81st.

By the 90th minute, Bayern's bench was already celebrating, and UEFA staff getting ready to carve the German club's name on the trophy.

"I was just starting to adjust to losing the game," Ferguson admitted. "I had reminded myself to keep my dignity and accept that it wasn't going to be our year. What then happened simply stunned me."

In the first minute of stoppage time, United won a corner. Schmeichel sprinted into the Bayern box to add chaos. Beckham swung the ball in, Bayern failed to clear and Giggs' scuffed shot went to the far post where Sheringham swept it home to pull United level.

Less than two minutes later, with Bayern still in shock, United won another corner. Beckham crossed, Sheringham flicked the header down across the face of the goal, and Solskjaer stuck out a boot to volley the ball into the roof of the net.

At the final whistle, Bayern players collapsed to the turf in tears. Samuel Kuffour pulverised the ground in frustration, later telling The Guardian: "I didn't mean to do that but when you are so down you can't control yourself. After the game I was just crying. I have never even watched the game, I don't want to see it."

Ferguson stood in disbelief before uttering the most iconic post-match verdict in football history: "Football, bloody hell."

The dramatic turnaround sealed an unprecedented Treble and earned Ferguson his knighthood.

A year on from a dismal season that almost brought a premature end to the most iconic managerial reign in football, Ferguson and Manchester United had solidified their place in history.
