Midterm elections 2026: AI data centers could decide races in these states

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Americans are flocking to the polls in record numbers ahead of nationwide midterm elections on November 3.

More than 54 million voters have already cast their ballots—voting early in-person and by mail. And as of late October, the early voter turnout numbers were higher than in both 2018 and 2022 at the same point in the election cycle—with predictions saying that early voting could make up two-thirds of all ballots cast.

Now, with just 1 day until the election, AI data centers have emerged as one of the hot topics in the midterms. As Brad Carson, head of Public First Action, told Fast Company, “There’s hardly a race in the country right now where data centers aren’t playing a role in some form.”

It is a key issue in both Ohio’s governor race and the Senate races in the Buckeye state, as well as in neighboring Michigan.

Ohio is one of 13 U.S. states that currently have AI data center-related measures on the ballot in multiple elections through the end of 2026. Some 84% of those 49 measures will be decided in the November midterms. Ohio has the most measures (21), trailed by Illinois (7), Maine (4), Wisconsin (3), Kansas (3), California (2), Arkansas (2), New Hampshire (2), Florida (1), Michigan (1), Nevada (1), New Jersey (1), and North Dakota (1).

Adding to that: In another 20 gubernatorial races (out of a total of 36), candidates are calling for a moratorium on AI data centers, according to Multistate, a policy tracking firm.

To understand why, you need only look at the latest polling: A staggering two-thirds of Americans oppose building new AI data centers, and 57% say artificial intelligence is bad for society—with one-third of those calling it “very” bad, according to The Washington Post.

And it’s not just Democrats that are running on anti-AI platforms. A number of Republican candidates are also trying to put some distance between themselves and President Donald Trump’s pro-AI policies, the Post reported.

That’s likely in part because 67% of Americans, both Republicans and Democrats, do not think the government is doing enough to regulate AI, according to the latest Washington Post-ABC News-Ipsos poll.
