Kai Havertz breaks Premier League scoring record, surpasses Man City legend in Liverpool vs. Arsenal

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

By Ben Knapton | 01 Nov 2026 17:11, Last updated: 01 Nov 2026 17:24

Arsenal striker Kai Havertz is now the Premier League's all-time leading German goalscorer following his opener against Liverpool.

The former Chelsea man was given the nod up front over Viktor Gyokeres at Anfield, despite a shoddy display in Tuesday's shock EFL Cup fourth-round loss to Fleetwood Town.

However, Havertz quickly repaid Mikel Arteta's faith in him, latching onto the end of Declan Rice's low cross to the back post and tapping home in the 16th minute.

In doing so, the 27-year-old broke the record for the most Premier League goals ever by a German player, registering his 46th in the English top flight.

Havertz was previously level on 45 with Ilkay Gundogan, but the former Manchester City man has now been surpassed, and the Arsenal centre-forward is in a league of his own.

Havertz's opening goal at Anfield was his third of the 2026-27 Premier League season, and his first on the road, following home strikes against Coventry City and Chelsea.

The former Blues striker scored 19 English top-flight goals for Chelsea, and his tap-in at Anfield marked his 27th in an Arsenal kit, including a personal best 13 in the 2023-24 season.

Havertz is one of three active players in the top 10 for Premier League goals for German footballers, as Brighton & Hove Albion's Pascal Gross is third in the charts with 34 to his name.

Kevin Schade of Brentford is eighth on the list with 24, while Gundogan, Mesut Ozil (33), Jurgen Klinsmann (29), Uwe Rosler (29), Leroy Sane (25), Robert Huth (21) and Lukas Podolski (19) make up the rest of the top 10.

Havertz's goal proved enough to send Arsenal into the half-time break with a 1-0 lead, although both Gunners and Liverpool fans were left bemused as to how.

Andoni Iraola's men dominated their North London counterparts following Havertz's historic goal, and had no fewer than four penalty appeals waved away by the officials.

One such incident saw Jurrien Timber grab Alexander Isak by the shirt as a cross came in, but the Swede did not protest vociferously, and the pull went unpunished.

Timber also escaped a potential handball when sliding to block a cross, while Alexis Mac Allister and Cody Gakpo also went down under pressure, but their appeals fell on deaf ears too.

However, Liverpool ultimately failed to score in the first half despite having 28 touches in the Arsenal box, the most the Gunners have ever faced in the opening 45 minutes of a Premier League game.
