'There's no getting away from it...' - Ex-refs chief re-analyses Jarell Quansah controversy vs Spain

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Spain's penalty and Elliot Anderson's red card should have been overturned during England's 3-2 Nations League victory.

That is according to former PGMOL chief and ex-FIFA referee, Keith Hackett, who exclusively told Football Insider that Jarell Quansah was fouled in the build-up to the game's most contentious moment.

In the 63rd minute, the game was flipped on its head, as Clement Turpin was left with no other choice but to send off Anderson.

Just before he pushed Lamine Yamal over in the box, though, Quansah had been dispossessed near the halfway line, allowing a quick Spanish break.

As Hackett pointed out previously, the Manchester City man could have avoided a red card by simply making a genuine attempt for the ball.

Either way, the situation probably should have been avoided, with the Three Lions deserving of a free-kick for the foul on Quansah by Yamal.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Hackett that, while England deserved a free-kick, at least the referee was consistent with his decisions.

"There's no getting away from it; that is a foul. However you look at it, in trying attempting to play the ball, it's also the man," he said.

"So it's a very easy foul. Football's a contact sport, and I think this is where the debate comes in. Given this referee and his skill set, he's not going come in on that.

"He's not going to give that. And because his, if you like, guide, his line of interference is much higher. Then he will say football is a contact sport and therefore it's okay to carry on.

"If you're in the local park refereeing, you're gonna give that as a foul. Look, England fans can complain.

"The reality is the same should have happened with Anderson. If you're going to give that, then you've got to give the foul from Anderson [on Marc Cucurella]."

On the two big decisions, Turpin was definitely consistent, but he let a few other things slide - ones that should have been called back, and vice versa.

Yamal was at the centre of multiple battles during the game, often throwing himself to the floor to win a cheap free-kick.

In the first half, the same happened up against Anderson. The Man City star won the ball cleanly, but because Yamal fell to the ground, a free-kick brought a halt to England's attack.

The referee also fell for Fabian Ruiz's play-acting in the penalty area, awarding Spain a free-kick after tussling with Anthony Gordon at a set-piece.
