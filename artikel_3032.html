Is IonQ Stock a Buy Right Now?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

IonQ has a long way to go before becoming a viable company.

By Keithen Drury – Nov 21, 2026 at 12:00PM EST

IonQ (IONQ -8.76%) is an intriguing stock. It hasn't done well in 2026 and is down nearly 50% this year and down around 70% from its all-time high. On the surface, that doesn't look promising. However, once investors wrap their heads around what is going on, it starts to look much more inviting.

IonQ is the biggest pure-play quantum computing investment available on the market, and its business continues to grow rapidly as demand for early-stage quantum computers arrives. But with the market seemingly not caring about this growth, the stock is cheap.

So, is now the perfect time to buy? I think so, as the market could care less about quantum computing right now.

IonQ is a pure-play quantum computing company, meaning it doesn't have any other businesses to fund its operations. So, it must produce a viable quantum computer or risk going bankrupt. That's a high-risk scenario, but the rewards will be immense if IonQ can pull it off.

The market selling off IonQ's stock during 2026 has little to do with how it's actually doing as a company. Instead, it is more of a reflection of the market's risk appetite. Investors have gotten wary of investing in higher-risk investments during 2026. AI is generally considered a lower risk than quantum computing because there's actually usable technology in that sector. So, with quantum computing being an even riskier segment than that, the market has heavily sold it off.

However, I think that makes the timing right to buy IonQ stock, as the time to buy is when there is no fanfare around the industry.

IonQ has been making great strides toward becoming a viable business. Next year, its 256-qubit Superion system launches, which will be one of the most powerful early-stage quantum computers available. It has several clients that have already preordered, and with $1.2 billion in revenue backlog, there is monster demand for this product.

However, IonQ isn't just focused on developing a viable quantum computer. Earlier this year, it acquired SkyWater, which is a semiconductor foundry. This helps decrease development time for IonQ and also gives it more control over its supply chain.

It also acquired Elisity, which will help IonQ pioneer quantum-focused cybersecurity. All of these moves were made to make IonQ a one-stop shop for all things quantum computing-related. It is a bold strategy and could turn it into the next tech giant if it works out, but there's no guarantee of success.

Investors must understand and manage risk with IonQ, as it is far from a surefire investment.

IonQ isn't the only company competing in this space. There are countless others, including several well-funded big tech companies. IonQ is also taking a different technological approach that prioritizes accuracy over computing speed. This may be an advantage in the early stages of quantum computing, as accuracy is one of the primary concerns for users. But if companies with faster processing speeds catch up to IonQ's accuracy levels, then IonQ could be in trouble.

The question of whether IonQ turns into a successful company pivots around how quickly it can launch its commercially viable quantum computer and how much of a head start (if any) it has on its competitors. That will determine the investing success of IonQ, and there's no way of knowing for sure if it will even reach the commercially viable state.

As a result, if you're going to invest in IonQ, then keeping position sizing small (say 1%) is a smart idea. I think IonQ is an excellent long-shot investment opportunity, but only if you have the mindset that this stock is more of a lottery ticket right now.
