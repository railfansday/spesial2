Baron International Growth Fund Q3 2026 Shareholder Letter

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Baron International Growth Fund (the Fund) declined 0.59% (Institutional Shares) during the third quarter of 2026, while its primary benchmark, the MSCI ACWI ex USA Index (the Benchmark), gained 0.48%. The MSCI ACWI ex USA IMI Growth Index (the Proxy Benchmark) declined 2.11% for the quarter. The Fund performed similarly to the Benchmark while outperforming the Proxy Benchmark during a quarter in which rising energy prices and a breakout in global bond yields favored value and commodity-oriented stocks over growth, evidenced by the MSCI ACWI ex USA IMI Value Index outperforming the comparable Growth Index by 566 basis points for the quarter, and now by 746 basis points for the year-to-date period.

In our view, the prevailing catalysts giving direction to markets were the increasingly pervasive influence of AI, which is now "crowding out" credit, resources and attention well beyond equity portfolios; the resumption of hostilities with Iran alongside an escalating Russia/Ukraine war; and a resulting breakout in nominal and real bond yields, which we believe is the critical variable for equity returns in the near term. While we are never comfortable trailing our core Benchmark, we are pleased to comfortably exceed the growth Proxy Benchmark and Morningstar Foreign Large Growth Category* (the Peer Group). Further, we suspect the recent historic rise in yields is likely overdone, while any reduction in energy risk premium, for which the upcoming U.S. mid-term elections could prove a trigger, would in our view drive a healthy mean reversion in relative returns. As always, we remain optimistic that our fundamental, theme-driven, and bottom-up approach can continue to deliver solid results over the long term.

In addition, poor stock selection in Industrials, owing to a handful of positions in our global security (Ohb Se (OHBTF) and Cummins India Limited), sustainability/ESG (Ceres Power Holdings plc (CPWHF) and Contemporary Amperex Technology Co (CYATY)., Limited), and semiconductors/AI (Innio N.V (INIO).) themes, was also a notable drag on relative results. Agilent Technologies, Inc. is a global provider of instruments, software, consumables, and services spanning life sciences, diagnostics, and applied chemical laboratories. Its shares outperformed during the quarter after fiscal third-quarter results handily beat expectations and the company raised its full-year guidance. Biopharmaceutical spending was strong, GLP-1-related demand is growing, and a broad slate of new products exceeded expectations. Orders tied to pharmaceutical reshoring also began arriving ahead of schedule. We retain conviction in Agilent, as its large installed base, expanding margins, and diversified launch cadence position it to compound through the life sciences tools recovery.

Shares of Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group, Inc. extended their gains in the third quarter of 2026 as investors grew more confident that Japan's move toward normalized interest rates is producing stronger, more sustainable earnings for the bank. Fiscal first-quarter profit jumped 48% year over year to Â¥809 billion, beating expectations, and the domestic loan spread widened from 0.95% to 1.15% on higher rates and healthy loan demand. Results were further lifted by a Â¥222 billion contribution from MUFG's stake in Morgan Stanley, along with higher trading revenue and asset management fees. Anticipation of additional rate hikes strengthened the outlook for loan margins, and the solid quarter helped MUFG participate in the broader rally in Japanese bank stocks. We continue to find MUFG attractive, as further rate normalization and improving loan demand should drive additional margin expansion and earnings growth.

Shares of Piraeus Bank S.A. rose during the third quarter of 2026, significantly outperforming the Greek bank sector average, driven by strong fundamentals, credit rating upgrades, and an index reclassification. On July 29, the bank reported record first-half 2026 net profit and a 16% return on tangible book value, with better-than-expected net interest margin and accelerating loan growth. Management responded by raising its 2026 net interest income guidance. Investment-grade ratings from all major rating agencies supported confidence in the bank's financial profile and drove further gains in the stock during July. A second leg of the rally followed in September, when Stoxx's reclassification of Greece as a developed market took effect, leading to Piraeus's inclusion in the STOXXÂ® Europe 600 Index. This structural demand catalyst attracted substantial passive and active fund inflows. Together, these developments built on an already favorable backdrop of accelerating earnings momentum, an improving credit profile, and a growing capacity to deliver shareholder returns.

South Korean semiconductor company SK hynix Inc. is a leading manufacturer of advanced memory chips. Shares declined sharply amid a broader sell-off in memory stocks as second-quarter results missed lofty forecasts, even as revenue more than tripled year-over-year and operating margin reached a record 76%. The shortfall was partly due to shipments of HBM4 high-bandwidth memory for AI chips slipping into later quarters. Management responded by launching a 40 trillion won share buyback and cancellation program in August and pledging to return more than half of cumulative free cash flow through 2027, with long-term agreements with around 10 key customers adding revenue visibility. We retain long-term conviction in SK hynix given its position as the current leader in high-bandwidth memory, large net cash position, and growing base of contracted customers.

Tokyo Electron Limited is a Japanese company that makes the equipment chip factories use to manufacture semiconductors, with leading positions in deposition, etch, and cleaning tools. Its shares detracted from performance during the quarter, as chip-equipment makers proved among the most sensitive to any doubt about the durability of AI spending. The company's own results argued the opposite. Fiscal first-quarter sales and gross profit reached records, management raised its outlook for the wafer-fab-equipment market on AI data center demand, and the company declared a record interim dividend while continuing a sizable share buyback. Even so, the shares were swept into a sharp late-July sell-off in Japanese AI stocks ahead of big-tech earnings, and chip-equipment stocks globally came under renewed pressure in September amid calls to pace the development of frontier AI. We retain conviction in Tokyo Electron as a core long-term holding. We view its exposure to advanced-logic and high-bandwidth memory process intensity, together with strong capital returns, as durable advantages that support multi-year earnings power.

Chinese chip-design company Montage Technology Co., Ltd. is the world's largest supplier of memory-interface chips, which enable high-speed communication between server processors and memory. Shares fell due to a company-specific overhang despite continued fundamental strength. In July, South Korean prosecutors opened an investigation into suspected price coordination involving Montage and two competitors. Montage said it is cooperating fully, and no charges have been filed. Meanwhile, the business continued to accelerate, with first-half revenue growing 27% year-over-year and second-quarter revenue increasing 33%, ahead of expectations. Rising adoption of DDR5, the latest generation of server memory, lifted shipments of newer-generation memory-interface chips, while emerging products supporting high-speed server connectivity and data movement continued to gain traction. Alongside these positive developments, the company proposed a share buyback. We retain long-term conviction and continue to view Montage's leading market share, rising interconnect content per AI server, and broadening product portfolio as durable advantages supporting multi-year earnings growth.

During the third quarter, we added a few new investments to existing themes and increased our weighting in certain positions established in prior periods. We endeavor to increase concentration in our highest conviction ideas.

We increased exposure to our biotechnology/diagnostics theme by initiating a position in Bachem Holding AG (BCHMF), a Switzerland-headquartered contract development and manufacturing organization (CDMO) specializing in the development and manufacture of synthetic peptide and oligonucleotide active pharmaceutical ingredients. The company serves leading globalpharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, including Eli Lilly (LLY) and Novo Nordisk (NVO). In our view, Bachem is a key beneficiary of the structural growth in peptide drug demand, driven by continued GLP-1 penetration, a shift toward higher-dosage therapies including oral formulations and dual-/triple-agonists, and the development of non-GLP-1 blockbuster peptide drugsâfor example in autoimmune and oncologyâwhich require substantially higher peptide dosages. As U.S. tariff uncertainty and biosecurity concerns around Chinese CDMOs prompt originators to reshore or friend-shore peptide production, Bachem's combination of Swiss manufacturing heritage and U.S. domestic facilities positions it as a natural beneficiary to gain market share. We are also encouraged by the company's ongoing capacity expansion to nearly triple its production by 2030, backed by long-term minimum revenue commitment contracts and prepayments, which provide strong visibility into topline growth over the medium term. Based on our estimates, we believe Bachem could deliver revenue growth of approximately 20% per year and EBITDA growth of 20% to 25% per year, on a compounded basis, over the next three to five years.

Adding to our digitization theme, we accumulated a position in Shopify Inc., a software services provider that offers e-commerce capabilities for millions of businesses, from first-time entrepreneurs to established brands, across multiple platforms, including online, in stores, on social media, and increasingly through AI agents. Storefront, checkout, payments, shipping, and financing all run on one platform, so a merchant can launch in a day and scale their business with lower operational friction. We view Shopify as the operating system for entrepreneurship at a time when the barriers to starting a brand have collapsed. Because Shopify earns a share of what its merchants sell, it grows as its best merchants grow, and successful merchants tend to stay; retention rises with customer size, and the cohort of merchants that joined in early 2015 now generates multiples of the quarterly sales it did at the start. In our view, contrary to current market perception, AI is increasingly becoming a tailwind for business growth as AI shopping assistants recommend products based on fit rather than advertising budgets, which favors Shopify's long tail of independent brands. In addition, Shopify's catalog is now available inside leading AI agents, including OpenAI's ChatGPT and Meta's newly launched Muse. We see a long runway for growth as more merchants adopt Shopify Payments (already used on 68% of gross merchandise value), international sales expand, larger enterprises move onto the platform in weeks rather than years, and newer business-to-business and in-store offerings scale. Together with continued margin expansion, we expect durable growth in free cash flow, which bodes well for long-term shareholder returns.

During the quarter, we initiated positions in Neste Oyj (NTOIY) and Golar LNG Limited (FLNG) as part of our sustainability/ESG and global security themes. Neste is the world's leading producer of renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel (SAF). The company hydrotreats waste and residue fats and oils, such as used cooking oil and animal fat, into drop-in fuels that run in existing engines and fueling infrastructure without modification. Its proprietary refining technology can process a wide range of lower quality waste and residue feedstocks, with its complex refineries located across Finland, the Netherlands, Singapore, and California. Neste, which began as a traditional oil refiner, now generates nearly half of its revenue and over 70% of its EBITDA from renewable products. Tightening regulation continues to lift demand. Germany's adoption of stricter EU renewable fuel legislation should add roughly 1.5 million tons of renewable diesel demand in 2026, which is being further boosted by higher U.S. biofuel mandates. Renewable diesel is priced off fossil diesel, so Neste has also benefited from tight diesel markets after the closure of the Strait of Hormuz pushed up energy prices. As a result, renewable product margins more than tripled year-over-year to a record in the second quarter of 2026. The company's Rotterdam expansion, which is expected to ramp up in mid-2027, will more than double that site's production footprint, lifting the company's total SAF capacity to 2.2 million tons. We believe Neste's scale and diversified waste feedstock supply chain give it a unique cost advantage over smaller producers, and we expect earnings to grow sustainably as Rotterdam scales and European SAF mandates step up.

Golar is a pioneer in floating liquefied natural gas (FLNG) production. FLNG vessels turn natural gas into liquefied natural gas (LNG) at sea, letting resource owners monetize stranded and flared gas without costly onshore terminals. Since we last owned the stock, Golar has exited its legacy shipping and power businesses to become a pure-play FLNG company. Following the disruption of LNG exports through the Strait of Hormuz, buyers in Europe and Asia are seeking a third source of supply beyond the U.S. Gulf Coast and the Middle East. We believe Golar offers the fastest route to that supply, since it converts existing LNG carriers into FLNG vessels, delivering new liquefaction capacity faster and at meaningfully lower cost than newbuild alternatives. The company has also locked up capacity at the only two shipyards with FLNG conversion experience, while competing newbuild yards cannot deliver before 2031. Argentina is emerging as one such source, and Golar's two vessels there will start liquefying gas from the Vaca Muerta shale formation in 2027 and 2028 under 20-year charters that also pay more when LNG prices rise. In August 2026, Golar ordered a fourth vessel for delivery in 2029, years ahead of any competing unit, and we expect Golar to charter it on attractive financial terms. We believe the share price ascribes little value to the fourth vessel and beyond or to the underlying commodity upside. We expect Golar's share of liquefaction capacity to grow roughly sixfold between 2026 and 2030 as these vessels come online, driving about a fivefold increase in earnings per share.

We also added to our semiconductor/AI theme by building a position in MediaTek Inc., the world's largest supplier of consumer and mobile system-on-chips. The company is undergoing a vector change in its growth trajectory as it transforms into one of the leading custom silicon partners for AI data centers. As AI workloads mature, the largest buyers increasingly require silicon designed around their proprietary workloads rather than a general-purpose merchant graphics processing unit. MediaTek sizes the market for this custom silicon, known as application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs), at approximately $70 billion to $80 billion by 2027, and expects to garner 15% to 20% market share. Designing ASICs demands exactly the capabilities MediaTek has spent decades building for its core business: leading-edge process expertise, high-speed interface and serializer/deserializer related intellectual property born out of its connectivity heritage, and a design centric organization that can tape out complex chips efficiently and at cost. That combination is rare. Two U.S. players have dominated the ASIC market globally, and a major U.S. cloud provider, widely reported to be Google, has anointed MediaTek as the credible third. MediaTek's first accelerator for this customer enters production in the fourth quarter, and management expects more than $2 billion of data center revenue this year, with a second, more powerful chip reaching volume production in early 2028. Management also expects the ASIC business to lift operating margins as it scales. Beyond the AI data center, the company also stands to benefit from the proliferation of AI on mobile devices, smart TVs, smart homes, and autonomous mobility related applications.

The third quarter of 2026 proved a period of consolidation for international and emerging markets (EM) equities, with the MSCI ACWI ex USA Index finishing roughly flat following a strong first half. In our view, the prevailing catalysts giving direction to markets were the increasingly pervasive influence of AI, the resumption of hostilities with Iran alongside an escalating Russia/Ukraine war, and a resulting breakout in global bond yields.

AI remained front and center, though in our view the impact is shifting from a market phenomenon to a regulatory and macroeconomic one. As we noted last quarter, AI fever has "crowded out" many attractive industries, stocks, and even entire countries; this crowding out now extends well beyond equity portfolios. AI-related investment is estimated to account for a quarter to a third of U.S. GDP growth this year, competing with the broader economy for capital, power, memory/compute, and skilled labor. Morgan Stanley estimates that AI-related debt issuance is on pace to approach $570 billion this year, more than double last year's paceâa meaningful new source of duration supply which in our view is helping drive bond yields higher. We remain convinced of AI's transformative potential, and EM remains the principal supplier of leading-edge logic, memory, and other critical components necessary to deliver compute capacity. However, when a single theme represents this much of economic growth, credit demand, market weight, and investor and public attention, we believe it is wise to remain on guard with respect to the various emerging political, regulatory, and commercial developments that could temper market enthusiasm, while also prioritizing prudent portfolio diversification.

Regarding geopolitics, last quarter we cautioned that risk premium and oil prices might not fall as far or as fast as anticipated following the cease-fire between the U.S. and Iran. Indeed, hostilities resumed in July, and Iran's de facto closure of the Strait of Hormuz and a retaliatory U.S. blockade remain in place, while Brent again hovers near $100 per barrel. Meanwhile, Ukraine's campaign against Russian refining has cut processing to its lowest level since 2005, halting Russian fuel exports and tightening global diesel markets. The result, in our view, is an unsustainable equilibrium of "neither war nor peace, " with energy anxiety feeding directly into inflation expectations and bond yields. We suspect the November 3 U.S. mid-term elections could likely prove a trigger for resolution. The Administration's sensitivity to energy prices is evident in President Trump's public pressure on Ukraine to halt refinery strikes, as well as his signal that renewed action against Iran is possible after the vote. Meanwhile, Trump's leverage may soon diminish should the anticipated shift in Congressional balance manifestâarguing for a resolution sooner than later. Whether through negotiation or escalation, a reduction in energy risk premium would be a meaningful positive for international and growth equities in general, and in our view, would likely trigger some mean reversion in relative performance.

This brings us to what we believe is the critical variable for equity returns in the nearer term: the breakout in nominal and real bond yields. In our year end letter, we suggested 2026 could represent a major test of Fed independence. After months of merely threatening the will to do what is necessary, Fed Chairman Warsh has gestured at orthodoxy over accommodation, raising rates in September for the first time since 2023âin our view as a result of the year-to-date inflationary impact of the Iran war and, to a lesser extent, tariffs. Meanwhile, the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield has breached its 20-year resistance to reach 5.3%. The move has been driven predominantly by real yields, now approaching 3%, while measures of breakeven inflation remain contained. In other words, markets are pricing not an untethering of longer-term inflation expectations but a structurally higher cost of capital, which challenges equity valuations broadly, and long-duration growth assets in particular. In the near term, should energy prices ease post the U.S. election or should circumstances slow or defer the growth in capital investment related to AI data center development, we would expect yields to retrace meaningfully, broadening market participation and driving a healthy mean-reversion in relative returns. For now, we are watching the bond market closely as we suspect the recent historic rise in yields is likely overdone. For the longer-term, the pressure on sovereign yields is by no means concentrated in the U.S., though we believe the return of the bond vigilantes will likely pressure those jurisdictions with the least credibility on deficit consolidation, and here sovereigns such as France, the U.K., and the U.S. stand out (Brazil not mentioned as its fiscal outlook is likely to improve pending second-round election results). The age of the savings glut and kicking the can down the road are giving way to a shortage of capital and a more discerning capital allocation functionâwhich is how it should be and actually a good thing.
