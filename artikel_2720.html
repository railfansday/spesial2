Finally a glimpse of what the Eagles' defense is supposed to look like

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Finally a glimpse of what the Eagles' defense is supposed to look like originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

This is what it's supposed to look like. Terrific against the run, relentless pressuring the quarterback, numbers to the ball, tight pass coverage, sure tackling.

It's a brand of Eagles defense we just haven't seen enough of this year.

On another day when the offense had a tough time scoring points, the defense carried the team to its first win in two months, 19-16, over the Steelers at the Linc. The Eagles improved to 3-7, ending their seven-game losing streak.

"I thought we played well," Cooper DeJean said. "We didn't need to give up the late touchdown. But we put together a solid game. And now we've got to keep stacking. We're going to continue to do this. We're going to fix things that we need to fix."

The Eagles held the Steelers to 255 yards – 60 below their season average – and 3.7 yards per carry – a full yard per carry below their season average. Aaron Rodgers threw for 227 yards but needed 44 passes to get there. That's the fewest yards he's ever thrown for in 32 career games where he's thrown at least 44 passes.

After allowing 27 points and 392 yards per game during the first six games of the seven-game losing streak, the defense has put together back-to-back solid performances, allowing 19 points and 249 points per game in a loss to the Giants and 19 and 255 in their first win in two months.

After recording just 10 sacks in their first eight games, they have 11 in the last two. And after allowing 132 rushing yards per game through eight games, they've now allowed just 77 per game the last two.

Every way you measure it, they've started to figure things out.

Sunday at the Linc, they were at their best, shutting down a Hall of Fame quarterback and the NFL's rushing average leader.

"We just came to play," said Jonathan Greenard, who had one of the Eagles' five sacks. "Stopped the run early. Got to make them get out of what they were comfortable being in. And we just executed when we needed to.

"Sometimes, man, you need to have a little bad side or something negative or a little bit of adversity to hit a little bit to kind of show you that, hey, man, just because you wear this Eagles uniform, they're not just lay down solely because of the history or the past success you have.

"I mean, this is an innovative league, so (if) you're not on that steady progressive upward trend, you get passed up. So I think that it just kind of took a little bit of that, sadly. And it happens around the league sometimes. I've been on teams where it happened like that. And I think we just responded well."

Considering the Eagles just traded away their best run stopper, the Steelers' lack of success running the ball was especially notable.

Jaylen Warren came into the day leading the NFL with 5.7 yards per carry. He ran nine times for just 28 yards with a long gainer of 11 yards.

"It helps out the whole game when you can stop the run and make a team pass," Jeremiah Trotter said. "Then you've got your guys that can rush and get sacks and put pressure on the QB. It really just makes the game look a whole lot easier when you're going to stop the run."

Maybe the biggest story of this game was how the defensive front without Jordan Davis controlled the line of scrimmage, shutting down the run and pressuring Rodgers.

Byron Young, Gabe Davis and Ty Robinson all played more than they have been and Young picked up two sacks and Davis one and all three played a part in stuffing Pittsburgh's vaunted running game. Young came in with only 2 ½ career sacks and nearly doubled that and Davis, a practice squad call-up, had the first of his career.

"It was amazing," Moro Ojomo said. "You know, at the end of the day, it was sad to lose J.D. But some other guys got an opportunity, you saw it today with Byron, you know, Byron's hungry, Ty's hungry, right? Even Gabe. So it was amazing to see those guys succeed."

There have been some ugly Sunday afternoons for this defense. The Bears put up 27 points and 375 yards. The Rams 24 and 415. It was 25 and 399 for the Panthers, 38 and 416 for the Cowboys, 27 and 339 for the Commanders.

But this is a proud group, and the last two weeks and especially Sunday against the Steelers they've held their own and showed that there's still some juice on this defense.

The Cowboys are up next Thursday in Arlington, Texas, and considering they're averaging 37 points and 421 yards in their last five games, this won't be easy. But maybe the Eagles have discovered something finally on that side of the ball.

"I mean if you have a sense of pride you got a little bit of pride, you ain't just gonna sit there and take a beating you know what I mean?" Ojomo said.

"What type of man are you? What do you represent? Who are the people that you're setting an example for? Your family members, your kids, whatever it may be. You know what I mean? You want to be able to look those people in the eyes and know that you did your best."
