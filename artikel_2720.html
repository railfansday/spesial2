Should Baker Mayfield sit if he’s hurt?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Tampa Bay fans caught wind of a story line they have become all too familiar with in 2026— Baker Mayfield is hurt. Even worse than Mayfield being injured, the injury does have potential to make him miss time, namely this week's game against the Detroit Lions. Mayfield did take an awkward hit in last week's prime time showdown with the Chicago Bears and while he limped around the field the injury wasn't given a significant level of credence, as limping around a football field is in part, Baker Mayfield's way.

Mayfield's injury may take this scenario off the board completely, however, the question would be if Mayfield can play, should he?

Mayfield has a long history of playing through injuries. The former number one overall pick made a choice to play through injury during his time with the Cleveland Browns. That decision was admirable, Mayfield gritted his way through pain and put his body on line for the team. Commendable, still, that decision cost Mayfield millions of dollars, his career in Cleveland, and a good bit of his reputation. Tampa has been lucky in the sense that Mayfield was able to suit for every start prior to his time missed earlier this season. Baker Mayfield has played mostly good football during his Tampa Bay tenure, but throughout last season the hits mounted the physical style of play that fans adore so much from their quarterback caught up to him and Mayfield was battered and bruised. Down the stretch in 2025, Baker Mayfield was battle injuries as much as he was battling defenses and his play reflecting that fight.

In the first 10 games of last season, the Bucs' passing game was humming— Chef Mayfield was cheffing it up. he had 17 touchdowns and only three interceptions. That highly impressive touchdown to interception ratio (better than 5:1) dipped drastically in the latter half of the year. Mayfield's final seven games were messy. He tossed nine touchdowns alongside seven interceptions.

Down the stretch the effectiveness of the Buccaneers' passing attack dwindled, Mayfield's success sunk swiftly, and the Bucs' season began to spiral— A spiral they arguably still have not gotten out of yet a year later. Would the season have turned out better if the Bucs elected to sit Baker Mayfield and allow to heal rather than just placing their quarterback out on the field at half mast to hoist the sails.

The Bucs had a chance to make a similar decision this year already— They made the very same choice. Against the Minnesota Vikings, in week three, Mayfield got dinged— His thumb on his throwing hand, a fairly serious injury for a quarterback. Tampa's quarterback was given a three-seven week timeline. Mayfield, as the fighter that he is, missed three games and was back in action. After three games of Jalon Daniels, Baker Mayfield was back on the field, throwing 11 passes against the Carolina Panthers. The Bucs won and Mayfield was back at again next week, three more pass attempts and not a single completion, but Tampa beat the Atlanta Falcons to get to 2-0 in the division. Last week, Baker Mayfield finally threw (32 times) and it did not look good. Save one beautiful perfectly uncorked deep ball to Tez Johnson the last Sunday was not Mayfield's day— 18-32, no touchdowns, two picks. Mayfield playing through injury, is, has been, and always will be laudable. Just because something is honorable does not mean it is the best decision and sometimes players need to be saved from themselves.

Mayfield is listed as questionable, so the decision may be made for them. Either way, the Tampa Bay Buccaneers will find themselves in an extremely important game this Sunday. They'll be squaring off the with Detroit Lions. the Lions are not a divisional opponent and the game likely won't have any tie-breaking ramifications for the postseason, however, half of the NFC South lost last week. The Atlanta Falcons were blown out by the Kansas City Chiefs and dropped to 3-7 (last place in the division). The New Orleans Saints are right beside them, as their loss to Carolina leaves them at 3-6. Tampa is also 3-6 and they will by no means be out of the race for the division crown if they were to lose Sunday, but as the season trudges forward keeping pace with Carolina becomes more and more important. The Panthers defeated the aforementioned Saints and pushed themselves to the front of the line at 4-5. Next up on the Panther's schedule are the Lamar Jackson-less Ravens— A game they'll likely be favored to win. The Buccaneers still have plenty of time and division games to go in order to make up ground, but with the season rapidly barreling towards Thanksgiving, Tampa's remaining season is running shorter and shorter.
