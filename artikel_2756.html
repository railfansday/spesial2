What we learned as Kings can't overcome brutal first quarter in loss to Warriors

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

What we learned as Kings can't overcome brutal first quarter in loss to Warriors originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SAN FRANCISCO, Calif. — The Sacramento Kings were doomed from the start.

After falling behind by 25 in the first quarter Wednesday night to the Golden State Warriors, they fought back to make the game respectable in the third quarter. But the Warriors ultimately pulled away — even Stephen Curry out with an oblique injury — and won comfortably.

De'Anthony Melton made eight 3s while scoring 24 points, and the Warriors won 129-110 at Chase Center. The Kings fell to 2-16 and will have three days off, including Thanksgiving on Thursday, before hosting a back-to-back against the Minnesota Timberwolves on Sunday and the New Orleans Pelicans on Monday.

Wednesday's loss was the Kings' final game of NBA Cup group play. Sacramento went 0-4 and won't make it out of the group stage after benig outscored by 49 points.

The Kings were missing Zach LaVine, who was out with knee soreness coming out of Monday's loss to the San Antonio Spurs.

The game got off to a terrible start for Sacramento, and they couldn't recover.

The Warriors scored 88 points in their win Sunday over the Boston Celtics, and they had half that total in the first quarter on Wednesday thanks to the Kings' horrendous defense. It was the most for Golden State in any quarter all season.

Golden State went 9-of-13 from deep in the opening frame, taking advantage of open looks repeatedly, while the Kings missed all nine of their attempts from distance. That 27-0 advantage from 3-point range led to Sacramento trailing 44-19.

The Warriors were without Curry, but Melton did his best to impersonate the NBA's best shooter. He had 12 points while making four 3s in his first eight minutes, while Santos matched him with 12 points in the opening period.

The Kings had 10 first-quarter points from Domantas Sabonis, but none of his teammates had more than four. Sacramento's bench was outscored 19-0, and they allowed 15 points off their eight turnovers.

The Kings weren't going to let their first-quarter performance define the game.

They opened the second half with far more energy than the first, and that showed up with its 3-point shooting. Sacramento began the third on a 25-10 run to cut the lead to four, while making 6 of 9 from distance.

However, Golden State ended the quarter on a 19-11 run and took a 12-point lead into the fourth.

The Kings came into the game with the NBA's second-worst plus-minus in third quarter, but they outscored the Warriors 36-29 in the third Wednesday; Sacramento held a 70-57 advantage across the second and third quarters.

The Kings' top players approximated their usual scoring. Sabonis had 27 points, with 10 rebounds and six assists. Keegan Murray had 18 points, and Darius Acuff Jr. added 22.

But the bench scoring favored Golden State 61-19.

Sacramento came into the game with the lowest-scoring bench in the NBA, averaging just 23.4 points per game while the Warriors ranked second at 44.7. Murray had to play 42 minutes. Malik Monk had just five points in 13 minutes.

Precious Achiuwa finished with 11 points, his highest-scoring game since Oct. 29. He missed three games recently with a lower leg injury. Emanuel Sharp started in place of LaVine and finished with nine points on 3-of-6 shooting from 3. He fouled out with 1:54 remaining after playing 25 minutes.
