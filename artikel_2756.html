3 observations from SDSU men's basketball loss vs Abilene Christian

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

BROOKINGS ― South Dakota State men's basketball dropped its second game in a row on Wednesday, Nov. 25, losing 101-95 to Abilene Christian at home.

It's the Jackrabbits' second loss in a row after starting the season 5-0. It's also their second consecutive game without starter Kalen Garry.

The Jackrabbits will turn right around for two games in New Orleans on Nov. 28 and 29 against South Alabama and Fresno State, respectively.

Here's three takeaways from South Dakota State's loss to Abilene Christian.

Joe Sayler needed a true running mate last season, and head coach Bryan Petersen was hellbent on finding one.

It appears as though Jon Mekonnen is that dude.

The 6-foot-8 wing isn't the best shooter in the world, but he attacks the rim hard, uses his wiry frame to slip between defenders and plays very under control.

Mekonnen went for 18 on 6-for-7 shooting, while also adding seven rebounds.

This has gone on all season, as the Colorado State transfer entered Wednesday averaging 15.3 points and 4.7 rebounds on the year while shooting 52.2% from the field.

The most important part about the dynamic is that Sayler and Mekonnen are different players. Sayler stretches the floor more to open up lanes, while Mekonnen is more than capable of working off the ball with success.

Sayler finished the game with 27 points on 7-for-17 shooting and two makes from deep.

Sayler and Mekonnen are turning into one of, if not the, top duos in the Summit League.

Semaj Stamplay has immediately worked himself in as South Dakota State's backup point guard, but the true freshman has all the makings of a future star.

Stampley has a really smooth game and doesn't force the issue too often, even if it appears as though he is. The Alton, Illinois native made a few pocket passes that raise some eyebrows and is just as comfortable facilitating as he is trying to make a play.

He's still a bit raw as a scorer, but that will come as he gets more used to the speed and size of Division I basketball.

Stampley finished this game with six points and three assists in 26 minutes.

There's so much room for growth here, too. It's going to be an exciting storyline for Jackrabbit fans the next couple months.

The Jackrabbits are now down two starters with Garry out for the foreseeable future and Jesse Van Kalsbeek yet to debut. Those losses were evident in the second full game without Garry.

South Dakota State lost its first game of the year last time out at Idaho State, and this one felt just slightly less clunky.

Garry was a big-time connecting piece for the Jackrabbits, helping defend some tough guards while providing a consistent third scoring option.

That offensive load now falls on Davis Kern, EJ Ryans and Alec Squires, who played well but aren't as proficient at putting the ball in the basket as Garry.

Kern went for 20 points and eight rebounds during his breakout season, Ryans, the starting point guard, ended with 13 points and two assists, while Squires had 11 points and five boards from his center spot.

Garry's absence was also noticeable defensively, with Sayler seemingly taking on more responsibility there.

Abilene Christian took advantage of that by shooting 55.9% (33-for-59) from the floor in the win.

There's time for the Jacks to figure this out. Sure, these non-conference losses aren't idea, but they were never getting an at-large bid to the NCAA Tournament.

They need to learn to play without Garry in case he is done for the season, and this feels like a step in the right direction, even in a losing effort.
