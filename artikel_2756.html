No. 7 Miami Seniors Face Bittersweet Farewell Against Boston College

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Saturday marks a hard stop for the old guard at Hard Rock Stadium. The No. 7 Miami Hurricanes host Boston College for their final regular-season home game, and the atmosphere feels heavy with history. This is the last chance for players who spent their entire college careers in Coral Gables to say goodbye on home turf. It is a rare moment in a modern era where most players jump to the NFL after one or two years. The team stands at 10-1 with a perfect 8-0 record in the ACC, but the emotional stakes for the seniors are just as high as the playoff hunt.

Senior defensive lineman Ahmad Moten Sr. knows the clock is running out. He spent five years building this defense and now faces the end of the line. "It's definitely going to be a bittersweet moment. Last home game, last regular-season home game at Hard Rock … I'm not going to cry, of course, but it went by quick," Moten said, according to Sun Sentinel. He admitted he didn't believe his teammates when they warned him time would fly. Now he sees it all happened in a blink. The feeling is shared by running back Mark Fletcher Jr., who plans to savor every second before the final whistle.

While the seniors get their flowers, head coach Mario Cristobal wants the team to keep playing with fire. The defense looked great against Virginia Tech, but the coach wasn't happy with the slow start. Cristobal demands consistency from the first snap to the last. "We've got to play to our standard," Cristobal said after the game, according to Sun Sentinel. He wants the speed and aggression seen in the final minutes to be there from the very beginning. That kind of intensity cannot be a switch that players flip only when the game is on the line.

The coach believes the team needs to hunt down the quarterback with the same drive they showed late in the last contest. "That can't be a switch because that is a different — if you watch it — it's a different level of acceleration, hunting down the quarterback," Cristobal said. The players agree that they need to fix their early-game lapses. Ahmad Moten Sr. told the team they must own their mistakes and get right. The defense gave up a long drive on the first possession against Virginia Tech but held the Hokies to just 133 yards after that. The goal is to make that strong finish the new normal.

Even as the current class finishes up, the program is already winning the next battle. To lock in the nation's leading 2027 recruiting class, Miami secured five-star defensive end DJ Jacobs, flipping him from Ohio State. The move puts the Hurricanes ahead of every other school in the rankings. No other squad can equal Miami's current total of seven five-star prospects within its class. This depth ensures the team will remain competitive long after the current seniors hang up their cleats.

The program is also still active in recruiting trails. Four-star linebacker Quinton Cypher, currently committed to Ohio State, noted that Miami has been "showing a lot of love," according to Sun Sentinel. Current commits are doing the work to keep the momentum going. On social media, linebacker commit Noah Glover stated that Cypher is "(You are) the key to this." The roster is in flux, but the pipeline of talent is wide open. The regular-season finale against Boston College at noon on Saturday is the final test before the ACC title game.
