Bills trending up, trending down: Who must keep it up and how must step up vs. Dolphins?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Buffalo Bills beat the New York Jets 31-12, and the defense that closed the game deserves most of the credit. With a second divisional matchup in sight, Miami is 1-8 and has lost three straight, and Buffalo is favored by 16.5 according to SI's betting preview. The record hides a defense that has allowed 320.5 yards per game, the 10th fewest in the NFL, while facing one of the toughest schedules of opposing offenses. That is the group the Bills will see on Sunday, and here are the players who can make the difference for Buffalo:

Parker had two sacks on consecutive plays early in the fourth quarter and five pressures on 26 pass-rush snaps, per PFF. He is the first Bills rookie with two or more sacks in a game since Greg Rousseau did it against Miami in 2021, and he now has 3.5 on the season. He played more than half the defensive snaps and made the most of his opportunities. Miami's line has given up 24 sacks on Malik Willis, and in Week 10 the Colts credited 8 pressures and 3 sacks on Kyle McCord, who was making his first NFL start. With McCord most likely under center again, a fast rusher who wins early is exactly the problem he does not want.

Moore finished with four catches for 90 yards and a 45-yard touchdown, plus a 32-yard catch on a crossing route. His 75.1 PFF grade was one of the five best on offense. The Dolphins play rookies at a higher rate than any defense in the league, and cornerback Chris Johnson and safety Michael Taaffe have played every defensive snap since Week 7. Taaffe has three interceptions, and Johnson returned one for a 59-yard touchdown against Indianapolis. A veteran who finds space against young defensive backs is what Buffalo needs against a ball-hawking secondary. Moore should find opportunities to haul in another big play or two if Joe Brady gives him the opportunity to capitalize deep again.

After taking heavy criticism following a rough 2025 season (48.8 PFF grade, 73rd of 88 linebackers) and a 39.9 grade in Week 5, Terrel Bernard deserves real credit for his recent turnaround. Over the last two weeks, he has totaled 14 tackles, two passes defended, an interception, and zero missed tackles against the Jets — highlighted by a 72.5 PFF grade in Week 10 and a massive, clean hit that knocked Geno Smith out of the game. That surge has boosted his season grade to 66.6 (29th of 82) with a 65.0 coverage mark and an 80.9 passer rating allowed. Buffalo will need that version of Bernard on Sunday to anchor the middle against Miami's Ollie Gordon II — who just posted 100 yards against Minnesota and 88 against Cincinnati — and contain quarterback Kyle McCord (17-of-32, 209 yards vs. Indianapolis), who thrives on quick, underneath throws.

Hear me out. Cook ran for 157 yards at 8.7 per carry against the Jets, and the run game is not the issue. The receiving side is. Through Week 10, he has 12 catches on 19 targets for 110 yards, with two drops according to Pro Football Reference, and an average depth of target of -0.2 yards. In Week 9, he failed to bring in his only target against Minnesota. Josh Allen said publicly that he needs to take more checkdowns, but we know that's not his favorite kind of throw. When he decides to settle for it, seeing his running back not getting the job done makes that harder. Cook is leaving receiving production on the table in a role that fits him. This offense can be way more dangerous if he can take advantage of those more often.

Khalil Shakir, wide receiver: The highest PFF grade on the offense (77.6) came on two catches for 26 yards, three rushes for 29, and an 80.3 blocking grade. His short-game work is what to watch against a young secondary that tackles well.

Spencer Brown, right tackle: Brown did not practice early in the week and was doubtful, yet played and allowed no pressures on 13 reps against Jets rookie David Bailey, per Next Gen Stats. Bailey came into the game with 40 pressures on 194 pass-rush attempts (20.6%). His PFF grade was 75.3. Chop Robinson is a different test, but Brown showed he can handle a top young rusher.

Dalton Kincaid, tight end: His 54-yard catch started the second half, and he earned a 73.1 PFF grade. Miami's rookie linebacker Jacob Rodriguez has played 99.3 percent of the defensive snaps, using Kincaid over the middle is a matchup to test the rookie in coverage.

Dawson Knox, tight end: One catch for 7 yards on three targets, a 41.2 PFF grade (the lowest of any offensive player on the team) and a 51.1 run-blocking grade. His best game of the year came a week earlier: four catches on five targets for 37 yards. Buffalo needs that level of play against Miami's linebackers.

T.J. Sanders, defensive tackle: A 39.6 PFF grade. Miami has leaned on Gordon, so the interior has to hold up. After a promising start to his season, he hasn't played well as his role diminished with Ed Oliver back.

Jordan Hancock, cornerback: A 43.7 PFF grade. With Christian Benford, Max Hairston, and Dee Alford banged up, more of the secondary will fall on him. He has to make the most of his opportunities, especially with how well Te'Cory Couch played in his last game.

The Jets game showed a defense that punishes opposing QBs and an offense that was grounded for most of the first half, but had an extra gear to make up for it in the second. Miami, 1-8 and starting a rookie quarterback, is the kind of opponent that they can't allow to give them trouble. Parker, Moore, and Bernard have the best case to build on Sunday, and Cook and Chubb have the clearest chance to change the story.
