Suns’ Collin Gillespie puts teammates on notice with ‘skill’ challenge to turn things around

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

PHOENIX– Another case of humble pie for the Phoenix Suns, but not in the way you would expect. Despite the 131-123 loss, the bench, and particularly Collin Gillespie, came alive yet again and even wheeled them back into the game.

78-28 was the bench point discrepancy between them and the Los Angeles Lakers. Besides Miles Bridges, no other starter played at all during the fourth quarter.

The second unit was the topic of discussion at the beginning of the year. Guys like Devin Booker and Bridges highlighted the Suns' group and their chemistry, saying it was better than the starting unit.

They don't have the skills of the first group, but they have intangible skills: heart and passion.

Sunday's loss was the clearest explanation, and it all started with the rookie, Koa Peat. The latter has been kept on the shelf because of the depth at the position. But the downhill driving ability, size, playmaking, and defense are all things Phoenix can use more of.

The rookie moniker isn't truly just for Peat, considering all his accolades before he even stepped foot in the NBA. Sunday just felt more like the flower that finally blossomed right when they needed it.

Much of it is the energy he brings, which fits that second group and, according to Gillespie, is a true skill everyone on the team needs.

"I think it's a skill," he explained postgame in the locker room. I think it's a decision that you have to make going into every game. There are some nights you might not have it offensively; you might not have your shot, but you can't let that impact the other little things that you do on the floor.

So, I think we have a lot of guys who carry themselves that way, and that's got to be a decision that we choose every single night, every single possession that we're out there. It's a non-negotiable."

The 78 bench points are an impressive metric, but it came at the expense of the starting unit. Besides Miles Bridges, no one logged one second in that final frame. People might exaggerate and say it's something that it's not.

The bench brought that juice and infectious energy that nearly won them the game. Towards the end, it looked like they ran out of gas, which is understandable.

With a 5-7 record, it's reasonable not to sound the alarm yet. A lack of energy from the main group is concerning. Granted, they weren't hitting their shots or making plays they usually do. Regardless, the attitude shouldn't change. It was the Suns' bread and butter last season that is slowly drifting away.

Some of it might come from the team's record and enduring some blowout losses. Some of it might come from the fit. Or, it can be as simple as the vibe and the energy in the locker room.

"When you're in a good place collectively, the energy feels different," head coach Jordan Ott said postgame. These guys play a kind of similar style at points, but right now, energy is a skill.

Energy, effort, spirit- you can see it in the other squad. They can feel it early in the first quarter: the way they were playing, the ball was moving, how hard they're playing defensively. That's what's coming across the league right now."
