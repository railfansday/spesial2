Sheffield Wednesday v Reading: Dave Challinor on short-term gains and long-term aims

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Dave Challinor always will. The penultimate month of the calendar year is when he has taken over at the last four clubs he has managed: AFC Fylde, Hartlepool United, Stockport County and now Sheffield Wednesday.

His early feats at Edgeley Park are unlikely to have gone unnoticed in the corridors of power at Hillsborough.

County were tenth in the National League when he took over in November 2021. The season ended in promotion back to the English Football League.

Reprising events in Cheshire by way of another spectacular second-half of the season on this side of the Pennines would be pushing it somewhat.

More especially given that Wednesday possess a squad hastily put together in the summer following a torrid couple of years that almost broke the club before Arise Capital Partners saved the day in May.

Challinor, who also achieved promotion at the end of his first season at Fylde, is conscious that his brief is two-fold.

Results will always matter, for sure, at a club like Sheffield Wednesday at this level. But developing a true playing identity - a stick used to beat his predecessor Henrik Pedersen with - also counts if you speak to Wednesdayites.

Getting his players to slip straight into his aggressive, front-foot style - complete with high-intensity pressing and what is described as 'purposeful possession' will take time.

In late autumn/early winter, when games are a bit all over the place with cup/trophy commitments and injuries and suspensions thrown into the mix for most lower-division clubs especially, winning with immediate style is not straightforward.

Wednesday have a number of players on the sidelines and currently lack a recognised left-back, for instance, with Max Lowe and Reece James both out.

Challinor is having to mix and match a little, while also watching the minutes of some players who only recently returned from injury.

In the event, a haul of seven points from his first nine - encompassing successive away matches - is not to be sniffed at.

Victories over Barnsley and Plymouth Argyle carried imperfections, for sure, but early points remain precious cargo for new managers and a starting point.

At the minute, Wednesday - handily placed in fourth - are grinding it out a bit, as many teams do at this time of year. Challinor is pragmatic about that, but adds a caveat.

He said: "There's been occasions this year where we've not played well (before) and we've found a way to get results.

"That's always going to have to be the case. What we need to do is accept that, which we will, but understand that the performances over the long term will dictate the chances of being in the mix at the end of the season regardless of what that looks like.

"You're always going to have to dig in, whether that's over the course of a game, phases within a game where you come under pressure or things aren't going well. There's going to be times when you don't play well, that's an absolute given.

"They're the moments when you need to dig in at times and defend your goal. If we need to rely on a little bit of luck in our favour, brilliant."

As Challinor's familiarity with his players increases and the fixture schedule relents and allows him and his coaching staff more quality time on the training ground with them, he expects that playing identity to come to the fore more.

He is also likely to be conscious of bringing in some signings of his own, perhaps better suited to his ethos in January and address certain deficient areas.

But for the time being, you cannot have everything.

He continued: "I think the big thing we've spoken about is structure.

"I've seen some really good stuff. It's different. I get we are exposing them a little bit and some of them are playing in positions they're not used to, never mind being asked to do things they're not used to.

"But there's been good stuff on and off the ball.

"We've got to put all that together and the most important thing is finding consistency and it not be (too) up and down between games.

"You see the best teams (do that) regardless of what division they're in. You know what they look like and we've got to get to that point.

"I truly believe we'll get closer to that when we get players back and get the opportunity to put players in positions they are a little bit more comfortable (in).

"But at the moment, in the middle of a League One season, we are trying to improve while still winning and still enjoying what we do.

"Fitting all those things together at times can be tough, but the players have been brilliant in terms of the buy-in."
