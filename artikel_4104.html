10 Best Romance Anime Where Falling in Love is the Easy Part

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The romance genre in anime is evidently meaningful and important to countless fans, myself included. From obsessively reading Horimiya (better than the butchered anime) to bawling my eyes out at the peak that was Clannad and then its After Story.Most romance anime also have a habit of making falling in love look like the hardest part of a relationship. After several episodes of awkward encounters, stolen glances, misunderstandings, and finally working up the courage to confess, you'd think the two lovebirds had overcome their biggest obstacle.

If you're tired of dumb misunderstandings and love polyhedrons, here are some Romance Anime for People Who Hate Cheap Drama.

However, the real work begins after that phase. You see, feelings can be mutual, but that doesn't mean two people automatically know how to navigate each other's insecurities, conflicting personalities, or the unexpected challenges that life throws their way. Thankfully, some romance anime understand that there's considerably more to love than the excitement of a first confession.So, armed with my extensive cult following of this genre since my teenage years, here are the best romance anime where falling in love is the easy part, from one-off slice-of-life seasonals to the more emotionally demanding dramedies.

For all my fellow video gamers, I thought I’d reel you in with My Love Story with Yamada-kun at Lv999. Getting over a breakup is never easy; I can attest to that, but Akane Kinoshita definitely wasn't expecting to find her next romantic interest through an online RPG.After discovering that her bf has been cheating on her, she meets Akito Yamada, a notoriously indifferent MMO gamer who initially seems uninterested in anything outside his favourite hobby. Their clashing personalities couldn't be more different, but their friendship gradually develops into something considerably more meaningful.

It’s a 13-episode show that beautifully explores emotional maturity and the importance of understanding another person rather than simply expecting them to reciprocate your feelings. The only downside I have to mention is that the anime doesn’t fully cover the story, so you might need to binge the manga to get the full experience here.

As someone who originally read the manga on and off on his phone, “I Have a Crush at Work” (2025) stayed on my radar as soon as I heard it was in production. Workplace romances are complicated enough without having to pretend you're not dating the person sitting a few desks away.But then you have these two coworkers, Yui Mitsuya and Masugu Tateishi, who start a relationship but decide to keep it secret from their colleagues. That arrangement, in turn, creates an amusing contrast between their professional behaviour and how they act together when nobody is around them.

Fair warning: It’s a little more comedy-based, but beneath that layer, the show neatly tackles a relatable problem: how do you maintain a healthy relationship when you spend so much of your day together, yet can't openly acknowledge it? And that’s exactly where this show will make you adore it wholeheartedly.

We’re keeping the Workplace Romance streak going, and this is the perfect time to give some appreciation to one of my and everyone else’s all-time favorites in this sub-genre of romance anime: Wotakoi.I genuinely think it needs little introduction, given how widely popular it is, whether it's the show or even the web manga. Two adults who are hopeless romantics in theory, but who actually have absolutely no idea how to navigate an actual relationship? This show literally answers that question with childhood friends, Narumi and Hirotaka.

And not just them; you also get a healthy dose of screen time with their respective co-workers, and if I’m being honest, I may slightly prefer Hanako and Kabakura’s cute moments to the two main characters. But again, we’re in a similar problem where the anime and OVAs don’t fully adapt everything, so either go read it or, like me, hopelessly wait for a follow-up season.

Like what My Happy Marriage has to offer? Well, here are some of the best shows that feature somewhat of a similar theme.

This next one’s from my nostalgic vault because, like Wotakoi, Golden Time is a popular romcom anime that’s beloved by (almost) everyone. You’ve got a fairly simple premise at the beginning: our MC, Banri Tada, is settling into college life when Kouko barges in, and the two fall for each other quickly.And that’s the easy part. The bigger elephant in the room is that Banri has lost the memories of his hometown and past after an accident, and the real kicker is what that gap does to a relationship once the honeymoon glow wears off.

What I like is how mature the drama feels between these two characters and the rest of the cast, especially at a time when I thought all romance anime felt the same, with their usual high school settings. The show obviously isn’t a masterpiece or anything, but if you want an anime where the feelings are the easy part and the baggage drives most of the plot, it’s worth the 24 episodes.

We’re taking things old-school here, back to the golden age of the late 2000s, when the shoujo series Itazura na Kiss aired. Look, a lot of romance anime are happy to wrap things up once the two leads finally get together, but this show gives you a broader look than your typical lovey-dovey couple story.It follows Kotoko and Naoki from their school days into adulthood, showing how their relationship changes as they navigate college, marriage, and just life in general beyond their adolescence.

Now, given that it is based on a 1990s Shoujo, Naoki’s behavior and character may be slightly concerning, but trust me, the show is genuinely a wholesome treat, especially when these two start living under one roof, getting through their daily frictions of life, and then eventually build a family together.

TONIKAWA, or rather, as I prefer to call it, Tonikaku Kawaii, is easily one of the sweetest romcoms I had the pleasure of reading long before the seasonal anime adaptation premiered.What’s so great here is how the show totally skips the confession part: Nasa meets Tsukasa, falls for her, and they then get married very early in the story. From there, the series focuses on the joys and growing pains of newlywed life, as the pair learn more about each other while adjusting to living together.

It's honestly a bit more lighthearted than you’d expect from the other entries I’ve mentioned here, but oh man, I’d be lying if I didn’t mention how almost every single moment of these two puts a gigantic smile on your face. Apologies in advance to any hopeless romantics out there, but trust me, this show is a massive ball of sunshine to help cheer you up.

Falling in love for the first time is one thing, but then figuring out how to make that relationship work is an entirely different challenge.It’s a universal experience almost all of us go through when we’re young and full of these different aspirations. Yet Tsuki ga Kirei captures that awkward transition beautifully through Kotaro Azumi, an aspiring novelist, and Akane Mizuno, a track-and-field enthusiast, who grow closer during their final year of middle school.

Their relationship begins with these little tender yet familiar moments, but the show doesn't treat getting together as the finish line. Instead, it explores everything that comes afterward with jealousy, parental expectations, and the looming uncertainty of moving on to high school. It's a sincere coming-of-age romance that understands exactly why staying together can be harder than falling in love.

My days of avidly consuming manga or watching almost every interesting anime in my piled-up backlog on MAL/Kitsu are long gone, but when a show like “You and I Are Polar Opposites” comes along, it instantly reminds you why this medium is still alive and kicking for you.It quite literally lives up to the title: on one hand, you have Suzuki, an energetic and sociable high school girl; on the other, you have Tani, her quiet, straightforward classmate who’s just the epitome of the silent-treatment energy at first glance. Despite their contrasting personalities, they develop feelings for each other and eventually take the plunge, starting a relationship.

And it’s not just about them; you get to see their everyday lives grow together alongside their wildcard cast of friends/classmates. It has those high school romcom tropes going for it, but trust me when I say the writing and character dramedy are exceptionally well done, and almost every episode feels like a joy to watch, with no filler.

Nana is one of my favorite anime and manga series of all time. I’ve got Nana Osaki as my avatar across most of my socials, and it's mainly because of how much this show means to me. What starts as a story about two young women navigating life in Tokyo slowly becomes one of the most emotionally complex explorations of love, friendship, and heartbreak I've ever experienced.

The series doesn't romanticize the idea of finding someone and living happily ever after; instead, it showcases two bittersweet parallels: Hachi's search for affection and stability, which often leads her into situations that make her question her own worth, while Nana's fiercely independent exterior hides a vulnerability she struggles to confront.I sometimes obsess over Ai Yazawa's writing in this series because nobody feels entirely innocent or irredeemable; almost all of these flawed young adults make messy decisions, hurt people they care about, and occasionally sabotage their own happiness.

To some of you reading, I hope you can relate to me on one thing: there’s some media you just can’t revisit with all earnestness because it’s left an emotional scar.That’s exactly what Clannad: After Story did to me, and I both love and hate it for doing that. While the original Clannad does a wonderful job establishing Okazaki and Nagisa's relationship, After Story takes things several steps further by exploring what happens once the sunshine of adolescence is over. It’s also where the show takes a full-blown descent into heartbreak.

The two are no longer simply teenagers; they're young adults trying to figure out what it means to build a life together. Marriage, parenthood, financial struggles, grief, and letting go. That one entire episode dedicated to Okazaki realizing and forgiving his Father has absolutely scarred my heart.The overall show and the OG VN are important to a lot of fans in the medium, almost being iconic for how the story beats were presented in it, like a train wreck. It's a bittersweet yet painfully gut-wrenching narrative, and one I still find hard to revisit without getting a lump in my throat.

Love isn’t always simple, but these 7 best romance anime on Crunchyroll capture its magic, heartbreak, and everything in between.
