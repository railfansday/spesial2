‘I Play Rocky’ Stars Unite With Original ‘Rocky’ Producer for Film’s 50th Anniversary Celebration

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Irwin Winkler, who was behind Sylvester Stallone's 1976 hit, joined Anthony Ippolito, AnnaSophia Robb and director Peter Farrelly at the Academy Museum event.

Rocky minds old and new came together on Tuesday night for a 50th anniversary celebration of Sylvester Stallone‘s breakout 1976 hit.

The iconic boxing movie is back in the culture not only for its milestone anniversary but also because of I Play Rocky, which chronicles Stallone’s journey of bringing his project to the big screen. At the event — held at the Academy Museum — I Play Rocky stars Anthony Ippolito, AnnaSophia Robb, P.J. Byrne and Kiki Seto, director Peter Farrelly and writer Peter Gamble were all in attendance, alongside Rocky producer Irwin Winkler. Winkler took the stage for a Q&A before the special screening of Rocky, recalling how Stallone came in for a meeting and mentioned the story he had in mind. “He said he would write the story very quickly, with the understanding that if we liked the script, we had to star him in the film,” Winkler remembered of the star’s comments to him and fellow producer Robert Chartoff. “And the rest you’ll see on I Play Rocky. It really told the story very, very well, probably better than I could.”

The producer added that “we made this little movie for a million dollars, we didn’t expect anything out of it” but credits its uplifting message — following the Vietnam War and Watergate where “people were really kind of down and depressed” — for its success. “This movie came along and said, ‘America is also a place if you believe in yourself, you have the opportunity to succeed.’ And I think, in a strange way, I think we’re back to some of the same feelings,” Winkler said. “I think what I Play Rocky shows you is the story about a man who believes in himself, that America can give you that opportunity to succeed. I don’t want to sound political but I can’t help it.”

Winkler also touched on the spinoff Creed films, saying “We thought we had put the franchise to bed with Rocky Balboa” but upon meeting Ryan Coogler, “he had the same passion that Stallone had shown 40 years before” and “I said to myself, ‘If we’re going to ever make another Rocky, I want to make it with this young Ryan Coogler.'”

Midway through the conversation, Winkler was joined by Farrelly and Ippolito; the filmmaker recalled watching Rocky as a freshman in college and “I remember walking out of the theater, being like, ‘Woo, woo!’ And I didn’t know what I was wooing about, but I was pumped up — to do what? I have no idea. But I was inspired, and I remember going back to school and getting my buddies and being like, ‘OK, you’ve got to see this movie.’ The closest I could compare it to is like having a sports team. It was like you fell in love with a sports team, and you were rooting for him, and Rocky was our team.”

Farrelly also called I Play Rocky “the best script I ever read,” noting, “the thing that grabbed me the most was that I realized this movie mirrors Rocky. It’s very similar. It was like [Stallone] was telling his own story.” Winkler said he questioned how Gamble knew so many of the inside details he put in the script and was surprised to find so much of the information available online. He also joked, “watching P.J. Byrne play me, I was a bit surprised because I thought Brad Pitt would really be me. But I’ll take P.J. anytime.”

Ippolito closed out the chat by musing that Rocky‘s success is because “the underdog story is a very universal one. I think everybody has a Rocky inside of them. I think we all have our own personal relationship to this story and to this character,” adding, “it’s an incredible, incredible film. You guys got great taste being here tonight.”
