3 Observations from Indiana men’s basketball’s 93-57 win over Bowling Green

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Indiana men's basketball dominated Bowling Green tonight, going on a run just over five minutes into the game that effectively put the Falcons to bed. The Hoosiers were shooting well, defending well, and rebounding well in what may have been the most complete performance from this group so far.

Though it wasn't a marquee win like last week's victories over Arkansas or Kentucky, the margin of victory was one that boosted Indiana's computer profile. KenPom only favored Indiana by 20 entering the game, so the blowout moved Indiana up five spots in the national ranking.

At 8-0, Indiana looks much improved over Darian DeVries' first season, with KenPom now projecting the Hoosiers to win 23 games and go 12-8 in Big Ten play.

Indiana had a season-high 18 assists tonight against Bowling Green, starting to resemble the fast-paced offense we saw from DeVries' group last year. While Indiana has more players that are capable of creating unassisted buckets, the ball movement tonight helped the Hoosiers take more quality looks from the perimeter, something that will be important for maintaining a respectable 3-point percentage this season.

Bryce Lindsay's deep shot against Arkansas will go down as a high-point of the season, but over the last couple games Indiana had gotten into the habit of chucking a few too many heat checks from deep or taking transition threes that negate the team's rebounding advantage.

Seeing the way the ball moved tonight was an encouraging step for this group not just cause it made the offense look a little better, but because it reflects well on the team's chemistry so early in the season. With a team full of transfers, having nine different players record an assist in a game is no small feat.

Aiden Sherrell had one of his stronger performances of the season, finishing with 16 points, nine rebounds, two blocks and an assist. He also went 2-6 from 3-point range.

Tonight's performance was a good example of why he's a potential NBA player. A seven footer who can score from the perimeter, rebound on both ends of the floor, and protect the rim is a valuable piece for any team. After an impressive year last year at Bama, he's averaging more points and shooting better from deep this year at Indiana.

Even though he cooled off by the end of the night, his early threes are exactly what Indiana will need from him going forward. Establishing himself as a threat at the beginning of the game will force defenses to account for him until the final whistle.

It's hard to say enough good things about Bryce Lindsay so far this year. Against Bowling Green, he scored 18 points, grabbed five rebounds, had three assists, and also added two steals. He was also super efficient, going 2/4 from 3-point range and making all of his free throws.

What I like most about Lindsay's game is his ability to be what DeVries needs on the floor, no matter who he's playing with. He can play point guard alongside Darren Harris at the shooting guard spot, or play shooting guard next to Markus Burton.

Calling him the sixth man doesn't even feel right, as he plays the fifth most minutes on the team, but he's been a huge asset for DeVries and Indiana this year, doing everything asked of him, and then some.
