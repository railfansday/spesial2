Seaport Entertainment (SEG) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Positive operating EBITDA returned for a second straight quarter despite weather.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Seaport Entertainment Group Inc. (SEG -9.61%) reported positive operating EBITDA for the second consecutive quarter, marking a shift toward improved operational efficiency despite weather-related challenges in its primary markets. Management reported that the company remains on track for its 2028 stabilization goal, supported by a leaner corporate cost structure and a pipeline of signed leases totaling 160,000 square feet. The company completed the strategic sale of its 85 South Street multifamily asset during the quarter, further strengthening a balance sheet that currently carries only $38.1 million in asset-specific debt. While total revenue declined due to the departure of former major tenants at Pier 17, underlying rental income from new tenants rose 22% year over year.

Coming off a momentum-building second quarter, we had high hopes for the third quarter due to a strong concert lineup on the rooftop at Pier 17, an already qualified for Aviators playoff run, anticipated Balloon Museum opening, and a burgeoning events calendar at the Seaport. However, the third quarter underperformed our expectations, largely due to transient weather-related factors that weighed on operating results, especially within our entertainment segment.

The issues impacting our results the most relative to our expectations were record-setting heat during the longest home stand of the season for the Las Vegas Aviators, and an unusually difficult stretch of weather in New York City, resulting in the need to reschedule or cancel multiple concerts. That said, the entertainment segment still meaningfully improved year-over-year, thanks to better expense management, and there were a number of sustainable operating trends throughout our various businesses that continue to positively influence our quarterly performance. Lenah will talk through the details of the quarter.

The Q3 2026 positive operating EBITDA improved 113% as compared to negative operating EBITDA in Q3 2025, marking the second quarter in a row and the second time in our company's history that we've achieved positive operating EBITDA. We expect operating EBITDA to further benefit from many of the previously announced new tenants and concepts that will open in the coming months, and our overall financial results should continue to see improvement from our increasingly leaner corporate cost structure, which will further improve as we find additional G&A savings and annualize the benefits of the organizational changes we've already made.

The holistic nature of our efforts corporately and within our operations is best reflected in our year-over-year change and our non-GAAP adjusted net income. in Q3, we grew non-GAAP adjusted net income per share by 60%, which is on top of an 87% year-over-year improvement in Q3 of 2025. Make no mistake, the improvements are not always going to be linear, and we will have quarters that are more impactful to our long-term trajectory than others. That said, these data points demonstrate the tangible progress we've made to our programming and operating models, and that progress should continue to show up in our year-over-year comparable results as we march towards initial stabilization in 2028.

In terms of Las Vegas and the unprecedented heat, we had a bit of a perfect storm in the sense that we had 25% more games in July and August as compared to last year. For context, the average high temperature on the dates of our home games was 110 degrees in July, which was Las Vegas' third hottest month ever, and 105 degrees in August, which was the hottest August on record. Not exactly ideal weather to be outside. As a result, individual and group ticket sales and overall attendance underperformed, resulting in lower concessions and merchandise sales. The weather challenges were partially offset by better game day expense management and another strong year on the field.

The Aviators clinched the Pacific Coast League first half title in June, set a franchise record with 87 regular season wins, and reached the postseason for the second consecutive year as defending PCL champions. They hosted the PCL Championship Series at the Las Vegas Ballpark in September, but ultimately lost to the eventual AAA champions, the Oklahoma City Comets. More broadly at the ballpark, we're continuing to build the calendar of events beyond the Aviators baseball season. October opened with three sold-out nights of Cosmic Baseball, and then for the first time in the ballpark's history, we hosted a one-day music and food festival.

Additionally, our annual holiday activation, Enchant, returns to the Las Vegas Ballpark in November, and so far, ticket sales are pacing ahead of last year. In New York, the weather also created real challenges during the quarter. A late September nor'easter forced us to cancel two sold-out concerts, which were expected to be among the most profitable shows of the season. The severity of the weather was felt across the New York market, with all major outdoor events also canceled that weekend. We also had to reschedule multiple shows during the quarter, resulting in lower day-of-show attendance and less spending on concessions and merchandise.

Despite those disruptions, demand for the rooftop remained strong, and we finished the year with an 86% sell-through rate across 61 shows. In addition to the cancellations, another reason we had to incrementally lower show count is because we've leaned into large-scale cultural and brand events that utilize dates on the rooftop. These events not only generate incremental revenue, but they bring new audiences to the Seaport, have the potential to deliver better economics and overall profitability, and can provide outsized demand that benefits our restaurants, tenants, and partners. During the third quarter, our event calendar began with the 50th anniversary of the Macy's 4th of July fireworks, broadcast by NBC from the rooftop of Pier 17.

On our last call, we spoke about what the day meant for the neighborhood and those of us who live in New York City. I should also highlight the fact the multi-network broadcast drew 11.2 million viewers, the largest audience since 2018. That level of national exposure extends beyond a single evening. It provides visibility for the Seaport and supports our conversations with sponsors, event producers, and prospective tenants long after the event itself. Other events during the quarter centered around sports, where we hosted numerous watch parties and brand activations related to the World Cup. For a three-week stretch, we brought back the Seaport Racquet Club in an expanded format to celebrate the US Open.

The activation included a public tennis court on Seaport Square, clinics with some of the world's top tennis players, including Naomi Osaka. We hosted match screenings and sponsored programming at Sadie's Garden Bar. Our partners at Racquet Media also transformed one of our spaces into the Racquet Clubhouse, which served as a hub for additional brands and events throughout the tournament. The activity of Q3 carried into the fourth quarter with a mix of recurring and new events. The Rooftop at Pier 17 hosted the first-ever Soho House Festival in the U.S., a full-day event for Soho House members and their guests, headlined by Alanis Morissette.

For the second consecutive year, we hosted the New York City Wine & Food Festival, drawing more than 35,000 guests to events throughout the festival and expanding its footprint to include signature events on the Rooftop at Pier 17. These events are a foundational component of our operations at the Seaport. While timing and rotation in and out of market can create year-over-year variances, we expect the Pier 17 event space, which is currently under construction, to give us additional capacity to host large-scale events consistently throughout the year, helping to reduce volatility over time and giving our events business room to grow.

Within the landlord segment of our business, the transformation of the Tin Building culminated with the opening of the Balloon Museum in early August, followed by a grand opening celebration during New York Fashion Week in September. The reception from visitors has been very positive. We are seeing encouraging activity at nearby businesses, particularly at Sadie's and Mister Dips, with Mister Dips' comparable revenue increasing 15% year-over-year since the opening of the museum. That becomes even more valuable as we enter the colder months, when the year-round indoor ticketed experience can help drive visitation, helping reduce some of the seasonality across the broader neighborhood.

On the leasing front, we signed a 10-year lease with Brooklyn DOP for their flagship Manhattan location across from the Balloon Museum in Pier 17. Expected to open in mid to late 2027, the pizzeria will add to the growing mix of approachable, fast-casual options in the neighborhood. That mix matters. It complements the destination dining and entertainment we already have, while adding to the amenities needed to support a quickly growing local population who not only visits the Seaport for events and special occasions, but also lives, works, and interacts with the neighborhood on a daily basis.

As we look into the future, we still have over 160,000 sq ft of new concepts and tenants who are signed but not yet open. With where we are in the life cycle of our repositioning plan, this is a primary focus for many of our team members. In terms of material updates, Flanker Kitchen + Sports Bar and Hidden Boot Saloon are now expected to open in late Q1, early Q2 of 2027. We experienced some contractor-related delays during construction, but those issues are largely behind us. We are working closely with our partners at Carver Road Hospitality to prepare for the opening.

The new concept from Public Service and the Pier 17 event space are both progressing through design and construction, with openings planned for early to mid-2027. Meow Wolf remains in process with a preliminary opening expected in early 2028. The team has also been busy thinking through space utilization and potential new concepts. I am pleased to share that we are planning to launch a new neighborhood bakery located in the Seaport's historic cobblestones, which we also expect to open in mid-2027. Similar to Sadie's, the bakery is being concepted, branded, and operated by our in-house team. We believe, in certain circumstances, there is value in owning the guest experience from beginning to end.

The bakery will leverage members of our existing culinary team and is expected to address a void in the neighborhood by filling a lot of unoccupied space. Today, much of the Seaport's food and beverage activity is concentrated in the afternoon and evening. The bakery will broaden our offerings with an approachable, everyday morning destination for our local neighbors, office workers, and visitors, giving people another reason to make the Seaport part of their daily routine. As a final update, during the quarter, we completed the sale of 85 South Street. The transaction strengthens our balance sheet and gives us greater flexibility to pursue higher return opportunities.

I cannot thank our team enough for their focus and commitment towards executing on all of our initiatives.

As a reminder, rental revenue from Nike accelerated into Q2 of 2026 in conjunction with the termination, which ultimately reduced Q3 rental revenue. Despite the absence of this event, landlord operations continued to generate revenue from a high volume of activations this summer, notably ESPN Deportes broadcasting live World Cup coverage from the Heineken Riverdeck on Pier 17 over multiple days. On the expense side, landlord operating costs declined approximately $350,000, or 5% year-over-year, reflecting continued contractual improvements across cleaning, marketing, and storage.

While quarterly landlord EBITDA declined approximately $970,000 year-over-year, on an underlying basis, the segment continued to improve, with landlord operating EBITDA increasing 34% year-over-year after adjusting for Nike and ESPN. Turning to 85 South Street, as previously announced, we completed the sale of the property for $8.3 million on September 29th. After work credits, transfer taxes, and other closing costs, net proceeds totaled approximately $7.2 million. 85 South Street was relatively EBITDA neutral year-to-date, and its disposition is expected to have a minimal impact on comparisons going forward.

As Matt mentioned, Q3 is typically a very active quarter within our entertainment segment, and despite softer revenue across both the Seaport Concert Series and our Las Vegas operations, entertainment operating EBITDA increased 26% year-over-year to $2.3 million, as we benefited from lower variable costs, proactive expense management, and certain timing-related efficiencies. Concert series revenue declined 18% year-over-year, reflecting three fewer shows, a series of lower-priced artists, and lower sponsorship revenue following the non-renewal of our Chase partnership, and as mentioned previously, the weather-related challenges, which included the cancellation of two sold-out performances that contributed meaningfully to the prior year quarter.

Tickets sold per show and per capita food and beverage spend remained strong, and per show margins remained consistent with the prior year. From a profitability perspective, concert series operating EBITDA improved modestly, benefiting from lower variable expenses, including artist fees and sponsorship fulfillment costs, along with lower administrative and insurance costs. In Las Vegas, Q3 operating EBITDA increased 15% year-over-year, driven by lower operating expenses despite a 4% revenue decline, primarily due to weather issues Matt outlined earlier and the absence of our historically strong July 3rd game. Turning to general and administrative expenses, G&A totaled $6.2 million in the third quarter of 2026, a decrease of $11.7 million, or 65%, compared to the third quarter of last year.

However, Q3 2025 included significant leadership transition costs. Excluding these leadership transition costs, third quarter G&A declined $250,000, or 4%, year-over-year. Sequentially, G&A increased $760,000, or 14%, primarily because of the timing-related items we discussed on our prior earnings call. Even with that quarterly movement, we continue to see year-to-date improvements by over 23% compared to the prior year when excluding one-time restructuring and leadership transition costs, reflecting the benefit of our initiatives to right-size and optimize our infrastructure, which we expect to continue to see the benefit from into 2027. We recorded approximately $760,000 of net expense in other income or loss during the third quarter.

The expense primarily reflected non-recurring items, including pre-opening costs for Flanker Kitchen and Sports Bar, Tin Building wind-down costs, and certain legal expenses. This was a $1.7 million year-over-year improvement, largely because the prior year quarter included a write-off of the rooftop winter structure. During the quarter, we realized a gain on the sale of 85 South Street of $1.3 million and a gain of approximately $400,000, reflecting a post-closing adjustment to the previously recognized loss on the sale of 250 Water Street, for a total gain on sale for the quarter of $1.7 million.

We recorded net interest income of approximately $420,000, a year-over-year improvement of $550,000 from net interest expense in the prior year, primarily reflecting the elimination of interest expense associated with the 250 Water Street debt, which was repaid earlier in 2026 in conjunction with the sale. Equity earnings from unconsolidated ventures declined approximately $400,000, or 34%, year-over-year. Lower earnings from Lawn Club were partially offset by reduced losses from the Jean-Georges Restaurant Group. Equity and earnings from Lawn Club declined 32% year-over-year, despite a record revenue month in July, reflecting an unfavorable comparison as the Garden Bar is now operated by Sadie's, as compared to inclusion in the Lawn Club in prior year.

Margins were also pressured by higher maintenance and labor costs. At Jean-Georges Restaurant Group, the team continues to focus on driving value through increased license and management fee income and cost reduction measures to improve existing restaurant operations. Third quarter net loss attributable to common stockholders improved 75% year-over-year to $8.4 million, while net loss per share narrowed to $0.65 from $2.61 in the third quarter of 2025. The year-over-year improvement reflects the actions we've taken across the business, tighter cost management, the closure of underperforming venues, and continued simplification of our operations. Together, those steps are creating a more efficient platform for long-term value creation.

Our non-GAAP adjusted net loss continues to narrow year-over-year, improving by $4.2 million, or 58%, to $3 million, compared to a loss of $7.2 million in the prior year quarter. On a per-share basis, non-GAAP adjusted net loss attributable to common stockholders improved to $0.23, compared to a loss of $0.57 per share in the prior year quarter, representing a 60% year-over-year improvement. While we did not repeat the positive non-GAAP adjusted net income achieved in the second quarter, the continued year-over-year improvement reinforces our confidence in the progress we're making towards stabilizing the business and building a more sustainable earnings profile.

On our balance sheet, capital expenditures for the quarter totaled $13.9 million, with the majority invested in landlord work related to the Balloon Museum, Flanker Kitchen + Sports Bar, The Hidden Boot Saloon, Meow Wolf, and other capital maintenance projects. At quarter end, we held net cash position of $72.2 million, with $110.3 million of cash equivalents, and restricted cash. During the quarter, we received an additional $5.9 million from the escrow established for post-closing obligations related to 250 Water Street. Cumulative distributions to SEG now total $26.7 million, with approximately $0.5 million remaining in the escrow account. Our only outstanding debt remains the $38.1 million Las Vegas ballpark loan.

We continue to maintain a strong balance sheet, providing us with financial flexibility to strategically deploy capital while continuing to improve our operations. Additionally, we're also working to potentially redeem our $10 million Series A preferred equity that was put in place at the time of the spin-off from Howard Hughes. Overall, we remain encouraged by the progress we're making, stabilizing our assets, improving operating performance, and maintaining the financial flexibility to invest in long-term value creation.
