SEC adds LSU men's basketball coach Will Wade to lawsuit as expulsion vote looms

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The SEC has filed an amended complaint adding LSU men's basketball coach Will Wade to its federal lawsuit asserting the authority to expel the school as a member.

Wade joins several LSU administrators and coaches already named in the lawsuit, including university president Wade Rousse, athletics director Verge Ausberry and football coach Lane Kiffin. Also named is Louisiana Attorney General Liz Murrill.

The amendment was filed "because the additional allegations reflect and relate to events that have occurred since the SEC filed its First Amended Complaint on Sept. 8, 2026," the supplemental complaint reads.

The conference has scheduled a vote of all 16 member schools for Dec. 3 "to consider the termination of LSU's membership in the SEC." To go into effect, at least two-thirds of members are required to vote in favor of LSU's dismissal.

This expulsion vote was triggered in part by the basketball program's decision to roster and play former All-America guard RJ Luis Jr., who went undrafted after leaving St. John's in 2025 and spent last season with the Utah Jazz and Boston Celtics, never appearing in an NBA regular-season game.

Luis signed with Wade and LSU in May and was granted a preliminary injunction that declared him eligible for the 2026-27 season. He made his debut on Nov. 18, scoring 11 points against Louisiana-Monroe. A day later, Rousse ordered Wade to remove Luis from the roster.

In an affidavit, SEC Commissioner Greg Sankey said that both Wade and Ausberry had said they would not play Luis.

"LSU's decision to play RJ Luis Jr. weeks after telling the court it was 'demonstrably false' that it would violate the rules hands the SEC its best evidence yet," Ryan Regula, a partner at Snell & Wilmer who works extensively with colleges and universities among his specialties, told USA TODAY Sports. "It also significantly undercuts LSU's position that the case is moot."

Next week's vote marks the second time in several months the SEC has openly discussed LSU's future in the conference.

In September, the conference and LSU were in dispute over Kiffin's wish to add to the Tigers' roster several former Power Four players who had spent the summer in NFL training camps.

A month earlier, the SEC had passed a new rule prohibiting the rostering of athletes who had signed a contract with an NFL, NBA or WNBA team (or minor-league affiliate) or been listed on a professional roster.

The SEC considered a vote on the school's membership but backed away after Kiffin and the football program backed off rostering the former college players, many of whom had been filed lawsuits to gain a season of eligibility in the wake of the NCAA's move to a five-for-five eligibility model.

"This thing is in litigation," Gov. Jeff Landry of Louisiana said on Wednesday. "We're going to leave it up to the lawyers. We're going to let LSU make those particular statements. So we'll just wait and see what happens and there will be decisions and comments made by the LSU leadership."

"Again, as we continue on this unfortunate path, we'll unfortunately have to leave it in the hands of the lawyers."
