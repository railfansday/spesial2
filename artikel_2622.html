Mavericks vs Suns Recap: 3 things as Dallas rallies for 113-109 win against Phoenix

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The Dallas Mavericks stayed undefeated in NBA Cup group play in dramatic fashion Friday night, beating the Phoenix Suns 113-109 in Phoenix.

Dallas rallied from a 21-point deficit, trailing 92-71 with less than two minutes left in the third quarter. Cooper Flagg led all scorers with a monster 34-point game that might have been his best game of his career.

What's funny is the Mavericks started this game extremely hot. Riding the defensive aggression that has carried them throughout the young season, Dallas jumped on Phoenix early and got out in transition. The Mavericks had an 18-5 in the blink of an eye, and just as quickly as they built that lead, it disappeared.

Kyrie Irving sat out for load management, and his presence was felt almost as soon as Dallas had to dip into its bench. The Mavericks started "Point Coop" tonight, with Flagg at point guard. But instead of Naji Marshall jumping into the starting lineup, coach Dusty May kept the starting lineup he's used over the last few games, but plugged PJ Washington back in. Once Marshall and the reserves checked in, things went downhill as the Suns bench exploded.

The Suns controlled the game from the midway point of the first quarter all the way to the fourth. Throughout the game Flagg was doing everything in his power to keep the team connected, but didn't have anyone running with him so to speak. That changed in the fourth, where Naji Marshall and Max Christie came alive. Marshall scored eight late points in the frame, including back-to-back threes, and Christie nailed a crucial three and secured the game-deciding rebound in the final seconds, along with throwing Flagg an alley-oop earlier in the quarter.

This was a special game from Flagg. With Dallas down 106-103 with 1:37 left, Flagg made three straight buckets, punctuated with a smooth 13-foot turnaround, to give the Mavericks a 109-106 lead with just under 24 seconds left. From there the Mavericks defense held on and the Mavericks are a perfect 4-0 in NBA Cup group play.

I think I've either typed or said out loud at least 10 times this season "Wow, that was the best game of Cooper Flagg's career" and, well, I'm typing that again: That was the best game of Cooper Flagg's career.

Flagg finished with 34 points, seven rebounds, eight assists, five steals, four blocks, and only two turnovers. It's the first time since steals and blocks were record that a player has notched this stat line. He's the third player ever to hit 30 points, seven rebounds, eight assists, five steals, and four blocks. And he did this without Kyrie Irving, who the Mavericks were 0-3 without Irving entering tonight. Oh and he made three straight clutch field goals in the final 90 seconds with the Mavericks down three. Remarkable.

I'm not sure what else to say about Flagg. This is a special, special dude. He was everywhere, with splash defensive plays, transition havoc, and clutch halfcourt shotmaking. He's the real deal and the full package. Can't be said enough how important it was for Flagg to do this without Irving, as the Mavericks desperately have to find ways to win games without their second star so Irving's workload can be managed. Turns out having Flagg turn into basketball god is a way to win those games.

For the first three quarters, this was looking like a waste of a historic Flagg game, mainly because the Mavericks bench couldn't get anything going. Without Irving, the Mavericks just don't have many reliable perimeter players that coach Dusty May trusts.

Naji Marshall and Max Christie came through in the nick of time. Christie scored 16 second-half points, including two three-pointers, while Marshall scored 10 in the fourth, including eight straight down the stretch while the Mavericks were rallying back into the game. Dallas already lacks a lot of pop on the perimeter even with Irving in the game, so without him, it's troubling. Kudos to Christie and Marshall for heating up in the second half, and kudos to May for sticking with Marshall after a rough first half. Dallas just needed any type of spacing at all, and Christie delivered.

The Mavericks offense doesn't look pretty when it's limited to slower, half-court play, but boy when the defense is humming and that aggression pays off, it's fun to watch. Dallas jumped to that 18-5 early lead thanks to that aggressive defense, and got itself back into the game with that same style.

Dallas had 12 steals, outscored Phoenix 15-9 in transition, and scored 21 points off the Suns 18 total team turnovers. The Mavericks just continue to amp the pressure against teams and it's paying off. There were moments in the fourth quarter where it looked like the Suns were completely unprepared for how up on the ball The Mavericks defenders would be — Dallas even forced an over-and-back call against Devin Booker when Zaccharie Risacher hounded him as soon as he crossed halfcourt.

The downside to this style is obvious — teams that can handle the pressure and pass over the top can burn the Mavericks for backdoor cuts, layups, and threes. The Suns shot 15-of-40 from three tonight, which nearly did the Mavericks in. But it wasn't enough and Dallas survived.
