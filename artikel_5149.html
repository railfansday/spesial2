XRP whales have taken more than $127 million out of Binance since November 10, based on on-chain data shared by researcher Darkfost on X. For months before this, whales were mostly sending XRP to the exchange.

XRP is trading at $0.9589. It is up 0.4% in the last 24 hours but down 8.3% over the week, CoinGecko data shows.

Whales Are Now Withdrawing, Not Depositing

Over the past week, more XRP has left Binance than arrived from whale wallets:

When big holders move coins off an exchange, it usually means they want to hold, not sell. Whales may be buying the dip below $1 and keeping the coins. If that continues, it could help build a stronger floor just under $1.

This Is Different From Earlier in the Year

Whales were doing the opposite a few months ago.

In August, XRP jumped nearly 72% in a few days and climbed back above $1.50. At that time, whales were mostly depositing XRP on Binance, with inflows of up to $75 million. Deposits like these often mean holders are getting ready to sell.

The same thing happened after XRP hit its peak above $3.55 in July 2025. Whales sent large amounts to Binance back then. Daily net inflows went above $100 million several times, and one day set a record of almost $270 million.

Whales Are Buying, ETF Investors Are Selling

ETF investors are going the other way. XRP exchange-traded funds saw $19.1 million in net outflows in the week of November 9 to 13.

The ETF selling has slowed, though. According to an analyst, money is still leaving the funds, but much less than before. In the past, smaller ETF outflows have often come near the end of a selling phase.

Some of the Binance Data May Be Misleading

Not all of these withdrawals may be whales buying. Some could be Binance moving coins between its own wallets. That happened after the exchange was hacked between October 29 and November 4, when Binance had to refill its reserves. So the outflow numbers may overstate how much whales are really buying.

If funding stays negative much longer without a price rise, the case for a rebound gets weaker. Holders could start selling spot XRP, which would push funding rates back above zero.

Ripple’s RLUSD stablecoin has grown by about $300 million to $400 million in market value since early November. It grew even as the total supply of stablecoins across the market fell.

Still, the wider crypto market remains weak, and XRP is in a downtrend on longer-term charts.

XRP has not confirmed a bottom yet. It has not moved back above a key level near $0.99, and it has not retested its recent lows.

For now, a further drop looks more likely. In a weak setup, XRP could fall to $0.90, bounce only a little and then keep sliding. In that case, $0.85 is the more likely place for a real bottom.

A strong bounce at $0.90 would change this view. Until XRP shows strength, the risk of a deeper drop stays, even with whales pulling coins off Binance.
