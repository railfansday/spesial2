Bitcoin wallets could be linked to user IP addresses with 100% accuracy and for just about $0.34.

The flaw relies on the network's automatic rebroadcasting of small transactions that may have been out on hold by miners prioritizing higher fee transfers.

This is another in a recent line of Bitcoin vulnerabilities, including a hashrate hijacking spree and rumours of AI breaking ECC encryption.

According to Bitcoin Core contributor Abubakar Sadiq Ismail, the intrusion begins when an attacker sends low-fee ‘dust’ tokens to a BTC address.

Since miners prioritize higher-fee transactions, sending low amounts naturally takes longer time frames. The Bitcoin Core software automatically broadcasts stuck transactions after 12-36 hours, at which point an attacker is able to see the receiver’s IP address.

The method achieved a 100% success rate across 30 simulations on a regression test (regtest) network for only 18-34 cents per attempt.

At present, the flaw remains unpatched, and attackers are still able to get the exact location of Bitcoin wallets, in addition to viewing their balances.

And while no funds are at risk, such information could be used in crypto wrench attacks where an individual is physically targeted in a cryptocurrency robbery.

For now, Bitcoiners can choose manual wallet broadcasting in place of automatic broadcasts.

CoinPedia recently covered how attackers are hijacking Bitcoin hashrate, directing mining proceeds to their own wallets.

Even more, rumours still abound of OpenAI and Anthropic breaking several cryptographic security methods, including Elliptic Curve Cryptography, which Bitcoin relies upon.

The price of BTC remains unaffected by these reports, trading at around $60,000 for the last couple of days.
