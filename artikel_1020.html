Michael Carrick’s future as Manchester United manager is in severe doubt after overseeing the club’s worst start to the Premier League season in 40 years that has left them in the relegation zone, with Mauricio Pochettino looking set to replace him.

A second Old Trafford comeback of the week, this time battling from two goals down twice to rescue a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa on Sunday, might not be enough to save Carrick’s job and he is at risk of being sacked after just 10 games of the season and with his side 18th during the international break.

United ended a nine-game winless run when they came from two goals down to beat Roma in the Champions League last week but they have not won in eight attempts in the Premier League and have just one top-flight win this campaign.

Carrick survived crisis talks with the United hierarchy after defeats to Como and Bournemouth last month and was given time to turn results around, but speculation over a change begun the day after Sunday’s chaotic draw and The Independent understands contact was initially made with United States boss Pochettino over succeeding Carrick on Monday.

The Old Trafford hierarchy, including Jason Wilcox, Omar Berrarda and Sir Jim Ratcliffe, are set to meet in Monaco today to discuss Carrick’s future as the club are now close to agreeing a deal with Pochettino should they decide to move on from the current boss.

The Independent’s Chief Football Writer Miguel Delaney reports that Manchester United are close to an agreement with Mauricio Pochettino to become their next manager.

It is not yet rubber-stamped that Michael Carrick will be sacked, as the United hierarchy meet in Monaco to fully decide, but that is the expectation and Pochettino is now lined up as a replacement.

United will need to agree a deal to release Pochettino from his contract with the United States and as of right now, it is not clear that they have yet contacted US Soccer to open talks

Michael Carrick said he still expects to be working with Manchester United over the international break and insisted he will not walk away.

United sit 18th in the table and they are now eight league games without a win – a club record in the Premier League era – with their only victory of the season back in August against newly-promoted Ipswich.

Asked on Sky Sports if he would quit if he felt it was the right thing to do for the club, Carrick said: “That’s not going to happen.”

Then, speaking in his post-match press conference, Carrick expressed confidence his side could still turn things around this season.

“One hundred per cent,” he said. “I think there’s more than enough. Goals change games and we’ve got to deal with it and it’s something we have to improve on drastically. But there’s more than enough, we’re just making it too difficult for ourselves at the moment in certain times…

“I’m quite clear with what direction the club needs to go in. We’ve just got to win games.”

Roy Keane then went on to explain what impressed him about Man Utd’s display against Villa despite the result and the ‘dreadful’ defending.

“I think we’ve said previously there have been question marks over this team but they’re showing a bit of fight, a bit of desire and, as bad as the game was from a footballing point of view, they kept going to the end,” Keane explained.

“So, on the one hand, you say dreadful to give up four goals at home, but they kept going, so we have to give them a little bit of praise.

“In terms of the bigger picture, it’s that rollercoaster of watching United. Some bits were so, so bad. Defensively the way it started, the start of the second half, but they found a way to get a point in a game where they looked dead and buried.

“Still nowhere near good enough to try and get towards the middle of the table.”

Former Manchester United captain, Roy Keane, also spoke on Sky Sports following Carrick’s interview with Pete Davison and gave his thoughts on the situation.

He said: “It’s obviously difficult for the manager, he’s under pressure no doubt. He talks a lot but doesn’t say much – maybe that’s a tactic. He’s like a politician.

“He’s clearly under pressure and says he knows there are things to work on but he better get moving on that... This is their worst start in however many years.”

Michael Carrick endured an awkward end to a post-match interview following the 4-4 draw with Aston Villa as he had an excruciating exchange about his future as Manchester United manager.

His post-match interview on Sky Sports with reporter Patrick Davison was an awkward watch.

Davison asked a lengthy question suggesting there were clearly going to be conversations about Carrick’s future during the international break, to which Carrick simply responded: "Is there?"

When it was suggested that one would assume so, Carrick tersely said: “Would you?” and when he was pushed for what he thought about his situation, the Man United boss said to Davison: “You’re telling me, what do you think?”.

The legendary Scottish manager hailed Mauricio Pochettino back in 2016, with the Argentine strongly linked a decade on to succeed Michael Carrick after a dismal run of form for United.

There are many problems at Manchester United and one of the perceived issues, certainly with fans, is that the club have brought in players who are not good enough to excel at the club.

Diogo Dalot was the most recent recipent of the supporters’ ire following the 4-4 draw with Aston Villa.

Dalot was substituted during the game and the decision to remove him was cheered.

“We’ve got to be careful with that, I think,” Carrick said after the game.

“I understand the frustration. I understand the disappointment of the supporters, so I get it. We’re all frustrated. We’re all disappointed.

“The players are as much as anyone, I guarantee you that. Diogo gives absolutely everything. He’s played in different positions. He’s played left-back. He’s played really well at times.

“He gives a penalty away. He knows that, maybe he can do a little bit better. Alright, that’s part of it, he’s made a mistake.

“But he cares an awful lot, and he’s given everything, so I like what he brings to the team. I like what he brings to the group.”

Michael Carrick spoke about Man Utd’s tough run of results ahead of this current international break.

Following United’s 4-4 draw against Aston Villa on Sunday, Carrick said: “Yeah, it’s been a right mixed week or two or three, really, if you’re looking at the events.

“But I’m pretty clear where we need to go and how we need to get there.

“Obviously some of the boys will be flying off around the world and different things, and the group will be split for 10 days or so.

“But as a normal international week is, you know, we’ll have some training. We’ll have one or two days off, but not too much. But we’ll train, and we’ll get ready for the next game.”

Sky Sports News say that a verbal agreement in principle has been agreed betwen Mauricio Pochettino and Manchester United.

They say that United will want to move quickly if they sack Michael Carrick.

There are some details still to be worked out with Pochettino but the feelings are strong that no issues will occur.

United must talk to US Soccer to buy Pochettino out of his contract with the national team. That will also include a £7m fee + more if Pochettino’s staff come with him.

The United States head coach has a contract until the 2030 World Cup but the former Tottenham and Chelsea boss could emerge as a candidate to succeed Michael Carrick with United lodged inside the relegation zone.
