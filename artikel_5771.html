Trump orders military to hold off on Iowa lead azide plan pending approvals

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The 'Fox & Friends Weekend' panel discusses a legal battle after a court blocks the U.S. Army's plan to detonate tons of hazardous bomb-making chemicals in Iowa, sparking local fears of a toxic lead dust cloud.

President Donald Trump declared on Monday that he had ordered the military not to detonate degrading lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant unless a court and the Hawkeye State's Democratic Gov.-Elect Rob Sand give the green light for the plan.

"I feel the proper course is to get rid of this longterm problem but, at the same time, have ordered the Military not to do so until such time as the Eighth Circuit agrees that we can go forward, and am further making this decision fully subject to the incoming Governor Elect, Rob Sand, agreeing to the operation," Trump declared in part of a lengthy Monday Truth Social post.

"If he or the Courts do not agree, we will have no choice but to not proceed with the controlled removal of these hazardous materials, and be forced to hold the incoming Governor responsible for any and all death or destruction caused by this very unfortunate, and longterm, situation," Trump said.

The Army had already delayed its plan to detonate the material until Wednesday, once more postponing the explosion expected to send "lead-containing particles" into the air.

The detonation was previously scheduled for Friday but was then rescheduled for Monday, with Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle noting in a Thursday statement, "This will delay the detonation until the latest possible moment prior to a forecasted temperature drop, which would risk further destabilization of the material."

But the Army on Sunday announced another postponement until Wednesday in order "to allow for more optimal weather conditions and all mitigation measures to be emplaced."

ARMY DELAYS CONTROLLED DETONATION OF UNSTABLE BOMB-MAKING CHEMICAL IN IOWA

A sign identifying the facility sits inside a fence surrounding the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant on Nov. 19, 2026 in Middletown, Iowa. (Scott Olson/Getty Images)

An environmental assessment prepared by the Army explains the reason for the plan to detonate the material in Iowa rather than relocating it.

"In March 2026, during a proactive facility safety review, IAAAP identified a stockpile of lead azide, a highly sensitive primary explosive used in munitions detonators, that had degraded and crystallized over time," the assessment noted.

"Due to its chemical degradation, the material’s legal status transitioned from a viable production component to an unserviceable hazardous waste. Lead azide in a crystallized state becomes extremely shock-and-friction sensitive presenting a serious safety hazard to plant personnel and preventing safe physical transport of the material to standard off-site demilitarization facilities," the document explains.

But the detonation will impact local communities, an advisory issued last week explained.

"Extensive modeling efforts predict that the potential level of lead particles in the air may be elevated but well below the level at which EPA would require protective actions be taken. The detonation will result in an air plume with some lead-containing particles which will disperse in the air and deposit on the ground," according to the advisory.

"While safety measures will be fully enforced, the controlled detonation will cause temporary noise, ground vibrations, and a dust plume that will contain some lead. Residents in Middletown will hear a loud noise; feel vibrations from two consecutive, rapid detonations; and see a plume of dust which is expected to linger for no more than one hour after the detonation, depending on wind speed," the notice stated.

A legal challenge was lodged last week and the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit order on Thursday declared, "An administrative stay of the emergency permit issued by the Environmental Protection Agency is granted until the emergency motion for stay is ruled upon by this court."

But the U.S. government in a document on Saturday alerted the court that the Army planned to proceed with the detonation anyway.

"The Government notifies the Court that the Department of the Army ("Army") intends to invoke the emergency munitions and explosives exclusion to EPA’s regulatory and permitting Resource Conservation and Recovery Act ("RCRA") requirements, under which the Army may perform treatment by controlled detonation of the lead azide waste without the Emergency Permit that is challenged in this petition," the filing noted. "The Army must perform a controlled detonation to protect the public from the more significant threat from an uncontrolled explosion, and the Army intends to act by November 24, 2026."

While the Army had announced on Sunday that the detonation would be delayed until Wednesday, Nov. 25, Trump, issued his Truth Social post on Monday declaring that the project would not move forward without the approval of the court and the governor-elect.

"Iowa is a very special place to me, a place that I won, by substantial margins, in all three Elections but, whether I won or lost, would never deter me from making a correct decision. As everyone knows, there is a Military ‘clean up’ operation, without which, a very dangerous situation exists. In 2009, the Obama Administration improperly stored a vast number of explosives, and the Experts, who know it well, and have been studying it for many years, feel there is a substantial chance that this material will explode of its own volition, perhaps sometime in the near future," Trump wrote in the post.

"We have an opportunity to do a highly controlled detonation of these dangerous and rapidly corroding materials. The Experts, in particular the United States Military, feel that this approach will be a far safer one than allowing the situation to fester, not knowing when they will detonate by themselves. This operation would, in other words, be a controlled one rather than a large, spontaneous explosion. The weather, right now, is optimal for this planned response to take place. I feel the proper course is to get rid of this longterm problem but, at the same time, have ordered the Military not to do so until such time as the Eighth Circuit agrees that we can go forward, and am further making this decision fully subject to the incoming Governor Elect, Rob Sand, agreeing to the operation," he noted, before noting his plan to defer to the court and Sand on whether to proceed.
