Cowboys react to defensive play-calling change: What Christian Parker said

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Dallas Cowboys defensive coordinator Christian Parker faced the music head-on on Thursday, Nov. 19.

One day after the Cowboys announced they were stripping Parker of his defensive play-calling duties, Parker answered questions about his present emotions and his future in Dallas.

According to Joseph Hoyt of the Dallas Morning News, Parker said he tried to push back against the decision to change the reassignment of play-calling to senior defensive assistant Mike Pettine. But head coach Brian Schottenheimer, owner Jerry Jones and executive vice president Stephen Jones were "all on the same page" about making the move, Schottenheimer said on Nov. 18.

COWBOYS: Dallas strips defensive coordinator of play-calling duties

"Not an ideal situation for me personally, but it's what's best for the team moving forward, so that's how we're moving forward," Parker said.

Though Parker will no longer call the plays for the defense, he remains the Cowboys' defensive coordinator. Schottenheimer said in his press conference on Nov. 18 that the DC will continue to lead meetings and walkthroughs for the unit.

"I want to be very, very clear that Christian Parker is a huge part of the solution," Schottenheimer said.

Parker, who is in his first season with the Cowboys this year, took over a Dallas defense after an abysmal 2025 that was among the worst in franchise history. Through 10 games, he was unable to right the ship as the unit's play-caller.

Dallas has allowed 28.2 points per outing, which is third most in the NFL, and 393.8 yards per game, which is the most in the NFL. Only the 2-7 Philadelphia Eagles and 3-7 Indianapolis Colts have fewer takeaways than the Cowboys' eight so far this year.

The former Eagles passing game coordinator and defensive backs coach said his future in Dallas past 2026 is uncertain after the change to his duties.

"We'll cross that bridge when we get there," Parker said.

Parker was more verbose during his Nov. 19 media availability than Cowboys defensive tackle Quinnen Williams was one day prior.

The four-time Pro Bowler delivered the same six-word response to 12 different questions reporters asked him on Wednesday, Nov. 18: "I'm gonna keep that to myself."

Williams' method of repeating the same response is not a new tactic at NFL press conferences.

His six-word refrain was reminiscent of the four-word response (and its variations) former Patriots head coach Bill Belichick used five times in a row during a 2014 press conference: "We're on to Cincinnati." It was also similar to the seven-word repeated response from former NFL running back Marshawn Lynch later that same season: "I'm here so I won't get fined."

Williams first joined the Cowboys in 2025 as part of a trade deadline deal with the New York Jets that included a 2027 first-round pick as part of the Jets' return. Dallas signed the defensive tackle to a three-year, $105.9 million extension before this year, his first full season with the team.

Through 10 games playing in Parker's defense, Williams leads the NFL with 15 tackles for a loss. He also has three forced fumbles, one pass defense and is halfway to matching a career high with six sacks so far.

The Cowboys are 4-6 and second place in the NFC East. They're coming off of a Week 10 loss to the 49ers in which they allowed 46 points and 584 yards of total offense. Dallas will host the Tennessee Titans for Week 11 on Sunday, Nov. 22.
