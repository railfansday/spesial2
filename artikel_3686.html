Unpacking Nicki Minaj’s MAGA Style Evolution

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

This is the October 28, 2026 edition of Fit Check, your front row seat to the power players, peacocks, and deep pockets of fashion—and how they influence the rest of the world.

Pieter Mulier’s Versace debut will be marked with a couture show and the runway revival of Atelier Versace in January, and Bottega Veneta is back on Instagram for the first time since 2021—pouring one out to the fan accounts that had been enjoying their window of opportunity. Otherwise, the fashion news cycle has taken a break so far this week, so I’ve turned my attention to the midterm elections. Below, find missives on Nicki Minaj’s Gucci skirt suit by Demna—which was not provided to her by the brand—and the old style trick the Dems have adopted to appeal to us common folk. Plus, I get into why people love to clown Drake for his outfits. (For the record, I thought his Hermès by Martin Margiela fringed cape was fabulous.)

I wrote this edition of Fit Check wearing a sequined ghost costume (a ghost writer, get it?). It was my attempt at finding the right mindset to unpack what the internet has dubbed “gay Halloween,” for which I enlisted some of my fellow friends of Dorothy, and other experts, to help explain the formerly niche, now very viral internet phenomenon.

Mentioned in this issue: Jon Ossoff, Ralph Lauren, Bailey Moon, Juliet Charlotte, Marc Jacobs, Ariana Grande, Jonathan Anderson smoking a cigarette by the Seine, the breakup Post-it from a 2003 episode of Sex and the City, and many more.

“These Dem men with their hiked sleeves are taking me out,” read a comment under a clip of Senator Jon Ossoff and Pete Buttigieg on TikTok. “So hot.”

Ossoff and Buttigieg are the latest notable adopters of what appears to be the Democratic sartorial strategy for the ongoing midterm elections: to roll up their sleeves, literally. In a stark contrast to the most visible men on the right, who are all properly suited up both on the Hill in Washington, DC, and out on the campaign trail, “Dem men,” including Barack Obama and Gavin Newsom, have all been sporting a more casual look while campaigning. Individually, there’s nothing particular about the look. Obama and Buttigieg are former government officials; they’re technically off duty. Ossoff seems to want to position himself as a man of the people, and Newsom is working on embodying the anti-Trump. And yet, when seen together, the Dem men seem to have all agreed in a group chat to look a bit more working-class. No meticulously tailored Ralph Lauren suits, just dudes at work.

“There is a certain formula for cultivating a relaxed, approachable image while campaigning, versus the more polished presentation that comes with actually holding office,” says stylist Bailey Moon, who consulted with Dr. Jill Biden and Joe Biden during their time at the White House and worked with Tim Walz on the campaign trail in 2024. “It’s amusing to me, the idea that exposing your forearms suddenly makes you a man of the people, but rolling up your sleeves has become a political and metaphorical shorthand for being hardworking, accessible, and ready to get it done,” he says.

So is there a Dem men ’fit-pic group chat? “I don’t think there’s a coordinated effort,” Moon says. “I do think that politicians respond to a certain visual vocabulary, and right now, in a bid to win back working-class voters, that vocabulary is less formal and more familiar.”

Politicians do, in fact, think about their clothes more than we might think. “The visual language is always considered, even when the clothing itself isn’t front and center,” Moon says. “The challenge is making that feel instinctive rather than rehearsed, which ultimately comes down to the principal (key player) and how they carry it.” Ossoff et al. may not be folding up their sleeve cuffs as a joint campaign effort, and they may not be doing so to invoke some thirst in the comments section, but they’re making choices.

Drake celebrated his 40th birthday in New York City last weekend wearing black leather pants, a black turtleneck, and a brown fringed cape that got everyone talking. The rapper became the punch line of the weekend online before his outerwear piece was revealed as being a design by Martin Margiela during his time at Hermès—an archival holy grail find that Drake’s stylist, Juliet Charlotte, sourced from Canadian vintage seller Alex Maxamenko. He was called “Pumpkin Spice Papi,” a nod to his nickname and Instagram handle, @champagnepapi. He was compared to the Real Housewives venturing to Aspen on a girls trip. He was described as a rich auntie, and, my favorite, as ready for “Christian-girl autumn.”

The jokes were admittedly quite funny, and as we get into the “gay Halloween” phenomenon below, actor and comedian Sima Sepehri has already turned the viral moment into her costume. Yet underneath the surface of this mostly good-spirited fun—and my apologies for being the fun police—lies a layer of homophobia and, yes, misogyny. Drake has long been a punch line in the music industry and within hip-hop. People online have mocked him for the decor of his home and suggested he’d had a BBL or was showing off fake abs. He’s been called a wuss, and, pardon my French, a pussy. Which is where the issues with his style becoming a punch line begin.

Most of the jokes around Drake’s style compare him to women—to white women and their keenness for the fall season, to rich reality TV stars, to family matriarchs, and more. It’s simply too easy to call Drake and his penchant for fashion “feminine” or “fruity,” as if most famous men today, from his fellow rappers to athletes and actors, didn’t share his same affection for fashion and style. In a way, it’s not that serious. (And how he wore the shawl did look like he belonged more at a ski chalet than in the city.) Except that it kind of is; we have somehow not been able to shake gay as a zinger, and that’s generally because, culturally, we still consider femininity—and therefore women—to be less than masculinity and manliness. Let Drake wear his shawl. You’ve paid for it with your streams.

President Donald Trump and Nicki Minaj at a campaign event in Michigan.

President Trump made headlines last week for his comments on Senator Jon Husted’s physique, particularly his rear end. But he wasn’t done. This week, Trump attempted to crack a joke about it. “By the way, I looked at one hell of a beautiful ass before, Nicki Minaj,” the president said on Sunday at a Republican campaign rally in Michigan. “That’s hard to miss,” he said, before looking at the rapper and instructing her to “stand up!” Minaj did, and waved to the crowd and the president as if thanking him for the…uh, compliment.

Minaj has been a puzzling character to watch as she ventures deeper into the MAGA-sphere—she’s even been jokingly floated as a potential next White House press secretary. (Minaj held a mock interview about Trump aboard Air Force One, which was posted to X by White House communications director Steven Cheung with the caption, “Ladies and gentlemen, I think we may have found the new White House press secretary! @NICKIMINAJ.”)

On Sunday, the rapper was wearing a skirt suit by Gucci, retailing for about $4,000. Her look was finished with Pink Friday press-on nails, her own brand, and matching Gucci-logo pumps. In a way, the outfit is a departure from what one usually sees onstage at MAGA rallies, where luxury fashion—and particularly logos—rarely reign supreme unless coming from the first lady. Minaj, unlike other Trump acolytes, is often clad in on-trend designer clothes (when she joined President Trump at the Treasury Department Trump Accounts Summit in January of this year, she was wearing an Alaïa dress by Pieter Mulier), and while the idea of a prim skirt suit certainly suggested she was dressed for business, or at least for politics, the loud, allover monogram logo felt like a remnant of the musical superstar that was, or that remains somewhere underneath the surface.

As for what this means for Gucci, its clients likely won’t find the image of Minaj deterring—it’s not like the brand is underwriting Minaj’s appearance or the messaging around it. But perhaps the image of Minaj onstage will inspire the MAGA set to adopt Demna’s revamp of the house—some of the caricatural archetypes in his most recent collection certainly leaned conservative. Funnily enough, Karlie Kloss, wife of Josh Kushner and sister-in-law to Jared, wore a similar pencil skirt at Demna’s first Gucci runway show.
