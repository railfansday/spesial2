Penn State Alum Indicted for Allegedly Distributing Cocaine to Fraternities

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A former Penn State student was indicted by a federal grand jury on narcotics distribution charges after he was accused of being “major supplier” of cocaine to the school’s fraternities in a drug trafficking ring scandal.

United States Attorney Brian D. Miller and Pennsylvania Attorney General Dave Sunday announced in a Wednesday, November 18, press release that Agostino Abbatiello was charged with one count of conspiracy to distribute cocaine and six counts of distribution of cocaine.

The federal prosecutors said that Abbatiello, 24, was the largest dealer of cocaine on the Penn State campus and that he was responsible for distribution of more than 11 pounds of the drug from January 2023 through December 2024, which went to the Sigma Chi and Delta Upsilon fraternities.

Abbatiello was allegedly both a dealer and frequent user of the cocaine, and he’s been accused of continuing his operation even after law enforcement launched an investigation into drug trafficking on the campus.

He allegedly purchased cocaine from suppliers in Philadelphia and New York and recruited others on campus to help him obtain the supply and transport it to the college.

Once he got the cocaine to the campus, Abbatiello allegedly had other fraternity brothers and pledges prepare and package it for resale.

“College should be a marketplace of ideas and not illegal drugs,” Miller said in a statement. “Our office will continue working with the Pennsylvania Attorney General’s office, and with our law enforcement partners to identify, investigate, and hold accountable those who profit from illegal drug activity in our communities.”

Sunday added that the “defendant was the top rung of the ladder for a high-level drug trafficking ring that supplied cocaine to Penn State University students, incorporating some fraternity pledges into the packaging process.”

“We are proud of the fine investigative work involved in building this case, thank the grand jurors for their diligence, and look forward to having a hand in prosecuting with our ready and able partners at the U.S. Attorney’s Office,” Sunday said.

Abbatiello has pleaded not guilty to the recent indictment.

The Westbury, New York, native previously pleaded not guilty in August when he was hit with 11 state charges in Pennsylvania, including corrupt organizations, conspiracy, dealing in proceeds of unlawful activity and manufacturing or delivering drugs.

Abbatiello was one of 14 people who has been charged in state court in connection to the drug trafficking ring at the college.

His lawyer, Philip Masorti, did not immediately respond to Us’ request for comment.
