Worried You'll Run Out of Money in Retirement? This Social Security Move Could Help

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Scoring larger checks could help ease your fears.

By Maurie Backman – Nov 24, 2026 at 4:18AM EST

Running out of money is something even wealthy retirees worry about. After all, you have no idea going into retirement how long you'll live, what your exact expenses will look like, and what financial surprises might be in store. So even if you manage to accumulate a few million dollars in a retirement account, there may be a nagging fear that it'll all be gone one day.

Boosting your Social Security checks may not entirely alleviate that fear. But it could help. And you should know that there's an easy way to score a massive boost to your monthly benefits.

You're allowed to claim retirement benefits from Social Security at any point once you turn 62. And you can collect those monthly checks without a reduction once you reach full retirement age, which is 67 if you were born in 1960 or later.

But for each year you delay your Social Security claim past full retirement age, until age 70, your benefits get a permanent 8% boost. So if your full retirement age is 67, you have an opportunity to set yourself up with monthly checks that are 24% larger. And that could give you the peace of mind you need to feel more comfortable navigating retirement.

The more money Social Security pays you each month, the less you should need to tap your savings. And the less you withdraw from your savings each month, the lower your chances should be of running out.

Being able to rely more on Social Security could be especially helpful during periods of market decline. Riding out downturns and letting your savings recover each time should reduce your chances of reaching $0.

Plus, Social Security benefits get built-in inflation protection. Each year, they're eligible for a cost-of-living adjustment, or COLA. The larger your benefits are to begin with, the more valuable those COLAs should be over time, and the more you can safeguard your finances during periods when costs are on the rise.

Delaying Social Security isn't necessarily the best choice for everyone. There may be good reasons to claim benefits sooner, such as poor health, a pressing financial need due to a layoff, a major expense that can't wait, or other circumstances.

But if running out of money in retirement is a big fear of yours, it pays to consider delaying your Social Security claim as long as possible. Boosted benefits could help you feel better about your financial situation, which could lead you to enjoy retirement more overall.
