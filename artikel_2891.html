Knicks 111, Raptors 102: “Let it be 15 straight”

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

One of the more prominent flies caught in the interwebs these days was the nomination of Robert Pattinson as the new lead for the James Bond franchise. Yes, the guy who made daughters melt by the millions about two decades ago and who always looks like the vampire that made him prominent drank all the blood from his veins. Apparently he's a really good actor, but pardon me if I'm not acting like I'm some kind of wannabe film critic just because I like movies every once in a while.

For my liking, the 007 I loved ended with the retirement of Roger Moore, a second gut punch not needed after the grim realization that Sean Connery's time in Her Majesty's Service was over (we ignore Roger Lazenby for obvious reasons). Before watching any of the newer films, I always much rather flopped in one of the classics, because with the world spinning faster every day, there are just some things in life I'd like to stay the way they are. Like that bank account from December before all the insurance companies wish you a happy new year or that favorite dish your Grandma used to make before she discovered light whipping cream. And while we're at it, the fact that the New York Knicks do not lose to the Toronto Raptors.

After watching the Knicks almost win a carbon copy of their game on Sunday in Toronto for their 15th in a row against the Dinos, it truly feels like some divine decree puts some prehistoric panic on the Raptors in the final minutes and sprinkles just enough calmness on the orange and blue to pull it out in the end. Just like Unmitigated Gall wrote in the celebratory Postgame Thread: "Sayeth the Lord: Let it be 15 straight". But maybe it's also just pure and legit NBA talent taking over, and prevailing over the guys who are airballing free throws or thought it was a good idea to give Jakob Poeltl north of 100 million dollars to play basketball.

Even if Jalen Brunson shot it as purely as "The Man with the Golden Gun," late everything for New York started and ended last night with Karl-Anthony Towns. Early in the season, he looks sharper than Oddjob's razor-edged hat (shoutout to "Tosh Togo" Harold Sakata) and, on top of it, more powerful than Richard Kiel's "Jaws". KAT's dominance was on full display against a team that could only put defenders on him who were either too small or too slow. Dismantling those adversaries wasn't even the most impressive thing the 31-year-old did, as his passing at times was as beautiful as Honey Ryder, Tatiana Romanova and Miss Galore combined.

To be serious for a second: It is astonishing how Karl-Anthony Towns has turned around his career after more than a decade in the league. He is by far the most consistent Knick right now, has cut down on his foul issues for the time being, and looks extremely decisive on both ends of the court. In Minnesota, he always gave the impression of lacking professionalism, and now he looks like the most serious player on a team full of guys who seemingly laughed the last time the Raptors or Nets beat the Knicks. Maybe "You only live twice" as a basketball player, but in KAT's case that looks to be plenty enough.

The "License to Kill" in the end, of course, rests with his buddy about a foot shorter than him. You wouldn't believe it watching the first half last night as the Raptors' eyes got as wide seeing Jalen Brunson defend them as Auric Goldfinger's were when he saw that shiny gold bar on the 18th green at Stoke Park. If there was a possession where Toronto didn't attack Brunson in the first half, I seriously cannot remember it. The same goes for any offensive possession that didn't end up with a late shot clock. It was really a desperate look at times, so desperate that Mike Brown even turned to a zone in the second half, called blitzes when Toronto barely crossed midcourt, and gave sparkplug Jose Alvarado the nod late in the third quarter. And as so often in the past, some of that stuff worked (we still do not like those traps, Mikey).

With the game on the line, the Knicks turned into a rolling "Thunderball" that just couldn't be stopped. Mikal Bridges locked down on defense for the second straight game, the backup point guard duelists provided numerous little plays, OG Anunoby smiled, and Landry Shamet once again looked like some cool device Q designed in an underground bunker – the red button unleashes movement threes and hellish defense on the opponent. And you have to deal with KAT and Brunson on top of all that? Good luck. When the orange and blue have it going like that, it seems as if they could even make some of those megalomaniac Bond villain plots for world supremacy work. Everything seems possible – even the Captain swatting shots and turning into Jalen Dikembe Mutombo Mpolondo Mukamba Jean-Jacques Wamutombo Brunson. RIP to the legend.

Since "Diamonds Are Forever," we really do not need a constant reminder of everything these Knicks are capable of; we still remember last year pretty well. That doesn't mean it does not feel extremely awesome and puts a comfortable calm into a fan's heart to see them clicking on all cylinders. It would be nice if these cylinders wouldn't take three quarters to get going, but as long as the Knicks go home with goddess Victory, there's nothing wrong with things the way they are. Just like there probably is nothing wrong with Robert Pattinson playing James Bond, so let us all wish him well.

And let's hope for him he's not a Raptors fan.
