Studs and duds from Houston’s 26-3 rout against the Colts

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Houston Texans have a win against each of their division opponents after dominating the Indianapolis Colts 26-3 on Thursday Night Football.

It is their second-largest win against the Colts in franchise history.

Indianapolis actually scored first with a field goal on their opening drive of the game. Houston's opening drive ended in an interception from quarterback C.J. Stroud, giving the Colts a chance to add to their lead. However, after their kicker missed a 56-yard field goal, the momentum greatly shifted in favor of the Texans and they never looked back.

Houston would go on to score 26 unanswered points, including touchdowns from Nico Collins, Kayshon Boutte and rookie tight end Marlin Klein. They also had a safety from All-Pro Will Anderson Jr and a field goal from kicker Ka'imi Fairbairn. Defensively, they forced the Colts to punt three times and also stopped them on third down 10 times.

Here are the studs and duds from Houston's fourth win of the season, including defensive end Jadeveon Clowney, who had a sack just before halftime, and receiver Xavier Hutchinson, who only had two receptions in this game.

For the third game in a row, Stroud finished a game with at least three touchdown passes. He is playing better than most quarterbacks in the league, finished this game with a completion percentage of nearly 80 percent and moved on quickly from his interception on the opening drive.

This game was nearly perfect for Stroud and he is playing like a quarterback who could compete in the playoffs.

It is not often Anderson accomplishes a career-first, but he did that on Thursday night. This was his first regular-season game with a safety. Houston's last safety was in the divisional round of the playoffs in the 2024 season; ironically, Anderson was also responsible for that safety.

In addition to his safety, he also had four quarterback hits and five total tackles, which tied for the third-most of anyone on the team. He greatly added to his Defensive Player of the Year campaign with this game.

After being targeted nine times last week against the Cleveland Browns, Hutchinson was a non-factor in this game with only three targets and two receptions for 13 yards. Part of this is because of Houston's early lead, which allowed the Texans to run out the clock with the run game. Still, early in the game, Hutchinson was not involved much despite being on the field most of the game for Houston's offense.

There are inevitably some players who will not get the ball as much as Stroud spreads the ball around, but as the No. 2 receiver, you still expect him to get the ball more.

Montgomery was out for several weeks before coming back in this game. Prior to his injury, Montgomery was incredibly ineffective, averaging just 2.7 yards per carry. However, he had the best game of his Houston career on the ground with 107 rushing yards, averaging 4.1 yards per carry.

He added another 17 receiving yards on three receptions. If the Texans can continue to establish a run game, they could go on a run in the second half of the season and become a playoff contender.

Clowney finished this game with four tackles and one sack, but his sack came at the perfect time. Indianapolis was trying to get something going on offense just before halftime when Clowney got into the backfield and sacked Colts quarterback Daniel Jones to end the first half. He now has four sacks in the past three games, which is incredibly impressive, especially for someone in their 13th season in the NFL.

Similar to Hutchinson, Dell was a non-factor for essentially the entire game. All three of his catches came on one drive and he was not on the field as much since Kayson Boutte seemed to step up more with Marvin Harrison Jr. out. He made the most of his three receptions with 27 yards, but it still seems like Dell could have done more in this game.

Houston's second-round pick finally found the end zone in this game. Rookie Marlin Klein had his first career touchdown and also had the most receptions of anyone on the team in this win after having just one catch before this game. Perhaps after this game, Klein is going to become a larger part of this offense as it seems like they are phasing out Dalton Schultz, who only had one target in this game.

Special teams always deserves attention, especially punter Kai Kroeger. He averaged 54.7 yards per punt in this game, including a 62-yard punt. However, his best punt of the night was a 56-yarder that pinned the Colts at their five-yard line. That punt ultimately set up Anderson's safety. Kroeger's contribution to that play should not be overlooked.
