SpaceX and Tesla have picked five independent directors to run the special committees negotiating a possible merger, the Financial Times reported on Tuesday, citing people familiar with the matter. SpaceX is represented by Roelof Botha and Randy Glein, while Tesla’s side includes board chair Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson and Jack Hartung. The committees have hired bankers to build the financial models that will shape each side’s position in an all-stock deal. Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs are advising SpaceX, and Evercore is working for Tesla. The advisers are also mapping where the two businesses overlap. Elon Musk said on SpaceX’s October earnings call that combining his AI, robotics, car and space companies would be a good idea, and confirmed the committees had been set up. Major regulatory obstacles remain, including SpaceX’s US defence contracts and Tesla’s heavy exposure to China. Bloomberg has reported a deal could be announced as soon as December.
