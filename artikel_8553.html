The way I've been approaching it, is that if the two funds are following different indexes they are likely not substantially identical for any number of reasons. Number of holdings was mentioned as one difference.
I came across this tonight: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/ ... 2024.06944
It's mostly about studying how common ETF tax loss swapping is, so the most interesting part of the paper is the endnotes, and the discussion on the top left of page 9893. Endnote 7 talks about Cottage Savings, which was not a 1091 wash sale case, but nonetheless has been talked about on bogleheads.org, and is a precedent suggesting one of the more pro-taxpayer interpretations of ETF wash sales might prevail.

Here: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/supp ... 44.sm1.pdf is the online appendix they mention, with a survey of opinions about when ETFs could be substantially identical, but it's mostly industry sources, and thus just a repetition of the same arguments made on bogleheads.org.
