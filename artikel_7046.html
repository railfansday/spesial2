Is the Kitchen the New Living Room?

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Find inspiration for your next kitchen renovation.

Every item on this page was chosen by an ELLE Decor editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

Above: Eleven Madison Park chef Daniel Humm’s loftlike apartment blurs traditional boundaries.

Plenty of living happens in the kitchen. It’s a creative comfort zone for the culinarily inclined, the hub of daily activity for many families, and often the most crowded room at a party.

So why shouldn’t the design reflect that? We looked back at some of our favorite kitchens featured in ELLE Decor, from chef Daniel Humm’s to designer Joy Moyler’s, to share what elevates these spaces beyond elegant function and material beauty into inviting destinations. “Where is the kitchen?” Humm asked. “It’s there, but I don’t want it to be all you see.”

Instead, this kitchen—and the rest that follow—make us want to look twice and stay a while.

Gather ’round. No offense to the ever-popular, undeniably functional kitchen island, which, paired with some counter stools, can be a great spot for grabbing a quick breakfast, tackling homework with a snack in the afternoon, or perching with an evening glass of wine. But certain spaces call out for something different. A deftly chosen table with chic, interesting chairs can fulfill the same duties with a welcome lightness and ease, not to mention a ton more flexibility.

Wave goodbye to the island, and vast new design horizons open up. What type of table will you choose? Square or round? Legs or pedestal base? Wood, stone, metal, or another material? Perhaps one with expansion leaves for holiday gatherings or blowout buffets? Whatever style suits your fancy, add the right chairs and it’s a party.

Choose from, say, India Mahdavi’s colorful seating, a structured option from starchitects Herzog & de Meuron, or a classic collectible by Jean Prouvé. Be sure to make plenty of space for stemware, meandering conversation late into the night. Don’t say we didn’t warn you.

Circle: Aqua table by Fabrice Berrux for Roche Bobois

Turn any dinner party into a roundtable chat with this shapely Roche Bobois design—no guest is a corner outcast.

The more space-efficient cousin of the circle table exudes modern grace, as exemplified by this Scavolini table.

Contemporary and crisply balanced, this Antonio Citterio design for Maxalto can be a perfect fit for certain small spaces.

Classic American craftsmanship, warm cherry wood, and elegant midcentury style define this piece by Stickley

Don’t be afraid to play with pattern and palette when it comes to cabinets. “I’m always looking for new materials and putting them together in an unexpected way,” says Elle Decor A-List designer Laura Gonzalez of a Paris project, below. Go for restrained whimsy with Ikea’s -terra-cotta-color wave pattern fronts or Isla Porter’s crafty cutout design. And don’t neglect the backsplash, which can be a statement-making creative canvas unto itself.

Consider trading the parade of industry-meets-farmhouse pendants for something more sculptural, more fabulous to play against the kitchen’s functional identity. There’s no reason you can’t bask in the amber glow of a Saint-Louis crystal chandelier while frying an egg.

Utility doesn’t have to mean boredom. “The brief was to create a gathering place,” says Elle Decor A-List designer Joy Moyler of her project, above. Form and function stylishly blend in faucets such as one by the Danish brand Vola in a -powder-coated pistachio-green finish or a Dornbracht design that has stone handles. For something sleek and understated, Kohler’s minimalist four-in-one faucet offers a choice: still or sparkling?

Out-of-the-ordinary pulls are a savvy way to add personality.
