SoftBank drops 15% as Wall Street AI sell-off ripples across Asian tech names

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Asian technology stocks tumbled Wednesday, led by a rout in Japanese semiconductor stocks, as renewed concerns over the artificial intelligence trade rattles investors following an overnight sell-off on Wall Street.

Japanese semiconductor stocks tied to the global AI supply chain were among the hardest hit. Advantest, a supplier of chip-testing equipment to Nvidia, plunged 13%. Tokyo Electron, which supplies chipmaking equipment to Nvidia's manufacturing partner TSMC, slumped 12.82%.

Technology-focused investor SoftBank Group shed 15%, while semiconductor inspection equipment maker Lasertec lost 11.23%.

Other Japanese chip stocks also came under heavy selling pressure, with Disco Corporation falling 9.41% and Renesas Electronics losing 9%.

Tech stocks have seen heightened volatility over the past few months amid concerns over capital expenditure, skepticism over return on investments, circular AI financing, with Jensen Huang's ouster as Nvidia CEO adding fresh worries, and his return sparking a massive rally.

Rolf Bulk, an analyst at The Futurum Group, said the renewed sell-off largely reflects lingering concerns over the sustainability of AI spending beyond 2027, with Huang's return doing little to fundamentally alter the outlook.

"There are still concerns on the sustainability of AI demand, ability of the sector to finance the build-out, and questions around power supply," Bulk said, adding that Japanese chip equipment makers were particularly vulnerable to a potential slowdown in AI infrastructure spending, given the highly cyclical nature of their business.

Spending on wafer fabrication equipment could decline materially if AI demand slows from 2028 onward, Bulk said. South Korean memory chipmakers, by contrast, could be relatively insulated due to supply shortages.

The country's memory chip giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, however, plunged more than 8%. The two are key suppliers of the high-bandwidth memory chips used in AI accelerators. Other South Korean technology stocks also traded lower, with LG Innotek shedding 5%.

While the memory industry is also cyclical, Bulk expects shortages to intensify over the next two years, making a return to equilibrium between supply and demand in 2028 unlikely.

In Taiwan, shares TSMC tumbled 7%, while Hon Hai Precision Industry plunged 8.6%. Electronic components maker Foxconn Technology shed 4.93%.

Some Chinese technology stocks listed in Hong Kong bucked the broader regional sell-off, with Xiaomi jumping 4.6%, Kuaishou gaining 2.8% and Alibaba adding 0.7%. Tencent was marginally higher, while Meituan and Baidu was up 0.31% and 0.16% respectively.

U.S. technology stocks fell overnight with the Nasdaq Composite giving back some of the sharp gains from the previous session that were fueled by Huang's return as Nvidia CEO.
