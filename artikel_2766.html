Bears' Jeffrey Simmons will force Lions to be 'on point'

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

ALLEN PARK — The Chicago Bears acquired a new monster midway through the season, and he'll be intent on wreaking havoc on the Detroit Lions' offensive line this Thursday, Nov. 26.

Defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons has been every bit of the player the Bears hoped they were getting when spending two first-round picks to trade for him before this year's trade deadline. He's had one sack in each of his first two games before delivering a three-sack performance in Sunday, Nov. 22's win over the New Orleans Saints, a player who clearly changes the complexion of Chicago's defensive ceiling in 2026 and beyond.

"When you're athletic and explosive like he is, you've gotta be locked in on every play, because he can blow a rep up, and he can blow a series up," Lions coach Dan Campbell said on Monday, Nov. 23.

"You just gotta be on point, man. You can't have any lapses. You've gotta be on top of your game when he's able to do that."

Detroit has averaged 33 points in its last two games, but on Thursday, the multi-time All-Pro might be the necessary ingredient to neutralize the Lions' offense in a pivotal NFC North showdown.

Since joining Chicago before the Bears' Week 8 game against the Seattle Seahawks, the 29-year-old Simmons has taken his game to another level. He has 12 pressures to go with five sacks, plus eight run stops with six tackles for loss.

And he's pulling his new teammates up with him. In the win over New Orleans, the Bears had a pressure rate of 47.1%, the second-highest they've had in a game this season. Edge rusher Montez Sweat has also become a more consistent player, registering 12 of his 25 pressures and two of his four sacks in that stretch; so has Austin Booker, who's recorded 11 of his 24 pressures and to go with a sack over his last three games; as has Grady Jarrett, who has nine of his 24 pressures in his last three games, along with his only sack of the season.

The Bears are also blitzing far less because of the firepower they have up front.

"Not only are you getting his production and what he's able to do, it makes everybody around him better," Campbell said.

The Lions have struggled at times to mitigate other teams' fronts, even in Sunday's win over the Buccaneers, when they scored 31 points and allowed only one sack despite facing pressure a season-high 46.7% of dropbacks.

Keeping Simmons in check will be paramount to the Lions' seventh straight win, putting Detroit's "growing" offensive line to the ultimate test.

"Man, we are youthful, but that youth is growing up fast. Penei (Sewell) is our anchor. Juice (Scruggs) has played some football, but he's new to us at center, but he's getting better. And those other three, (Christian) Mahogany and (Tate) Ratledge and (Blake) Miller, they're gaining strength, man," Campbell said after Sunday's win. "They're getting better."
