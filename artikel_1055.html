All you need to know about World Darts Championship

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

There is less than a month to go before London's amphitheatre of arrows, Alexandra Palace, kickstarts the festive period by hosting the World Darts Championships 2027; watch all the action on Sky Sports from Friday December 11th

Luke Littler, Luke Humphries and Gerwyn Price - the modern day Three Wise Men. The trio will be igniting Christmas spirits at 'The People's Palace' as they battle it out in darts' most prestigious competition - the World Darts Championship.

A total of 128 players will arrive ready to let fly, but only one will leave crowned as world champion and claim the joint largest prize in the sport's history... £1m.

Alongside this record prize pot, the event will for the first time be held in the Alexandra Palace's Great Hall, meaning new attendance records will be set.

The 34th World Darts Championship starts on Friday December 11, running until Thursday January 3, 2027.

Arrows will be flying every day over the festive period, except for Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, Boxing Day, and New Year's Eve.

Alexandra Palace's West Hall has been the competition's home for over 19 years.

However, this year fans will revel in all the action in the Great Hall, which has allowed for the sale of around 180,000 tickets across all sessions.

More fans, a bigger atmosphere, but at the same legendary venue.

Forty-eight of the 128 players competing have earned their places via international and association qualifying events wth the remaining 80 places going to those top-ranked on the PDC Order of Merit (40) and ProTour Order of Merit (40) circuits.

Thirty-eight players have already earned their place at the oche via these qualifiers; most recently Gary Mawson, José Marquês and Anton Östlund via the CDC Continental Cup, PDCE Mediterranean Qualifier and PDC Nordic & Baltic Championship respectively.

Though not officially secured, reigning champion Luke Littler, former World No 1 Luke Humphries and darting royalty Michael van Gerwen are mathematically assured to be there.

Ten places at the oche remain, though that does include the World Youth Championship Final on November 29. If the winner has already qualified via the ProTour, then that place will defer to the Tour Card Holder Qualifier.

November 13: PDC Europe Super League (DACH) - 1 place

November 29: Czech Republic Qualifier - 1 place

November 30: PDC Tour Card Holder Qualifier - 4+ places

Nineteen-year-old sensation, Luke 'The Nuke' Littler enters this year's tournament as the two-time reigning champion.

Last year, Littler defeated Gian van Veen 7-1 to retain the Sid Waddell Trophy and become the fourth player in PDC history to win consecutive World Championships, following Phil Taylor, Adrian Lewis and Gary Anderson.

Littler is the current world number one and already has 30 senior PDC titles, including 14 major singles, ranking him third on the all-time PDC major winners list.

In 1994, the total prize fund was £64,000. Today, each of the 128 players competing is guaranteed at least £15,000 each just for making the first round and a total prize fund of £5m will be played for

Last year, the prize for first place doubled from £500,000 to a record-shattering £1m - which remains the same this year.

This makes it the joint-highest winner's prize pot in the sport's history.

The runner-up will receive £400,000 and the two losing semi-finalists £200,000 each.
