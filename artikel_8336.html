Investing - Theory, News &amp; General • Re: TIPS are they really worth holding?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

One advantage of nominal is that they have a known payout. TIPS has a portion of know payout (low nominal) and an unknown adjustment. That unknown component is adjusted based on Fed data at a given point. Seems that there is quite a bit of risk involved with this unknown component. If you find nominal FI at a desirable rate, it seems like the less risky choice. Is assuming that there will be higher inflation based on current conditions an example of market timing?
1. Say you had a <3% mortgage (fairly common from a few years ago) you decided to pay down. Investing in nominal treasuries to pay it later would be a potentially good move because the T's yield more (than the mortgage) and a (fixed rate) mortgage payoff is a known number of nominal $'s at any given future date. Using TIPS in that case would introduce an uncompensated risk.

2. But if you're covering future living expenses it's as Watchnerd said, nominal T's are the ones easier to shoehorn into the BH beloved put down 'market timing'. It's a guess inflation will turn out lower than the BE rather than higher, whereas with TIPS the reduction/increase in TIPS return in lower and higher inflation will tend to cancel out v the change in living expenses (not necessarily exactly). Using nominal T takes uncompensated risk.

Again why 'uncompensated'? Because it's hard to argue today's TIPS BE is higher than the true market expectation of CPI-U growth, which it would be if there was a net expected 'penalty' for holding TIPS to maturity. It seems if anything the other way around, it actually costs you a bit in expected return to play the game in 2 there's no reason to play for free.
