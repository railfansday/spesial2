Verstappen wins extraordinary race as Ferraris collide

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Red Bull's Max Verstappen won an extraordinary Las Vegas Grand Prix that saw a non-stop battle for the lead for the entire race.

The leading group of 10 cars were tied together for the vast majority of the race as the lead constantly changed hands between Verstappen and the Mercedes drivers George Russell and Kimi Antonelli.

But Verstappen was the one constant, always seeming a step ahead of his rivals as he debated electrical energy deployment strategy with his team.

And in the final laps, Mercedes' challenge unravelled as first Russell made a mistake and crashed trying to pass Antonelli and the Italian lost out to the two McLarens of Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri going into the final lap.

The world champion dropped to fifth going on to the Strip on the last lap but was able to reclaim the place from Alpine's Pierre Gasly at the end of the long straight.

And there was more drama at Ferrari as Charles Leclerc earned a 10-second penalty for tipping Lewis Hamilton into a spin.

Earlier, Hamilton had been given a 10-second penalty of his own for passing Norris off the track in a fight for position that also involved Leclerc.

It was a grand prix more reminiscent of American oval racing than Formula 1, and it depended on unique circumstances revolving around the Las Vegas circuit and energy starved hybrid engines this year.

But there was no questioning the showbiz value in the city that prides itself as the entertainment capital of the world.

The key stories of an engrossing race were:

Verstappen's remarkable intelligence and control in the lead in the most demanding of circumstances

Another dip in a dreadful season for Russell

More controversy at Ferrari, as both drivers earned 10-second penalties for driving infringements in battles involving the other

Challengers came and went but Max Verstappen was never without the lead for long in Las Vegas [Getty Images]

"How many overtakes did I do?" Verstappen asked his engineer on the slowing down lap, as he reflected on a race the like of which F1 has not seen since the famous Monza slipstreaming battles of the 1960s and early 1970s.

"Yeah, and not many of them on the straight," his new engineer Tom Hart said, summing up how Verstappen had secured the win against the Mercedes challenge.

Lap after lap after lap, Verstappen, who started from pole, would lose the lead to Russell on the Strip only to reclaim it at the last corner, usually going around the outside.

If that did not work, and Russell passed later in the lap, or led across the line, Verstappen developed a trademark move around the outside of Turn Three to get back in front.

The race went on and on like that, punctuated by a mid-grand prix safety car, but through it all Verstappen always seemed in control.

As the race wore on, Antonelli came into the picture after starting sixth, and passed Russell with 12 laps to go to become Verstappen's main challenger.

Now it was the Italian's turn to swap places with Verstappen, but again the Dutchman had the measure of Mercedes.

At one point, Verstappen asked his team to come up with a better deployment strategy for the final lap because he thought Mercedes had "figured it out".

They came back to him to say that he had already come up with the best approach.

In the end, though, he need not have worried, as his rivals behind him gave him enough of a gap at the start of the final lap to not have to worry too much about defending his lead.

Charles Leclerc (right) dropped to 10th as a result of being given a 10-second penalty for colliding with Ferrari team-mate Lewis Hamilton [Getty Images]

That critical final phase started with Russell deciding to make a move to become Verstappen's challenger again.

Russell went for a move into Turn Five with two laps to go but he locked up his brakes over the bumps heading into the corner - bumps that had earlier taken Red Bull's Isack Hadjar out of the lead battle when his front wing broke over them - and went straight on into the barrier.

Russell managed to reverse out of the barrier but a ninth place finish was not what he was expecting.

The incident was also unfortunate for him in the context of his calling out Verstappen's driving after qualifying, when he said "somebody has got to stand up to that guy".

Verstappen had the last word, both on track and literally. After seeing Russell's embarrassing mistake, Verstappen came on the radio to say: "Hah, hah. Well done. My God."

Now Antonelli looked to be the man who would challenge Verstappen for the win.

But that was counting without the McLarens. Piastri had already driven an outstanding race to recover from starting 17th because of an engine penalty and Norris had at one point dropped out of the top 10 in the chaotic lead battle in the first part of the grand prix.

But they were third and fourth around the penultimate lap and in what looked like a co-ordinated attack but certainly wasn't, both swept around Antonelli around the outside of the fast final corner heading into the last lap.

That gave Verstappen breathing space, and Antonelli was delayed sufficiently that he could not recover to challenge them.

A hectic end to the season means Formula 1 moves from Las Vegas to the Middle East next weekend, for the Qatar Grand Prix at the Lusail International Circuit, from 27-29 November.
