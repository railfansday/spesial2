Jets' Demario Davis, Breece Hall stress consistency and balance

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The New York Jets aren't lacking in talent, but that hasn't stopped the team from struggling to put together a complete performance and do so consistently. Sitting at 2-7 and staring at another divisional clash, head coach Aaron Glenn's team has spent much of the season showing flashes of what it can be without sustaining that level of play long enough to change its fortunes.

The frustration is understandable, particularly on defense. There have been stretches when the unit has looked capable of controlling a game, followed by performances that have left far too many questions. Linebacker Demario Davis offered a blunt assessment of those inconsistencies while speaking with reporters ahead of Sunday's matchup against the Buffalo Bills.

"It's the tale of two units. We've played like the best in the league, and we've played like, frankly, the worst in the league at different moments. How do we get that consistency of doing what we do at a high level all the time?" Davis said.

Davis' comments capture one of the Jets' biggest problems. The defense has demonstrated that it can make impact plays, but those moments haven't translated into the kind of dependable production needed to help a struggling team find its footing.

The offense has faced its own challenges, particularly when it comes to finding a rhythm and making the most of its available playmakers. Hall acknowledged that the group is still working through how to distribute opportunities while maintaining its desire to improve.

"We're just trying to figure out how to get everybody the ball," Hall said. "A team full of guys that are still hungry and still looking to get better every week."

Hall's comments point to an offense that believes it has more to offer. Getting everyone involved can help create a more balanced attack, but the Jets also need to turn that approach into consistent production on the field.

Sunday's meeting with the Buffalo Bills presents another opportunity to see whether those concerns can begin turning into progress. Both players are pointing to areas where the Jets believe they can be better. Now the challenge is turning that belief into a complete performance against a division rival that won't give them many chances to recover from their mistakes.
