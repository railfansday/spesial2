Johnson & Johnson vs. Pfizer: Consistency vs. Volatility in Revenue

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

J&J's quarterly sales have climbed with few setbacks, while Pfizer's have swung between $13.7 billion and $17.8 billion over eight quarters.

Johnson & Johnson (JNJ -1.14%) primarily generates revenue by developing and selling pharmaceutical and medical technology products globally.

It recently entered a definitive agreement to acquire Ollin Biosciences for $2.2 billion upfront, and it received U.S. Food and Drug Administration approval for a new targeted cancer therapy.

Pfizer (PFE -1.46%) primarily generates revenue by developing, manufacturing, and commercializing prescription medicines and vaccines for individuals worldwide.

It recently announced a $1.5 billion funding agreement with Blackstone Life Sciences, and it secured expanded regulatory approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for a cancer treatment regimen.

Revenue here refers to the data provider's standardized income-statement revenue line item, and it shows the total money a business brings in from sales before deducting any operating costs.

Johnson & Johnson vs. Pfizer Revenue over the last 8 quarters.

First off, don't be concerned about the drops in revenue you see in the chart for both companies as you read left to right. That reflects the seasonality of the businesses, which often can be traced back to doctors and patients loading up on prescriptions toward the end of a calendar year, when many patients have met their health insurance deductibles.

While ideally, a quarterly revenue chart would show rising bars from left to right, this seasonality means a better evaluation would be to compare each quarter to its prior-year quarter. For instance, JNJ's third quarter of 2026 saw positive gains over the third quarter of 2025. Yet Pfizer's sales actually show a slight dip in Q3 2026 compared to the same period in 2025. That's a red flag for investors to pay attention to.

We also do see less fluctuation from JNJ, in part because the business has 28 products and platforms that each generate more than $1 billion in annual sales. Pfizer, meanwhile, is trying to recover from a post-COVID-19 letdown, with fewer people getting the COVID vaccine each year as we get further from the pandemic.
