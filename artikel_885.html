SFA to deliver revised proposal after young player vote delayed

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The Scottish FA will continue to consult with SPFL clubs about youth development after initial plans failed to reach a member vote.

At a summit in September, Scottish football's governing body proposed financial incentives and penalties along with quotas, in a bid to get more homegrown talent playing for Scottish clubs.

Initially, the plans were to have been voted on at an SFA general meeting in October and the SPFL's annual meeting this month.

But the SFA are now aiming to put refreshed proposals to the clubs early in the new year, with the plan to implement them for the start of the 2027-28 season.

Despite being "encouraged", the governing body added that "recent discussions have spawned a range of additional potential innovations that all parties agree will require further consultation."

"The passion we all have for Scottish football has been demonstrated by the public debate sparked since the all-club meeting in St Andrews," a Scottish FA statement read.

"We will continue to engage in dialogue with the aim of delivering a proposal that will achieve the stated objective of improving opportunities for young homegrown players to fulfil their potential at the top end of the Scottish professional game."

"It is an objective everyone benefits from: players, clubs, supporters and Scotland national teams."

At their initial consultation with clubs, the SFA put forward three proposals:

Two under-21 Scotland-eligible players start in all Scottish Cup matches, with one player on the pitch at all times.

Two under-21 Scotland-eligible players start in all Premier Sports Cup matches, with one player on the pitch at all times.

Clubs should field Scotland-eligible Under-21 players for an average of 60 minutes in each game across all four SPFL divisions
