Ranking the 10 best midfielders in world football: How many are better than Declan Rice?

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Who reigns supreme among world football’s central midfielders? The competition has rarely been fiercer, with superstar names from Barcelona, Arsenal and PSG shining so far in the 2026-27 campaign.

To be crystal clear, this list is reserved for proper midfielders, i.e. No.6s and No.8s – players you’d happily trust in a midfield two. Attacking midfielders and No.10s aren’t eligible, so Jude Bellingham – for example – does not belong here.

We’re weighing up the opening months of the new campaign alongside the bigger picture. Here’s our ranking of the 10 best central midfielders in world football right now.

The likes of Federico Valverde, Martin Zubimendi, Sandro Tonali, Bruno Guimaraes, Elliot Anderson, Enzo Fernandez and Bernardo Silva all have compelling cases for inclusion. Most still have something to prove, while Valverde has somehow ended up dislodging Real Madrid’s two actual right-backs. Consider those your honourable mentions.

We’re backing Tchouameni, who has arguably suffered more than anyone from Real Madrid’s post-Toni Kroos and Luka Modric identity crisis. He’s looked lost in a dysfunctional midfield over the past couple of years, even finding himself plugging gaps in defence.

Jose Mourinho finally looks to be putting the pieces together, with Jude Bellingham further ahead and Silva alongside him. Madrid might just have a midfield that makes structural sense, rather than a collection of individually brilliant players. Watch out, Europe.

It says everything about the quality of Caicedo that he’s widely regarded as one of the best midfielders in the Premier League while turning out for the utter basket case of BlueCo-era Chelsea.

The Blues’ iffy start under Xabi Alonso was partly explained by his injury absence. Just as Manchester City are a different beast without Erling Haaland, Chelsea look lost without their most important player.

His return brought an immediate improvement, although he’s not been entirely convincing since. Chelsea were completely overrun by Tottenham, while Manchester United had the better of the midfield battle in a ragged first half.

Caicedo can’t fix everything that’s wrong at Stamford Bridge, but Alonso will need him at his absolute best to stand any chance of getting this project off the ground.

Few midfielders in Europe have improved as rapidly as Aleksandar Pavlovic since Vincent Kompany arrived ar Bayern.

The 22-year-old announced himself on the biggest stage during Bayern’s run to last season’s Champions League semi-finals, with that extraordinary 5-4 defeat to PSG offering a valuable lesson in what it takes to compete with the very best.

Pavlovic isn’t the finished article, but he’s on the right trajectory, getting better every season.

Germany’s disastrous World Cup, and Nagelsmann’s decision to shunt him to right-back, shouldn’t count against him too heavily. Jurgen Klopp surely won’t make the same mistake.

Does Joshua Kimmich get the recognition he deserves? We’re not sure he does, perhaps because his weekly excellence for Bayern Munich is so easily taken for granted.

Vincent Kompany certainly knows. Kimmich remains the beating heart of his Bayern side, as demonstrated by his masterclass in their 6-1 demolition of RB Leipzig. He was sorely missed against Arsenal, having understandably taken compassionate leave following his father’s death.

Graeme Souness’ verdict that Declan Rice “doesn’t have enough football in him” divided opinion across social media in the summer. He then went on to write a whole column quibbling with the Arsenal star being called “world-class”.

Who are we to disagree with a man who has forgotten more about great midfield play than we’ll ever know? Well, we’ll defer to another Liverpool icon.

“He’s the best midfield player in the Premier League by a long way. It’s not even a debate,” declared Jamie Carragher after Rice’s outstanding display against Liverpool, in which he set up the winner and ran the show in the Gunners’ first Anfield victory since 2012.

We’re inclined to agree. Rice tops our Premier League contingent by some distance.

Indeed, Arsenal’s record signing doesn’t really possess ‘pausa’, as pretentious Twitter tacticos would put it, but he offers something different. His physicality, relentless energy and quality from dead balls were fundamental to Arsenal’s title triumph, and he’s already surpassed last season’s assists tally with five.

We still can’t quite place him among the five Champions League and World Cup-winning maestros above him. His Madrid masterclass showed what’s possible, but he hasn’t consistently reached those heights in the biggest games, and he looked exhausted at the World Cup.

For now, sixth feels fair. But the gap is narrowing.

Portugal didn’t see the best of Neves at the World Cup, but he’s picked up where he left off at PSG, remaining absolutely indispensable to Luis Enrique’s all-conquering side.

We love a little Scrappy-Doo midfielder, and Neves is the king of them. Everything that Gavi aspires to be. Worth his weight in gold, which admittedly isn’t saying much for someone so diminutive.

Tune into a Barcelona match on any given La Liga weekend, and you’ll swear you’re watching the finest technician in world football.

He’s made a blinding start to the 2026-27 campaign, playing a fundamental role in the almost effortless control with which Hansi Flick’s side have steamrolled most of their opposition.

Unfortunately, Pedri has a slightly frustrating tendency to go missing when the spotlight is at its brightest. He’s yet to win the Champions League, and was unable to impose himself in the recent defeat to Real Madrid (although he was good against PSG).

Spain arguably improved after the injury he suffered at Euro 2024, and he lost his place in Luis de la Fuente’s preferred starting XI as the knockout stages progressed at the World Cup.

But he was outstanding after being introduced at the hour mark in the final. Produce more performances like that in the legacy-defining games and he’ll surely be pushing for the top spot in the coming years.

The perennially underrated Fabian Ruiz, as we’re obliged to call him.

“Who wouldn’t want to win the Ballon d’Or? I’ve won the Champions League and the World Cup. I couldn’t have dreamed of a better season,” the Spaniard reflected after his remarkable 2025-26 campaign.

He was never realistically going to win it, but a top-10 finish, second only to Rodri among central midfielders, feels about right.

The 30-year-old has now gone 50 international appearances unbeaten, a remarkable run that has brought a European Championship and a World Cup title – adding to the back-to-back Champions League crowns he’s won with PSG.

He may not be the most glamorous midfielder, nor does he produce eye-catching numbers. Ruiz is not a player who’ll have you searching out his individual highlight reel. But he is ruthlessly effective, tactically superb and remarkably dependable.

The quality around him is undeniable (see the rest of this top five), but Ruiz is much more than a passenger in both the best club and national teams on the planet.

Rodri spent the best part of two seasons either on the treatment table or gradually getting back up to speed. It doesn’t seem like a coincidence that Manchester City failed to win the title after claiming four in a row.

During that period, Vitinha stepped into the void as the undisputed midfield star of world football. Absolutely majestic on the biggest European nights for PSG, time and again setting the terms and dictating the tempo against elite-level opposition. He’s continued in that vein this season, scoring in statement victories over Manchester City and Barcelona.

A minor black mark that he wasn’t able to replicate that influence for Portugal at the World Cup, where he went a bit missing.

Manchester City’s match-winner in the 2023 Champions League final. Player of the Tournament when Spain won Euro 2024. Golden Ball winner and captain when they won the World Cup.

Rodri is one of a handful of players in history who have completed football. The only Ballon d’Or winner on this list, he underlined his enduring status by finishing fourth in the voting this year.

Barcelona already looks like the perfect fit. Back home in La Liga, he’s been a Rolls-Royce, making it all look ridiculously easy as the Spanish champions have steamrolled their way through the opening months of the campaign.

There are minor caveats. He can’t cover ground like a Rice, wasn’t at his best in the Clasico defeat, and his introduction for Pedri on the hour mark against PSG failed to bring any control to a ragged finale. As was occasionally the case for Spain, there remain questions over how effectively the pair fit together in a midfield two.

But those are small concerns in the wider picture. For now, the king keeps his crown.
