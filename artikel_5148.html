U.S. government funding expires on December 11 unless Congress passes a new spending bill.

Trump has requested an additional $350 billion to rebuild Pentagon weapon stocks.

Pentagon cancellation costs could exceed $10 billion if planned weapons purchases are blocked.

The U.S. government is heading toward another shutdown, with federal funding set to expire on December 11. Congress has less than two weeks to pass a spending bill or stopgap measure to avoid a shutdown.

That is because Democrats and Republicans are struggling to agree on the funding bill over Iran war funding, defense spending, and the next budget.

The biggest fight over the U.S. budget is now about the Iran war and President Donald Trump’s request for another $350 billion for the Pentagon.

Republicans have proposed a $1.1 trillion defense bill, while the Trump administration is seeking an additional $350 billion to rebuild weapon stocks depleted by the war.

On the contrary, several Democrats want to restrict all the funding that could prolong the Iran war.

The group includes Senators Tim Kaine, Chris Van Hollen, Tammy Duckworth, Kirsten Gillibrand, Elizabeth Warren, Michael Bennet, Bernie Sanders, Elissa Slotkin, and Chris Murphy, along with Representatives Ro Khanna and Yassamin Ansari.

A similar disagreement over healthcare subsidies caused a 43-day government shutdown last year, the longest in US history. Federal workers missed paychecks, government services stopped, and key economic reports were delayed.

The shutdown risk now carries a direct financial cost for the military as well. The Pentagon has warned that if Congress does not approve the planned weapons purchases, taxpayers could have to pay more than $10 billion in fees to cancel deals with RTX and Lockheed Martin.

As of November 28, none of the 12 regular FY2027 spending bills had become law. The House had passed three, while the Senate had passed none.

If lawmakers fail to approve new funding, the government would enter a funding gap on December 12.

The U.S. has already faced several government shutdowns. The 2025 shutdown lasted 43 days, the longest in U.S. history. During that period, Bitcoin reached $126,200 before falling nearly 20%.

The shutdown also reduced money flowing into financial markets and delayed crypto ETF decisions. Important reports on inflation, jobs, and the economy were also held up, making it harder for investors to predict interest rate changes.

The U.S. faced another three-day funding gap in early 2026, followed by a 75-day shutdown affecting the Department of Homeland Security.

A new shutdown starting December 12 could create further uncertainty for financial markets.

However, the political situation could change on January 3, when Democrats are expected to take control of both the House and Senate. They could then push their own funding bills to reopen the government.

However, President Donald Trump would still have the power to veto those bills, meaning the funding fight may continue.
