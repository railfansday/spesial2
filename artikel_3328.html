While contemplating his would-be final feature film, Quentin Tarantino reportedly almost pulled the trigger on a project titled The Movie Critic, in which he would have put his own alternate history spin on iconic films of the '70s. The project fell by the wayside because Tarantino went in other creative directions, but casting rumors had already begun to swarm. The filmmaker's list of unproduced projects is long and surprisingly diverse; before The Movie Critic, he was also attached to a Star Trek reboot, a '60s-set James Bond movie, and a Pulp Fiction spin-off. One film avoided this fate even though Tarantino publicly announced he was shelving it, and it's now streaming for free.

The movie in question hit a major roadblock when its script was leaked online during the casting phase. However, when Tarantino organized a live-reading of the screenplay, he was convinced that he shouldn't give up on the idea of filming it. The movie serves as Tarantino's follow-up to his highest-grossing hit, the revisionist Western Django Unchained, which grossed around $450 million worldwide against a reported budget of $100 million.

The follow-up movie, which was released in 2015, was also a Western. However, unlike Django Unchained, it was largely set in a single location. Tarantino paid homage to the spaghetti Westerns of Sergio Corbucci and even the locked-room mysteries of Agatha Christie. The movie was filmed using vintage lenses on 70 mm film, making it one of the few movies to be shot on the format in that decade.

We're talking, of course, about The Hateful Eight. The nearly three-hour epic follows a group of disparate characters who are locked down in a saloon during a snowstorm. The film featured several heavy-hitters in the cast, including longtime Tarantino collaborators such as Samuel L. Jackson, Tim Roth, Michael Madsen, and Kurt Russell. Also featured were Jennifer Jason Leigh in a commanding central role, and Channing Tatum in a cameo.

Produced on a reported budget of around $60 million, The Hateful Eight grossed a little more than $160 million at the worldwide box office. It now holds a "Certified Fresh" 74% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, where the consensus reads, "The Hateful Eight offers another well-aimed round from Quentin Tarantino's signature blend of action, humor, and over-the-top violence — all while demonstrating an even stronger grip on his filmmaking craft."
