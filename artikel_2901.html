TOYO: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

TOYO's Ethiopian cell output avoids Chinese-origin wafers, setting it apart from peers under a Commerce Department anti-circumvention inquiry.

On its third-quarter 2026 financial results conference call, held on Nov. 16, 2026, TOYO (TOYO -2.01%) outlined plans to scale its U.S. solar manufacturing footprint. Investors focused on domestic energy infrastructure need clarity on supply chain risks and execution. Three things management said stand out.

TOYO expanded its Humble, Texas facility to approximately 2 gigawatts of annual module manufacturing capacity. Management secured cell supplies from non-foreign entities of concern (non-FEOC suppliers) to protect committed U.S. customer deliveries through 2027 against trade disruptions. That gives the company clear top-line visibility as demand for American-made equipment grows.

TOYO is developing a 1.5-gigawatt heterojunction (HJT) solar cell facility in Humble, Texas, featuring high-efficiency silicon cell technology. Management expects pilot production to begin in the first quarter of 2028 through non-dilutive financing sources such as Section 45X advanced manufacturing tax credits. Building domestic cells reduces dependence on imported components.

The U.S. Department of Commerce initiated an anti-circumvention inquiry concerning solar cells completed in Ethiopia using components from China. TOYO sources its silicon wafers from Indonesia and polysilicon from the United States and Malaysia, establishing a supply chain record without Chinese-origin wafers. That distinction helps protect its supply chain from prospective trade tariffs.

U.S. module capacity in Texas is expected to run at or near full capacity in 2027, and execution on domestic manufacturing is taking center stage.

Operational advantages like localized manufacturing scale, disciplined capital deployment, and supply chain traceability anchor long-term strategy while leadership monitors regulatory policy developments.
