How Dave Challinor’s strongest Sheffield Wednesday XI will look as new boss gets to work

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Sheffield Wednesday confirmed Dave Challinor’s appointment as first-team manager over the weekend, and after watching the 1-1 draw with Blackpool from the stands, he starts his work now.

The former Stockport County boss is tasked with leading SWFC back to the Championship as they rebuild after relegation and administration. He has a strong squad at his disposal, and the pressure is on to succeed where the departed Henrik Pedersen failed.

One of the first challenges Challinor faces is to identify his strongest and favoured starting XI. His squad is brimming with talent and boasts depth in all areas, so there is strong competition for a place in the plans of the new manager.

Lumley; Bernard, Cooper, Weaver; Valery, Mitchell, Slattery, M. Lowe; Barry, Keillor-Dunn; Burstow

The Owls’ summer rebuild means they have good squad depth across the board, so Challinor will be able to tinker with his setup as he works out the best XI. However, there will be a familiar feel to the setup, as the 51-year-old’s preferred formation has been 3-4-2-1 over the course of his career.

This setup was Pedersen’s go-to last season, although he occasionally switched to a 3-1-4-2 or 3-5-2 at times. Danny Rohl also used a 3-4-2-1 during his tenure, but like Pedersen, he switched to a 4-2-3-1 – which Challinor has deployed too – later in his reign.

As for the personnel, Joe Lumley will be backed to reclaim his starting spot from Ashley Maynard-Brewer when fit.

The centre-back positions will be a point of contention, with all of Di’Shon Bernard, Liam Cooper, Ernie Weaver, Ricardo Santos, Sil Swinkels, Gabriel Otegbayo and the versatile Max Lowe to pick from. All of those players could feasibly start, but Bernard has been a Championship standout at his best, Cooper provides leadership, experience and a left-footed presence for balance, and Weaver has the highest ceiling of the lot.

Yan Valery seems a shoo-in for Challinor’s right wing-back spot, and Lowe should be favoured on the left.

In the middle, Callum Slattery could emerge as Billy Mitchell’s regular partner with Barry Bannan enduring a tricky start to the campaign. This is another area where competition is strong, with all of Bannan, Jaden Heskey and Jarvis Thornton up for consideration while Tyler Onyango recovers from his ACL injury.

In the two roles behind the striker, Davis Keillor-Dunn is emerging as an influential player, and Louie Barry will be backed to land a key role when fit given his past success under Challinor. Slattery could also play in one of these advanced roles.

Up top, Mason Burstow could get the nod over top scorer Jamal Lowe. Again, there is strong depth here with Harry Gray, Ali Al-Hamadi, Will Grainger and Keillor-Dunn all capable of playing at striker.

The 3-4-2-1 has been Challinor’s go-to, but a 3-5-2 at Sheffield Wednesday could see Barry start as a striker with a midfield three of Mitchell, Slattery and Heskey or Bannan.

A 4-2-3-1 would facilitate a similar midfield, while a centre-back drops out. The depth in the SWFC squad means Challinor is spoiled for choice as he works out his best lineup.

Challinor has plenty of experience now, but this is a new task for him. With the greatest respect to his previous jobs, Sheffield Wednesday are a different beast in terms of club stature and expectation.

At Stockport County and AFC Fylde, he built them into teams that were truly his, and they grew over time. At Hillsborough, he faces the task of getting the most out of a group with big names and personalities. It will be a test of his abilities as both a coach and man manager.

Challinor’s record is excellent, but this is the biggest challenge he has faced in his managerial career to date.
