Why Sandisk Stock Keeps On Going Down

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Nvidia's CEO scandal keeps dragging on, weighing on AI chip sales forecasts, and damaging Sandisk stock.

For Nvidia (NVDA -1.57%) investors, the bad news is a little less bad on Tuesday.

Ever since Nvidia's CEO resigned, the stock has been sliding. Shares of the GPU AI chips giant are now down about 25% for the month of November. (The good news is that as of 1:10 p.m. ET, Nvidia stock is down only about 1.5% more today.)

For Sandisk (SNDK -1.54%) investors, the news is quite a bit worse. Not only is Sandisk stock down 32% in November. It's also down more than Nvidia stock today -- 2.7%.

Why are investors so down on Sandisk stock, whose own CEO -- unlike Nvidia's ex-CEO -- has not yet admitted to having an affair with an alleged Chinese Communist spy?

As I explained yesterday, Sandisk's problems basically stem from the fact that Nvidia AI chips and Sandisk's NAND flash memory chips are sold in pairs. You can't build an AI data center without both of them, and so if demand for Nvidia chips drops because of this scandal, Sandisk's sales might also suffer.

Granted, there's also the possibility that Sandisk might not take any hit at all. Nvidia's chip sales might not suffer after all, and even if they do, other producers of AI chips, such as AMD or Intel, might pick up the slack. In either of those situations, Sandisk's memory chips should still be in demand.

But here's the latest bad news: As my fellow Fool Travis Hoium reports, semiconductor research firm SemiAnalysis thinks capital spending on AI chips in 2027 could be 19% below previous estimates, 2028 spending might be 28% below, and 2029 spending could fall 41% below estimates.

SemiAnalysis cut their capex estimate by $1.6 TRILLION over the next four years! - 2026: ~$392B, roughly unchanged - 2027: $770B to $627B (-19%) - 2028: $1.054T to $758B (-28%) - 2029: $1.352T to $796B (-41%) - 2030: $1.531T to $923B (-40%) This calls into question all forward multiples the market has been arguing are cheap over the past 6 months.

How bad is this news? Well, luckily, it still works out to 24% annual sales growth in AI chips over the next five years. At a forward P/E ratio less than 4.0, and a PEG ratio less than 0.2 (!), I remain bullish on Sandisk stock.
