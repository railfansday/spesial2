MS NOW Hosts Sign On To Letter Urging Adoption Of “Fair” WGAE Contract; Network Says Talks Are Ongoing And “Constructive”

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Writers Guild of America, East said that a number of MS NOW hosts — including Rachel Maddow, Jen Psaki, Chris Hayes and Nicolle Wallace — have signed on to a letter of solidarity urging the network to adopt a new contract that expands representation and boosts annual and minimum wage increases to keep pace with inflation.

The network, meanwhile, says that talks have progressed and have been “constructive,” while stating that it supports employees’ right to organize. But it has contended that proposed wage increases would be out of line with what other employees across the company and at other networks have received. The WGAE represents about 300 members, and, per the union, is looking for recognition of 50 additional employees in digital, audio and longform, newsgathering, planning and the DC bureau. Watch on Deadline

“As hosts and guests of MS NOW programs, we stand in solidarity with the MS NOW Union as they fight for a contract that addresses inflation, AI protections, and retirement security. As one of the most trusted names in news, we are proud of our programming and the MS NOW producers that make it possible,” the letter stated.

“We’re disappointed that Versant Media’s decision makers expect unit members to work extra hours creating new digital content, without treating it as extra work or paying for it. On top of that, we are disappointed that Versant Media will not agree to the Union’s demands for annual and minimum wage increases that keep pace with inflation,” the letter added.

Other on-air figures who signed the letter include Ali Velshi, Amy McGrath, Alicia Menendez, Jacob Soboroff, David Noriega, Jillian Frankel, Ayman Mohyeldin, Katie Phang, Christine Jansing, Symone Sanders Townsend, Antonia Hylton, Jonathan Capehart and Michael Steele.

Rebecca Kutler, the president of MS NOW, wrote in a memo to employees, “Let me be clear: we support our employees’ right to organize, to be represented, and to advocate for themselves. That has never been in question, nor should it be.”

She noted that the WGAE had reached agreement with other labor unions including IATSE and the DGA, and that they have already reached tentative agreements on a number of issues with the WGAE and have “shown a willingness to find common ground where differences remain.”

“We offer the highest starting salaries among our peers, having raised entry-level salaries significantly over the last five years, and the starting point for each role’s salary range is typically higher than our broadcast and cable news competitors,” she wrote.

She also cited the network’s hiring for more than 200 positions over the past year, as other newsrooms cut back. MS NOW has been building its newsgathering operation since the split from Comcast meant that NBC News was no longer a sister network.

The first contract for the MS NOW Union [previously MSNBC] was reached in 2023 and included a 3% pay increase for every year of the agreement’s duration. It also included “vital improvements to policies regarding extra pay for additional work, overnight work, and out-of-title work,” per the WGAE.

According to the network, they have offered guaranteed 3% salary increases, enhanced pay for filling in at an upgraded position, and a 33% increase in the night shift allowance per shift for exempt employees, crediting on-call time as time worked, as well as AI-related protections, among other proposals.

Per the network, WGAE is seeking 5% annual wage increases over the next two years and a 4.5% increase in the third year, as well as a 4% increase in salary minimums at signing and another 4.5% increase in 2029.

Kutler wrote that the network’s audience growth “does not insulate us from the financial pressures reshaping our industry. It takes a realistic understanding of these market pressures for us to continue our success.”

The letters from the WGAE and Kutler are below:

As hosts and guests of MS NOW programs, we stand in solidarity with the MS NOW Union as they fight for a contract that addresses inflation, AI protections, and retirement security. As one of the most trusted names in news, we are proud of our programming and the MS NOW producers that make it possible.We’re disappointed that Versant Media’s decision makers expect unit members to work extra hours creating new digital content, without treating it as extra work or paying for it. On top of that, we are disappointed that Versant Media will not agree to the Union’s demands for annual and minimum wage increases that keep pace with inflation.We are calling on the Company to agree to a quick and fair process to recognize all employees’ rightful place in the MS NOW Union without division or delay. Additionally, we call on the Company to agree to a contract that will protect wages, benefits, and security at work.

Team,As we continue to negotiate with the Writers Guild of America East, I’d like to give you an update about where we stand.For those unfamiliar with this process, the WGAE represents a group of our editorial employees in a collective bargaining unit that negotiates their compensation and other terms of employment. We reached an initial agreement with this union in 2023, and now that contract is set to expire on Dec. 3.Let me be clear: we support our employees’ right to organize, to be represented, and to advocate for themselves. That has never been in question, nor should it be. In the past year, we have successfully reached new collective bargaining agreements with other labor organizations, including two International Alliance of Theatrical Stage Employees locals and the Directors Guild of America, and have approached these negotiations with the same mindset. We have already reached tentative agreements with WGAE on numerous issues throughout this process and shown a willingness to find common ground where differences remain. We have made meaningful proposals to increase compensation, address workload concerns, expand opportunities for growth and advancement, and provide protections against AI, among many other offerings.We offer the highest starting salaries among our peers, having raised entry-level salaries significantly over the last five years, and the starting point for each role’s salary range is typically higher than our broadcast and cable news competitors. We are a workforce that is embracing new technologies and creating opportunities for employees to broaden their skill sets and advance their careers. Our goal is to ensure our employees will grow alongside our business and the broader industry. These commitments are part of the reason we have a company-wide attrition rate well below national averages. Plus, while many other news organizations are cutting back their newsrooms and trimming roles, we have filled more than 200 in the past year at MS NOW and continue recruiting for dozens of open positions.We are enormously proud of MS NOW’s performance; our strong audience metrics are a testament to the work you do every day. But this success does not insulate us from the financial pressures reshaping our industry. It takes a realistic understanding of these market pressures for us to continue our success. We must weigh every financial commitment against our ability to invest in journalism, develop new programming, hire journalists, cover the 2028 presidential campaign, and build out platforms that will carry MS NOW into the future, including our direct-to-consumer offering.We will continue to compensate our employees fairly and competitively, while protecting our long-term growth. Those responsibilities are not mutually exclusive, but they require thoughtful decisions.As we approach the final weeks of these negotiations, let’s stay focused on what unites us: our shared commitment to the journalism we produce, the audiences who count on us, and the future of MS NOW. I am optimistic that together we will reach a fair and responsible agreement that reflects the respect we have for our colleagues and the work we do together.If you have any questions, please don’t hesitate to reach out to me, Greg, Madeleine, Scott, Marcus, or anyone on our leadership team. Our doors are always open.Rebecca
