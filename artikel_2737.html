Tom Brady doesn't approve of Aaron Rodgers' comments on Mike McCarthy: 'Nobody wins when the family feuds'

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Aaron Rodgers ended his Pittsburgh Steelers career in a way that will be remembered long after his time as a player ends. Tom Brady does not appear to approve.

The former New England Patriots and Tampa Bay Buccaneers star and current Fox Sports broadcaster weighed in on Rodgers going scorched-earth against Steelers coach Mike McCarthy during the Philadelphia Eagles-Dallas Cowboys Thanksgiving game on Thursday, suggesting Rodgers should have taken a more graceful route out of Pittsburgh:

"The sanctity of an NFL locker room, it's a very sacred place. We shouldn't ever lose track of that as players. It's a privilege to be in that locker room. You're fighting with your teammates, you're fighting with your coaches, you're always acting as a team together. Shouldn't always be the place for private things to become public, that's what I'll say."

The trouble for the Steelers began late Tuesday night, when the team announced it was benching Rodgers and starting backup quarterback Mason Rudolph against the Denver Broncos on Thursday. Rodgers responded by going onto his friend Pat McAfee's show and unleashing a torrent of claims about McCarthy's aptitude for the job.

The Steelers officially released Rodgers soon after the interview. Subsequent reports indicated Rodgers demanded the team either give him play-calling duties or release him, with McCarthy later confirming Rodgers "made an ultimatum."

Brady praised Rodgers' career, but again indicated there wasn't much to be proud about this week:

"Certainly a Hall of Fame career and he's a tremendous player and it has nothing to do with his play-style. I was fortunate to be around a lot of great teammates over the years, and what made a great teammate to me was you cared about your teammates and your coaches, and you cared about what your team was trying to accomplish. That supersedes any individual aspect of your game that you want to promote.

"Again, I was very fortunate. It was a great locker room. I played for my teammates, I played for my coaches. Nobody wins when the family feuds."

Aaron Rodgers and Tom Brady both had tension with a longtime head coach. The two situations ended very differently.

For his part, Brady played under a grand total of three head coaches during his NFL career: Bill Belichick with the New England Patriots and Bruce Arians and Todd Bowles with the Tampa Bay Buccaneers.

Perhaps reinforcing his point, it's well-known that Brady and Belichick had their friction while working together. Brady acknowledged last year that "a natural tension" developed and only ended when he left New England, but there was no public blow-up and Brady has otherwise supported and defended the coach since their split.

Rodgers' time with McCarthy goes back to his sophomore year in the NFL in 2006, two years before his first career start. McCarthy's hire in Pittsburgh reportedly wasn't intended as a way to lure back Rodgers for another year, but it felt like a natural reunion for a pairing that once won a Super Bowl together.
