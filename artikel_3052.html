Why Applied Aerospace & Defense Stock Tanked This Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

There's more than one reason why investors are motivated to move this defense stock out of their portfolios.

By Scott Levine – Nov 21, 2026 at 11:21AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Applied Aerospace & Defense ›

A wave of analyst downgrades following management warnings about supplier bottlenecks and margin pressure sent Applied Aerospace & Defense (AADX -0.31%) dropping 23.5% this week, according to data provided by S&P Global Market Intelligence.

Applied Aerospace & Defense stock changed hands at $6.41 as of the close on Friday, Nov. 20, down 23.5% from Friday, Nov. 13's close. Across those five sessions, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) fell 8.6%, and the Nasdaq-100 fell 10.4%.

Wall Street turned cool on the company after management detailed operational friction during its third-quarter earnings update. Jefferies downgraded the stock to hold from buy and cut its price target from $20 to $10 on Monday, Nov. 16, while Guggenheim trimmed its target from $30 to $15. The rating cuts followed management disclosures that supplier constraints and production ramp costs are squeezing profit margins and pushing key contract deliveries into 2027. Adding to the pressure, law firms including Rosen Law Firm and Levi & Korsinsky announced investigations this week into potential securities claims on behalf of shareholders.

The stock is down 73.6% (as of the close on Friday, Nov. 20) from its 52-week high of $24.24, reflecting the severity of market sentiment deterioration over the past year.

Applied Aerospace & Defense manufactures specialized components and systems for space and defense programs, serving government agencies and aerospace contractors. While contract backlog reached $1.26 billion as of Sept. 30, 2026, manufacturing delays mean the business cannot turn those orders into profitable revenue as quickly as investors anticipated. Third-quarter revenue rose 48.6% year-over-year to $182.1 million, indicating strong demand, but production inefficiencies are eroding the bottom line.

The company ended its last reported quarter with $11.5 million in cash and short-term investments as of Sept. 30, 2026, against $409.5 million in total debt. Analyst downgrades and emerging legal investigations shift the thesis from a high-growth defense expansion story to a margin-constrained execution test.

Investors should focus on the company's next earnings update, scheduled for Dec. 2, 2026, in which management must demonstrate progress in resolving supplier bottlenecks and stabilizing operating margins. The defense stock trades at a trailing price-to-sales (P/S) ratio of 1.4 on a market cap of $1.1 billion, a valuation that will likely stay under pressure until execution improves and legal investigations are resolved.

While there was a lot to like about Applied Aerospace & Defense's third-quarter financial results, it's unsurprising that investors are acutely focused on the weak spots. Sure, a growing backlog is impressive, but it means little if the company has its hands tied in delivering on the backlog due to supplier constraints. Couple that with rising production costs, and it's clear why the market is unimpressed with the company's recent report.

At this point, those with Applied Aerospace & Defense on their radars should wait for the company's next financial report to see whether supply constraints have eased before proceeding with an investment. In the meantime, investors may want to consider a defense ETF that includes Applied Aerospace & Defense.
