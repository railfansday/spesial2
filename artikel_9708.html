Nvidia lost $600B in 4 days, then its CEO resigned

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Nvidia has lost about $600 billion of market value since Friday's close. This morning, Jensen Huang — the founder who ran the company for 33 years — resigned as president, CEO, and board member, effective immediately, five days before earnings.

The announcement is one page, and it says less than you'd expect. From Nvidia's own newsroom, published this morning:

The release gives no reason for the resignation. It says operations, customer commitments, and the product roadmap "continue without interruption."

I pulled Nvidia's daily prices from Yahoo Finance this morning and did the arithmetic myself:

Today's session opened at $151.01 and touched $148.80 — 14.4% below yesterday's close — before recovering to about $162 by mid-morning.

Nvidia has about 24.15 billion shares outstanding (stockanalysis.com). At Friday's close the company was worth about $4.51 trillion. At this morning's price it's about $3.91 trillion. That's roughly $600 billion gone in four trading days — and about $287 billion of it came this morning, after the resignation was announced.

Huang himself owns about 3.6% of the company (USA Today). His own stake is down roughly $21 billion since Friday.

This didn't come out of nowhere. It came out of a four-day sequence in which every official statement aged badly within 24 hours:

The investigation the board announced on Sunday never published findings. Four days later, the man it was investigating is gone — and the release doesn't mention the investigation at all.

To be clear about what is verified and what isn't: the resignation is confirmed by Nvidia itself. The affair allegations come from anonymous sources in NYT reporting; the espionage claim comes from an intelligence memo that has never been made public; Nvidia has denied the spy claims, and Nvidia and Powell declined to comment on the second affair report. The company has not said the resignation is connected to any of it. The timeline is the evidence; the causation is not stated.

For developers, the question isn't the scandal — it's the succession. Kress is a finance chief, not a technologist, and she's interim while keeping the CFO job. The board says the roadmap continues without interruption, but the person who was the roadmap — the one who bet the company on CUDA in 2006 and on AI data centers a decade later — just left with no transition period.

The stock is telling you the market doesn't buy "without interruption" yet: down 14% intraday at the low, still down 7% at mid-morning. A $4.5 trillion company just lost its founder, its chief technologist, and its chief salesman in one paragraph — and its interim leader is the person who counts the money, not the one who draws the roadmap.
