So length and viscosity act linearly, but the radius acts to the fourth power. That is why a slightly narrowed artery or a slightly clogged tube cuts the flow so sharply.

Poiseuille's law needs smooth (laminar) flow. The mean speed in this tube is Q / (pi r^2) = 0.25 m/s, and the Reynolds number rho v d / eta is 500 for water in a 2 mm tube. Laminar flow in a tube holds below about 2000, so the law applies. A quick check like this is worth adding to the practical file.
