FOMC minutes drop today, offering fresh clues on the Fed’s December rate decision.

Christopher Waller has backed a December hike and warned that a 50-basis-point increase could be considered if inflation stays high.

Bitcoin remains under pressure near $64,400-$64,900, with the $68,000 200-week moving average acting as key resistance.

Markets are bracing for the Federal Reserve’s October meeting minutes today, with one question: will the Federal Reserve keep tightening its grip on inflation? The minutes could reveal how strongly officials backed rates and whether another hike is gaining support.

Any signal could affect Treasury yields and the dollar, putting crypto and other risky assets on alert.

The FOMC will release the minutes of its October 27–28 meeting today, when the Fed held interest rates steady at 3.75%–4.00% after a 25-basis-point hike in September.

The minutes will give investors a closer look at why policymakers decided to pause and how much support there was for another rate increase. The committee voted 10–2 to keep rates unchanged, while Governors Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack favored a 25-basis-point hike.

Since that meeting, October payrolls rose 165,000 against a 65,000 forecast, while unemployment fell to 4.1%. October CPI also came in hot, with headline inflation rising 0.4% month over month and core CPI increasing 0.3%.

Meanwhile, CME FedWatch data shows a 95%–96% chance of a 25-basis-point hike at the December 8–9 meeting, while the chance of a larger 50-basis-point increase stands at around 5%.

During the Productivity and Jobs Task Force Conference, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh did not give any clear signal about the December rate decision. Instead, he spoke about how AI could improve productivity and help the economy grow.

Governor Christopher Waller offered a much more hawkish view. Speaking at the Bank of England Watchers Conference, Waller said he was leaning toward a December rate hike, even if economic data weakened. He also said the Fed should consider a 50-basis-point increase if inflation remains high.

St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem also sees more rate increases ahead. He said the Fed may need to raise rates several times before next spring to bring inflation under control.

Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan has taken a similar stance. She said the Fed may need to move more quickly with rate increases and had previously called for at least two more 25-basis-point hikes.

However, not every Fed official is pushing for a larger move. New York Fed President John Williams said on November 17 that current conditions do not support a major increase, pushing back against expectations of an aggressive 50-basis-point hike.

Governor Michelle Bowman has also backed a slower approach, favoring smaller and gradual increases to bring inflation back toward the Fed’s 2% target.

According to CME FedWatch Tool data, traders see a 67% chance of another 25-basis-point hike in January 2027. Expectations for March are more divided, with 44% expecting rates to remain unchanged and another 44% pricing in a 25-basis-point hike.

Looking further ahead, CME data points to at least three rate hikes by the end of 2027.

Meanwhile, Bitcoin has been struggling to reclaim its 200-week moving average near $68,000 after falling to $62,000, trading around 64,900.

A more hawkish message could trigger a sharper crypto sell-off, while a dovish surprise could spark a strong rally.
