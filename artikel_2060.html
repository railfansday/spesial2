Top 7 best value-for-money EFL Championship signings this season

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The Championship is jam-packed with talent across the board, and naturally, clubs will pick up incredible bargains along the way when building their squads, as Football League World has taken a closer look at the biggest bargains made by English second-tier clubs this summer.

Roughly over £300 million combined was spent by Championship clubs in the recent summer transfer window, as all 24 teams delved into the market to prepare their squads for the upcoming campaign.

Some teams, however, whether that be for parachute payments, wealthy owners, or incredibly savvy business over the years, were able to spend more than others.

This, however, doesn't mean it was necessarily money well spent, as ever, there were bargains to be had in the market, and some teams were luckier than others to find them, as FLW has attempted to rank the top seven best value-for-money signings made by Championship clubs this summer.

Scottish centre-back Stephen Welsh has had a fairly unique career by modern-day standards.

Coming through the youth ranks at Celtic, despite being a part of the Bhoys' first-team squad since around 2019, he was never truly able to nail down a starting berth at Parkhead, instead spending time out on loan with the likes of KV Mechelen and Motherwell.

Welsh would star on loan with Motherwell during the 2025/26 campaign, and with his future at Celtic uncertain upon returning to Glasgow, the Scot had a big decision to make about his future.

In a gesture of goodwill, he was allowed to end his contract with the club early, as Swansea City snapped up the 26-year-old on a free transfer, and so far, that has looked to be a masterstroke.

With Cameron Burgess out for the season, Welsh has shone in the Swans' back line alongside either Filip Lissah, Ben Cabango, or Jenson Seelt, becoming a mainstay in Vitor Matos' defence.

With Matos set to leave for Strasbourg, a new head coach will benefit from the signing of Welsh, as on a free transfer, Swansea have picked up one of the best-performing defenders in the division this season.

West Bromwich Albion, under James Morrison, underwent a mass clearout in their squad over the summer, as the likes of Jed Wallace, Josh Maja, Karlan Grant, Daryl Dike, Alex Mowatt, and Krystian Bielik left the club.

In different circumstances, this could have been a disaster for the Baggies, but they have made sure those departures have not been missed with the additions of Rabby Nzingoula, Kareem Tunde, Felix Horn Myhre, Brayann Pereira, Barney Stewart, and Jimmy-Jay Morgan, with the latter being the clear standout at this stage.

Morgan, 20, came through the ranks of Southampton before joining Chelsea in early 2023, starring in the Blues' youth squads, with loans to Gillingham and Peterborough United following.

The forward shone on loan with the Posh in League One last campaign, scoring 12 and assisting three in 34 league games at London Road, attracting the interest of Sheffield United, Swansea, and Preston North End.

West Brom would eventually agree a deal worth around £4 million for the Englishman, and with six goals and four assists in 17 appearances across all competitions so far, he has hit the ground running in the Championship and looks worth every penny and then some.

Morgan would most likely command a fee in the region of three times the amount West Brom paid for him just months ago, as the 20-year-old would be difficult to leave off the list.

Stoke City have never been regarded as an especially savvy club when it comes to transfers, with only really Marko Arnautović and, more recently, Sorba Thomas being sold for substantial profit in the last decade.

That transfer strategy of buying experienced heads such as Sam Vokes, Sam Clucas, and James McClean would appear to be over now, as the Potters brought in the likes of Ethan Galbraith, Svante Ingelsson, Djibril Soumare, Jack McGlynn, and Luca Bombino over the summer.

However, the pick of the bunch, and the one that looks to be the best value-for-money at this stage, is Scottish centre-back Luke Graham, who made the move to North Staffordshire for a mere £2 million from Dundee.

Before his move to the Potteries, as many as 43 clubs were reportedly interested in Graham's signature, including the likes of Derby County, Norwich City, Lincoln City, Charlton Athletic, Preston, and Portsmouth in England, as Stoke won the hotly contested race.

It hasn't taken Graham long to show exactly why he was attracting that level of interest, as the 22-year-old has stood out on the left of a back three defence alongside each of Maksym Talovierov, Ben Wilmot, and Bosun Lawal, and recently earned his first few caps for the Scotland national side under Sebastien Pocognoli.

For a mere £2 million, already a Scotland international, and still just 22, Graham can only get better, as Stoke have well and truly hit the transfer jackpot with his addition.

Canadian striker Cyle Larin joined Southampton on loan from Spanish outfit Mallorca in January 2026 as one of Tonda Eckert's first additions at St Mary's, and eyebrows were certainly raised following his addition.

The 31-year-old, despite his 95 caps for the Canadian national team, has never truly been able to nail down a place at one team for too long across spells with Orlando, Besiktas, Zulte Waregem, Club Brugge, Real Valladolid, Mallorca, and Feyenoord, and upon his arrival, was replacing current top goalscorer Adam Armstrong.

Armstrong, who netted the crucial winner for the Saints in the 2024 Championship play-off final against Leeds, headed to Wolverhampton Wanderers in the Premier League, as Larin has big shoes to fill. However, fill them and then some, he did.

Larin netted eight and assisted once in 18 league appearances in his first six months at the club, as his move to St Mary's was swiftly made permanent in the summer.

Southampton were obliged to pay a fee of around £3.4 million to Mallorca if they were promoted to the Premier League, and although they weren't, a fee in a similar region was agreed, and with seven goals in 11 league appearances already this season, he has announced himself as one of the best centre-forwards in the division.

Neither Divin Mubama nor Lewis Dobbin have been successfully able to replace Larin's output with the Canadian out injured, and as far as value-for-money signings go, Larin is right up there.

Irish midfielder Jack Moylan has enjoyed a meteoric rise to prominence over the past few years, as Cardiff City have emerged as the major beneficiaries of his exploits.

The 25-year-old began his career with Bohemians in his native Ireland before shining with Shelbourne in the top flight with 15 goals and three assists in 36 league games from midfield during the 2023 campaign, eventually heading to Lincoln City in January 2024 for a mere £50k.

Moylan would have a fairly slow start to life at Cincil Bank, but under Michael Skubala, he exploded into life during the 2025/26 season, scoring 11 and assisting seven in 31 League One games for the Imps, who romped to the third tier title.

Despite only signing a contract extension with the club in March 2026, Cardiff spent around £3 million up front for the Irishman, potentially rising to £5 million with add-ons, with a healthy sell-on clause.

This was a record sale for Lincoln, who, on the face of it, found an extraordinary deal with Moylan, but since his move to the Welsh capital, the midfielder has only gone from strength to strength, emerging as Cardiff's star man with ten goals and three assists in 15 Championship games already.

With five goals in eight caps for the Republic of Ireland, Moylan is in the form of his life, and Cardiff, despite Moylan not being a cheap deal by their standards, must be laughing now, as he is under contract until the summer of 2030; the Bluebirds will surely rake in a lucrative fee if he were to leave anytime soon.

Coming in as runner-up in this ranking is USMNT midfielder Sebastian Berhalter, who has slotted in at the Riverside seamlessly alongside compatriots Aidan Morris, Rokas Pukstas, and Max Arfsten.

With Hayden Hackney heading to Everton in the summer, replacing the academy product appeared a near-impossible task for Middlesbrough head coach Kim Hellberg, but in Berhalter, there can’t have been many better possible additions the Swede could have made.

The American spent his entire career up until his move to Teesside in the MLS with the likes of Columbus Crew, Austin FC, and Vancouver Whitecaps, whilst remaining a regular in the USMNT squad and amassing 24 caps for his nation as things stand.

With his contract in Canada due to expire this winter, Middlesbrough agreed to pay a fee in the region of around $2 million (£1.5 million) for the 25-year-old’s services six months early, and he has so far justified that fee and then some.

With three goals and two assists in 15 games, Berhalter, alongside Morris, have formed one of the best midfield pairings in the entire division, and if they can both stay fit throughout the campaign, promotion to the Premier League is almost certainly inevitable.

Selling a homegrown talent for around £24 million in the final year of their deal and bringing in their replacement for around a sixteenth of that price is truly incredible business from Boro, as only one player could surpass the American on this list.

Coming in at the top spot, to the surprise of probably nobody, is Middlesbrough's Will Lankshear, as Hellberg's side claim the top two in this ranking of best value-for-money signings this summer.

There isn't a lot to say about Lankshear that hasn't already been said, with the English striker already attracting the interest of numerous clubs across Europe, including the likes of Manchester United, Brighton and Hove Albion, Everton, Newcastle United, Sunderland, Leeds United, Arsenal, and Borussia Dortmund.

Boro have reportedly slapped a price tag in excess of £70 million on the 21-year-old just months after joining the club for an initial £10 million, plus a further £10 million in potential add-ons from Tottenham Hotspur.

With 11 goals and four assists in a relegated Oxford United side last season, Lankshear has proven that he is no one-season wonder, with 15 goals in 16 games for Boro across all competitions already. If he continues on this trajectory, numerous existing records will be broken, including Aleksandar Mitrovic's 43-goal season for Fulham back in 2021/22.

Although a potential £20 million is not cheap by any stretch of the imagination, and certainly not by Championship standards, the profit Boro stand to make on the Englishman is astronomical, and if he fires them to the Premier League in the process, he will not only go down as one of the best value-for-money signings this summer, but of all time.
