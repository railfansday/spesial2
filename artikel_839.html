'Holes in the system' - LET star wants more pathways to earn LPGA cards

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Casandra Alexander has won the Ladies European Tour Order of Merit after three wins and five runner-up finishes in 2026; Alexander secured her LPGA Tour card via Q-Series ahead of the season-ending Andalucía Costa del Sol Open de Espana, live on Sky Sports Golf

Ladies European Tour Order of Merit winner Casandra Alexander has called for more pathways to be introduced to avoid players being ‘stuck’ in the same way she was to secure her LPGA Tour card.

Alexander is inside the world's top 30, has won three times on the Ladies European Tour this season and wrapped up the Order of Merit title with two events to spare, yet still needed to negotiate Final Qualifying to secure her LPGA Tour card for next season.

The South African has been a dominant force on the LET this season, posting five runner-up finishes before following KPMG Women's Irish Open victory with further wins at the Buccellati Ladies Italian Open and Wistron Ladies Open.

PGA Tour membership is offered in the men's game via the DP World Tour's Race to Dubai rankings, with the top 10 - not already exempt - earning their card this season, whereas top LET performers still need to earn LPGA cards via their Q-Series.

"It's definitely a little disappointing," Alexander admitted to Sky Sports. "There's not one person to blame - there's structures in place and I think it just shows the holes in the structure that we need to change.

"There's a lot that goes into it, but it was also a proving point for me this year, to get my world ranking as high as possible. I was hoping to break into the top 20 and go 'really, guys, are we still going to say there's only one way to get a card and that's Q-School?'

"We need to fix the system and we need to work together, because we want the best players in the world playing against the best players in the world and all having that opportunity."

Alexander enters the season-ending Andalucía Costa del Sol Open de Espana without a practice round having safely negotiated LPGA Qualifying, finishing Monday, where a final-round 64 helped secure her card on the American circuit next season.

"I'm not saying we need to hand out [LPGA] cards, but there surely has to be more than one pathway to earn it?" Alexander added. "I don't think the point of it is to say, 'oh, look at this, it's absolute rubbish', which it is, it's going, 'cool, but how do we do better?'

"I'm sure I wouldn't have the same attitude if I didn't get my card. I went there, I got my card, but how do we do better for the next people coming through? Because this isn't going to sustain itself - you're going to lose great players over this.

"A lot say the top three or top five on the LET Order of Merit should get cards - that's not necessarily how it starts. I think the conversation starts around 'how do I play my way into LPGA events, into gaining points and into potentially getting a full card?'

"It also shows that we need to be line-up with world ranking points because I don't understand how I was 23rd in the world and could play every major but couldn't play a general LPGA Tour event - that shows another hole in the system.

"We need to have a 'top 100 in the world' spot for any LPGA event. We have that on the LET and that would have given me opportunity to play eight or nine LPGA events this year. I hope someone else doesn't get stuck in the same situation I did in the future."

Alexander was keen to taper expectations heading into the season finale, given she only arrived in Spain the day before the tournament and without her luggage, although she is keen to enjoy the experience and celebrate an impressive season.

"I felt a little lighter leaving Alabama with the [LPGA Tour] card in my hands," Alexander admitted. "We're just coming in this week to have fun. We'll give it our best and I think it's important to show face as winner of the Order of Merit at the final event - that shows a lot of character.

"I'd love to be at home celebrating but, at the same time, it's going to be a fun week. There's the award ceremony afterwards and I think now I can take it all in. We've got a lot to do and a lot to plan [for 2027], and it's very exciting.

"It has been a really big, successful year. A lot learned and I have a great team around me that I can be thankful for, for all these achievements. It's a bit surreal, but still so much I want to do."
