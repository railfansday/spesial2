The COD Profit Blueprint: How to Model Cash-on-Delivery Economics in Morocco, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Cash-on-Delivery (COD) powers over 65% of e-commerce transactions across North Africa and the Middle East. For performance marketers and media buyers scaling direct-to-consumer (DTC) campaigns in Morocco, Saudi Arabia (KSA), and the United Arab Emirates (UAE), COD represents an enormous market opportunity—paired with brutal unit economics.

Unlike standard credit card e-commerce where revenue is collected upfront at checkout, a COD sale is merely an intent to purchase.

Between the click on Meta or TikTok Ads and the actual cash deposit into your bank account, three silent profit killers operate:

If your unit economics model treats shipped COD orders like prepaid orders, you will burn cash while celebrating a 4.0x ROAS in Ads Manager.

Here is the exact mathematical framework to calculate true profitability, minimum delivery rates, and break-even CPA across Morocco and the GCC.

A profitable COD operation is governed by four sequential conversion rates:

Leads Generated ➔ (Confirmation Rate) ➔ Confirmed Orders ➔ (Dispatch Rate) ➔ Shipped Orders ➔ (Delivery Rate) ➔ Delivered Cash

Imagine you sell a beauty or home product in Morocco:

Net Cash Profit = 21,000 - 5,600 - 4,000 - 600 - 5,000 - 1,000 = +4,800 MAD

Net Profit per Shipped Order = 4,800 / 100 = 48 MAD (16% net margin)

A 15% drop in delivery rate erased 75% of net profit, even though Ad Spend, CPL, and ROAS stayed identical in your ad manager dashboard!

To determine the maximum advertising Cost Per Delivered Order you can afford without losing money:

Max Break-Even CPA = [(Selling Price - COGS) × Delivery Rate] - Forward Shipping - [RTO Fee × (1 - Delivery Rate)] - Confirmation Cost

Never optimize for Cost Per Lead (CPL) in isolation. Always track Cost Per Delivered Order (CPDO):

Cost Per Delivered Order (CPDO) = Ad Spend / Delivered Orders
