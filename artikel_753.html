The Six Wild Campaign Ads Definitely Not Destined for the Paley Center

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

As the election season comes to a merciful close, maybe the real treasure is the absurd spots we met along the way.

There are many things the country can’t agree on, as will be evident when results for a series of close Congressional races begin rolling in Tuesday night.But there is one area on which we can find common ground: the campaign ads need to stop. They have been everywhere, like ants at a picnic, or aunts in a political WhatsApp chat. And they need to go.On Wednesday they will, but not before putting a big dent in our collective psyche. Republicans and Democrats combined to spend $11.6 billion on campaign ads, a record-breaking sum for any cycle. Elon Musk himself spent nearly half a billion dollars on GOP candidates, which may or may not turn out to be a better investment than the $1 billion SpaceX spent on Skydance to maybe not distribute a documentary.

Imagine if that $11.6 billion was spent on, like, going to the movies. (Actually, that figure is literally $3 billion more than Americans shelled out at the box office last year and on par with the all-time $11 billion-plus highs of the late 2010s.)Of course all that money led to studios producing some pretty cool movies. Did this cash yield any meaningful cultural output? Is there some hidden masterpiece, some Madison Avenue Black Panther, to show for all the outlays?Well, not exactly. But we do have Obama shooting hoops in a gym, a senior citizen shooting an intruder in her home and Steve Hilton going softcore. Here are the six wild anti-classics this cycle yielded, and what you might have missed if you weren’t in these localities/was watching anything more sane including a Jets game.—1. Shooting Cranks

“Four score and seven years ago.” Ask not what your country can you do for you.” “You break into my house, you’re gonna get shot.” The North Carolina state legislator Jennifer Balkcom wasn’t terribly well-known before this election cycle. Then she ran for the competitive House seat in a district centered on Asheville in the western part of the state. And thanks to going full Lowry Gang, and her mother going full Ma Barker, we now can’t not know her. Apparently Balkcom’s nice white-haired mom was home once, someone broke in, and she shot the intruder, as a smiling and nodding Jennifer recalls sitting next to her storytelling mother over a kitchen-table cup of coffee. “Mom’s a fighter,” Balkcom smiles and shrugs. We’re not sure why any of this would be a reason to vote for her, but it does make you rethink that family vacation to the Blue Ridge Mountains.

2. Don’t Even Ask About The Lunch Special

Barack Obama was on the campaign trail a lot this season, but perhaps his most notable stop was in an Iowa gym with Democratic Senate candidate (and former Paralympian Gold medalist) Josh Turek to shoot hoops and support the candidate’s surprisingly viable bid to turn the seat blue. You won’t learn much about the candidate’s policy views, but you will learn that Turek has perimeter game, that Mr. President still loves a good matchup of H-O-R-S-E (or, I-O-W-A) and, also, where each of the two men ranks Kareem all time. (Obama has him higher.) They both put Jordan ahead of LeBron, an empirically wrong take, but otherwise this ad is harmless. The subtle message is that Turek is a doer, and that he hangs out with Obama. There are worse codes to transmit — the former president won the state handily in both 2008 and 2012, the last time a Democrat did.

Speaking of Iowa, is it particularly cinema-obsessed? Turek’s opponent Ashley Hinson did a whole number in a movie theater with an awkward “working across the aisle” metaphor,” in which she sits in a multiplex and hands popcorn and candy to an unseen denim-shirted hand reaching out creepily from another section (is this supposed to be indicative of what we do in a theater?) while talking about her allegedly bipartisan legislative efforts. She says, for instance, that she collaborated with local Democrats to expand benefits to veterans (this is challenged by Democrats) and that she also worked “with President Trump to take on health-insurance companies to lower your costs.” What such work entails is unclear; the closest that can be surmised is that Hinson supported Trump’s notorious Medicaid cuts as well his notion of moving ACA tax credits into HSA accounts — an idea often discredited for putting millions into debt. Neither of which are feats one might be expected to brag about. The ad ends with someone spilling popcorn in Hinson’s lap as they’re trying to get to their seat, which seems metaphoric, or at least a lot more indicative of what actually happens in a movie theater.

5. At Least It Was Good Product Placement?Back in 2012, in an incident you might want to Google, Ken Paxton was entering a Dallas-area courthouse when he spied a $1,000 Montblanc pen that had been left behind the security tray by an attorney named Joe Joplin entering the courthouse, prompting Paxton to rummage around and quietly put it in his pocket, with surveillance footage capturing the whole episode. Michael Haneke himself could not dream up this scenario. Anyway, the incident has rightly dogged Paxton on the campaign trail ever since, and it surfaced again this cycle in a James Talarico ad (part of a record-breaking $600 million Texas-race spend), with the Talarico campaign going so far as to actually track down Joplin and put him on camera to endorse the candidate. The ad asks: “If Ken Paxton can steal a pen, what will he steal from you?” We’re not sure. But at least he gave us this cultural treasure.

6. He’s Never Done Anything Like This BeforeSteve Hilton has proved to be one of the more entertaining figures on the campaign trail this season. The British-born, Silicon Valley-dwelling former Fox News host has made full use of his scrapper political past — there’s a reason he inspired a character in Armando Iannucci’s satire The Thick of It. Nowhere was that more evident than this (possibly AI-generated) spot. A softly lit Skinemax-esque tableau featuring two young women in bed, accompanying a “Just Try It” message, all hinting at sapphic experimentation (but really voting Republican in California) is so unbelievably obvious and low-aiming it almost works. Not sure the “Now lick it” double-entendre at the end will win any subtlety awards (it was the mail-in ballot). But Hilton did prove an important point: when you have no chance of winning, go for the most shameless and laugh-inducing form of virality. Hilton is extremely unlikely to win, but he did move the needle by proposing an eye-popping 60 percent tax credit. Thanks to this ad, we now know the kind of mise-en-scene it might have been used for.
