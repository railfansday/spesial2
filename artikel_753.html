He Lives in Madonna’s Old Mansion. Now He’s Made a Movie About Cambodian Genocide

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Taz F. Stark used to run the hottest club in London. Today, he’s a first-time director whose harrowing short film is wowing folks like Al Pacino, Bill Nighy and Rufus Swell.

By Benjamin Svetkey and Julian Sancton, Editors

Taz F. Stark has always known how to get a bunch of movie stars into the same room. He’s the former London nightlife impresario who in 2017 opened Laylow, the red-hot Notting Hill club where Rihanna partied, Mick Jagger unwound after a Stones show and Robert Downey Jr. privately screened an Avengers movie.

But now Stark, 41, is pulling off something far more impressive: He’s getting movie stars to watch his movie about the Cambodian genocide. His directorial debut, a harrowing 14-minute short called Child of the Rouge, has been earning raves from A-list fans like Al Pacino, who screened the film for friends; Rufus Sewell, who recently introduced it at a showing at the British Consul-General residence in Hancock Park; and Bill Nighy, who’s lined up for a London screening. “Directing and the hospitality business aren’t all that different,” Stark tells Rambling. “In both, you’re managing crazy people. But it has a similar magic. You gather a team of creatives, and they all get together and make something.” In this case, that all-star team includes exec producer Brady Corbet (Oscar-winning director of The Brutalist), Dhani Harrison (George Harrison’s son, who helped compose the score), and Olivia Neergaard-Holm (editor on The Apprentice). “My gaffer did The Big Lebowski,” Stark adds. “My AD was straight off CSI.”

Stark, who grew up in Britain, spent a chunk of his 20s teaching English in Cambodia, which is what inspired the film. He wrote the screenplay — it follows a couple delivering their baby in an abandoned hospital during Khmer Rouge’s seizure of Phnom Penh in 1975 — on a flight from London to L.A., where he now lives with his girlfriend, actress Stacy Martin, in Madonna’s old castle above the Hollywood sign. Soon after landing, he was introduced — by his dog — to the guy who’d produce the picture. “I was at this coffee shop, and our dogs started playing,” he recalls of his first meeting with Joe Russell, whose credits include the Marisa Tomei-Minnie Driver short Laboratory Conditions. “It was canine intervention.” Before long, they were shooting in a warehouse in Los Feliz decked out as a Cambodian hospital, with François Chau (Lost) and Pisay Pao (Z Nation) as the leads.

“The biggest difference between directing and hospitality,” Stark notes, “is that with restaurants, all your staff want to be doing something else. They all want to be writing and directing and acting. When you’re on a film set, everybody’s just grateful for the job they have.”

Stark’s film starts streaming on Mubi on Dec. 7.
