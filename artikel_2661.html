Mets Get Tempting Trade Calls for Struggling Slugger

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The New York Mets decided to keep Mark Vientos for now, but other clubs have shown interest. The team tendered him an arbitration contract Friday, a move that surprised some observers given his recent struggles. Will Sammon of The Athletic reports that the Mets have received trade inquiries about the first baseman, although no deal appears imminent. Tendering Vientos keeps him under team control while the front office evaluates its options ahead of the expected Dec. 1 lockout.

Vientos is out of minor league options, which puts the pressure on the Mets to find him a new home or make him work. If the two sides cannot agree on a salary, they could go to an arbitration hearing. Vientos hit just 13 home runs this year and posted the fifth-lowest on-base percentage among batters with at least 250 plate appearances. FanGraphs credited him with -1.1 WAR, one of the 10 lowest marks among position players.

It is easy to forget that Vientos looked like a future star just two years ago. He hit .327 with a team-leading five home runs during the 2024 postseason, establishing himself as one of the Mets' most dangerous bats. Things have gone downhill since then. Across 463 plate appearances in 2025, the 26-year-old posted a .233/.289/.413 slash line with 17 homers. In 2026, he hit .212 with 13 home runs in 87 games, with a broken right hand limiting his playing time.

The front office knows they cannot carry all three of Vientos, Jorge Polanco, and Jared Young. Although Young served as the team's most effective first baseman this year, Polanco remains bound by a $20MM contract. The Mets likely need a right-handed hitting platoon partner for Young or a true everyday first baseman. They are reportedly among the teams involved in the race for Ty France, for instance. Keeping Vientos around without a clear spot on the roster makes little sense unless a trade partner steps up.

Even with the bad numbers, Vientos still offers something the league desperately needs right now. He provides right-handed power potential at a time when teams are starved for that kind of profile. In 2026, he hit .252/.296/.513 against left-handed pitching, compared with .182/.229/.308 against right-handers. That specific skill set keeps him valuable even if his overall defense and baserunning are below average. Although Arizona is more focused on adding left-handed bats, the Diamondbacks are the only team that had a worse first base group than the Mets this past season.

The Twins are among the teams looking for help, but Vientos' path forward remains unclear. The Mets will need to decide whether they can find a trade partner before the season begins. If they cannot, they could keep him and hope he rediscovers the power that made him so productive in 2024. Because he is out of minor league options, the Mets cannot send him to Triple-A without first exposing him to waivers.
