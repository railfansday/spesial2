Should You Buy Anthropic Stock on Day 1 or Wait?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Anthropic is expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq this week.

Anthropic is scheduled to make its stock market debut on Thursday, Nov. 19. The artificial intelligence (AI) developer is targeting a listing on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol ANT, with an expected IPO price between $70 and $78 per share.

Deciding whether to purchase Anthropic stock immediately depends largely on an investor's goals. Someone hoping to capitalize on short-term price movements has a different set of decisions to make than someone planning to own the company for the next decade.

Although both approaches can generate profits, historical IPO performance suggests that patient investors may have an advantage.

IPO stocks frequently generate excitement because retail investors want exposure to promising businesses before their stock prices appreciate. That enthusiasm often creates a difference between the offering price established by underwriters and the price shares command when trading begins. As a result, investors buying stock on the open market wind up paying substantially more than those who received allocations at the IPO price.

For example, electric vehicle (EV) manufacturer Rivian began trading as a public company in late 2021. According to LPL Financial, Rivian stock gained 22% immediately following its first trading session before eventually experiencing an 88% maximum drawdown during its first year. The company's initial valuation reflected aggressive expectations that became difficult to justify as investors reassessed its growth prospects relative to larger, better-funded rivals like Tesla.

Meta Platforms offers another example. Following its IPO in 2012, the social media conglomerate experienced a maximum first-year drawdown of 54%, despite already operating one of the world's most popular digital ecosystems. Although Meta eventually became a successful investment, investors who bought in its early days were forced to endure brutal losses before benefiting from the company's long-term growth.

Not every IPO follows an initial downward trajectory. Alphabet (GOOGL -1.24%) (GOOG -1.30%) delivered triple-digit first-year returns following its 2004 public debut. These contrasting outcomes illustrate why attempting to predict stock price performance is inherently speculative.

For investors seeking to build wealth over a long-term time horizon, Anthropic's initial trading performance should be less important than the company's underlying fundamentals. The developer behind the Claude family of models operates in an industry with enormous growth potential.

However, Anthropic’s path to sustained profitability remains uncertain as competition from OpenAI intensifies and the cost of compute continues rising. For this reason, evaluating Anthropic's investment prospects requires much more than simply projecting demand for AI-powered applications.

One important consideration is valuation. Anthropic's proposed offering price could imply a market capitalization around $1.4 trillion at the high end. If the company maintains its revenue growth pace but struggles to generate profit margins, Anthropic stock could plummet even as the underlying business expands.

Quarterly earnings reports should provide meaningful insight into revenue growth, operating expenses, customer demand, and management's ability to translate accelerating AI adoption into sustainable profitability. In addition, investors can also benchmark Anthropic's valuation against competing AI businesses.

For day traders, Anthropic's public debut could present a lucrative opportunity to capitalize on sharp price movements. However, this approach comes with risk, particularly if Anthropic stock opens significantly above the offering price or experiences an abrupt reversal.

For long-term investors, waiting until after the IPO is the more sensible approach. Anthropic's opportunity won’t disappear just because its shares started trading on a major exchange. If the company evolves into one of the world's most profitable AI businesses, investors will have years to participate alongside its growth.

Furthermore, waiting provides more time to evaluate whether Anthropic can grow into its valuation. As the company matures, investors will gain a better understanding of Anthropic's competitive position, capital requirements, and ability to generate free cash flow. That information is more valuable than securing shares in the days leading up to the IPO.

While Anthropic may become a generational business, that doesn't necessarily make the stock an attractive buy at any price. For patient investors, valuation discipline and long-term earnings growth matter more than buying shares on the first day of trading.
