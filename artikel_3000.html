UTEP Miners men’s basketball chase progress as new era comes home

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

The UTEP men's basketball didn't get the start it wanted in an 84-76 loss to Northern Kentucky in the Field of 68 24-Hour Opening Day Marathon, but it did lay down a marker heading into its home opener against Incarnate Word.

The Miners looked like a team that could improve on its 11-20 2025-26 season, but the rebuilt squad without an obvious star also has a long way to go to reach the middle of its new league.

UTEP is projected to finish in ninth-place in the 10-team Mountain West. UTEP opens conference play at home against the league favorite, New Mexico, on Jan. 2. The Miners, once a traditional power that won the 1966 national championship, last made the NCAA tournament in 2010 and last won a tournament game in 1992.

By the eye test, this is a slower Miners team than in the past, which they knew, but which got exposed a bit against NKU. They need to shoot better, particularly from the free-throw line, where they lost the opener (14-of-24 for the Miners; 30-of-33 for NKU), and rebound better.

Intangibly, coach Joe Golding said they need to be tougher, and that's what UTEP is going to get to work on Sunday, Nov. 8, at the newly christened GECU Arena at the Don Haskins Center.

"We want to play more physically and tougher than we did on Monday," sophomore point guard Leif Moeller said. "Have more energy than the opponent, being tougher. If we make shots or don't make shots we can't control it, we want to control things we can control like toughness."

Golding said he wants to see improvement everywhere, but the focus in practice this week has been on defense. Northern Kentucky shot 50% from the field against UTEP and got to the free-throw line 33 times, where it made 30.

"Defensively, there was a lot of frustration on our end," Golding said. "We did not guard it the way we've guarded. We expect a lot better defensive effort. They got the point at yesterday's (Wednesday's) practice, they got the point at yesterday's film session, they'll get the point today (Thursday). They have to own it, we'll get it fixed."

As for long-term goals for this team, those take a distant back seat to the immediate aim of improving against Incarnate Word. As matters stand at the moment, a realistic ending point for this regular season would be a top-six finish and a first-round bye in the league tournament. A fifth-place finish would put them in the top half of the Mountain West.

In that opening loss, only two players in the final year of their eligibility (starter Reece Miller and sixth man Jace Whiting) played double-figure minutes and two of their best players in that game, starting center Youssouf Traore and top reserve point guard Donovan Criss, are true freshmen with five years (minus one game) of eligibility remaining.

If this Miners team can have some success in the next few months and recruit and retain in the spring, it would be set up to contend for a title as soon as next year.

In terms of generating hope for the future, Traore and Criss had to excite Miner fans. The 19-year-old Traore, a 6-foot-10 center with extensive experience internationally with both the Mali national team and in Spain, was the last addition to the roster and the first player to jump out in the opener.

He led UTEP with 18 points and seven rebounds, which was completely in line with what he showed in practice since he arrived in the summer. More than his obvious athleticism, what blows everyone away around UTEP is how hard their best athlete plays. Traore could end up being the Miners' next star.

"Youssouf is great," Golding said. "He's growing as a player. Where he scored his baskets was rolls to the rim. We have to do a better job of finding him on rolls to the rim. Now tape is out on that and people are going to take that away and we're going to have to find other ways to get him the basketball.

"He's scoring the ball one-on-one in the block, that's something he's gotten better at. The motor is something we've been talking about from Day 1. He's got an incredible motor, he's an incredible athlete and there's a great energy about him."

What Traore isn't ready for yet is his first English interview. Fluent in Spanish and French (and Bambara; he's fluently trilingual) but still learning English, at his request he isn't comfortable on camera yet. But his teammates know they need to be more like him.

"Unbelievable motor," Miller said. "He's a freak athlete. He's the hardest worker on the floor. He pushes himself to exhaustion. We need more of that from everyone else."

UTEP has a long journey in front of it, which will take another step when it plays its first game of the season at the GECU Arena at the Don Haskins Center on Sunday.
