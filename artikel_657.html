Lucy Vives, Sofia Monroy, OKAN, Moy Bobadilla & More Emerging Latin Artists on Our Radar

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Here are the rising stars we've discovered in November, and who should be on your radar.

Every month, Billboard Latin and Billboard Español editors spotlight a group of rising artists whose music we love. Think “diamantes en bruto,” or “diamonds in the rough.” These are newcomers who have yet to impact the mainstream — but whose music excites us, and who we believe our readers should make a point to discover. Our latest edition of On the Radar Latin includes a wave of emerging artists, who we discovered by scrolling on social media, networking, or coming across their music at a showcase, for example. Why They Should Be on Your Radar: Earlier this year, Colombian artist Pipe Bueno teamed up with Cumbia Pedregal on “Dime Que Me Hiciste” — a winning blend of smooth, irresistibly-catchy cumbia with a distinct sound. Made up of brothers Emilio, José Pablo, and Luche Zepeda, this trio feels like a band of old souls reincarnated in 2026 with influences from pop, banda grupera, and regional Mexican music, among others. The more I listened to their music, the more I discovered elements of 1980s Latin America: long hair, colorful suits, and soft vocals that seem to sigh through the verses.

Grupo Pedregal’s musical and visual style stands out amid today’s hard-hitting, rhythmic sounds — embracing love songs, heartbreak and clean lyrics rooted in the timeless spirit of Mexican music. In May, the group released its debut album, Sabor a Llanto, featuring “Mi Peor Apuesta,” its second collaboration with singer-songwriter Eden Muñoz (following 2025’s “Sin Ti No Sé Vivir”). — INGRID FAJARDO

Song for Your Playlist: “Que Se Pudra El Amor”

Why They Should Be on Your Radar: When I first discovered Cuba’s reparto music, one of the first artists that captured by attention, mainly because of his lyrical content, was El Dukesito. With songs such as “La Oportunidad” and “Voy A Mi” — songs that are rooted in personal growth and potent affirmations, rather than the common provocative and street slang that characterizes the genre — I knew he was a special breed.

Born Nassir Cristo Duquesne Dublón in La Habana, the Miami-based artist dubs himself as “el más completo y versátil de la nueva” (the most complete and versatile artist of the new generation) — and his “No Llores Por El,” a ranchera-tinged pop song released this year with Bambino the Boss attests to that. With his crystalline vocals, the 30-year-old artist, who’s signed to Rami Récords, has received the stamp of approval from Cuban pioneers including Gente de Zona, Chocolate MC, and Wow Popy. Dukesito is currently making the rounds with his new singles “Solo Solito” and “Tu No Sientes” with Yeyo Pa’ Que Sepa — both released under Plus Media. — JESSICA ROIZ

Why They Should Be on Your Radar: Throughout this year, Eley Romero has demonstrated that he possesses all the qualities needed to transition from an emerging talent to a star of the new generation of Mexican music, with a repertoire centered on songs about love. In May 2026, he signed with La Empresa Music, which significantly boosted his streaming numbers — his tracks have now amassed over 74 million plays, at the time of publishing. One of these, “Qué Se Siente” — a collaboration with Adrián L. Santos — proved to be a turning point in his career, drawing the attention of major artists within the genre, such as Kane Rodríguez, Los Chavalitos, and Los 2 de La S. Born in Los Mochis, Sinaloa, the artist— who is also a songwriter — recently released the track “¿Cómo Le Digo A Mi Amá?” alongside Los Primos del Este. Looking ahead to early 2027, he has a series of significant collaborations lined up with El Rabanito, Raúl Beltrán and Efecto Elegante, which will mark another major milestone in his career. — TERE AGUILERA

Song for Your Playlist: “Qué Se Siente” feat. Adrián L. Santos

Why They Should Be on Your Radar: Powered by her wistful and seductive vocals, Lucy Vives gives life her debut EP called cimiento. The five-track project released earlier this month via M3 Records brings to the forefront her progressive project that’s rooted in an indie-alternative-pop realm with an intimate, vulnerable, and imaginative storytelling. Each song is penned by the Colombian-Puerto Rican artist herself, further making each song personal and relatable. “This first EP was a deeply introspective journey for me, one that ultimately became something like a book of short stories. Several of the songs are little stories of their own, and to me, this project is an introduction, a beginning for me and my music,” she explains in a press statement. An emerging act in her own right, the 30-year-old singer-songwriter first arrived on the Billboard charts in 2021 when the tropical “Besos En Cualquier Horario” in collab with Carlos Vives (her father) and Mau y Ricky reached No. 10 on the Latin Pop Airplay chart. — J.R.

Why They Should Be on Your Radar: In October, I went to see Grupo Firme perform at Guadalajara’s Palenque during the Día de Muertos celebrations. While I was backstage, I came across a tall young man wearing a white shirt, cowboy boots, and a black cowboy hat, who was accompanied by his parents. His charm and cheerful, ambitious energy were immediately apparent. His name was Moisés “Moy” Bobadilla Martínez, one of Firme’s surprise guests, who was born in 2001 in Santa María de los Ángeles, Jalisco.

Midway through Grupo Firme’s set that October night, Moy joined Eduin Caz for a duet of “Corazón,” then performed one of his solo songs called “Tus Mentiras.” I was struck not only by his voice and impressive high notes, but also by his commanding stage presence. In a venue where the stage feels so close to the audience, his presence made an even greater impact because of the way the place is set up. Following that performance, Moy had several key shows, including at the Plaza de Toros in Mexico City, Domo Care in Monterrey, Palenque de Querétaro, and more. Bobadilla’s latest single is called “Del Temu” in collaboration with Luis Mexia. — I.F.

Why They Should Be on Your Radar: With a joyous blend of Afro-Cuban roots with jazz, classical music and global rhythms, OKAN has been gaining recognition in North America, performing on stages like the Lincoln Center, the Kennedy Center and the Montreal Jazz Festival, as well as NPR’s Tiny Desk. Comprised of vocalists and multi-instrumentalists Elizabeth Rodríguez and Magdelys Savigne, who were born and raised in Cuba and now live between Toronto and Miami, this two-time JUNO Award-winning, Grammy-nominated duo just released its fourth studio album, Ife Ara (Yoruba for “self-love”) under Lulaworld Records, expanding their musical vocabulary with contemporary, more commercial sounds including reparto, trap, Latin pop and electronic music. — SIGAL RATNER-ARIAS

Song For Your Playlist: “Te Falta Grasa” feat. Daymé Arocena

Why They Should Be on Your Radar: After writing or co-writing hits for artists such as Alejandro Sanz, Danny Ocean, Fonseca and Ca7riel & Paco Amoroso, Venezuelan songwriter Santander made his debut as a singer this year under Universal Music Latino, proving that his voice and presence are well worth seeing and hearing. His initial releases (“Peliculear,” “Once Again” and “Full En Serio”) feature a poetic narrative, a pleasing, velvety tone and a fusion of Latin and global rhythms that’s highly commercial. With credits on proven hits like “Imagínate” by Danny Ocean and Kapo (which reached No. 1 on Billboard’s Latin Airplay chart — and Tropicalia by Fonseca (winner of the Latin Grammy for best contemporary tropical album) Santander is emerging as one of the most promising new voices in today’s Latin music scene. — S.R.A.

Why They Should Be on Your Radar: Born in Mexico City and raised in Sweden, Sofia Monroy spent years figuring out how to reconcile her two cultural identities through music. She initially wrote songs in English before incorporating Spanish when, as she put it, “something’s missing.” Drawing from the Swedish pop and Latin rhythms she grew up with, Monroy has carved out a multilingual strain of alternative pop that moves fluidly between Spanish, English and Swedish. “It’s just a culture clash, but that’s me at the end of the day,” she told Billboard Español at Billboard Brasil and Meta’s Latin Grammy-week activation.

While her 2025 debut, Morena, introduced that evolving identity, her more introspective 2026 sophomore album, WO[E]MAN, explores how women are perceived, judged and defined by others. Its standout “Pegadito” showcases Monroy’s knack for pairing emotional tension with rhythmic finesse. For the Latin Grammy best new artist nominee, finding her voice ultimately meant embracing the plurality of her upbringing. “I don’t have to choose,” she said. “I can do both.” — ISABELA RAYGOZA
