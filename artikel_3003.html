NextNav (NN) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Debt-free balance sheet and a SpaceX spectrum deal frame NextNav's regulatory push.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

NextNav Inc. (NN -5.83%) reported significant progress in its regulatory and technical objectives during the third quarter, supported by a substantially strengthened balance sheet following the elimination of all corporate debt. Management indicated that market validation for low-band spectrum assets has intensified, citing a proposed acquisition of spectrum by SpaceX as a key precedent for the scarcity value of these resources. The company continues to advance its proposal for a terrestrial Positioning, Navigation, and Timing (PNT) system as a complement to GPS, which is currently undergoing an interagency review process facilitated by the Federal Communications Commission. Management stated that the core issues of PNT resiliency and national security maintain strong bipartisan support as the regulatory process moves into 2027.

The third quarter was productive on all fronts, marked by continued regulatory progress, important technical milestones, new partnerships and perhaps most notably, highly visible market validation of the increasing value and strategic importance low-band spectrum assets. The proposed acquisition of Grain's low band spectrum assets by SpaceX defines an important market precedent, which highlights the finite nature and scarcity value of low-band spectrum. We believe this creates an increasingly constructive backdrop for our long-term opportunity and further validates the trend we have discussed for some time.

As demand for low band spectrum continues to grow, we believe there is an urgency to ensure these finite resources are maximized in support of the public interest. Beyond the transaction, we were particularly pleased to see SpaceX file 2 ex-parte letters referencing our lower 900 megahertz proposal. We believe these filings reflect growing industry recognition that additional low-band spectrum will be needed to support next-generation connectivity services. Furthermore, we believe the filings underscore the significant value that can be unlocked by putting these scarce spectrum resources to productive use in the near future.

Among other things, SpaceX urged the FCC to encourage fast investments to put scarce low- and mid-band spectrum to use, including for innovative services like terrestrial, direct-to-device and IoT services. Further, on the regulatory front, as we have discussed previously, the FCC has formally submitted a draft notice of proposed rule-making or NPRM focused on promoting the development of PNT technologies and solutions for an interagency review process. And now with the midterm election behind us, it is important to reiterate that the core issues underpinning this proceeding, including PNT's resiliency, national security, critical infrastructure protection, economic security and public safety have consistently received bipartisan support.

We continue to work with the administration and the FCC and are well positioned to engage constructively with the new Congress to advance a ground-based complement and backup to GPS as the process advances. While differing viewpoints are a standard feature of major spectrum proceedings, our team continues to engage extensively across the federal government and with key stakeholders, leveraging every appropriate avenue to equip decision-makers with engineering and data-driven analysis to advance the process. We believe they have resulted in meaningful progress within the administration and various federal agencies. We also continue to engage constructively and have conversations with users in the band, including key industries such as railroad operators, tolling and other industry stakeholders.

We seek to resolve coexistence matters through a fact-based approach that relies on testing and demonstrations. Notably, we concluded testing with the freight rail industry where we have been conducting real-world collaborative testing and detailed discussions on coexistence. Further, we have been holding early conversations with additional stakeholders in the band to develop appropriate test plans that will satisfy their systems and operational needs. NextNav continues to collaborate on coexistence testing and we have illustrated how our 5G PNT and sensing solutions can benefit their operations. Our team continues to engage with the public safety community as we advance our 5G-powered 3D location technology to meet their needs.

To date, we have engaged with Fire, Police and 911 officials across the country to advance indoor positioning capabilities that will enhance emergency response and first responder safety. These efforts have included discussions with Fairfax County, Arlington County, Phoenix, Philadelphia and Plano fire departments, North Central Texas 911 and the International Association of Firefighters. Commercially, we have been strategic in assembling the building blocks needed to enable new applications on our spectrum, including 5G capable PNT and sensing technologies designed to operate on standardized 5G networks. On positioning, we have partnered with Safran on timing with Oscilloquartz. And through our partnership with Tiami and AiRANACULUS, we are extending our capabilities into long-range sensing.

Together, these partnerships demonstrate the growing range of applications that can be deployed on spectrum including for national security, critical infrastructure and public safety. Specifically with Tiami, a recognized leader in drone sensing, we are showing how their existing capabilities can be integrated with NextNav signaling and deployed on our low-band spectrum. Importantly, this work highlights how sensing applications can operate on standardized 5G networks using our spectrum, creating a path towards scalable deployment of wide-area sensing capabilities. With AiRANACULUS, we are combining our technology and spectrum with our advanced signal processing, machine learning and AI capabilities to improve detection, identification and tracking of multiple drones over long distances.

What's key is that NextNav uniquely brings together the combination of the spectrum and technology capabilities needed to enable a wide-area long-range sensing solution at scale, including with sensing applications directly relevant to national security. Moreover, as these building blocks come together, we believe they expand the range of high-value applications that can be deployed on our spectrum. Separately, we achieved important technological validation milestones through real-world deployment and testing. Since early this year, we have been operating in Santa Clara County, California, under an experimental license utilizing the proposed band configuration and transmitting on a live 5G network environment.

These activities provide a real-world opportunity to validate our solution and further support the extensive technical analysis already on the record. Notably, we recently announced field testing results showing single-digit-meter positioning performance for both indoor and outdoor. This includes accurate positioning that can serve as a backup and also a complement to GPS in areas where GPS is challenged. Importantly, throughout our testing in Santa Clara, there has not been a single claim of interference. Alongside our regulatory process and commercial milestones, we remain in active dialogue with potential strategic partners across telecommunication providers and technology companies.

The demand for finite low-band spectrum continues to grow as the range of applications expand across wireless carriers, satellite operators and D2D providers as well as critical infrastructure, public safety and national security use cases.
