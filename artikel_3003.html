Union County stampede: Fightin' Tigers win latest SSAA road upset

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

For Robby Pruitt, the head coaching journey of a lifetime began in Jacksonville 42 years ago.

Some 42 years later, he's winning postseason games on the First Coast again, steering the stampede for Union County in a 28-21 upset of Paxon in the Sunshine State Athletic Association Atlantic League North semifinal on Nov. 6.

They've got eighth-graders in the lineup, even seventh-grader Ethan Williams. They field barely a dozen combined juniors and seniors, some of them injured. Now, Union County (7-5) is off to round three at No. 1 seed West Nassau on Nov. 13.

"I tell you, it's great winning a game like this with a bunch of young kids," Pruitt said.

In the same city where he landed his first coaching job with University Christian in 1984, on his way to the unprecedented feat of 200-win careers in both Florida and Georgia, he's still going strong in the first year of his second stint in Lake Butler. And so is his team.

During his previous tenure there, he guided Union County to three consecutive Florida High School Athletic Association championships and a state-record 52-game winning streak between 1994 and 1997. Two weeks after Paxon (9-3) overcame Union County 36-19 in the regular season, the veteran coach leaned on old-school football to bring back that old-time postseason success for the Fightin' Tigers.

"We came out with a different mindset," sophomore running back Ricky Barr said. "We could not let them beat us again."

Yes, in the year 2026, there's still a team running the ball 68 times in a high school football playoff. Union County made it work, too, an approach that keeps working and keeps winning.

No matter the setbacks: They were already without injured leading rusher Bryce Lawton and lost key senior lineman Jaxson Register to an apparent fourth-quarter leg injury that stopped the game for more than 10 minutes.

No matter the score: After Paxon seized a 14-7 lead midway through the third quarter on KJ Palmer's 4-yard run, the Fightin' Tigers refused to abandon the run, churning out 315 yards on the ground and striking back for 21 unanswered points.

Sophomore Barr carried 30 times for 137, joined by freshman Ja'len Jonas (15 attempts, 81 yards) and two-way touchdown machine Edward Durant (16 attempts, 67 yards). A week after running 26 times for 285 yards to take down unbeaten Cottondale, Barr is embracing life as a backfield workhorse.

"I owe it all to my O-linemen," said Barr, who scored the touchdown that staked Union County to a 15-14 lead late in the third quarter after Durant's two-point conversion.

They may need more of the same next week against West Nassau, which like Paxon leans heavily on the ground game. But after consecutive road win, Pruitt sees his young squad growing by the week.

"For them to come in here and do that, that was big," Pruitt said. "I'm proud of them."

Facing off against a 2,000-yard rusher in Paxon senior KJ Palmer, who finished just short of 5,000 yards in Golden Eagles career records, Union County was able to limit the damage.

Palmer got his yards, 113 in all, and threatened to take over the game in the second quarter during a 99-yard touchdown march that included runs of 20, 20 and 29. But a Union County defensive front anchored by eighth-grade tackle LiDarius Seymore and freshman Jareem Jenkins controlled the trenches for most of the second half.

"They're a good football team, and that game could have gone either way tonight," Pruitt said. "It was just our turn."

Two second-half takeaways, the second on an interception by Cason Scott, set up clock-chewing Union County drives that ended in Durant's second and third touchdown dives of the game.

Paxon set up a tense finish late on Javier Dixon's touchdown to Josh Marshall with 28 seconds left, but when the ensuing onside kick failed, Union County drained the scoreboard to zero.

With seventh-graders and eighth-graders in the lineup, and a coach who's been winning games since before some of his players' parents were born, the Fightin' Tigers aren't lacking confidence on the road to West Nassau.

"We're coming for them," Durant said. "We're going to make a statement."
