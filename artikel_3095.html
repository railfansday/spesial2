Retailers face more AI-driven cyberattacks ahead of Black Friday

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Retailers are heading into Black Friday on 27 November facing faster and more frequent cyberattacks, as AI helps criminals move more quickly. Many of them also have no insurance to cover the damage, Reuters reported.

Claims data from insurer CFC shows that ransomware attacks on retail, leisure, and hospitality businesses are 50% more frequent in November than in an average month. Ransomware is malicious software that locks a company’s systems until a payment is made.

The sector is also among the least protected. In CFC’s cyber insurance survey, only 43% of retailers said they carry cyber cover, compared with 81% of financial services firms.

“Black Friday should be treated as a resilience exercise as much as a revenue opportunity,” Lindsey Maher, CFC’s head of global cyber development, told Reuters.

Retailers rarely buy extra cover just for a busy weekend, according to David Molony, who leads cyber for Europe, the Middle East, and Africa at broker Aon. Instead, they focus on testing their systems, because an attack that stops them trading during peak season can quickly turn into a large financial loss.

Insurers also say AI is shortening the time companies have to react. At Beazley Security, chief product and technology officer Francisco Donoso told Reuters that exposed software vulnerabilities are almost six times higher than a year ago.

“It’s really hard for organisations to keep up,” Donoso said.

Sydonie Williams, head of international cyber risks at Beazley, said long supply chains and large stores of card data make retailers an attractive target. However, she added that AI is also helping defenders.

UK businesses have already seen how costly a single attack can be. Marks & Spencer estimated that last year’s ransomware attack cost it about £300m, although its cyber insurance was expected to pay out more than £100m of that, Insurance Business reported.

A separate attack on Jaguar Land Rover cost an estimated £1.9bn, according to the Cyber Monitoring Centre. The centre called it the most economically damaging cyber event ever to hit the UK.

In the US, Target spent a net $ 202 million dealing with a breach during the 2013 holiday season, which exposed at least 40 million payment cards.
