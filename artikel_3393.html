5-Episode Netflix Thriller Series Adapts One Of Harlan Coben's First Novels

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

While Harlan Coben had penned a handful of Myron Bolitar novels before writing the book that Netflix adapted into a show in 2021, the series was based on one of his first standalone novels. If Prime Video’s Reacher franchise proves one thing, it is that streaming fans love a good page-to-screen adaptation. To be more specific, streaming show adaptations of best-selling crime, thriller, and mystery novels tend to fare extraordinarily well on Prime Video, Netflix, Hulu, and Peacock alike.

Whether it is an adaptation of Andrea Mara’s novel All Her Fault breaking viewing records on Peacock or Netflix’s 30-episode take on Matthew Quick’s book The Night Agent rivaling Reacher in its popularity, the formula has proven a hit over and over again. Recently, Prime Video’s Cross cashed in on this trend, although the streaming service’s 2014 hit Bosch and its many spinoffs pioneered the approach over a decade earlier.

However, the legendary crime writer Harlan Coben is something of an unusual case when it comes to streaming shows. Coben first became a bestselling author back in 1990 with his standalone novel Play Dead, followed, by 1991’s Miracle Cure. However, it was the 12-book Myron Bolitar series that made Coben a star, beginning with 1995’s Deal Breaker. Despite the popularity of this novel series, Netflix has mostly adapted his standalone books, like Gone for Good, to the screen rather than Myron Bolitar novels.

Related in 2002, Gone for Good was a major hit for Coben. The novel follows Will, who is horrified to learn the disturbing truth about his brother Ken’s disappearance eleven years earlier after his girlfriend goes missing in the present day. Released in August 2021, Gone for Good’s Netflix adaptation relocated the story to France and changed some names accordingly, starring Finnegan Oldfield as Guillaume and Nicolas Duvauchelle as his missing brother, Fred.

Unlike Prime Video’s Reacher franchise, Netflix’s Coben adaptations all feature different main characters in stories that have no canon connections. Even though there are plenty of Myron Bolitar books to adapt, and many more YA spinoff novels starring his nephew Mickey, the streaming service has thus far opted to focus instead on Coben’s standalone efforts. These include No Second Chance, The Stranger, The Innocent, The Woods, Gone For Good, Stay Close, Hold Tight, Fool Me Once, Missing You, Run Away, and I Will Always Find You.

If that list seems long, that’s because it is. All told, there are 13 Coben shows on Netflix, with seven being English-language productions, while a further six are foreign-language shows like Gone For Good. Interestingly enough, much like Michael Connelly’s The Lincoln Lawyer was a Matthew McConaughey movie before the novel series became a Netflix show, the first screen adaptation of Coben’s work was a feature length movie, director Guillame Canet’s 2006 thriller Tell No One.

If the fact that Tell No One and Gone For Good are French-language productions didn’t give it away, Coben’s work has a massive worldwide fan base that spans the globe. His twisty thrillers translate well into other languages, which is why The Woods, Just One Look, and Hold Tight were Polish productions, The Innocent is a Spanish series, and 2025’s Caught hailed from Argentina.

Between that Coben series and the same year’s long-awaited comic adaptation The Eternaut, Argentina is emerging as a major hub for Netflix shows. However, it is tough to beat some of the writer’s earliest shows. With its clever twists, inventive plotting, and poignant character work, Netflix’s 2021 hit Gone For Good remains one of the author’s most underrated adaptations to date.
