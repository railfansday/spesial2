Walker Kessler gets chance to redeem himself against former team

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

For an NBA player, there's always a little something extra when he faces his former team.

He wants to impress. He wants to remind the franchise what it's missing. He wants to have the last laugh.

That's why Walker Kessler's return to Utah was so disappointing.

Walker Kessler of the Los Angeles Lakers drives to the basket against the Phoenix Suns in Phoenix, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

Amid an impressive season in which Kessler has been the perfect fit alongside Luka Doncic and Austin Reaves on a soaring Lakers team that has won nine straight games and has the best record in the league at 13-2, Kessler's game against the Jazz was his nadir.

After opening the season with 26-point and 22-point performances, he had a disappearing act against his former team. He finished with season lows in points (two) and rebounds (seven) in the Lakers' 132-123 win over the Jazz on Oct. 25, during which Doncic saved the day with a 52-point masterpiece to claw the Lakers out of an 18-point hole.

"I definitely let my emotions get the best of me," Kessler said after the game.

Kessler should get a chance to rectify things when the Lakers host the Jazz on Wednesday at Crypto.com Arena. Kessler, who missed Tuesday's game against the Hornets because of right lower leg soreness, is expected to be available against his former team on the second night of a back-to-back.

The 7-foot-1 center spent the first four years of his career with the Jazz before the Lakers acquired him in a sign-and-trade in July, giving up two first-round picks and two first-round pick swaps on top of a four-year, $130 million deal.

Utah is where Kessler made a name for himself. It's where he established himself as one of the league's top rim defenders and rebounders. It's where he drew interest from one of the NBA's biggest superstars.

Walker Kessler of the Los Angeles Lakers plays against the Phoenix Suns in Phoenix, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

Last offseason, Doncic made it clear to Lakers brass that he wanted Kessler as his franchise center. The team responded by going all in to acquire the 25-year-old, emptying its treasure trove of assets to get the Slovenian superstar his dream big man.

Kessler is averaging 14.5 points, 10.9 rebounds and 2.2 blocks per game. He's the sixth-leading rebounder in the league and third in blocks. He has had a double-double in five straight games.

When it comes to Kessler, box scores only tell part of the story.

He deters players from entering the paint. He alters shots. He emboldens the perimeter defense to take risks, which has led to the Lakers leading the league in steals per game (11). And he sets excellent screens for Doncic and Reaves, who are the NBA's highest-scoring duo, averaging a combined 63.7 points per game.

Phoenix Suns forward Miles Bridges, left, fouls Los Angeles Lakers center Walker Kessler under the basket during an NBA game, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, in Phoenix. AP Photo/Darryl Webb

When Kessler played the Jazz, he wanted those things to shine. But his grand homecoming was a dud.

"Obviously, Utah was the first four years of my career," Kessler said Oct. 25. "It's a special place for me. Kind of jump-started my career, you know, a lot of good memories. But definitely that was, yeah, just letting my emotions get the best of me. [I] kind of hyped myself up more than I usually do and kind of got too amped and then just couldn't find the ball, couldn't find the rhythm, just kind of was off. A couple fouls that were tough. A couple blocks I usually [make]. It's one of those nights. But fortunately basketball is not a one-man sport."

Kessler now has a chance to rectify that performance.

He has played 14 games with his new team. He's comfortable. He'll have fresh legs.

And he'll have an opportunity to turn that nadir into nothing but a forgettable divot.
