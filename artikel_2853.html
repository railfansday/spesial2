Bye Week Perfect Time to Address Red Zone Woes

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

PITTSBURGH — At 6-2 and heading into their bye week, the Pittsburgh Steelers have won four games in a row and seem to be getting better each week. One area of concern, however, is their red zone offense.

The Steelers have struggled in the red zone all season, ranking near the bottom of the NFL at roughly 47% to 50% in touchdown efficiency, primarily because they abandon their dominant run game to force low-percentage passes.

Offensive coordinator Brian Angelichio noted that the playbook features run-pass options (RPOs) and line checks where quarterback Aaron Rodgers evaluates loaded boxes or pressure looks.

"I think it all goes into it," Angelichio said. "There's run-pass options, there's checks, you know, is it a loaded box? Is it a pressure look? So you're kind of hunting all of those things, and we just got to look at that."

Some critics and analysts suggest that Rodgers has often checked out of runs or targeted low-percentage throws instead of sticking to the ground. But personnel issues have compounded the problem, with two of the Steelers' top four wide receivers having been sidelined with injuries since Week 4.

Head coach Mike McCarthy and Angelichio have both openly acknowledged the questionable play-calling sequences and committed to self-evaluating and fixing the red-zone package during the team's Week 9 bye.

"That's something that we're going to look at in the next two days," Angelichio said. "Because, to your point, we have to be better down there."

Brendan Howe provided reporting from Pittsburgh.
