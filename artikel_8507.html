Getting your phone service and home internet from one company can cut your combined bill, since phone carriers discount their home internet and cable companies sell low-priced phone lines to their internet customers. Before you switch, add up the bundle’s monthly total and compare it with the cheapest phone plan and internet plan you could buy separately.

The discounts are real, but their conditions decide whether the bundle saves you money.

The big phone carriers discount the internet bill:

Cable companies cut the phone bill instead. Xfinity Mobile’s Mobile Select plan costs $30 a month per line, and Spectrum Mobile charges $30 a month for each Unlimited line after the first. Both sell only to their own internet customers and run on Verizon’s network.

A bundle is worth it only when its total beats buying both services separately. Add up its monthly cost with every condition met, such as AutoPay and the per-line price for your number of lines. Then price the cheapest phone plan that covers your data and the cheapest internet plan at your address. Count any money still owed on a phone too, since switching carriers with a balance makes it due.

Say a couple keeps Xfinity Internet and pays a major carrier $110 a month before taxes for two unlimited lines. Two Mobile Select lines cost $60 before taxes, $50 less than the carrier’s $110, though each line’s data can slow when the network is busy once it uses 50GB in a month.

Dropping either service can cost you the discount. T-Mobile’s bill credits stop if you cancel a line, and Xfinity Mobile’s current plans cost $10 a month more for customers who drop Xfinity Internet. Check coverage too, since 5G home internet isn’t sold at every address and a cable company’s phone lines rely on Verizon’s signal where you live and work.

Compare the totals at the prices you’ll pay after the first year, since a free first-year line or a low introductory internet price won’t last.
