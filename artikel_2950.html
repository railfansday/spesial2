Did Nvidia Make a Big Mistake by Reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO so Quickly?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

It took less than two weeks after resigning for Huang to return as Nvidia CEO.

By David Jagielski, CPA – Nov 24, 2026 at 3:45PM EST

Nvidia (NVDA -5.86%) made a big announcement yesterday: Jensen Huang was returning as CEO. It was a sharp and seemingly sudden turnaround from recent days when it looked as though the company's board was ready to move on with Colette Kress as its Interim CEO while it took a long look at the situation and considered a permanent replacement.

While many investors were likely bracing for a potentially long, drawn-out process, that didn't end up happening. Instead, on Monday morning, news came out that Huang was back. Will that prove to be a big mistake?

Nvidia's board was clearly swayed by the U.S. president, who said he was behind the deal to bring Huang back as CEO. While he provided assurances that there weren't national security risks, the fact that an investigation remains ongoing raises questions about whether that assessment might ultimately change.

It also calls into question how the president convinced the board so quickly to reinstate Huang, especially since it recently said it wasn't considering making the move at all. To do a complete 180 so quickly is definitely surprising. It certainly looked as though the board had moved on from Huang.

Although the tech stock was sinking amid news of Huang's resignation, the move to reinstate Huang seemed rushed, calling into question the board's overall governance. It did, however, state in its decision to reinstate Huang that the investigation "will proceed to conclusion." But given that Huang has already been reinstated, it would be an even worse look for the board to change its mind yet again.

Meanwhile, Huang is still scheduled to meet with Congress relating to his involvement with a possible Chinese spy. The issues related to Huang are by no means resolved, and the board's quick and sudden move to reinstate him could end up backfiring if more serious issues are indeed uncovered.

Nvidia's board effectively caved. Even before the president stepped in, it appeared that with the massive decline in Nvidia's share price, the market had spoken -- it wanted Huang back. I believe even without the president's push, Huang would have returned as CEO; it may have just taken a bit longer.

While it is disappointing to see the board not diligently and thoroughly investigate the matter first before making a decision, I don't believe it'll prove to have any lasting or serious effects on the stock. For the time being, it didn't appear to have much of a choice given the stock's massive sell-off.

Nvidia remains a top tech stock to own, and any issues relating to Huang likely aren't going to be enough to weigh on its future. In fact, now can be a good time to buy the stock, as its valuation has taken a beating in recent weeks.
