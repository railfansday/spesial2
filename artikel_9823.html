Capacitor vs No-Code Wrappers: Shipping Your Web App to iOS and Android in 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

You have a web app. Users keep asking for "the app". Rewriting everything in Swift and Kotlin is out of the question. So you wrap it.

There are two realistic ways to do that in 2026: Capacitor, or a no-code wrapper service. I work at Glideep, which is one of the no-code options, so I'll try to keep this honest and say where Capacitor is the better pick.

Capacitor wraps your web app in a native iOS and Android project and gives you plugins for native APIs.

Then you open the projects in Xcode and Android Studio, set icons, signing and permissions, and build.

Costs: free, but every OS update, plugin upgrade and store requirement change lands on you.

You paste your web app's URL, configure the app (icon, splash, navigation, push), and the service builds signed apps for you. Median, WebViewGold and Glideep work this way.

Trade-off: you're limited to the native features the service supports.

And on Android: new personal Google Play accounts (created after 13 Nov 2023) must run a closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 days before production. Plan for it.

If native features are your product, use Capacitor. If your web app is the product and you want it in the stores this week, a no-code wrapper is usually the faster and cheaper path over the first year.
