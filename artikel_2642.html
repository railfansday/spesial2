Despite strong efforts from Ben Saraf, Drake Powell, Long Island fall to Delaware, 108-107

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Long Island Nets suffered a heartbreaking defeat on Monday night, falling 108-107 to the Delaware Blue Coats in their second home game of the 2026-27 NBA G League season. The loss came one game after Long Island had won its own last minute context with the College Park Skyhawks.

The basketball gods giveth as well as taketh away.

Long Island entered the contest looking to build on its early-season momentum but ultimately came up just short in a tightly contested matchup, dropping to 3-2 on the year. With the two teams parent clubs — Brooklyn Nets and the Philadelphia 76ers — playing at Barclays, the game only drew 861 fans to Nassau Coliseum.

Despite the disappointing result, there were several encouraging individual performances, particularly from Brooklyn Nets players on assignment, Ben Saraf and Drake Powell, who combined for 32 points.

Long Island trailed 30-27 after the opening quarter before erupting for 35 points in the second, taking a 62-59 advantage into halftime. The Nets extended their lead to four points entering the final period, but Delaware outscored Long Island 23-18 in the fourth quarter to escape with a one-point victory. Turnovers and missed opportunities ultimately proved costly, as the Nets committed 23 turnovers and struggled to find their offensive rhythm down the stretch.

One of the biggest stories of the night was Saraf, who continued his encouraging start to the season. The Brooklyn Nets guard finished with 17 points, six assists, four rebounds and three steals in 30 minutes. Saraf shot 6-of-12 from the field, although he struggled from beyond the arc, connecting on just one of his five three-point attempts.

Saraf became the first player in franchise history with six or more assists in each of a season's first five games, and his 43 total assists lead the NBA G League so far this season.

Saraf has quickly established himself as one of Long Island's most important playmakers. After recording 21 points and 12 assists against Greensboro earlier this season, the young guard has continued to demonstrate his ability to create opportunities for teammates. While his five turnovers against Delaware were concerning, his overall development remains an encouraging storyline for Brooklyn's organization.

"I think I always had the ability to pass," Saraf tells NetsDaily about his evolution into becoming a bit more of a playmaker. "I have a little bit more experience from last year, and I'm just trying to get everybody the ball."

One of the biggest benefits of Saraf's assignment to Long Island has been the opportunity to receive consistent playing time and take on significant offensive responsibilities. Rather than playing limited minutes at the NBA level, Saraf has been able to gain valuable experience running an offense, making decisions, and adjusting to the professional game in North America.

"It's helped a lot," Saraf told ND about how his consistent playing time with Long Island has helped with his confidence and development. "Of course I cannot play with Brooklyn, so I have to be here. I think we have a great group. Today was an unnecessary loss, but in general I think we're doing great things and we will do great things, and I'm happy."

Although Saraf has shown plenty of promise, there are still areas of his game that need improvement. His outside shooting remains inconsistent, and limiting turnovers will be important as he continues developing into a more complete guard. However, the early results suggest that his time with Long Island could be extremely valuable in preparing him for future opportunities in Brooklyn. Saraf is the second youngest player on the Brooklyn roster, younger than Mikel Brown Jr. and older than Nolan Traore.

"I think getting more and more comfortable being a point guard," Saraf tells ND on what he wants to improve the most. "I have Jorge and Swords who are helping me a lot with that, and I'm getting a lot of reps so it's great for my development."

While Saraf continued showcasing his playmaking ability, Powell delivered one of his better offensive performances since joining the Brooklyn organization. The Brooklyn Nets rookie finished with 15 points, three assists, four steals, and one block in 30 minutes, shooting 6-of-11 from the field.

Powell's performance was particularly encouraging considering his struggles during Long Island's season opener against Greensboro, when he scored just six points on 2-of-6 shooting. Against Delaware, Powell looked more comfortable offensively, attacking opportunities and finding ways to contribute despite connecting on just one of his three attempts from beyond the arc.

"No adjustment really, I'd say," Powell tells ND, if he had to make any adjustments between playing with Brooklyn and Long Island. "There's no difference between being with the big club and the G League team. We're running the same concepts. I think I just have to take it within myself."

The former North Carolina standout entered the NBA with a reputation for his defensive potential, and that ability was on display against Delaware. Powell's four steals were a team high, while his combination of length, athleticism, and defensive instincts helped him make an impact beyond the scoring column.

However, Powell's offensive development remains one of the biggest areas to monitor. Finding consistency with his jump shot and becoming a more reliable scoring threat will be important as he continues working toward a larger role at the NBA level.

"Yeah, just playing my game, playing free," Powell tells ND about his biggest offensive focus coming to the G League to further develop. "Just trusting myself out there to make the simpler reads."

For Powell, his time with Long Island represents an opportunity to continue building confidence and gaining experience against professional competition. While his defensive abilities provide a strong foundation, demonstrating improvement offensively could be the difference in determining how quickly he earns additional opportunities with Brooklyn.

"Just the consistency," Powell tells ND about his personal goals and what he hopes to prove to the Brooklyn coaching staff. "I think that's the main piece. I want to win too."

Outside of Saraf and Powell, Grant Nelson continued his productive start to the season, finishing with 15 points, eight rebounds, and three assists in 25 minutes. This marked Nelson's 10th consecutive game scoring in double figures dating back to last season. The Brooklyn Nets two-way player was extremely efficient, shooting 6-of-8 from the field while collecting three offensive rebounds. Although Nelson fell short of another double-double, he remained an important presence in Long Island's frontcourt. Nelson went down at the end of the game and had to be taken to the locker room early. We will closely monitor this at ND.

Dain Dainja also continued his impressive opening stretch, contributing 14 points and four rebounds in 22 minutes off the bench. Dainja shot 4-of-5 from the field and converted three of his five free-throw attempts. After recording consecutive double-doubles against Greensboro and College Park, the big man once again provided Long Island with efficient scoring around the basket.

Dion Brown added 14 points on 7-of-14 shooting, continuing his consistent offensive production off the bench. Dwight Murray Jr. also made a valuable contribution, finishing with 11 points, six rebounds and six assists in 24 minutes. His ability to facilitate the offense provided Long Island with another playmaking option alongside Saraf.

Joshua Jefferson finished with 12 points, eight rebounds, five assists, one steal, and one block. He has reached double figures in scoring in each of his first four NBA G League games. The Brooklyn Nets rookie struggled from the field, shooting 4-of-11, but continued demonstrating his versatility by contributing across multiple statistical categories. Tyler Bilodeau added six points, four rebounds, and two blocks, although foul trouble limited him to 23 minutes before he fouled out.

As a team, Long Island shot an efficient 40-of-75 from the field, approximately 53%, while connecting on eight of its 25 three-point attempts. However, the Nets' 23 turnovers proved particularly damaging in a game decided by a single point. Long Island also committed 31 personal fouls, another area that will need improvement moving forward.

Now sitting at 3-2, the Nets have demonstrated their potential on both ends of the floor, but Monday's narrow defeat serves as a reminder that limiting turnovers and executing in crucial moments will be essential moving forward. They return to the court on Wednesday at 7 p.m. EST at home for a rematch with the Delaware Blue Coats.
