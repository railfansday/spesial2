Jensen Huang apologizes to Nvidia employees and says he's in therapy

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Huang admitted that he had failed employees, owned up to breaking Nvidia's code of conduct, and said he appreciated being given another opportunity

Nvidia $NVDA CEO Jensen Huang apologized to employees at an all-hands meeting on Tuesday and told them he is in therapy.

Huang called it "the hardest company meeting" he had held, according to Bloomberg. He admitted to employees that he had failed them, owned up to breaking Nvidia's code of conduct, and said he appreciated being given another opportunity to run the business he helped start.

Nvidia's board reinstated Huang as CEO on Monday, less than two weeks after he resigned following revelations of multiple affairs, including one with a woman suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset. The board said its vote was unanimous and followed a series of meetings with Huang and assurances from top U.S. officials that he did not post a national security risk. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress had served as interim CEO while keeping her CFO duties.

Huang credited the Trump administration with speedily sharing intelligence with Nvidia, which he said allowed the board to conclude that the dangers of bringing him back were small, according to The Wall Street Journal.

He said the board reached its decision after reviewing the evidence and getting assurance that national security was not at risk, and that the wishes of top government officials to have him back did not influence it, according to Bloomberg. Huang said President Donald Trump loves Nvidia. "He is my president," Huang said. "I am in service of making our president — our country — successful."

Huang focused on the code of conduct during the meeting. On Sunday, he told the board that there had been more affairs than the two widely reported in the media, according to The Journal. Those two involved an employee and an executive of a company that buys Nvidia's products, a person U.S. officials suspected of acting as an asset for Chinese intelligence.

Nvidia said a board committee of independent directors is investigating reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, and that the inquiry will proceed to conclusion. Huang supports the investigation and is committed to cooperating with it, the company said.

Nvidia stock fell more than 7% in Tuesday trading after a rally on Monday.
