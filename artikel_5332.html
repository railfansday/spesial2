E.U. plans to revamp oil reserve rules to match emergency stocks to national fuel use

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

A draft document would require at least one third of emergency stocks to be held as products other than crude oil

The European Commission is preparing to overhaul E.U. oil stockpiling rules so the product mix in emergency stocks reflects each member state's fuel use, according to Bloomberg. A draft document seen by Bloomberg on Thursday lays out the plan, which the Commission intends to approve in early December, though the text may change.

Current rules do not oblige countries to align the kinds of fuel they hold in reserve with what they actually burn at home, according to Bloomberg. Under the draft, stock composition would need to feature a minimum of one refined product, with options including fuel oil, diesel, jet fuel and gasoline. The draft would also let armed forces tap emergency liquid fuel reserves on a temporary, exceptional basis, provided certain requirements are met.

The draft says: "At least one third of emergency stocks must be held as products other than crude oil, and composition should reflect the national risk assessment, including refining capacity and domestic needs," according to Bloomberg. The Commission declined to comment, Bloomberg said.

The Commission said in an April communication that it would begin revising the Oil Stocks Directive to address weaknesses identified in the current crisis. It said it would consider whether different oil products need specific stock requirements. The document said about 40% of E.U. jet fuel consumption is imported and about half of those imports pass through the Strait of Hormuz.

The Middle East conflict added €40 billion ($44 billion) to the E.U.'s spending on imported diesel and between €16 billion and €19 billion to its jet fuel costs from March through September, according to Bloomberg, citing E.U. estimates. The same estimates put the cost of a one-week interruption to fuel supplies at 0.15% to 0.2% of the average member state's yearly GDP. Oil accounts for about 38% of E.U. energy consumption.

European governments have been releasing reserves to ease prices. French Economy Minister Roland Lescure said on Oct. 29 that Europe would put 42 million barrels of its oil reserves on the market over three months, with half of the volume in diesel. Lescure said at the time that Europe would leave open the possibility of additional releases.
