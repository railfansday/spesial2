Knicks eke out lucky win in Minnesota after fourth-quarter collapse

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

MINNEAPOLIS — Sometimes, it pays to be lucky.

The Knicks did just about everything they could to throw away a win. Their brutal collapse deserved a loss.

But somehow, they escaped with a 102-99 win over the Timberwolves on Thursday night at the Target Center.

Karl-Anthony Towns of the New York Knicks drives to the basket against the Minnesota Timberwolves on Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026, in Minneapolis. NBAE via Getty Images

The Knicks led by 12 entering the fourth quarter and were quickly up by 16 after Tyler Kolek's four-point play to start the quarter. But the Knicks proceeded to miss their next 13 shots and saw their lead dwindle to four.

It appeared they had stabilized a bit when Karl-Anthony Towns' driving layup and Mikal Bridges' subsequent 3-pointer put them back up by nine.

But Towns, after review, was given a technical for flopping for trying to get a foul called on Rudy Gobert. A free throw, a Gobert alley-oop and an Ayo Dosunmu 3-pointer later, and the Knicks led by just three. Towns was then called for an offensive foul and Mikal Bridges badly missed a jumper on the Knicks' next offensive possession.

New York Knicks guard Miles McBride (2) and Minnesota Timberwolves forward Jaden McDaniels reach for the ball on Nov. 19, 2026, in Minneapolis. AP Photo/Ellen Schmidt

But the Timberwolves couldn't find a quality shot in between and never got closer than that three-point deficit. LaMelo Ball badly missed a potential game-tying 3-pointer with under 10 seconds left, and the Timberwolves weren't able to foul the Knicks after they corralled the rebound.

Despite the ugly finish, it meant the Knicks closed their four-game road trip 3-1 before heading back to New York.

Towns had 17 points at halftime, and Jalen Brunson erupted for 14 points in the third quarter. It appeared the Knicks would cruise to an impressive win over a quality opponent.

But Towns went 1-for-5 in the fourth quarter, while Brunson went scoreless in the quarter. As a team, the Knicks went 2-for-14 and scored just 12 points in that fourth quarter.

"I wanted to put on a show for these fans," Towns, returning to Minnesota as an opponent for the third time since the Knicks' blockbuster trade, said. "In the second half I didn't shoot well, I ain't gonna lie to you."
