At least 24 killed and dozens missing in confectionery factory fire in Indonesia

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Rescuers brave unstable structures in search for missing as DNA needed to identify victims

Indonesia has confirmed the deaths of 24 people in a fire that tore ⁠through a factory, as rescuers braved unstable structures in the burned wreckage to search for ⁠about 30 people ⁠still missing.

Authorities ​were still investigating the cause of the fire, which broke out on Saturday in the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia confectionery factory in Cikande in Banten province, about 50km west of Jakarta.

Said Iqbal, ⁠a presidential adviser on labour issues, told reporters 24 people had died. He said the ​implementation of safety protocols was being ‌assessed and that he ‌would summon the factory’s chief executive.

Preliminary reports indicated the fire might have ‌been caused by an air-conditioner short circuit, Said added. The factory is owned by United Family Food, which did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The condition of the victims made visual identification impossible, prompting authorities to urge family members to provide DNA samples and other information that could be used to identify their loved ones, said Wahyu Hidayati Dwi Palupi, who heads the national police’s victim identification division.

She said body bags containing the remains of the victims were transferred to a hospital for identification.

The blaze had largely been contained on Sunday but firefighters were carrying out intensive cooling operations on Monday before they could access all parts of the factory, said Tubagus Mahfud, operations chief of the Serang fire department.

“We are conducting sweeps in the areas that have already been brought under control,” Mahfud said. “Parts of the structure had collapsed, while poor lighting conditions required firefighters to proceed cautiously.”

He also said the factory’s production materials could cause the fire to reignite, making it critical to complete cooling operations.

Anxious relatives have waited for news outside the burnt-out factory.

The Banten province governor, Andra Soni, said an estimated 60 people were inside the factory when it caught fire.

The firm employs about 5,000 people and is a major supplier of consumer goods to the domestic market. Its ‌products include Milkita, a popular confectionery brand, and Lemonilo, an instant noodle variety, according to its website.

The company’s chief executive, Steven Wijaya, said the factory regularly conducted emergency and evacuation drills in conjunction with local firefighters. He said the facility had adequate evacuation routes and fire protection systems that complied with applicable safety standards and were functioning at the time of the fire.

Indonesia has experienced a series of deadly industrial fires in recent years, raising concerns about workplace safety standards and emergency preparedness at manufacturing facilities.

In December last year, a blaze that was likely caused by a drone battery explosion tore through a seven-storey building in Jakarta and killed at least 22 people, police said.

In 2023, at least 13 people were killed in the country’s east after an explosion at a nickel-processing plant.
