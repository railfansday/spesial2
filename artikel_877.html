Soldier Reeves emerges as future star with Long Distance Hurdle win

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Soldier Reeves banished doubts about his staying ability with a magnificent display in the Coral Long Distance Hurdle. In the feature race, Dan Skelton's charge proved too good for previous winners Impose Toi and Strong Leader - who was gallant in second.

Soldier Reeves emerged as the new kid on the block in the staying division with an impressive comeback victory in the Coral Long Distance Hurdle at Newbury.

Dan Skelton's five-year-old mixed it with the best around in novice hurdles last season, most notably finishing fourth behind King Rasko Grey when upped in trip for the Turners Novices' Hurdle at the Cheltenham Festival before finding only high-class stablemate Bossman Jack too strong at Aintree.

He faced another step into the unknown on his reappearance, taking on more hardened stayers on his first start over three miles under rules, but travelled powerfully throughout in the hands of Harry Skelton and was right on the heels of the redoubtable Strong Leader on the approach to the final flight.

The latter did his best to regain his crown, having emerged victorious two years ago and finished a close second to Impose Toi last term, but Soldier Reeves (3-1) was the stronger at the finish and passed the post with three and a quarter lengths in hand.

Skelton will now point his emerging stayer to Ascot's Long Walk Hurdle just before Christmas.

He said: "I was very impressed with him and I wasn't surprised he won, but I was surprised he could do it first time out.

"He still didn't relax all that well, but he relaxed well enough for Harry to be able to get him switched off and he came home strongly.

"I didn't go to three miles in the spring and this makes me glad I didn't. We may have left a big race behind not going three miles at Aintree, but I just knew if I did that when he was just a five-year-old I might have been asking too much. I trusted him today and he delivered."

He went on: "He was under the radar, but that's because he wasn't quite behaving well enough to win one of those last year.

"I put that down to the fact he was very raw at four and five. We only went hurdling last year because I was so annoyed with him for pulling so hard in a bumper at Newton Abbot that I sent him hurdling to try to slow him down with a few jumps in front of him.

"He has just improved and improved and improved and he has a brilliant engine. He had some good rivals around him at the last and I was very impressed he could come out on top as I thought it was a very competitive renewal.

"I have a lot of respect for the horses he has beaten there and now he will have to try to back it up. The one thing about this horse though is he has a fantastic attitude and we'll go now to the Long Walk."

Race regular Strong Leader could renew rivalry at Ascot, although Olly Murphy will also consider a trip to Ireland.

Murphy said: "He ran well but just bumped into a younger, better horse on the day. We have no excuse whatsoever and although people love to knock this horse and he's not always great at Cheltenham, nine times out of 10 he turns up and runs a great race.

"He always jumps the last looking like the winner, but today he has bumped into a better one and we'll dust ourselves down and go again.

"Whether we go Ascot at Christmas or Ireland, I'm not sure, but there is still life in him yet. He loves a left-handed flat track and yes he ran very well in a Long Walk last year, but Ireland is certainly something we'll have a good look at.

"We'll see how he comes out of today and he's run a cracker and I'm really proud of him. He deserves to win a big one again, but it's life and sometimes you bump into a nice young horse who is just too good on the day."

Taurus Bay followed in the hoofprints of illustrious stablemate The Jukebox Man by winning the Coral John Francome Novices' Chase.

It is two years since Harry Redknapp's subsequent King George hero made a winning debut over fences in this Grade Two contest and in his younger stablemate the popular owner, trainer Ben Pauling and jockey Ben Jones appear to have unearthed another potential star.

Taurus Bay lined up having already won over the larger obstacles in the Rising Stars Novices' Chase at Aintree three weeks ago, a victory which meant the 11-8 favourite had to concede 3lb to each of his four rivals in his bid for back-to-back Group Two wins.

Harry Skelton ensured the race was a stern test of stamina and jumping aboard the front-running Royal Infantry, but Jones steadily nursed Taurus Bay into contention and he toughed it out on the run-in to prevail by half a length.

Pauling said: "It's lovely to work with horses who try as hard as that when they need to. He didn't have the run of the race and was in a tight pocket and Ben was patient when he needed to be today, which was great.

"We all know the Skelton team are going to be very hard to pass and are never here to make up the numbers, but I was always confident jumping two out and after the last he was going to tough it out.

"Ben would have liked him to have travelled a little bit better early doors, but that might have been because the ground was a little lively for him. I would love to get some soft ground for this horse soon because that's when we'll see him at his best.

"We definitely won't be going up in trip and I would come back in trip before I go up. I even think two miles on soft ground could be where he's at his best.

"There were no punches held there and the very good two-milers do stay really well as well. As for the future I would love to keep him at two or two and a half and he could be an Arkle horse - it's an option.

"The obvious place would be (to target) the Manifesto and then you could Wayward Lad (Kempton), Scilly Isles (Sandown) and then Aintree. But it's just lovely to see a horse win two Grade Twos in his first two outings over fences and in doing so he has hardly made a mistake."

Koala Rose looked every inch a filly bound for the big spring festivals judged on an impressive second success over obstacles.

A 77-rated performer on the Flat, having been placed on each of her five starts for Ralph Beckett without quite managing to get her head in front, the daughter of Australia won by 62 lengths on her jumping bow at Hereford, earning her a step up to Listed level in the Coral "Daily Rewards Shaker" Fillies' Juvenile Hurdle.

Supporters of Dan Skelton's exciting recruit will have had few concerns, with the 8-13 favourite jumping nimbly in front throughout in the hands of Harry Skelton before powering 16 lengths clear of another previous winner in Echo Valley on the run-in.

Skelton said: "I thought she was very good and I was delighted to see her win as well as that.

"I think there was a bit of market negativity around some of the others, so you might have to take the form with a pinch of salt. Hereford taught us only that she loves to jump, which we already knew from home, but she was very nimble around Hereford and today she's answered another question.

"There's harder questions to come, but I was really impressed with her attitude and hopefully she is progressive as well and there's more lurking under there.

"Hereford was quick ground, but there on slightly softer ground you could see she was barely getting her toe off the ground and it does play on my mind a little bit. Maybe we shouldn't ask her difficult questions too soon on soft ground and wait for some nicer conditions that allow her to show us what she can do.

"There's a Listed juvenile fillies' race at Doncaster at the end of January and that could be most appropriate. There's sure to be other races open to her as well, but she's a pretty effective three-year-old filly and not many have the substance and aggression in their jumping like she does and it really helps."
