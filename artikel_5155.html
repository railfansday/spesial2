Disney (DIS) stock is back in focus after the company delivered a quarterly earnings beat and issued an upbeat growth outlook for fiscal 2027, giving investors fresh reason to reassess the stock. Revenue climbed 16%, supported by stronger performances across streaming, theme parks and sports, while management signaled further earnings growth ahead. But with content spending set to rise and shares approaching a key technical resistance level, the market faces a bigger test:

Can Disney’s latest results finally turn its recovery into a sustained rally?

Disney reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $25.95 billion, compared with $22.46 billion a year earlier. Adjusted earnings reached $1.77 per share, exceeding analyst expectations of $1.67.

The improvement extended beyond the top line. Total segment operating income climbed 43% to approximately $5 billion, while income before income taxes increased 71% to $3.5 billion. The results highlight a stronger operating performance across Disney’s businesses, although the additional 53rd week in fiscal 2026 contributed to reported growth. Investors will therefore be watching whether the company can maintain its earnings momentum through the next financial year.

Disney’s streaming operations delivered one of the quarter’s strongest performances. Subscription revenue increased 19% to $6.1 billion, while streaming operating income more than doubled to $824 million from $402 million a year earlier. The Experiences division continued to provide a significant earnings contribution. Revenue reached approximately $10.1 billion, with operating income rising 20% to $2.25 billion. Global guest attendance increased 4%, indicating continued demand across Disney’s parks.

Sports also delivered stronger results. Revenue climbed 16% to $4.6 billion, while operating income increased 53% to $1.4 billion. The combination matters for Disney’s investment case. Streaming profitability is improving, parks remain a major earnings contributor, and sports continues to support the company’s broader entertainment strategy.

Disney’s fiscal 2027 outlook is now central to the stock’s investment narrative. Management expects double-digit adjusted EPS growth and approximately $11 billion in free cash flow. The company also plans to repurchase around $9 billion of its shares and increase its annual dividend by 33% to $2 per share.

However, the growth strategy requires heavier investment. Disney expects cash content spending to rise 9% to approximately $26 billion in fiscal 2027, reflecting continued investment in entertainment programming and sports rights.

Management also anticipates approximately 8% growth in total segment operating income. One change investors will need to assess is Disney’s decision to stop separately reporting streaming operating income. The company intends to place greater emphasis on its broader Entertainment segment, but the move will reduce visibility into the standalone profitability of streaming.

The key question is whether higher content investment can generate enough additional revenue and operating income to justify the spending.

Disney shares have been recovering within an ascending parallel channel since their July lows, according to the daily chart. The formation of higher highs and higher lows indicates improving buying momentum, with the stock gradually approaching the upper end of its previous trading range.

The immediate hurdle is the $116–$120 resistance zone. This area has previously attracted selling pressure, making it an important test for the current recovery. A decisive daily close above $122 could strengthen the bullish setup and signal that buyers are gaining control of the broader trend. It could also open the way for a move toward higher resistance areas.

However, another rejection would leave DIS vulnerable to a pullback toward the lower boundary of its rising channel. The moving averages currently provide support beneath the price, but a sustained move above resistance is needed to confirm a stronger breakout.

For DIS stock, the next test is whether the improved fundamentals can help shares clear the $120 resistance zone. A sustained breakout could strengthen the recovery narrative; another rejection would leave investors waiting for a clearer catalyst.
