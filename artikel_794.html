Selena Gomez’s Wondermind Fraud Lawsuit Has Been Dismissed With Prejudice

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Investors dropped their suit against the singer, her mother and a former business partner over the mental health startup that fell apart within three years.

The investor fraud lawsuit against Selena Gomez, her mother Mandy Teefey and their former business partner Daniella Pierson over their mental health startup Wondermind has been dismissed with prejudice, according to a court filing submitted Tuesday.

On Monday, the plaintiffs voluntarily dismissed the suit against the three women and their company with prejudice, according to court records viewed by The Hollywood Reporter, meaning the claims against the women and how their company fell apart from this set of investors can not be refiled. “Selena is pleased by the dismissal of the case and looks forward to putting the matter behind her,” Gomez’s attorney, Mathew S. Rosengart, said in a statement to THR on Tuesday.

Two investment entities that backed Wondermind filed the suit in Delaware federal court in August. They alleged that the three women “falsely represented” that the company had the infrastructure, leadership and resources it needed, and that the roughly $1.2 million they put into it was squandered while they were kept in the dark. The complaint accused Gomez, Teefey and Pierson of securities fraud, common-law fraud and breach of contract, and asked for the investment contract to be rescinded and for damages.

According to the complaint, Gomez was pitched as central to marketing the brand, and Pierson was presented to investors as a “$200 million executive” with a “full slate of revenue-generating initiatives” already in motion.

“The partnerships did not exist. The initiatives never materialized. The app was never built,” the complaint stated. “And for three years, while the company quietly collapsed around them, not one of its founders, officers or directors said a word to the investors whose money was funding the collapse.”

About two weeks after the suit was filed, Rosengart moved to dismiss the claims against Gomez. He argued that the plaintiffs relied on “vague, generalized, and contradictory allegations” about his client, and that she could not have committed fraud.

“Although she invested in and holds a minority stake in the company, Ms. Gomez never agreed to and did not manage the company,” the motion stated. Gomez had “agreed to support her mother, Mandy Teefey, who co-founded Wondermind,” it said. “Management was vested in Ms. Gomez’s mother, Mandy Teefey, and Ms. Pierson, who ran the company as co-CEOs and board members. The company, in turn, retained Ms. Gomez as a consultant and conferred on her the title of Chief Impact Officer.”

The three women founded Wondermind, which focuses on mental fitness practices and tools, in November 2021. According to the suit, they told investors an app would be the centerpiece of the brand, supported by major advertising deals and high-profile celebrity photo shoots and cover stories.

Very little of that came to pass, and Wondermind staffers eventually began talking to the media. The investors said a story in The Cut was how they learned the company had fallen far short of what they’d been promised.

Teefey has denied accusations of company mismanagement and erratic behavior. Pierson, who left the company in 2023, “categorically denies” all of the allegations against her in the suit.

“To be clear, she has never used investor funds for personal expenses. Quite the opposite: Daniella invested her own money into the business and did not draw a salary from the company,” her rep said at the time of the filing.
