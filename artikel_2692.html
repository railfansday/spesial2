Tapia's Brace Puts Messi's Inter Miami on Brink of Elimination

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Gonzalo Tapia scored twice as the seventh-seeded Columbus Crew stunned defending MLS Cup champion Inter Miami 3-0 at Nu Stadium on Saturday, taking a 1-0 lead in their best-of-three first-round series. Taha Habroune added the third goal, and Lionel Messi was held without a goal or assist for the night.

Miami must now win Game 2 in Columbus on Friday to force a decisive Game 3 back in Florida on Dec. 2. A second loss ends the title defense in the opening round.

The result turned on moments Miami created for itself. Goalkeeper Dayne St. Clair came off his line and brought down Tapia in the box in the eighth minute, and the Chilean forward converted the penalty himself. Twenty-eight minutes later, defender Gonzalo Luján picked up a second yellow card for a dangerous tackle, leaving Miami to play the final 54 minutes with ten men.

Columbus used the extra space to control the second half. Habroune doubled the lead in the 52nd minute with what was described as a team move, and Tapia added his second six minutes later after turning Yannick Bright and running in alone on goal. Patrick Schulte made five saves at the other end, denying Messi on a point-blank effort and a free kick in the first half.

This was not a one-off. Columbus sat 15th in the Eastern Conference on Sept. 1 but entered the playoffs having won six straight regular-season matches, the longest active streak in the league. Interim coach Laurent Courtois even pulled Tapia with more than half an hour left to manage his fitness and avoid a second yellow card that would have ruled him out of Game 2 — a sign the Crew felt the result was already secure.

Tapia himself is part of a broader scouting pipeline between MLS and Brazil's top flight. The Chilean forward joined Columbus in August on loan from São Paulo, where he had spent the previous year in Série A after arriving from River Plate. His scoring burst in Miami adds to a growing trend of MLS clubs pulling frontline talent directly out of Brazilian football rather than waiting for players to wash out of South America first.

Miami also lost Luján's availability for Friday's game, and Messi picked up a yellow card late in the first half for throwing a ball into Tapia's face during a throw-in, an incident replays showed clearly but the referee did not act on beyond the booking. None of that changes the math: Inter Miami, the No. 2 seed and reigning champion, now needs a road win in Columbus simply to survive. A repeat of Saturday's pattern — an early set-piece concession, a card, and a Tapia finish — would end their season in the first round for the second time in three years.
