Cowboys co-owner says fans 'deserve better' and play is 'unacceptable'

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Dallas Cowboys fans have gotten their point across to the team's top executives. That doesn't mean the executives have to listen.

Cowboys co-owner, executive vice president and CEO Stephen Jones responded to disgruntled fans on local sports radio station 105.3 The FAN on Monday, Nov. 16. After Dallas' sixth loss of the season in Week 10, Jones was asked if he understands why Cowboys fans are calling for head coach Brian Schottenheimer and defensive coordinator Christian Parker to be fired.

"I certainly understand their frustration because there's no doubt that we're frustrated. We feel like the way we're playing ball right now is unacceptable. Our fans certainly deserve better," Jones said.

COWBOYS: Jerry Jones calls team's historic defensive struggle vs. 49ers 'glaring'

The biggest culprit for the Cowboys' 4-6 start has been their defense. Dallas has allowed 28.2 points per game, which is third most in the NFL, and 393.8 yards per game, which is the most in the NFL. Only the 2-7 Eagles and 3-7 Colts have fewer takeaways than the Cowboys' eight so far this year.

Discipline has been an issue as well. Through 10 games, the Cowboys have had penalties against them accepted 79 times – fifth-most in the NFL, per NFLPenalties.com. Their 702 lost yards from penalties are second-most in the league.

Despite those issues and their losing record, the Cowboys are second place in the NFC East and two games behind the New York Giants.

In Week 10, Dallas had a chance to tighten the gap three days after the Giants' third loss of the season on "Thursday Night Football," but the team squandered it by losing a 46-40 shoot-out to the 49ers on Nov. 15. The 584 yards the Cowboys allowed in the game were the most the team has allowed in a home game in franchise history.

For now, Jones – and his father, who is team president, owner and general manager Jerry Jones – say Schottenheimer and Parker are safe. Stephen Jones said he believes in the current staff and roster enough to get back on track this season.

"I feel very resigned to trying to figure this out. And I don't think our season is over," Stephen Jones said. "I think we're making it very difficult on ourselves, and we're digging a big hole that we're going to have to come out of.

"But I do think we have the players. I think we got the pieces in place if we could get the chemistry right and execute."
