The Cheap-Lane Green Light: Equal AI Scores at 1/45th the Price

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

For two years, the honest advice about cheap AI models has been cautious: audit your tiers, pilot the budget lane, see what sticks. This month, the evidence changed shape. In OpenAI's own launch data for GPT-6.2, the cheap model in the lineup — Sol — scored 73.0% on Agents' Last Exam v1.1 at $1.32 per task. Claude Fable 5.2, running the same benchmark at max effort, scored 72.8% at $60.05 per task. The cheaper model didn't just keep up. It scored a hair higher at roughly one forty-fifth of the price.

One benchmark tie can be a fluke. Two is a pattern.

On Artificial Analysis's AA-3D, human judges look at two anonymous interactive 3D experiences built from the same prompt and vote for the one they prefer. GPT-6.2 Sol lands at 1,259 Elo — statistically tied with Claude Fable 5.2 at 1,271, their confidence intervals overlapping. Blind judges could not reliably tell the cheap model's work from the expensive model's work.

Read those two results together and the volume-lane switch stops being a gamble. If a model at $1.32 per task matches a model at $60 per task on multistep professional work, then the constraint on your AI budget is no longer model capability. It's knowing which of your tasks are volume work.

Volume work is the output nobody would frame and hang on a wall: ticket triage, tagging, summarizing, first drafts, data cleanup, routing. Jobs where the per-task cost multiplies across hundreds of runs a month, and where "good enough, checked by a human" is the quality bar.

Run the math on your own volume. Five hundred tasks a week at $60.05 per task is $30,025. The same 500 at $1.32 is $660. No small business survives that gap by being careful — it closes it by moving the right work to the right lane.

Benchmark scores are evidence about the model. They are not evidence about your work, because your tasks differ from benchmarks in every direction that matters — messier inputs, house style, domain vocabulary, edge cases the benchmark never tests.

Equal quality on your volume work means you switch. Anything you can't absorb means the premium lane keeps that job — and that's a result too, because now the premium spend is aimed at work that needs it.

Per-task price isn't the only number that diverges. In OpenAI's GDPval v2 runs, Claude Fable 5.2 produced 222,344 output tokens where GPT-6.2 Sol produced 33,428 on the same work — nearly seven times the output volume. When a model writes six times as much to say the same thing, bills diverge well beyond the per-token price difference.

This is why the 3-run test scores cleanup time, not just accuracy. A verbose model that buries the answer in preamble costs you twice: once in tokens, once in the human attention needed to excavate the usable part.

The expensive lane still wins judgment work. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, Fable 5.2 leads at 64.6 versus 62.1 for GPT-6.2 Astra — at 6.6 times the cost per task. On Cognition's FrontierCode 2.0, Fable leads Sol 49.1% to 43.8%. Cheap lanes are for volume, not judgment. Deep expert review, high-stakes analysis, and novel problems are exactly where the premium models still spend your money well.

Effort settings can quietly erase the win. Sol's parity scores exist at high and max effort. At low effort, its LifeVal score drops to 25.3% where the previous generation hit 66.8%. If a low-effort default carries over from an old config, you'll blame the model for a decision you never made. Set the dial deliberately, and run your 3-run test at the setting you'll actually ship.

Every launch number is a chosen cut. OpenAI's own footnote says Fable 5.2's Agents' Last Exam result excludes nine tasks where a fallback model answered. That doesn't make the comparison false — it makes it curated, in both directions. Vendor benchmarks are inputs to your test, never verdicts.

The era of treating the cheap lane as a compromise is ending, benchmark by benchmark. The discipline that replaces it is simple: know which of your tasks are volume work, test the cheap lane on three of them this week, and let the results — not the launch hype — decide where each task runs.
