Lloyd Geering, Pastor Who Disputed Christ’s Resurrection, Dies at 108

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

“It is faith that man needs, not past doctrines,” he said in defense of his views, which became a cause célèbre for many but were condemned by others.

Lloyd Geering, an iconoclastic pastor and Presbyterian theologian once accused of heresy for disputing the physical resurrection of Jesus Christ, the divine inspiration of the Bible and other pillars of Christian faith dating back centuries, died on Thursday at a retirement village in Lower Hutt, a city on the North Island of New Zealand. He was 108.

Mr. Geering was New Zealand’s oldest man.

The death was confirmed by his daughter, Elizabeth Geering.

Within the country, his break with time-honored tradition in the 1960s became a cause célèbre, lauded by supporters who regarded it as an emblem of modernism in a world of anachronistic dogma. But others across the mainstream denominations condemned his views — questioning the very nature of God and rejecting the notion of an immortal soul — as a denial of the fundamental tenets and mysteries of the Christian faith.

The charges before the General Assembly of the Presbyterian Church of New Zealand in 1967 included the assertion that Mr. Geering had disturbed the “peace and unity” of the church. Coupled with the claim that his disavowal of scriptural teaching constituted “doctrinal error,” the allegations of theological wrongdoing were widely interpreted as a rare charge of heresy. He was the first New Zealander to face such accusations.

At the time, Mr. Geering was the principal of the Theological Hall, the center of Presbyterian religious education. The case against him, led by a prominent lay Presbyterian, Robert Wardlaw, and a pastor, Robert Blaikie, unfolded in a blaze of publicity. The religious hearing, in November 1967, drew an audience of 1,000 to St. Paul’s Church in Christchurch, where the proceedings were bathed in the glare of television crews’ lighting, recorded in detail by local newspapers and chronicled internationally in the pages of The New York Times, among other publications.

“It is faith that man needs, not past doctrines,” Mr. Geering said in his 90-minute defense. He enjoined New Zealanders to abandon past securities and “launch out into this world with the same insecure faith as marked the patriarchs and prophets, the apostles and reformers.” He said he had not disturbed the peace of the church, only its somnolence.
