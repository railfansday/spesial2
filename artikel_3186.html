BioNTech (BNTX) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Oncology pivot and a CEO transition shape a quarter with a revised revenue outlook.

BioNTech SE (BNTX -0.70%) reported a strategic shift toward a tumor-centric clinical development approach, prioritizing its lung cancer franchise and bispecific antibody candidates. Management reported a revised full-year revenue midpoint and a stable financial position with EUR 16.2 billion in cash assets. The company stated that it is transitioning several oncology programs into pivotal stages while optimizing its operational efficiency through divestitures and restructured partnerships. Management confirmed that it remains on track to become a diversified multi-product company by 2030, with multiple registrations planned for its late-stage pipeline candidates.

In parallel to these registrational trials, we are generating novel combination data with our ADCs and our mRNA immunotherapies to inform the first wave of chemotherapy-free combination trials with registrational intent. Gotistobart is another key candidate of our non-small cell lung cancer strategy.

As a reminder, gotistobart is a selective Treg-depleting antibody targeting CTLA-4, developed with OncoC4. We are advancing gotistobart through the pivotal stage of a global phase III trial in patients with metastatic squamous non-small cell lung cancer who progressed following platinum-based chemotherapy and checkpoint inhibitor treatment. This is a setting with very few effective options and poor prognosis, where median survival with current standard of care is less than one year. At the World Conference on Lung Cancer, we presented updated overall survival data from the non-pivotal initial part of this trial, with a median follow-up of 25.4 months. Median overall survival was 18.5 months with gotistobart versus 10 months with docetaxel, with a hazard ratio of 0.56.

Gotistobart reduced the risk of death by 44% compared with chemotherapy. The observed safety profile of gotistobart was consistent with previous findings. These results reinforce the potential of gotistobart to provide a significant overall survival benefit as a chemotherapy-free monotherapy in this large, underserved patient population. In the pivotal part of the trial designed with identical protocol inclusion and exclusion criteria to stage I, we anticipate an interim analysis before year-end. This serves as an early data point ahead of the next pre-planned interim analysis next year. Turning to small cell lung cancer, where we are evaluating pumitamig in combination with chemotherapy, in combination with a DLL3 T-cell engager, and in combination with Alpha-D in front and later lines.

Alpha-D is our B7-H3-targeted ADC developed with DualityBio. Alpha-D's clinical profile positions it as a potentially versatile oncology asset across a wide range of solid tumors. More than 1,000 patients have now been treated with Alpha-D across over 10 tumor types, including more than 500 patients treated with Alpha-D in combination with pumitamig. This was a global dataset from our phase I/II trial of pumitamig plus Alpha-D in patients with advanced small cell and non-small cell lung cancer. This combination brings together three independent mechanisms of action: checkpoint inhibition, VEGF-A neutralization, and the tumor cell-targeted cytotoxic payload of Alpha-D.

In 71 patients with small cell lung cancer across 4 dose levels, the combination showed a remarkable 92% overall response rate in first line, 76% objective response rate in second line, and 52% objective response rate in third line and later. Importantly, we saw activity in patients previously treated with immunotherapy, with a 60% objective response rate, and in the small group of patients previously treated with a DLL3-targeted T-cell engager, with an encouraging 88% response rate. The safety profile was manageable and consistent with data from each agent individually, with limited overlap between pumitamig and Alpha-D-associated toxicities. No dose-limiting toxicities were observed, and the ILD rate for the combination was 2.9%.

These combination data support our broader strategy of evaluating pumitamig as a potential backbone for combination regimens of complementary mechanisms. They will help to inform the clinical development strategy to address today's unmet need and tomorrow's evolving small cell lung cancer standard of care. These data we have seen in this trial will help guide our novel combination strategy in lung cancer into potential registration, with first trials expected to begin in 2027. In triple-negative breast cancer, our pumitamig plus ADC combination strategy is now entering the pivotal stage.

At ESMO Breast in May, pumitamig in combination with a TROP2-targeted ADC showed a confirmed objective response rate of 76.7% and a disease control rate of 96.7% as first-line treatment in 30 patients with metastatic triple-negative breast cancer. These data confirm that pumitamig combined with a TROP2 ADC has a manageable safety profile and warrant further investigation as first-line treatment. When we initiated ROSETTA Breast-01, chemotherapy was the only option for patients with PD-L1-negative advanced TNBC. However, in 2026, the standard of care for first-line TNBC changed following updated NCCN and ESMO guidelines, as well as the FDA approval of TROP2 ADCs. TROP2-targeted ADCs, such as sacituzumab govitecan, are quickly becoming the new standard of care.

As a result of the evolving treatment landscape, we are adapting ROSETTA Breast-01 to replace chemotherapy with sacituzumab govitecan. We have entered into a clinical collaboration with Gilead. Pending the green light from regulators, this would be the first global phase III trial of our PD-L1/VEGF bispecific, pumitamig, in combination with an antibody-drug conjugate. Adapting ROSETTA Breast-01 to reflect the evolving standard of care gives the trial the best chance to inform clinical practice and ultimately treatment decisions for patients. Moving to mRNA cancer immunotherapies. As announced in August, the decision was made in alignment with Genentech to discontinue the phase II trial of autogene cevumeran as adjuvant monotherapy in ctDNA-positive stage II high-risk or stage III colorectal cancer.

This decision followed a review from the independent data safety monitoring board in which they identified a numerical imbalance in overall survival between the treatment arms in this specific patient population and determined that continuing the trial was unlikely to change the efficacy outcome. No new safety signals were identified. The phase II IMcode003 trial in adjuvant pancreatic cancer, where autogene cevumeran is being evaluated in combination with atezolizumab and modified FOLFIRINOX, has completed enrollment and continues as planned. BNT113, our off-the-shelf mRNA immunotherapy, is being evaluated in a pivotal phase II/III trial in combination with pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab alone as first-line treatment for patients with metastatic HPV16-positive PD-L1-expressing head and neck cancer.

In October, we announced top-line results from the first pre-specified interim analysis of AHEAD-MERIT. While the trial did not meet its first dual primary endpoint of progression-free survival, it is continuing as planned to assess the second dual primary endpoint of overall survival. The independent data monitoring committee recommended that the trial continue in accordance with the trial protocol. As immune-mediated anti-tumor effects build and deepen over time, the analysis of the overall survival endpoint will be critical to fully assess the potential of this approach to deliver durable benefit for patients with HPV-driven head and neck cancer.

Under the trial's statistical design, which pre-specifies dual primary endpoints of PFS and OS, a statistically significant improvement in OS would establish a positive trial outcome, irrespective of the PFS result. BioNTech is leveraging novel combinations with next-generation IO, targeted therapies, and mRNA cancer immunotherapy to maximize pipeline potential and elevate outcomes for patients with solid tumors.
