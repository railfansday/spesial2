Jimmie Johnson's Legacy Motor Club is valued at $450 million after new fundraise

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Knighthead Capital Management led the $85 million round, which will fund growth and a new headquarters expected to open in 2028

Legacy Motor Club, the NASCAR team that Jimmie Johnson, a seven-time Cup Series champion, owns, wrapped up an $85 million fundraising round headed by Knighthead Capital Management, according to Bloomberg.

The round puts Legacy's worth at $450 million, according to Bloomberg, which attributed the figure to a source with knowledge of the deal who lacked permission to discuss it in public.

Legacy will put the money toward growing its operation and building a new headquarters in Mooresville, North Carolina, according to Bloomberg. The headquarters is expected to open in 2028.

In July, Legacy revealed an earlier capital raise backed by investors such as Philadelphia Phillies player Bryce Harper, tennis champion Andy Roddick, musician Darius Rucker and chef Guy Fieri, according to Bloomberg. Johnson told Bloomberg that Legacy returned to investors for additional capital so it could keep expanding.

Johnson said the investment positions Legacy to reach its goal of being "a tier one racing operation in the Nascar series," according to Bloomberg.

Legacy operates three NASCAR teams with charters, and each of them runs the Toyota $TM Camry XSE. Josh Berry, Erik Jones and Riley Herbst will drive for the team in the coming season, according to Bloomberg.

Knighthead's leader is co-founder Tom Wagner, who launched the investment company back in 2008. Wagner is also a part owner of both the Las Vegas Raiders and Birmingham City Football Club, according to Bloomberg.

Bloomberg noted that Knighthead bought into Legacy in January 2025, taking a minority stake by purchasing a large portion of the shares airline entrepreneur Maury Gallagher held. That transaction made Johnson the team's majority owner.

Kyle Kneisly, a Knighthead partner and Legacy board member, told Bloomberg that deploying growth capital was part of the plan from the start.

The new round follows NASCAR's settlement of a federal antitrust lawsuit with Michael Jordan's 23XI Racing and Front Row Motorsports. The settlement made charters permanent for NASCAR teams, a change that could raise the worth of clubs, according to Bloomberg.
