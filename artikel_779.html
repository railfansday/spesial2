Vans Warped Tour Orlando: Bands Support Each Other Over Two Days of Camaraderie and Rock Music After Flights Were Grounded

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The event featured nearly 150 bands, including Simple Plan, Sublime, Third Eye Blind, The Used, Hoobastank, 311 and more.

The weekend’s delayed flights were certainly on everyone’s minds at the Vans Warped Tour in Orlando this weekend, as nearly 150 bands played to a crowd of nearly 50,000 wildly enthusiastic fans on each of its two days outside Camping World Stadium.

The festival kicked off over the weekend after the Federal Aviation Administration’s decision to ground flights on Friday and the aftermath of missed and delayed flights once the FAA allowed air travel to resume. On Saturday morning, Warped Tour founder Kevin Lyman said in an Instagram video that “a few bands” had to cancel due to the shutdown but “not as many as we thought. … Some bands have jumped in vans and [are] driving from as far away as Chicago and Houston.” The schedule for each day was adjusted, he said, to accommodate those delays and allow the bands more time to get to Orlando. From a concertgoer’s perspective, the entire operation ran smoothly and on time despite many of the musicians having to overcome major travel hurdles to get there. Third Eye Blind frontman Stephan Jenkins told the crowd on Saturday night that he had left the previous morning at 9 a.m., spent the night in the airport and didn’t arrive in Orlando until just a few hours before his band hit the stage. “I didn’t know how it was gonna turn out; I didn’t know if I was gonna make it at all,” he said.

“I’m so glad I got my ass here today,” Hot Chelle Rae’s Nash Overstreet told the crowd on Sunday afternoon. “I wish the rest of the band could have been here for you guys, but I could not let this go without showing up.” Also on Sunday, Set Your Goals, fronted by Jordan Brown and Matt Wilson, revealed that two of their band members were stuck at various airports across the U.S., and they had a substitute drummer and rhythm guitarist “step up to make this Set Your Goals show happen today. We have a bounty of [musicians] stepping in.”

Similarly, Reel Big Fish lead singer Aaron Barrett, who said he’d arrived only a few hours before his band took the stage, shared that fellow Warped Tour band Millington had agreed to help out his group if they needed anyone to step in. In fact, the two bands did take the stage together at the end of Reel Big Fish’s set for a high-energy ska take on A-ha’s “Take on Me,” which had the crowd going wild.

There was a feeling of camaraderie across the two days, with the artists encouraging those in the crowd to make new friends and also support one another. On Saturday night, 311 lead singer Nick Hexum asked the crowd if they were “feeling the unity,” which received deafening applause. Third Eye Blind’s Jenkins told the concertgoers to “tap someone you don’t know on the shoulder and say hi,” and later, before the band launched into “Semi-Charmed Life,” he told festivalgoers to “put your arm around somebody.”

Sublime lead singer Jakob Nowell encouraged the Warped attendees to “try to make some friends out there. It’s about the memories you make.” In a poignant moment, he spoke about keeping traditions alive. “If my dad were up here, he’d say the same thing. This is for him. I love you, Dad,” he said of his father — Bradley Nowell, Sublime’s original lead singer and guitarist, who died in 1996 — before the band launched into “Badfish.”

Pierre Bouvier, lead singer of Saturday night’s closer Simple Plan, noted that he’d wished he’d been able to see Third Eye Blind’s performance, “but I could hear them, and they were great” before giving a shout-out to Sublime. Lolo, who’d performed the day prior for passengers on a Southwest Airlines flight, along with Simple Plan, The Used and Hoobastank, hit the stage with Simple Plan to perform “Jet Lag.” On the main stage, Hawthorne Heights’ JT Woodruff gave thanks to New Found Glory, saying his band wouldn’t exist without the latter, which the next day played on that very same stage.

Throughout the weekend, various bands and solo artists also paid tribute to others by performing covers. Simple Plan did a rousing medley that included Smash Mouth’s “All Star,” Avril Lavigne’s “Sk8er Boi” and The Killers’ “Mr. Brightside.” Hot Chelle Rae performed a mashup of Spin Doctors’ “Two Princes,” LFO’s “Summer Girls” and Third Eye Blind’s “Semi-Charmed Life.” Third Eye Blind, meanwhile, included David Bowie’s “Heroes” in their set, while Bowling for Soup sang Wheatus’ “Teenage Dirtbag.” On another stage, Tillie sang “Lucky,” which she called Britney Spears’ “most emo song.”

In a bit of a switcheroo, Simple Plan’s Bouvier took the drums from Chuck Comeau during “I’m Just a Kid,” which saw Comeau go crowd-surfing, while Bowling for Soup’s Jaret Reddick came out and finished the vocals for the song. (Earlier in the day, Bowling for Soup also hit the main stage, including their theme song to Phineas and Ferb on their set list. Continuing the TV theme song trend, Simple Plan invited fans dressed in Scooby-Doo costumes onstage while they performed their theme song to What’s New, Scooby-Doo? — a regular feature of their tours that allows the fans a special experience onstage.)

Other bands on the two-day bill included Phantom Planet, who ended their set with “California” to a wildly appreciative crowd; Jimmy Eat World, who got the crowd pumped up with “The Middle”; Gym Class Heroes, who performed “Stereo Hearts” sans Adam Levine; Save Ferris, who did two sets, including on the “Warped Unplugged” stage; and plenty of others, from Taking Back Sunday to The Summer Set to Thrice to Insane Clown Posse to Sleeping With Sirens.

As you might expect, festgoers were mostly decked out in black, Vans’ signature checkerboard pattern or a combination of both, with colorful hair, impressive mohawks and lots of tattoos and piercings, as befitting a rock concert. But there were some who donned costumes ranging from the aforementioned Scooby-Doo to a Ratatouille to bananas and hot dogs to one man who was dressed like Jesus Christ from head to toe, including a crown of “thorns.” On that theme, another man had a sign that read “Free Hugs & Prayers” and was seen speaking intimately with a woman who appeared to be in need of the latter and embracing another woman the next day.

There was plenty of crowd-surfing inside the VIP area, with one of those banana-suited attendees spotted on the big screens. At one point, a woman in a wheelchair was being treated to the crowd-surfing experience. This, despite the event’s rather cleverly worded signs warning against the activity: “No moshing or crowd surfing. You mosh or crowd surf. You get hurt. Your mom sues. No more Warped.”

A half-pipe kept folks entertained with skateboarders doing stunts, while BMX athletes also did tricks inside the festival. Several bands engaged in meet-and-greets during the weekend, which also featured an assortment of food from giant turkey legs to smash burgers to funnel cakes to poké bowls.

The weekend ended with a literal bang, as Sunday’s closer, The Used, performed a cover of Nirvana’s “Smells Like Teen Spirit” while fireworks exploded in the night sky.
