‘9-1-1: Nashville’ Boss Teases What’s Next After That Shocking Season 2 Pharaoh Reveal

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The first six episodes of 9-1-1: Nashville's second season have seen the first responders and other characters deal with a mysterious killer called the Pharoah (A.J. Buckley), who's been terrorizing Nashville with murders inspired by the Biblical plagues. The mid-season finale is an explosive episode that sees the Pharoah escalate things with his latest scheme after he catches Rogue (Ryan Phillippe) trying to lure him into a trap. As of the end of the mid-season finale, the first responders were able to save the group of children that the Pharoah just targeted, but Don (Chris O'Donnell) and Rogue are currently caught in his latest trap.

Following the mid-season finale for Season 2, 9-1-1: Nashville's showrunner, Rashad Raisani, sat down with Collider to talk about the episode and break down the first half of the season. Raisani discusses that shocking cliffhanger, 9-1-1: Nashville's expansion into the crime thriller genre this season, and the compelling character dynamics that have been at the center of this season. He also teases some storylines from the back half of Season 2, including the upcoming crossover with 9-1-1.

So, the Pharaoh has finally been revealed in this great moment where Don has stopped to help this stranger, right after viewers have found out what the Pharaoh looks like. We see Don weigh this decision for a moment, because he has ice and a cake in his car, and he's already cutting it close to the baby shower. Why do you think he ultimately chose to stop?

RASHAD RAISANI: I think Don stopped because that's who he is: if he sees somebody stranded on the side of the road with a smoking car, he's always going to help. And Pharaoh knows that 100%. That would be Don's greatest weakness: there's no way he's going to pass by somebody who needs help and not help them.

That cliffhanger was already very intense with the Pharaoh taking Don, only for it to then be revealed that he has Rogue as well. How early on in the season did you know you wanted to end the mid-season finale with both of them in danger, and why is it specifically these two characters?

RAISANI: I think we knew pretty early that we wanted there to be a big price that they had to pay. Pharoah warns them in the second episode, “Don't interfere, or else there will be consequences,” so I think we knew early that we were going to do something big with them. And I think it always had to be Don and Rogue because – along with Cammie (Kimberly Williams-Paisley), who's the dispatcher, who does the most to try and stop Pharaoh — Don's fire team has saved many of his victims, and Rogue is in charge of trying to catch this guy. So, it made a lot of sense for him to take two of the three most important people who were both firstborns as his way of exacting revenge and proving his point that there would be consequences.

There was an interesting clash this episode between Rogue and Nick, when they fight over Rogue’s unconventional approach vs. Nick’s by-the-book FBI approach, and Nick makes a comment about Rogue's past of rule-breaking in New York. We already know about Rogue’s wife's death and the Strangler case, but there's also been some mystery about why he moved to Nashville in the first place. How much can you tease about Rogue's past in New York and this reputation of his that Nick’s referring to?

RAISANI: Well, I think I can say it was a reputation well-earned, that Rogue did break some things, and that he bent the rules for some people, maybe a little too much. And so I think that will be definitely something that comes out and continues to get revisited, and we'll see that tendency of Rogue’s come out even more and lead to some issues in Nashville as well. So it's definitely in his character. Nick is right onto it when he says that, and it will lead to more clashes, frankly, between them. So, yeah, it's going to be a part of things.

Alongside the Pharaoh, there's this really compelling villain this season in Marcia Zellner (Camryn Manheim), who continues to make the disturbing prank calls to Cammie this episode while showing no remorse and still being her friend at work. How did you decide to have this additional villain in Zellner, who's completely separate from the Pharaoh, and is there anything you can hint about her motivation in targeting Cammie specifically?

RAISANI: Marcia is a direct inspiration from Ryan Murphy. It was one of the big things he wanted to do this season: he wanted to have a mysterious criminal; he wanted to have Cammie be tormented by somebody like Camryn Manheim, who ended up being Camryn Manheim, who, at a certain pivotal moment, would be prank-calling Cammie, and then take off a wig and reveal scars, and you [realize], “Oh my god, she has a big problem.” So, we knew early we wanted to do that, and we wanted to explore these two things, and we wanted these two balls in the air about Pharaoh and Marcia, and we're not necessarily saying that they aren't related.

Like, we want the audience to just wonder. It's possible, on the one hand, that bad things can be happening at the same time that they're unrelated; it's also possible that they're connected. It's also possible that Marcia might be using the stress of Pharaoh too, for her own motivation, to torment Cammie even more. So, I think that was part of what made us feel like it'd be fun to have another crazy thing from left field, to suddenly have multiple fronts of badness. And then Marcia is coming from a very specific place, a very specific incident that corroded her, and that led her to become the person she is now, and that has made her so determined to torment and torture Cammie.

Ryan (Michael Provost) showed a particular interest in the Pharaoh investigation, and we saw him do some detective work with Rogue this season. Was it just about this particular case for him, or does he have more of a real interest in this, and could he be more involved in this alongside Rogue once he goes back to work?

RAISANI: I think yes. I think, first, he was just taken by this guy who, Ryan – Michael Provost’s character – is very rule-following; he’s grown up as a rule follower, he was always trying to please Dad, and he's done a good job of that, but then he watches this guy from New York come in and just break all the rules and be a maverick, and there's something very alluring to Ryan, because he's so different than anything Ryan's ever seen, and so Ryan is really enchanted by him.

And I think it really starts from a character level that he was so fascinated by Rogue, and then he starts to get into the investigation, because he's so excited to be around Rogue. So I think that there is a definite chance that he'll continue to pursue law enforcement, and to continue working with Rogue. But Ryan has his own drama to get through; his wife has cancer, she's pregnant, he’s going to take time off from the firehouse… He's got to juggle that also with the things that he's interested in doing at work.

So, Ryan's going to be taking a whole month off work for Sam (MacKenzie Porter); how much of their journey are we going to see in the back half of the season while Ryan's away from the 113?

RAISANI: There's going to be some episodes where they’ll be offscreen for a bit, because they have to go and travel, as he said, but then we are going to do a really rich story devoted to what her surgery looks like, what the post-surgery looks like, what the caretaking looks like, and explore what that journey is for them, and how it also affects our other characters. So, it's going to be a huge part. And then, tracking her pregnancy and her cancer through the back half of the season is going to be a major driver for us.

Will another firefighter be brought in temporarily to replace Ryan at the 113?

RAISANI: No. But we will play, we will dramatize how the leadership void... him being gone, their Lieutenant's gone — that does change the pecking order a little bit, and it will force our other characters to see who's going to step up and fill that, and that's kind of where we play in those episodes.

Don and Blythe (Jessica Capshaw) had a major breakthrough last episode, and they seem to be on solid ground this episode. Blythe and Dixie (LeAnne Rimes) are still clearly in conflict with each other, but are Don and Blythe completely past the hurdle of Dixie in their marriage?

RAISANI: I don't know about completely… I mean, I think the wound is always there, but they're healing; that's what I would say. They are healing from it. And I think it's always going to be a memory. It's like, they say you can forgive without forgetting, and I think they're now in that stage, that they are getting back to solid ground.

It doesn't mean Dixie's going to disappear, and it doesn't mean Blythe and Dixie don't have their own relationship and their own frenemy status, because they are now hitched together with this record label that Blythe's in charge of, and we're going to play some stories in that world, where we're going to find out that those two women, as much as they can't stand each other all the time, they actually work really well together, and they complement each other. We're going to play the fun and the irony of them being kind of stuck together and thriving together.

What lessons did you learn while showrunning Lone Star that you've taken with you to Nashville?

RAISANI: That’s a great question. What I've learned, and it's still the hard way, is that you never have enough time to do all the things you want. And that you just end up... like, on Lone Star, our last season went from 18 episodes to 16 episodes to 12 episodes, and suddenly, the character arcs got smushed, and smushed, and smushed, so that Marjan (Natacha Karam) would have to meet the parents in the same episode that they introduced her fiancé, [and it's] the same episode where she has to get married, whereas that would normally take three or four episodes. So what I've learned is that you always need to have good drive for the characters, but you don't have to have them do a bunch of different things in tight clumps; the audience will go with them on their journeys.

And then the other thing I've learned is how to do these big action cases and how to interweave them, and that if you don't.... if the case is – and we've been guilty of it before, where we just did a big case for a big case, but the best cases have an emotional connection to what's happening in the characters' lives, and that makes them much richer. And I think that was the biggest lesson of Lone Star, we were at our best when those things were in sync with each other.

Rogue has been such a great addition to the season. The Pharaoh investigation has tied his storylines to that of the 113 thus far in every episode, but have there been challenges in navigating the process of weaving him into personal storylines as well and determining how he'll stay connected to them after this case?

RAISANI: I think so. I mean, I think that the good news is that because Ryan was so wrapped up in the case at the beginning, and because Don, as the season goes along, gets more inevitably put in the center of this, it hasn't been that hard to keep Rogue with our characters. I think the hardest challenge now is that if we're telling a call center story, and we're telling a story about Pharaoh, it just sometimes can be harder to play some of our — what I also love to do — firehouse family scenes. That's been our biggest juggling act, we're still turning the dials on how to balance everything with telling this hard-driven thriller mystery.

Cammie has this crazy boss, Marcia, who's tormenting her in a psychological thriller, but we also don't want to give short shrift to our beloved characters in the firehouse. And I think, to be honest, that's been one of the biggest things I've been wanting us to kind of figure out how to do better, frankly, in the back half of the season: just bringing some of that joy that comes from people like Taylor (Hailey Kilgore), and Blue (Hunter McVey), and Roxie (Juani Feliz), and the firehouse, because I think that's where a lot of the warmth of our show comes from. And so how do you balance them? How do you get Dixie in the show when there's this mysterious killer killing people?

The mid-season finale had me on the edge of my seat the entire time, and Season 2 in general has had really heightened stakes, both on the calls and outside of them, through the introduction of the Pharaoh. What's the process been like of expanding the genre of 9-1-1: Nashville this season beyond both the procedural model and the family drama aspect to include the crime thriller element?

RAISANI: Now, I’ve absolutely loved it. I mean, I've just been thrilled. I've always loved police thrillers. Whether it be, you know, True Detective, or Sherlock, or Seven, or Zodiac. And so I think to infuse that, I think it ended up being… I never knew how it was going to work, but I think that blend of taking our heightened Nashville fun and soap, and then giving it this bass note of a real high-stakes killer and thriller, and somebody like Rogue, who's very hard-bitten. I think it ended up, to me, making the chemistry all work better. And so, it's been a joy. I love crime thrillers, and I think that's one thing that's evident. Our writers love it, the actors love it, and so I think it's really elevated the show to have that.

And this episode revealed another piece of backstory for Rogue. We already knew about his sons, but he also has a little sister who's seven years younger. Is this a potential character that we could see in the future?

RAISANI: It is, as are his sons. And we will see one of his sons very soon. So yes, every time we make those little stray comments, we're trying to tell – and our audience is so smart, they're just like you, up on it. They always go, “Oh, I see what you're doing.” But yeah, we want to world-build, and we want to let people know that these people have lives off the few minutes of screen time they get in an episode, that they do have rich backstories and lives, and we will be bringing them in.

Can I ask, is his son going to be in the LA crossover with 9-1-1?

And then, Don and Rogue have come a long way from Don briefly letting him choke in the season premiere to them now having a friendship. What do you think the shift was for Don to finally trust him?

RAISANI: I think the shift was that Don began to respect that Rogue has integrity. He may not be everybody's cup of tea; he may be gruff, he may be a little bit more brusque than anything Don's used to, but Don sees what guides him is goodness, even if it has an interesting package, and they don't agree on how to do things, and they don't agree on how to talk to people even sometimes. At the end of the day, Don sees that Rogue is a force for good, in his own, sometimes misguided, way – at least, misguided in Don's opinion. And so I think he sees the light in Rogue, even if Rogue isn't the guy he would have imagined he'd want to hang out with.

The procedural is back with an intense opening emergency.

There are so many great dynamics amongst the main characters in Nashville, especially now that Rogue is also in the picture. Are there any dynamics we haven't seen yet between characters who haven't had much cause to interact, that you would love to play around with?

RAISANI: Yeah. I mean, there's a lot. One thing that we just scratched the surface of, funny enough, is Taylor with Don. I think Taylor and Roxie is one of the great friendships we just haven't had the time for yet, but that's coming in the back half of the season, is to play more of Taylor and Roxie's friendship, to play more of Rogue with people like Cammie, who I think — and Blythe — he has great chemistry with them.

I feel like we're just getting started. There's so many people who’ve never really had scenes, or certainly not stories, together. So I'd like to see Dixie meet Rogue. They have a brush in an episode that's coming up, so I like to think we're just getting started. There are so many relationships we haven't even… we haven't even seen Blythe’s dad, Edward (Tim Matheson), so far this season. So, like I said, I always feel like we're running out of time before we even get started.

9-1-1: Nashville will return to ABC on Thursday, January 7 at 9:00 P.M. EST.
