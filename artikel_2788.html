Chicago Bears vs. Detroit Lions: Picks and Predictions

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

On Thanksgiving at Ford Field, the 8-2 Bears travel to face the 8-2 Lions in a battle for sole possession of first place in the NFC North. It doesn't get any better than this, folks. Chicago arrives on a five-game winning streak behind Caleb Williams and a physical rushing attack that has slowed somewhat in the last few weeks with the loss of Cole Kmet, while Detroit is rolling on a six-game run with Jared Goff and one of the league's most explosive offenses. On defense, the Bears are as healthy as they have been all season, minus Kyler Gordon, and will feature Big Jeff Simmons for the first time against the Detroit offense.

While the Lions defense has gotten back Branch and Joseph in their secondary, they have lost two Pro Bowl players in Jack Campbell and Aidan Hutchinson to season-ending injuries. Ben Johnson returns to his former home against Dan Campbell in a high-stakes divisional showdown that could shape the rest of the season. The questioning narrative that has emerged in Ben Johnson's second season with the Bears points to his 2-6 record against the division. Can Ben and the Bears start to turn that storyline around on Thursday? What follows are our score pick, winner, and projected stat lines for this Thanksgiving classic.

Thursday, November 26, 2026, at Ford Field.

TV: CBS (Jim Nantz, J.J. Watt, Tracy Wolfson)Streaming: Paramount+ and NFL+ (local markets)

Radio: ESPN Chicago (1000 AM / 100.3 FM HD2) with Jeff Joniak, Tom Thayer, and Jason McKie.

TJ: I do think the Bears are the better team, but Detroit has shown they are still electric on offense, and the last two outings make me nervous about Chicago's ability to hang in a shootout if it comes to it. The Lions also appear to have Ben Johnson's number through one year, and until the narrative changes, I'm going to give them a slight edge. 27-23 Detroit

Gary Baugher Jr.: I feel like we've done enough on defense in 2026 to avoid getting boat raced by the elite talent the Lions have on offense, and I'm fully confident in our offense to keep pace against their defense. It might be a back and forth affair that comes down to the last possession but I'll take the Bears by a field goal, 24-21.

Josh: Detroit is a terrible matchup for Chicago. Their offense can stress all of the defense's weaknesses, their defense can exploit the limitations of the OL, and Campbell seems to be able to win the chess match with Ben Johnson. Call it 27-23 Detroit, with a late rally falling short.

Jacob Infante: I don't think the Bears will get swept in this series like they did last year, but I personally think the Lions are playing better football right now. Chicago will get its lick back later in the season, but with this game being in Detroit and with the Bears undergoing some inconsistency on offense, I trust the Lions' explosive offense a little bit more to get the job done, unfortunately. Lions 26, Bears 21

Sam: Detroit is playing great, while the Bears just haven't looked themselves. I think they've been saving it all up for this. It's going to be a showdown with points, defensive plays and more. I think Jahmyr Gibbs and Jared Goff edge the Bears and we endure 10 days of "Ben Johnson and Caleb Williams can't beat the NFC North." Detroit 31, Chicago 28

JB: Bears 34, Detroit 20. I'm convinced the Bears spent a big chunk of the bye week prepping for this game, knowing they could handle New Orleans with a simple game plan. Offense is going to go off in the dome, defense is playing great, and the Bears haven't trailed since Caleb has returned from injury.

GOOCH: I think Ben has had this one circled on the calendar for a while. He wants the Detroit monkey off the proverbial back, and what better place to do it than in Detroit on Thanksgiving. I personally buy into the theory that the Bears went vanilla against the Saints in order to keep most of the game plan under the lid for the Detroit game. I expect a revived Bears rushing attack as both Swift and Monangai establish the run game with the Guy posting a TD and over 100 yards on the ground. This grinding approach wins the Bears the time of possession (like most games this season) and keeps the explosive Detroit offense off the field. Caleb torches the Detroit secondary for 3 TD passes and over 300 yards on the fly, as Loveland and Odunze score TDs and Burden has over 100 yards recieving yards. On defense Big Jeff, Booker, and Jennings notch sacks on Goff, who is under pressure all day. The Lions settle for three field goals rather than touchdowns. The Bears control Gibbs for the most part, although he does rush for over 100 yards and a TD. Bears pick off Goff twice. Final Score: Bears 34, Detroit 23.
