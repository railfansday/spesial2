Red Sox Sign Jake Bauers For $58 Million And Another First Baseman

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

The Boston Red Sox made the first major free agent signing of the off-season when they locked up the National League Championship Series MVP Jake Bauers to a four-year, $58 million contract. That deal well exceeds what Tim Britton of The Athletic predicted — two years, $33 million.

Bauers' story, which was well-trod during the playoffs, had him drafted by the San Diego Padres in the seventh round in 2013. He made his MLB debut in 2018 while playing for the Tampa Bay Rays. After that season, Tampa Bay shipped him to Cleveland as part of three-team trade that netted them Yandy Díaz.

In June of 2021, Bauers was sent to Seattle for a player to be named later, and wasn't re-signed. The Cincinnati Reds signed him as a free agent, and didn't last half a season before he was sent to the New York Yankees. A year later he was traded to the Milwaukee Brewers for two minor leaguers.

After the 2024 season, having played for five teams in five years, after logging four consecutive negative bWAR seasons, and after slashing .199/.301/.361 in 116 games for the Brewers, Bauers informed his agent he was retiring. Then his wife informed him that she was pregnant with their second child. So he then informed his agent that he was not retiring, and that he was ready to play in 2025. He made $1.4 million that year, and another $2.7 million for the World Champs last season. Five days after the World Series, he once again found himself on the free agent market, but this time with considerably more cachet.

Bauers put up 3.3 bWAR this year, with 26 homers, 86 RBI and a 140 OPS+. That was one homer less and five RBI more than Boston's leader in both of those categories this year — Willson Contreras. That makes sense as, according to Baseball Savant, Contreras grades out as a much better defender at first base than Bauers (a point not lost on fans who watched the post-season).

But this move raises all sorts of other questions for Boston. The majority of their DH at-bats last year went to Masataka Yoshida, who has one year left on the five year, $90 million contract he signed in 2023. Suffice it to say, he has not lived up to the money, with a total of 34 home runs and a .759 OPS in just 340 games over four seasons. The Red Sox may elect to eat the remaining $18 million to free up roster space.

And then there is the issue of Jarren Duran, whose name has been the subject of trade rumors almost every year. Coming off his worst professional season (.627 OPS and 76 OPS+), he is arbitration eligible. The predictions are that he would be paid eight figures. But the Red Sox could simply not tender him a contract, release him, and allow him to become a free agent. The more likely scenario is that they again seek a trade partner (again) to see if they can obtain some value for the mercurial outfielder.

And then there is the issue of Triston Casas. Once viewed to be the club's slugger of the future, he missed all of last season and most of 2024 and 2025 with various, serious injuries. He is currently in the Dominican Republic hoping to regain his form and find a place on a major league roster, even if it is not in the Back Bay. The Red Sox do not have to offer him a contract for 2027. He is arbitration eligible, but his leverage is pretty minimal considering he made just $1.6 million last year, and hasn't played in nearly two years.

Boston had an odd roster last season, and they look to have the same issue again in 2027. But signing Bauers does provide some additional thump in the lineup — something that was sorely needed as the team ranked 26th in home runs last year. Adding a left-handed bat wasn't ideal, as the team could use more from the right side, but slug is slug, and runs can come from either batter's box. Craig Breslow, now the President of Baseball Operations whereas he was previously Chief Baseball Officer, is fortified by a long-term extension and the right and ability to build his team. The Bauers signing was the first in what one believes will be a long journey to get the Red Sox into the playoffs and past the Wild Card round.
