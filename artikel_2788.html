Fernando Mendoza draws praise in Week 12 NFL Power Rankings for Las Vegas Raiders

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Fernando Mendoza is two games into his tenure as the Las Vegas Raiders' starting quarterback. While he's 0-2 in those outings, there have been a handful of promising moments, and he's coming off a three-touchdown performance against a strong Denver Broncos defense. As a result, Mendoza has drawn plenty of praise in the Week 12 NFL Power Rankings across the league's media.

The Las Vegas Raiders have lost both games in which Fernando Mendoza started. Yet, the resiliency seen from this year's No. 1 overall draft pick has been impressive. The Denver Broncos built a 17-0 lead Sunday. Mendoza is going to have his ups and downs, of course. At the same time, the rookie signal-caller led his team to 17 straight points before everything started to fall apart again. In doing so, he opened some eyes among the Raiders' division rival.

"There certainly was a lot of respect for Fernando Mendoza in the Broncos locker room," James Palmer reported. "What stood out was how calm he was in the pocket and his feel in the pocket, as well as how unpredictable he is when he starts to move. Most young players have something they do and go to it over and over again. Mendoza scrambles out a variety of ways and scans the field well while doing it." — Brent Sobleski

To be fair, the No. 1 pick did play a role in everything falling apart for the Raiders. After tying the game, he missed Jalen Nailor on an open fourth-down conversion and had the pick-six that sealed the team's fate. However, the defense was undoubtedly the bigger issue. Denver scored touchdowns on its two possessions after the contest was knotted up at 17 with about five minutes left in the third quarter.

Also, it's hard to win when the other team's offense scores 31 points and has 350 yards from scrimmage. Granted, the Raiders outgained the Broncos by 19 yards despite running four fewer plays and losing the time of possession battle by about 7.5 minutes. Mendoza has to avoid the big mistake, but he's far from the biggest reason the Silver and Black lost on Sunday.

I'm fascinated to see what the Raiders can do to improve their defense this offseason because I know this offense can put up points. Fernando Mendoza produced the first strong game of his career, and the Silver and Black delivered an entertaining contest against a superior Broncos squad in Denver, which is a good sign about where Las Vegas is headed in the bigger, multiyear picture. — Nick Shook

This picks up right where the section above left off. Las Vegas ranks third-to-last in yards allowed (382.0) and dead last in points allowed (29.7) per game, signalling big changes are needed on defense in the offseason.

Defensive tackle should be the starting point, and it will be interesting to see how the young corners — Jermod McCoy, Hezekiah Masses and Darien Porter — finish the season. Adding another edge-rusher wouldn't hurt either, but the Raiders will get Keyron Crawford back from injury next season to give the position group a young talent to turn to.

Something to be thankful for: Fernando Mendoza's learning curve

After making his first two starts against Seattle and Denver, the rookie quarterback is a predictable 0-2. The good news is he threw for 267 yards and three touchdowns against the Broncos, and the road gets easier from here. "I was really impressed with (Mendoza's) off-schedule stuff," Broncos coach Sean Payton said. "I was more impressed with his size and his escapability than maybe I saw on tape." — Josh Kendall, Chad Graff

It's funny, being able to make out of structure plays was viewed as one of the Indiana product's weaknesses coming out of college. But something that stood out about his game during the Mendoza Mania series this summer was his ability to make defenders wrong when scrambling; either using his legs to move the ball or dumping it to receivers when defenders don't plaster. We've seen that pop up a few times in the last two weeks, and, as Payton mentioned, Mendoza's escapability was much better from start one to start two.

Bowers will be eligible for an extension in the offseason, and Las Vegas shouldn't waste time locking down a franchise cornerstone. Despite battling knee and ankle injuries this season, he still has been productive, with 50 catches for 676 receiving yards and six touchdowns. Having Bowers around for the early stages of quarterback Fernando Mendoza's career will be essential for the No. 1 pick's development. Negotiations between Bowers and the Raiders could get interesting, given the number of times he has lined up on the outside this season. — Ryan McFadden

McFadden brings up a point that helps add some context/encouragement about Mendoza's first two starts: Bowers has only been on the field for 11 snaps in the last two weeks, per Pro Football Focus. That leaves the rookie quarterback with an underwhelming crew of pass-catchers, and he's still managed to turn heads. The problem is that's music to Bowers' agent's ears, as he's about to get paid like a 'WR1' when the time comes.

Luckily, the Raiders can buy themselves some time in the negotiation by picking up his fifth-year option this spring. According to Over The Cap, that will cost $17.35 million for 2028, which would make him the fifth-highest-paid tight end in terms of dollars per year. Seeing as the two-time Pro Bowler will undoubtedly make more than that when his contract is up, exercising the option is a no-brainer.

Some coaches spend minutes saying absolutely nothing. Klint Kubiak is the polar opposite in terms of meaningful words per second in pre-halftime interviews. — Conor Orr

We're veering away from the main topic here; Orr is referencing Kubiak's halftime interview where he said the refs need to call the game both ways when asked about the penalty discrepancy between the two teams last Sunday. The Raiders were flagged nine times for 63 yards, while the Broncos had just two penalties for 10 yards in the contest.

Even more interesting, Vegas averages the third-fewest penalties per game (5.7) this season while Denver is tied for 16th (7.1), according to TeamRankings. The one that stood out to me the most was Cody White getting called for offensive pass interference, which wiped away a touchdown, while the play below by Courtland Sutton wasn't penalized. Then again, this is nothing new for Raider Nation.
