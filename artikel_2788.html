Ravens Bench Marlon Humphrey After He Quips Baltimore Is Best at ‘Making Criminals’

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Baltimore Ravens have deactivated cornerback Marlon Humphrey after he made an offensive comment about the city

Humphrey said Baltimore is "best at making criminals" on a recent episode of his podcast

Ravens head coach Jesse Minter said Humphrey is "very remorseful"

The Baltimore Ravens have deactivated cornerback Marlon Humphrey after he made an upsetting comment about the city.

On a recent episode of his self-titled podcast, Humphrey, 30, discussed what Baltimore is "best at making" when he suggested, "They're best at making criminals."

On Wednesday, Nov. 18, Humphrey apologized for the comments while speaking to reporters at his locker, as seen in a video by The Baltimore Banner reporter Kyle Goon.

"I would like to apologize for some comments on my podcast I had yesterday to the city of Baltimore," he said, adding, "Obviously, I do know the effect that words have, just would like to apologize for those comments."

Marlon Humphrey on Sept. 20, 2026 in BaltimoreCredit: Patrick Smith/Getty

The Ravens sent Humphrey home from the team facility on Thursday, Nov. 19.

Head coach Jesse Minter said Thursday the decision to send Humphrey home was made after "internal conversations" with "leadership of the organization." "We've decided to deactivate Marlon for this game this upcoming week," Minter, 43, said.

The first-year coach told reporters he knows there will "be a lot of questions" around the subject, but said the "conversations are very, very personal and private," and believes the matters "need to remain that for the good of the team and the organization."

When asked if Humphrey was with the team, Minter said, "He won't be at practice."

Minter went on to tell reporters that Humphrey is "very remorseful" for the comments. "I think he really understands that words mean a lot, especially to people in this city, and you know, when you say something like that, it's not a reflection of our team and our organization and how we want to operate," Minter said.

One reporter asked if Humphrey will need to "earn back" trust from the team, but Minter said he prefers to "keep most of that in-house."

Concluding his comments about Humphrey, Minter explained, "Having to be a leader when the team needs you the most is my job," and said Humphrey will have to "deal with a lot of stuff from this."

The Ravens play the Carolina Panthers on Sunday, Nov. 22 at 1 p.m. ET.
