‘Perfect weapon’: diabetic woman found guilty of murdering her 94-year-old parents with insulin

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Jury convicts Raelene Polymiadis after four-month trial in South Australian supreme court

Get our breaking news email, free app or daily news podcast

A jury has convicted a diabetic woman of murdering both her elderly parents with insulin after a marathon criminal trial.

Raelene Polymiadis, 66, will face two mandatory life sentences after jurors on Friday delivered unanimous verdicts after a four-month South Australian supreme court trial.

She clutched the dock as the first verdict was read out, then dabbed at her eyes with a tissue after the second guilty verdict was announced, repeatedly shaking her head and blinking rapidly.

Polymiadis’s bail was revoked and she was remanded in custody ahead of sentence.

After a trial that began on June 15 and blew through its 12-week estimate due to illnesses and other delays, the jury needed less than five hours before returning verdicts on Friday morning.

They accepted Polymiaidis, who has type 1 diabetes, injected Brenda and Lynton Anderson with fatal doses of insulin in separate incidents in March 2022 and April 2023 respectively. Both were aged 94 at the time of their deaths.

Each had faced declining health and both were strongly opposed to being moved into aged care. One potential motive raised by prosecutors was a misplaced idea that killing her parents would spare them from entering a nursing home.

After Brenda had a fall in February 2022, she was admitted to Noarlunga hospital with a fractured vertebra, complicated by pneumonia and another fall.

She had been recovering and had a discharge plan in place, but after Polymiadis visited her mother on March 12, nursing staff found Brenda “largely unresponsive”.

The prosecution alleged Polymiadis administered insulin to her mother on two occasions – on 12 March and between 13 March and 15 March.

Lynton continued to live at home after his wife’s death, but became more frail and his four children began staying there at night on a shift basis.

Polymiadis left the house on 30 April, 2023, after staying with her father for the weekend.

Two hours later, a carer found Lynton unconscious. He died on 1 May as a result of insulin toxicity.

Polymiadis’s former boyfriend Ian Edward Willcourt told the court she had told him that “insulin was the perfect murder weapon because it’s natural in the body and it can’t be detected”.

In other key evidence, a police listening device captured Polymiadis alone in her car allegedly confessing to God that she had killed her parents.

Police also seized notes written by Polymiadis, including one which read: “What they will do is wait for a few weeks, months, year to see if I ‘think I got away with it’ and I get cocky and slip up.

“Hang on buddy, it could be a long ride, otherwise the suspect would be arrested now.”

Polymiadis’ husband, Michael, and her son, who had accompanied his mother to court throughout the trial, did not comment as they left the courthouse.

Justice Sandi McDonald thanked the jury for their service during the “long and difficult trial”.

Polymiadis will reappear in court on 16 December for a directions hearing.
