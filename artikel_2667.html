Recap: Avalanche go down swinging in Utah

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

To conclude their road trip, the Colorado Avalanche met with a division rival in the Utah Mammoth for their first meeting of the season in yet another start of a back-to-back. The game was physical early which led to a lot of penalties and multiple fights. Ultimately the Avalanche couldn't cash in on their opportunities and fell this round to Utah in a 4-1 final.

Brutality was the word of the night as these two division rivals got the nasty going early and often. It started with Martin Nečas who missed the puck in the neutral zone and fell awkwardly but he kept playing. Artturi Lehkonen left briefly for a puck that hit off his foot. Rookie Alex Gagne stepped up on Liam O'Brien in the neutral zone and then the Mammoth retaliated by Jack McBain hitting Gavin Brindley up high, who had to retire to the dressing room. Gabe Landeskog took a four-minute cross checking penalty on Logan Cooley but the Avalanche killed it off. There was no score on the board after 20 minutes of play but many bruises gained.

Despite the Avalanche starting the second period on the power play, it was Utah who drew first blood. Liam O'Brien sent a bit of a knuckle puck that went through Mackenzie Blackwood for his first goal of the year. Shortly after, Gabe Landeskog engaged in a fight with Jack McBain which the latter handily won.

There was a continuous parade of penalties to close out the second period led to Utah's second goal of the game. As a very short 5-on-3 expired Vincent Trocheck got a puck through Blackwood from the left circle to extend the lead to 2-0. The period wouldn't end without another fight as Alex Gagne got Liam O'Brien to drop the gloves. Neither party was very interested in throwing any punches so it ended without any fireworks.

To start the third period, the Avalanche, again, had a power play but instead of scoring gave up a shorthanded goal to Dimitri Simashev. It was the fifth time that's happened this season and now the Avalanche share the league lead in that measure. There wasn't a shutout at least when Jaden Schwartz was initially credited with a goal that went to Parker Kelly which took several hops and went past Utah goaltender Sebastian Cossa. With a glimmer of hope restored, Colorado went with the extra attacker in the final minutes of the game but Kevin Stenlund found the empty net and Utah secured their 4-1 victory.

With 11 total minor penalties called in this game, there was an opportunity for the Avalanche to turn the tide on one of their four power plays but they were the same slow, perimeter and ineffective waste of two minutes we've grown accustomed to. The icing on the cake was Shmashev going coast to coast without any opposition to score his shorthanded goal.

It's nice the Avalanche showed some fight early in the game sticking up for each other but that brand of physicality is clearly not in their nature and Utah took advantage of winning that battle.

Brindley was ruled out for the rest of the game and would be a shock if he plays tomorrow. We'll see if any other ailments come up before tomorrow's puck turn around. If there isn't then perhaps the newly acquired Mike Reilly makes his Avalanche debut as the seventh defenseman.

Another quick turn around with a game back at Ball Arena tomorrow night, November 19th, as the Avalanche host the Anaheim Ducks. Puck drop is at 7:00 p.m. MT.
