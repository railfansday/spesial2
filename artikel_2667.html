The Doncaster teenager setting his sights on NFL stardom

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

When a teenage American football player from Doncaster received his first offer to play for a university in the US, he was "sceptical" of it being real. Now, Zak Pye has 33 offers and his eyes set on a team that would see him play in front of about 20,000 people.

One more season playing for Sheffield Giants stands between the 19-year-old and his potential move next year to Eastern Kentucky University.

He says: "I feel like from there you can get spotlighted, whether it's to a better Division 1 school or to just to progress at Kentucky.

Pye says he did not get selected for the UK-based NFL Academy - a common path for young British players - because he is too old.

But this did not stop him from creating his own opportunities.

He says he sent "150 messages a night" on social media to coaches in the US, trying to attract their attention.

When the first offer of scholarship came, he was "honestly in shock," he says.

"The first offer that I got, the school was brand new, so I was a little bit sceptical because I was like, 'is this even a real school?'

"I obviously did all my research and it was, but then from there on I guess every single offer just feels like the first one.

"It's so exciting to be offered to go to a different school, a different division, different coaches and just learn more about the schools."

Pye currently plays for Sheffield Giants [Ben Frewin]

Pye says his dream is to play professionally, whether in the National Football League (NFL) or elsewhere.

The top level of American football is a difficult league to get into.

Of the more than 83,000 people playing college football each year, only about 250 of them will be drafted to play in the NFL, according to the National Collegiate Athletic Association.

As of the start of the 2026 season, four Brits were playing in the NFL, including Jermaine Eluemunor, who is marking his 10th season in the league and, like Pye, plays offensive line.

Asked why he likes to play American football, he says: "Honestly, I just like hitting people.

"I like the physicality. I did boxing when I was in Year 7.

"I did it for like seven years, up until probably Year 11, Year 12."

Beyond being a contact sport, success in American football depends on having good strategies and tactics - something Pye is keen to get stuck into.

He says: "There's so much to learn about and I think I just enjoy that.

"It's not like you pick it up and you're good, I like having to put work in and I like learning all about the sport."

Pye describes his training and preparations as being "basically a full-time job", which he says can sometimes be difficult to balance around seeing his family and girlfriend.

"I feel like sometimes it's easy to get drifted away from what you want to do," he says.

"It's easy to want to go out with your friends and go out partying and clubbing, but then I think, well, I can do that later on.

"I just need to focus on this right now because the two don't really go hand-in-hand."

The NFL Academy, which opened in 2019 and is now based in Loughborough, is available for 16 to 18-year-olds.
