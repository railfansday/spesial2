Buccaneers waste five takeaways in 27-24 loss to Bears

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

The Tampa Bay Buccaneers had every chance to pull off an improbable comeback against the Chicago Bears on Sunday night, but offensive mistakes and a late defensive breakdown led to a 27-24 loss at Soldier Field. Despite forcing five turnovers, including four interceptions of Caleb Williams, Tampa Bay couldn't capitalize consistently enough to overcome a disastrous opening quarter.

Bucky Irving scored twice, and Baker Mayfield converted a two-point attempt to tie the game with 2:51 remaining. However, Williams connected with Colston Loveland for a 38-yard gain on Chicago's final possession, setting up Cairo Santos' game-winning field goal as time expired.

The Buccaneers outgained Chicago 352-349, led 18-15 on first downs, and controlled the ball for 32:52. Those advantages were undermined by a 2-for-12 performance on third down, four sacks allowed, and two Mayfield interceptions, including a touchdown return that proved particularly costly.

Tampa Bay's defense looked overwhelmed on Chicago's opening two possessions. Williams connected with Rome Odunze for a 9-yard touchdown on the first drive, then found Luther Burden III for a 56-yard score on the next, putting the Buccaneers behind 14-0 with six minutes left in the first quarter.

The defense responded by forcing four interceptions over the next two quarters. Tykee Smith started the turnaround with a takeaway near Tampa Bay's goal line, while Zyon McCollum intercepted Williams twice before halftime. McCollum's second interception required a replay reversal after officials initially ruled his diving catch incomplete.

Alex Anzalone added another interception in the third quarter when Jacob Parrish deflected a pass intended for Burden, giving the offense possession at Chicago's 37-yard line.

Rookie linebacker Josiah Trotter also made an impact beyond the turnovers. He repeatedly worked around blockers to meet runners near the line of scrimmage and pressured Williams through the middle of Chicago's protection. Trotter finished with eight combined tackles, while the Buccaneers limited the Bears to 72 rushing yards on 22 attempts.

The defense delivered its fifth takeaway late in the fourth quarter. Rueben Bain Jr. forced a fumble from Burden after a short completion, and Ifeatu Melifonwu recovered the football before returning it four yards to Chicago's 17-yard line. That play gave Tampa Bay a chance to tie the game without a lengthy offensive possession.

Tampa Bay's offensive line struggled with discipline and pass protection, creating unnecessary obstacles for an offense already struggling to sustain drives.

On consecutive second-half possessions, the Buccaneers committed penalties on their opening plays. A false start pushed the offense behind schedule after taking over at its own 18-yard line, and a holding penalty against tight end Bauer Sharp erased a five-yard Irving run after Tampa Bay gained favorable field position following a short Chicago punt.

The protection problems were just as damaging. Mayfield was sacked four times for 17 lost yards, including consecutive sacks during a critical fourth-quarter possession after the Buccaneers had pulled within eight points.

Chicago generated pressure without consistently needing extra rushers, leaving Mayfield little time to develop routes. The Buccaneers finished 2-for-12 on third down, and penalties, negative plays, and incomplete passes repeatedly forced Riley Dixon onto the field.

Tampa Bay committed eight penalties for 45 yards. Although Chicago was penalized nine times for 74 yards, the Buccaneers' mistakes often came when the offense needed to establish rhythm or capitalize on favorable field position.

Mayfield completed 18 of 32 passes for 243 yards without a touchdown, threw two interceptions, and took four sacks.

He produced two of Tampa Bay's biggest offensive gains, escaping pressure to find Kenneth Gainwell for 44 yards in the first quarter and connecting with Tez Johnson for 58 yards on the opening possession of the second half.

The completion to Johnson helped set up Irving's 2-yard touchdown run, trimming Chicago's lead to 17-13 with 11:37 remaining in the third quarter. Johnson finished with 76 receiving yards on two catches, but two drops, including one on third down, made it hard for Tampa Bay to sustain possessions.

Mayfield's most costly mistake came after Anzalone's third-quarter interception. With the Buccaneers taking possession in Chicago territory and trailing by four, Mayfield attempted a pass intended for Cade Otton. Cornerback Jaylon Johnson stepped in front of the route and returned the interception 67 yards for a touchdown, extending Chicago's lead to 24-13.

The turnover erased a chance to take the lead and forced Tampa Bay to overcome another double-digit deficit, despite its defense repeatedly creating scoring chances.

Irving was Tampa Bay's most productive offensive player, rushing for 97 yards and two touchdowns on 20 carries while adding four receptions for 28 yards.

He supplied several explosive runs, including a 25-yard gain early in the fourth quarter that helped set up Chase McLaughlin's 35-yard field goal. Irving also scored on Tampa Bay's opening second-half possession and delivered the tying touchdown late in the fourth.

However, the Buccaneers scored touchdowns on only two of five red-zone possessions. McLaughlin converted field goals from 27, 21, and 35 yards, but settling for nine points on those drives kept Tampa Bay from taking full advantage of its opportunities.

The offense finally capitalized after Melifonwu recovered a fumble. Irving gained 11 yards on the first play, then scored from six yards out on the next. Mayfield then dropped back on the two-point attempt, surveyed the field, and scrambled up the middle to tie the game at 24 with 2:51 remaining.

The comeback was impressive, as Tampa Bay trailed 14-0 in the first quarter and 24-13 entering the fourth, but the Buccaneers needed more production from their earlier scoring chances.

After Tampa Bay tied the score, Chicago took possession at its own 31-yard line with 2:51 remaining.

Williams moved the Bears into Tampa Bay territory before finding Loveland wide open for a 38-yard completion with 47 seconds left. The tight end reached the Buccaneers' 16-yard line, putting Chicago comfortably within Santos' field-goal range. The Bears then drained the remaining clock, and Santos connected from 38 yards as time expired.

Williams finished 22-of-37 passing for 279 yards, two touchdowns and four interceptions. Despite his mistakes, Chicago produced the decisive play when it mattered most.

The Buccaneers showed they could disrupt Williams, pressure him into difficult throws, and limit Chicago's rushing attack, but the final defensive possession left Loveland with enough separation to set up the winning kick.

Tampa Bay's 3-6 record reflects a team capable of making game-changing plays but unable to consistently turn them into victories.

The defense recovered from an awful start and created five takeaways, while Irving provided the offensive production necessary to erase an 11-point deficit. Yet the Buccaneers failed to finish drives, struggled to protect Mayfield and surrendered seven points on an interception return.

The most concerning aspect of Sunday's loss wasn't the absence of opportunities. Tampa Bay created plenty of them. The problem was an offense that repeatedly failed to convert favorable situations into touchdowns, leaving the defense with almost no margin for error when Chicago got the ball back.

The Buccaneers nearly overcame those mistakes, but another close defeat leaves them searching for the consistency needed to turn competitive performances into wins.
