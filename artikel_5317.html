Apple's camera and photo software chief has retired with no named replacement

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Jon McCormack's former deputies are taking over his duties under other Apple executives, according to Bloomberg

Jon McCormack, Apple $AAPL's head of camera and photo software, left the company through retirement at the end of October, ending a tenure of more than eight years, according to Bloomberg.

Apple has not appointed anyone at the vice president level to fill the position McCormack held, according to Bloomberg. His onetime deputies now handle the work he led, each reporting to other executives. An Apple spokesperson declined to comment.

McCormack was responsible for the Camera and Photos apps, along with the core technology behind how users shoot, browse and edit photos and video, according to Bloomberg. He reported to Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice president of software engineering. The retirement puts Federighi in charge of managing a handover of leadership in a niche, technical field.

Among the projects McCormack led was the iPhone 17 lineup's redesigned selfie camera, which debuted last year, according to Bloomberg. Apple also built a variable-aperture camera into the iPhone 18 Pro lineup this year, and it rolled out a customizable Camera app interface with professional controls.

McCormack objected to generative AI being used to make or change photos, because he thought an image ought to depict something that really happened, according to Bloomberg. That view clashes with the direction Apple has lately taken by adopting AI-powered tools. His retirement did not stem from any dispute with Apple about its AI strategy.

Google $GOOGL, a unit of Alphabet, and Samsung Electronics have rolled out generative AI tools that let users substantially alter their pictures, according to Bloomberg.

In a June interview with Tyler Stalman, McCormack discussed iOS 27 features including Clean Up, Extend and Spatial Reframing. He said the features "preserve the integrity of the moment," according to 9to5Mac.

McCormack is another vice president to exit Apple this fall, following Jennifer Bailey, who led Apple Pay and Wallet services for many years, according to Bloomberg. Chief Executive Officer John Ternus has shaken up management, aiming to help Apple operate with more speed and efficiency.
