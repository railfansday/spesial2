Kirby Smart’s Search to Replace Glenn Schumann Could Take an Interesting Turn

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

ATHENS, Ga. — Georgia head coach Kirby Smart has another major decision approaching as the Bulldogs prepare for the postseason.

However, one potential solution to his latest coaching vacancy could create an interesting situation elsewhere in the SEC.

Florida State officially hired Georgia defensive coordinator Glenn Schumann as its next head coach Monday.

The departure leaves Smart searching for a replacement for one of his longest-serving assistants.

Several candidates have already emerged as potential options.

And one familiar name could make the search particularly intriguing.

Will Muschamp has emerged among the names worth monitoring as Georgia considers its next defensive coordinator.

The former South Carolina and Florida head coach previously served as Georgia's co-defensive coordinator under Smart.

However, Muschamp now holds the defensive coordinator position at Texas.

Georgia could theoretically pursue a coach currently working for another SEC program with national championship aspirations.

Muschamp's familiarity with Smart and Georgia's defensive system makes him a logical name to discuss.

However, there is an important distinction.

There is no confirmed report that Georgia has offered Muschamp the position or that he intends to leave Texas.

His inclusion among potential candidates reflects his previous relationship with Smart and his experience coordinating defenses.

Nevertheless, the possibility adds intrigue to an already important coaching search.

Muschamp's current position would not necessarily be easy to leave.

According to the Atlanta Journal-Constitution, Texas is paying Muschamp approximately $3 million as its defensive coordinator.

That figure exceeds Schumann's reported compensation at Georgia.

Consequently, pursuing Muschamp could require a significant financial commitment.

The Longhorns enter Rivalry Week ranked among the nation's leading College Football Playoff contenders.

They have an opportunity to strengthen their postseason position Friday against Texas A&M.

Losing a defensive coordinator during or immediately after a championship pursuit would create another challenge for the program.

For now, Muschamp remains an intriguing possibility rather than a confirmed target.

Smart may not need to look outside Athens.

Travaris Robinson currently serves as Georgia's co-defensive coordinator and safeties coach.

He joined the Bulldogs before the 2024 season and has become an important member of their defensive staff.

Robinson's familiarity with the current roster could make an internal promotion attractive.

Furthermore, retaining continuity would allow Georgia to avoid a more extensive transition.

The Bulldogs have maintained a consistent defensive identity throughout Smart's tenure.

Promoting an established assistant could help preserve that approach.

However, Smart has previously demonstrated a willingness to make unexpected staffing decisions.

That leaves the search open to several possibilities.

Former Baylor head coach Dave Aranda is another potential candidate identified in reporting surrounding the vacancy.

Aranda has extensive defensive coaching experience and previously worked as LSU's defensive coordinator.

His background could make him an appealing option for a program seeking an experienced defensive strategist.

Georgia assistant Andrew Thacker also has previous defensive coordinator experience.

Thacker worked at Georgia Tech before joining the Bulldogs' staff.

However, his candidacy would represent another internal solution rather than a major outside hire.

The ultimate decision will depend on Smart's priorities.

He must balance continuity, recruiting relationships, defensive philosophy and long-term staff stability.

Schumann joined Georgia in 2016 as part of Smart's original coaching staff.

He eventually became the Bulldogs' primary defensive play caller and helped guide the program to national championships in 2021 and 2022.

His departure represents a significant change for a program that has relied on his leadership for more than a decade.

Georgia enters Rivalry Week at 10-1 and has already secured a berth in the SEC Championship Game.

The Bulldogs also remain firmly positioned for another College Football Playoff appearance.

That makes the timing of Schumann's departure particularly noteworthy.

Smart must manage the immediate demands of a championship pursuit while preparing for a major staff transition.

Georgia has consistently developed assistant coaches who eventually pursue larger opportunities.

Schumann's move to Florida State continues that pattern.

However, replacing him presents an important test for Smart.

An internal promotion could preserve continuity.

An outside hire could introduce new ideas and experience.

And pursuing a coach such as Muschamp could create consequences for another SEC contender.

For now, Georgia has not publicly announced Schumann's successor.
