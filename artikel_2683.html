Burnt Hills-Ballston Lake edges Troy in final seconds for epic Class A football title win

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

COLONIE - The waning stages of the fourth quarter Friday night for the Burnt Hills-Ballston Lake football team were looking painfully dire, as the Spartans found themselves down a point against Troy and forced to give the ball back on a punt.

In a span of a few minutes, BH-BL senior standout Login Tomlinson made two of the biggest plays of his career - the first with his hand and the second with his foot - to flip things back in favor of his team in the Section 2 Class A Super Bowl.

First, Tomlinson forced Troy junior quarterback Kyair Carr to fumble the ball away at the Flying Horses' 23-yard line. Then, BH-BL had just 17 seconds left when it had Tomlinson line up for a 32-yard field goal on third down and without a timeout remaining. The senior - following a timeout by Troy - drilled a go-ahead, line-drive boot down the middle in what ended up being a thrilling 23-21 Super Bowl victory over the Flying Horses at Colonie High School.

"I have been dreaming about this since I was a little kid and first starting kicking," said Tomlinson, a four-year varsity player who is headed to Siena University to play lacrosse. "It happened. It came true."

"It was really nerve-racking out there," BH-BL junior quarterback Tristan West said. "I believed in him every moment when he went out there to kick."

The pulse-pounding triumph secured BH-BL (11-0) the program's 11th Class A sectional title in 20 years, and first since 2021. It also marked the first championship guided by fourth-year coach Kevin DeBonis. The loss denied Troy, playing in its second straight Super Bowl, a 10th title since first winning one in 1993.

"This feels amazing. Since we were little kids, this is what we have been working for," West said.

Last year, the Flying Horses lost to Niskayuna. Troy senior fullback/defensive Ben Mergil, who produced two touchdown receptions and recovered a fumble by BH-BL, emerged from an emotional postgame gathering to put the defeat into context.

"It is hard, but, at the end of the day, it is football," Mergil said. "Anybody that has ever played football knows it will teach life lessons. Sometimes, things just don't shake out for you, but you just have to roll with the punches. I want to thank my coaches, my teammates and God for giving me this opportunity to play for such an amazing school and play my heart out."

Both squads left everything they had on the field. There were colossal swings of momentum swapped between the two teams all evening, especially down the stretch.

Troy led 14-0 and went to halftime up 14-6. West connected with Michael Bies for a 21-yard touchdown pass and Owen Huggins scored on a 2-point conversion to provide BH-BL its first lead at 20-14, but Troy senior running back Nymiear Jenkins - lined up in a wildcat formation - found Mergil on a 6-yard touchdown pass with 3:32 left to tie the score. Troy freshman kicker Crosby Murray booted the extra point through the uprights to provide Troy a 21-20 advantage.

"I really thought when we went ahead, our defense would get the stop and we did," Mergil said. "We went out there to run out the clock and things sometimes don't shake out the way you want."

Troy had the chance to run out the clock after stopping BH-BL and forcing a punt with 2:27 left, but Tomlinson helped set up the game-clinching sequence by punching the ball away from Carr on a first-down run down the Troy sideline.

Tomlinson saved his best for last. Originally summoned to the varsity as a freshman to be a kicker and punter, the senior has excelled at linebacker over the last three years. Friday, he reminded everyone how good of a kicker he is with the biggest field goal of his career.

"That is a hell of a play, and a hell of a player," Mergil said.

"This was a true nail-bitter. We knew Troy was going to be tough to beat and real physical," West said. "We knew it was going to be a close game and it feels great to come out with a 'W.'"
