Leeds United v Coventry City: Daniel Farke looks back on a year of progress – but not a turning point

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Most good stories have a plot twist – a sliding doors moment, as the film of that name put it into the English lexicon – where fortunes change. A year ago this weekend is widely seen as Daniel Farke's at Leeds United.

Starting to feel the heat after three straight defeats dropped his team into the Premier League relegation zone for the first time, 2-0 down at half-time Manchester City on November 29, 2025, manager Farke did what his critics said he could not and rethought his tactics. He abandoned the back four which had been an article of faith throughout his managerial career and switched to three central defenders.

Leeds pulled two goals back and although Phil Foden won the game with a late City goal, the course of the club's post-Bielsa history changed. Quite where it will end, supporters are still excitedly waiting to find out.

Had the Premier League season begun that night, Leeds would be eighth with 53 points from 36 matches, two short of a full campaign. They would take that when the real table ends on May 30.

But Farke is not a fan of such clear-cut simplicity, looking on past events not as a turning point, just a reference point.

Crucially, the Whites followed up that Manchester City performance with a home win over then-world champions Chelsea.

Just shy of 12 months on, they welcome Coventry City on Saturday on the back of another milestone win over Chelsea – this one at Stamford Bridge, where they had not won this century, and in a manner they have rarely if ever won anywhere since Marcelo Bielsa watched on from his bucket.

"Hopefully it's not the end of the good period but you never know," chuckles Farke, a man able to make jokes right now.

"We've spoken so much about the Man City game and the change of formation even though we have turned many games after it by playing a four-man (defensive line).

"I wouldn't label us, at least not in possession, a three-man formation any more but the pundits still speak about it. If we're pressing high it's two or three centre-backs but nowadays you get so drawn into, 'Oh, the Man City game, this was the turning point and after it, everything was easy' or we interpret something into the Chelsea game.

"I don't believe there's one big moment that changes it all.

"You have to deliver with hard work on the training pitch and in the games every day.

"It's not just one day that is important for your fate. You create your own fate by hard work."

If they ever make a film of Leeds’ 2025-26, the actor playing Farke will be fretting at his desk, chewing fingernails and pulling his hair but the reality, he insists, was different.

"It was not difficult, not more difficult than other periods," he says of November 2025.

"Working at Leeds United there will always be lots of noise, even after we played the best season in the history of this club with 100 points and winning the title and an open bus tour through the city.

"You have to make sure you win the next game and the next game and the next game.

"I've always handed a club back in a better position, so I've always taken confidence from this.

"If there is then a spell when you have a proper performance but you can't translate it into wins, that's football. You perhaps work a bit more concentrated to steady the ship and turn things around.

"Even at the top level there is always something to improve and pressure when there is noise. So no, I was not overly concerned or doubting myself too much or what we were doing, I was full of belief."

Whilst he is not prepared to read too much into this point last season in that respect, he will in others.

"From a win and the manner of it you can judge a lot from how far we have come,” he argues. “For an away game (at Chelsea) it was such a dominant, front-foot and fully-deserved performance with great team moves. It's not like we are just like a promoted side who has to suffer and play long balls in search of luck.

"We develop more and more into a side who can also be dominant and play even a bit like we did at Championship level. Don't get me wrong, we are not in a position right now to fight for the Premier League title.

"To play the football we are doing right now is something I'm pretty proud of. It describes the path we want to follow."

A year is a long time in football. Who knows what the Leeds narrative will be this time next year?
