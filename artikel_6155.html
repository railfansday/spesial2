Alaska Airlines Working With Multiple Agencies To Reduce Law Enforcement After ICE Arrests

Dozens of airport workers at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport (SEA) marched to Alaska Airlines headquarters on Tuesday, demanding the carrier call for US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) to be removed from the airport. This comes as the employees and airport workers demand greater protection after two passengers were arrested by immigration at the gate last weekend.

Alaska Airlines has confirmed to KING 5 News that it is currently engaging with government authorities and other industry partners for advice on ways to minimize the impact of law enforcement activities on other passengers, employees and general airline operations.

On Saturday, two individuals, Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza and his daughter Sofia Larrondo Hernandez Mejia, were arrested as they boarded an Alaska Airlines flight destined for Los Angeles International Airport (LAX). During boarding, ICE officers allegedly pushed past a customer service agent to arrest the two. The customer service agent asked the ICE agent for their credentials before pushing past, and ICE did not notify its own security team before the arrests.

This has raised concerns among employees and other guests, leading demonstrators to gather at Angle Lake Park before marching to the carrier's headquarters. On arrival at the airline's headquarters, they delivered a letter with demands to the airline's management team.

In this letter, the group of union workers and other employees have asked Alaska to publicly call for ICE to be removed from airports, and demand that warrants are required for agents to access the aircraft jet bridges, limit the information shared between airlines and ICE, and protect employees, who, following the recent arrest, feel unsafe at work.

Alaska spoke about the situation in a statement to Simple Flying on Friday.

"Alaska Airlines takes great pride in our long history of running a safe, caring and reliable operation. The safety, dignity and well-being of our guests and employees are fundamental to us, and we believe every traveler should feel safe and respected throughout their journey.

The events of Nov. 21 at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport deeply impacted our employees, guests and community, and we recognize the concerns and questions that have been raised. Our employees are trained to follow Alaska Airlines’ standard procedures and company policies when engaging with federal agencies.

The arrests have affected not only Alaska's workers, but other airlines operatingat the airport as well. A cabin lead at Delta Air Lines, the second-largest operator at SEA, noted that the high-profile arrests have left Nancy Kibocha afraid to return to work. Executive Director of OneAmerica, Washington State's largest immigrant and refugee advocacy organization, Roxana Norouzi, suggested that while airlines are expected to accommodate immigration, carriers do have some power to limit exposure to these arrests for other passengers and crew.

"These longstanding procedures give law enforcement officers, flight crews and airport teams clear, consistent guidance designed to support a safe, orderly environment for employees and guests," the carrier explained. "As an airline, Alaska is required to comply with applicable federal laws and regulations."

In the lead-up to Thanksgiving, this marks one of the busiest travel weekends in the country. Port of Seattle, the operator of Seattle-Tacoma International Airport, expects more than one million travelers to pass through the airport this week between Tuesday and Monday. A pilot reportedly walked off and two flight attendants asked to be replaced after the arrests.

With this demonstration coming on one of the busiest travel weeks of the year, union workers have called on the traveling public to join their demands, which have raised ongoing concerns about airport security procedures and enforcement, despite safety and security protocols being jeopardized.

Alaska confirmed that all its employees receive training and that the company has longstanding procedures for working with federal agencies. The carrier is required to comply with all federal laws and regulations across its network. The Port confirmed that law enforcement officers have broad access across the airport facilities, and the airport cannot interfere with immigration enforcement operations.

Federal agents must have a judicial warrant and other legal authority to access restricted areas of the airport, such as jet bridges, which require access credentials. Alaska has confirmed it will continue to prioritize the safety and well-being of guests and employees.
