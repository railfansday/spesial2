10 2026 Movies That Are Officially Oscar Contenders

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

This year, the record-breaking MCU movie Spider-Man: Brand New Day and Christopher Nolan's historical epic The Odyssey headlined the biggest summer of cinema since the COVID-19 pandemic. But the late summer nights are turning to cold winter evenings, and the attention of cinephiles has turned to the biggest event in the film calendar: the Academy Awards.

The glitz and glamor of Oscars night is coming into view, as the 99th ceremony readies to celebrate the best of the past twelve months of film. Scheduled to take place March 14, 2027, at the Dolby Theatre in Los Angeles, next year's Academy Awards will feature all the brightest stars in Hollywood today, with ambitious blockbusters, indie darlings, and even a documentary hoping to earn a coveted Best Picture nomination. But who are the current favorites? Here's a look at 10 2026 movies that are officially Oscar contenders.

The film on everyone's lips, A24's You Can See Everything, might finally make history as the first documentary to earn a Best Picture nomination in 100 years of Academy history. The documentary, co-directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, explores the days leading up to the incarceration of the infamous Theranos founder, Elizabeth Holmes.

"The year's most original movie will make you question reality," wrote Collider's Samuel R. Murrian in one of countless glowing reviews for this mind-bending documentary. With its viral success helping rocket the film up the box office charts, and with the world stunned by the life-altering contents of this three-hour masterpiece, it's possible that You Can See Everything ​​​​​​won't just earn a Best Picture nomination; it could win the trophy.

One of the defining films of 2026, it would be difficult to find someone who hasn't yet seen Project Hail Mary. This epic Phil Lord and Christopher Miller-directed space story follows Ryan Gosling's Ryland Grace as he leads a one-man mission into the stars to try and save the universe. However, he isn't alone in the dense emptiness of space, as he is joined on his mission by an alien he names Rocky.

A favorite to earn plenty of nominations in technical categories, with lead puppeteer and voice actor James Ortiz potentially in the race for Best Supporting Actor, Project Hail Mary will most definitely make its mark on next year's Oscars. As one of the most critically and commercially successful films of 2026, come March 14, 2027, the world could watch Grace Rocky save Oscars.

Another of the most popular films of the year, Focus Features' Obsession will hope to follow in the footsteps of The Substance and Weapons by becoming this year's horror darling at the Academy Awards. Star Inde Navarrette seems most likely to take home Oscars gold, as she currently ranks as one of the favorites in the Best Actress category for a performance that Collider's Hannah Hunt called "one of the year's best" in her review.

A box office record-breaker, becoming the highest-grossing original horror movie of this century among many more titles, Obsession shocked the world as it overcame the latest Star Wars movie through sheer viral appeal. Curry Barker's breakout is nothing short of a horror masterpiece, as one of the most important visionaries in Hollywood's next era gets off to a flying start.

Although some titles lead the charge during the Oscars race, others perform better under the radar, lurking beneath the competition before surprising cinephiles with several nominations. Right now, it seems like one of the best outside contenders for Academy recognition is Olivia Wilde's The Invite, a remake of the Spanish-language comedy The People Upstairs.

Unfolding like a great play, The Invite takes a simple premise, a single location, and four actors at the top of their game and transforms them into an utterly unmissable comedy. But this comedy isn't without its dramatic flair, with every twist and turn playing out like a hilariously realized thriller. Starring Seth Rogen, Edward Norton, and Penélope Cruz alongside Wilde, The Invite feels destined to earn recognition in writing and acting categories.

If, for the aforementioned Penélope Cruz, Oscar night doesn't equal gold for Wilde's The Invite, rest assured she stands a second chance as part of the ensemble of one of the bookies' favorites. Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi's La Bola Negra is a 155-minute drama inspired by the unfinished work of the same name that explores the connections between three gay men and the pain they endure.

Themes of love and loss in the queer community, as well as the erasure of queer identity, become ever more relevant each passing year, with these topics better represented at the Academy Awards in the process. La Bola Negra is the latest LGBTQIA+ story to receive the Oscars spotlight, as it is deemed a favorite to not just earn a Best Picture nomination, but to win the prize.

When forecasting the Oscars nominees for 2027, it's wise to look back at the historical voting of the often predictable Academy. Both 2021's Dune and 2022's Dune: Part Two received nominations in the Best Picture category, having combined to win eight trophies so far. So, with that in mind, the final installment in Denis Villeneuve's acclaimed trilogy seems destined to pick up a handful of nominations once again.

The ambitious attempt to turn the once-thought "unfilmable" Frank Herbert story into cinematic magic has already been the subject of endless praise from critics. But in his last installment, perhaps it's time for Villeneuve to win in the biggest categories? That's what many sci-fi fans will think, and as December 18 and the consequences of Paul Atreides' (Timothée Chalamet) power inch closer, whispers of a Dune: Part Three Best Picture bid grow.

Andrew Scott has quietly proven to be one of the most consistent actors working today, submitting a fine selection of truly terrific performances over the past few years. Come Oscars night next year, it seems the Sherlock star will finally receive recognition as one of the favorites to take home the Best Actor prize as Ian Charleson in the stunning drama, Elsinore.

Directed by Simon Stone and written by Stephen Beresford, Elsinore looks likely to be the second Hamlet-related film in two years to take home an acting prize at the Oscars, following Jessie Buckley's deserved win for Hamnet. Also like Hamnet, Elsinore looks to be this year's biggest tearjerker, with Collider's Therese Lacson writing, "For god's sake, bring more than just one pack of tissues to the theater," in her review.

In 2023, The Banshees of Inisherin was one of the year's biggest nominees, having earned nods in eight categories. Sadly, it won zero prizes, a fate that Irish filmmaker Martin McDonagh will be hoping to avoid with his latest Academy Award play, Wild Horse Nine. The film follows two CIA agents on a mission from Santiago to Easter Island in 1973, and is packed with all of McDonagh's typical dark, comedic wit.

As usual, a McDonagh movie looks set to take home nominations in writing categories, with star John Malkovich heavily tipped to take on the aforementioned Scott for Best Actor. Sam Rockwell, Steve Buscemi, Parker Posey, and the rest of the supporting cast deserve just as much credit for this uniquely encapsulating effort from an unstoppable filmmaker.

It's rare that any given year's Palme d'Or winner doesn't make an impact at the Academy Awards. In recent years, the likes of Bong Joon Ho's Parasite and Sean Baker's Anora have won both Cannes' biggest prize and the Best Picture trophy, so it's fair to assume that Cristian Mungiu's Fjord will also make its presence known come Oscar nominations day.

Mungiu's second Palme d'Or winner is arguably his most controversial film, touching on deeply nuanced topics and never offering an easy moral way out. But it is in this nuance that Fjord thrives, with Sebastian Stan and Renate Reinsve delivering perfectly detailed performances that capture both the best and worst of these troubled characters and their terrifying situation.

The front-runner to take home the Academy's biggest prize is Christopher Nolan's historical epic The Odyssey, dubbed an "unrivaled cinematic spectacle" in Joe Schmidt's review for Collider. This famous tale's star-studded retelling hasn't been without its detractors, but those with a say have made it clear their love for The Odyssey, placing it firmly at the #1 spot in many people's predictions.

In fact, The Odyssey could very well overtake Ryan Coogler's Sinners as the most nominated film of all time, considering its sure impact in both the major and craft categories. Nolan delivers his typically ambitious scale and scope to one of history's grandest stories, culminating in a final thirty minutes that are, without doubt, some of the most encapsulating cinema of the decade so far. Could The Odyssey sail to a record-breaking Oscars night? All will be revealed in a few months.
