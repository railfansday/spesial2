Aboriginal mother allegedly kicked off a bus in regional NSW because her baby was crying

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The NSW transport department says passenger safety is its ‘highest priority’ and the driver is no longer employed by the operator

Get our breaking news email, free app or daily news podcast

Transport for NSW is investigating allegations that a young Aboriginal mother and her family were told to leave a bus and left by the side of a regional highway because her 22-month-old child was crying.

Wiradjuri woman Kaneesha Lee Williams was travelling home from Sydney to the Riverina town of Darlington Point, 155km west of Wagga Wagga, with her young daughter, Audria, and her mother, Tamara, on Sunday.

They had been in Sydney for a family birthday and had caught the train from Sydney to Wagga Wagga, before transferring to a regional bus operated by NSW TrainLink, with tickets to Hay.

But about 15 minutes into the journey, on Coolamon Road, Williams alleges, the driver pulled over and told her and her family to disembark because her daughter was crying.

“We got on the road and my daughter Audria started getting a little bit restless so I sat her on my lap and put her to sleep because she was crying and stuff,” Williams said.

She said her daughter had just begun to settle when the driver pulled over.

“I thought, ‘why did we stop for?’ I didn’t think he was coming up to me. Then he walked up to me and he said, ‘I can’t drive this bus while that baby is crying, you’re gonna have to get off’.

“I just looked at him and I looked at my daughter and my eyes started swelling up into tears.”

She said the driver gave her no warning. “We weren’t aggressive or anything, we were more in shock … Are you really gonna kick a little baby off the bus for crying?

“She’s 22 months old, she’s overtired, overstimulated and doesn’t want to be on the bus … We had little food, no water. He didn’t even ask if we were able to get a lift or anything, he just kicked us straight off.”

Williams said they were deposited on the side of the road with low phone battery and no alternative transport arrangements. The driver did not offer to make alternative arrangements or ask if they had anyone to pick them up, she alleges. They eventually found somewhere to charge their phones to contact family, who drove more than two hours to pick them up.

Teenager Alexis Longdon, who was sitting three seats back on the opposite side of the aisle, told Guardian Australia she saw Williams and her family being asked to get off the bus, and was confused when they then left without them.

“We were just outside of Wagga and the bus driver stopped the bus and said: ‘can you get off the bus and calm her down please’, because apparently it was creating an unsafe environment for the driver,” the 14-year-old said. “And then she got off the bus and I guess she couldn’t calm her baby down, we waited five minutes maybe and then he got on the bus and left.

Williams then wrote a formal complaint to the NSW transport minister demanding an independent investigation into the incident, including an examination of all relevant CCTV, and asking whether the driver’s actions complied with TrainLink and the transport department’s policies and standards.

The letter, seen by Guardian Australia, also asked whether this would have happened to a non-Aboriginal family.

On Tuesday, the NSW minister for regional transport, Jenny Aitchison, said her office had reached out to Williams to offer support and that the “alleged behaviour of this driver was completely unacceptable”.

“It’s extremely disappointing that a mother and her young child were treated so poorly on a service that they had every right to use,” Aitchison said in a statement to Guardian Australia. “It’s not the service we accept from our bus drivers.

“I hope to speak to the family soon and reiterate my concern about this incident and apologise for what happened to them.

“I’m advised the bus driver is no longer employed by the coach operator.”

A Transport for NSW spokesperson said the incident was being investigated.

“Transport for NSW is aware of an incident involving passengers travelling on a NSW TrainLink coach service operated by Dyson between Wagga Wagga and Mildura on Sunday,” they said in a statement. “Transport for NSW takes the concerns raised extremely seriously.

“The operator is investigating the incident and Transport for NSW has requested updates as those enquiries progress.

“Passenger safety is our highest priority and we expect all operators and their employees to uphold the highest standards of safety and professionalism when interacting with passengers.”
