Fantasy Football: What happened to the tight end position this season? Scott Pianowski investigates

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

I'm spending my mid-November the way most good fantasy football managers are. I'm doing roster audits, schedule surveying and trying to figure out who's in the Circle of Trust and who's not. We wanted to get off to a fast start, we wanted to control October, we wanted to get into position to get into position. Check, check, check. Now let's bring this thing home.

And as I went through my checkpoints this week, a sneaky theme begged for my attention. What the heck happened at tight end? When did this position get so gross?

Isaiah Likely (TE11 in draft season, TE2 thus far) has been the position's biggest hit, and George Kittle (TE9/TE4) has shown that basic rehab rules don't apply to him. See you in the Hall of Fame, 85.

But what's causing so much agita elsewhere? Let's investigate.

For the most part, it's been smooth sailing for the vanity tight ends. Bowers has been a little dinged up, missed two games. He might not be healthy the remainder of the year. But he's shown the ability to produce in any kind of environment. McBride has long shown his ability to command targets, and the Cardinals routinely face negative game script — good for garbage-time production.

Alas, the Raiders and Cardinals have both rebooted at the QB position. Kirk Cousins was surprisingly sharp for nine games and the Raiders decided to flip the asset and bank the profit, shipping him to QB-needy Minnesota. The Cardinals did a similar thing to a lesser degree, moving Jacoby Brissett to desperate Baltimore after the Ravens lost both Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley. Sure, Brissett was already benched in Arizona, replaced by Gardner Minshew. But Minshew wound up getting hurt in his first start.

Bowers and McBride now have to make things work with unproven rookies Fernando Mendoza and Carson Beck. Obviously, the ceilings are different for these freshmen — Mendoza won the Heisman Trophy last year and was the first overall pick in the draft, while Beck was merely the 65th pick in the same class. But neither player looked ready last week, albeit the assignments were stark — Mendoza faced the Seahawks, Beck drew the Rams.

Bottom line with Bowers and McBride, we usually bet on talent in these spots. They've produced with lesser QBs before, and Mendoza and Beck will likely improve as they gain more reps. But you can't blame anyone who's pining for good old days, like September 2026, when the Raiders and Cardinals had experience in the QB chair.

It's easy to forget, but for about 10 weeks last year, the Colts had an elite offense. They were sitting at 8-2 after an overtime win over the Falcons, racking up 519 yards of offense. The NFC South was there for the taking.

And nothing has gone right since. Close losses to Kansas City and Houston followed after a bye week, and then Daniel Jones got hurt. Indianapolis finished the year on a seven-game losing streak and didn't make the playoffs.

Just about everything has crapped out here in 2026. Alec Pierce received a big extension but he's hardly played, hounded by injury. He's likely done for the year. Jones's return from his torn Achilles tendon has been a mess — league-high 12 picks, lousy 67.0 rating, just 183 yards per game. Jonathan Taylor has been credible but he's down to 4.2 YPC, the worst average of his seven-year career.

This is a roundabout way of saying Warren's frustration-filled second year isn't really his fault. Warren's also had his own injury concerns, missing two games with a groin pull. Desperate fantasy managers are wondering if forgotten backup QB Anthony Richardson Sr. might be an improvement as the Colts start scouting for the future.

Although Pitts' disappointing year is obviously tied to Atlanta's mediocre QB room, Pitts also has to own some of his disappointing metrics. He's down to 9.3 YPC and he doesn't command targets, averaging fewer than five per game. A little touchdown deodorant (four spikes) has slightly cushioned the blow, but Pitts has zero floor in any week. You need a better option if you want to have a legitimate playoff chance.

Loveland was being fitted for Bust of the Year horns after a horrible opening two weeks (one piddly catch, three yards), and the Caleb Williams injury at the end of Week 2 also muddied the story. But since Williams returned, Loveland has joined the party: TE4, TE3, TE14, TE15. And those solid returns have come despite some pesky drops.

The Bears have a lovely schedule moving forward, and Loveland is ostensibly needed more with Cole Kmet out for the season. Loveland might never fully justify his summer helium, but he's a trustworthy candidate as we start making playoff plans.

Kraft was having a glorious breakout year in 2025 before tearing his ACL in Week 9. But the draft market couldn't help itself all summer, pointing to Kraft's splashy metrics and figuring that even if he started the year less than 100%, he'd get there eventually.

Kraft hasn't been a bust — just landing anyone who's inside the starting line at tight end feels like a win this year. But his YPC is down over five yards, and his yards per target has dropped from 11.1 to 6.6. The Green Bay offense has been forced to rely on the pass most of the year as the run blocking has struggled, and Kraft has been pressed into more blocking assignments than fantasy managers would like. But it's also tough to watch Jordan Love, Christian Watson and Matthew Golden all have breakout years while Kraft is left in the station.

I'm ready to draft Kraft proactively next year, a full season removed from the ACL blowout. But we likely have to keep expectations modest for the rest of 2026, as unsatisfying a conclusion as that might be.

These guys are basically returning their ADPs, and sometimes a par is what you need. But the upside is always capped with Fannin and LaPorta because of how talented their teammates are. Cleveland landed two impactful wideouts in the draft, instant-ready talents, and LaPorta will always be fighting Jahmyr Gibbs, Amon-Ra St. Brown and Jameson Williams for opportunities. It will be interesting to see if the Lions and LaPorta can figure out a contract extension; negotiations have been somewhat acrimonious, and for now, they've broken off.

In mid-September, it looked like Kincaid was a screaming right answer. He threw 130 yards at Houston in the opener, then gashed Detroit with a 7-95-1 line. Finally healthy and ready for an increased snap share, we got ready for the moon.

Physical attrition has taken over since then — a rib injury, a sprained ankle. Kincaid hasn't missed any time, but he's seen more than three targets in just one of his last seven games. If you sort through tight end stats starting in Week 3, Kincaid falls to TE24.

Part of this is life with Josh Allen, a superstar who hasn't peppered any teammate with targets since Stefon Diggs left. Consider that in Allen's MVP season two years ago, Mack Hollins's five touchdowns were the most on the team. Injuries are part of the 2026 story in Buffalo, but no one has more than 45 targets or three touchdown catches. Allen and James Cook III are the stat hogs on this Super Bowl contender; everyone else is something of a fringe play.

Age and natural attrition have kept Travis Kelce on the TE1/2 seam, good enough to roster but never an automatic start.

The Rams always have a slew of interesting tight ends, but even with cluster injuries at WR, none of the tight ends have scored consistently. Terrance Ferguson has had trouble staying healthy.

Pat Freiermuth always feels like a preferred streamer, but Aaron Rodgers has started to look his age over the last month.

If T.J. Hockenson can get healthy, maybe Kirk Cousins can cook up some PPR scam down the stretch.

Brenton Strange is never going to be a target hog in Jacksonville, but at least his efficiency metrics are good. The Jaguars have a favorable schedule moving forward.

Mike Gesicki is never a volume bet, but at least he offers touchdown equity on one of the NFL's most bankable passing games.
