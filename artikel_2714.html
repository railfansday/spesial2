Trae Young’s Early-Season Slump Could Become A Major Problem For The Wizards

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The Washington Wizards accumulated some interesting talent ahead of the new campaign. With the arrival of Trae Young and Anthony Davis, the Wizards were on the verge of ushering in a new era led by two superstars. Now, 12 games into the 2026-27 season, however, Washington may need to revisit its plans.

Despite the talent at their disposal, the Washington Wizards have struggled to establish themselves as a playoff team. Following their latest 114-101 loss to the Indiana Pacers, their fifth consecutive one, the Wizards have slid down the Eastern Conference leaderboard, landing up in 12th place with a 4-8 record.

In many ways, calling this situation a disaster may be an exaggeration. Given how impactful Anthony Davis and AJ Dybantsa have been during this period, there are some positive takeaways to build on. However, concerns about Trae Young's form should not be overlooked.

At the time of his arrival, Trae Young was expected to be the floor general who could bring out the best in Washington's players. Given his track record, which includes four All-Star appearances, an All-NBA appearance, and an assists title in the 2024-25 season, Young had the accomplishments to support this. Sadly, this season has started off on a bad note for the 28-year-old.

In 12 appearances thus far, Young is averaging 18.3 points, 3.2 rebounds, and 8.3 assists per game, which seems okay on paper. However, when factoring in his shooting splits of 40.0% from the field and an underwhelming 29.6% from three-point range, there is clearly much to be desired.

With career averages of 24.9 points and 9.7 assists per game, Young is clearly falling short of expectations. However, as a career 35.0% shooter from deep, his current efficiency from the perimeter, especially in light of Washington's 29.8% shooting from beyond the arc (29th in the NBA) as a team, is alarming.

On the face of it, Trae Young has the tools and the ability to be one of the best floor generals in the NBA. Despite his size, Young's creativity as a ball-handler and playmaker is on par with some of the best. When specifically referring to his ability to create scoring chances in pick-and-roll situations, Young is arguably the best in the league.

So what exactly is going wrong for the 28-year-old?

Given the quality of big men in Washington, which includes the likes of Anthony Davis, Alex Sarr, and Deandre Ayton among others, Trae Young should be thriving in pick-and-roll sets. Still, the guard finds himself averaging nearly 3.1 turnovers a game.

Currently, Young averages 11.1 pick-and-roll sets per game (3rd in the NBA). On these plays, he creates only 10.8 points per game on 41.8% FG. More interestingly, Young posts a turnover rate of 15.8%, which is on the lower end of the spectrum, along with a scoring frequency of 42.9%, which falls somewhere in the middle.

Thus, the issue may not lie in Trae Young's playmaking, as much as it does in the Wizards' overall offensive schemes. As a team, Washington ranks among the lowest in field goals attempted, with 85.8. In light of this, their 38.9 field goals made shouldn't come as a surprise.

By the looks of it, the Wizards are struggling to establish an identity. While it is abundantly clear that they aren't a team that gets out in transition or a team that focuses on creating scoring through isolation plays, Washington isn't capitalizing on the opportunities created through their pick-and-roll sets, which is their most commonly used play (21.8 possessions).

In this regard, suggesting that Trae Young is the odd man out simply because he's going through a shooting slump is excessive. Thus, even though his poor efficiency is concerning, the Wizards' coaching staff may see more merit in adjusting their strategies to maximize the production from the remaining players on the roster.
