Washington Commanders Whiteboard: Notes, observations from MNF Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Before the advent of media devices, football coaches at all levels used simple whiteboards to outline plays, map out player positions and explain schemes in real time. Nowadays digital playbooks and tablets have taken over, but coaches still use whiteboards on occasion to impart information quickly and effectively. The following are some quick notes and observations from the Washington Commanders' 22-20 cardiac win over the Cincinnati Bengals on Monday Night Football.

Headed into Monday night's matchup with the Bengals, the odds of the Washington Commanders' defense slowing down Joe Burrow and the NFL's top-ranked passing attack seemed long and, in truth, the Bengals quarterback did throw for 300 yards and 2 touchdowns. The key, however, was that Commanders defensive coordinator Daronte Jones found a way to pressure Burrow from the opening whistle. And while the secondary allowed some plays, they were made by players like Samajae Perine and Mike Gesicki and not Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins.

After getting absolutely torched by Puka Nucua in Week 9, the Washington Commanders took away not one, but two star wide receivers in Monday night's matchup. In fact, Perine's 71 receiving yards almost surpassed both Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins' combined total of 74, their lowest since Week 1 against the Tampa Bay Buccaneers.

Jones crafted a blitz-heavy attack that was effective early and Washington blitzed on 19 dropbacks and caused a 26.3% drop back success rate in those spots. Cornerback Trey Amos and backup Rasul Douglas played plenty of Cover 0 behind their blitz packages but Daronte also switched up the looks, playing both Cover 4 and Cover 6 about half the time. The Commanders utilized plenty of three safety looks in the first half, with Quan Martin playing well in a deep zone. While the cornerbacks had some lapses, they did a good job overall.

Interestingly, free agent addition Odafe Oweh played a season-low 31 snaps, and was once again was missing in action, recording no statistics in the prime time. Instead, Dorrance Armstrong played more defensive snaps (34) and Joshua Josephs played a career-high 24 snaps while recording two quarterback hits. Defensive tackle Tim Settle led the team with 1.5 sacks, 1 TFL and seven tackles (five solo) despite only playing 23 snaps.

When the clock struck zero, the Washington Commanders had held the Bengals' offense to twenty points, the fifth straight game in which the defense limited the opposing offense to 23 points or less. Even without starters Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw, Daronte Jones' squad is starting to mesh, and the Washington Commanders' 18.8 PPG allowed ranks 7th in the league during that same stretch. That includes matchups with two of the NFL's best offenses in San Francisco and Cincinnati and, perhaps not coincidentally, also coincides with the arrival of defensive advisor Steve Wilks.

On the opposite side of the ball, David Blough still does not appear to have an advisor of his own and, unfortunately, the Commanders' offense showed it in the first half. Following a game in which Washington established the run versus the Giants, the rookie offensive coordinator chose to move away from the rushing attack against a defense that was susceptible to it. The offense looked disjointed coming off a 10-day prep period or "mini-bye," and started off the game with four consecutive punts with 28 net yards of offense right around the two-minute warning.

While their performance in the second half was record-breaking, the Commanders' offense can't continue to start slow in every matchup and expect to win going forward. On the plus side, fans should have been happy to see the Commanders finally use a goal line package with Andrew Wylie, Nick Allegretti, and Ben Sinnott at fullback to end the first half.

Blough did call a better game in the second half. Jacory Croskey-Merritt and Austin Ekeler combined for 20 rushing attempts with Ekeler finishing with an impressive 5.9 yards per carry. Jayden Daniels chipped in with the longest run of his career on a 47-yard burst up the middle, and while the game was won on the back of No. 5's heroics, Blough deserves credit for calling the right pass concept not once, but twice. Make sure to note Stefon Diggs "helping" his teammate to get down to preserve the clock.

The Week 8 edition of Whiteboard lamented that Antonio Williams' breakout game hadn't happened yet, but speculated it might be coming in the near future. While his usage has been up, the statistical production hadn't followed. On Monday night the rookie wide receiver cemented his breakout, finishing with nine receptions for 122 yards, with 93 receiving yards coming on the last two catches of the game. Williams was the first Washington rookie with 120-plus receiving yards since Terry McLaurin in Dec. 2019, and his 93 yards receiving in the fourth quarter were the most by a rookie in the fourth quarter of a game since Week 17 of 2020. Most importantly, Williams has won the trust of Jayden Daniels, as evidenced by the anticipation in the game's final throw.

Trust is one of the things Daniels' prioritizes most in his wide receivers and, with that bond established, Williams should start to cash in with his separation and precise route running.

The other rookie to establish trust and confidence is kicker Drew Stevens. After a missing two field goals and three extra points in the first five weeks, the undrafted free agent has established himself as the answer to Washington's kicker woes. While he may have an unorthodox style, Stevens is a player the team will trust after his five field goal performance in prime time.

We asked this same closing question last week, but Monday night's win deserves a repeat inquiry. Following a surprising win by the New York Giants and a dominating one by the Cowboys over the weekend, many Washington fans were discouraged about the chances of the Commanders winning their division. Functionally, Monday night's win helps the team keep pace in the NFC East and keep their hold on a 16% chance to make the playoffs, but last night's performance also tells fans something else. Dan Quinn has his players believing in their ability to play any opponent and keep winning.
