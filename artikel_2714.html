Colts WR Alec Pierce on Achilles injury: 'Felt like I let some people down with my absence'

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

INDIANAPOLIS — When Alec Pierce pulled up in Kansas City with a sharp pain in his heel, the possibility of a catastrophic injury flashed through his mind.

Pierce had been through surgery in March to correct an issue with his heel bone rubbing his Achilles tendon, causing damage and pain, and the pain had lingered through a rehab that took longer than the Colts expected.

Then-Colts general manager Chris Ballard initially said Pierce would only miss one to two weeks of training camp; in reality, Pierce only got two weeks of training camp before playing in the season opener. Pierce returned to the lineup despite the fact that he was still experiencing significant pain in the heel.

"That was a decision I made myself," Pierce said. "I wanted to play. I knew Week 1 was a lofty goal I'd set for my return, and that was a goal I'd set for myself."

When he pulled up a week later at Arrowhead Stadium, Pierce felt a sharp pain in the area.

"I was trying to play through it early in the season," Pierce said. "I ended up, kind of, reinjuring the Achilles where it attaches to the heel."

Pierce's partial tear is not the same as the type of injury suffered by former Colts quarterback Daniel Jones or several other players.

The way Pierce described his injury, it is not the way most NFL fans and analysts understand a torn Achilles tendon to be.

"I think that's the silver lining you can look at," Pierce said. "I injured my Achilles, but I didn't rupture it."

Pierce described the injury he suffered against the Chiefs as a "partially torn" Achilles, or more accurately, an issue at the base of the Achilles, where the tendon attaches to the bone.

If he had ruptured his Achilles or suffered a significant tear, the Colts wide receiver would not be rehabilitating the injury right now and trying to return to the field before the end of the season.

But Pierce was able to avoid a major injury, and the Colts have monitored his recovery closely. Pierce has undergone MRIs every couple of weeks, hoping for a breakthrough that he believes finally came a month ago.

"Rehabbing, doing a lot better," Pierce said. "Getting better each day, every week. I've been really happy with the progress."

A report earlier this week suggested that the Colts had shut Pierce down for the season, but the wide receiver is still trying to play at some point this season.

"Keeping the pain low, using that as kind of the guide for what we can do and what we can't do," Pierce said. "It's been really good, especially the last month, kind of progress-wise. It's started to heal up and get a lot better."

Pierce believes the progress he's making now is different than what he experienced in August.

Back then, Pierce was dealing with significant pain in his Achilles tendon and playing through it, but he is now making progress without pain.

Pierce is being more cautious this time, trying to listen to his body more. A ruptured Achilles tendon, the kind of injury that would cost Pierce a full season, is what he's spent a year trying to avoid. Pierce doesn't want to tempt fate by returning to the field without being confident in the tendon.

"The way I've been progressing, getting better each day, if that turns into next week or the week after that, I'm able to be getting up to higher speeds, sprinting and stuff like that. … If soreness is the only pain I'm feeling, that's kind of my goal, is to get back at some point," Pierce said. "I miss being out there with the guys. I miss being out there to help out, to add to this offense."

Pierce was one of the Colts' priority free-agent signings in the offseason, a deep threat who landed a four-year, $114 million contract that makes him one of the highest-paid players in Indianapolis.

When he hit free agency, Pierce acknowledged that he wanted to play with Daniel Jones, the quarterback who helped Pierce earn that payday with a 1,000-yard season in 2025.

Without Pierce in the lineup, Jones' play collapsed; the Colts released Jones last week.

"The news with Daniel was really tough for me, because he was a guy I felt like I really wanted to be out there and support," Pierce said. "I know he was signing up to play with me. I just felt like I let some people down with my absence. Not being out there's been tough. … The issue is with myself. I feel bad that I couldn't have been out there and helped him, been able to make plays for him."

Pierce is still under contract, one of the few building blocks on offense who can be a factor in the team's rebuild plans.

"I view it as an opportunity, an opportunity to be somebody who's here, with us getting rid of some of our cornerstone-type players, captains, stuff like that, I view it as an opportunity to step into a leadership role, step up and be somebody that's been here, been consistent, been through things," Pierce said. "This league is crazy. Things turn one way so fast, the other way so fast. You never know what can happen."

Whether that's for the end of this season or the start of next.
