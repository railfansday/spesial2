Who Is the Biggest Winner (And Who Got the Biggest Snub) From the 2027 Grammy Nominations?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

This week's Five Burning Questions looks at our takeaways from the recently announced nominations for the 2027 Grammys.

The nominations for the 69th annual Grammy Awards were announced on Monday morning, and the biggest categories include many of the biggest artists from the last year of popular music, including Taylor Swift, Olivia Rodrigo, Bruno Mars, Ella Langley, Olivia Dean, RAYE and Rosalía. All seventh of those stars were nominated for each of record, song and album of the year — Langley also added a fourth general-field nomination for best new artist, and RAYE earned one for songwriter of the year — while Phoebe Bridgers, Tame Impala & JENNIE and Ariana Grande also make appearances in those categories. Some big names thought to potentially be in the mix did not end up making any appearances in the general field, however, including Noah Kahan, Charli xcx, Harry Styles and Madonna. Which artist was the biggest winner from the year’s nominations? And which has the biggest cause for grievance? Billboard staffers answer these questions and more below.

1. Hundreds of artists got their names called as a nominee over the course of the nominations on Monday morning — is there one who you think has the most reason to be excited?

Eric Renner Brown: Honestly? Taylor Swift. After The Tortured Poets Department proved she wasn’t critically bulletproof, The Life of a Showgirl earned her the worst reviews of her career — and not just from critics. But while even some Swifties didn’t mince words when it came to Showgirl, the Recording Academy’s voters thought it was redeemable — enough so that is edged popular new records by fellow past album of the year winners Harry Styles and Kacey Musgraves for a nomination in the category. An added bonus: for the first time in a long time (maybe ever?) Swift will enter these Grammys as an underdog, the role she has always relished.

Hannah Dailey: RAYE. That might be the obvious answer given that she’s the most-nominated artist this year, but it’s so hard not to be ecstatic for her when you know how much she went through – including years of being told “no” and struggling for the chance to even release music in her first record deal – to get to this very well-deserved moment. It’s also such a huge win for indie artists in general!

Lyndsey Havens: To be more specific than “all the women nominated,” I think RAYE has the most reason to be excited, for sure. And not only because she leads the pack with the most 2027 Grammy nominations (11 total) and not only because she ties a record set by Beyoncé’s two years ago for the most nods by a woman in one year, but because of what these nominations represent for RAYE. Her journey to get this kind of recognition has been far from easy or assured and yet, she never lost sight of her artistry. And in fact, becoming an independent artist after parting ways with her former label only helped her refine her sound, vision and goals — and ultimately brought her here.

But at the same time, Rosalía (who has nine nods) should also feel exceptionally proud, because she made an ambitious and risky album, marrying operatic and orchestral influences with pop, and is seeing that pay off. And Phoebe Bridgers should also feel proud, for making and releasing a now Grammy-nominated album that grapples with grief and its complicated emotions without doing any press and without even allowing phones into her shows. So, I guess this brings me back to my first thought: that the nominated women this year should all feel very excited.

Andrew Unterberger: Rosalía blanketing album, record and song of the year is a huge win for her. Getting album of the year recognition for LUX seemed practically predestined, since that album was such a critical phenomenon, a decent-sized commercial hit and the backbone of a hugely successful world tour. But getting song and record nods for “Berghain,” a structurally and melodically challenging song with very little commercial profile? That means voters are really tapped into Rosalía this year.

Christine Werthman: RAYE by a landslide. The British powerhouse heard her name called 11 times, more than any other nominee. The news brought her to tears, as seen in RAYE’s own Instagram post, whose caption she began with “WHAT IS HAPPENING.” Answer: It is all happening! This has got to be a thrilling moment for her and her team.

2. Other artists were denied from categories where their presence was expected, and/or shut out from the nominations entirely — is there one who you think has particular reason to be feeling the hurt from their snub today?

Eric Renner Brown: Charli xcx has to be a little bummed. She crossed into the mainstream in a major way with Brat, which picked up eight Grammy nominations, including two in the general field. Its follow-up, this year’s Music, Fashion, Film, rivaled Brat in quality but didn’t move the zeitgeist — and the Recording Academy proved to have a short memory, only tossing the album one nomination, for best alternative music album. (Even Charli’s soundtrack for the Wuthering Heights soundtrack from this year received more Grammy nominations, with two.) It’s an instructive moment that indicates that Charli will always be something of an outsider when it comes to pop’s frontline superstars.

Hannah Dailey: I’ll say Noah Kahan. He’s another person who’s been working so hard for so long, and who’s been open about feeling like he’s constantly on the outside looking in when it comes to validation from the music industry. His work has clearly paid off in pretty much every commercial way, from topping the Billboard 200 with The Great Divide to headlining an arena/stadium tour this past year — but I’m sure it can sting to feel like your peers don’t recognize that. (That said, at least in the major categories, I can’t honestly say that he definitively deserves to be there over anyone else who did get a nomination, aside from maybe Bruno Mars. Sorry, Noah.)

Lyndsey Havens: Well, the one I feel particularly hurt by is the exclusion of Noah Kahan in the general categories. I do believe Noah released a career defining album with The Great Divide that, similarly to Phoebe’s, contemplates and closely examines complex and sometimes ugly emotions — but sets them to stadium-worthy production. The Great Divide not only had major chart success but genuinely meant a lot to so many listeners — and still does — so it was hard to see that it didn’t quite resonate the same with the Academy.

Andrew Unterberger: Madonna is probably decently happy with her four nominations across pop and dance — but I have to imagine she was also holding out for an album of the year nod for her chart-topping, rapturously received Confessions II set, which she was denied. She’s used to it by now, no doubt; Ray of Light‘s 1999 nod remains her lone career look in the Grammys’ marquee category.

Christine Werthman: I’d feel some type of way if I were Noah Kahan. He’s one of the biggest artists in America, with a chart-topping album and a huge tour, and his only nod was best contemporary folk album for the deluxe version of The Great Divide. It’s not nothing, but it’s pretty shocking not to see him anywhere in the Big 4. What else could the man have done? I’d be hard-pressed to come up with an answer.

3. Which category of nominees do you find the most interesting this Grammy year?

Eric Renner Brown: Interesting in a WTF way: best rock album, where octogenarians (The Rolling Stones) will compete with alternative radio staples (Foo Fighters, Muse, Jack White) and ascendant, anonymous microtonal math-rockers Angine de Poitrine. Taken alongside the similarly peculiar best alternative music album category (Rosalía versus Geese versus Alabama Shakes… sure?), there’s an argument to be made that the whole rock/alterative situation at the Grammys needs to be rethought. But for now, we live in a world where Angine’s Khn and Klek could in all their polka-dotted splendor rob rock legends of Grammys gold — this is what music’s biggest night is all about.

Hannah Dailey: The rock categories. It’s frankly hilarious to see Olivia Rodrigo competing against slightly less mainstream, more alternative acts like Phoebe Bridgers and Geese, and to see them competing against legacy acts such as The Rolling Stones and Bruce Springsteen. They come from such starkly different generations, not to mention the difference in their music and interpretations of the genre. I truly have no idea who will win. I can’t wait.

Lyndsey Havens: I’m pretty interested by song of the year because of the eight nominees, the song with the most writers still only boasts five — in other words, it seems that this year songs that were made by a smaller group were really rewarded. In fact, three of the nominees only boast two writers. (In 2026, one nomination had three writers, two songs had two writers and Doechii’s “Anxiety” was a solo write; the other nominations ranged from six to nine co-writers).

Andrew Unterberger: How about those best dance/electronic recording nominees, huh? John Summit may have his understandable-if-reductive grievances, but you’re not going to find a much better assemblage of generation-spanning artists in any 2026 award category than Madonna, Tame Impala, Fred Again (with Jamie T), Tinashe, Slayyyter and Underscores.

Christine Werthman: Best new artist is always my favorite category because it’s usually got a couple good wildcards in there, and this year did not disappoint. Out of the eight nominees, I’m most surprised and delighted to see Angine de Poitrine, the Womack Sisters and Remi Wolf. (I do wish Audrey Hobert had made the cut though.) I suspect the award will go to not-so-new artist Ella Langley, but it would be cool to see a rock band like Geese take it.

4. As always, the best new artist category is proving controversial for its nominating of artists like Don Toliver and Ella Langley who have already enjoyed significant commercial success before their leveling up in the past Grammy period. Do you think the category would benefit from any particular tightening of its qualification process, or does it benefit more from being so flexible in its definition?

Eric Renner Brown: This is the first year where the Grammys’ loosening of the best new artist criteria took effect — so for better or for worse, this category is headed toward broader qualifications, not narrower ones. I’ve tended to prefer what some both inside and outside of the Recording Academy have suggested, which is renaming the award to reflect that it is recognizing artists who came to prominence in a given year (although even that is subjective, like how anyone who is tapped in to indie rock was aware of the band before its seismic Getting Killed arrived in 2025). It’s a bit of a goofy award, but I don’t find it particularly goofier than other Grammy idiosyncrasies like forcing artists into genre categories where they don’t totally make sense or recognizing bigger but not necessarily better artists in many categories.

Hannah Dailey: I don’t think any change is necessary, honestly. I like that artists who’ve been working hard for years can still get their moment once they finally break through, even if those years leading up to it did yield some level of fame/commercial success. I know that the name “best new artist” is a little contradictory sometimes, but the spirit of it is what matters.

Lyndsey Havens: I think it benefits from being so flexible in its definition. I often check in with my friends who do not work in music and are casual fans for a trusted temperature check of this particular category each year — and to be honest, a handful are still discovering Ella Langley and “Choosin’ Texas” despite its 28-week run atop the Hot 100. Of course, the argument exists that tighter rules would help actual new artists to reach a much wider audience rather quickly, but I do think a lot of people watching the show each year are relatively unfamiliar with everyone in this class of artists. Plus, it benefits the show’s ratings to have “new” artists that double as established hitmakers because those who are familiar will surely tune in — and I do think that is as much as a driver as anything else.

Andrew Unterberger: Yeah, it’s fine. We’ll either end up complaining about the rules being too relaxed or we end up complaining about certain seemingly deserving artists getting snubbed due to the rules not being relaxed enough.

Christine Werthman: It could probably benefit from stricter parameters, but honestly, the flexibility ups the drama, and I find that quite fun. Erase the boundaries and bring on the outrage!

5. Make a prediction — any prediction — about the 2027 Grammys.

Eric Renner Brown: Ella Langley is shut out of the general field. Country has had a rough go of it at the Grammys in recent years, and in each category where she’s nominated she has viable competitors — Olivia Dean, Bruno Mars, RAYE and Rosalía all make total Grammy bait; Olivia Rodrigo, Phoebe Bridgers and Tame Impala have the Cool Lane locked down; Taylor Swift is Taylor Swift. The Recording Academy may recognize the biggest Hot 100 hit in years in the general field, but with competition this stiff, it’s far from assured.

Hannah Dailey: I think Rosalia takes AOTY, Ella Langley takes song and RAYE takes record. Taylor Swift will be one of few people standing while Geese performs, and watching her dance specifically to their music will be awesome.

Lyndsey Havens: Taylor will announce… something. And a hopeful prediction is that Ariana will return to perform at the ceremony for the first time since 2020.

Andrew Unterberger: Whatever hardware she goes home with, Rosalía ends up one of the night’s biggest winners by giving a spellbinding performance that results in her streams doubling over the following week.

Christine Werthman: In my heart, it would go Rosalía for record of the year, Phoebe Bridgers for album of the year, Olivia Rodrigo for song of the year, Geese for best new artist. But I think it’s more likely to be RAYE for record of the year, Olivia Rodrigo for album of the year, Ella Langley for song of the year and best new artist. Or the Grammys could really Grammy and go Bruno Mars for record, Taylor for album, Taylor for song, Ella for best new artist.
