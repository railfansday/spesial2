UK unemployment unexpectedly eases to 4.6%, as government prepares to release Milburn report

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Wage growth falls to to 3.5% in September, as ministers examine how best to get young people into work

The UK unemployment rate has unexpectedly fallen as the government prepares to release a major report on the youth jobs crisis.

The unemployment rate was 4.6% in September, dipping from 4.7% in August, according to the Office for National Statistics. City economists had expected it to remain at 4.7%.

However, wage growth fell to 3.5%, excluding bonuses, in the latest official figures for the three months to the end of September, down from 3.7% in the three months to August.

With inflation also at 3.5% and expected to rise for a fourth consecutive month to 3.7% when figures are released on Wednesday, the average worker faces a fall in real wages growth to zero or even into negative territory.

The figures highlighted how difficult it will be for ministers to improve living standards as they consider the findings of a key report by the former health secretary Alan Milburn examining how to get young people not in employment, education or training (Neet) back into work, released later on Tuesday.

The jobs market has proved difficult for analysts and politicians to assess in recent years. It has been called weak after a dramatic slide in the number of vacancies, which illustrates the reticence of companies to hire new staff.

However, employment has proved resilient, with unemployment falling between July and August from 4.9% to 4.7%, indicating that few employers were ready to cut large numbers of staff.

Ministers are known to be concerned that some large employers, including several banks, have said they intend to use artificial intelligence to reduce their staffing levels.

Economists are divided over whether AI will enhance productivity across all major sectors of the economy – after signs it has boosted GDP – or lead to improvements in certain industries, such as healthcare.

Employer groups have complained that Labour’s increase in national insurance payments, a higher minimum wage and changes to employee rights have increased costs, leading to a fall in the number of advertised vacancies.

The Bank of England increased interests rates earlier this month after a rise in the consumer prices index to 3.5% in September.

It has said that its own surveys showed employers did not expect to increase pay beyond 3.5% to 4% next year, despite the higher level of inflation, and also reported little change looking ahead in their own prices.

However, a growing number of the Bank’s policymakers believe the prospect of high oil and energy prices next year as the Iran war drags on will push inflation towards 5% and force employers to offer higher pay, which will be passed on in higher retail prices.
