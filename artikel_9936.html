Pedagogy is a very indoctrinated field; it's been a few generations since the main focus was on actually transferring knowledge better. [1] Language learning is a good microcosm of this. We have seen a few gains like spaced repetition, but much academic effort even opposes sensible definitions of learning (demonizing things like memorization). Bloom's 2 Sigma effect is cool, and grad students are plentiful.

The last 20 years of my life have been crawling through 19th and early 20th century methods until now where I can memorize a page of text in a few minutes. Designing a good text (and easily searchable) is a hard problem. I wish I could find existing work to save me another 20 years.

If, if you, if you read, if you read out, if you read out loud... A text in such a form, paragraph by paragraph, or trailing with the prior sentence, you can memorize arbitrary texts in almost any language, learning a few new vocabulary pieces a minute on high yield texts (with glosses). If you stop during normal reading to summarize something in speech every 5 minutes... Then in writing on the hour... You will see great progress. Better notations/systems a la APL are very useful here (explaining much of my focus on this.)

Generally, I find people dillydally a lot, spending time on the "mouthfeel" of new words, laughing at how they sound etc. instead of saying them as fast as possible 30 times. Indeed, if you suspend the idea of deep sorrow in your mind and physically repeat "Trübsal" 30, yea 50 times quickly, then another 50 for good measure, easily achievable within a single minute, you will have etched it into your brain with boring trauma. Rote is powerful. Yet I find irrelevant details really help with longer term recall. If you (bad example) want to memorize your country's presidents, you can quickly power through them by rote, but if you sew them together with some anecdotes or look for patterns in how many children they had, whether their wives outlived them etc. you will forget a lot of this "irrelevant" information quickly, yet be remember the main parts well (as well as the more interesting surplus.)
