Casting HBO's Baldur's Gate Series: All 10 BG3 Party Members

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Streaming platforms are in full swing with live-action video game adaptations, and HBO’s upcoming Baldur’s Gate series is among the most exciting currently in development. Reportedly set after the events of Baldur’s Gate 3, the show looks set to bring one of the most iconic settings from Dungeons & Dragons to the small screen. The city of Baldur’s Gate offers enormous possibilities for a live-action D&D TV show, especially since it follows on from the events of the hugely popular video game series.

Details remain scarce, but for fans of Baldur’s Gate 3, a key question is who will play its beloved characters if they return. Many want original performers such as Neil Newbon and Jennifer English to reprise their roles, and the HBO show’s developer, The Last Of Us’s Craig Mazin, has expressed interest in involving the game’s cast (via Deadline). However, his enthusiasm doesn’t guarantee that they’ll definitely return, or that they’ll play the same characters. Fortunately, there are some more than suitable actors to fill the shoes of any familiar party members returning in HBO’s Baldur’s Gate series.

Astarion can make an appalling suggestion sound like the most entertaining idea in the room. He has a unique combination of elegance, malice, and impeccable comic timing, all of which is familiar territory for Tom Hiddleston, whose performance as Loki made the MCU’s trickster god’s wounded pride and theatrical mischief irresistible. He’s also got prior experience playing a vampire thanks to 2013’s Only Lovers Left Alive. Both roles capture something that’s essential to Astarion: the arrogance of immortality being used to mask a sense of desperation and need for a greater purpose than simply living forever.

Tom Hiddleston could deliver Astarion’s most withering remarks with ease while also making the sudden disappearance of that confidence feel just as unsettling in HBO’s Baldur’s Gate TV show as it was in BG3. Any actor playing Astarion requires an understanding of how a charming character can quickly become a terrifying one. Hiddleston has the skills to make Astarion hilarity land without him being reduced to comic relief. He could make BG3’s vampire rogue funny, unsettling, and emotionally complex, all of which would be essential when following in the footsteps of Neil Newbon’s performance in the game.

Ben Barnes has already shown how fun to watch a charming, articulate man with dangerous magical ambitions can be. His time as General Kirigan/The Darkling in Netflix’s Shadow and Bone is a great glimpse of how he might handle Gale’s susceptibility to power in a live-action Baldur’s Gate story, while The Chronicles of Narnia: Prince Caspian demonstrates the sincerity needed for the wizard’s more approachable side. Any actor playing Gale needs to balance both sides for the performance to land.

The greatest challenge if Gale makes an appearance in HBO’s Baldur’s Gate series is ensuring the Wizard of Waterdeep feels approachable despite his intellectual ego. His elaborate explanations and social awkwardness make him considerably warmer than a stereotypical mysterious fantasy sorcerer. Still, this should never mean he comes across as weak or incapable. Roles like Billy Russo in The Punisher prove that Ben Barnes has no issue portraying characters who are charming yet still dangerous, especially those driven by ambitions those around them would struggle to understand.

Karlach should look capable of throwing an enemy across a room without breaking a sweat, and WWE superstar Rhea Ripley would make that immediately believable. While not an experienced actor, her wrestling career supplies the strength, explosive movement, and physical confidence needed for a tiefling barbarian who has survived the Blood War. Just as important is her ability to command attention through posture and facial expressions. Ripley can make a grin feel like a challenge or an invitation, a promising quality for a character whose excitement about being alive is just as memorable as her capacity for destruction.

True, it would be an ambitious move into dramatic acting for Rhea Ripley, especially given how much pain sits beneath Karlach’s enthusiasm. Her infernal engine makes the simple possibility of touching someone devastatingly important, and those scenes demand tenderness as much as rage. Ripley’s suitability for the role lies in the combination of playfulness with being physically intimidating. If she can pull of the more nuanced side of Karlach’s character too, she’ll be more than ideal for HBO’s Baldur’s Gate TV show cast.

Anya Taylor-Joy’s work as the titular road warrior in Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga makes a solid argument for giving her a sword and very little patience in the live-action Baldur’s Gate series. Characters like Furiosa as well as Margot in The Menu are all the evidence needed that she can communicate determination through a fixed stare and a stubborn refusal to show weakness, which is essential for Lae’zel. The Githyanki fighter’s severity comes from a culture that treats strength as survival and obedience as virtue. Taylor-Joy’s filmography leaves little doubt that she could make that conditioning visible in every moment Lae’zel is on-screen.

Anya Taylor-Joy's performance as Beth Harmon in The Queen’s Gambit in particular reveals that she can bring a convincing blend of near-unshakable pride even in the face of profound isolation. Lae’zel’s confidence in her abilities is compelling in BG3 because it survives encounters that dismantle her understanding of the world, and Taylor-Joy would have no issue ensuring this stays intact in a live-action performance. Her penchant for precise delivery would also suit the accidental comedy of Lae’zel’s blunt observations, keeping the humor rooted in the warrior’s absolute conviction that everyone around her is being ridiculous when she finds herself at odds with the room.

When voiced by Jennifer English in BG3, Shadowheart could make even an admission of ignorance or failure feel like somebody else’s embarrassing mistake. The half-elf cleric needs an actress who can deliver that same sarcasm while also making it clear that keeping those around her at a distance takes effort. Her damaged memories and religious conditioning mean that certainty about her place in the world has to be actively maintained. Fallout, Yellowjackets, and Sweetpea star Ella Purnell could ensure the contradictions that define Shadowheart survive the transition to live-action in HBO’s Baldur’s Gate series.

Viewers should want to understand Shadowheart even when she’s doing something atrocious, a quality Ella Purnell’s performance as Rhiannon in Sweetpea hinges on. Her portrayal of Lucy MacLean in Prime Video’s Fallout series shows how effectively she can capture someone discovering that the beliefs shaping her life conceal horrifying truths, another essential Shadowheart trait. Finally, her work as Jackie Taylor in Yellowjackets reveals a capacity to capture wounded pride and defensive cruelty. Together, these roles suggest that there are few actresses out there more suited to playing Shadowheart in live-action.

Regé-Jean Page already has experience in projects based on D&D. Not only that, he’s already played a character who exists in the same world as the city of Baldur’s Gate, the Forgotten Realms. As Xenk Yendar in 2023’s Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves, Page brought effortless authority and wonderfully straight-faced humor to a hero whose defining personality trait is an unwavering commitment to doing good. Wyll would benefit from that same lack of embarrassment about heroism. While his character is far from simple, Wyll’s most immediate trait is that he sincerely wants to protect people.

Playing Xenk is all the proof needed that Page could make his borderline-theatrical confidence appealing while ensuring his willingness to put himself in danger feels sincere rather than sanctimonious. However, Wyll’s story also offers considerably more internal conflict than Xenk Yendar’s. His pact with the dark entity Mizora directly contradicts his noble intentions. Thankfully, Regé-Jean Page’s Simon Basset in Bridgerton showcased an ability to play polished charm that acts as a mask for concealed vulnerability and self-doubt. That experience would suit Wyll’s complicated relationship with his father and the personal costs of his pact with Mizora.

Few actors have a knack for playing approachable yet believable leadership figures like Anson Mount. His Captain Christopher Pike in Star Trek: Strange New Worlds can command a crisis and then make his colleagues feel comfortable around a dinner table. This is an excellent foundation for playing Halsin in HBO’s Baldur’s Gate series. The archdruid’s imposing appearance comes with an unusually gentle temperament. His experience gives him authority, but he rarely needs to flex his superiority. Mount could bring that reassuring confidence to a BG3 character who feels equally believable offering guidance, expressing desire, or becoming terrifying when someone threatens the vulnerable.

Any actor playing Halsin also needs to create a sense that the Baldur’s Gate character has interests beyond whatever emergency currently occupies the party. His devotion to nature, openness about pleasure, and concern for people damaged by conflict make him earthy and approachable despite his age. Anson Mount’s relaxed warmth would help those qualities emerge naturally, while his more rugged work in Hell on Wheels prove he has the action credentials for the druid’s less peaceful moments. With Halsin, the attraction is partly that strength and safety belong together, and Mount could definitely deliver this.

Long before the adventure in Baldur’s Gate 3 begins, Jaheira had already accumulated enough victories and losses to distrust a promising adventure. The veteran half-elf druid and Harper leader needs a cast member in the live-action Baldur’s Gate series who can bring that world weariness to life without turning every exchange into a solemn lesson. Hollywood veteran Michelle Pfeiffer could inarguably bring that authority, and her skills with dry humor would be just as valuable.

Pfeiffer’s Catwoman in Batman Returns is a perfect demonstration of how she can combine sharp wit with emotional baggage without making one quality cancel out the other. Her work as Lamia in 2007’s Stardust also shows how entertaining she can be in a high-fantasy setting. Jaheira needs a more grounded performance than many other Baldur’s Gate characters, with her sharpness honed by years of experience. Pfeiffer could make a cutting observation feel affectionate or devastating depending on who deserves it, while giving the character’s rarer admissions of grief the emotional weight they need.

Put physical powerhouse Dave Bautista beside a tiny hamster and much of Minsc’s visual appeal is already accounted for. The more important qualification is his understanding of how to make an enormous warrior funny through complete sincerity. As Drax in the Guardians of the Galaxy movies, Bautista repeatedly made literal-minded pronouncements entertaining because the character believed them so completely. Minsc needs that same commitment. His conversations with Boo, his miniature giant space hamster companion, should sound perfectly reasonable to him, however bewildered everyone around him becomes.

The BG3 ranger’s uncomplicated commitment to fighting evil would also give Bautista something substantial beneath the comedy. Just like Drax, Minsc’s understanding of a situation may be eccentric, but his love for his friends and determination to protect them are never minor details. Bautista has shown how tenderness can sit within an intimidating physical performance, and he could make those loyalties the reason audiences invest in the character. His size would sell the action immediately; his ability to express hurt or delight with disarming openness could ensure that Minsc remains lovable between the battles, too.

A quiet threat can be considerably more unnerving than a shouted one, and Rebecca Ferguson’s filmography proves she understands that distinction. Her Lady Jessica in Dune and Dune: Part Two combines controlled authority with a willingness to manipulate events toward ruthless ends. Those qualities would translate beautifully to Minthara, a drow paladin whose noble upbringing and brutal education inform nearly every judgment she makes. Ferguson could convey that instinctive expectation of obedience through her voice and stillness, making Minthara formidable before she ever reaches for a weapon.

However, alongside being terrifying, Minthara is also unexpectedly funny. Her devastating observations aren’t meant to be entertaining, but they often account for some of the funniest dialogue in Baldur’s Gate 3. Ferguson’s restrained delivery would suit these moments perfectly. The biggest challenge in the live-action Baldur’s Gate series would be showing the vulnerability beneath. Fortunately, thanks to roles like Juliette in Silo and Anna in The Girl on the Train, there’s every reason to believe that Rebecca Ferguson could do the subtleties of Minthara just as much justice, allowing moments of intimacy to complicate her character without erasing her more immediate, and troubling, qualities.
