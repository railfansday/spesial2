Texas State football grades: Bobcats continue to roll in win over Washington State

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Texas State football trailed 7-0 in the opening minutes of its Saturday contest against Washington State. The Bobcats locked in for the rest of the game, scoring 42 unanswered points and winning 42-14 in the final regular-season conference game.

Despite finishing second in the Pac-12 standings, Texas State will miss the championship game after San Diego State upset Boise State on Saturday night. The Aztecs hold the tiebreaker over the Bobcats, having defeated them 31-29 in October.

Here are the grades from Texas State's win.

Texas State continued to dominate on the ground, racking up 299 yards on 45 attempts for an average of 6.6 yards per attempt. Running back Torrance Burgess Jr. led the backfield with 146 yards and a touchdown on 19 attempts, led by another dominant performance from the offensive line.

Quarterback Brad Jackson threw for 122 yards and a touchdown on 11-of-19 passing. He did most of his damage with his legs. Jackson sliced open the Cougars' defense, rushing for 127 yards and three touchdowns on nine attempts. Wide receiver Beau Sparks led the team with four receptions for 39 yards and a touchdown.

Texas State finished 2-of-3 on fourth downs. The Cougars' second touchdown came when Bobcats running back Jaylen Jenkins fumbled the ball in the fourth quarter. Cougars linebacker Isaiah Hung picked it up and returned it 30 yards for a touchdown. Washington State nearly picked off Jackson in the second quarter.

Texas State defensive lineman Tymere Jackson (56) rushes the passer during the game against Washington State at UFCU Stadium on Saturday, Nov. 21, 2026 in San Marcos, Texas. (Aaron E. Martinez/Austin American-Statesman)

Texas State faced a Washington State team that just put up 73 points on Colorado State last week. The defense gave up an opening touchdown to the Cougars, then proceeded to shut them out for the rest of the game.

The Bobcats sacked Cougars quarterback Owen Eshelman three times and held running back Kirby Vorhees to 4.6 yards per carry, a stark contrast to the 16.4 yards he averaged against the Rams. Edge DonTerry Russell had his best game of the season, recording 1 ½ sacks and leading the defense with six tackles.

Washington State averaged four yards per play and finished with 255 total yards. Texas State defensive back Craig Royal Jr. stood out with three pass breakups.

Linebacker Jordan Sample blocked a punt in the first quarter, which led to a Texas State touchdown. Defensive lineman Phillip Bradford then blocked a field goal in the second quarter, ruining a solid drive by Washington State. Punter Tom Flintoff looks more and more comfortable every week. He punted twice for 88 yards, with his longest kick going 48 yards.

Texas State coach GJ Kinne and offensive coordinator Landon Keopple overwhelmed Washington State. Kinne dipped into his bag on a few plays; notably, a fake screen pass that shifted into a 34-yard quarterback draw in the third quarter.

Kinne said he liked defensive coordinator Will Windham's game plan. Windham challenged the Cougars to run, and the Bobcats stopped them with relatively light boxes, neutering their opponents' passing game.
