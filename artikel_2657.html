Wales must look to back up All Blacks win - Tandy

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Wales head coach Steve Tandy has challenged his side to back up their wonderful win against New Zealand when they host Australia next weekend.

After beating the All Blacks for the first time in 73 years with the 35-28 win at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff, Wales face the final game of the Nations Championship - before the play-offs - against the Wallabies on Saturday evening (20:10 BST).

Tandy says Wales will attempt not to dwell on what they achieved on Saturday against New Zealand as they aim for a third successive home win.

"We have to keep going and we've got a massive game against Australia next weekend," said Tandy.

"It's going to be a different kind of test for us after we have had back-to-back home wins.

"We've gone up another notch or another couple of levels, so it's important we back that up. It's about making sure when we come in that we're ready to work."

After defeat to England, Australia lost 15-12 against Scotland on Sunday, a result which leaves Wales fifth in the northern hemisphere table.

Wales are behind behind Gregor Townsend's side on points difference with Scotland finishing their campaign against Japan.

Tandy's side have won three out of five games in the Nations Championship against Fiji, Japan and New Zealand with defeats in the summer against Argentina and South Africa.

Captain Dewi Lake has highlighted what has changed for this campaign to enable Wales to defeat New Zealand and rise one place in the world rankings to ninth.

"Our skills are better and I think we're a better team which is credit to Steve and his coaches," said Lake.

"I think the belief is also different and the mindset has shifted.

"We've spoken about it a lot and it's probably easy sometimes to say we've changed our mindset and believe in ourselves.

"It feels different. Everybody has come in with a voice and a belief and are challenging each other to get better. Ultimately that's what grows a group quickly."

Tandy believes Wales' young players need to be given the environment to express themselves.

"There's a young group that needed care," Tandy said.

"Those connection pieces we speak about a lot and it's something we pride ourselves on."

He highlighted players like Eddie James, Dan Edwards and Sam Costelow, among those gaining caps and growing in confidence.

"It's trying to give them the freedom to play, because it's a tough place to play in Wales when things are not going right," the Wales head coach added.

"They're young men and we want these players to go out there and play with freedom.

"They need to have that and we believe in that support for them, because sometimes it may be a little bit of a burden.

"We've got to make sure these boys have a safe space."
