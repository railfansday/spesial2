1 extremely early Canucks trade candidate not named Elias Pettersson

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The Vancouver Canucks are not doing well on the ice in 2026-27. However, this was an expected outcome. The Canucks have committed to a full rebuild, which has led to trade rumors around the likes of Elias Pettersson. Rebuilds can be a pain, of course. Ask the Detroit Red Wings how things are going right now.

Vancouver is currently dead last in the NHL at this time. They are the only team in the league with fewer than 15 points as of November 28th. They have lost three games in a row and seven of their last 10. It's not a fun time to be a Canucks fan.

A lot of speculation this season will center on the core veterans. Pettersson has seemingly found his stride again, producing 23 points in his first 24 games this season. He is going to dominate Canucks trade rumors throughout the entirety of the season.

He won't be the only one mentioned, though. Brock Boeser leads the team in points. Jake DeBrusk has a solid track record of offensive success. Paul Cotter could be a decent depth piece as well.

There are a lot of possibilities on the trade market for Vancouver this season. And they don't need to rush things, either. A lot of their prime trade candidates are locked up long term. They can play the waiting game a bit before making their move.

In saying this, there is one player who should be considered for a trade this season. He is one of the team's stars, but he is also a valuable asset. It would be wise for Vancouver to at least gauge his market this year.

In 2023, the Canucks took a big swing. They traded a first-round pick and a second-round pick to the Red Wings for Filip Hronek. It was a huge move at the time. Hronek showed promise as an offensive defenseman. He was also in his mid-20s and was about to play next to Quinn Hughes.

Suffice it to say, things didn't work out in the long term, at least as far as the team's success is concerned. Hughes was traded by the Canucks last winter, and Vancouver entered its rebuild after one playoff run. Hronek has held up his end of the bargain, though.

In 255 games with Vancouver, he has scored 19 goals and 148 points. He is producing at a higher points-per-game rate than he did in Detroit, and in 50 fewer games. His offensive ability is the same, if not better, than it was when he was with the Red Wings.

Why would Vancouver trade him then? It's simple. Hronek is about to enter his 30s, and he is on a long-term contract. The Canucks are unlikely to contend anytime soon. It would be well worth trading him for future assets that aid the rebuild.

Hronek is a clear top-four defenseman who can spark his team from the blueline. There are a number of contenders who would love to have someone like him on the back end. In a rising salary cap environment, there are also a number of teams that can fit him into their books.

The Canucks defenseman controls his own destiny, at least for this season. He has a full no-movement clause through 2027-28. Vancouver should at least start doing their due diligence now, though. If something comes across that helps all parties, that would be quite beneficial.
