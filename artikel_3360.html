Stephen King's 8-Episode Sci-Fi Masterpiece Delivers A 10/10 Time Travel Story

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It is hard for sci-fi writers to get time travel right, thanks to its many contradictions and paradoxes, but one Stephen King adaptation released by Hulu in 2016 managed this feat. There is a reason that Rick and Morty’s titular scientific super genius had a strict “No Time Travel” rule when it came to the anarchic show’s weekly animated misadventures. Even in a comedic context, fictional time travel is always tough to follow and tougher to explain.

Where a dystopian sci-fi show like Station Eleven can flash back and forth between before and after the end of the world with panache, any story where the main characters themselves travel through time opens itself up to all manner of tricky questions. Many great screen depictions of sci-fi, like Netflix’s three-season masterpiece Dark, have constructed elaborate systems to keep track of intersecting realities and overlapping timelines.

However, horror legend and Pennywise creator Stephen King made this achievement look easy with one 2016 adaptation. Based on the author’s 2011 novel of the same name, 11.22.63 sees the English teacher Jake Epping use his friend Al Templeton’s experimental time travel machine to return to 1960 in the hopes of preventing John F. Kennedy’s infamous assassination on the titular fateful date.

This sounds like a fairly straightforward plot, but, like any time travel story, 11.22.63 inevitably introduces some twists and turns into the plan. Primarily, Epping discovers that he has become unexpectedly attached to his new life in the swinging ‘60s, and he is subsequently unsure whether he wants to go through with the plan since it will mean returning to his own present-day reality.

Disgraced actor James Franco plays Epping, while American Beauty supporting star Chris Cooper plays his conflicted friend Templeton. The uncertainty that 11.22.63’s protagonists face is complicated by characters like Lucy Fry’s Marina Oswald, George MacKay’s Bill Turcotte, and Daniel Webber’s Lee Harvey Oswald but, fortunately, this is the only part of 11.22.63’s story that feels uncertain and complicated.

Although he is best known as a legendary horror author thanks to hit books like Carrie, It, and The Shining, King also penned the iconic fantasy saga The Dark Tower and has written many crime, mystery, and thriller novels in his time. In 11.22.63’s elegant time travel story, the writer proves he is also a master of this infamously tricky sci-fi subgenre, too. 11.22.63’s handling of time travel finds a perfect balance between intricacy and simplicity.

Often, time travel stories either become extremely convoluted and technical, as seen in director Shane Carruth’s infamously complex indie movie Primer, or extremely simplistic and cavalier. This approach is exemplified by the Back to the Future trilogy, a set of movies that offer viewers a ripping good story that makes absolutely no sense in terms of its in-universe time travel mechanics.

11.22.63 finds a perfect balance between these two extremes, with a fictional take on time travel that feels rooted in recognizable reality, but never becomes complicated enough to overshadow the story’s emotional core. The show’s success emboldened HBOMax’s later hit King adaptation It: Welcome to Derry to add a time travel twist to Pennywise’s story in its season 1 finale, a clever addition that made the shared universe of King’s stories feel more immersive and lived-in than ever before.

It is always risky for a creator to establish a shared universe containing many of their works and characters, and it is even riskier when some of the stories in this universe bring time travel into the mix. However, Hulu’s confident adaptation of 11.22.63 proved that, despite being best known as a horror maestro, King can be trusted with sci-fi’s most infamously slippery trope.
