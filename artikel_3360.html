‘The Hunting Wives’ Season 1 Recap: What To Remember Before Netflix’s Steamy Thriller Returns

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Netflix’s The Hunting Wives is one of the streamer's most addictive series: a soapy, raunchy romp that is delightfully unhinged from start to finish. Grounded in the sizzling chemistry between stars Malin Akerman and Brittany Snow, the show is steamy, sexy, and wildly entertaining, the sort of unashamed guilty pleasure TV that was made for the “just one more episode” binge model.

Based on the novel of the same name by bestselling author May Cobb, the series exploded in popularity following its debut last summer, racking up streaming minutes and TikTok edits while thrilling viewers with its nonstop plot twists. The story revolves around a group of wealthy Texas housewives who sport designer fashions and are all heavily armed. They’re at the center of everything in their small conservative town of Maple Brook, Texas, whether it's passing judgment from the pews at church, throwing back margaritas at a local honky tonk, or having ill-advised affairs.

These women are messy with a capital M, and that’s before they find themselves at the center of a murder investigation. Here's a quick recap of everything you need to remember about The Hunting Wives’ twisty first season before Season 2 premieres.

The series follows Tesla-driving, East Coast liberal transplant Sophie O’Neill (Snow), who moves to Maple Brook with her family for a fresh start after a devastating personal trauma. Her husband Graham (Evan Jonigkeit) lands a job with Jed Banks (Dermot Mulroney), a wealthy oil businessman who is being courted to run for governor. Sophie hits it off immediately with his wife, Margo (Akerman), bonding over prescription drugs and anxiety in a fundraiser bathroom.

Drawn into the lavish world of the Texas elite, Sophie quickly learns that things are much more complicated (and salacious) than they initially appear. Margo’s essentially open marriage means that she’s not only sexually involved with her BFF Callie (Jaime Ray Newman), but she’s also secretly having an affair with her other BFF Jill’s (Katie Lowes) teenage son, Brad (George Ferrier). (Anything goes in Texas!) And when Brad’s high school girlfriend Abby (Madison Wolfe) turns up dead after a night of drinking and partying at Margo’s forest cabin — the same night Sophie’s gun goes missing — everyone’s a suspect, including Sophie herself.

Abby’s murder is the primary narrative drive of The Hunting Wives' first season, pushing Margo and Sophie closer together as they scramble to cover up their night of blackout-drunk escapades with a pair of high school boys. Along the way, the two ultimately give in to the attraction that’s been simmering between them since the series’ first episode and become intimate, not only sharing steamy hook-ups, but building a real emotional bond.

Unfortunately, all of that is shattered when Sophie realizes that Margo actually killed Abby. The teen found out that Margo not only had an affair with Brad, but had gotten pregnant and had an abortion. Abby's threats to expose Margo's secrets led to Margo shooting her with Sophie’s gun. Margo ultimately frames Brad’s mother, Jill, for the crime, leading to a standoff in the Thompson home that results in several other deaths, including Abby’s mother, Starr (Chrissy Metz), and Jill herself. (It is easier for the police to blame a dead woman, after all.)

Although Jill’s death — and the fact that the police now believe she's responsible for Abby's murder — results in Sophie being released from prison, it’s far from the end of her problems. Margo tells her volatile brother Kyle (Michael Aaron Milligan), who has a substance abuse problem and has frequent run-ins with the law, that her friend/ex/situationship knows the truth about what she did.

After a lifetime of playing the protective big brother, Kyle takes things into his own hands, running Sophie off a deserted road and trying to intimidate her. Terrified, Sophie runs Kyle over with her car before dragging his body to a nearby quarry to hide the evidence. In the season’s final moments, Sophie answers a call from Kyle’s phone — it’s Margo, asking if he “took care” of Sophie.

If The Hunting Wives’ first season is anything to go by, we can expect even bigger, wilder swings in Season 2. Both Margo and Sophie may technically still be free, but they also have literal bodies in their closets, and in a town as small as Maple Brook, it seems unlikely that those secrets will stay buried forever.

It’s also unlikely that the two women will stay at odds for too long. After all, their relationship is the emotional engine that propels the series, and things feel very unfinished between them. Sure, Sophie knows the truth about Abby Jackson’s death, but now that she’s a murderer herself, it’s basically impossible for her to go to the police. And while Margo’s currently in the dark about Kyle’s fate, even if/when she finds out, would she even be able to tell anyone, given Sophie’s knowledge of her own crimes? (We won’t talk about what’s going to happen when Margo realizes Kyle’s dead at her girlfriend’s hand, er, wheel...) But whatever happens, Season 2 is guaranteed to be a wild ride in more ways than one.

The Hunting Wives Season 2 premieres November 26 on Netflix.
