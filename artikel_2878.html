Knicks 112, Raptors 108 (OT): “This was a low key huge win”

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

There aren't really many so-called "must-win games" during a basketball season. Technically, you only need to win elimination games, but the meaning of the term has broadened quite a bit over time and gets thrown around plenty over the course of the year for every team. Sometimes you even hear it after an early season contest, in a seemingly meaningless Sunday night—in a different country, to boot—and you wonder if we aren't getting ahead of ourselves here. We probably are, but no matter what, it sure beats the alternative: it's better to root for a team that we expect to win than one we expect to lose.

JA311 must have been mindful of this when he posted about the "low key huge win" in Russell's Postgame Thread, implying a reality that Knicks fans didn't want to inhabit this morning. It's a world where we would have had to accept that the shorthanded, slumping Raptors had snapped their 14-game losing-streak to our Knickerbockers, thus making the orange-and-blue ballers doubt their very existence.

If that were the case, New York would have fallen below .500 again after seemingly righting the ship against some Division-II level competition, only to crash back to earth when semi-competent NBA teams appeared before them. Yeah, luckily we don't have to deal with that feeling today and instead can bask in the glory of a hard-fought overtime victory on the road. Awesome, isn't it?

It would be even sweeter if, for the first forty minutes of the game, the Knicks hadn't put a serious scare in their faithful like a Michael Myers showing up at your front door on Halloween.

Some of the mistakes the team made last night put us at a loss for words, as when the easiest of passes rolled out of bounds, Austrian Jakob Poeltl skiied around them in the pick-and-roll as if he was Marcel Hirscher jetting down a slope, and Jamal Shead all of a sudden looked like the third Splash Brother. Sure, OG Anunoby was awesome in many facets of the game against his former team, but his dribble drives felt like you were watching your little one on a playground, climbing on a jungle gym that's only suited for much older kids. It feels like fingernails on a chalkboard sometimes and usually doesn't end well. Plus, in OG's case, I think we are beyond the "let him discover himself" phase.

Josh Hart was the hardest Knick to watch yesterday, and that is putting it mildly. Mike Brown certainly agreed, as Hart was pulled in the third quarter and never returned. The head coach probably also didn't like how the Dinos dominated the mismatch in the paint, which at times felt like the T-Rex dealing with that poor, sleazy lawyer in the first Jurrassic Park movie (Toronto, as has become a trend for New York, outscored the good guys 52-36 in the painted area). As the smallish Knicks were lured inside, more and more threes started falling on them like rain on a greyish Autumn day. But to say it with the words of Mr. Ian Malcolm: "Life, uh, finds a way." Or your beloved Knicks, for that matter.

Down 14 in the fourth quarter, the Knicks dug deep and found, among other things, James Wiseman (who was solid), their pride, and then that patented extra gear we've all grown accustomed to over the last 12 months. Karl-Anthony Towns dominated his matchup as he should, the defense buckled down (helped by a strong showing of Mikal Bridges out of all people), and Landry Shamet screamed: "I made fire!" Jalen Brunson, indifferent for most of the night, rose to the occasion and made big-time decision after big-time decision. Did you miss the word shot here? That's the amazing part of Brunson's late-game heroics yesterday, as he made his biggest impact by using his gravity to free up everyone around him. Of course, the captain wouldn't be the captain if he hadn't had a few massive jumpers himself, the biggest one being a tie-breaking dagger from the left corner in overtime.

The fact that the Knicks needed to come back and win in overtime can be attributed to some out-of-body shooting luck by Scottie Barnes (who we all respect and wish well, but come on) and a debatable strategic decision by Mike Brown. I can understand him putting OG on former Knick Immanuel Quickley for the final possession, as nobody would have expected Barnes and his 20-something-percent downtown average to take the last top-of-the-key jumper. He did, though, but luckily we got a replay of that scenario a few minutes later with Anunoby defending the big Raptors forward this time around. It ended as it should: Barnes barely clanking the tying jumper off the backboard, and the oversized lady clearing her throat. Seconds later the Knicks were 7-6, with plenty to think about—but also a lot to be proud of. Among other things, there's the never-say-die attitude, their calmness in tight situations, and a knack for role players to show up on any given night looking anything but scrub subs.

And then there is the quality of pulling out a must-win game that really wasn't one. Or maybe it was? We'll see down the line, I guess.
