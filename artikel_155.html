Dolores Huerta honored in ceremony at California state capitol

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Labor leader was remembered for her advocacy and co-founding union that would become United Farm Workers

California officials paid tribute to Dolores Huerta, one of the nation’s most influential labor leaders, civil rights icons and feminist activists, at a memorial ceremony Monday at the state capitol.

They remembered her as a relentless advocate for a more just and fair world. Huerta co-founded a union that became the United Farm Workers with César Chávez and made famous her rallying cry, “Sí, se puede!” – which translates to “Yes, it can be done!”

Huerta, 96, died of cancer at her home in Bakersfield, California, last month. A celebration of life, with an interfaith memorial service, will be held in her hometown on Friday.

Robert Rivas, the Democratic assembly speaker, had called Huerta a family friend and mentor. Rivas’ grandfather, Servando Flores, moved from Mexico to California in the 1960s and went on to organize fellow farmworkers alongside Huerta.

“She is the mother of our Latino civil rights movement,” Rivas said. “Her place in the history of our state and nation is enduring.”

Rivas stood beside fellow Democrats Xavier Becerra, the governor-elect, Monique Limón, the senate president pro tempore, and Nancy Pelosi, the former US House speaker, in the Ccapitol rotunda as they spoke about Huerta during an invitation-only ceremony. There were performances of songs that included We Shall Overcome and Put a Little Love in Your Heart before the space was opened to the public.

“As we honor one of the towering figures of our time, we make her this solemn promise: the march goes on,” said Limón, who is serving as acting governor while Gavin Newsom, the governor, is out of state.

Before the event began, Huerta’s casket was transported to the capitol by a hearse adorned with flags reading “Sí, se puede!” Hundreds of people, including union leaders, politicians and members of the public, followed behind, many with marigolds in hand.

Huerta was born in New Mexico in 1930 and raised in California’s farm-rich San Joaquin valley. She became an elementary school teacher before turning her focus to the plight of farmworkers and teaming up with Chávez to create the National Farm Workers Association in 1962, which became United Farm Workers.

Then president Barack Obama, who borrowed her rallying cry for his campaign slogan, “Yes we can,” awarded her with the nation’s highest civilian honor, the Presidential Medal of Freedom, in 2012. Well into her 90s, Huerta was a familiar face at news conferences and rallies in Sacramento, where she advocated for the rights of workers, women, voters and others.

She revealed this year that she was sexually abused by Chávez, leading to the birth of two children, a secret she kept for 60 years.
