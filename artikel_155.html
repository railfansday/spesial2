Mary Peltola beats Republican Dan Sullivan in Alaska to give Democrats 56 Senate seats

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Republican incumbent beaten in race dominated by controversy over second candidate named Dan Sullivan

Democrat Mary Peltola will be Alaska’s next senator after beating the Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan, with the result called two weeks on from midterm elections which saw Donald Trump’s party lose ground across the country.

Peltola, a former US House lawmaker, is the first Democrat the state has elected to the Senate since 2008. Her victory comes just two years after Alaska voters unseated her while supporting Trump by a double-digit margin.

“From Juneau to Utqiagvik, the message from Alaskans is clear: the status quo in DC is not working for Alaska,” Peltola said in a statement on Tuesday.

“As a lifelong Alaskan who has dedicated my life to serving our state, there is no greater honor than having Alaskans’ support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs, and put Alaska first,” she said. “As our next United States Senator, I will stand up to anyone to take on the rigged system in DC and deliver results for Alaska’s working families.”

Alaska was the last Senate race remaining to be called, and with Peltola’s victory, Democrats will control 56 seats in the upper chamber, after winning campaigns in conservative states including South Carolina, Texas, Kansas and Mississippi. The party gained nine Senate seats in the midterms, a sweeping victory that will put them decisively back in the majority beginning next year, when they are expected to embark on an aggressive campaign of both lawmaking and investigating.

Peltola, who is Yup’ik, will join Minnesota senator-elect Peggy Flanagan, a member of the White Earth band of Ojibwe, as the first two Native American women to serve in the US Senate.

Peltola faced significant controversy on the campaign trail, after the New York Times reported that her staffers were involved in a scheme to recruit another Republican challenger, also named Dan Sullivan, to appear on the ballot.

Peltola’s campaign had denied any involvement in encouraging the second Sullivan’s run, and after state election officials tried to remove him, Alaska’s supreme court in June ruled he be allowed to appear on the ballot. Trump called for Peltola to be prosecuted over the apparent ruse, and Alaska’s top federal prosecutor announced that criminal and civil inquiries were under way, while the state’s governor separately announced plans to open a criminal investigation into the alleged scheme.

Under Alaska’s voting system, the top four candidates from the nonpartisan primary advance to the November general election. Voters then rank the candidates in order of preference. If no candidate receives more than 50% on the initial round of counting, the last-place finisher is eliminated and that candidate’s supporters will have their second-choice votes counted.

With 99% of ballots counted, Peltola had secured 50.1% of the vote, according to the Associated Press, while the incumbent senator received 45.8%. The other Sullivan had received just 1.7% while a third Republican, Gerald Heikes, received 2.1%.

Though Alaska is a Republican stronghold in presidential years, Peltola was viewed as a uniquely potent challenger to Sullivan, a two-term senator first elected in 2014 after defeating a Democrat.

A former state legislator who grew up in small communities along the Kuskokwim River in Alaska’s interior, Peltola rose to prominence in 2022, when she won a special election to replace the longtime Republican at-large congressman Don Young following his sudden death, and became the first Alaska native elected to Congress.

Peltola won a full term later that year, but lost re-election to Republican Nick Begich in 2024

In the race against Sullivan, Peltola’s fortunes appeared to rise as Alaskans grew frustrated with the price increases brought on by Trump’s tariffs and his decision to attack Iran earlier this year.

The state is the largest in the union, and dotted with cities and villages that are disconnected from the road system, and thus particularly hard-hit by the increases in oil prices the war caused. Gas prices in some interior Alaska communities jumped into the double-digits per gallon, while the rise in the price of diesel affected everything from groceries to alcohol, since many communities rely on shipments by sea.

Alaska is nonetheless a right-leaning state heavily reliant on its fishing and oil and gas industries. On the campaign trail, Peltola adopted the slogan “fish, family, freedom” to highlight how she would stand up for the state’s unique interests in Washington DC.

She has cast herself as an advocate for expanding oil production in the state, and addressing the issue of bycatch, or the accidental fishing of unwanted species, which some Alaskans believe is to blame for declining salmon runs.

Late in her campaign, Peltola posted a photograph of herself on Instagram from 1995, wearing the winner’s sash from the Miss National Congress of American Indians pageant. “Then and now, Alaska is at the heart of who I am,” she wrote.

Sullivan, meanwhile, stuck to Trump’s side, even as the president’s popularity declined nationwide, and especially in Alaska, according to some surveys. He did not join other vulnerable Republicans in urging an end to the war with Iran, while accusing Peltola of disrespecting voters after a report emerged that she had used a hompohobic slur and abused her staff.
