The Junior Developer Comeback: Seniors will struggle to get a job?

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Whether you're trying to become a developer, a designer, or a data analyst, the entry-level door feels like it's closing. Seniors, on the other hand, are mostly getting hired or retained.

I think that's about to reverse. Not this year, but within the next five to ten years, I believe juniors will do very well, and seniors will be the ones fighting for jobs.

The current state of AI is good, but it isn't senior-level good.

It can write code, generate designs, and crunch data. But it still needs someone experienced to guide it, catch its mistakes, and make the calls that actually matter. That's exactly what a senior does.

That's the world we're in right now, and it explains why the junior market feels so rough.

By then, AI will probably be senior-level good. Not "impressive for a tool," but genuinely capable of senior-level work.

Here's the part that matters: someone still has to operate it.

Even with extremely capable AI, people will need some technical skill to use it well. Not a lot, but some. You need enough understanding to describe what you want, to judge whether the output is right, and to know when something is off.

Most people won't have spent time learning graphic design, web development, or data analysis. The people who did will have a real edge, because they understand the fundamentals well enough to operate the AI properly.

If AI closes the gap in raw capability, then the senior's main advantage, years of hard-won technical expertise, matters a lot less. The junior with a bit of technical knowledge gets the same job done just as well.

The senior costs more and delivers about the same result.

From a business perspective, the decision becomes easy. Why pay more for a result you can get for less? The incentive to hire a senior drops, and hiring a junior with some technical knowledge starts to make far more sense.

If you're a junior: Don't give up on learning the fundamentals. The market is hard right now, but the technical foundation you build is the thing that could set you apart later. Learn the craft, and learn to work with AI, not against it.

If you're a senior: Don't rely on years of experience alone. Lean into what's hardest to automate, such as decision-making, system thinking, and being the person who can be trusted with outcomes, not just output.

Today's market rewards seniors because AI isn't senior-level yet. Tomorrow's market might reward cheaper people who can operate a senior-level AI.

The people who win won't necessarily be the most experienced. They'll be the ones who have just enough technical skill to get the most out of the tools.

I could be completely off, so I'd love to hear what you think.
