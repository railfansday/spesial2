Palmetto Publishing Releases Catherine M. Wehlburg's New Book on Higher Education Strategic Planning

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Drawing on her work as a university president, Wehlburg presents a project-based model that replaces shelved multiyear plans with defined priorities, named owners, and measurable outcomes.

CHARLESTON, S.C., Nov. 24, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Palmetto Publishing has released Project-Based Strategic Planning, a new book by Catherine M. Wehlburg, Ph.D. The book addresses a familiar condition in colleges and universities: strategic plans that are written, approved, posted online, and then set aside. Wehlburg argues the problem is structural rather than a lack of good intentions, and proposes a different way to organize institutional strategy.

The book opens with a bookcase. When Wehlburg became president of Athens State University, she found binders holding the strategic plans of earlier administrations, most of them untouched for years. The documents were not careless. They contained vision statements, ambitious goals, and detailed action steps. What they lacked was evidence of having shaped a decision. Conversations with faculty and staff confirmed what the dust suggested. From that starting point, the book moves through diagnosis, framework design, project construction, metrics, leadership, campus engagement, resistance, and the work of sustaining momentum.

Wehlburg's model replaces the multiyear document with three to five clearly defined institutional priorities. Each priority is advanced by discrete projects with defined scope, named owners, realistic timelines, and metrics that connect project outcomes to framework-level measures. As one project concludes, another begins. The approach distributes ownership across faculty and staff rather than concentrating it at the top, and treats accreditation and assessment reporting as a byproduct of real work instead of a parallel administrative burden.

Catherine M. Wehlburg, Ph.D. stated, "Higher education has no shortage of strategic plans. What we often lack is a way to turn those plans into meaningful, measurable progress. Too often, institutions invest enormous time and energy creating ambitious plans that ultimately become documents rather than drivers of change. I wrote Project-Based Strategic Planning to challenge that approach and offer a practical alternativeâone that moves strategy from aspiration to action, creates shared accountability, and builds continuous momentum. Strategic planning shouldn't be something we finish every five years. It should be how we lead, innovate, and improve every day."

The book is written for university presidents, provosts, deans, and institutional effectiveness directors, most of whom hold advanced degrees and follow accreditation guidance and higher education commentary alongside books on planning and assessment. It arrives as institutions face enrollment pressure, budget constraints, and increased scrutiny of outcomes. Reflection questions close each chapter. The appendices supply a framework template, a readiness assessment, and a guide to selecting indicators that inform decisions rather than decorate reports. Wehlburg's career spans decades in academic leadership, assessment, and institutional effectiveness.

Project-Based Strategic Planning is available for purchase online at Amazon.com and BarnesandNoble.com. For more information about the author, please visit any of their social media platforms.

About the Author: When Catherine M. Wehlburg, Ph.D., began her presidency at Athens State University, one of the first things she found was a bookcase of binders holding the institution's previous strategic plans, most of them unopened for years. That bookcase became the starting point for Project-Based Strategic Planning. Conversations with faculty and staff confirmed what the dust suggested: the plans had been crafted with care, approved with ceremony, and then quietly set aside while the daily work of the university went on without them.

Her career in higher education spans decades and has moved through department chair, dean, and provost roles before the presidency, with a consistent through line in assessment and institutional effectiveness. She is a nationally recognized voice in that field, and her work has centered on a single stubborn question: how institutions connect what they say they value to what they can actually demonstrate. That question shaped her approach to accreditation and compliance, to measurement, and eventually to planning itself. The framework in this book grew out of practice rather than theory, tested in the ordinary conditions of institutional life, including budget cycles, shared governance, leadership turnover, and the skepticism that follows plans that have not delivered.

She credits much of her thinking to colleagues at Athens State and at other institutions who have argued with her, candidly and at length, about what makes a strategic plan worth the time it takes. She writes with evident gratitude for her daughter, Brooke Krasowsky, whose steady presence reminds her that meaningful work is always connected to the people we love.

She can be found on LinkedIn and Facebook.
