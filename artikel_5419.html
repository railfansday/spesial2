Fed Chair Kevin Warsh warned that AI could trigger job losses or a stock bust if it disappoints

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Warsh outlined three illustrative scenarios for super intelligence, from broad gains to a slowdown, and said they are not forecasts

(Tierney L. Cross/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh outlined three scenarios on Monday for how AI could affect the U.S. economy, including one in which job losses rise and another in which the technology disappoints and stock valuations fall.

Warsh spoke at the opening of a conference on the Fed's Productivity and Jobs Task Force, hosted by the Federal Reserve Board and the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco. He said Board staff have begun evaluating contingencies using model-based frameworks. He described the scenarios as illustrative, not forecasts, and said many other outcomes are possible.

In the first scenario, AI lifts productivity, and the benefits are spread widely among workers and those who own capital. Real GDP growth would run durably higher than its historical norm, and the U.S. would remain near full employment.

In the second, an AI-driven expansion brings substantial labor market disruption as technology displaces some jobs rather than complementing them. GDP growth would rise above its long-term average, but widespread automation combined with labor market frictions could push the jobless rate above levels consistent with full employment.

In the third, AI disappoints. "After significant capital investment and stock market gains, expected returns could turn down and equity valuations could fall, leading to an economic slowdown and a more notable rise in unemployment," Warsh said. "In time, the economy would recover, but without the productivity gains."

Warsh said productivity growth has been "reasonably strong" in recent quarters but "hasn't yet broken out definitively and structurally to the upside." He said the Fed's role on AI is "neither advocacy nor opposition," and that broad uptake of the technology will reshape the economy considerably, bearing on monetary policy "down the road." Warsh referred to the technology as "super intelligence," a term President Donald Trump required federal agencies to use in a September executive order.

Warsh unveiled five working groups in June, each headed by specialists from outside the Fed, according to Bloomberg. He has said the groups are expected to deliver their findings near year-end, and the Federal Open Market Committee would then weigh them.

The conference's final session is scheduled to be a fireside chat with Warsh and task force leader Marc Andreessen, co-founder of Andreessen Horowitz.
