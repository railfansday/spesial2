Dan Ives Just Delivered Great News for AI Stocks. Here’s the Part That Matters.

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The sell-off has been brutal, but demand numbers from Nvidia, Google Cloud, and Anthropic’s IPO filing tell a different story.

By Patrick Sanders – Nov 20, 2026 at 1:19PM EST

Artificial intelligence stocks are taking an absolute beating this month. The Nasdaq-100 is down 13% since the beginning of November, and the losses seem to be accelerating. Nvidia (NVDA -10.83%) dropped 37%, Micron Technology (MU -12.64%) is off by 33%, and Alphabet (GOOG -0.64%) (GOOGL -0.44%) is down 20%.

It hasn't been pretty. But analyst Dan Ives, a partner at Yorkville Ives, isn't abandoning his bullish outlook. In a post on X on Friday, Ives identified seven factors contributing to "a perfect storm" that's driving the sell-off.

And here's the most important part of his message: "The $4 trillion AI build-out is still happening," he wrote, "and our tech thesis into 2027 remains."

That's key -- particularly for investors who are nervous about their portfolios right now. And if Ives is right, this is also an opportunity to buy tech stocks at a discount.

In his social media post, Ives said the Democrats' midterm election victory is creating uncertainty for AI stocks, as investors worry that Washington will impose tougher restrictions on AI development and data center construction.

He also singled out the sudden resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang "in his black eye moment." Huang was forced out after the board began investigating his affair with a Chinese businesswoman who U.S. intelligence officials believe was spying for Beijing.

Meanwhile, regulatory concerns involving China, persistent inflation, and the prospect of additional Federal Reserve interest rate increases have weighed on technology stocks. Ives also pointed to forced selling and investment funds suffering losses that have reduced their ability to buy shares.

Ives even singled out Anthropic's (ANT -9.54%) successful IPO on Nov. 19, speculating that investors shifted money out of tech stocks and into the newly public AI company.

A perfect storm, indeed. But here's the thing -- none of those factors mean that customers are slowing their AI spending. And the demand for computing capacity remains sky-high.

That's why analysts, including Ives, have projected that capital expenditures and AI spending will reach $4 trillion.

Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in revenue in its fiscal third quarter of 2027, up 103% from a year ago. And data center revenue was $107.6 billion, up 110%. But perhaps the most important thing is that Nvidia increased its forecast for fiscal 2028 revenue growth from 70% to 75%. And notably, that outlook came in the first earnings report since Huang's departure. Management expects demand to accelerate even faster.

Alphabet is seeing similar strength in cloud computing. Google Cloud revenue in the third quarter was $30.7 billion, up 103% from a year ago. Operating income more than tripled to $11.1 billion, and its backlog reached $561 billion.

Those figures suggest businesses are still signing contracts for computing infrastructure, even as investors question whether AI spending has gone too far.

And Anthropic's IPO prospectus offers another example. The Claude developer has committed to spending at least $110 billion with Amazon (AMZN -3.46%) and $121.3 billion with Google under long-term cloud computing arrangements.

That's an enormous amount of planned infrastructure spending from just one AI company.

Ives can't predict when the technology sell-off will end. But his central argument deserves attention: The market's enthusiasm for AI stocks has cooled considerably, while the companies supplying the infrastructure continue to report massive demand.

For investors, that means don't panic over this month's slide in tech stocks. And if you're feeling opportunistic, this could be an ideal time to add to positions while prices remain depressed.
