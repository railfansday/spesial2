King Charles III Gets Into the Bahamian Spirit As He Joins Locals at a Junkanoo Parade

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The monarch kicked off his nine-day Caribbean tour

King Charles III is living it up on his Caribbean tour. The British monarch is currently on his three-day state visit to the Bahamas, after which he will head to Guyana for two days and then Antigua and Barbuda—where he will attend the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM)—for four days.

Queen Camilla will meet him for the final stop, but for now, he is fully enjoying his solo adventure. Yesterday, the king joined a Junkanoo parade in Nassau’s Parliament Square and, in his crisp light-gray suit, danced the day away with locals. Photos and videos show the royal shaking a pair of cowbells in the national colors of the Bahamas while jigging along next to festively-dressed dancers.

He wore his gray suit with a white button-up, a black-and-red striped tie, caramel suede boots, orange-frame sunglasses, and a patterned pocket square. Vacation attire!

A Junkanoo parade is a vibrant street festival with dancing, music, and colorful costumes. While the celebrations usually just take place during official holidays—like Boxing Day and New Year’s Day—a special one was coordinated for Charles’s visit.

Charles’s nine-day Caribbean tour leads up to the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting, and therefore serves as a diplomatic mission to reinforce connections between commonwealth nations, discuss shared priorities, and get to know local communities.

The trip marks Charles’s first time in the Bahamas as king, and his first visit in 53 years (he visited the country for Bahamian Independence Day in 1973, when he was Prince Charles).

During his engagements this time around, the king has been sure to address the region’s colonial history and Britain’s involvement in the transatlantic slave trade.
