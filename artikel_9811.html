The Model Mix Is Growing, and Moving In-House

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Posted on Nov 6 • Originally published at blog.kilo.ai

Developers are spreading their work across more models from more labs, and more of that work is running on models teams host themselves. Data published this week from OpenRouter, our own usage dashboard, and a talk by Revolut's AI ops lead all point the same way. The single-model setup is fading, and the default model is increasingly one you can run on your own hardware.

OpenRouter reported on November 2 that customers spending $100 or more a month now use a median of 6 model families, which is double the figure from a year ago. The top 10% of those customers went from 7 to 14. For a while, the trend ran the other way: the count fell through the first half of 2025 and bottomed at 3.8 in June, then climbed every quarter to 7.3 in October 2026; the highest OpenRouter has measured.

The heavier the usage, the wider the spread. In October, the median customer spending $100 to $1K a month used 5 labs, those spending $1K to $10K used 8, and those above $10K used 12. Among the $10K+ group, 90% used five or more labs.

Loyalty to a single lab has become rare. Fewer than 4% of any major lab's users on OpenRouter use that lab alone, and even Anthropic, which OpenRouter calls the stickiest, accumulates only 39% of its customers' spend. OpenRouter does point out the obvious selection bias, since people come to a router to use more than one model. What stands out is the reversal: after a period of consolidation, the number of labs per customer is rising again.

Our model dashboard shows the same spread inside a single tool. The leading model changes with the job, and no model takes more than 30% of any role.

Nvidia's Nemotron models lead code, plan and debug. Review is the odd one out, and it is where frontier models from Anthropic and OpenAI show up most.

In review, Claude Opus alone takes 11.5% of usage and GPT models add another 14.4%. In code, the only frontier model in the top 10 is gpt-6-luna at 1.4%.

Developers appear to use cheaper models to write and fix code, then pay for a stronger model at the point where mistakes get caught.

Another story came out today about Revolut, and how it now serves a self-hosted open model, GLM 5.3, as the default coding model for about 2,000 of its engineers. David Abitbol, who leads AI operations at the bank, described the setup at AI Engineer NYC on October 13, and the talk spread widely on X this week.

The move started with cost. Abitbol said some engineers were spending $1,000 to $10,000 a month on frontier coding tools "with not so much ROI." Revolut began self-hosting in April 2026 with GLM 5.1 and now runs GLM 5.3 on about 56 Nvidia B200 GPUs, at a blended cost of about 30 cents per million tokens. By his account, the bill is one tenth of what Revolut spent on Anthropic and OpenAI.

The bigger result was speed. Revolut measured a 70% improvement in lead time and a 60% improvement in coding time after moving to self-hosted GLM 5.1. Abitbol's explanation is that a flat bill meant engineers stopped rationing tokens and started using AI for Jira, Confluence and inbox work too.

Both trends lead to the same setup: a cheaper model you control handles routine work, and frontier models are saved for the steps where they pay off - and if your hardware can handle it, local AI has been proven to work at enterprise scale.
