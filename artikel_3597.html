How to change time in GTA 6

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Being able to change time in Grand Theft Auto 6 is quite helpful, as you can only start some missions at a certain time of day. Progressing through the campaign is not the only reason to do so. Some businesses are open only for a period of the day, like the bank, for example.

To help you plan your day, we've put this guide together explaining how to change time in GTA 6, as well as how long a day lasts.

In GTA 6, if you want to change time, the best option is to simply go to bed. At Jason's house, when you approach the bed, you can interact with it and choose the "Sleep" option. Ironically, this won't make the characters fall asleep immediately.

After lying down on the bed, you can either choose to check your phone, sleep and get up. Select the "Sleep" option, and you will be able to decide when the character is waking up by setting an alarm. You can set a specific time, or you can select one of the following options: morning, night, evening, or noon. For reference, when you wake up in the morning, it means you will start a new day at around 7 a.m.

If you're too far from Jason's house to sleep, your next best option is to simply keep playing. Time passes naturally in GTA 6. By simply exploring the map and visiting locations, the time of the day will eventually change.

In-game days might seem like they pass too quickly as you play through the campaign, but, in general, 24 in-game hours take approximately 2 hours and 24 minutes in real life. This duration only applies outside missions, like when you're freely exploring the game and doing side activities. Whenever you start a mission, time follows the script. For example, I headed to the Rusty Anchor bar to begin the "Day Release" mission at 8 a.m., but since this mission's story takes place later in the day, the clock jumped to 3 p.m. when the mission actually started.

Although there's no official information on how long an in-game day lasts, we ran some tests to verify that. If you check the time by looking at your phone, pressing down on the D-pad, or even looking at the clock on the wall of Jason's kitchen, you can time the duration of a minute and discover that one in-game minute is equivalent to around six seconds in real life.

Multiplying six seconds by the number of minutes in 24 hours (1,440 minutes) gives us 8,640 seconds. Divide this value by 60 to find out minutes, and you'll get the final result. How time passes varies depending on the context too, which can affect the overall duration of an in-game day. When in bed in Jason's house and checking the phone, time won't pass. The same happens in certain segments of missions.
