Prime Video's New Epic Fantasy Series To Rival LOTR Can Last At Least 8 Seasons

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

While it would take a lot for any fantasy show to rival Prime Video’s Lord of the Rings prequel The Rings of Power, one of the streaming service’s upcoming series could manage this if the show fulfills its ambitious plan. Ever since HBO’s fantasy epic Game of Thrones debuted in 2011, the hunt has been on for the next massive small-screen hit in the genre, with cable networks and streaming services alike sparing no expense in their attempts to outdo the show.

HBO’s adaptation of George R.R. Martin’s A Song of Ice & Fire saga set records with its unprecedented season 1 budget, but what seemed like exorbitant production costs back then appear quaint now. Not only have the official Game of Thrones spinoffs House of the Dragon and A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms attempted to replicate the scale and scope of the original show’s success, but projects like Netflix’s The Witcher franchise, Prime Video’s The Wheel of Time, and the three-season Lord of the Rings prequel The Rings of Power have outspent the 2011 series.

Nowadays, every major player in the streaming wars has at least one expensive epic fantasy franchise that is designed to replicate the breakout global mainstream success of Game of Thrones. That said, even within this context, there is one upcoming Prime Video show that could make The Rings of Power’s billion-dollar vision look meek. Carrie creator Mike Flanagan’s forthcoming adaptation of The Dark Tower is even more colossally ambitious than these earlier efforts.

The Dark Tower is far from being The Haunting of Hill House writer/director Flanagan’s first attempt to bring King’s stories to life onscreen. Before Flanagan rewrote King’s debut novel Carrie in October 2026’s hit miniseries adaptation, his earlier movies The Life of Chuck, Doctor Sleep, and Gerald’s Game were all adapted from King stories and novels. However, this doesn’t make The Dark Tower’s ambitious adaptation any less daunting.

The seven books of The Dark Tower series run over 4,000 pages in total, telling the story of Roland Deschain’s quest to reach the titular monument before the villainous Man in Black beats him across the desert and destroys the entity that holds time and reality itself together. Set between our recognizable reality and the post-apocalyptic Western setting of Mid-World, the Dark Tower saga is arguably King’s most ambitious work ever, blending horror, fantasy, Western, sci-fi, and character drama in its complex story.

Although The Dark Tower’s story is incredibly long, complicated, and ambitious, the saga also benefits from King’s crowd-pleasing prose and deft character work. Thus, the lengthy saga never becomes too overwhelming for the casual reader, which explains how the series stayed on the bestseller list even as later books saw King undertake some truly daring meta-twists that could easily have alienated many fans.

This makes adapting the books a daunting task even for a seasoned filmmaker like Flanagan, as translating the appeal of King’s saga to the screen could prove expensive, time-consuming, and difficult. Luckily, both Netflix’s live-action One Piece and SyFy’s acclaimed space opera series The Expanse prove this isn’t impossible.

Both of these earlier small-screen critical hits refused to condense their source stories, despite the creators knowing that the respective shows probably aren't getting enough seasons to cover all their material. Whether it is the incredibly complicated in-universe history of relations between the Martian Congressional Republic on Mars and the United Nations of Earth and Luna in The Expanse or the ever-evolving saga of the Straw Hat Pirates in One Piece, neither show has enough screen time for absolutely everything.

Crucially, however, neither show uses this as an excuse to start cutting out great swathes of source material and dropping or merging entire major characters right from the start. Thus, Prime Video’s The Dark Tower show has a blueprint to follow when it comes to bringing a massive, unwieldy tome to the screen unscathed.
