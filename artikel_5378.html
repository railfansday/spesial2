NYC is raising fines for improper needle disposal as weight loss drug use grows

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The fine rises to $250 from $100 after sanitation workers were stuck by needles more than 140 times over three years

The New York City Department of Sanitation raised the fine for improper disposal of needles and other sharps in household trash to $250 from $100, under a rule that took effect Monday.

The department said the increase is needed to protect its workers and discourage improper disposal. Sanitation employees have been stuck by needles more than 140 times over the past three calendar years, the department said. That count excludes cases in which discarded needles caused no injury.

The New York City Administrative Code permits a penalty of $50 to $250 for violations of the rules on regulated household waste. The $100 penalty had been in effect since 2002.

The department tallied 73 injuries from needle pricks in the current year, versus 37 in 2024, according to Bloomberg. Vincent Gragnani, a spokesperson for the department, said the city's records do not specify what kinds of needles were involved, but the climb in injuries probably tracks the spread of GLP-1 injectable medications.

About 11% of American adults now use GLP-1 drugs to lose weight, a share that stood near 3% in 2024, according to Bloomberg.

Needle-stick injuries can send people to the emergency room and may call for antiviral drugs, laboratory testing, appointments with infectious-disease specialists and time off work, according to Bloomberg. Residents should store used needles and other sharps in a closed container that resists leaks and punctures and is labeled "home sharps," according to Bloomberg.

In a comment on the rule dated Sept. 15, Small Property Owners of New York said penalties should fall on the person who discards the waste, not on the owner of the property where it is found. The group said building owners and staff cannot control what tenants or passersby put in trash and face the same needle-stick risk as sanitation workers. It urged the department to focus on public education and to identify convenient drop-off locations, including participating pharmacies.
