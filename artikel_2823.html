Packers By The Numbers: Stats from the Week 12 loss to the Rams

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

As we do every week in this space, we're looking at some stats from the Green Bay Packers' most recent game, courtesy of SumerSports. Today we're looking at the 26-13 loss to the Los Angeles Rams in Week 12.

I don't have metrics on the broadcast, but I can tell you with some amount of certainty that there was less dead air in this game than any other game this season. Luke Kuechly brought some really nice insight into some of the plays, but my goodness, fellas, you're allowed to take a breath.

And stop trying to make "Thanksgiving Eve" happen. It's not going to happen.

After leaning into a heavy personnel grouping last week, this week saw the Packers go back to primarily playing out of 11 personnel (1 RB, 1 TE, 3 WR).

And, lest you think the numbers are skewed due to a small number of plays in the second half (and needing to catch up when they did have the ball), those full game numbers are roughly in line with the first half approach.

They were primarily a shotgun-based passing attack offense in this game. For the game, they were in shotgun on 84.1% of snaps and had a pass rate of 68.2%. Again, those numbers basically align with the 1st half. They were 78.6% shotgun and 64.3% pass in the 1st half.

As we've seen them do all year, they switched up their run game approach in this game. After going with a power-heavy approach against the Vikings, they were back to a zone-based attack this week.

Inside Zone didn't work overly well for them, but they did well with Outside Zone and Power. I'm curious to dig a little deeper into that when the film hits. Was that a matter of a smaller sample size, or was that something they could have leaned a bit heavier into if they wanted to?

Jordan Love had a pretty rough day in the stat sheet. He went 17/29 for 198 yards, 1 TD and 2 INTs for an EPA per Play of -0.31. That would go down as his worst game of the year by EPA per Play (behind the -0.22 he posted against the Vikings last week). As I mentioned at the top, I thought Kuechly did a nice job breaking down what Love was expecting on that 1st INT, and the second INT certainly seemed to be a case where Bo Melton cut off his route. So there are certainly things to consider (and things we'll certainly be breaking down this week), but, right now, the numbers are the numbers and the numbers are bad.

Love did seem to have a little more time to throw in this game, but, as with every game this season, time is relative. The average time to pressure against Love was 2.3 seconds, 0.26 seconds below the NFL average. His average time to throw of 2.62 seconds seems downright functional, and would make this his 5th longest time to throw in a game this year. It's still 0.08 seconds below the NFL average. So protection was better, but still below average. Still, it felt functional in a way it certainly did not against the Vikings.

Love came into this game shredding Man coverage, and the Rams came into the game playing one of the highest rates of man coverage in the league. Love continued playing well against man, but the zone stuff is ugly.

We don't know how long Christian Watson will be out, but it sure would be nice if Tucker Kraft could find his game a little bit. He clearly is not all the way back from his injury (whether that's physical or mental, I don't really know), but the offense won't really have a chance if he can't find a way to turn it on.

Micah Parsons played limited snaps in his return to the field, but he was impactful while he was there, notching a 21.4% pressure rate and an average time to pressure of 2.47 seconds.

Parsons posted a pressure rate of 20.2% and an average time to pressure of 2.76 seconds last season, so he looked like Micah Parsons while he was on the field. That was certainly encouraging.

With Parsons back, I was certainly hoping for more from Lukas Van Ness, but we didn't really see that. He had a pressure rate of 6.7% and an average time to pressure of 2.6 seconds. With as run-heavy as the Rams were (66.2% run rate for the game, 81.4% in the 2nd half) it's hard to glean too much in terms of how the pass rush will look with Parsons back. Something to keep an eye on going forward.

Chris McClellan turned in a nice game with 4 assisted tackles, 4 solo tackles and 1 tackle for loss. I'm excited to take a look at him when the film comes out.
