LeSean McCoy advances in Pro Football Hall of Fame selection process

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

LeSean McCoy advances in Pro Football Hall of Fame selection process originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

Eagles all-time leading rusher LeSean McCoy is among 52 former NFL stars who have advanced to the second round in the Pro Football Hall of Fame's selection process.

The voting committee will reduce this list of 52 players to 25 in the next couple weeks. The committee will then select 13 players, one coach and one contributor as finalists and then vote on the 2027 Hall of Fame class Super Bowl week in Los Angeles.

McCoy, selected to the team of the decade for 2010 through 2019, rushed for 11,102 yards and 73 touchdowns with 518 catches for 3,898 yards and 16 more touchdowns in six years with the Eagles, four with the Bills and one each with the Bucs and Chiefs, winning Super Bowls as a backup the last two.

He ranks 23rd in NFL history in rushing yards, and his 4.5 per-carry average is 8th-highest among the 32 running backs who ran for at least 10,000 yards. He's one of only five backs in history with 11,000 rushing yards and 500 catches, and his 4.5 average is highest among them. The others – Marshall Faulk, LaDainian Tomlinson, Marcus Allen and Emmitt Smith – are all already in the Hall of Fame.

During the decade of the 2010s – from 2010 through 2019 – McCoy led the NFL with 10,434 rushing yards – 648 more than anybody else. Frank Gore was next with 9,786.

McCoy's 15,000 scrimmage yards are 19th-most by a running back, and during the decade from 2010 through 2019 he led the NFL with 13,923 scrimmage yards – nearly 1,700 more than any other player. One-time Eagle Julio Jones was next with 12,243.

McCoy was named to six Pro Bowl teams, three as an Eagle and three with Buffalo. Since he was drafted in 2009, McCoy made more Pro Bowls than any other NFL running back. In NFL history, only eight running backs have made more Pro Bowl teams. All who are eligible are already in the Hall of Fame.

McCoy, the Eagles' 2nd-round pick out of Pitt in 2009, retired after the 2020 season and is in his second year of Hall of Fame eligibility.

Maxie Baughan, who in past years would have been a leading seniors candidate, is now on the general ballot with the other linebackers .Baughan, the Eagles' 2nd-round pick in 1960, spent his first six seasons here and made the first five of his nine Pro Bowls as an Eagle before finishing his career with the Rams and Washington. He had 18 interceptions and 24 ½ unofficial sacks in his 12-year career.

Watters rushed for 10,643 yards and had 14,891 scrimmage yards in his 10-year career. He spent 1995 through 1997 with the Eagles, making two of his five Pro Bowls playing here.

Defensive tackle Haloti Ngata is also on the ballot. He finished his 13-year career with the Eagles in 2018. Ngata made five Pro Bowls and five all-pro teams – two first team, three second team – as a Baltimore Raven.

Also on the ballot is Philly native Jahri Evans, who went to Frankford High School. He was a six-time Pro Bowl offensive lineman and made five all-pro teams – four first team, one second team – and won a Super Bowl during his 12-year career. He was at the Linc this weekend as the Steelers' offensive line coach.
