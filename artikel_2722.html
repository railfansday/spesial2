‘A Total Failure,’ ‘A Big, Fat, Giant F on the Report Card’ — NFL Analyst Blasts Patriots’ Trade for ‘Aging’ WR A.J. Brown Amid Injury Concerns

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The New England Patriots' decision to acquire A.J. Brown from the Philadelphia Eagles is proving to be one of the biggest mistakes the organization has made in recent times.

The franchise traded for Brown in June in exchange for a 2028 first-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick. Analysts have criticized the organization, with one describing the decision as a "total failure."

Quarterback Drake Maye's right ankle injury has added another major problem for New England, although his play had been one of the team's strengths before he was hurt.

In a recent segment on CBS, Pete Prisco shared his views on the organization's decision to acquire Brown and said:

"You traded a first-round pick for an aging wide receiver. You can say all you want. He's getting up there. He's got a lot of wear and tear on the body, who came with injury questions, and by the way, he never really ran by a ton of people. And as you get older, you don't get faster," Prisco said.

"So you're paying for an aging receiver who's slowing down, who has injury concerns, and you gave up a first-round pick to get him," he continued, "It's a total failure. There's no other way to describe that trade right now, as we sit here. It's an F. A big, fat, giant F on the report card."

Brown, who ranked 12th on PFN's WR Metric last season, suffered a hamstring injury in the late third quarter. The Patriots are entering their bye week, with games against the 1-8 Los Angeles Chargers and the 8-1 Buffalo Bills scheduled afterward.

The next few games will be crucial for the wide receiver, as they could determine his future with the franchise.

Bill Simmons also recently criticized the Brown trade, calling it "one of the worst trades in Boston sports history." He also mentioned that Brown's first season in New England has been a failure in almost every respect.

Brown has dealt with ankle and knee injuries throughout his first season in New England, limiting both his availability and production.

His latest setback came in Munich, where he suffered a hamstring injury against the Lions. Given that he had only recently returned from a two-game absence and New England was already trailing heavily, the decision to keep him on the field has drawn scrutiny from fans and analysts.

It will be interesting to see how the franchise deals with ongoing injuries and how the organization addresses its shortcomings and moves forward.
