Nicolas Cage Says He's ‘A Little Bit Homeless’ After Sinkhole Damages House

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Nicolas Cage is sharing an update on his living arrangements after a massive sinkhole caused damage to his Malibu home.

“I’m currently a little bit homeless,” Cage, 62, said during a Los Angeles Comic Con panel on Saturday, October 31, per E News. “Something happened to my house in Malibu, so I’m just sort of living in hotels.”

Cage explained that the move altered his Halloween plans with his 4-year-old daughter, August, whom he shares with wife Riko Shibata. (The pair tied the knot at the Wynn Hotel in Las Vegas in 2021. Cage is also the father of son Weston, 35, with ex Christina Fulton and son Kal-El, 21, with ex-wife Alice Kim.)

“I don’t have normal trick-or-treaters that I would like to enjoy tonight,” he said. “I’ll take my daughter out trick-or-treating somewhere in Santa Monica, but I am going to watch a movie where the director was actually nominated for Worst Director at the Razzies — called The Shining.”

Cage continued, “He directed the hell out of that movie. I’m going to watch that tonight. After I put my daughter to bed, I’m going to put on The Shining.”

Cage bought his beachfront home in Malibu for $10.5 million in 2024. In September, a sinkhole collapsed in part of his driveway and on the road near his mansion. Photos published by TMZ at the time showed debris in the hole with part of the driveway hanging over the edge.

Malibu declared an emergency that month, sharing in a proclamation that the sinkhole “poses imminent danger to the homes and residents” on the street.

“As of the time of this proclamation, seven residences have been red-tagged due to hazardous conditions resulting from the sinkhole, and an additional 24 residences will be red-tagged due to the loss of safe vehicular access,” the message read, in part. “Gas service to the affected area has been shut off, and water service may also be discontinued as conditions warrant. These circumstances have significantly impaired access to affected properties and created conditions requiring immediate emergency response and protective measures.”

The proclamation noted that “emergency personnel access is severely constrained and may be impassable, due to the large portion of the road that has collapsed.”

“The intact portion of the roadway is too narrow to accommodate emergency response vehicles, including fire engines and other large apparatus, creating a substantial risk to public safety and emergency response capabilities,” the message read.
