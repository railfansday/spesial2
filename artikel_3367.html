10 Mad Magazine Mascot Alfred P. Neuman Quotes To Live By

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The humor periodical Mad started off as a comic in 1952, before taking on its more recognizable magazine with 1955's Mad #24. That cover featured a prominent appearance by the caricature of a character who would come to be known as Alfred E. Neuman. A few issues later, on the cover of Mad #30, Alfred took center stage, officially becoming the magazine's figurehead and mascot.

From its inception, issues of Mad included an epigraph, a quote from a historical figure, politician, or celebrity that set the tone. The first time Mad opened with a quote attributed to Alfred E. Neuman was Mad #38. From that point on, Alfred has had the first, and definitive, line in practically every issue of the magazine since.

Sometimes Alfred's quotes are bawdy, and sometimes they are politically charged. Often, though, they are low-key life lessons, the kind of wisdom that only a comic character can get away with sharing.

"It's a good idea to keep your words soft and sweet because you never know when you'll have to eat them!" Here, Alfred E. Neuman riffs on a centuries-old idiom. Be careful what comes out of your mouth, Neuman warns, because if what you say ends up getting shoved back in your face, down your own throat, you might find yourself choking on the tone and the meanings of your words.

It is sensible advice, especially for comedians, but knowing Alfred, and knowing Mad magazine, this is really a "do as I say, not as I do" case of cautionary advice.

"You'll never get rid of a bad temper by losing it!" There's another saying, which is that you can't control your immediate reaction to something, but you can control your reaction to the reaction. If you're prone to getting angry, giving into it is only going to make the problem worse, that's what Alfred E. Neuman is saying here.

It is a slyly clever bit of wordplay. In fact, this bon mot could be remixed into a riddle. "What can you never get rid of by losing it?" "A bad temper!" The cover for this issue, Mad #71, shows Alfred's mug at the center of a dartboard, and with its opening quote at least, the magazine hit the comedic bullseye.

"Learn from the mistakes of others." That is age-old wisdom. But why? That part is usually left unsaid, but leave it to Alfred E. Neuman to say the quiet part loud. It is "'cause you'll never live long enough to make 'em all yourself!"

Life is finite. There's only so much failure and foible the average person can cram into one lifetime. Recognizing and avoiding others' failures isn't about being more likely to succeed in your own endeavors. It is simply a time-saving exercise. Plus, in some cases, those mistakes might have been fatal, and avoiding them is the only way to guarantee a long life full non-lethal errors in judgment.

The art of the one-liner is trickier than people give it credit for. (Just ask Henny Youngman.) In just a few words, the speaker has to set up and execute the punchline, but there is more to it than that. The line has to conjure an image in people's heads. And that image can be vague, or it can be concrete.

Here, it is literally the latter. "Some minds are like concrete," Alfred E. Neuman says in Mad #82, in the sense that they are "all mixed up and permanently set." In other words, some people are defiantly confused. Obstinately misguided. Flat out wrong and unwilling to revise their opinions. Don't be like those people, Alfred suggests. Be willing to admit your wrong.

Long before Gordan Gecko told the world "greed is good" in Wall Street, Alfred E. Neuman gave Mad readers the real truth about cold hard cash. "Sometimes, the best scheme for doubling your money is to fold it in half and stuff it back in your wallet," Neuman declared in Mad #87.

Meaning, get-rich-quick schemes and seemingly lucrative investments are often not all they seemed cracked up to be. Everyone wants to hustle, and maximize their earnings, but sometimes the smart thing to do is just stack paper. Save up. For Alfred, a folded dollar is worth more than one that is tied up in escrow.

The funny thing about Alfred E. Neuman's advice is that he was often both the angel and the devil on readers' shoulders. His line in Mad #145 exemplifies this. "Sometimes it pays to resist a temptation," he begins. Sounds totally reasonable, and also morally sound, right?

Well, let him finish. Because Alfred also answers why it pays. Not because having will power and self-control are important. Not because resisting temptation is the right thing. No, because temptation is a gamble. Why take the one in front of you when "a better one may be coming along." This hilariously, and cheekily, takes the matter from an ethical equation to a practical matter of when it is the right time to do the wrong thing.

"Experience is something you never have until just after you need it!" This quote from Mad #204 is the perfect companion to the issue's cover, which parodies the Jaws 2 poster, with Alfred E. Neuman piloting a mechanical shark to sneak up on a jet-skiing girl in a bikini.

In the movie, only Chief Brody has the ability to save the day, because only he has the experience of having fought a killer great white before. For most people, most of the time, the toughest moments of their lives are trial and error, giving them the experience that would have made it easier on them only after the fact.

This quote from Mad #221 evokes the earlier entry on this list from Mad #54. It is another way for Alfred E. Neuman to remind readers: "Watch what you say." Especially if you've already gotten yourself in trouble by talking too much.

"When you're in deep water, it's a good idea to keep your mouth shut!" It is a great case of applying a literal lesson to figurative language. If you're in proverbial deep water, or hot water, or any kind of water, talking, or shouting, is only going to result in one thing. Drowning.

Mad #267's cover features a Top Gun parody, and Alfred E. Neuman's quote in the issue makes it clear what kind of people he'd like to strap to the nose of a fighter jet and send flying. "People on ego trips," who, Alfred says bluntly, "should buy one-way tickets."

It is a slick play on words, but the point doesn't mince any. If you are ego-driven and willing to do whatever it takes to feed your own sense of self, take a hike. Take a vacation. Take off and don't come back. For Mad fans, it is a reminder to check themselves before they wreck themselves, and ruin someone else's day too.

The theme of Mad #293's Alfred E. Neuman quote is the same as the prior entry. And it contains essentially the same valuable life lesson. Which is, life is hard for everyone, so don't go around making it harder.

Or as Alfred puts it: "It's not just the ups and downs that make life difficult. It's the jerks." People who go around being miserable all the time need to do more than just sit down and read a Mad magazine, or a Far Side comic, or even a Garfield, and try to lighten up. They need to think about how they are impacting other people. That's what Mad's irreverent, but often on-point mascot is saying here.
