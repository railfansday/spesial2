Liverpool vs Manchester United - Premier League: All the reaction as calamitous Red Devils are thrashed 4-0 by bitter rivals in Mauricio Pochettino's first game in charge

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

By HARRY BAMFORTH, LIVE SPORTS COVERAGE REPORTER

Follow Daily Mail Sport's live coverage for the latest updates from Anfield as Mauricio Pochettino takes charge of Manchester United for the first time at rivals Liverpool in the Premier League.

Andoni Iraola, Liverpool boss, speaking to Sky Sports:

At the end of the match, the Liverpool fans were asking their boss to go full Jurgen Klopp-style and give them some fist bumps, just like in the old days.

Arne Slot never obliged when asked, but a beaming Iraola was happy to give it big to the Kop in his own unique way. There was something very wholesome about it all.

The Liverpool fans are starting to really take to their new head coach; the bond between them is being built.

This feels much more like the Liverpool of old.

Just when you think Manchester United can't sink any lower, they somehow manage to.

I thought the season they finished 15th under Ruben Amorim is the worst United team we would see this century, but I think this bunch is even worse.

Accounting for three points for a win all-time, United’s eight points this season are their fewest after 11 games of a league campaign since 1972-73 (7).

That is also the only previous season in which the Red Devils only managed one win through their first 11 league matches.

Things are looking pretty damn bleak at Old Trafford.

Kobbie Mainoo, Manchester United midfielder, speaking to Sky Sports:

I said it before and I will say it again: it doesn't get much worse than that for Mauricio Pochettino.

That was as bad as a first defeat you can get as a manager.

Pochettino suffered the joint-heaviest defeat by a Manchester United manager in his first game (0-4), along with a defeat by the same scoreline for Lal Hilditch in October 1926 (0-4 v Bolton Wanderers in the top flight).

The United boss was also the first manager in Premier League history to see his team concede twice and receive a red card in the first half of his first match in charge of a club.

Rio Ngumoha, Liverpool goalscorer, speaking to Sky Sports:

Now it's time to laud praise on Rio Ngumoha.

The young winger got his chance starting in the Premier League today, and my word, did he take it. What an exceptional performance from the trickster.

He had Diogo Dalot on strings all game; the Manchester United defender can add him to his bank of nightmares.

Ngumoha obviously added another assist to his repertoire, but what he has been craving all season is a goal. And well, as you saw, he finally got his first of the season - what a team to score it against.

Tonight he became the youngest player to ever score in this fixture, even younger than the great Michael Owen. Not only that, but he is the youngest to both score and assist in a Premier League game since Owen on Boxing Day 1997 against Leeds United.

I feel Ngumoha may well stay a fixture in this team now.

We will bark on about his brilliance in a moment, but for now, let's take a look at his goal that truly killed off Manchester United.

He was there to round off what was a brilliant bit of solo play from Bradley Barcola, who seems to be exceptional at everything but scoring himself.

Once this went in, it was truly game over.

Daniel Sturridge, former Liverpool striker, speaking to Sky Sports:

While all the focus will be on how dismal Manchester United were, do not let it cloud how brilliant they were. Against 10 men or not, the Reds were electric.

Sometimes playing against 10 men is harder than playing against 11, but Liverpool didn't make it look that way. They ripped their biggest rivals to shreds.

Liverpool's front four were having a field day out there and could have easily bagged six, seven or even eight.

Andoni Iraola's identity is finally coming to the fore; the intensity in this team is well and truly back.

The vibes coming away from Anfield could not be different for Liverpool and Manchester United.

Those United players and fans will want the ground to swallow them up.

The Red Devils stay in the bottom three, with just eight points to their name from 11 Premier League games.

If people involved with United weren't already panicking about a potential relegation, they may well be now.

Liverpool fans will be on cloud nine right now.

Not only have they just thumped the team they hate most, but they have also closed the gap to the teams at the top.

They now sit in fourth place, just one point behind Manchester City and Brighton.

Is there a point to be made that they could end up being Arsenal's closest title rivals? I think there is.
