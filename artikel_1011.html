Birmingham beaten at 10-man Stoke

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Stoke City vs Birmingham City. Sky Bet Championship.

Report and free match highlights from the Sky Bet Championship match between Stoke City and Birmingham City at the bet365 Stadium on Saturday; George Hirst nets the winner as 10-man Potters then hold on to topple the Blues.

Please use Chrome browser for a more accessible video player

George Hirst scored the only goal of the game as 10-man Stoke moved into the automatic promotion places with a 1-0 victory over Birmingham.

Hirst broke the deadlock in the early stages of the second half before Luca Bombino was shown a second yellow card for the hosts in the 63rd minute.

The two teams were level on points towards the top of the Sky Bet Championship at the start of the day, with Birmingham top and Stoke third.

Both had won their last three games, and it was the hosts who prevailed as Hirst scored for the first time since early September.

Despite seeing 10 of the 20-man matchday squad that beat Portsmouth last time out go away on international duty, it was the hosts who came out of the traps quickest.

Jack McGlynn took up a deeper role in Stoke's midfield in the absence of Northern Ireland international Ethan Galbraith, one of four changes to the Stoke side from the win at Fratton Park, and the American fizzed a long-range shot past the upright inside the first two minutes.

Bombino also returned to the Potters' starting XI and was picked out by McGlynn's inviting back-post cross, heading his effort against Ethan Laird and out for a corner.

The American forced the returning James Beadle into his first save shortly afterwards, working space for a shot on his lesser-favoured right foot.

As Blues struggled to contain Mark Robins' side in the early stages, McGlynn played in Ben Johnson, who forced Beadle to push his low effort around a post.

Beadle and top scorer August Priske were both back in Blues' starting line-up after missing the last two games with injury, while Seung-ho Paik replaced Jhon Solis, who was absent from the squad.

It took Birmingham half-an-hour to register a shot as Phil Neumann met Alex Cochrane's corner, only to see his header saved by Viktor Johansson.

Bae Junho was making his first start in over a month and should have done better with Bombino's bobbling cross but headed his effort comfortably wide of Beadle's left-hand post.

Stoke started the second half in similar fashion to the first and were ahead within three minutes of the restart.

Bombino played in Djibril Soumare who whipped in a cross from the left which Hirst calmly turned beyond Beadle, scoring just his second goal since moving from Ipswich in the summer.

Stoke almost doubled their lead just past the hour with a free-flowing counter-attack. Hirst played in Johnson down the right and he was denied by the huge frame of Beadle.

Seconds later, the hosts were down to 10. Already on a booking, Bombino's strong challenge on Patrick Roberts earned him a second yellow card.

Blues went about trying to make their man advantage count through Demarai Gray, whose inswinging cross was diverted wide at the back post by Laird.

Birmingham threw on a plethora of attacking options in the closing stages as the clash became attack against defence, as Marvin Ducksch glanced Roberts' cross narrowly wide.

Jay Stansfield's curling effort was beaten away by Johansson as Stoke stood firm to remain joint-top of the table.

"I am delighted with that performance from start to finish. That first hour gave us a different level of football.

"The only blight was the sending-off. Other than that, we controlled it, I thought we were really good.

"We were good in possession, we moved the ball, and ourselves, really well. We got into really good spaces to be able to play, so it was more fluid than it has been since the start of the season.

"The pleasing thing for me is the intensity to match theirs, because these are a pressing team. We are a work in progress but today was that step in quality against different opponents.

"Intensity, bravery and the intent we showed was everything that we asked for, and on the back of that we've got the support who are right behind it, they trust the players and that is a really good feeling.

"They're really humble and they've got to stay there. We're 16 games in but you're looking at two points a game now, and that's with the three games we gave away at the start of the season.

"I think George was exactly what you want from your centre-forward today, and that's on the back of working hard.

"It's taken time for him to settle down but we've seen what he's capable of doing and the goal that he got was really smart, a brilliant goal, and he deserved that for his hard work."

"We expected them to have their tails up at the start, as they did, without really creating much in the way of chances, but you could definitely feel the atmosphere was there.

"I thought we weathered that reasonably well and we grew into it a little bit with some set pieces towards the end of the first half, but we weren't probably calm enough when we did win the ball back, whether that was to keep possession or break away.

"We came in with a foothold and then we could push on from there. The message at half-time was that we'd got through the hardest part of the game and now it's a case of looking to exert ourselves more on the game.

"The goal we did concede was disappointing because it was a clear chance and it was a poor goal from our point of view.

"When it went 11-v-10 we had a real lifeline in the sense that they camped in for that last half an hour, understandably.

"They defended that doggedly enough as the home team, but from our point of view we were well short of what it takes to create clear chances with our method, with our movements, our quality, our positions in the box.

"We were just below the level you need to be and maybe we created 2-3 half-chances when if that could be 2-3 big chances then you probably go and get something from the game."
