Taylor Sheridan’s New Western Hit Beat ‘Yellowstone’ at Its Own Game

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Jeffrey is a freelance features writer at Collider. He is an MPA-accredited entertainment journalist and a Tomatometer-approved critic based in the LA area. He graduated from the University of Texas at Austin with a Bachelor of Science in Radio, TV, & Film and a Bachelor of Arts in Theater.

With over eight million same-day viewers and 20 million viewers across streaming platforms for its Season 2 premiere, Marshals, the spin-off of Taylor Sheridan's Yellowstone, is a bona fide hit for CBS. Centered around Kayce Dutton (Luke Grimes), the former Navy SEAL joins the U.S. Marshals and becomes an officer of the law, apprehending wanted fugitives. Marshals' success proves that the Yellowstone franchise can move beyond the Dutton family drama and that stories set in the universe can survive and thrive without the departed Dutton family's patriarch, John Dutton (Kevin Costner).

Marshals offers something completely distinct from its flagship series. The spin-off series presents an action-thriller meets a modern-day western drama, set in the heartland of America. The stories are far more action-oriented compared to Yellowstone, adopting a television police procedural format for its episodic storylines. The show diverges from the occasional soap opera-like antics of the Dutton family and their general dysfunction in Yellowstone, instead focusing on Kayce and his team, led by his former Navy SEAL teammate Pete "Cal" Calvin (Logan Marshall-Green), hunting wanted fugitives.

Marshals proved to be a major experiment that paid off. Initially, the series was meant to be titled Y: Marshals, referencing its lineage as a Yellowstone spin-off. CBS instead opted to quietly drop the Y from the title in early 2026, just two months before the show's premiere. Now, as Marshals continues its second season, dropping the Yellowstone reference from the title proved to be a brilliant move, allowing the new show to forge its own path and identity.

True, this is a Yellowstone spin-off and continuation. However, as Grimes pointed out in an interview with Entertainment Tonight earlier this year, that's already obvious from the presence of Kayce Dutton and other characters from the original series. Marshals distinguishing itself from Yellowstone enables the writers and CBS to brand the new series as something completely different from the original, that adopts its own unique style and plays like a completely different genre.

Many questioned whether the Yellowstone franchise could survive once John Dutton exited the series in Season 5. Marshals and another successful spin-off/continuation series, Dutton Ranch, now prove that Dutton wasn't the true star of Yellowstone, but rather the show's intricate, textured world and strong ensemble of richly diverse characters. Characters such as Mo (Mo Brings Plenty) and Thomas Rainwater (Gil Birmingham), two other Yellowstone alumni, get expanded, beefed-up roles in Marshals, where the series explores the plight of the Confederated Tribes of Broken Rock and political conflicts between the federal government and Native Americans.

Granted, the stories involving the tribal lands and business are presented with faster-paced, suspenseful, and action-heavy sequences. However, series showrunner Spencer Hudnut still brings the trademark contemplation and depth demonstrated in franchise creator and executive producer Taylor Sheridan's earlier projects.

The series also dedicates significant time to developing and exploring Kayce's father-and-son relationship with his son, Tate Dutton (Brecken Merrill), as they try to move forward with their lives following Monica's tragic passing. Tate is growing into a man, and Kayce regularly struggles with figuring out the best way to protect his son while supporting Tate's growth, independence, and coming-of-age journey. These are all great characters and relationships featured in Marshals, proving that the franchise offers more than just The John Dutton Show.

Yellowstone was an iconic, groundbreaking series. However, Marshals' overall smashing success proves that the Yellowstone Universe is capable of growing beyond its predecessor. The Yellowstone franchise has now released multiple prequel spin-offs, including 1883 and 1923, and new continuations of the saga with Marshals and Dutton Ranch. The prequels were great period pieces that expanded the show's overarching mythology, providing more background information on the present circumstances.

However, Marshals marks a change in the franchise for the better, showing that it can continue to grow, unencumbered or anchored by the past, and that the franchise can encompass multiple genres and narrative styles. It's a microcosm of the series as a whole, as Kayce Dutton searches for identity and a new path forward, beyond the Dutton family name and the Yellowstone Ranch. In a way, John Dutton leaving the franchise has allowed it to grow and evolve into something completely new and different from where Yellowstone began as a neo-Western meets dysfunctional family drama.
