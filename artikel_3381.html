Michael C. Hall's 8-Episode Harlan Coben Netflix Series Is An Intense Binge

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Even though Michael C. Hall has been on plenty of disturbing shows over the years, Netflix’s underrated Harlan Coben series is one of the actor’s most intense series to date. Michael C. Hall has never been an actor who worries too much about playing likable or relatable characters. From the role of the predatory David Kammerer in 2013’s literary biopic Kill Your Darlings to his most famous role as a “serial killer killer” in Showtime’s Dexter franchise, the actor is entirely open to morally ambiguous roles.

This has often been a good thing, with Hall’s uncompromising portrayal of John F. Kennedy marking a high point in 2017’s The Crown season 2. Meanwhile, although Six Feet Under’s David Fisher was never a villainous figure, the hardships that he faced during the HBO drama’s five-season run also called on Hall to explore the darker sides of the human psyche at length.

While the Dexter franchise is still Hall’s most iconic exploration of a deeply disturbed but strangely compelling character, it is far from his only unmissable role as an amoral protagonist. Netflix’s underrated Harlan Coben adaptation Safe is one of the streaming service’s most intense shows based on the Myron Bolitar creator’s books, and this is thanks in large part to Hall’s mesmerizing central turn in the eight-episode miniseries.

Safe follows Hall's Tom Delaney, a pediatric surgeon who has a hard time connecting with his daughters after the death of his wife. When one of Tom’s daughters goes missing, this sparks a complex plot that exposes years of lies, cover-ups, and family secrets, all with Hall’s protagonist at the center of everything. While early reviews hinted that Safe could be Netflix’s answer to Broadchurch, critics soon noted that the Coben show was soapier and more dramatic than that darker murder mystery.

While this might sound like a condemnation, fans of Coben’s output will likely know better. Some of Netflix’s best psychological thriller shows, like Mindhunter, are dour, maudlin series with patient pacing and a bleak tone. Others, like The Hunting Wives or the five-season hit You, are campy, playful takes on the psychological thriller genre that are no less thrilling for their winking sense of humor and more outrageous tone.

Like many of the best Coben adaptations, Safe dances on the line between these two extremes. While Gone for Good, No Second Chance, The Stranger, The Innocent, The Woods, Stay Close, Hold Tight, Fool Me Once, Missing You, Run Away, and I Will Always Find You aren’t all perfect, Netflix’s Coben adaptations do all use their grounded tone to sneak in some ludicrously shocking plot twists and absurd moments.

Where shows like Mindhunter or HBO’s Mare of Easttown are grounded and realistic throughout and hits like The Hunting Wives or You abandon this in favor of self-aware fun, Safe maintains a serious, somber tone while its plot pulls off all manner of wacky and unlikely revelations. Central to the success of this tonal juggling is Hall’s performance.

Subtle and layered, Hall’s take on Tom makes the surgeon feel like a real, believable character trapped in a bizarre story, rather than a self-aware character or a soap opera protagonist. Thus, even Safe’s silliest moments still hit hard thanks to Hall’s years of practice taking larger-than-life characters in outlandish situations and unearthing their underlying humanity.

All of Netflix’s 12 Coben shows will appeal to viewers who just love a thriller full of plot twists, but some are sillier and less believable than others. In Safe, Michael C. Hall’s unmissable turn makes Netflix’s psychological thriller the perfect balance of grounded believability and gripping, unpredictable fun.
