A £4.7m West Brom transfer led James Morrison to an unexpected discovery

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

West Brom will be full of confidence following their heroics at West Ham last weekend, with a 3-1 victory on the road a statement win to boost their promotion credentials.

The Baggies lifted themselves up to fourth in the Championship standings after their Halloween treat at the London Stadium, with goals from Jimmy-Jay Morgan, Felix Horn Myhre and Callum Styles making it three wins from their last four league outings.

It’s been a remarkable turnaround ever since James Morrison stepped into the Baggies hotseat, and there’s so much positivity about where the club can go under his guidance.

One player who is beginning to flex his muscles in particular is goalscorer Myhre, who, despite a slow start in the blue and white stripes, is now beginning to show supporters how important his type of game is to the side.

But his move to The Hawthorns is an interesting story, with the transfer all coming about from the original scouting used to recruit Norwegian counterpart Aune Heggebo.

Felix Horn Myhre was part of 10 new recruits to walk through the West Brom building following a summer of change, with the Norwegian international one of many signings who was taking on the challenge of the Championship for the very first time.

And it’s safe to say that it’s taken a bit of time for the 27-year-old to get used to it, with boss James Morrison previously calling for patience with the midfielder as he gets used to the increase of physicality and intensity compared to his previous playing days with SK Brann.

And after 10 second tier games under his belt, Myhre is starting to show why Morrison was so keen to get him into the group for this season, with his tidiness on the ball and technical qualities bringing so much more composure in the final third, which has been needed for quite a long time.

Myhre has already been used in different positions so far in the early stages of the campaign, playing as either a central midfield, second striker or lone centre forward, with that kind of versatility so important over a gruelling Championship term.

As Myhre began life in the Championship, he was given starts immediately after signing, named in the starting XI for the first four games of the season, but the next five games would see him flip-flop between having a starting role to then being an unused substitute.

And while he was brought on late against Blackburn Rovers, he contributed immediately by grabbing his first goal for the club and sealing a 3-1 victory, with starts then coming once again against Preston North End and West Ham.

In those two games, Myhre’s performances have really gone up a notch, with the midfielder starting to link up effectively with the likes of Isaac Price and Jimmy-Jay Morgan, and that triangle of attack massively contributed to their destruction of West Ham last weekend.

Myhre’s goal at the London Stadium was an example of how the midfielder is getting into the right positions to score goals, while his layoff to Morgan for their second strike against the Hammers showed the technical quality he brings and composure when in tight and pressurised situations.

It’s so encouraging and brings excitement over what he can produce in the coming weeks and months, with Myhre having the potential to be one of the standout creative midfielders in the division if he keeps up his current form.

Meanwhile, an interesting story emerged from The Telegraph’s John Percy as to how West Brom ended up signing Felix Horn Myhre, with it all stemming from their capture the previous summer of Aune Heggebo.

Albion paid SK Brann a reported fee of £4.7m to sign Heggebo in the summer of 2025, with the frontman linking up under then Baggies boss Ryan Mason.

And James Morrison, who was working as part of the backroom staff at the time, watched Heggebo’s performances at Brann before the club went ahead with the transfer.

During the scouting, he was left impressed with the displays of Myhre, who was also playing for SK Brann at the time, but Brann reportedly refused to sell Myhre and Heggebo together in the same window given they were such important players.

Because of that, Albion had to wait until the summer just gone to see Myhre eventually join the club when Morrison had his full say on transfers, with the midfielder signing for an initial £2.5m fee, rising to £3.1m.

It’s amazing how one piece of transfer business can have a much wider impact than first expected, with West Brom never likely to even have considered Felix Horn Myhre as a target if they hadn’t scouted Aune Heggebo in the first place.

And due to that piece of recruitment, Albion have landed themselves on a gem of a midfield player, who is finding his feet and showing a technical side to his game that is reminiscent of the likes of Robert Koren, who was such a wonderful player for the Baggies both at Championship level and then stepping up to be an accomplished Premier League asset.

Myhre's influence is now beginning to grow ten-fold, as demonstrated by his glittering performance at West Ham, and all connected with Albion will be hoping those types of displays can continue and help the Baggies cement themselves towards the top end of the table.

This kind of signing could be so key to Albion being true promotion contenders this season, albeit being such an unexpected discovery in the summer of 2025.
