Cinemark Posts Sharply Higher Third-Quarter Results Amid Hollywood Box Office Rebound

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The movie theater giant, led by CEO Sean Gamble, saw concession and admissions revenues rise from year-earlier levels as tentpoles like 'Spider-Man: Brand New Day' and 'The Odyssey' played on its screens.

Exhibition giant Cinemark Holdings on Tuesday unveiled its third-quarter financial results, posting higher revenue and earnings compared to a year ago.

The big reveal was how a strong movie slate allowed the exhibition giant to sustain gains from this summer’s Hollywood box office rebound into its latest financial quarter after record results in the most recent second quarter. Cinemark saw total revenue rise to $1.05 billion, against a year-earlier $857.5 million in the same period of 2025.

Admissions revenue during the latest quarter was $530.8 million, up from a year-earlier $427.7 million, with worldwide attendance of 63.7 million customers, up 18 percent year-over-year. And concession revenue came to $413.9 million, against $336.7 million in the same period of 2025.

The latest financial results for Cinemark for the three months to Sept. 30, 2026 came amid a summer box office rebound for Hollywood titles at the local multiplex that has lifted the hopes of major studios and exhibitors coming out of the pandemic era. The cinema circuit also said premium screening offerings like XD, Imax and ScreenX also continued to attract audiences and higher ticket prices.

Net income at Cinemark for the third quarter came to $124.2 million, rising from a year-earlier $50.5 million. The company’s third-quarter adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA), another profitability metric, rose to $276.6 million, compared to a year-earlier $177.6 million.

Sean Gamble, Cinemark’s president and CEO, in a statement that accompanied the firm’s latest financial results, said: “Our ability to translate positive box office momentum from a compelling slate of summer releases into consistent, meaningful earnings growth reflects the strength of our operating model, the effectiveness of our team and the sustained impact of our strategic initiatives.”

During a morning analyst call, Gamble reiterated his support for the recent takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery by Paramount Skydance and that enlarged studio’s pledge to debut at least 30 films a year and keep them in theaters for a minimum of 45 days.

“I think we were comfortable moving forward and we’ll see how things go from here,” he argued. His confidence came amid industry concerns Paramount’s acquisition of storied Hollywood studio will down the road lead to fewer movies made and theater closures.

Gamble added he was optimistic Apple Original Films will continue to ramp up theatrical releases after the box office success for F1: The Movie, and saw Netflix potentially raising its own game in theatrical releases, though that remained to be seen. “There’s obviously a lot of change happening at that company right now, we’ll see where things go,” Gamble said.
