Chicago Bears Stock Watch: Burden, Booker up, Johnson, Santos down

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

It's over the halfway mark of the season for the Chicago Bears. Since the 17-game season came in, there is no longer a nice and neat halfway point to the schedule.

But having a bye week after nine games is a good-enough mark to take stock over players that have risen and fallen over the first nine games of the season.

I am sticking to my usual three in each category, so it isn't going to be comprehensive. There are many players worthy of inclusion on the up list. I'll toss in some honorable memtions for good measure, too.

Luther Burden, WR – Ben Johnson told us to buy stock in Burden way back in May. Now I was already holding some from his days at Missouri and more following his rookie season in Chicago. But the buy has paid off. Burden leads the team in receptions, yards and touchdowns. He's on pace for a 1,200 yard season. He's been great at gaining YAC and making catches at all levels. I am excited to see what he's capable of in the stretch run of the season.

Austin Booker, DE – Expectations have perhaps been unfairly high for Booker. He flashed enough that his injury-shortened second season and lack of moves on the DL put pressure on him by fans to step up. And he has. While still not consistent, I think he's been a great complement to the line alongside Jeffery Simmons and Montez Sweat. He has a new-career high in sacks with 5 this year and he is solidly the DE3 following Dayo Odeyingbo's injury.

Malik Muhammad, CB – Coming into the season, the expectations for the offense and the roles of those players were straightforward. The defense was the side with all the questions. In some ways, the worst has been the case (see the stock down), but Muhammad has actually been Chicago's most consistent corner. He's been a notable absence since his injury.

Honorable mentions: Dillon Thieneman, Devin Bush Jr., Kalif Raymond and Daniel Hardy

Jaylon Johnson, CB – Any hopes of a return to form from before his core injury in 2024 are long gone. Johnson had a rough start to the season. His interception touchdown against the Bucs was a high-point, but I think we all know that those moments will be fewer and farther between. Hopefully, he can build on it and use his experience to win matchups in the second half of the season.

Tyson Bagent, QB – Pressed into action following Caleb Williams' hamstring injury, Bagent unfortunately didn't do much to inspire confidence about him being a potential NFL starter. He's still a decent backup QB but any hopes of a trade with meaningful draft capital disappeared following his interception-filled games against the Jets and Packers.

Cairo Santos, K – He's coming off a game-winning field goal, so I understand there might be some frustration with his appearance here. But he currently has the lowest FG percentage of his Bears career at 78.9 percent. Sure, the one block got his long snapper back on the practice squad, but he missed one from 40-49. If you're not a big-leg kicker, you have to make 'em all from inside 50 yards. Plus, landing-zone penalties are unforced errors.

Honorable mentions: Garrett Bradbury, T.J. Edwards, Tyrique Stevenson

Who do you think has been up and who has been down over the first nine games?
