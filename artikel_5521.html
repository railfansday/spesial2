U.S. lawmakers are nearing a deal to ban Chinese cars, sparing Mercedes

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Lawmakers aim to send a bill to President Donald Trump by year-end, with Mercedes-Benz shielded from an outright ban

CFOTO / Future Publishing via Getty Images

U.S. lawmakers are close to striking an agreement that would prohibit Chinese vehicles from being sold in the American market, according to Bloomberg. Last-minute negotiations are partly meant to protect Mercedes-Benz Group AG, where Chinese entities together own nearly a fifth of the shares.

Republicans and Democrats from states that build cars are aiming to deliver legislation to President Donald Trump before year-end, according to Bloomberg. Backers of the measure are racing to get it passed ahead of the new Congress that convenes in January. Details are still being worked out.

If the measure becomes law, automakers such as BYD Co. and Geely Automobile Holdings would lose access to the U.S. car market, according to Bloomberg. Heavy tariffs, along with Commerce Department curbs on technology in Chinese-made vehicles, already keep those cars from reaching that market.

On Wednesday, Republican Senator Bernie Moreno walked automaker representatives through proposed revisions to prior legislation aimed at Chinese connected vehicles, according to Bloomberg. The changes include language that would exempt Mercedes from being banned outright. Automaker representatives were on board with the plan, but a Mercedes official raised concerns and talks are ongoing.

A bill that cleared a Senate panel earlier this year would have blocked automakers with Chinese ownership above 15% from selling vehicles containing data-transmitting technology, according to Bloomberg. China's state-owned BAIC Motor holds a stake close to 10% in Mercedes, and Geely founder Li Shu Fu holds another close to 10%. Together, those holdings put Mercedes above the 15% threshold.

Among the ideas being discussed is a more lenient standard for companies whose ownership is passive and carries no voting power, as well as how that approach would be set up and verified, according to Bloomberg. Bloomberg cited one person with knowledge of the discussions as saying lawmakers are optimistic a deal could be struck as soon as this week.

"We continue to meet with all stakeholders who have a shared interest in ensuring this important legislation becomes law in this Congress," Mercedes said in a statement, according to Bloomberg.

Republican Senator Katie Britt of Alabama, a key center of Mercedes SUV manufacturing, said she reviewed the bill alongside Moreno and expects Mercedes and her state to be protected, according to Bloomberg. "We understand that there will be some adjustments that have to be made, but feel good about Mercedes continuing to be able to thrive in our state and across the country," Britt said.

The White House did not respond to Bloomberg's requests for comment.
