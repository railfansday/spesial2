NFL odds, betting, storylines: 2 underdogs to back in Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

What do NFL underdog bettors have in common with a certain billionaire tech-company CEO? Let's just say both have had a brutal couple of weeks.

Speaking specifically of the former group: NFL underdogs have won exactly four of 29 games the past two weeks. And they've covered precisely eight point spreads.

That pencils out to a 27.6% success rate.

Think that's dismal? Check this out: NFL underdogs catching four or more points since Week 9 are 1-16 SU and 4-12 ATS.

So how should an NFL underdog bettor approach Week 11, which sees eight of 13 favorites laying more than six points? Carefully. Very, very carefully.

Fact: The Broncos are on a 7-1 SU and ATS run, winning and covering their last five in a row.

Fact: Exactly one of Denver's seven victories was lopsided (a 30-16 rout of the Cardinals in Arizona in Week 8).

Here's what else is true: The Broncos have not scored more than 30 points all season; they've exceeded that plateau just three times since the 2025 campaign kicked off. And, according to the folks at SportsOddsHistory.com, they haven't covered the spread in six consecutive games since starting the 2009 season 6-0 ATS.

Speaking of history, while Denver is on a 4-0 heater in this bitter AFC West rivalry, it has defeated the Raiders by more than 10 points exactly once in 18 meetings since the start of the 2017 season.

The Raiders' point-spread record in the last 21 head-to-head matchups with Denver: 16-5.

I get it, the NFL is a constantly evolving beast. What happened three weeks ago means a whole lot more than what happened 10 years ago. Still, the Raiders have been a disaster for the better part of two decades, and yet Denver has pounded them out one time in the last nine years.

Want to focus on recently? Yes, Las Vegas is 1-7 since starting out 3-0. But it is still 6-4 ATS overall and 3-2 SU and ATS on the road. Also, a week ago with a rookie quarterback making his first start against the defending Super Bowl champs, the Raiders took a 10-7 halftime lead. It didn't hold up, but the result was still respectable (21-13 loss).

Finally, while the Broncos are 21-5 since the start of last year, only four of those wins were by more than eight points. The opposing quarterbacks in three of those blowouts? Jake Browning (currently out of the league), Jacoby Brissett (now on his seventh team) and Trey Lance (one of the biggest first-round busts of all time).

Since Kellen Moore took over in New Orleans at the start of last season, the Saints have won 10-of-26 games. Not great, right?

But the Saints have been taken to the woodshed just twice in 17 games. And both defeats were against the same division rival at home (24-10 loss to Atlanta in Week 12 last season; 45-24 loss to Atlanta in Week 4 this season).

Remove those two games, and New Orleans is 9-7 SU and 11-5 ATS in its last 16 contests — including 5-3 SU and 8-0 ATS in true road games.

The Saints' results as visitors this season: 31-30 overtime loss at Detroit as 7-point underdogs in Week 1; 24-17 win in Baltimore as 7.5-point underdogs in Week 2; 34-27 win at the Giants  as 1.5-point underdogs in Week 6.

Looking solely on this season, New Orleans has been competitive in every game but the 21-point "Monday Night Football" loss to the Falcons. What about the 23-13 home setback to Minnesota the following week? It was 13-13 going to the fourth quarter.

This is a long way of saying I trust the Saints to hang around in Chicago on Sunday.

I know the Bears emerge from their bye week on a four-game winning streak. And that the offense has been humming since Caleb Williams returned from a three-game injury absence, putting up 31, 30, 34 and 27 points.

But I also know that while the offense has averaged 28 points in the last four contests, the defense has yielded 23.8 points in the last five. And while the Bears opened the season with a 59-37 beatdown of Carolina followed two weeks later by a 27-7 thrashing of Philadelphia, they have since recorded just one victory by more than 11 points.

Perhaps most significantly, take a look at Chicago's closing stretch: at Detroit (Thanksgiving Day), vs. Jacksonville, at Miami, at Buffalo, vs. Green Bay, vs. Detroit and at Minnesota.

I wouldn't be the least bit surprised if the Bears aren't entirely focused on New Orleans this week. And even if I'm wrong about that, the fact remains Chicago is navigating uncharted waters here: It hasn't been favored by more than a touchdown since Week 16 of the 2020 season — and hasn't been a double-digit favorite since Week 9 in 2018.
