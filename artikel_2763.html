Celtics' Loss To Nuggets Exposes A Problem Boston Cannot Ignore

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Celtics' Loss To Nuggets Exposes A Problem Boston Cannot Ignore originally appeared on NESN. Add NESN as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

The Boston Celtics had enough opportunities to leave Denver with a victory. Instead, a troubling stretch of offensive basketball helped turn a 17-point lead into a 105-101 defeat against the Nuggets on Wednesday night.

Boston entered halftime ahead 61-49, but the Nuggets used a 20-4 third-quarter run to seize control. The Celtics went scoreless for 4:35 during that stretch, allowing Denver to erase the advantage and eventually move in front.

For a team capable of producing explosive offensive stretches, the sudden inability to generate reliable points is particularly concerning.

The Celtics made only 11 of their 44 attempts from beyond the arc. That represented a dramatic departure from the team's 38.2% season shooting percentage entering the game.

Missing perimeter shots is not automatically a sign of poor offense. Boston's larger problem was its inability to find enough alternative scoring opportunities when the outside shots stopped falling.

Jayson Tatum finished with 24 points, but only four came after halftime. He also committed six turnovers, including four during Denver's third-quarter comeback.

Payton Pritchard contributed 15 points, while Neemias Queta provided one of Boston's brighter performances with 17 points and 10 rebounds.

The final 16 minutes featured 15 lead changes, demonstrating that Boston recovered sufficiently to remain competitive after its disastrous third-quarter stretch.

Yet Denver made the decisive plays. DeMar DeRozan's free throws with 49 seconds remaining put the Nuggets ahead for good.

The loss marked Boston's first consecutive regular-season defeats since March. One difficult road game should not define a season, but the details matter.

Boston needs to manufacture quality possessions when its perimeter shooting disappears, especially against opponents capable of capitalizing on extended droughts. The Celtics have enough offensive talent to overcome an occasional difficult shooting night, but their execution must improve when games become close.
