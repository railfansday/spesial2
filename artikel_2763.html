2026-27 NCAA D1 Men's Wrestling Week 2 Roundup: Bo Bassett Bonanza

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

The NCAA wrestling season is back, and so is the Weekly Roundup!

But Spey, you ask, isn't this already week 2? Where was the Week 1 Roundup?

Well, you see, I was in Kazakhstan, and while it was theoretically possible for me to have written a roundup from the Holiday Inn Express in Astana, to answer your question: I didn't. So there is no collection of noteworthy happenings from week 1 of the 2026-27 NCAA D1 men's wrestling season.

But there is one for week 2, and that is what matters. Because I missed you, the wise and learned wrestling fans, and I am so thrilled that we are together again for another season.

If you want to know what I was doing in Kazakhstan, check out the Senior World Championship recaps Kozak and I did from outside our yurt (and the rest of our award-winning coverage on YouTube).

Not only do we have new rankings for the new season, but our rankings page has a new look. Pretty slick, in my opinion. But your opinion is more important than mine, so let us know what you like and don't like about them, and we'll continue to make improvements.

Now, with all the administrative babble out of the way, let's get to the recapping.

It's been far too long since our last DotC:otW. This year, I'd like to try and highlight duals that go above and beyond in their efforts to create fun and enjoyable experiences for fans. Because dual meets are the best competition format for creating new fans, and new fans grow the sport, and we're all about growing the sport around these parts.

Though it wasn't a competitive dual, as Oklahoma State defeated Drexel 45-0 and kept the Dragons of Philadelphia from scoring any match points save escapes, Gallagher-Iba still looked extremely cool and the over 6,000 fans in attendance were treated to one of the more unique and impressive themed dual meets in the sport.

The turquoise singlets were particularly inspired.

And no, we won't be featuring Oklahoma State every week, even though the Cowboy community has been extraordinarily focused on turning their home duals into a spectacle for fans. Just had to give credit where credit was due for this early season tilt.

The Virginia Tech freshman phenom has been making quite a few headlines recently.

After winning a U20 World Championship gold medal at 65kg in Bratislava, Slovakia, in August, his first U20 gold and second U20 medal after winning bronze in 2024, Bassett suffered a setback at the U23 World Championships, held in Las Vegas two months later. Bassett was upset by Jaskaran of India and failed to podium.

However, the Johnstown, Pennsylvania, native rebounded in a big way, winning a gold medal at the senior level at 65kg, the first gold medal for Team USA at this weight since Bill Zadick, now the head coach of the men's freestyle team, won a 66kg gold (the weight classes have changed a bit over the years) in 2006.

It was quite a scene, and one Bassett fans had predicted, as the most dangerous time to wrestle Bassett is after he takes a loss.

You might think Bo would then take some time off to get ready for his first collegiate match, as that gold medal match took place on October 30, halfway around the planet.

Machine Gun Mindset was back in business a week later, in Bethlehem, PA, where Virginia Tech would wrestle Lehigh. That meant a matchup against Luke Stanich at 141 pounds, the two-time All-American and number-one-ranked wrestler in the nation.

Stanich was also the guy who beat Bassett at the 2025 US Open and U20 World Team Trials, keeping Bo off the U20 world team. Quite the test for Bassett's first match in a Hokie singlet.

Bassett won the match in typical indefatigable Bassett manner, firing off shot after shot and going full throttle for the full seven minutes and ultimately winning 13-9.

Stanich, and college wrestling fans, won't have to wait long for the rematch, as those two are slated to tussle again at the NWCA All-Star Classic on November 24 in State College, PA. Get your tickets here.

Virginia Tech won eight bouts, taking the dual, 30-6. A bright spot for the Mountain Hawks was #18 Logan Rozynski upsetting #7 Collin Gaj, 5-3, at 157lbs.

The dual with Lehigh was Friday, and Bassett was back on the mat Sunday at the Journeymen Collegiate Classic, where he wrestled and won three more matches. Sounds unbelievable, right? Like, no way would he enter a round robin on Sunday after that dual on Friday and Senior Worlds a week before that? Right?

Well, believe it, because it happened, and we have proof!

The great Frank Popolizio of Journeymen Wrestling brought a doubleheader of independent collegiate wrestling events back to Bethlehem. A collection of duals was held at Liberty High School at WrangleMania on Saturday, and then a collection of round robins was held on Sunday at Freedom High School at the Collegiate Classic.

What a great weekend for wrestling fans in the Lehigh Valley.

Lehigh shook off the Virginia Tech rout with a 36-8 win over Indiana at WrangleMania, fueled by Kelvin Griffin's pin of #15 Tom Crook at 149. #18 Logan Rozynski couldn't keep the momentum going from his upset over Collin Gaj, dropping a 4-0 bout to Hoosier #15 Kai Owen.

Rutgers started the season 2-0, beating LIU 51-0 and Navy 24-12. The highlights of the RU vs Navy dual included 157, where Scarlet Knight #23 Conner Harer beat #22 Jonathan Ley, 2-1, and at 285, where Midshipman #12 Spencer Lanosga defeated #24 Daniel Herrera, 2-0.

The mighty Bloomsburg Huskies posted two shutout wins, over Sacred Heart and Duke.

Sacred Heart got one for the win column with a 20-17 win over LIU in a dual that came down to heavyweight.

App State and Illinois also picked up wins at WrangleMania.

On Sunday at the Collegiate Classic, Bo Bassett attracted a crowd for his 19-4 shellacking of #10 Zach Redding of NC State.

Cornell freshman #24 Jude Correa majored VT's #9 Sonny Sasso 13-3. Another Cornellian laid some tracks for the Big Red hype train when Michael Dellagatta upset #14 Chris Moore of Illinois. Additionally, Cornell freshmen Isaiah and Elijah Cortez went undefeated at 125 and 133, respectively.

The Wolfpack received good news with the return of Jackson Arrington to the lineup. The senior missed most of last year with an injury, but was back in rude health last Sunday, winning three bouts by bonus, including a major over the aforementioned Kai Owen.

George Mason hosted a dual meet event, and the USS Yorktown, a decommissioned aircraft carrier currently found near Charleston, South Carolina, hosted the third annual Throwdown on the Yorktown.

Virginia and Purdue each registered a brace of dual meet wins on the deck of the Yorktown, while Air Force beat host The Citadel in a battle of military schools.

George Mason swept four non-D-1 programs by a combined score of 209-0 at GMU's titular Duals.

Box scores for all those events can be found in the finely crafted graphics below.

Michigan crowned six champs at the MSU Open, which featured deep brackets and many highly touted wrestlers fighting for starting spots at power programs.

22 Wolverines made the trek across the Mitten State to East Lansing (the event was split into an Open tournament and a Freshman/Sophomore tournament).

#5 Cam Catrabone won a loaded 165lb bracket, with a win over Oklahoma State's Ladarion Lockett in the semis and #2 Nicco Ruiz of Arizona State in the finals.

Henry Aslikyan, Moses Mendoza, Haakon Peterson, #5 Brock Mantanona, and Niko Sahakian also won titles, at 125, 133, 157, 184, and 285, respectively.

Oklahoma State sent a squad of All-American contenders who nonetheless might not make the postseason roster in Stillwater.

Tagen Jamison, Lorenzo Norman and Bennett Berge all finished as runners-up at 141, 174 & 184, respectively.

Jordyn Raney helped his cause by winning the 149lb division over #21 Pierson Manville of ASU.

Arizona State had a pair of champs, thanks to the efforts of the Larkin brothers. Kyler, the younger, won 141, while Kaleb, the elder, won 174.

Bucknell's #16 Dillon Bechtold defeated Ohio State's Ryder Rogotzke 6-5 in the finals. Rogotzke may get the 197lb postseason start for the Buckeyes after Seth Shumate got injured at the U23 World Championships.

Rogotzke was doing Rogotzke things at the event.

Minnesota and South Dakota State shone at the Daktronics Open. The Jackrabbits also beat Buffalo 36-3 in a dual meet.

The Knights of Bellarmine are determined to put Kentucky wrestling on the map. They defeated the Ohio Bobcats 20-17, in what has the makings of a very decent rivalry.

Mizzou swept three non-D1 opponents on Monday, during a week in which legendary Tiger head coach Brian Smith announced this would be his last year at the helm of the program he built into a power over 29 years. Associate Head Coach Kendric Maple will pick up the reins beginning next season.

Utah Valley continues to make its wrestling program one of the most popular teams on campus. The Wolverines fell to Iowa State 31-11 on Friday, but 2,588 fans showed out for the primetime dual. UVU's unranked Layne Kleimann gave the attendees reason to cheer when he defeated #9 Garrett Grice 13-2 at 133.

The best dual of Sunday was in Lincoln, Nebraska, where the trophy-hunting Cornhuskers handled Wyoming 28-9, though the Cowboys did notch two noteworthy wins at 133, where #17 Luke Willochell upset #7 Jacob Van Dee, 5-4, and at 197, where #4 Joey Novak beat #5 Camden McDanel, 4-1.

Oklahoma also registered a dub over Northern Colorado, 41-4.
