Why WWE is pushing ahead with Crown Jewel in Saudi Arabia despite safety concerns

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

WWE's Crown Jewel event on Saturday is getting a new location, but the company isn't leaving Saudi Arabia just yet.

And despite reports of concerns from some of its biggest stars and criticism from fans, WWE appears determined to move forward with the premium live event.

Riyadh Season confirmed last week that Crown Jewel, originally scheduled for Riyadh Season Stadium at KAFD, would move to a new venue. The announcement said ticket holders would receive full refunds, with tickets going back on sale within 48 hours.

The event will now take place at the 8,000-seat The Venue at ANB Arena, according to the annual Saudi festival's social account. WWE hasn't publicly addressed the relocation.

The venue change hasn't done much to ease concerns surrounding the event, which comes amid ongoing regional conflict and security concerns following attacks in Saudi Arabia. The situation has prompted U.S. travel warnings and raised questions about whether major sporting events should continue in the region.

According to Fightful Select, several WWE performers, including some top talent, contacted the outlet to express concerns about being booked for the event because of the ongoing dangers.

One performer reportedly said that even some talent scheduled for Crown Jewel had communicated their concerns to WWE officials. The company, however, assured performers that safety precautions would be a priority during the trip.

Fightful also reported that travel to the region would largely be limited to performers scheduled for Crown Jewel and a streamlined production team.

There have been no confirmed reports of performers officially pulling out of the event. However, Wrestling Observer/F4WOnline reported that declining to travel wasn't something WWE talent was being encouraged to do.

One top star told the outlet that, to "put it in the nicest of terms," declining to go was something that was very much not being encouraged.

There's a significant financial incentive for WWE to keep its Saudi Arabia relationship intact.

The company's partnership with the country dates back to 2018, when WWE entered into a 10-year agreement to bring major wrestling events to the region. Since then, Saudi Arabia has become a regular stop on WWE's international schedule, with Crown Jewel among its marquee PLEs.

According to reporting from wrestling observer Dave Meltzer, WWE PLEs in the region generate approximately $55 million each, making them an important part of the company's international business.

That figure could become even more significant next year when WWE brings WrestleMania 43 to Riyadh in 2027. The annual PLE is one of the company's biggest revenue generators, and its Saudi edition is expected to bring even greater financial returns.

The business relationship also appears to have become more deeply integrated into WWE's operations.

Fightful also reported that Saudi PLEs have been built into WWE talent contracts for quite some time. The outlet indicated that most, if not all, newer contracts no longer include the additional bonuses that performers once received for participating in Saudi.

In other words, these shows are no longer necessarily viewed as optional opportunities for performers to earn extra money. They have become part of WWE's regular business schedule.

So WWE isn't just deciding on a single international show here. They're weighing the major financial fallout of messing up a long-standing partnership that includes multiple big events and a massive WrestleMania deal.

Pro Wrestling Illustrated writer Lyric Swinton said WWE has found itself in a difficult position because of the number of vested parties involved in the partnership.

"I think WWE has found themselves in a situation where they have a lot of stakeholders to answer for, namely Saudi Arabia who have given them some huge payouts for their shows over the past decade," Swinton said.

TKO President and Chief Operating Officer Mark Shapiro addressed the company's upcoming events in the Middle East during an employee town hall last Wednesday.

According to audio obtained by POST Wrestling, Shapiro acknowledged the importance of the parent company's upcoming WWE and UFC events while assuring employees that safety would ultimately determine whether they move forward.

"If there is danger or further escalation of any of these conflicts that are going on, we will not hesitate to cancel those events, damned be our budget," Shapiro said.

But Swinton believes WWE needs to make clear that its performers and production personnel come before the financial gains.

"It's important to send a message that the safety of WWE's talent and crew is more important than any other factor," Swinton said.

Meanwhile, Shapiro acknowledged the financial importance of the events as TKO looks to close out 2026.

The executive told employees that the company had "several goals we still need to accomplish and events that we need to be successful for us to close out 2026."

He mentioned that those goals "gets wiped away to a certain degree if those events get canceled."

His comments highlight the balancing act facing TKO. The company is publicly emphasizing its commitment to employee and talent safety while recognizing that canceling these events would carry significant financial consequences.

Shapiro didn't provide specific security benchmarks that would trigger a cancellation, leaving questions about where the company draws the line as the situation in the region continues to develop.

Beyond the financial and logistical considerations, WWE also has to consider how its decision to move forward looks to its audience.

The company has faced criticism from fans who question why Crown Jewel is still taking place amid the regional conflict, particularly with reports that some performers have expressed concerns about traveling there.

Swinton noted the company's handling of the situation could create a difficult disconnect for fans, saying, "It's going to be very hard for fans who see WWE as their escape from a tumultuous political climate to reconcile that with WWE's involvement in the tense political climate internationally."

That criticism also comes at a time when TKO's influence over WWE has become a frequent point of frustration among portions of the fan base.

Since TKO acquired WWE in 2023, the company's business decisions and increased corporate oversight have drawn scrutiny from longtime fans. The Saudi partnership is another example of the tension between WWE's business interests and what some viewers expect from the product.

For critics, the issue isn't necessarily just the location change. It's the decision to continue with the event at all, especially when the safety of the performers involved has become part of the conversation.

WWE has navigated similar criticism before.

The inaugural Crown Jewel took place in November 2018, just weeks after the killing of journalist Jamal Khashoggi inside the Saudi consulate in Istanbul, Turkey. WWE faced calls to cancel the event but ultimately honored its agreement and moved forward.

Several prominent performers also withdrew from that show. WWE figurehead John Cena was among them, pulling out of his scheduled match.

Current WWE champion Sami Zayn was also absent from Saudi events for several years, in part because of his Syrian heritage, before eventually competing in the country in 2023.

The latest situation presents a different set of circumstances, but WWE is once again facing questions about the relationship between its business commitments and the broader issues surrounding its host country.

For now, Crown Jewel remains on the company's calendar.

The bigger question is whether the company can continue to protect its lucrative Saudi partnership without creating additional problems with its performers and fans — particularly with WrestleMania 43 already on the horizon.
