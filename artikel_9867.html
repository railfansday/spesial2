Core Web Vitals in Next.js: A Practical Checklist for Fast Small-Business Sites

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Most performance guides are written for huge apps with dedicated platform teams. But a lot of us spend our days building sites for plumbers, dentists, salons and law firms. Those sites are small, yet speed matters even more for them, because a visitor on a phone usually wants one thing: call, book, or get directions. If the page stalls, they hit back and tap the next result.

This post is the checklist we use at CodeHunterz when we build or rescue a Next.js site for a local business. It focuses on the three Core Web Vitals Google documents on web.dev: Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), Interaction to Next Paint (INP) and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS). Google's published "good" thresholds are an LCP of 2.5 seconds or less, an INP of 200 milliseconds or less, and a CLS of 0.1 or less, measured at the 75th percentile of page loads.

Let's go through what actually moves those numbers.

Lab tools are great for debugging, but field data is what Google uses. Start with PageSpeed Insights, which shows both. Then add real user monitoring to your own app so you are not guessing.

Render once in your root layout and log the results somewhere you can query. After a week you will know which template is actually slow on real phones, which is often not the one you expected.
