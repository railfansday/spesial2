UWM’s rights offering draws just $1.4M from investors, forcing Ishbia family backstop

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

UWM Holdings Corp., parent of United Wholesale Mortgage, raised only $1.4 million from investors in a rights offering that was expected to bring in at least $400 million, forcing CEO Mat Ishbia and his SFS Group Capital vehicle to buy nearly all of the remaining securities as part of a broader $2.05 billion capital package with Oaktree.  AI Summary

UWM Holdings Corp., the parent of United Wholesale Mortgage (UWM), raised only $1.4 million from investors in a rights offering that was expected to bring in at least $400 million, leaving the Ishbia family to purchase nearly all of the remaining securities.

The rights offering, which began Oct. 5, is one part of UWM’s previously announced $2.05 billion capital raise with Oaktree Capital Management and the Ishbia family’s investment vehicle, SFS Group Capital LLC, disclosed in August.

Under the terms of the deal, UWM offered 200 million new Class A shares at a subscription price equal to the greater of $2 per share or 85% of the volume-weighted average price of UWM’s Class A stock over the 10 trading days from Oct. 27 through Nov. 9.

Investors ultimately bought just 684,040 Class A common shares at $2 per share, according to the company. Mat Ishbia, the company’s CEO and chairman, purchased 232,635 of these shares, which was required under the agreement. UWM’s stock closed at $1.16 on Tuesday, down 2.52%, leaving the rights offer price well above the current market level. Oaktree, which had an option to purchase the rights under its agreement with UWM, elected not to exercise that option.

Instead, the Ishbia parties — made up of SFS Group Capital and Mat Ishbia – purchased 398,631 shares of UWM’s Series A-3 preferred stock, with an aggregate initial liquidation preference of $398,631,000. They also received Class A warrants to buy 39,863,100 Class A common shares and Class B warrants to buy an additional 39,863,100 Class A common shares.

Analysts at Keefe, Bruyette & Woods (KBW) said the rights offering represents about 12% of UWM’s existing economic share count, noting that the backstop structure allowed the capital to be raised through common equity or additional preferred stock with warrants. This means that weak participation did not remove the risk of dilution for existing shareholders.
