Steelers owner Art Rooney II says Aaron Rodgers was not a 'good teammate,' expresses confidence in Mike McCarthy

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The Pittsburgh Steelers are feeling good about their decision-making following the fallout from Aaron Rodgers' appearance on "The Pat McAfee Show" on Wednesday. Rodgers was promptly released from the team and head coach Mike McCarthy was retained despite some scandalous accusations from his former quarterback.

While it was overall a pretty awful day for the team, Steelers owner Art Rooney II expressed optimism Thursday, saying he has "100 percent confidence in Mike McCarthy," via an interview with the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette.

Rooney also spoke about the decision to release Rodgers, saying the quarterback was not being a "good teammate" when he publicly aired the team's grievances following his benching, per the Post-Gazette.

"There's maybe a time and place after the season if he wanted to kind of unload," Rooney said. "He talks about wanting to be a good teammate, but you don't do that to your teammates a couple days before the game, that's for sure."

Then he added, "In general, I think, historically, what's in the locker room stays in the locker room. That's the way it's always been. That's the way it should be as far as I'm concerned."

Rodgers didn't take that track. Instead, the quarterback ripped his head coach relentlessly during an appearance on McAfee's show. Rodgers claimed McCarthy routinely fell asleep during meetings, led the team in a "1-2-3 Packers" chant and questioned the coach's physical health.

Rooney addressed that final accusation, saying McCarthy is "as healthy as the rest of us." The owner added, "health is not an issue."

Rooney then further continued to express support for McCarthy, saying the coach took criticism for changes Rodgers made during games, particularly in the red zone.

"The sad thing is Mike took some of the bullets for Aaron in terms of some of the plays that were called, particularly in the red-zone troubles that we had," Rooney said. "Mike took some of the bullets for things that were going wrong down there. He tried to protect him, and it just wasn't working."

Rooney then confirmed that Rodgers was released after trying to deliver an ultimatum to McCarthy. Rodgers reportedly said he wanted to call offensive plays or be released. McCarthy told Rodgers he doesn't get to make those calls, and decided to bench the veteran with the intention of releasing him in the coming days. Rodgers apparently fast-tracked that release with his interview on Wednesday.

The owner also expressed confidence in now-starter Mason Rudolph, saying the team was "excited and lucky" to have him step into the starting role.

At 6-4, the Steelers are still very much in the playoff hunt in the AFC. And after everything that occurred Wednesday, it might take a playoff appearance to salvage McCarthy's tenure with the franchise.

Rooney may have given the coach a vote of confidence, but Rodgers' words will be tough to ignore if the team continues to struggle on the field.
