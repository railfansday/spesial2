A Menorca Farmhouse Finds Its Easy Elegance

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Isabel López-Quesada honored the historical essence of a 19th-century Menorcan retreat while introducing vibrant colors and an elevated ease.

Above: In the living room, López-Quesada replicated the original ceiling beams and finished the walls in traditional Menorcan lime plaster. A sofa of her design is grouped with a 1960s Tito Agnoli rattan daybed, a Mexican palm leaf stool, and a custom cocktail table upholstered with a Moroccan rug.

For Spanish designer Isabel López-Quesada, summers tend to revolve around Biarritz, in southwestern France, where she has a home. Still, she always carves out time for Menorca, regarded as the most laid-back of Spain’s Balearic Islands. That vibe suits the ELLE Decor A-list designer, whose signature style is luxurious but unfussy, harmonizing old and new with breezy refinement.

Typically, López-Quesada goes there to relax and spend time with friends, but around five years ago she received a call from a Bilbao family who asked her to refresh their vacation home outside Mahón, Menorca’s capital. An old farm property, or finca, the five-bedroom residence required extensive updates. López-Quesada was steadfast in preserving such historical elements as a well and donkey stables, which she transformed into an indoor-outdoor bathroom with a patio and marble tub. “It was important to respect the style of Menorca,” she says. “We were very careful with the materials, so it all goes together and conveys the sentiment I want the house to have.”

López-Quesada founded her namesake studio as a precocious 20-year-old Madrileña 40 years ago. Initially inspired by her mother’s yen for decorating and the work of celebrated Spanish designers such as Paco Muñoz and Fernando Benjumea, López-Quesada garnered wide attention in the early 2000s, when she converted an old Madrid wax factory into an atelier and a luxurious home for her family, filled with antiques and contemporary pieces. Soon she had interest from clients all over the world. Today López-Quesada’s 12-person team includes one of her three adult children, daughter Isabel Llanza. Five years ago the pair co-founded the lifestyle brand Isita Home, selling products they make themselves or discover on their travels.

The Menorca project began by gutting the house’s interior and rebuilding it “in an old style,” López-Quesada says. Central to it all was adding custom windows and doors to each room so the island’s golden light could flood the spaces. Vaulted and beamed ceilings were left intact or were recreated. Reconfiguring the floor plan, López-Quesada eschewed a proper dining room in favor of an eat-in open kitchen. “Most of the time you are eating outside,” she says.

With Menorca’s unrelenting summer sun, bougainvillea-draped pergolas were a must for leisurely alfresco lunches and dinners. Swaths of white lantana and lily of the Nile border the terraces, while “the pool reflects the sky like a mirror and provides a refreshing feel,” López-Quesada says.

Her collaborator on the gardens was Spanish landscape architect Luis González-Camino. “My vision was to design a water-efficient Mediterranean garden, divided into distinct yet connected ‘garden rooms’ for different seasons and times of day,” he says. González-Camino preserved the property as a natural Mediterranean woodland, with wild olive trees providing cover.

Indoors, López-Quesada installed French terra-cotta floor tiles chosen for their aged look. The three fireplaces were all handcrafted in traditional Menorcan style—a little imperfect, painted white—and decorated with vintage tiles the designer keeps in her Madrid warehouse.

She offset the white ceilings and lime plaster walls with vibrant textiles and tilework in blues and greens inspired by Menorca’s native palette. An upstairs bedroom is anchored by an iron four-poster bed with a blue-and-white-striped canopy; its bath has a shower lined with lapis-colored zellige tiles. Doors, which across Menorca are customarily painted in bold shades, here are finished in verdant hues.

Custom cabinets are designed with whimsically patterned cutouts for airflow, since humidity is a major factor. “On the islands, if you close the cupboards completely, mushrooms will grow inside,” López-Quesada says.

She arrayed the spaces with a lively mix of vintage and new furnishings, emphasizing organic materials: rattan chairs, bamboo shades, Moroccan rugs, lots of linen and cotton fabrics in spirited florals or stripes. “If I use a heavy piece, then the rest is lighter,” she says. The tableware maintains the blue and green theme, including pieces from Isita Home and hand-blown glassware from neighboring Mallorca.

In the end, the owners asked López-Quesada to redo their home in Spain’s Basque region as well. It’s one of several projects she currently has across the country, including reimagining a hotel in Barcelona, and she’s also designing homes in Paris, Shanghai, and the Dominican Republic. Many of her projects are with repeat clients. “That’s how it works,” the designer says. “It’s like a chain.”
