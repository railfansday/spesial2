Cruel Xabi Alonso exposed Wesley Fofana by starting under-fire star: Chelsea boss was as much at fault at his liability of a defender - and he is risking losing the fans with his baffling decisions, writes KIERAN GILL

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Published: 02:00 EST, 28 November 2026 | Updated: 02:00 EST, 28 November 2026

We know what Chelsea’s away fans thought of Wesley Fofana starting. A few times, there was one song filling the City Ground. ‘F*** off Fofana, you’re not wanted here,’ it went.

It started from the first minute, but was at its loudest when he fouled Liam Delap to gift Nottingham Forest their penalty for 2-0 and then when he was put out of his misery after 56 minutes, replaced by Levi Colwill. Whenever he had the ball, his own supporters were booing him.

As much as it was on Fofana – after that unauthorised interview in which he laughed off his penalty miss which confirmed their Carabao Cup exit to Liverpool, far from the winning mentality these fans expect at their club – it was also on his manager Xabi Alonso.

It was the easy decision to drop Fofana. It was right there, starting at Alonso. Poor performances have become the norm from the 25-year-old Frenchman this season. Against Leeds. Against Liverpool. Against Manchester United. He was a liability against them all.

Then he gave his manager an extra reason to leave him out of his starting line-up with that interview he decided to do behind his club’s back as he also mentioned the money still to be made from his £200,000-per-week contract which does not expire until 2029.

But Alonso exposed him. We could even describe that as cruel. Chelsea must have known Fofana might be subjected to such abuse from the fans. It was surely flagged by someone inside of the club as a risk, and should have been taken into more serious consideration than it was.

Xabi Alonso's decision to start Wesley Fofana was cruel - and the defender returned a mistake-addled performance

Fofana fouled Liam Delap to give away a penalty on another night of chaos for Chelsea

Alonso was wrong to start Danny Welbeck and wrong to put Moises Caicedo at centre-back

Alonso cannot even blame starting Fofana on not having other options at centre back. The very fact he used Moises Caicedo in central defence in the place of Colwill showed it could be done. That was a problem in itself, taking Chelsea’s best midfielder out of their engine room.

And so, Fofana started, struggled, made a mess of one long ball and then tripped Delap. With that, Chelsea were two goals down, and on their way to a fourth defeat in five league games. It is hard to see how he starts for the club when Crystal Palace visit Stamford Bridge on Wednesday.

Alonso has now overseen the same number of Premier League losses as Liam Rosenior – six – but in fewer games managed – 12 to his predecessor’s 13. With these strange selection decisions, the Spaniard risked losing the support of the fans, and he stood and stared at the away end after full-time, presumably to take his medicine.

Starting Fofana was not the only mistake made by Alonso in his line-up. He also opted to use Danny Welbeck as his striker, dropping a young buck in Emmanuel Emegha who had scored a good goal during their 4-1 defeat by Leeds last weekend.

Imagine you are Liam Delap, Igor Jesus or Morgan Gibbs-White. You see Chelsea have named their centre backs as Fofana and Caicedo. You are happy with that.

Then imagine you are James McAtee or Xaver Schlager. You see Chelsea have taken Caicedo out of their midfield and replaced him with Jordan Henderson. You are happy with that.

Then imagine you are Jair Cunha, Nikola Milenkovic or Murillo. You see Chelsea have selected 36-year-old Welbeck rather than their unpredictable young buck in Emegha. You are happy with that.

Emmanuel Emegha was a bright spot for Chelsea, coming off the bench to score twice

Forest fancied their chances from the moment the line-ups dropped, and Alonso’s changes facilitated that. The fact that Welbeck was hooked at half-time told us that he knew he got it wrong. Emegha came on, and scored twice, becoming the first substitute to be involved in a goal for Chelsea this season. The 23-year-old gave his side a chance while Cole Palmer put in yet another anonymous performance and Morgan Rogers joining him on this occasion.

Alonso cannot hide from these bad showings. His tactics are there to be blamed. There are no discernible patterns of play. It resembles total chaos and no control, the football equivalent of a Jackson Pollock painting. But Palmer especially is letting his club down, having only signed his new deal until 2034 last month. Perhaps he is now too comfortable with that tidy bump.
