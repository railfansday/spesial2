West Ham 1 – 3 West Bromwich Albion | Final whistle, first impressions in Halloween horror show

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Remembrance day at London Stadium. West Ham had good cause to celebrate the absence of VAR in this division in the first few minutes as Mukasa’s dreadful lunge and an Engels clumsy hand ball both went un-noticed and unpunished inside their own penalty box or else the Hammers may have been behind with the first ten minutes once again.

Remembrance Day appropriately marked at London stadium

With young Airidas Golambeckis starting in place of Mavropanos, you sensed the home crowd would be hugely relieved to see out the first half without conceding: Max Kilman getting a yellow card for an unsightly lunge on 16 minutes only adding to the Hammers’ sense of back-line jeopardy.

Whether that yellow card caused Kilman to drop off more – who can say – but almost inevitably, another few minutes was all it took for Albion to take the lead thanks to the Irons’ lazy, loose defending, allowing their opponents to play through them once again with a simple tap in finish following good WBA build up play.

Could have been worse, in truth, as there was almost a second a couple of minutes later as the ball flew just over Hermansen’s bar.

Fortunately, West Ham had Jarrod Bowen to rely on to try and pull them out of the brown smelly stuff: Beating his full back on the outside he fired in a deflected low shot with his weaker foot, to bring the Irons back into a game they had no right to be on level terms in at 30 minutes.

Bowen’s first involvement of the game finding the Baggies’ net. And that was how the half finished with a couple of scares at both ends. No changes at the break – which in itself was a surprise.

More pathetic defending and the Baggies were immediately back in front after the restart. Engles and Kilman between them failing to land a challenge on Jimmy-Jay Morgan who slotted past Hermansen from the left. All that half-time talk gone out of the window inside two minutes. West Ham needed to react immediately so the whole stadium demanded – where were the changes, Nuno?

Piroe and Solomon for Mukasa and Pablo was Nuno’s response on the hour mark. Walker-Peters went close as the chances racked up. West Ham eighth as things stood on 66′. Worse followed as Walker Peters popped a thigh muscle, forcing Nuno to bring Joel Veltman back ahead of schedule, along with Soucek and Castellanos at the same time – Kantė and Golambeckis departing. All five changes made with 20 minutes to go.

Despair in the crowd. And on this keyboard. More boos. Nuno stood, static, deep in thought as another modest opponent was made to look like a table-topper thanks to his line up, his tactics and some terrible defending. Max Kilman – and his manager – are going to cop another shedload of criticism for this effort. West Ham’s third London stadium  loss of the season and six goals conceded in two consecutive home games. Managers have been fired for less.
