Louie Barry possibility emerges at Sheffield Wednesday & Plymouth Argyle must be wary

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Despite the ongoing international break, Dave Challinor will take charge of his first League One match as Sheffield Wednesday boss against Plymouth Argyle on Saturday, and he may welcome back a personal favourite from injury during it.

The Owls have over three players on international duty this weekend, but not all of them reached the game threshold that would require a postponement. Therefore, an already stretched squad due to injury will be pushed even further against tough opposition.

Indeed, Plymouth come into this one sitting fifth in League One, and only league-leading Huddersfield Town have a better record on the road than Tom Cleverley's side.

It presents a good opportunity for the Pilgrims to reaffirm some promotion ambitions, after picking up just one win in their last four league outings, and put some more distance between themselves and the chasing pack.

However, despite injuries crippling Sheffield Wednesday as of late, some good news on the injury front revealed by their new manager on Wednesday night will ensure that Argyle and the rest of League One are on red alert.

New Wednesday boss Dave Challinor saw Wednesday night's Jersey Mike's Trophy matchup with Accrington Stanley as an opportunity for some younger players and academy products to make their debuts, resting the first-team squad after their extra-time battle with Oldham Athletic in the FA Cup last Sunday.

You'd imagine that, due to this, the senior players not absent through injury or international duty will be fully rested for the Plymouth clash, and according to Challinor's post-match on Wednesday night, they may be joined by a few returning faces.

The headline of that group in contention for a return, which includes Liam Cooper, Joe Lumley and Billy Mitchell, is Louie Barry, who Challinor states has been back in training ahead of the match with Argyle.

The 23-year-old had an electric start to life at Sheffield Wednesday, after being one of the marquee signings of a busy transfer window this past summer, scoring three and setting another up in his first four league outings.

One of those goals came in the early stages of a win over Wycombe Wanderers in early September. Unfortunately, he was forced off after 16 minutes due to a nasty ankle injury.

Initially ruled out for three months, he could make an early return against Plymouth on Saturday.

If Wednesday supporters felt that Barry was impactful in the four league games he featured in under Henrik Pedersen, they'll be waiting in keen anticipation to see the 23-year-old back in action under Challinor, for whom he's played his best football.

The new Owls boss loaned Barry three separate times when he was at Stockport County. The first saw him net nine in 20 League Two outings in a campaign disrupted by a hamstring injury.

He managed to keep the then-Villa youngster as the Hatters ventured into League One in the 2024/25 campaign. There, he netted 15 times in the opening 23 third-tier games, which prompted the Premier League outfit to recall him and relocate him to Championship side Hull City in January.

Barry still finished eighth in the scoring charts that season, despite not being in the division for half of the year, and was nominated for League One's Young Player of the Year, too.

Various injuries in 2025 halted that momentum and saw him back at Edgeley Park in the second half of last season. He struggled to get going, with him only being available for the final month of the campaign, but still managed four goals in nine league and play-off games.

Now, Barry is back under Challinor once more, this time for good. If he can get the 23-year-old playing as he was a couple of seasons ago, there won't be any worries about a recall, and there'll be plenty of concerns across the rest of League One about how to stop him.

Despite the excitement surrounding the potential of Barry returning to Hillsborough under Challinor as soon as this weekend, Sheffield Wednesday will need to be very cautious about his workload.

The 23-year-old's young career has been plagued with injury thus far. Outside that run in 2024 at Stockport, the versatile attacker hasn't had the best record when staying available for long stretches. In 2025, he played just 13 times across spells at Hull and Sheffield United.

This specific injury, which ruled him out for the last couple of months, may not be a leg muscle injury, and the likelihood of him re-injuring his ankle isn't as strong as a re-injury to a hamstring, for example, but Wednesday will still need to be wary of throwing him right back into the deep end.

If the Owls are to climb back up the League One table, they'll need Barry fit for as many games as possible between now and May.

With that being said, though, even if it's just a 20-minute cameo off the bench against Plymouth on Saturday, League One clubs will be nervously waiting to see what Barry can do back in the Wednesday fold under Challinor.

It's clear that injuries are the only thing stopping him from being a Championship-level player, and if he can remain on the pitch, third-tier sides will have a tough time halting his momentum once he gets a head of steam.
