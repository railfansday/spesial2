Rams run over Packers in NFL's first Thanksgiving Eve game: Winners and losers

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Call it Thanksgiving Eve, call it Drinksgiving if you want. But the NFL created another football holiday on Wednesday, Nov. 25, with its first prime-time regular-season game on the night before Turkey Day.

And, in a game with significant playoff implications, the Los Angeles Rams largely took the Green Bay Packers to the woodshed with a 26-13 victory … even if the entire affair was largely secondary to U2's halftime show (more on that later). Despite all the adversity LA has faced this season, the Rams go into the holiday on a five-game heater and looking like they might be rounding into the playoff team everyone expected them to be, at minimum, three months ago. Meanwhile, the Packers, even on a night when they got Micah Parsons back in the lineup, continue to head in the wrong direction.

Music? Postseason stakes? Turkey Day-adjacent football? So many winners to discuss … and a few losers:

As my dear colleague Mike Middlehurst-Schwartz said, "Rams, Packers in attendance for U2 concert." Nailed it, buddy.

Fifty years into their legendary, incomparable run as brilliant musicians and world-changing citizens, the boys from Dublin's Mount Temple Comprehensive School, with a healthy Larry Mullen Jr. back behind the drum kit, haven't lost a step − maybe just a little range in Bono's voice − as they return to the forefront of the global stage once again with their already acclaimed 16th studio album, 'Carnaval De Luz.' However the only new song they played at halftime on Wednesday night was "Torn," the band's collaboration with the late Dolly Parton in what was her final recording − and three years after she performed herself on an NFL Thanksgiving stage in Dallas.

Otherwise, the group, which will kick off a worldwide tour next year, played snippets from some of their most impactful songs of the 1980s: "Sunday Bloody Sunday" (intercut with backup singers doing the Supremes' "Stop! In the Name of Love"), "Pride," and, naturally − in another 360 NFL moment − "Where the Streets Have No Name," the capstone song of the quartet's unforgettable Super Bowl 36 halftime show, which paid tribute to the victims of Sept. 11.

Thanks, fellas. I'll be seeing you again (and again) soon.

The only guy on either team who had a memorable night on offense, the third-year back out of Michigan had the first 100-yard rushing game of his career − and blew well beyond the century mark with 184 yards on 32 carries. Corum also scored in the third quarter and bled quite a lot of clock in the fourth. Without their top two wideouts and nursing a banged-up tight end corps, an effective ground attack led by Corum and Kyren Williams seems to be the Rams' best bet to remain viable in the coming weeks.

The Rams' 10th-year HC beat Green Bay's Matt LaFleur, his former offensive coordinator, for the first time in what was their sixth head-to-head matchup.

One of the league's dominant defensive forces returned to action nearly a year after shredding his knee in Denver. Parsons, unsurprisingly, didn't make a huge impact Thursday while performing on a limited snap count but did collect his first sack since ... Thanksgiving last year in Detroit. Now he gets 11 days to shake off any further cobwebs − while emphasizing the import of run defense − before the Pack take the field again on Dec. 6 at New Orleans.

Wait, what? Don't worry, it's what the Rams dubbed their new, mostly white throwback uniforms, which debuted for this game. It's the first time they've sported the white horns since moving into SoFi Stadium in 2020. The entire ensemble is meant to honor the team's legendary "Fearsome Foursome" defensive line from the 1960s, which featured Rosey Grier, Lamar Lundy and Hall of Famers Deacon Jones and Merlin Olsen. Nice, clean look.

Won't touch the pre-game show right now, nor a postgame that's very similar to Amazon's formula. But play-by-play man Noah Eagle is a blossoming superstar point guard who deserves to play for a higher-profile team. Hall of Fame linebacker Luke Kuechly is an insightful analyst who provides nice football pearls without a lot of fluff. (He's also doing a better job looking into the camera during the booth shots than he did in Australia.) He should continue to elevate with more reps in the role. And our old USA TODAY Sports colleague Tom Pelissero is one of the best insiders in the business and a delightful guy with a cutting sense of humor − yet also versatile enough to do the sideline role ... before he starts chucking turkey into the stands. Here's hoping for more Netflix inventory in 2027.

The win vaulted them into the NFC's final projected wild-card spot, LA improving to 7-4 while extending its winning streak and only having to play one more game − though against the formidable Kansas City Chiefs − over the next 2½ weeks.

A team that's resided as the NFC's seventh seed in recent years slipped to ninth place Wednesday night behind the Rams (and Seattle Seahawks). Parsons might be back, but the clock is ticking for a team that needs to iron out its run game and offensive line issues, amid other shortcomings.

Probably a game tape the Rams' Matthew Stafford and Packers' Jordan Love would prefer to burn after combining for one TD pass and three INTs. Neither cracked 200 yards through the air.
