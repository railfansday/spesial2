HBO's 26-Episode R-Rated Thriller Series Has One Of Its Best Casts

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Although one famous HBO thriller has arguably the best TV cast of the 2020s, this ironically hurt the show’s reception in its later years. From classic sitcoms like Seinfeld to dark psychological thrillers like True Detective, every TV show lives and dies based on the strength of its casting. Even shows that are seemingly driven by action rather than character need great casting to succeed.

One need only look at the critical acclaim and audience love heaped on Prime Video’s action thriller Reacher, compared to the muted reception of Tom Cruise’s earlier movie adaptation Jack Reacher, to see this principle in action. Cruise is a massive A-list and was even more famous and bankable back in 2012, but the comparatively little-known Alan Ritchson was a much better fit for the role of Reacher. As such, the lower-budget, less explosive series earned a much better reception.

However, as surprising as it might sound, a great cast can also sometimes prove to be a problem for some shows. Prime Video’s Patricia Cornwell adaptation Scarpetta assembled an astounding cast including Nicole Kidman, Rosy McEwen, Simon Baker, Bobby Cannavale, Ariana DeBose, and even Jamie Lee Curtis, but the show had no idea what to do with all these A-list names. Similarly, True Detective season 2’s starry cast left the series with no clear main character, and no narrative thrust as a result. Then, there is the tragic case of HBO’s R-rated three-season thriller Euphoria.

It has only been seven years since Euphoria debuted in 2019. However, the show’s immeasurable impact on the pop culture zeitgeist makes it difficult to introduce the series in 2026. Technically a high school character drama, Euphoria presented an absurdly heightened, debauched world of sex, drugs, and violence that made Gossip Girl’s worst excesses look like Cocomelon.

The show also assembled a cast that included Zendaya, Sydney Sweeney, Jacob Elordi, Maude Apatow, Hunter Schafer, Colman Domingo. Austin Abrams, and many, many more. However, this rundown might be a little misleading. Technically, Sweeney had appeared in HBO’s Sharp Objects and Hulu’s adaptation of Margaret Atwood’s The Handmaid’s Tale before Euphoria.

However, she was nowhere near the A-list mainstream fame that hits like Anyone But You and The Housemaid later afforded her. Similarly, Elordi was years away from Wuthering Heights, Frankenstein, or even Saltburn. Schafer’s roles in The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes and Blade Runner 2099 were still years ahead of her, as was Abrams’ one-two punch of Resident Evil and Weapons.

Of the show’s massive, starry cast, it was mainly Zendaya and Domingo who were already established stars, and even they were both nowhere near as big as they later became after the show’s success. At this stage, Zendaya had Spider-Man: Homecoming and The Greatest Showman under her belt, but the Dune movies, The Odyssey, and three more Spider-Man sequels awaited.

Domingo had an astounding history of consistent work on the small and big screen, but his blockbuster roles were all ahead of him. This is important to note since it explains why, rather than helping the HBO show gain more attention, the astounding cast also unintentionally helped derail Euphoria’s season 3 story and the show’s legacy more broadly.

To be clear, the bizarre ending of Euphoria was an unforced error, as there are countless better ways that the show could have ended. However, the fact that the entire main cast were busy with bigger, better projects meant the final season was repeatedly delayed and pushed off, which, in turn, prompted a big in-universe time jump to justify their new ages and life stages. This meant that Euphoria season 3 looked and felt nothing like its predecessors, exacerbating the existing cultural consensus that the HBO show had become a parody of its old self.
