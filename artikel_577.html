Jason Iskandar’s ‘First Breath After Coma’ Revisits Lunar New Year’s Public Return to Indonesia After a Three-Decade Ban, First Looks Unveiled (EXCLUSIVE)

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Indonesian filmmaker Jason Iskandar‘s “First Breath After Coma,” a drama set as the country’s Chinese community prepared to celebrate the Lunar New Year openly for the first time in more than 30 years, has revealed first-look images of its three leads.

The stills were unveiled at Indonesia’s JAFF Market, which takes place alongside the 21st Jogja-Netpac Asian Film Festival (JAFF) in Yogyakarta. They show Ladya Cheryl, Morgan Oey and Abby Latip as three siblings who find their way back to one another in Jakarta at the turn of the millennium. Written and directed by Iskandar and produced by Florence Giovani for Jakarta outfit Studio Antelope, the film follows a Chinese-Indonesian family during a period of national transition, in the days before Chinese New Year in 2000. That holiday was the first to be marked after the government formally dropped the curbs on public displays of Chinese culture.

From 1967 to 1998, Indonesia tightly limited how Chinese culture could be expressed in public. The New Year festivities, the Chinese language and other customs survived largely behind closed doors, kept alive within families. By 2000, those traditions were starting to resurface.

“When the restrictions were lifted, their effects did not simply disappear. They remained in everyday life, in ways that I feel are often overlooked. That is why I wanted to approach this history through a family. Through the relationships between these three siblings, we can see how our personal lives are intertwined with a much larger history. As a Chinese Indonesian, I feel that even the most personal things are already political,” said Iskandar.

In one image, Cheryl grips a microphone at home, apparently in the middle of a karaoke session, a favorite pastime among Chinese Indonesians.

Oey sports period glasses and hair, with a car of the era visible behind him.

Latip wears a red cheongsam and stands among New Year lanterns, though her face suggests unease.

Cheryl won the best actress Citra Award at the 2022 Indonesian Film Festival (FFI) for “Vengeance Is Mine, All Others Pay Cash,” which also won the Golden Leopard at Locarno. Oey starred in “Ghost in the Cell,” which world premiered in the Berlinale’s Forum strand this year. The film marks the feature debut of Latip, who appeared in the short “The Rootless Bloom,” a competition title at the 28th Busan International Film Festival in 2023.

The project has been a long time coming. It began at the SGIFF Southeast Asian Film Lab in Singapore in 2015 and later went through EAVE Ties That Bind (2023/2024) in Taiwan and Italy. It was pitched at the Golden Horse Film Project Promotion (FPP) in 2024 and at the Hong Kong-Asia Film Financing Forum (HAF) in 2025.

Giovani, Iskandar’s filmmaking partner since their early shorts, said the pair spent more than a decade developing the film. “As a Chinese Indonesian, I saw parts of my own life in this family’s story, and that personal connection is a big part of why I wanted to help Jason bring his vision to the screen. The bond between the siblings and the pull of home are things I think audiences will recognize wherever they’re from. Over the years, we found collaborators in Indonesia and across the region who connected with the story in their own ways and helped us make the film,” she said.

Those collaborators include Singaporean cinematographer Teck Siang Lim (“Ah Girl,” “Crocodile Tears,” “Whether the Weather Is Fine”) and production designer Dita Gambiro (“Four Seasons in Java,” “Sore: A Wife From the Future,” “Cigarette Girl”). To recreate the period, the team hunted for Jakarta locations that still look much as they did in the late 1990s, and found that some corners of the city have barely changed.

Co-producers are Yulia Evina Bhara of KawanKawan Media, Wanyi Pratiknyo and Hannan Cinthya of Arungi Films. Bhara’s “Autobiography” debuted in Venice’s Horizons section in 2022, and she co-produced “Tiger Stripes,” winner of the Cannes Critics’ Week Grand Prize in 2023. Pratiknyo co-produced “Levitating,” which played in Sundance’s World Cinema Dramatic Competition this year, while Cinthya was a co-producer on Next Step Studio Indonesia, a four-short program that screened at Cannes Critics’ Week this year.

The film is in post-production and aiming for a festival premiere in 2027, to be followed by theatrical releases in Indonesia and select international territories. The film’s local title is “Perayaan Pertama.”

“First Breath After Coma” follows Iskandar’s 2021 debut feature “Akhirat: A Love Story,” a romantic fantasy made by Studio Antelope with Base Entertainment and Ivanhoe Pictures. It opened in Indonesian theaters that December. Iskandar and Giovani founded Studio Antelope in 2011. The company has since moved into series, including “CinLock: Love, Camera, Action” for Vision+ and Netflix, and in October released Fajar Nugros’ “Bapak Paling Jujur” in local cinemas.

Yogyakarta is familiar ground for the director. His short “Territorial Pissings” was named best short film at JAFF in 2010.
