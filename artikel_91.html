US midterms: Democrats gain control of Senate from Republicans

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

The Democratic Party gained control of Senate, delivering a stinging rebuke to US President Donald Trump and his Republican Party. Follow DW's live updates.

Follow the latest developments from the US midterm elections on November 3, 2026:

Susan Collins, the longest-serving female Republican senator in US history, has conceded defeat to Democratic challenger Troy Jackson, local media reported.

The Associated Press has reported her concession but has not called the race for Jackson.

After two-thirds of the votes were counted, projections showed Collins at 47.5% to Jackson's 52.4%.

Despite vowing to only serve two terms when she was first elected, Collins has represented the northeastern state in the Senate since 1997.

Maine is often called the "whitest" state due to its lack of diversity as well as having one of the highest rural populations per capita. Although it usually votes Democratic in other races, it has often sent Republican senators to Washington.

Despite its relatively small population, Collins' reputation as a moderate Republican who sometimes voted alongside Democrats often gave her outsized power in Washington, where she was sometimes regarded as a swing voter on Senate legislation.

US President Donald Trump acknowledged that the war in Iran and its impact on gasoline prices contributed to Republicans' midterm losses, while expressing surprise at the result.

Projections show Democrats taking control of the Senate and also likely the House of Representatives, giving them greater power to challenge his legislative agenda.

"I think a part of it was Iran, but at the same time people understood the importance of what I've done with Iran," Trump told broadcaster Fox News. He suggested voters had been unwilling to accept the short-term rise in gasoline prices.

"We had tremendous rallies," Trump said. "I'm a little bit surprised." He conceded that Democrats "did a good job."

Trump said he could cooperate with Democrats on several issues, including immigration, but warned that he would veto proposals he opposed.

"They'll send over things that are insane, and I'll veto them," he said. "And after a couple of months of vetoes, they'll call and they'll say, 'How do we get this stuff done?' And we'll work together."

Trump indicated he did not expect to change the members of his Cabinet following the election.

Claim: "Over twenty thousand fraudulent ballots uncovered in FBI raid of Houston storage locker warehouse all for Democrats," a viral post claimed on X, viewed more than 400,000 times, along with a photo of an FBI truck at a crime scene.

"That information is false and no election-related operation occurred," a Houston FBI public affairs officer told the AFP news agency.

The first image is clearly borrowed from a different news story. The truck says "FBI Atlanta," which is a city in the state of Georgia, and a reverse image search reveals that the photo is from an FBI raid of the Fulton County, Georgia, elections warehouse earlier this year.

The account that posted the fabricated story belongs to a comedian named Jonathan Gregory. His Twitter bio reads, "digital content creator for pro Trump administration" — it links to a Facebook page marked "comedian," with earlier posts advertising comedy shows.

Claim: "A highly coordinated scheme to steal an election is happening in real time...," a right-wing media outlet said in a X post, viewed more than 600,000 times.

Attached was a CCTV video, showing a man helping people who appear to be Somali-American voters at the polling booth, flipping through papers, marking ballots, and speaking to them as they write.

If the video is authentic, nothing shown in it appears to be illegal. Maine’s election law expressly permits "assistance in reading or marking a ballot" because of a voter's "physical disability, illiteracy, or religious faith."

The Maine Secretary of State’s office told Snopes that the office hasn't received any credible reports of interpreters committing election fraud.

That hasn't stopped users from calling the practice a "blatant violation of state law" or claiming that Somalis were "rigging the election in broad daylight." The Maine Wire — the outlet that shared the video — has been accused in the past of harassing Somali voters at the polls.

Democrats are projected to gain control of the Senate, according to AP data. The development could hand both houses of Congress to Trump's opponents, with Republicans losing in both battleground and traditionally Republican states.

As the day ended, Democrats managed to flip Senate seats in at least six states, including shock wins in Texas, Kansas and South Carolina.

Many more statewide results are still expected, but for now, the midterm elections have changed the balance of power in Washington, in a major rebuke to Donald Trump.

Democrat and Methodist megachurch pastor Adam Hamilton pulled off a stunning upset in the state of Kansas, defeating Republican Senator Roger Marshall.

The win flips another seat once believed to be safely Republican, after shock Democratic victories in states like South Carolina.

Hamilton's was historic victory, as Kansas Democrats had not won a Senate race in 94 years.

To win, Hamilton ran as a centrist, seeking to appeal to moderate Republicans and independent voters, frustrated with the rising cost of living and unhappy with Trump's handling of the economy and the war with Iran.

The Democrat managed to raise more money than Marshall, as he recieved from Kansas residents and Democratic groups, which poured tens of millions of dollars into the state.

Republicans attempted to rescue Marshall's campaign, with Trump flying to Kansas to hold two rallies in October.

Democrat James Talarico won the Texas US Senate race, defeating President Donald Trump-backed Republican Ken Paxton, according to AP data.

The win was monumental for Democrats who had not won a Senate election in Texas in 38 years. Talarico's win also moves Democrats closer to clinching control of the Senate.

Trump carried Texas by 14% in 2024. He endorsed Paxton, the Texas attorney general, despite the fact that he was at ​the ‌center of many controversies, including a 2023 impeachment and a securities fraud case.

Talarico, a four-term state legislator, overcame a flood of heavy outside spending, in what became ‌the ​most expensive Senate race of the cycle. Billionaire and Trump ally Elon Musk poured over $200 million in support of Paxton's campaign.

Supporters of the Talarico campaign in Austin, Texas were jubilant, DW correspondent Mischa Komadovsky reported.

The night was a political earthquake in Texas, as Democrat Gina Hinojosa captured the governorship, unseating incumbent Governor Greg Abbott.

The 52-year-old attorney and state lawmaker, courted independents and voters disillusioned by Trump's unpopular war in Iran and skyrocketing prices.

The two victories could signal that Texas, the country's second most populous state could become competitive in national elections.

Texas has long been a safe Republican state in presidential elections, with 40 electoral votes. A shift to Democrats could reshape the electoral map for president in the future.

Amy Acton will be the first woman to become Ohio governor after defeating Donald Trump-backed Republican Vivek Ramaswamy.

Acton's win also ends 16 years of Republicans holding the Midwestern state's top post.

Acton, a public health physician and former state health director during the COVID-19 pandemic, beat biotech billionaire Ramaswamy, who had run for the 2024 presidential election.

US President Donald Trump spoke for the first time about the ongoing election results, as sweeping Democratic victories continued.

Trump wrote on his Truth Social platform that the Republican Party would have won in a "landslide" if he had been running, appearing to distance himself from the results, implying that the election was not related to his performance as president.

"I wasn't on the Ballot and if I was, we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE! Thank you for your attention to this matter," Trump said.

Democrat Annie Andrews has narrowly beaten Republican Darline Graham, in one of the biggest upsets of election night so far, according to AP projections.

Graham, who was hand-picked by US President Donald Trump, was seeking to take over from her brother, Lindsey Graham, after he suddenly died this year.

Andrews' victory is particularly notable because Graham had prevailed by nearly 18 percentage points two years ago.

Andrews' win and that of Roy Cooper in neighboring North Carolina mean the Democratic Party has secured two of the four seats it needs to gain a majority in the Senate.

Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms made history by capturing the governorship in the state of Georgia, becoming the first Black woman elected to the top office in any US state, according to Associated Press election projections.

A former Atlanta mayor, Bottoms defeated billionaire health care executive Republican Rick Jackson. The two were contesting the outgoing Republican Governor Brian Kemp, who was term-limited.

"What we do in this moment will echo through history," Bottoms said ahead of election day.

The win is consequential, as Kemp himself fended off two challenges from Stacey Abrams, a prominent voting rights advocate and Democratic fundraiser. Abrams opted against a third run for governor, paving the way for Bottoms' candidacy.

Bottoms is a descendant of enslaved Georgians, but she did not lean on the historic nature of her candidacy during the campaign, but did recount her family's history.

"I am at least a fifth-generation Georgian," she said. "I say 'at least' because we can only trace our family back five generations — to a plantation in Crawfordville."

Bottoms said that her grandfather moved to Atlanta from rural Georgia and had to "walk through these back doors" of downtown buildings she would later enter through the front as mayor.

The Georgia governor's office had been held by Republicans for over two decades, and the result represents a major setback for President Donald Trump, who endorsed Jackson in July and campaigned with him in the Atlanta area in the closing weeks of the race.

Bottoms focused her campaign on promises to expand Medicaid health benefits for those of lower income, to lower costs for families and to protect voting rights.

Meanwhile, Jackson presented himself as a political outsider who had grown up in Georgia's foster care system. He spent more than $100 million (€88 million) of his own fortune on the gubernatorial campaign.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio's old Senate seat in Florida was also up for grabs in this election, with Republican Ashley Moody having been appointed to the seat after Rubio took his job in Donald Trump's cabinet.

Moody, who has previously served as Florida's attorney general, was the favorite to win the seat in Florida, where Trump is a resident. The state voted for him by double digits in 2024.

Despite a heavy advantage, Moody had to fend off an unusual challenge from Democrat Angie Nixon and in the end prevailed 51% to 48%, with 99% of ballots reporting, according to Associated Press data.

Trump ally Byron Donalds also succeeded outgoing Republican Governor Ron DeSantis, fighting off a surprisingly strong challenge from Democrat David Jolly. With the win, Donalds became Florida's first African American governor.

Jolly, a former Republican congressman who switched parties and ran a centrist campaign, fell short of a win, with Donalds defeating him by roughly 1.5 percentage points with 99% of ballots reporting.

Ultimately, Republicans held a roughly 1.5 million voter registration advantage over Democrats and both Nixon and Jolly were vastly outspent.

Jon Ossoff, the 39-year-old Democrat who has won reelection in the southern state of Georgia, has said that his party's "sweeping and decisive" victories in several midterm races on Tuesday show that American voters reject US President Donald Trump.

"The American people have rebuked the depravity, corruption and incompetence of the failed lame duck President Donald J. Trump," Ossoff told supporters after retaining his seat against Republican challenger Mike Collins.

The economy, political division, and domestic issues have dominated the conversation ahead of the vote, which is pitting Republican Ken Paxton against Democrat James Talarico.
