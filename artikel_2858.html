Winners and losers from Ravens’ Week 11 loss to the Panthers

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Baltimore Ravens lost their sixth consecutive game with a 35-33 loss to the Carolina Panthers, and we all lost by watching it.

WR Devontez Walker: The third-year receiver got his first shot as a kick returner this week, and he made the most of it with an 86-yard return for a touchdown. Walker also added a 34-yard catch on offense.

OLB Mike Green: Green followed up his two-sack performance from last week with another pair on Sunday. The former second-round pick appears to be forming into the impact edge rusher many thought he could be.

CB T.J. Tampa: Tampa had the team's lone defensive turnover with an interception in the end zone. This was the first interception by a Ravens cornerback this season. His 90.5 PFF grade was the highest among the team as well. The third-year corner has made a case to remain on the field going forward, even with Marlon Humphrey potentially returning next week.

WR Zay Flowers: Flowers bounced back from his game-losing fumble last week with a solid performance against the Panthers, leading the team with 97 receiving yards on eight catches.

WR/PR LaJohntay Wester: Wester may have lost his job as the Ravens' primary punt returner with two muffs against Carolina. Only one of his fumbles was recovered by the Panthers, but it resulted in a touchdown in a game that was ultimately decided by two points.

RB Justice Hill: It is a shame to include Hill on this list, as he had a strong day outside of a costly fumble through the back of the endzone at the end of a 36-yard run. The play resulted in a Panthers touchback instead of a Ravens touchdown that would have given Baltimore a two-score lead.

TE Mark Andrews: Andrews caught just two passes for 14 yards this week after leading the team with eight catches for 82 yards last week. A lack of production in the passing game is not ideal, but what really landed the veteran on this list was yet another false start. Andrews now has six false starts on the season — tied for second most in the NFL.

Kick coverage unit: Baltimore's special teams have been a weakness all season, and the unit had another poor showing against Carolina. Alongside Wester's two fumbles, the Ravens allowed two 36-yard returns and a 37-yard return on kickoffs.

HC Jesse Minter: Another week, another disappointing outing for Minter's defense. The rookie head coach has been dealt a bad hand with Lamar Jackson missing most of the season, but the side of the ball he was supposed to elevate has failed to improve from a year ago. Minter's game management has been poor during several games this season as well, including on Sunday against the Panthers, with a questionable lack of timeout usage in a two-minute drill at the end of the game. Minter will almost assuredly be back for a second season in Baltimore, but it is safe to say his leash will be tighter than anyone could have expected after just one year.
