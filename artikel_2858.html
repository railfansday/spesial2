Maverick Vinales Eyes Freelance Return To MotoGP In 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Maverick Vinales does not have a ride for the 2027 MotoGP season, yet he refuses to quit racing. The ten-time grand prix winner told reporters he is willing to work as a freelance rider if a team needs a replacement. The freelance approach would let him compete on any bike that requires a service. He wants to race, and he is looking for a way to do it without surgery.

Vinales admitted he has zero plans for next year as things stand right now. His shoulder injury from last year still bothers him, but he feels much better after resting for two weeks. He said the pain has eased considerably since he stopped riding in Qatar. The Spaniard hopes to recover fully before the season starts so he can find a spot on the grid.

The Red Bull KTM Tech 3 rider is ready to become an independent worker if the market allows. He joked that he could even ride every single bike in the championship if given the chance. "Well, that would be perfect, I could even do the whole season and try all the bikes," he joked.

Vinales put a price on his services, though he did so with a laugh. He mentioned a figure of one hundred thousand euros for a race weekend. He knows this is not a serious offer, but it highlights the value of an experienced rider who knows the track. Teams often need quick replacements when injuries strike, and Vinales fits that role perfectly.

He made it clear that he is not considering racing in any other category. His focus remains entirely on MotoGP. "I am not considering racing in any category other than MotoGP," he stated firmly. This commitment separates him from riders who might move to other series for easy money or less pressure.

Shoulder surgery would keep Vinales out of the race until the summer. He wants to avoid that outcome at all costs. "I want to recover without having surgery on my shoulder, because that would leave me out until the summer," he predicted. The goal is to heal naturally so he can return to the track sooner.

He left Qatar two weeks ago thinking his season was over if he could not fix his left shoulder. That injury started after a crash at the Sachsenring last year. However, his outlook has shifted since then. Thursday, ahead of the Portuguese GP, he appeared more optimistic about his physical state.

Vinales said the fatigue from consecutive races was a major factor in his pain levels. "We had a lot of consecutive races and a lot of fatigue had built up," he explained. Now that he has rested, he needs to see if his shoulder holds up throughout the weekend. The test will be whether the pain returns when he gets on the bike on Friday.

Last April, Motorsport.com disclosed plans for MotoGP to establish a permanent role for third riders within every team. This move is similar to how Formula 1 squads have reserve drivers. It creates a new avenue for riders like Vinales who have the skill but lack a full-time seat.

Several full-time riders missed races this year due to injuries. This situation proved the need for a reliable backup plan. Vinales sees this as a great alternative to sitting on the sidelines. He knows teams will need someone who can step in immediately and perform well under pressure.

The idea of a freelance rider allows teams to manage costs while keeping a high level of talent available. Vinales is willing to compete with whichever manufacturer required his services. This openness makes him a prime candidate for the new reserve system being discussed in the paddock.

The 2027 season will bring new technical rules with 850cc engines and Pirelli tyres. These changes will shake up the performance of every bike on the grid. Honda technical director Romano Albesiano believes the new bikes will not be much slower than the current 1000cc machines. "The simulation already showed that the difference in lap time is not huge," he told Motorsport.com.

Albesiano noted that top speed will drop, but cornering speeds will stay close. "What is not huge, but big, is the difference in top speed," he said. This means teams will need riders who can adapt quickly to different machine characteristics. A freelance rider who has tested many bikes would be a huge asset in this new era.

Vinales has already shown he can handle different machinery. His willingness to try all the bikes fits the needs of a grid that is about to change. If he can stay healthy, he could be the perfect solution for teams looking for a third rider.

Vinales remains optimistic about his future despite the lack of a contract. He is working hard to recover and find a way back into the race. "I want to stay in MotoGP," he said clearly.
