Treat the creative in your life with these Black Friday deals, all under $200/£200.

When you purchase through links on our site, we may earn an affiliate commission. Here’s how it works.

Buying for a designer can be a minefield. They're fussy about their tools, they have strong opinions on everything from colour gamuts to nib sizes, and the kit they crave often costs more than a sensible gift budget allows.

So it's great news that Black Friday has brought prices down on some creative gear that's both desirable and useful. And you don't need to spend a fortune: every gift below costs less than $200/£200, and they're all on sale in both the US and the UK right now – from Apple accessories to a quality pair of aired earphones.

Below, you'll f something for every kind of creative, from digital designers to people who still love the feel of ink on paper. US deals are immediately below, but if you're on the other side of the pond, skip to our UK deals.

Whether they're shutting out a noisy open-plan studio or focusing on a tricky edit at home, a good pair of noise-cancelling earbuds is a gift any designer will use every day. Apple's AirPods Pro 3 deliver up to twice the noise reduction of the AirPods Pro 2, plus adaptive audio, voice isolation and even heart-rate sensing for workouts. If your giftee is already in the Apple ecosystem, they'll pair seamlessly with their iPhone, iPad and Mac.

For the illustrator, sketcher or designer who still loves working on paper, a set of alcohol markers is a real treat. Ohuhu's Honolulu set includes 48 colours, each with a brush tip for expressive strokes and a chisel tip for broader coverage. The fast-drying ink is non-toxic, the markers are refillable so they'll go the distance, and a colourless blender is included for smooth gradients.

For the creative who loves making, a Cricut cutting machine opens up a whole world of new projects. The Joy 2 is Cricut's compact, beginner-friendly model: small enough to sit on a desk, yet able to cut personalised cards, gift tags, labels and vinyl decals with precision. This Essential Bundle comes with materials, starter tools and digital content, so they can start crafting as soon as it's unwrapped.

If the creative in your life sketches on an iPad, the Apple Pencil Pro is the best stylus they can get. Clever squeeze gestures bring up tool palettes, haptic feedback lets them feel their actions, and it pairs and charges magnetically. Do check compatibility, though: it works with recent iPad Air, iPad Pro and iPad mini models, but not every iPad.

Wacom's Intuos is a great first drawing tablet, so perfect for anyone who wants to get into digital art or retouch photos. The deal here is on the compact Intuos Small with Bluetooth, so they can use it without a cable, and it works with all the major creative apps on Mac and PC.

For a music-loving designer, a pair of audiophile in-ear monitors (IEMs) can be a revelation. The Kiwi Ears KE4 is the IEM we'd recommend to most people: two 10mm dynamic drivers deliver hefty, punchy bass, while two balanced armatures handle a rich, layered midrange that's the real star of the show. Note that it's wired, so they may need a USB-C dongle for their phone.
