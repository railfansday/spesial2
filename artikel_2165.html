What clauses do Tottenham have in Will Lankshear's Middlesbrough deal?

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Tottenham Hotspur sold Will Lankshear to Middlesbrough for a fee which could rise to £20 million, but Spurs will continue to take an interest in the young striker's progress on Teesside.

With another two goals in Middlesbrough's 3-0 win against Charlton Athletic on Saturday, Will Lankshear's explosive start to the 2026-27 season has continued unabated. The result left Boro two points clear of Swansea City at the top of the Championship, with Lankshear five goals clear at the top of the division's top scorers list with 13 league goals, and 15 goals in 16 appearances in all competitions.

The rumour-mill has already started turning over Lankshear, with talk that his goalscoring exploits so far this season could even result in him being of interest to Premier League clubs in the January transfer window. Lankshear has already made his impression at an international level, being called up by Lee Carsley for the England Under-21 team at the end of September.

But although Spurs' calamitous start to their Premier League season has turned itself around with three successive wins which have lifted them into the top half of the table, one of the shortcomings that remains for them is their lack of a straightforward number nine. They've scored ten Premier League goals so far this season, but only one of them has come from a striker - Dominic Solanke - while their top league goalscorer is defender Jean-Paul van Hecke, who's scored twice.

Spurs' decision to sell Will Lankshear in the summer of 2026 caused some scratching of head among Spurs fans because the striker had already impressed in one of his two previous loan spells in the Championship.

His first, at West Bromwich Albion for the second half of the 2024-25 season, was not a success. He failed to score or register a single assist in eleven appearances for the Baggies and returned to The Tottenham Hotspur Stadium with his future direction as unclear as it had been when he left.

The 2025-26 season, however, was a different matter. Lankshear scored 12 goals and contributed four assists in 47 appearances for Oxford United, and earned rave reviews despite their relegation to League One.

The upshot of this was that there was enormous interest in him during the summer of 2026, when it became apparent that Spurs could be preparing to sell him. They were reported to have rejected offers from Wolverhampton Wanderers, Southampton and the Portuguese giants Sporting Clube before agreeing terms with Middlesbrough.

The agreement between Spurs and Middlesbrough for Will Lankshear's signature was reported as a £10 million fee, with £10 million in performance-related add-ons, but this wasn't the end of the conditions that were put into his new contract.

As is standard with the sale of a young player, a sell-on clause was inserted into the contract entitling Spurs to 20% of the proceeds should Middlesbrough opt to sell him on. These clauses are there to ensure that they receive a cut in the event that Boro profit hugely through selling him on.

But Spurs didn't quite get what they wanted over a buy-back clause. Such clauses are often inserted into contracts when players could, at some point in the future, be sold on to a big Premier League or European club. Teesside Live reported in the summer that Spurs had requested this, but that Boro had rejected it.

​​What was agreed instead was a 'matching rights on future bids' clause, in which Spurs will have to be notified and given the option to match the bid and attempt to re-sign the striker, in the event that Middlesbrough accept an offer from another club for him. This is effectively a watered-down buy-back clause because Spurs do not have the first refusal to buy him before other clubs.

There were Spurs fans who were very unhappy at the club's decision to sell Lankshear, especially considering that he'd barely made any appearances for their starting XI. He never made a single one for them in the Premier League.

And those who were critical of the decision will have felt vindicated, given that he's already scored three more goals in the Championship this season for Middlesbrough alone than their entire team has managed in nine Premier League matches.

For all the talk that Lankshear's explosive start on Teesside could lead to him being tempted away from The Riverside in January, the clauses in his contract do not force this upon Middlesbrough. Lankshear signed a five-year contract with the club last summer, and the Boro owner Steve Gibson is a wily enough operator to know that they have no obligation to sell.

Given that a return to the Premier League would be worth a minimum of £200 million to Middlesbrough, it seems highly unlikely that Boro will sell their revelation of a striker unless the offer is extremely tempting indeed. With the team top of the league and unbeaten in the Championship since the 22nd August, it's unlikely that Gibson will want to rock the boat, with the possibility of a return to the Premier League for the first time in a decade potentially on the horizon.
