Yes, you have our permission, and our congratulations on a well positioned Early Retirement.
Ask daughter what she would like to do, if she could have her next two years, exclusively with 100% of both parents. (Our kids would have said... "Let's move back to Europe, or do a 24 month RTW adventure". And that would have been a great choice, and a great time. Life, can be very short - and tomorrow is not guaranteed. My dad had a medical event when I was age 17. He never recovered, and I was his caregiver for 30+ yrs. Gift your daughter... yourselves. Your time with her is preciously short.

I left employment pre age 50, as a low wage, single-earner provider. That was also the first year our kids attended school. (Free college instead of HS, available in income tax free WA State since 1993). That worked well for kids, as they still traveled, and we had lived (and worked) internationally since they were age 5. No formal school until college gave them plenty of free time to learn and experience lots of things.

Financially, accts continue to grow, and this is the first year I touched deferred retirement dollars, and that was just to gift $38k to each kid, since Roth alone grew $300k+ in the last 11 months. We're still only spending ~$80k annually. (been without wage income for 22yrs). No pension, no HC, still making house payments, but could have written a check to pay it off since 2005. We travel over 50% of the time and have rural view homes in 3 very different USA states.

Learn to spend, on what really counts to your family. Our kids will inherit our DAF, and they each give generously, so that is our legacy. Others have mtn or lake cabins, or viable, sustainable, self-managed businesses such as national franchises, commercial income properties, or (leased out) farms.

There's lots you can do with $3m, and still have $3m + in your portfolio+ pensions and SS.

Be sure you have a robust estate plan. Our state has $3m cap (including couples), so $6m would assure the government will be getting ~$1m in estate taxes. So we're busy fixing that expectation.
