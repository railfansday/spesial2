Freelancer quarterly tax deadlines for 2026: the dates, the math, and the safe-harbor trick

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

If you freelance in the US, nobody withholds taxes from your income. The IRS still wants its money during the year, not next April - which is why quarterly estimated payments exist. Miss them and you owe a penalty. Pay them wrong and you either overpay (an interest-free loan to the government) or underpay (a surprise bill).

Notice the quarters are not even three-month blocks. Q2 covers two months, Q4 covers four. This trips people up every year.

When your freelance income is unpredictable, last year's number is the anchor. Take your total tax from last year's return, divide by four, pay that each quarter, and you are penalty-proof no matter how good this year gets. You will settle any difference when you file.

Freelancers pay both sides of Social Security and Medicare: 15.3% on about 92.35% of net profit, before income tax even enters the picture. This is the piece that shocks people in their first year - it gets you to a real tax bill at roughly $7,000 of profit, which is also about where the $1,000-owed threshold for required estimated payments kicks in.

A reasonable first-year set-aside is 25-30% of every payment, moved to a separate account the day money arrives.

The penalty works like interest on the shortfall - it tracks the federal short-term rate plus three points, recently in the high single digits annualized. Annoying, not catastrophic. The real danger is spending the tax money all year and hitting April with a bill you cannot pay.
