OpenAI executive vows to keep building data centers after midterm election rebuke

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Free daily briefing on global business news.

CFO Sarah Friar said OpenAI will keep raising capital to meet customer demand after voters backed curbs on data centers in local ballot measures

OpenAI Chief Financial Officer Sarah Friar told CNBC on Friday that the company will press ahead with spending on AI computing capacity, despite Tuesday's midterm elections in which voters sided with limits on data centers.

Friar spoke at the Citi TMT Leadership Summit at Pebble Beach, California. "When I think about the midterms, I need to keep thinking about how do we keep investing to meet that customer demand," she said, according to CNBC. "It starts early with having built relationships with all of the hyperscalers, and it will continue to mean that we raise capital."

She does not see Tuesday's vote as a major turning point for OpenAI, according to CNBC. "If the midterm means more focus on data centers and communities, we're going to keep doing the work to get into those communities and to build trust with them," Friar said.

On Thursday, OpenAI published a National Community Compact and a Compute Campus Policy Blueprint. The compact covers future campuses that OpenAI owns and operates, the company said.

Under the compact, OpenAI will pay the full cost of the electricity it uses and the infrastructure needed to serve it. It will provide financial assurances before major spending begins, so other customers are protected if a project is delayed, reduced or canceled. The company also said it will publish site-level water and electricity use and will not use nondisclosure agreements with public officials, other than narrow protections for security information, trade secrets and competitively sensitive information.

The blueprint asks federal, state and local policymakers to set clear rules for how the industry builds compute campuses, OpenAI said.

Voters sided with limits on data centers, or turned down measures favoring development, in 40 of the 41 local ballot questions held in 10 states, according to CNBC. Opposition built through the summer, fueled by public fears about environmental damage, rising household utility costs and lost jobs.

OpenAI’s chief global affairs officer, Chris Lehane, said at a Wednesday briefing that some projects could be delayed as data centers become more politically unpopular. “People are certainly going to be mindful of how voters express themselves,” he said. OpenAI said it had scheduled the rollout of the compact before any vote tallies were known. Developers had called off at least $260 billion in planned U.S. data center projects as of mid-October.

Investors have become wary of AI-linked stocks amid pricier borrowing, worries over mounting debt and public backlash, according to CNBC. The Nasdaq $NDAQ posted its worst single-day drop in 19 months on Thursday, with chipmakers and other firms linked to AI expansion leading the slide.
