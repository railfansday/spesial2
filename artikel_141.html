Invest in international rail services to boost UK-EU reset, Burnham urged

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Campaigners call for reopening of high-speed Eurostar stations and new infrastructure for London St Pancras

Britain should throw its weight behind international rail services to reset the UK-EU relationship, transport campaigners have urged the prime minister.

A coalition of groups has called on Andy Burnham to commit to reopening high-speed stations for Eurostar and other services in Kent, and to support new infrastructure for passengers at London St Pancras and for rail freight.

The call came alongside research showing that annual rail passenger numbers through the Channel tunnel could more than double in five years and hit 28 million by 2040.

The report said that planned increases in services and routes, including competition to Eurostar from new operators and significant investment in rolling stock, should drive demand for international rail travel, but that the government needed to help realise the potential.

A letter to Burnham from the Campaign for Better Transport, co-signed by sustainable transport groups and train operators, said the numbers would come “only if a strategy is put in place and the barriers to growth are unlocked”. The campaigners urged him to tackle high fares and border delays and invest in more depot and station capacity.

Ben Plowden, the chief executive of the Campaign for Better Transport, said: “The Channel tunnel is one of Britain’s greatest, underused transport assets. Improving our international rail network would give people a genuine alternative to flying, making international travel more affordable and accessible, and delivering major benefits for the economy and the environment.”

According to research by the Transport and Environment (T&E) thinktank, three times as many Britons support expanding international rail rather than airports. Labour has approved expansion at London’s Stansted, Luton and Gatwick airports, although, significantly, Burnham has not expressed support for Heathrow’s third runway.

Georgia Whitaker, a rail campaigns manager at T&E, said: “If the EU and UK want to get back on track, ensuring the Channel tunnel is no longer half-used is a no-brainer.”

Gareth Williams, the general secretary of Eurostar, said: “The UK needs a big picture plan to unleash international rail growth. There is a real opportunity to deliver economic benefits through rail and to provide low-carbon connections, including direct routes as far afield as Germany and Switzerland. To get there, there’s a need to be ambitious about infrastructure investment – particularly in stations and depots that are fit for the future.”

The letter calls on Burnham to open Stratford International station in east London as a second gateway into the capital, something the government committed to providing funding for in the autumn budget.

It also urged him to commit to reopening international services at Ashford and Ebbsfleet International stations. The letter is signed by Eurostar, despite the current cross-Channel operator having deemed the Kent stations commercially unviable.

A strategic approach to international rail should mean more investment, the report said, but it also pointed to “proportionate and efficient border and security arrangements”, better connections and integrated ticketing.

The campaigners also suggested levying more taxes on aviation to reflect the climate impact of short-haul flying compared with rail.

A Department for Transport spokesperson said: “We also want to see a thriving, competitive international rail market that gives passengers more choice and supports economic growth.

“That’s why we’re supporting industry and local partners to deliver international services at Stratford International, Ebbsfleet and Ashford, and are exploring options for new international rail depot capacity.”
