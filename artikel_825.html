Carter Faith Helps Give Netflix Film ‘Heartland’ Its Country Soul: ‘The Basis of Country Music Is Storytelling’

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Faith and 'Heartland' music supervisor Randall Poster discuss how music leads the new Netflix film.

When country music firebrand Carter Faith had the opportunity to filter her artistry and experience into her first major film role, portraying country singer Charlie Heart in the newly-released Netflix film Heartland, she was on a quest for authenticity. “Me and my best friend, who’s also my stylist, we took him vintage shopping, because we were like, ‘These are the boots that will look like this person’s actually a country singer, and these are the boots that will look like they just bought them for a country concert,’” Faith tells Billboard. “I loved being behind the scenes and going through the wardrobe. We took him to the Skinny Dennis Vintage Market. He came at a good time because I was like, ‘These booths will have legit country stuff for sure.’ And he loved it, it was so fun.”

Country music is the centerpiece of the Netflix thriller, which premiered Nov. 13 and stars Jessica Chastain as a former singer who emerges from seclusion to search for her missing niece, Charlie Heart.

The cast is filled with actors with ties to the genre, including Faith, but also Jennifer Nettles, known for her work as part of country duo Sugarland (as well as her theatrical role as Roxie Hart in Broadway’s Chicago), while Chastain previously portrayed Tammy Wynette in the miniseries George & Tammy.  Rounding out the cast are Garrett Hedlund, Ross Lynch, Paul Sparks and John Hawkes.

Though her portrayal of Heart in Heartland is Faith’s first major acting role, that cinematic pulse has long been visible in her work. Faith has gained acclaim in the country genre thanks to her debut album Cherry Valley, an ACM album of the year-nominated set filled with songs that traverse retro-tinged country and soothing pop elements, while her videos for songs such as “Betty” and “Bar Star” (the latter featured BIlly Bob Thornton) have theatrical leanings, signaling Faith as an artist as intentional with the visual aspects of her career as her music.

The film’s arrival coincides with another marker in her career. Faith was just nominated for a Grammy for her debut album Cherry Valley, and is nominated for new artist of the year at the CMA Awards on Wednesday, Nov. 18.

For Faith, it was seeing Heartland director Shana Feste’s prior work on films such as Country Strong that excited her about the prospect of portraying Heart.

“I honestly was such a big fan of Shana. I love Country Strong,” Faith says. “I watched that so many times growing up, so I was excited to just read a script written by her. When I read the script, like you said, I had a lot of connection to the character and I loved the storyline. I could never tell where it was going to go. And I found it so entertaining just to read on a script, so I thought it would be that much more entertaining on a screen.”

As a film immersed in country music, songs play a key role in propelling the film’s story arc forward.

“The live performances had to serve the narrative, but also link through them so the audience doesn’t feel like they’ve paused,” Randall Poster, the film’s music supervisor, tells Billboard.

The film’s accompanying soundtrack (which released Nov. 13 on Netflix Music) blends original songs, such as Faith’s “Still the Sparrow Sings” (which she wrote with Tofer Brown and Hillary Lindsey), with carefully selected reinterpretations.

Poster chose Charles Wesley Godwin’s “Family Ties” for the soundtrack after seeing Godwin perform it live in New York. Chastain performs the song on the soundtrack.

“It felt like there was an evangelical quality to it, the way he had the audience engaged in the music,” Poster says.

Elsewhere, Carly Pearce revisits “Ready and Waiting,” a song that appeared in the 1993 film The Thing Called Love. Channing Wilson offers a version of “Tall, Tall Trees,” a song that was a hit for Alan Jackson in 1995 and had earlier been recorded and co-written by George Jones. Nettles joins Chastain on the title track “Heartland,” which was originally written and recorded by Hailey Whitters, and offers a solo rendition of “You’re Gonna Burn for That.”

Poster leaned on many of Nashville’s top-shelf songwriters including Jessi Alexander, Trannie Anderson and Whitters, and says it was Nashville’s songwriting community that connected him with Faith.

“We had nonstop conversations with publishers in Nashville,” Poster says. “Carter was everybody’s first response within the community.”

In joining Heartland, Faith also joins the strong lineage of women country artists who have been involved with or starred in both music and film/TV projects, including Wynette, Dolly Parton, Reba McEntire and Lainey Wilson.

“I think the basis of country [music] is storytelling, so anyone in this genre is an amazing storyteller,” she offers. “There is an expectation as a country singer, as a woman, to go on stage and also tell the story visually and to get all gussied up — it’s a play sometimes, almost. It’s theatrics. We can go back to daily life or we can go glamour. That’s what I feel like Dolly did so well. You look at her and she’s diamonds and rhinestones and sequins, but you don’t hear her that way. You hear her as your family member almost.”

Still, Faith says working on a real movie set for the first time, alongside an actor of Chastain’s caliber, was both thrilling and an adjustment: “I’m used to seeing an audience and having applause after every take, and [Chastain] just could do the craziest performances with no one giving any feedback. She had such a confidence about her, which I’m sure obviously comes from all her years of experience, but her aura was so big in the room. I was there for about a month and a half filming and I was totally an open nerve the whole time. I felt like a new kid, but I know that I know how to tell a story with songwriting, and I think that helped me have perspective on how to tell this story in a truthful way.”

While Faith bringing her own music industry experiences into helping shape the role was essential, so was recording music for the film from the perspective of the character, rather than her own.

“I remember having to hold myself back from putting too much into it. There was a world where strings to me would’ve sounded so beautiful, but it wasn’t [Charlie],” she says. “And I love to belt and wail and do crazy vocal runs and everything, and it just didn’t fit her character and also the emotion. I feel like this song was just wanting to be honest and lay it all out there, and you didn’t have to do that with acrobatic vocal runs.”

Faith has her sights set on more theatrical projects beyond Heartland.

“The reason I even auditioned for this was because I love musical theater so much,” she says. “I love Broadway, that’s a big dream of mine. I love that Jennifer Nettles is in this movie and she was Roxie Hart [in Chicago] on Broadway. That’s beautiful to me.”

She already has in mind a specific type of film she would love to inhibit.

“One of my favorite movies is Daddy and Them. Billy Bob Thornton is in that and I just love that Southern movie of life. The plot is not this crazy thriller or mystery or twist. It’s just a slice of life film. I think that would be so fun to tell a woman’s story in that.”
