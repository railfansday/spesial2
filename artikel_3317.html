The 10 Most Memorable Thanksgiving Halftime Shows

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

One of America’s weirdest holiday traditions: The Thanksgiving NFL halftime show. There’s always football on in the background, somewhere between the kids’ table and the drunk uncles’ poker game — with a halftime soundtrack. Turkey Day halftimes aren’t a high-stakes annual ritual like the Super Bowl — not yet. But that might change this year, with some heavy hitters taking the field. This year, we’ve got a feast of a lineup. Wednesday night U2 will take the stage in L.A. when the Rams play the Packers. Then on Thanksgiving, we get the Wu-Tang Clan with Chad Smith at the Lions-Bears game, Miranda Lambert in Dallas for the the Cowboys vs. the Eagles, and hometown show from Buffalo’s own Goo Goo Dolls when the Bills host the Chiefs.

So let’s give thanks and raise a toast to the ten best Thanksgiving halftime shows in history. Or at least “most memorable” — let’s face it, when you’re in a turkey coma, there’s some grey area, especially when Creed is involved. But here’s our tribute to the halftime heroes who have missed their tofurducken dinner to serve our nation at game time, when most of us hit the bathroom or go for thirds on Aunt Marilee’s blueberry pie.

Good luck to this year’s Thanksgiving stars.

Shaboozey was in the middle of his big rookie season when he donned a Detroit Lions varsity jacket to perform at the Thanksgiving festivities. He did three songs: “Last of My Kind,” “Highway,” and — obviously — “A Bar Song (Tipsy),” which had a 19-week drive at Number One that year. The Lions were not tipsy at all, hanging in there to pull off a 23-20 win over the Bears.

Eric Church held it down in 2016 when the Cowboys battled the Washington Redskins, still rocking their terrible old name at the time. He did a hits medley that doubled as a salute to his heroes, from “Springsteen” to “Pledge Allegance to the Hag.” With “Record Year,” the Thanksgiving-appropriate “Gie Me Back My Hometown,” and the very, very Thanksgiving-appropriate “Drink In My Hand,” Church did it right for Texas.

Selena Gomez was a longtime Disney kid blowing up as a pop star in 2013, making her big move to the gridiron, just as the Jonas Brothers did a few years earlier. (And a few years later.) Selena was right at the peak of her “Slow Down” era, though she had even bigger heights to hit in the dozen years since. “Like a Champion” went out to the Cowboys, who defeated the Oakland Raiders.

Sheryl Crow is the perfect rock star for Thanksgiving football — she’s a Midwest gal from the heartland, understands the occasion, and knows how to live up to the big moment. In 2005, she rocked the Texas Stadium crowd with “Good Is Good” as well as the inevitable “If It Makes You Happy.” Maybe she also could have included “Every Day Is A Winding Road,” since the Denver Broncos ended up topping Dallas 24-21 in an overtime nail-biter. These are the days when anything goes.

Well, you have to admit it was memorable. Bebe Rexha’s halftime gig has gone down in history as a classic moment of Thanksgiving comedy — the equivalent of Les Nessman witnessing the horrors of the WKRP Turkey Drop. (“The turkeys are hitting the pavement like bags of wet cement!”) She was definitely out of her lane, performing on some kind of Matrix-inspired stage in a corner of the stadium, where evidently the crowd had trouble even seeing her. Maybe that’s why they were booing so loud — yeah, that must be it. Oh, the humanity. Unfortunately, it didn’t go over any better on TV. Let us evoke the tragic words of Arthur Carlson: “As God is my witness, I thought turkeys could fly.”

Now that’s a star. Mariah got carried onto the field by a team of musclemen in Lions uniforms as she entered the game between Detroit and the Atlanta Falcons on Thanksgiving 2005. She belted “Shake It Off” and “Don’t Forget About Us,” two highlights from her then-new comeback triumph The Emancipation of Mimi. “Shake it Off” turned out to be sadly apt advice for the fans at Ford Field, as Atlanta won 27-7.

Your challengers are a young group from Houston. The hometown girls in Destiny’s Child came out for Texas to cheer on the Cowboys as they battled the Chicago Bears. (Brothers Thomas Jones snd Julius Jones were on opposite teams.) Beyonce was already a full-fledged solo superstar, a year after “Crazy In Love,” but she, Kelly Rowland, and Michelle Williams were a team at peak strength with “Lose My Breath” and “Soldier.” The Cowboys were the game’s survivors — they crushed the Bears 21-7,

Local boy Jack White did right by Detroit Rock City with his wham-bam glitz-fest halftime blowout. He kicked off with “That’s How I’m Feeling,” then brought out fellow hometown hero Eminem for a surprise duet. Jack and Shady did a medley of the White Stripes’ early nugget “Hello Operator” and the Eminem Show deep cut “Till I Collapse.” To nobody’s surprise, Jack brought down the house with his stadium-shaker “Seven Nation Army,” on a guitar designed like a Lions helmet. It all felt loose, spontaneous, high-energy, in the Motor City style.

Dolly did not phone it in — does she ever? The country legend did the honors for the Cowboys in 2023, donning a star-spangled Dallas Cowgirl costume for her magnificent halftime gig. She made us weep with “Jolene,” poured herself a cup of ambition in “9 to 5,” then gassed up her new rock album with her medley of Queen’s “We Will Rock You” and “We Are The Champions.” As a live spectacle, it was up there with Queen at Live Aid. And the sentiment “no time for losers” applied to anyone who bet on the Commanders — Dallas whupped Washington 45 to 10.

An easy pick for Number One. Let us all give thanks for one of the most bizarro spectacles ever aired on live TV. Creed let the healing begin all across this nation, as families everywhere dropped their drumsticks and asked each other, “What in tarnation is going on here?” Scott Stapp, in his own Cowboys uniform, leads this surreal butt-rock ballet. Dancers in red devil costumes frolic with horrific ear-to-ear grins. Mousse Jesus starts punching the air while he belts “Higher.”

It’s a bald mutant shirtless angel demon flying through the air in silk chiffon wings. Red, white, and blue ribbons flutter in the air. Who is that guy — an alien? A eunuch? The naked dude on the cover of Rush’s Hemispheres? Now there’s more of them. Two of the bald angel men make sweet love in mid-air as the camera cuts to horrifying 9/11 footage. Scott Stapp grins with demented joy. But we’re only halfway through. The mimes are here! They’re dancing in black leotards! Wait, now there’s a gospel choir in Roman collars, marching in to sing the old African-American spiritual “The Worst Creed Song Ever.”

Oh wait, no — the bald sex angels are still at it. Will somebody grab a butterfly net? “Children, don’t stop dancing,” Scott sings to a swarm of terrified-looking toddlers. The gospel choir sways side to side. Hey, is that Jennifer Aniston? No? Okay, we might be finished here. No, oh no, we’re not. It’s the big final moment. Oh, that poor little girl in her white dress. She looks like her parents just told her they lost her pony in a bet on the Cowboys. Now she’s here to hold up a white-winged dove. Fly away, dove! Fly free! Flutter on, feathery symbol of human hope! Meanwhile, this girl is trapped here on a football field with Creed. There’s smoke in the air. Seven minutes of hell. What have we done, America? What have we done? From sea to shining sea, viewers crawl on the carpet and weep and promise their higher powers it’s the last time they’ll ever slip shrooms into the cranberry sauce.

This Creed halftime show happened 24 years ago. It is a legend. None of us will ever forget it. A nation that allows this to happen deserves no better. Let us give thanks to Creed for giving us an immortal Thanksgiving miracle that keeps on giving. Top that, Jack White.
