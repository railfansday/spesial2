If you’re pregnant, recently pregnant, or raising a child under 5, WIC provides healthy food, infant formula, and breastfeeding help. In most states the income limit for a household of three is $50,542 a year before taxes, and Medicaid or SNAP enrollment qualifies you on income automatically. Apply through your local WIC office.

Only 32% of people on Medicaid who qualified for WIC were getting it in an average month of 2023, and 49% of eligible pregnant women, by USDA’s estimates.

WIC’s income limit is 185% of the federal poverty line, and most states use USDA’s chart of the limit for each household size. If you get Medicaid, SNAP, or TANF cash aid, you qualify on income even with pay above the limit.

Take a couple earning $45,000 a year and expecting their first baby. As two people they’d be over the $40,034 limit, but the baby on the way can count as a third person, which puts them under the $50,542 limit for three. After the birth they’re still three, so the baby can get WIC for the first year, and the mother for six months, or the full year if she breastfeeds.

WIC’s monthly benefit goes on an eWIC card, used like a debit card at WIC-approved stores and farmers markets. It buys set amounts of foods such as milk, eggs, produce, beans, and baby food, plus iron-fortified formula for babies who aren’t fully breastfed. Staff also offer nutrition counseling and breastfeeding help.

WIC pays only for those set foods, so for the rest of your household’s groceries, check whether you also qualify for SNAP.
