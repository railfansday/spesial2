Audiences don't seem to care about Johnny Depp's personal troubles over the past decade; they've awarded his new film a promising A- grade on CinemaScore. Not only does this signal an incredible level of popular support, but it also bodes well for the film's future at the box office. The holiday-themed horror comedy is the latest adaptation of Charles Dickens' A Christmas Carol, with Depp playing the most memorable curmudgeon in pop culture history, Ebenezer Scrooge.

Depp's last major role in a mainstream film came in 2018's Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald, which grossed over $620 million worldwide. He was famously fired from the film's sequel, Fantastic Beasts: The Secrets of Dumbledore, which underperformed with around $400 million worldwide in 2022. Depp was also sidelined from the Pirates of the Caribbean franchise, but plans to proceed without him never took off. He's now in talks to return to the series following his cultural rehabilitation.

The last time he played Captain Jack Sparrow was in 2017's Pirates of the Caribbean: Dead Men Tell No Tales, which grossed around $800 million worldwide against a reported budget of more than $300 million. In the last several years, Depp has appeared mainly in European films, and also directed his first movie in nearly three decades: Modì: Three Days on the Wing of Madness.

Depp's new movie, Ebenezer, grossed around $20 million in its debut weekend at the domestic box office, taking the number-one spot. The movie generated another $23 million from overseas markets, for a gobal debut of $43 million. Directed by horror specialist Ti West, the movie comes with a reported price tag of under $40 million. The film's A- CinemaScore grade is accompanied by a "Verified Hot" audience score of 90% on Rotten Tomatoes.

The critics' score on the aggregator website, however, is sitting at 54%. The site's consensus reads, "Decking its halls with evocative production design while Johnny Depp is filled with the 'bah humbug!' spirit, Ebenezer doesn't scrooge Dickens' classic tale up but plays things too safe to stand out amongst its long line of remakes." Also starring Daisy Ridley, Sam Claflin, Rupert Grint, Andrea Riseborough, Tramell Tillman, and Ian McKellen, Ebenezer fell around $10 million shy of the opening-weekend haul of 2009's big-budget A Christmas Carol, starring Jim Carrey as Scrooge. However, it just about outperformed the Dickens-adjacent The King of Kings, which made $19 million in its first weekend.
