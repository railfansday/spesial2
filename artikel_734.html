What impact will tyre-limit rule have on Qatar GP?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Why a lap limit on tyre usage is again in place at Lusail as we explain, with the help of Bernie Collins, what impact it might have on the weekend; watch every session from the Qatar GP live Sky Sports F1, with qualifying at 6pm on Saturday and the race at 4pm on Sunday

Formula 1 returns to the Middle East this weekend for the first time since pre-season testing in February for back-to-back races to finish the 2026 campaign.

Qatar stages the first of those, with the fast, flowing Lusail International Circuit hosting a Grand Prix for the fifth occasion since its debut in 2021.

As was the case last year, a tyre limit regulation is in place for the race, so what is it and what impact might it have on the weekend?

Pirelli, Formula 1's tyre supplier, last week announced there will be a limit of 27 laps on a set of tyres for this weekend in Qatar.

It is the third time a tyre limit has been implemented for the race at the Lusail International Circuit after punctures at the inaugural 2021 event, and safety concerns being raised during the 2023 weekend.

In 2023, Pirelli mandated a three-stop race and last year they implemented a 25-lap limit which effectively created a two-stop race.

The Qatar Grand Prix is 57 laps so the 27-lap limit for this weekend means, like last year, each driver will need to pit at least twice.

Pirelli explained "the increase [in the limit] has been made possible by the lower load levels recorded so far on the current generation of cars and by a further reinforcement of the tyre construction".

Laps to the grid, formation laps and laps after the chequered flag do not count towards the lap total but laps will be counted cumulatively across all track sessions.

In effect, that means if a driver does three laps on a set of tyres in qualifying, and uses those tyres in the race, they can only do another 24 laps on them before changing in the Grand Prix.

"This precautionary measure is linked to the unique characteristics of the Lusail circuit, which is particularly demanding in terms of energy loads, thermal stress and tread wear," added Pirelli.

Sky Sports F1's Bernie Collins, a former head of race strategy at Aston Martin, answers several of the key questions around the rule…

Bernie: "Qatar is a very high-speed track and there used to be some very aggressive, sharp-edged kerbs that led to some cuts in the sidewalls of tyres.

"That resulted in quite strong limits on the number of laps in 2023 when an 18-lap tyre limit was imposed.

"That very short limit combined with very hot conditions that year, when the race was held in October, made it very difficult for drivers. They were pushing very hard every lap in very high temperatures, which is one of the reasons why we now declare a heat hazard if it's over 31 degrees and drivers can wear a cooling vest.

"Since then, the track has removed some of the harshest edges of the kerbs, which resulted, alongside some cool conditions, teams attempting a one-stop strategy in the 2024 race.

"The difficulty with that was that even though the tyres were now not getting damaged, there was still lots of wear on the tyre because of the high-speed nature of the track. The tyre was wearing down on the surface more than Pirelli were happy with on those one-stop strategies which is why last year we had a 25-lap limit imposed and this year we have 27-lap limit."

How will it change how the teams prepare?

Bernie: "I think teams will have a heads up from Pirelli way before we found out, so they'll have had much longer to prepare for it.

"I think because it's not a Sprint weekend, teams have quite a good allocation of medium or hard tyres in order to do what they need to do. The problem with a Sprint weekend, which Qatar was last year, is that the 25-lap limit also applied through the Sprint itself and Qualifying as it's all cumulative through the weekend.

"So this year, with a normal weekend, and particularly with the medium tyres, the teams have loads available to play with.

"So I'd expect the race to be similar to last year in terms of something like two sets of medium and one set of hard. They will probably still run a hard tyre in Practice Two to try it and then they'll save one for their race allocation. Some teams might save two for the race allocation.

"But I think with it not being a Sprint, the teams should have loads of tyres available to make it happen and it'll just depend on whether they think medium or hard is their preferred tyre for the race."

Bernie: "I don't think the 27-lap limit is very restrictive in terms of strategy because there is lots of freedom with the 57-lap race.

"The earliest you could stop and still do a two-stop race is lap three. So you'd do three laps on one tyre and then one stint of 27 laps followed by another of 27 laps. That strategy might be possible if there's a Safety Car early in the race, meaning you'd have to do one more stop to the end.

"We're not expecting really hot conditions, but not really cold conditions either, so I think the 27-lap limit, depending on when you stop, won't necessarily mean that the drivers can push every single lap. It won't be tyre management and more tyre management because drivers are doing a one-stop, but it won't be pushing every single lap with really short stints like we had in 2023 with the 18-lap limit either. It will be somewhere in between."

Could the rule advantage or disadvantage anyone?

Bernie: "It's hard to say which teams might benefit, or who the rule might hurt, because unlike previous years, we've not had teams really suffering more than others with tyre degradation.

"So I think it's not necessarily going to benefit a lot of teams."

Will teams be wary of avoiding McLaren's 2025 mistake of not pitting under an early Safety Car?

Bernie: "In 2025, the Safety Car came out on lap seven, which was exactly the right lap that you could make it to the end by doing one more stop. McLaren were the only team not to take that and suffered, losing positions as a result.

"This year the lap that can happen is lap three.

"That is very early in the race but because the pit loss in Qatar is so long (26.5s) if the degradation is low enough, which will be driven by track temperature, then you should take the Safety Car stop early on and try to do one stop from there because the saving under the Safety Car is significant compared to the time lost with the pit stop."
