Home Depot Has Raised Its Dividend 17 Straight Years. Here's What $10,000 Invested Pays Annually.

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Shares are down 15% in five years, masking the company's impressive dividend streak.

By Neil Patel – Nov 25, 2026 at 3:15AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Home Depot ›

As of Nov. 23, shares of Home Depot (HD +2.21%) were down 15% over the past five years. That’s not a particularly impressive performance — but income investors who hold the shares might not complain. The leading home improvement chain continues to report steady profits and free cash flow, supporting a dividend yield of 2.7% even during a prolonged rough patch in the housing market.

The track record is noteworthy. This retail stock has raised its dividend for 17 straight years. If you bought $10,000 worth of Home Depot now, here’s what it would pay you annually.

Home Depot pays a quarterly dividend of $2.33 per share, or $9.32 annually. At the current share price of $346.06, $10,000 would buy you just under 29 shares. That stake will bring in about $269 in dividend income every year.

That might not sound like much. But as noted, management has a streak of annual payout hikes that began in 2010. Back in 2009, the quarterly payout was $0.225 per share. Today, it's more than 10 times higher.

The recent raises have been modest, however. February's hike was just 1.3%, following a 2.2% increase in 2025. Those were the smallest bumps of the entire streak. Management is being more careful with its funds during this rough environment for the housing market.

That's a prudent strategy. Blue chip dividend stocks aim to protect their streaks in difficult periods. And the last few years haven't been easy. High inflation, high interest rates, and low housing turnover have put a damper on Home Depot's growth.

The company's economic moat is built on its scale. The world's largest home improvement retailer generated $43.4 billion in sales in its fiscal 2026 third quarter, which ended Nov. 1.

It now runs 2,372 stores and more than 1,320 SRS distribution locations. That size brings buying power, as well as inventory availability that smaller rivals can't match. And the brand pulls in professional contractors and DIYers alike.

The advantage shows up in the company’s cash flow. Through the first nine months of its fiscal 2026, operating cash flow hit $15.5 billion. Subtract $2.7 billion in capital expenditures, and free cash flow came to $12.8 billion. Dividends amounted to just $7 billion, or about 55% of it. That's a comfortable cushion.

The valuation is reasonable, too. Shares trade at about 24 times trailing earnings, in line with the stock's 10-year average of 22.8. That's a fair price. It's certainly not a steal.

Income investors don't need a bargain. They're after reliable payouts that keep growing. Home Depot delivers in this regard.
