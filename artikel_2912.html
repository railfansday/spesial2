HP: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Margin pressure from memory chip costs and a consumer slump sits alongside a $1 billion savings target that is still ahead.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 25, 2026 at 11:10PM EST

On its fourth-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 24, 2026, HP (HPQ +11.49%) announced fourth-quarter revenue of $16 billion, representing 9% growth year over year. Commercial hardware demand and the adoption of artificial intelligence helped offset consumer market softness and rising component costs. HP returned over $2.3 billion to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases in fiscal year 2026. Three takeaways from the call stand out for long-term investors.

Enterprise clients are accelerating device upgrades as local artificial intelligence (AI) processing reduces latency and keeps proprietary corporate data on-site. Demand for higher-value commercial devices and workstations lifted Personal Systems segment revenue 14% year over year.

Escalating memory chip costs and excess channel inventory pulled consumer revenue down 7% year over year, compressing Personal Systems operating margins to 3%. Management is reducing bill-of-materials costs and adjusting pricing to restore segment margins to target levels before fiscal year-end.

Declining office printing supplies volume reduced overall print segment revenue 1% year over year, but expansion in subscription services and refillable tank printers protected segment profitability. Refillable tank hardware placements increased total customer lifetime value and lifted the printing segment's operating margins above target levels.

Component inflation and soft personal computer demand create near-term friction, but structural cost reductions and expanding enterprise adoption of edge computing create a clearer path to profit recovery. Management relies on disciplined pricing, software attach, and commercial workstation growth while modernizing global supply chains.

Free cash flow generation supports consistent shareholder returns, including an 11th consecutive annual dividend increase. Enterprise customers migrating artificial intelligence tasks locally provide HP a durable structural tailwind.
