Citgo posted record quarterly profit as fuel-making margins boomed

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Net income reached $2.2 billion, up from $936 million in the second quarter, as the refiner's plants ran at record crude rates

Citgo Petroleum reported record third-quarter net income of $2.2 billion on Thursday, citing robust refining margins and record refinery performance. Net income was $936 million in the second quarter. The Houston-based refiner is owned by Venezuela's state-owned Petróleos de Venezuela SA, according to Bloomberg.

EBITDA was $3.0 billion in the third quarter, compared with $1.4 billion in the second quarter, the company said. It called the quarter its best on record for earnings. Year-to-date adjusted EBITDA of $4.7 billion already exceeds the company's previous full-year record, set in 2022.

Total refinery throughput was 904,000 barrels per day, up 10% from the second quarter. Crude processing hit a record 855,000 barrels per day across the three refineries, at an average crude utilization rate of 103%. Distillate production also set a record at 365,000 barrels per day.

Citgo built $2.0 billion in cash during the quarter and ended it with $4.9 billion in liquidity.

"Our refineries ran at record rates with strong margin capture," Carlos Jordá, Citgo's president and CEO, said in a statement. He said the company generated significant cash while continuing to invest in strategic projects.

Citgo projects full-year 2026 EBITDA of $7.2 billion to $7.5 billion and a year-end cash balance of $6.7 billion to $7.0 billion, assuming no material changes in working capital, the company said. It described current refining markets as reflecting a tight supply-demand balance and a geopolitical risk premium. The EBITDA forecast marks an increase over the $5.8 billion figure Citgo issued alongside its August second-quarter report.

Projected 2026 capital spending and turnaround and catalyst spending total $856 million, the company said. That includes three strategic capital projects, two at the Corpus Christi Refinery and one at the Lake Charles Refinery.

Refiners and oil majors alike have enjoyed strong profits lately as the Iran war and the aftereffects of fighting in Ukraine upended fuel markets, according to Bloomberg.

Citgo's profits are drawing scrutiny amid a contest between Venezuela and the U.S. government, plus investors, over whether the refiner should be sold to cover what Petróleos de Venezuela owes its creditors. A U.S. federal judge picked Amber Energy, an Elliott Investment Management affiliate, to win the sale last year on the strength of a $5.9 billion bid, according to Bloomberg.
