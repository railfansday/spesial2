Christian Parker will remain Cowboys' defensive coordinator, but play-calling duties handed to Mike Pettine

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Christian Parker's job as Dallas Cowboys defensive coordinator is safe for now, but he will no longer be calling the plays, head coach Brian Schottenheimer said on Wednesday.

Mike Pettine, who was added last month as a senior defensive assistant to support Parker, will have play-calling responsibilities going forward.

"A different perspective [will be] good for us," Schottenheimer said. "Play-calling is a very collaborative thing."

The decision comes after another poor showing by the Dallas defense during Sunday's 46-40 loss to the San Francisco 49ers. After the game, head coach Brian Schottenheimer didn't exactly show support for the 34-year-old Parker, who was hired in the offseason after two seasons on Nick Sirianni's staff with the Philadelphia Eagles.

"At the end of the day, we tackled like s**t in the second half," Scottenheimer said after the 49ers recorded 584 yards of offense, the most the Cowboys have allowed at home in franchise history.

Cowboys owner Jerry Jones expressed some support for the young defensive coordinator, while also pointing to winnable close games as an issue.

"It's what it is. He's a young coach, it's his first year of coordinating and calling the plays. So you have to say you would hope you'd win games like the last that we've lost," Jones said, via The Athletic's Jon Machota. "I'll weigh his early times. I've been with teams that had pretty good coordinators on defense that ended up winning Super Bowls."

During his his weekly appearance on 105.3 The Fan in Dallas on Tuesday, Jones didn't say change was coming, but did acknowledge that Parker's position has been discussed.

"We're talking about that, but I'm not going to talk about it here," Jones said. "But we are talking about those things."

Through 10 weeks, the Cowboys' defense is 32nd in total yards allowed (393.8 per game) and rushing yards allowed (154.1 pg), 31st in points allowed (28.2 pg) and has the third-fewest takeaways (8), including only four interceptions.

It's not just a 2026 season issue, either. The Cowboys have allowed opponents to score at least 20 points in each of their last 17 games, dating back to Week 12 of last season. Dallas finished 30th in total defense last season and allowed 511 points, which resulted in the firing of Parker's predecessor, Matt Eberflus.

"He's still the DC of our football team," Schottenheimer said of Parker. "He's still running meetings. He's still leading walkthroughs. The only thing different is he's not calling plays on game day."
