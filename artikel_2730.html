Fans fuel runners through record-setting Philadelphia Marathon weekend

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The finish line brought relief and celebration for runners who completed 26.2 miles through Philadelphia on Sunday.

Participants from around the world took part in the Philadelphia Marathon, experiencing both the rewards and challenges of race day.

"I'm excited to sit down, it's gonna be good," said Vincent Lacovara of Haddon Township after crossing the finish line.

When asked what he planned to do next, Lacovara said, "I'm gonna drink a beer probably."

Olivia Valentin echoed that sentiment, saying the race was, "Hard. Harder than I thought it was gonna be. The crowd was awesome. Hills were a little longer than I expected but we pushed through," said Olivia Valentin of Arlington, Virginia.

Along the course, thousands of runners received support from spectators, family members and friends who witnessed the culmination of months of preparation and training.

Masum Abdul Wadud of Tacony described the effort required to reach race day as "Grueling. Some injuries here and there. She ran the 8k yesterday so she's feeling good today."

"He's following a strict training schedule, he's got a new book he follows and reads it every night and he's eating right and doing all his carbs hes really dedicated to this now," Carol Massa of Wilmington said of her son, Evan.

Among those participating was Action News reporter Caroline Goggin, a stroke survivor who ran the marathon carrying a list of more than 100 people affected by stroke.

"When I got to mile 17, I started to hurt. I looked down and I saw some of these names and I thought, you know what? There are people who are going through much worse in life. I saw a sign that said, 'what a privilege it is to do hard things,' and it's just the fuel, the motivation you need, to keep going," Goggin said.

Organizers said 40,000 runners participated in the three races held during the weekend, marking a record level of participation.
