NBA power rankings: All 30 teams ranked entering Week 4

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

The past couple of weeks in the NBA have been chaotic. The Grizzlies looked like contenders early on, but that hot streak didn't last long. The Warriors and Stephen Curry initially opened the season with an uninspiring start; now they have cemented themselves as true contenders. Between unexpected turns and weekly momentum shifts, the NBA power rankings remain in total flux. Here is where teams stand this week.

Utah has had one of the worst starts in the league and looks like it will be near the bottom of the Western Conference for most of the season. Jaren Jackson Jr. gives the Jazz a talented player to build around, while rookie Darryn Peterson has an opportunity to develop with a large role. However, this roster still has a long way to go before it can compete consistently.

Milwaukee has struggled to find its identity after moving on from Giannis Antetokounmpo. Tyler Herro and Jaime Jaquez Jr. have been among the team's leading offensive options, but the Bucks have not been able to turn that production into wins. Their early struggles suggest this could be a long season in Milwaukee.

Sacramento has been one of the league's biggest disappointments to start the season. Darius Acuff Jr. has been the team's leading scorer, averaging around 25 points per game, but he has needed a tremendous amount of shots to get there. Acuff has been one of the few bright spots, but the Kings need much more from the rest of their roster.

Chicago has had a rough start to the season, with Caleb Wilson being one of the only bright spots. Wilson has been the team's best player, giving Bulls fans something to look forward to despite the team's struggles. Chicago has had one of the toughest opening schedules in NBA history, but after tonight's game with the San Antonio Spurs, things should get a bit easier. With a revamped roster and front office, Chicago still has plenty of work to do before it can become competitive.

Brooklyn has gotten consistent offensive production from Julius Randle and Michael Porter Jr., but that has not translated into many wins. Both players have been productive, but their roles also create questions about how much opportunity younger players such as Mikel Brown Jr., Egor Demin and Nolan Traore will receive. The Nets need to prioritize developing their young talent as they continue through a rebuilding season.

The Clippers are in a completely different position after trading Kawhi Leonard to Toronto. Rookie Keaton Wagler has an opportunity to develop with a bigger role, but this roster is clearly in a rebuilding phase. It is going to take time for Los Angeles to find its footing without the star power it once had.

Orlando has not gotten off to the start it expected, especially with Paolo Banchero expected to miss four to six weeks. Franz Wagner and Desmond Bane will need to carry more of the offensive load while Banchero is sidelined. The Magic still have plenty of talent, but they need to start putting together wins before falling further behind in the East.

Oct 23, 2026; Indianapolis, Indiana, USA; Indiana Pacers guard Tyrese Haliburton (0) looks on in the first half against the Dallas Mavericks at Gainbridge Fieldhouse. Mandatory Credit: Trevor Ruszkowski-Imagn Images

Indiana continues to deal with the absence of Tyrese Haliburton, who is still recovering from the Achilles injury he suffered in the 2025 NBA Finals. Pascal Siakam has been asked to take on a larger role, but replacing Haliburton's production and playmaking is a difficult task. The Pacers are going to have a tough time climbing the standings without their star point guard.

Phoenix has struggled to find consistency early in the season. Devin Booker remains the centerpiece of the offense, while Dillon Brooks brings another scoring option and a physical presence. However, the Suns have not been able to put together enough complete performances to establish themselves as a serious playoff contender.

Charlotte has had an up-and-down start after moving on from LaMelo Ball. Brandon Miller and rookie Kon Knueppel give the Hornets some young talent to build around, but the team is still figuring out how to operate without its former franchise point guard. There are some encouraging pieces in place, but Charlotte remains near the bottom of the East.

Washington has some intriguing pieces after bringing in Trae Young and Anthony Davis, while rookie AJ Dybantsa gives the team another young player to develop. The Wizards have shown some signs of life, but there are still questions about how all of these pieces will fit together. They need more time before they can be considered a serious threat in the East.

New Orleans has enough talent to be better than its current record, but it has struggled to put everything together. Zion Williamson remains the biggest piece of the offense, while Dejounte Murray gives the team another experienced option. The Pelicans need more consistency from their roster if they want to climb out of the lower half of the Western Conference.

Miami has not gotten off to the start it hoped for after bringing in Giannis Antetokounmpo to play alongside Bam Adebayo. The Heat have plenty of talent in their frontcourt, but they are in need of more prominent guard play to take pressure off their two stars. Until Miami finds more consistent production from its backcourt, it will be difficult for this team to reach its potential.

Cleveland has gotten off to a relatively slow start despite having one of the more talented rosters in the Eastern Conference. Donovan Mitchell and James Harden give the Cavaliers two experienced offensive playmakers, while Evan Mobley remains an important piece on both ends of the floor. The talent is there, but Cleveland needs to start turning it into wins.

Toronto has been a middle-of-the-pack team to start the season, with Scottie Barnes, RJ Barrett and Immanuel Quickley leading the way. The Raptors have a solid group of players, but they need to find more consistency if they want to separate themselves from the rest of the Eastern Conference. For now, Toronto remains in the mix but has not done enough to move higher.

Atlanta has been one of the more encouraging teams in the East to start the season. Jalen Johnson has continued to develop into a key piece for the Hawks, while Nickeil Alexander-Walker has provided another important scoring option. Atlanta has put together some impressive performances, but it needs to keep this level of play up to stay near the top of the conference.

Portland has been one of the more surprising teams early in the season. Deni Avdija has continued to play at a high level, while Damian Lillard's return from injury and the acquisition of Ja Morant this offseason has the Blazers among the top in the West. Morant is expected to miss some time. If Portland can keep this level of play up, it could be a team that sticks around in the playoff picture.

The defending champions have not been quite as dominant to start the season, but the Knicks still have the talent to compete with the best teams in the league. Jalen Brunson continues to lead the offense, with Karl-Anthony Towns giving New York another major scoring threat. The Knicks have some early work to do, but it would be foolish to count them out.

Denver has gotten off to a slower start than expected, but Nikola Jokic and Jamal Murray give the Nuggets one of the most dangerous duos in the NBA. DeMar DeRozan adds another veteran scoring option to the roster. Denver has the talent to turn things around, but it needs to start putting together more consistent performances.

Memphis is starting to look more like the bottom-feeder many expected it to be coming into the season. Despite its record, the Grizzlies have struggled to find consistency and are beginning to show some of the limitations of their roster. Cameron Boozer and the younger players have opportunities to develop, but Memphis has a lot to prove before it can be taken seriously.

Dallas Mavericks forward Cooper Flagg (32) moves the ball against the Brooklyn Credit: Jerome Miron-Imagn Images

Dallas has gotten off to a promising start, with Cooper Flagg continuing to develop in his second NBA season. Kyrie Irving gives the Mavericks another experienced offensive option, but the team still needs to show it can compete with the top teams in the Western Conference. For now, Dallas has done enough to stay in the middle of the rankings.

Detroit has continued to build on its success, with Cade Cunningham leading the way. However, the Pistons will be without Jalen Duren for at least two more weeks, which is a huge loss for the team. Duren's presence in the paint and ability to contribute on both ends of the floor will be difficult to replace as Detroit tries to keep pace near the top of the East.

Minnesota has done a great job staying afloat without Anthony Edwards, who is expected to miss an extended period of time. LaMelo Ball has played great in his absence, while Ayo Dosunmu has stepped up and taken on a huge role in the team's success. Their contributions have helped keep the Timberwolves near the top of the West despite losing their biggest star.

Houston has gotten off to a strong start, with Kevin Durant providing the veteran scoring presence the team needed. Fred VanVleet and the Rockets' young core give the team a good balance of experience and potential. Houston has looked like a legitimate contender early in the season and has the pieces to remain near the top of the Western Conference.

Golden State has completely turned its season around after opening the year with three straight losses. Stephen Curry is still playing at an elite level, averaging around 30 points per game, while rookie Yaxel Lendeborg has been a major addition to the rotation. Brandin Podziemski has also stepped up, and the Warriors have now won eight straight games with impressive victories over Denver, Minnesota and the Lakers.

Boston Celtics forward Jayson Tatum (0). Credit: Bob DeChiara-Imagn Images.

Boston has started to find its rhythm, winning three straight games. Jayson Tatum has returned to superstar form, while Paul George has been a strong second option, averaging around 20 points per game. The biggest question for the Celtics remains their center position, but they have shown they can compete with the best teams in the league.

Oklahoma City remains one of the best teams in the NBA, even with key players dealing with injuries. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander is averaging nearly 30 points per game, while the Thunder have managed to stay competitive without Chet Holmgren and Jalen Williams. Jared McCain and Ajay Mitchell have stepped up during their absence, helping Oklahoma City maintain its place near the top of the West.

San Antonio has been one of the hottest teams in the NBA after losing its first two games and then winning nine straight. Victor Wembanyama has been dominant, averaging 30.1 points and 12 rebounds per game while continuing to make an impact defensively. Dylan Harper has also taken a major step forward in his second season, giving the Spurs another young player who can make an impact.

The Lakers have been one of the best teams in the league, led by Luka Doncic's incredible start to the season. Doncic is averaging 39.3 points, 7.8 assists and 7.9 rebounds per game, putting together one of the most impressive individual starts in recent memory. Walker Kessler and Austin Reaves have also played well, although the bench remains inconsistent. Los Angeles has won six straight games and looks like a legitimate championship contender.

Philadelphia takes the top spot after getting off to one of the strongest starts in the NBA. The 76ers' starting five of Tyrese Maxey, LeBron James, Jaylen Brown, Joel Embiid and VJ Edgecombe has given them one of the most talented lineups in the league. The chemistry is continuing to improve as the group gets more time together, and Philadelphia has earned the No. 1 spot with its early-season success.
