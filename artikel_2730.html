Horsepower or Horseplay? Week 10 individual highlights from Colts’ home win vs. Dolphins

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Indianapolis Colts (3-7) won the 'battle of bad teams,' defeating the Miami Dolphins (1-8) 24-22 at home on Sunday, but the latter may have actually won the war, keeping their projected 2027 first rounder as a Top 3 pick.

While this felt more like a preseason game in intensity and vibes, with neither team playing all that productive or well executed professional football, it was the Colts who benefitted from struggling Dolphins kicker Zane Gonzalez inexplicably going 1 for 3 from field goal range, as just making one of those two missed field goals was the difference in Miami's obtaining the victory in this one.

Without further ado, let's get to the individual highlights:

RB Seth McGowan: The rookie rusher has been an uncovered 7th round gem by this current maligned football operations department (which already saw longtime general manager Chris Ballard fired) and one of the few silver linings of an increasingly disappointing 2026 campaign. Once strictly a change-of-pace option behind star workhorse Jonathan Taylor, it's now evolved into more of a time-share and running back platoon since Taylor was the subject of recent trade talks, with McGowan rushing for 77 total rushing yards and a rushing touchdown on 12 carries (6.4 ypc. avg.) during Sunday's home win. Perhaps that's to keep Taylor fresher and take some mileage off his tires in a lost season, but McGowan runs hard with burst, acceleration, and physicality. If the day comes where the Colts ultimately trade Taylor this offseason, they appear to be in solid hands with McGowan who appears to be both a reliable and viable starting running back option.

DT Ade Adebawore: It was "Tomi Time" again, as the recently extended blossoming young defensive tackle continues to make plays. On Sunday, he recovered a Justin Walley strip-sack in the end zone to score a 'big man' touchdown. He's so far filled a lot of the big shoes left by departed veteran DeForest Buckner.

NT Grover Stewart: For a longtime veteran, who was also the subject of trade rumors like his ex-teammate Buckner, credit to 'Big Grove' for hanging in there with this young defense, getting those "big paws" up, and deflecting three passes in this one, for a typical space eater, who's more known for his interior run stuffing.

LB CJ Allen: It's hard to believe that Allen was recently a healthy benching to begin the 2026 season because the rookie's seemingly caught fire in recent weeks. He recorded 9 tackles (3 solo), a crushing sack, 2 tackles for loss, 2 tackles for loss, and a pass defensed (narrowly missing an interception) against the Miami offense. He's consistently been Indy's best linebacker as of late, routinely bringing impact plays and making his presence felt from the 2nd level, and looks like a building block for this rebuilding Colts defense going forward.

CB Justin Walley: Speaking of Walley, after the de facto rookie slot cornerback experienced initial slot growing pains this season, like Allen, he's clearly settled in as of late. He was a stat-sheet stuffer during Sunday's win, with 5 tackles, a sack, two tackles for loss, a forced fumble (*which was recovered for a TD), and 2 passes defensed. Walley presents another potential young building block to form a solid foundation for this Colts defense going forward into the future. There's growing levels of optimism for their young defensive core collectively.

QB Daniel Jones: The Colts embattled starting quarterback, who was booed by the home crowd during player introductions, continued his brutal stretch of starts. Jones completed 11 of 22 pass attempts for just 108 passing yards, a passing touchdown, an interception, and almost lost a self-induced fumble (pictured above). He recorded a season-low QBR of 1.7, the lowest of any starting NFL QB so far this season. Sure, Colts head coach Shane Steichen appreciates his preparation, but his on-the-field performance has been routinely poor this season. The Jones' starting era in Indianapolis appears to have long run its course, but Steichen continues to be stubborn and still won't make a long overdue change in favor of his primary backup Anthony Richardson Sr.

TE Tyler Warren: With a Colts wide receiver room that now closely resembles a practice squad, with Alec Pierce remaining on IR and the recent trades of Keenan Allen and Josh Downs, the thought was that Warren, Indy's sure handed safety valve, may benefit the most as far as receiving targets and touches volume. Instead, Warren had 2 catches for *check notes*, 1 total receiving yard, on only 3 receiving targets on the afternoon. I'm not sure what's going on with Warren as of late, who's receiving production has clearly regressed in recent weeks, but either he has to evolve and improve as a more diverse route runner and pass catcher, or Steichen has to be better at scheming his tight end open over the middle of the field. There's probably blame to go around for both sides.
