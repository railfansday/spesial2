Ilia Topuria Says He Was Half A Millimeter From Career-Ending Surgery After Gaethje Broke Both Orbitals — And Joe Rogan Now Wants Him Back At 145

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Joe Rogan knows his stuff, but that doesn't mean he's always on point with his UFC takes.

Recently, Rogan suggested that former UFC lightweight and featherweight champion Ilia Topuria should return to 145 pounds because of the danger of continuing to compete at 155 after suffering fractures to both orbital bones in his loss to Justin Gaethje at UFC Freedom 250 in June.

Is that sound advice, or is it a bit of an overreaction after a loss, albeit a brutal one? Let's talk UFC.

Gaethje def. Topuria, TKO (corner stoppage before Round 5)

Broken nose, fractures to both orbital bones

Rogan said on his podcast that the gap between divisions is bigger than it looks. "It seems like it's only 10lbs but it's not," he said.

No, moving back to 145 pounds isn't the smartest path for Topuria. He can still compete at 155, and the evidence is his knockout victory over Charles Oliveira to become the champion in that weight class.

It's not as though he beat just any 155-pounder.

Topuria knocked out Oliveira in the first round at UFC 317 in June 2025 to win the vacant lightweight title.

When we look at what happened against Gaethje, one of the issues that stood out was Topuria's game plan. It wasn't stellar, and there were mistakes made in that fight.

There was a point when Topuria hurt Gaethje to the body. Rather than forcing the wounded fighter to stand and potentially finishing him, Topuria went to the ground. That wasn't the best decision, and it essentially gave Gaethje an opportunity to recover.

Rogan is also someone who regularly speaks about fighters competing closer to their walk-around weight. He has consistently cautioned against the dangers of weight cutting.

So it's a little surprising to hear him suggest that Topuria should return to 145, considering all the difficulties Topuria has expressed having while making that weight.

Yes, Topuria's injuries were severe, but severe injuries can happen in any weight class.

Topuria told La Gazzetta Georgia that he "narrowly avoided surgery by half a millimeter." His brother and coach, Aleksandre Topuria, threw in the towel before the fifth round.

I don't think his two broken orbital bones happened simply because he was fighting a guy at 155 pounds. That's a bit of an overreaction.

We also have to remember how dangerous weight cutting can be.

Dehydration from severe weight cuts can increase the risks associated with taking head damage. That's a serious consideration when we're talking about moving a fighter back down to a weight class he already struggled to make.

I think most people in combat sports would attest to the dangers of that.

So while the injuries Topuria suffered against Gaethje shouldn't be ignored, they also shouldn't automatically lead to the conclusion that he's fighting in the wrong weight class.

Images of Topuria's facial injuries circulated days after the fight.

The smartest path forward for Topuria is to stay at 155 pounds. But he also has to be wiser about his approach to fights.

He's admitted that he was a bit overconfident against Gaethje. His confidence has been one of his biggest assets, but that trait is a double-edged sword.

And at UFC Freedom 250, it cut the wrong way for Topuria.

While choosing to wrestle Gaethje after hurting him to the body was a bad idea, choosing to wrestle more often instead of striking, when that's the path of least resistance, is probably a smart thing for Topuria to do at 155.

Many have forgotten that he's an outstanding grappler. In fact, he was known for his grappling as much as, if not more than, his striking before he blew up in the UFC and we found out just how heavy and dangerous his hands are.

He doesn't necessarily have to abandon his striking. But he should be more willing to use every part of his skill set instead of relying so heavily on his punching power.

The injuries he suffered against Gaethje, and the visuals that are stuck in our heads after that fight, shouldn't be ignored. But they also shouldn't be the reason he makes a knee-jerk decision about his career.

Topuria will be fine as long as he makes the proper adjustments, assuming he hasn't suffered a lasting mental setback from losing to Gaethje.

We won't know that for sure until he comes back, which is reportedly expected sometime in 2027.

Topuria is reportedly targeting April 2027. Gaethje has said he isn't interested in a rematch, and Paddy Pimblett has been mentioned as a possible opponent.
