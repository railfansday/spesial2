Cardi B and Offset See Beverly Hills Mansion Lawsuit Dismissed After Settlement

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The lawsuit accusing Cardi B and Offset of duping the owner of a Beverly Hills mega-mansion into letting them film a music video for her song “Like What” for a discounted TikTok video rate has been dismissed after the parties reached a confidential settlement, a source and court documents obtained by Rolling Stone reveal.

A source confirmed the parties reached a settlement “subject to confidentiality.” The private pact came after both sides told a judge in April they were open to mediation and just weeks before a trial set to begin Jan. 5. Lawyers for both sides did not provide comment when contacted by Rolling Stone.

When property owner Bi LLC first sued in 2024, it claimed Cardi B, born Belcalis Almánzar, and her estranged husband, whose real name is Kiari Cephus, had their eyes on the striking glass-and-aluminum mansion once occupied by Justin Bieber and “devised a plan” to use it “without paying full market value.” The owner alleged the couple’s representatives anonymously booked the mansion “under the false pretense” that it would be used for a TikTok video. Cardi and Offset then showed up and made the property’s “distinctive architecture” the “centerpiece” of a full-length music video, the lawsuit claimed.

Cardi fired back on X, formerly Twitter, the same day the complaint was filed, saying the lawsuit was riddled with inaccuracies. “We paid those people $10,000 IN CASH to rent the property for a whole 24 hours that same day 6am to 6am the next morning and we went over by ONE hour which we paid overage fees to the realtor for in March,” she wrote. The “Money” singer added that the property “had cameras all over” and both the realtors and the owners were present “the ENTIRE time.”

“I got pics and videos of them on set!! Now they wanna finesse us trying to say we told them it was a TikTok video when that was nowhere in the contract and like they didn’t hear the whole song playing and see how long we was shooting,” she wrote. “Why would it take us 24 hours to shoot a TikTok? Her lawyers sought to toss the lawsuit, arguing it failed to state any viable claims, but the judge found sufficient grounds for the case to proceed to discovery. “The complaint adequately alleges that plaintiff justifiably relied to its detriment on defendants’ misrepresentation that the intent was to merely film a TikTok, when in fact defendants filmed a professional music video,” the judge wrote in February 2025.

The lawsuit included still photographs from the video shoot and noted “Like What” had garnered more than 26 million views on YouTube in six months.
