Fernando Mendoza’s shaky starting debut primary focus for Las Vegas Raiders in Week 11 NFL Power Rankings

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Fernando Mendoza's starting debut for the Las Vegas Raiders has come and gone, and 'shaky' feels like an appropriate way to describe it. Mendoza was sharp in the first half but then struggled to decipher Mike Macdonald's pressure looks and the Seattle Seahawks' defense in the second, leading to the Raiders struggling to move the ball and ultimately losing the game.

To be fair, that's expected from a rookie quarterback against the league's top-ranked defense, and the No. 1 pick's performance is the primary topic of discussion surrounding Las Vegas in the Week 11 NFL Power Rankings roundup.

Here's a column on the Fernando Mendoza debut and the question that's been bothering me: If the Raiders were going to wait on playing Mendoza, why change their minds with the Seattle Seahawks on the schedule? — Conor Orr

This is ironic because Orr was one of the many people pounding the table for the Silver and Black to trade Kirk Cousins. I don't think that it was ever the plan to have Mendoza's first start against the Seahawks, but John Spytek got the trade offer for Cousins that he was looking for ahead of the deadline. Sure, Aidan O'Connell could have played instead. However, Klint Kubiak and Co. were going to have to rip the band-aid off eventually. Look at it this way: Mendoza probably won't face a tougher opponent than he did last week, so it's all downhill from here!

The Fernando Mendoza era officially began with an exciting opening touchdown drive. That was it for the day's Silver and Black entertainment. The Raiders' limitations were on full display against the Seahawks in the second half, in which they had -17 offensive yards prior to a late field-goal drive. I expect this offense to eventually find a groove with Mendoza after clearing this brutal stretch of games played against Seattle, Denver and Cleveland. — Nick Shook

While what I said above is true, the Seahawks are the best defense Mendoza will face this season, it's not like the Broncos and Browns are cakewalks. Those two rank 13th (-0.05) and 10th (-0.06), respectively, in defensive EPA per play, according to Sumer Sports. That being said, Denver does give up the sixth-most yards per game (355.0), meaning the rookie could build some confidence by being able to move the ball on Sunday. The Broncos do a really good job of buckling down in the red zone, though, ranking ninth in points per game allowed (20.7) despite giving up so many yards.

Fernando-mania has officially arrived in Las Vegas. And for a moment, it appeared that Fernando Mendoza's first start was going to be a magical one—Mendoza led the Raiders to a touchdown on his first drive. But the first NFL start for any quarterback is usually bumpy—even the first overall pick. And sure enough, for much of the rest of the game Mendoza looked like a first-year quarterback. He wasn't terrible, and the loss of tight end Brock Bowers to an ankle injury most assuredly didn't help matters. But Mendoza looked confused at times and held the ball too long, taking six sacks.

The reality is that when the Raiders traded Kirk Cousins, they essentially white-flagged the 2026 season. It's less about winning now than developing the team's franchise quarterback of the future. In that respect, hanging around against the defending Super Bowl champions has to be at least somewhat encouraging. — Gary Davenport

Davenport's assessment of Mendoza looking confused and holding onto the ball too long is on point. It started toward the end of the second quarter, when Macdonald really ramped up the exotic pressure looks. That led to the rookie having the fifth-longest average time to throw (3.22 seconds) of the quarterbacks who had at least 22 dropbacks in Week 10, per Pro Football Focus.

To be fair, even veteran quarterbacks have a tough time deciphering the Seahawks' pressure packages. For example, Patrick Mahomes' highest ATTT came in Week 7 against Seattle and was very similar to Mendoza's at 3.17 seconds. That's also the only game Mahomes has been above the three-second mark this season.

Bowers has played on another level in his third season. Until Sunday's loss, which he had to leave early because of an ankle injury, he had seven straight games with 70-plus receiving yards. He has also recorded at least 100 receiving yards in three contests. Bowers' season is even more impressive when factoring in how a lot of his production has come from being lined up on the outside. He has run 96 routes from the outside, totaling 27 catches for 409 yards and four touchdowns on 36 targets. — Ryan McFadden

We're shifting gears to the tight ends here, and it's hard to argue with McFadden's logic. Perhaps the biggest ringing endorsement for how valuable Bowers is to the Raiders is that the offense was completely inept last week after he left the game. Even if he isn't getting targeted, the 'football robot from heaven' changes the entire coverage plan for defenses to help get other receivers open. Luckily, he's considered day-to-day because the No. 1 pick really needs the offense to have its best player on the field to have success moving forward.

As tempting as it is to just give this to Fernando Mendoza after the rookie quarterback's debut as a starter, we're going with the fourth-year tight end. Mayer already has career highs in yards (404) and catches (41), and his role is only going to grow after Brock Bowers left Sunday's game with another ankle injury. — Josh Kendall, Chad Graff

Kendall's/Graff's logic is correct here, in theory. Mayer should have a bigger role without Bowers in the lineup. However, the former's numbers are actually much better when he's on the field with the latter this season.

According to Sumer, Mayer has run 88 routes without Bowers and logged 15 targets, 11 catches and zero touchdowns for 0.78 yards per route run and a 17 percent rate of targets per route. With Bowers on the field, Mayer's target rate is about the same (17.2 percent), but his yards per route run more than doubles at 1.69 with 198 routes, 34 targets, 30 receptions, 335 receiving yards and a touchdown.

That reinforces the point above about how the two-time Pro Bowler makes life much easier for his teammates, even if they technically play the same position.
