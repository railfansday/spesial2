Semtech: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

LoRa, Semtech's long-range wireless line, grew 69% to $68 million, giving the company a second high-margin growth engine beyond data centers.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 25, 2026 at 12:03AM EST

On its fiscal third-quarter 2027 earnings call, held on Nov. 23, 2026, Semtech (SMTC -16.97%) announced record net sales of $423 million, up 58% year over year. The chipmaker is capitalizing on the rapid expansion of artificial intelligence (AI) networks through its high-speed optical and copper interconnect products. Accelerated data center demand and expanding operational leverage position the business for long-term growth. Three key takeaways from management stand out for long-term investors.

Data center net sales grew 158% year over year to $145 million as hyperscalers, or massive cloud service providers, expanded network capacity. Management expects data center revenue to double in fiscal year 2028, driven by robust order backlogs and expanding market share in high-speed optical components.

CopperEdge linear equalizers, which perform signal conditioning for high-speed copper interconnect cables, gained design wins for data center line cards. By replacing digital signal processing chips known as retimers, Semtech eliminates latency and significantly reduces rack power demands.

Net sales for LoRa, the company's proprietary long-range, low-power wireless technology, rose 69% year over year to $68 million. Expanding deployment across industrial sensors, satellite tracking, and smart home ecosystems provides high-margin revenue diversification beyond data center infrastructure.

Data center expansion continues at a rapid pace, and management is not hiding its satisfaction regarding execution across optical, copper, and wireless platforms. The company maintains structural advantages through proprietary signal-conditioning designs and multi-generation customer alignment, while monitoring gross margin expansion and the timing of capacity expansion.

As compute clusters require faster throughput at lower power envelopes, Semtech's low-power analog architecture remains central to next-generation hardware designs.
