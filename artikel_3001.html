SpaceX Is Now Nvidia's Biggest Stock Holding, Ahead of Intel. Here's What That Says About SpaceX.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Is the chipmaker actually betting more on the rocket and AI company?

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 18, 2026 at 2:57PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

Nvidia (NVDA -2.79%) filed its quarterly list of stock holdings on Monday, Nov. 16, and there's a new name on top. As of Sept. 30, SpaceX (SPCX +1.66%) was the chipmaker's biggest disclosed stake, worth around $19.7 billion, or 40.7% of the reported portfolio. Intel (INTC -1.56%), which led the list three months before, slid to second with 40.1%.

Nvidia owned around 130.5 million SpaceX shares at the end of September, up from about 122.8 million at the end of June. Meanwhile, it shrank its Intel stake by a quarter, to around 161.1 million shares. Intel's still the bigger holding by share count. SpaceX leads in value.

It's tempting to take this as Nvidia betting more on SpaceX. But I think the filing says less about that than it seems -- and more about how much SpaceX's own plans rely on Nvidia.

The filing (a Form 13F, the quarterly holdings report big investment managers send regulators) lists positions on one date. It doesn't say why a position changed.

And Nvidia had a way to get more SpaceX shares without buying any. It invested in artificial intelligence (AI) coding firm Cursor in late 2025. SpaceX later bought Cursor in a deal that closed on Aug. 14, paying Cursor's shareholders in SpaceX stock.

Some or all of the 7.7 million or so extra shares may have come from the deal. Neither company has said.

The bigger shift was on the Intel side. Nvidia's SpaceX stake lost value in the third quarter, dipping from around $21 billion to $19.7 billion as SpaceX's share price dropped. But its Intel stake fell far more, from around $30 billion to $19.4 billion, after Nvidia cut its position by about 54 million shares and Intel's share price slid.

Without the extra SpaceX shares, Intel would still have barely kept the top spot. In other words, SpaceX rose to first mainly because Nvidia shrank its Intel stake.

For SpaceX, the stake's small. Nvidia's 130.5 million shares made up about 1% of the 13.6 billion or so SpaceX had outstanding in mid-October. At roughly $141 a share as I write, they're worth about $18 billion, versus a market value of about $1.9 trillion.

They don't come with much say, either. At the June initial public offering (IPO), CEO Elon Musk had about 82% of the total voting power.

The more important tie runs the other way. SpaceX is a big buyer of Nvidia's chips, and its fastest-growing business relies on them.

Showing how quickly that business is scaling, SpaceX's super intelligence (SI) segment (its AI cloud services, Grok, Cursor, and X) grew revenue to around $7.6 billion in the third quarter. That's nearly triple the second quarter's $2.6 billion and over nine times the $813 million it took in a year earlier. And the segment swung to $431 million of operating income after losing around $1.3 billion in the second quarter. Its nameplate computing capacity (a measure of the AI chips it's installed) hit 1.7 gigawatts at the end of September, against 1.4 in June and 0.6 in September 2025.

Most of SpaceX's capital spending now goes into this build-out. SI took $16.4 billion of the company's third-quarter capital expenditures, or around 84%.

"We're tracking to be able to bring roughly 200,000 GB300s online per month for every month for the remainder of this year," Musk said on SpaceX's third-quarter earnings call on Oct. 20.

The GB300 is one of Nvidia's AI systems, and Musk said next year's build would be mainly Nvidia's newer Vera Rubin platform. Also, SpaceX's prospectus said its compute deal with Anthropic, which agreed to pay $1.25 billion a month through May 2029, includes around 325,000 Nvidia graphics processing units (GPUs).

Of course, Nvidia does fine either way. It makes money when SpaceX buys chips, whether or not those chips end up paying off for SpaceX shareholders.

What does Nvidia's filing say about SpaceX, then? Not that the chipmaker is making a big new bet. The stake is around 1% of the company, and it might have grown in the Cursor deal.

What it does show is how tightly SpaceX's growth is tied to one supplier's products. At around 33 times the revenue SpaceX would bring in over a year at its third-quarter rate, the stock already assumes those chips pay off. I don't find anything in Nvidia's filing that makes the stock look cheaper.
