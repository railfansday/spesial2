Is AMD Stock a Buy Amid the Uncertainty Around Nvidia?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Nvidia's CEO, Jensen Huang, recently resigned, and questions about the company's leadership could give AMD a perceived advantage.

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -2.46%) is having a terrific year, with the tech company posting impressive results and its share price more than doubling thus far, up 135% since the beginning of 2026.

Now, news that its longtime rival Nvidia (NVDA -1.57%) is in the midst of a leadership change after Jensen Huang resigned as CEO may have given investors yet another reason to be bullish on the much smaller chip stock.

Is now a good time to buy AMD stock, or has it run out of room to rise a whole lot higher?

It was just last week that Huang resigned from Nvidia amid reports of multiple extramarital affairs, one of which may have even involved a Chinese spy, and thus, it's far too early to know who his permanent replacement will be. There have even been calls to have Huang reinstated, with many prominent people in the tech sector signing a petition for his return.

However, even if there ends up being an entirely new person leading the company, that doesn't mean Nvidia will be significantly worse off, nor that AMD will take more market share in the chip market. Investors only need to look at the success of Apple after its iconic CEO Steve Jobs stepped down amid health issues in 2011. The company still went on to achieve great success under Tim Cook, and the stock surged in value.

A new CEO may result in some near-term uncertainty for Nvidia, but that alone isn't a reason to expect AMD's stock to suddenly take off in value, or that it will lead to much more growth.

AMD's business has been doing remarkably well this year, as demand for its chips has been rising. In its most recent quarter, for the period ending Sept. 26, its revenue rose by 45% year over year, totaling $13.4 billion. And profits nearly doubled to $2.4 billion.

Those are solid numbers, but at the same time, AMD is also very expensive, with the stock trading at more than 100 times its trailing earnings. Its forward price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple, which is based on analyst estimates, is also not cheap at 35. Nvidia, by comparison, trades at a forward P/E of only 10 due to its recent downfall.

AMD has been a much hotter stock to own than Nvidia this year, and that's precisely why investors should be careful not to assume it'll keep rising, given its fairly steep valuation and limited upside. While it can still make for a good buy over the long term, investors shouldn't expect the stock to take off anytime soon.
