Arteta on Saliba, scoring goals, the January window, and more

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Let’s do a quick Saturday round-up, starting with Mikel Arteta on William Saliba. I was a bit surprised that he didn’t feature against Borussia Dortmund in midweek, which could tell you something about where he is in terms of building his fitness, or maybe what the manager thought of the opposition (while also applying the context of the French international’s long absence).

At his press conference yesterday, the manager was asked if the plan had been to give him minutes on Tuesday had the game been scoreline been more comfortably in Arsenal’s favour, and said:

It depends, but obviously he hasn’t played football for a long time. He can play a certain amount of minutes, and we have to be really sure – especially in those kinds of games – that when he comes in, he can sustain a certain amount of minutes otherwise we won’t have enough subs or opportunities to make those subs. He’s training well, he’ll have minutes in the next few games, and then he’ll be able to prove what he’s already done, which is show what an unbelievable player he is.

It is interesting to look at the fixture list and wonder where exactly those minutes are going to come from. It’s Man City on Sunday, then Brentford and Sp*rs away, before Real Madrid and then Bournemouth who are on a great run of form and who beat us at home last season. Those are games where you’re going to players at their sharpest, and Saliba is someone who has, in the past, needed a few matches under his belt to find his best rhythm.

Perhaps one answer is about being more comfortable. There were questions about Arsenal scoring goals too, and when asked if it matters how many his team score as long as they win, Arteta said:

I prefer to win by five, or six, or seven, for sure. I’m going to enjoy the game much more, especially the last few minutes. The boys are going to have more fun. I think scoring goals is ultimately what creates a good feeling, joy, happiness, celebration around the stands. It’s the most important thing in football, apart from winning.

What scoring more also does is allow a manager to make different changes. If you have a 1-0 lead, you’re not going to disrupt your central-defensive partnership because it’s so tight. You’re reluctant to take off your all-action central midfielder because he’s so important to your side. If you’re 3-0 up with 20 minutes to go, you have more leeway to make those substitutions, and over the course of a season that can protect some players for becoming overly-fatigued as well as providing opportunities to others. How nice would it be, particularly with this run of tricky fixtures, if he could do exactly that?

Here’s a stat from the presser: if Arsenal score tomorrow it will be the 700th goal under Arteta, reaching that tally 23 games quicker than Arsene Wenger. That doesn’t mean a great deal, but it’s mildly interesting, and who can ask for more on a chilly November Saturday? I’d like to see us hit 704 goals tomorrow, not being greedy.

Martin Odegaard spoke to Sky Sports about a new deal, and sounded a very positive note, saying:

It’s not something I stress about. I’ve said it so many times, I’m so happy here, I love it here. Hopefully I can stay for many more years – and I think the club is happy as well.

So it’s no stress, we’ll sort it out. I want to win more things here for sure. I want to win everything with Arsenal. The Premier League again, the Champions League, the cups – that’s what we want. We want to win everything. The rest of it, the individual stuff, will come as a result of what happens with the team. The team is the most important bit.

For me, he’s an important player to tie down. There aren’t many like him around, and if there was the vague outline of a succession plan in the shape of Ethan Nwaneri, that doesn’t seem to be particularly likely at this point. The next young man up is probably Max Dowman, but he’s still only 16. In four years time he’ll only be 20, which is ridiculous really, and in that period of time we have to be conscious of how he is integrated and developed.

Odegaard remains Arsenal’s creative hub in many ways, and this season looks like a player who has shaken off the impact of a series of injuries which prevented him from finding form and rhythm. So far this season he has created 43 chances across the Premier League and Champions League, the most of any player in the team (with Declan Rice and Noni Madueke behind him), and I think securing his future makes a lot of sense. With other renewals under way, let’s hope this is one they can sort out without much delay.

Finally, the manager was asked about the January window, and whether we might make a first addition to the squad since Leandro Trossard (and others) were brought in in January 2023:

We’ve always been very active in the transfer window. Especially that we have certain issues. Have we managed to get a deal over the line? Sometimes not. So we will see if there is an opportunity there and something that we believe is going to make a difference for the team.

In midweek, James reported that Arsenal are ‘aware’ the departures of Trossard and Gabriel Martinelli (as well as Gabriel Jesus let’s not forget), have left the squad light on that left hand side, and for many that’s the key for the winter window. That said, centre-forward is another position where you could make a strong argument that we need to improve. It’s always a tricky time in January, but not impossible, so let’s see what happens in due course. There’s plenty of football to play before then.
