Arm vs. ASML: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Arm and ASML sit at opposite ends of the chip supply chain, but a sharp gap in their valuations may tip the scales for cautious buyers.

Investors looking for growth in the tech sector often find themselves choosing between Arm (ARM -4.63%) and ASML (ASML -0.13%), two companies that provide the essential building blocks of modern computing.

While both are critical to the global electronics market, they operate at different stages of the supply chain with Arm focusing on chip architecture and ASML providing the machinery for manufacturing. Understanding their unique business models and financial health is key to deciding which stock fits your strategy.

Arm designs the foundational technology used in almost every smartphone on the planet today. By licensing its architecture to other chipmakers like Apple and Qualcomm, the company sits at the heart of the global electronics ecosystem. Its expanding presence in artificial intelligence and data centers makes it a prominent name among semiconductor stocks.

In the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, revenue reached nearly $4.9 billion, representing a year-over-year increase of approximately 22.8%. Net income for the same period was approximately $904.0 million, resulting in a net margin of roughly 18.4%. This growth reflects the increasing adoption of its latest architecture designs, which generate both upfront licensing fees and ongoing royalty payments for every chip sold.

As of its March 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was roughly 0.1x. The debt-to-equity ratio compares total debt to the money shareholders have invested, while the current ratio of close to 6.0x measures the ability to cover short-term debts with assets that can be quickly turned into cash. Free cash flow, the cash left after paying for operations and equipment, reached nearly $979.0 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 69% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

ASML holds a unique position by providing the critical machinery required to manufacture the world's most advanced microchips. It is the only supplier of extreme ultraviolet lithography systems, which are used by major manufacturers such as Intel and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing. This specialized equipment is essential for producing the high-performance chips found in modern smartphones, servers, and artificial intelligence hardware.

In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, revenue reached nearly $36.2 billion, which was roughly 15.6% higher than the prior fiscal year. The company reported net income of approximately $10.7 billion, yielding a net margin of close to 29.4%. These results demonstrate the steady demand for its lithography systems and the growing contribution of service revenue from its large installed base of machines.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was approximately 0.2x. The debt-to-equity ratio compares total debt to the money shareholders have invested, while the current ratio of roughly 1.3x measures the ability to cover short-term debts with assets that can be quickly turned into cash. Free cash flow, which is the cash left after paying for operations and equipment, reached nearly $12.3 billion for the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025.

Arm faces competition from open-source alternatives like RISC-V, which could pressure its licensing fees over time. The company also deals with geographic concentration risks and the potential for slowing innovation in the smartphone market to impact its royalty streams. Any shift in how major customers approach proprietary chip design could also alter the company's long-term growth trajectory.

ASML is subject to strict export controls that may limit its ability to ship its most advanced tools to certain international markets. Additionally, the semiconductor industry is historically cyclical, meaning the company could face periods of lower demand if chipmakers pause their capital spending plans. The massive technical complexity of its next-generation machines also creates risks if adoption by manufacturers is slower than anticipated.

ASML looks significantly more affordable than Arm because it has a much lower Forward P/E and P/S ratio.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

The deciding factor for me is how much of the AI story already shows up in reported results versus how much a buyer has to take on faith. ASML's management says some customers are expressing interest in placing orders for 2029 and 2030, and that planned supply for 2027 and 2028 won't meet demand, so growth is already showing up in the order book as well as the income statement. Arm's AI story is real, but much of it sits ahead of the numbers. Licensing has run well ahead of royalties, and the investment case depends on royalties catching up and on the company's new chip-selling business delivering on schedule.That second piece changes what Arm is. A pure licensor collects fees on other companies' designs, while a company selling its own silicon assumes supply, inventory, and the risk of competing with its own licensees. Arm's multiple already assumes this goes well, which leaves little cushion if a quarter disappoints.In my case, ASML is the better buy for 2027. I've got a multi-year horizon and want AI exposure without paying a massive/ risky premium. I also feel confident that ASML is built to last, both as a company and in its culture. Arm fits a smaller position for investors who can hold through volatility and wait for the royalty and chip ramps to justify the price. That's not as appealing to me at my stage of the investing journey.
