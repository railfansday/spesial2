DistroKid Gives AI Upload Data to UMG and Sony for Suno Copyright Lawsuit After Initial Refusal

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The leading DIY distributor responded to a subpoena requesting the data after UMG and Sony took legal action to force compliance.

Amid legal pressure from Universal Music Group (UMG) and Sony Music, DistroKid has provided data about the prevalence of AI-generated music moving through its pipes.

A Monday (Nov. 16) court filing, obtained by Billboard, says DistroKid has complied with the third-party subpoena served by UMG and Sony as part of their copyright infringement lawsuit against AI music service Suno. The subpoena sought numbers regarding how much of the music that passes through the leading DIY digital distributor is thought to be made with Suno and other AI generators.

UMG and Sony say this data is key to proving that Suno-generated music is eating into their market share and royalty distributions. Competition between AI songs and human music is a key part of the “fair use” inquiry — that is, the test a judge will use to decide the seismic question of whether Suno infringed the majors’ copyrights by training on millions of unlicensed tracks. DistroKid is not a party to the Suno suit.

DistroKid initially refused to provide AI data to UMG and Sony, leading the labels to initiate a legal action last month seeking to force compliance with the subpoena. The distributor has since changed its tune and produced responsive documents, according to Monday’s court filing, which withdrew the subpoena petition.

Reps for DistroKid and Sony declined to comment on the matter on Tuesday (Nov. 17). Reps for UMG did not return a request for comment.

While the subpoena dispute is now over, UMG still has an ongoing lawsuit that alleges DistroKid is to blame for a surge in AI music on streaming services like Spotify. That case, filed in September, alleges that the distributor has engaged in deceptive business practices and violated copyright law by flooding streamers with “mass AI-generated, SEO-optimized, and copyright-infringing slop.”

In a statement when that lawsuit was filed, DistroKid’s rep said, “We take copyright protection, fraud prevention and the integrity of the music ecosystem seriously, and work closely with digital streaming platforms, rightsholders and other industry partners on these efforts.”

“We strongly disagree with UMG’s allegations and are disappointed that it chose litigation rather than established industry processes to address these issues,” added the DistroKid spokesperson. “We are confident in our practices and intend to defend DistroKid vigorously.”
