NFL Fantasy Football Week 11 2026 Start ‘Em Sit ‘Em: Tight End

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

NFL Fantasy Football Week 11 2026 Start 'Em Sit 'Em: Tight End appeared first on ClutchPoints. Add ClutchPoints as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

Week 11 could be one of the more challenging weeks of the fantasy football season for managers trying to figure out what to do at tight end.

With several players at the position on bye, the available options are getting thinner, and managers may have to take a few risks to find the production they need.

Pat Freiermuth has quietly put together some solid performances recently and is worth considering as a starting option this week. The Steelers tight end is coming off back-to-back 11-point fantasy performances against divisional opponents and has continued to give Aaron Rodgers a reliable option in the passing game.

The matchup against the Philadelphia Eagles is not necessarily an easy one. Philadelphia has generally done a good job defending tight ends, which makes this a tougher test for Freiermuth. However, the Eagles have struggled recently, and Pittsburgh should have opportunities to move the ball.

Freiermuth has already found the end zone five times this season. If Rodgers can get the Steelers into the red zone a handful of times, there is a good chance the tight end gets a few opportunities near the goal line. He doesn't necessarily need a huge receiving day to make an impact for fantasy managers.

With so many tight ends unavailable this week, Freiermuth is a reasonable option to plug into your lineup.

Dalton Kincaid could be in line for a productive afternoon against the Miami Dolphins. Josh Allen and the Buffalo offense have the potential to put up plenty of points in this matchup, and Kincaid should have opportunities to get involved.

One of the biggest reasons to trust Kincaid is his involvement in the passing game. He has received at least six targets in every game this season in which he has scored double digits in fantasy.

The matchup against Miami also gives Kincaid an opportunity to make a big play. Buffalo should be able to move the ball, and there should be chances for him to find space underneath or get involved near the end zone.

Darren Waller is certainly not the safest tight end to start every week, but I like his chances of producing against the Baltimore Ravens in Week 11.

Waller has been a hit-or-miss option with Carolina this season. Last week was a disappointing performance, but his Week 9 outing showed what he can do when he is more involved in the passing game. He finished that game with eight receptions on 10 targets for 90 yards and a touchdown.

That type of production is exactly what fantasy managers are hoping to get from Waller. He has four touchdowns on the season and has recorded at least 10 fantasy points in four games. The consistency is not always there, but the upside is worth considering.

Sam LaPorta has not been producing at the level fantasy managers expected, and it is becoming increasingly difficult to trust him in starting lineups.

Ever since the tight end expressed frustration surrounding his contract situation, his targets have seemingly gone down. More importantly, the production has not been there. Since Detroit's Week 6 bye, LaPorta has not scored more than 7.2 fantasy points in a game. He has now put together four straight disappointing performances.

That is a tough stretch for a player who is usually considered one of the more reliable fantasy options at the position. The matchup against the Tampa Bay Buccaneers does not necessarily give managers enough reason to expect a turnaround, either.

Tyler Warren and the Indianapolis Colts are in an interesting position heading into Week 11. The offense looked good against Miami last week, but Warren was barely involved, finishing with just two catches for one yard.

That is not the type of production fantasy managers want to see from a tight end who showed so much promise earlier in the season. Warren opened the year with three consecutive games of at least 10 fantasy points and later put together a 21-point performance. However, things have gone downhill since then. Even when he has received opportunities, the production has been inconsistent. Against Jacksonville in Week 8, Warren earned 10 targets but managed only four receptions for 18 yards.

The biggest question is whether Daniel Jones can get him going again. Warren has shown that he can be a productive fantasy tight end, but the recent performances make him difficult to trust. Until the connection between Jones and Warren starts producing again, fantasy managers should look for another option.

Juwan Johnson has had a good season overall, but his recent fantasy production is becoming a concern. He has struggled to put together consistent performances, and Week 11 could present another difficult challenge against the Chicago Bears.

Four of Johnson's last five games have resulted in 5.6 fantasy points or fewer. That is not the type of production managers can afford, especially during a week when every lineup decision matters.

The Bears have a strong defense and should provide a difficult test for the New Orleans offense. Johnson has the ability to step up and become a reliable target, but it is difficult to predict what the Saints' passing game will look like against a defense that can make life uncomfortable for opposing offenses.

I would rather take my chances with another tight end this week.
