True crime channel EWU slams YouTube’s “extreme censorship” after losing monetization

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Popular true crime YouTube channel Explore With Us (EWU) has been demonetized, prompting its creators to blast the platform’s “extreme censorship” and announce plans to shift more content to their own streaming service.

EWU is one of YouTube’s biggest true crime networks, producing documentaries featuring police interrogations, bodycam footage, investigative records, and other evidence obtained through extensive research.

The channel has even helped solve real crimes. In May 2026, EWU helped crack a 42-year-old murder cold case after funding DNA testing that contributed to a suspect’s arrest.

However, despite its investigative work, YouTube has reportedly determined that EWU’s videos violate monetization rules concerning repetitive or generic content.

In a lengthy community post, the creators revealed the decision and made it clear they weren’t happy.

“Despite the countless hours our team spends investigating cases, obtaining public records, uncovering evidence, writing original scripts, and producing each documentary, YouTube has determined that our content violates its monetization policies concerning repetitive or generic content,” EWU explained.

“We strongly disagree with this decision, but we’re not giving up.”

EWU went on to criticize YouTube’s content restrictions, claiming its team has spent years heavily editing documentaries to comply with the platform’s rules.

“For years, producing documentaries for YouTube has meant working within restrictions on what we can show, what we can say, and how much of the original evidence we can include. Even certain words need to be muted to comply with their extreme censorship.”

The creators added that crime scene footage, interrogations, and bodycam recordings often require substantial editing before they can be uploaded.

EWU has built a reputation for obtaining footage and evidence that might otherwise never reach the public, including bodycam recordings exposing an innocent man’s arrest following an AI facial recognition error.

The demonetization also comes after YouTube clarified its policies targeting “unsatisfying or off-putting” content, including videos deemed repetitive, generic, or designed primarily to shock viewers.

However, YouTube’s guidelines distinguish between repetitive content and original videos that offer educational value or meaningful commentary. EWU says its demonetization specifically concerns repetitive or generic content.

Rather than abandon its documentaries, EWU is doubling down on EWU Plus, an independent subscription streaming platform the team says it began developing around six months ago.

The service promises less censorship, no advertisements, extended documentaries, exclusive footage, and fewer restrictions on the evidence shown.

“Our vision is to build more than another true-crime streaming service. We want to create a place where viewers can watch our documentaries, explore the evidence behind them, and gain a deeper understanding of the investigations, and interact with fellow members.”

Despite the dispute, EWU confirmed it isn’t abandoning YouTube or deleting its existing documentaries.

“We’ll continue publishing documentaries here and working toward restoring monetization. However, we’ll be focusing much more of our new content and resources on EWU Plus, where our viewers can get the in depth documentaries as they were intended.”
