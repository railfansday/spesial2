Investing - Theory, News &amp; General • Re: TIPS are they really worth holding?

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

I agree that individual TIPS if purchased for a specific use at a specific time seem to be the best vehicle. Just not sure if they make sense as a vehicle for lumpy situations, especially in fund form. Seems lack of short term issues (5 years for shortest) and interest rate sensitivity might make them less than optimal for general Fixed Income portion of your portfolio. If conditions don’t go the right way in the future, TIPS might not perform as expected. In other words, they could be worse than nominals
For lumpy expenses of unknown timing where you would like to keep up with inflation, I-series savings bonds are a good vehicle. You do give up some real interest rate (usually) versus holding TIPS, but that is the price of dodging interest rate risk. I found them handy in early retirement for this purpose. Also, I deferred taxes on interest from working years to retirement years.

You can buy TIPS of almost any maturity (2037 through 2039 maturities presently excepted). You just have to buy them on the secondary market or in fund form if you want something different than 5, 10, or 30 years. For "general fixed income portion" where you seek to rebalance between fixed income and equity, rather than liability matching, nominals may work better. TIPS sometimes don't benefit from the flight to safety impulse that nominal bonds get.

It is hard to imagine TIPS being more than a percent or two worse than nominals other than selling before maturity. Nominals could fare well worse than TIPS if inflation is bad enough. High inflation is one of the things that can ruin portfolio longevity in retirement. A case could be made for holding nominals while working/accumulating then switching to TIPS as one de-risks heading into retirement.
