‘I Put the Helmet on My Head': Franco Colapinto Reveals Hilarious Airport Trick on Way Home From Las Vegas

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Franco Colapinto found a funny way to beat an airport baggage limit after the Las Vegas Grand Prix. The Alpine driver decided to wear his Formula 1 helmet rather than pay for extra luggage.

"I put the helmet on my head because wearing it meant it wasn't an extra item, and the staff member stopped complaining," Colapinto revealed. He later joked, "If you want any tips for flying, folks, you can write to me."

The Argentine had a backpack, two helmets, and a bag carrying his Mate flask. He did not want to check his helmets because he feared they could get damaged. "I don't check my helmet in – they'll just break it, scratch it, ruin it, who knows."

As such, the Alpine driver looked to two fellow Argentine passengers for help. "I found two who were queuing with me and asked, 'Can you carry my mate flask for me?' We put it in his suitcase – he couldn't carry it as hand luggage, as they're very strict about the number of bags in the US."

Franco Colapinto had to ask his fellow passengers for help carrying his luggage. (Source: MEGA)

Colapinto eventually solved the problem by wearing one helmet and giving its cover to another passenger. His unusual trick turned an ordinary airport problem into a funny story after the Las Vegas weekend.

Colapinto found a way to make his journey home from Las Vegas easier, using his racing helmet to avoid an airport luggage dispute. The Alpine driver has recently been giving plenty of reasons for his fans to smile, and one such moment came when he met with his supporters in Brazil.

Franco Colapinto took out some time to meet with his fans during the Brazilian GP weekend. (Source: MEGA)

After the Brazilian Grand Prix, Colapinto walked over to fans waiting near the track. He signed shirts, caps and flags, and stopped to take photos with them. Alpine later shared a video of the meeting on social media.

Before meeting supporters near the track, the Alpine driver had also surprised three friends who had traveled more than 2,000 kilometers to see him.

Hernán Bonvín, Claudio Goddio and Pablo Quaino drove from Esperanza, Santa Fe, to São Paulo in a restored 1977 Renault 6. They painted the car in Alpine's pink and blue colors and added Colapinto's number 43. They also gave the car a special name: the Renoleta.

When Colapinto spotted the car, he went over to greet the group. He then climbed behind the wheel and drove it through the streets of São Paulo. The fans filmed the moment as the Argentine driver joked about the car's radio and enjoyed the ride.

While the Argentine's fans are incredibly supportive of him, that support can sometimes turn into rage for others. Colapinto has urged his supporters to stop attacking rival drivers and Alpine staff online. The Argentine warned that their behaviour could harm his Formula 1 future.

Franco Colapinto urged his fans to stop the online hate against other drivers on the grid. (Source: MEGA)

"I know you think you're defending me, but you're actually doing the opposite. You're not helping me or my future," Colapinto said. He shared the message in a video, thanking fans for their support while asking them to change their behaviour.

Colapinto later explained why he felt the need to speak out. He revealed that his mechanics and engineers had also received hateful messages. Seeing his daily colleagues targeted upset him and made him feel that certain limits had been crossed.

The driver said he had discussed online abuse for several races before posting his video. In November, he also said his message appeared to have helped, with fans becoming calmer and more positive. However, he believes Formula 1, the FIA and other drivers must also help tackle the problem.
