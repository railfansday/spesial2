Broncos vs. Raiders: 3 Players to Watch in Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

This Sunday, the Denver Broncos take on the Las Vegas Raiders in a classic AFC West rivalry matchup. With an extra week of preparation, the Broncos had plenty of time to figure out a winning gameplan. In order to earn their eighth win of the season, these players need to execute at a high level.

All eyes will be on Bo Nix this Sunday. Nix hasn't experienced the uptick in production Broncos Country expected in his third season. Through nine games, he is completing just 61-percent of his passes [a career low], thrown for just 1,730 yards, 11 touchdowns and 4 interceptions. Coming off an ankle injury, he hasn't been as productive as a runner, though has added 116 yards and two touchdowns on the ground.

Over the past three games, the former first-round pick has played better than he started the season. In that timeframe, he has six total touchdowns and only one turnover. Things appear to be going in the right direction, and I remain optimistic he can turn it up in the second half of the season.

On Sunday, Nix will face Raiders' defense that is allowing nearly 250 passing yards a game—a bottom 10 mark in the NFL. With that in mind, I'm hopeful to see more production from the third-year signal caller. Nix has just one game this year over 250 yards passing—his Week 2 effort versus the Jaguars.

With extra time to gameplan, I am confident he and the offense will be able to take advantage and put together one of their best performances of the season. I think Nix sizzles in his first game after the bye and starts the second half of the season on a high note.

Earlier in the week, the Broncos rewarded cornerback Riley Moss with a four-year contract extension worth $67 million dollars. The fourth-year pro has been a key starter in Vance Joseph's defense the past two and a half seasons.

It's no easy task playing opposite of All-Pro Patrick Surtain II. For the most part, Moss has passed that assignment with flying colors. The former Iowa standout has a knack for defending passes but has just three interceptions in his career. I'd like to see him start taking the ball away more.

Sunday's matchup provides that opportunity. The Raiders will be starting Fernando Mendoza. Last weekend against the Seahawks, he struggled but was relatively safe with the football. I'm hopeful Denver's defensive front seven can get after Mendoza early and often and force him into some bad decisions.

I don't expect the 2026 NFL Draft's top pick to test Surtain much at all. That means Moss should be in store for a high-volume day target-wise. Can he rise to the occasion and make a big splash play in his first game after his extension? We are fixing to find out.

When the Broncos traded for Jaylen Waddle this offseason, fans across Broncos Country were confident he would propel their passing attack to the next level. Count me in as one of them. Unfortunately, Waddle hasn't had that type of impact yet.

His Week 2 performance against the Jacksonville Jaguars sticks out. However, he hasn't had over 50 receiving yards in any other contest thus far. Through nine games, the former 2021 first-round pick is sporting career lows across the board. In order for Denver's offense to take flight in the second half of the season, Waddle has to perform much better.

The Raiders have been opportunistic on defense creating turnovers. That's what they're good at. Otherwise, they sport a bottom 10 pass defense. Las Vegas has given up the most passing touchdowns of any defense with 22 on the season. They are also allowing the most yards per reception of any team in the NFL.

Opposing quarterbacks have the best rating in the NFL going up against their secondary. With that in mind, this Sunday seems like a prime matchup for Waddle (and the aforementioned Nix) to have a strong connection and create some splash plays down the field.
