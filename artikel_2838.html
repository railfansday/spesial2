Rafael Nadal reveals what he told his uncle during the rain delay in the 2008 Wimbledon final with Roger Federer

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

In 2008, Rafael Nadal and Roger Federer delivered one of the greatest tennis matches of all time.

Nadal and Federer met in the Wimbledon final for the third consecutive season that year, and this time the Spaniard came out on top after a thrilling five-setter that lasted four hours and 48 minutes. The match had started at 2 pm local time, but it was in danger of having to be postponed until the next day due to rain delays.

A second rain delay occurred at around 8 pm after Federer had rallied from two sets down to force a decider, and when the players returned to the court, darkness had started to settle in. At this point, there was no roof on Centre Court.

During that interruption, Nadal had a conversation with his uncle and coach, Toni Nadal, which he recalled in a recent interview with Brut.

"For me, the 2007 final of Wimbledon loss, I think helped me to be ready for the 2008 final," said Nadal. The 22-time Grand Slam champion had fallen to Federer in the championship match at the All England Club in 2006 and 2007. He lost in four sets in 2006, but forced Federer to a fifth set in 2007.

Recalling his conversation with Toni in 2008, Nadal said: "I would say, okay, I can lose, but I will not fail. That's true. I said, okay, no worries. I can lose. Maybe he beat me, but I'm not going to lose. That's what I told him [Toni] on the last rain delay.

"Because I was in a tricky situation. I was two sets up. Then I lost the next two. I lost two match points in the fourth. So the match was not in my way in that moment. But I said, okay, I mean, I am closer than ever to winning Wimbledon. So it's not the moment to fail. I said to my uncle, no worries. I will not fail. Maybe he beat me because he's very good, but I will be there all the time."

Having squandered two championship points in the fourth set, Nadal ultimately triumphed 6–4, 6–4, 6–7 (5-7), 6–7 (8-10), 9–7 to hoist his first of two Wimbledon titles. Nadal made a habit of winning throughout his illustrious career, but always dealt with losses well.

"In some way, the loss needs to be accepted to play," he said. "Because when you play, especially when you play in a sport like tennis, that every day is one against the other, one will lose. And maybe it's you that day.

"And especially, even imagine when you play against Federer or Djokovic or these kinds of players. You go on court knowing that even if you play well, you can lose. So it's not about talking about that or have fear about losing. It's about, you know, that's there. But you can't think about that. You have to think about the things that you need to do to try to win."

Nadal played Federer 40 times on the ATP Tour, with the two recording what was arguably the greatest rivalry in tennis history. Nadal emerged victorious in 24 of those 40 meetings and finished his career with two more major titles than Federer.

"I don't know if we needed each other, but we helped each other without a doubt," Nadal said Federer. "It's obvious that when you have somebody very good in front of you, you wake up every day knowing that you don't have time to waste and you don't have time to relax.

"Because if you don't do it, the other will do it. I will say, normally you do the things for yourself, but when you have somebody in front of you that is very, very good, it's easier to see the things that you have to do to improve."

Federer retired from tennis in 2022, while Nadal called it a day on his career in 2024. Their longtime rival, Novak Djokovic, is still playing, though, and is in pursuit of a 25th Grand Slam title.
