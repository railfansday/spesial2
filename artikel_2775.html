NBA power rankings: Giannis, Heat surge; Warriors plunge without Curry

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

And if there's one thing that has become clear in the 2026-27 NBA regular season, it's that the balance of power can quickly change.

Take the San Antonio Spurs, who lost the first two games of the season, only to rip off 12 consecutive victories. That streak, though, was snapped Tuesday, Nov. 17 by the Detroit Pistons.

Or take the Miami Heat, who started winless through their first four, only to win eight of their next 10 and their last five in a row.

Or the Oklahoma City Thunder, who entered the season as co-favorites to win the NBA Finals, but who have lost three consecutive as devastating injuries continue to impact the team's performance.

Here are the Week 4 USA TODAY Sports NBA power rankings:

It's not only that players like Kel'el Ware and Jaime Jaquez Jr. have underwhelmed at times, but the recent success of Giannis Antetokounmpo in Miami also has to sting. And on Wednesday night, both teams face off for the first time since the late June trade.

No team is allowing opponents to make field goals at a higher percentage – at nearly the shocking rate of half the time (49.4%) – than the Kings.

If we have to say something nice: at least No. 2 overall rookie Darryn Peterson is getting plenty of reps with all of Utah's injuries. And they finally snapped a 12-game losing streak Tuesday over the Clippers.

Given that Brooklyn has a pair of veterans in Michael Porter Jr. and Julius Randle and a bunch of younger players, you have to wonder if this is a team that will ship either of those two players – Porter most likely – to continue to build for the future.

Could a pair of wins this week against solid teams (the Hawks and Timberwolves) be the sign of things improving? The catalyst was a defense that course corrected after an abysmal start to the season.

It's not looking good for the revamped Wizards, losers of eight consecutive. Trae Young, for the first time in his career in a loss Sunday, Nov. 15 against the Rockets, finished a game with 0 points.

Darius Garland has been the bright spot, but his play is nowhere near enough to mask L.A.'s glaring roster deficiencies.

They entered Tuesday night as the only team not averaging at least 100 points per game and were posting a turnover rate of 20% – meaning one in five of their possessions was resulting in a giveaway. And then, Tuesday night, Charlotte blew an 18-point lead late in the third quarter against a Lakers team without Austin Reaves and Walker Kessler.

They finally snapped a six-game losing streak and Zion Williamson has been solid, but the Pelicans need to figure out what Dejounte Murray's future is going to be with the franchise, if there is one at all.

They're in free fall and have lost six of their last seven. Worse yet: Kawhi Leonard is still at least a week away from his return.

For Indiana, without Tyrese Haliburton, the battle will be consistency. And it looks like it's going to fall to Pascal Siakam, who has been the team's leading scorer in the last five games, to lead the way.

Dallas lost all three of its games last week. Not surprisingly, it coincided with the worst three-game stretch of Cooper Flagg's season, one in which he shot just 34.8% from the field.

It has been better, recently, for the Suns, who have won five of their last seven. That should carry over into next week, with their next three coming against teams ranked in the bottom third: the Kings, Jazz and Pelicans.

The Magic have won four consecutive, as Franz Wagner has been on a heater; he's averaging 31.4 points over his last five.

It's quickly becoming a massive (fatal?) flaw: Nikola Jokić (24.1 points per game) and Jamal Murray (26.1) are carrying the offense. The next closest player, Christian Braun, is averaging just 12.6 points per game, and Aaron Gordon's decline – particularly from 3 – is a concern.

Without Stephen Curry and without Jimmy Butler, this team will simply find it difficult to compete. The Warriors have lost all three without Curry and rank dead last in offensive rating (98.0) over that span.

The FAA's national ground stop – and Donovan Mitchell's complaints about Cleveland's challenging travel schedule – couldn't stop Cleveland against the Mavericks Saturday, Nov. 14, but the Cavs need much more out of their defense, which is approaching the bottom third of the NBA in rating (114.7).

Memphis has been a nice story, but it has faced the NBA's easiest schedule – by far. Entering Tuesday night, the Grizzlies' strength of schedule ranked dead last (.403), and the next closest team, the Pelicans (.443) were significantly ahead.

Nickeil Alexander-Walker ranks fourth in the NBA in made 3-pointers (50), and he's leading the team in scoring (21.4 points per game), but his efficiency has dipped some. Overall, the same goes for the Hawks, in general.

They've dropped a pair of consecutive games against the Timberwolves, but the good thing about Portland is that its losses have come to teams with a .770 winning percentage.

The defense has been swarming, Miami is getting a dose of vintage Klay, Giannis Antetokounmpo is playing himself into the MVP conversation and Davion Mitchell, the NBA's leader in assists per game (9.2) is making his case for Most Improved Player.

Overall, the Knicks have been more competitive than where they started the season. The interesting development has been the reduced role of Mikal Bridges, though his defensive impact remains noteworthy. Yet, given their cap constraints, could Bridges be a trade target?

The Thunder recently lost three consecutive games. It was the first time since April 2024 that they had dropped three in a row. In fact, last season, it took 32 games for the Thunder to lose their fifth game.

They erased a 14-point Spurs lead Tuesday night to claim another signature win. And Cade Cunningham, as usual, is putting this team on his back.

The Rockets entered Tuesday night second in the NBA in offensive rating (120.9 points per 100 possessions) but the Heat bullied Houston in its most recent loss, using their speed to blow by defenders. Consider that more of an aberration than anything.

It's hard to find much to fault with this group, but the most impressive stat might be how the Sixers, even with all their new players, are just an average of 0.2 turnovers off the league lead for fewest per game, with 12.7.

Once again, the Celtics are emphasizing the 3, leading the NBA with 16.8 conversions per game. They did, however, get outscored 40-28 in the fourth quarter of a Monday, Nov. 16 loss against the Magic.

The job this group has done without Anthony Edwards has been remarkable. When he returns, they may become legitimate threats to win the West.

San Antonio's 12-game winning streak was snapped Tuesday and two concerns emerged. One, the non-Victor Wembanyama minutes continue to be uneven. And two, ascending star Dylan Harper went down late with an apparent groin injury.

They're the first team to 13 victories, and it's a fantastic sign for the Lakers that they've been winning without stars like Austin Reaves and Walker Kessler. Quentin Grimes erupting for 47 Tuesday night also helps.
