Itoje warns against win-at-all-costs culture amid Sandpapergate fall out

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Sandpapergate is threatening to tear Australian cricket apart; Rassie Erasmus was handed a six-game ban for an explosive rant, hitting out at officials following South Africa's defeat to France; Maro Itoje has since distanced England's rugby team from a 'win-at-all-costs' culture

Maro Itoje has distanced his England side from the mindset of winning by any means necessary as he reflected on the issue of gamesmanship raised by a week of controversy in rugby and cricket.

Itoje leads England into Saturday's clash with New Zealand at Allianz Stadium, knowing victory could secure a place in the Nations Championship final on November 29.

A win against the All Blacks a year out from the World Cup final would issue a telling statement about the team's recent resurgence, but the British and Irish Lions captain believes there must be boundaries in the pursuit of success.

It comes as South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus serves a six-match ban for criticising officials during the Springboks' defeat by France last week, questioning whether the decisions they made amounted to "match fixing".

The furore surrounding repeat offender Erasmus receiving another suspension erupted while fresh accusations were made in the Sandpapergate scandal that is tearing Australian cricket apart, with Test captain Pat Cummins' role now under intense scrutiny.

"There's a line," Itoje said. "I don't believe in the metric of win-at-all costs because, depending on what that cost is, it can be extremely damaging.

"Integrity is really important. I'm Christian as well, so there are certain things which I wouldn't do.

"There's a passage in the Bible that says something along the lines of 'what does it gain for a man to gain the world but lose his soul?' Part of that is why I don't believe in that kind of thing."

Australia's win-at-all-costs mentality has been cited by some as part of the reason why Cummins' side used sandpaper to elicit more reverse swing from the ball during their 2018 tour of South Africa, with Sky Sports Cricket's Michael Atherton arguing that it created a "toxic dressing room culture".

"Don't get me wrong, I want to win. I want to do everything within my power to win," Itoje added.

"But if that power is taking performance-enhancing drugs or betting on the game, or I can list a number of other examples if that is the cost of winning, if I did those things and then win, what does the win really mean? I don't believe in that."

Erasmus, meanwhile, has now been banned three times in five years for outbursts at match officials, prompting rival head coaches such as Steve Borthwick, Eddie Jones and Fabian Galthie to call for them to be shown respect. Itoje echoes their view.

"Often in this game referees get a lot of stick," Itoje said. "It's a tough old job. They get stick because people care about the game and want the right results.

"But when you're out and talk to people from different sports - particularly footballers - what people commend rugby for is how much respect we have for the referees.

"It's really important for us as a game and a sport to make sure we continue to respect the referees.

"Some of the decisions they make will go in your favour, sometimes it'll go against you. Sometimes that's fair, sometimes that's unfair. Unfortunately, it's just the game.

"Otherwise perhaps we need to get AI to do the job, but I don't think anyone wants that either."

England have yet to decide how they will greet the haka, but Itoje insists whatever happens should only be a footnote to the afternoon.

"Our response to the haka isn't what is going to move the needle, it's what you do afterwards that matters," he added.

"What wins you the game is being really physical, incredibly accurate and really clinical against a top side like New Zealand. That's what we endeavour to do."
