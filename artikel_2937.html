LA Galaxy &quot;Fail To Meet the Moment&quot;, Fall 2-1 at RSL and Miss Playoffs

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Sandy, Utah -- The mission was straightforward.

Unfortunately for the LA Galaxy, a Decision Day collapse ends their season and eliminates them from the MLS Cup playoffs for the second straight year after finishing 11th in the Western Conference table.

LA had playoff qualification in their own hands, and they let it slip in 13 disastrous first-half minutes, conceding two goals to lose 2-1 away at Real Salt Lake on Saturday, November 7, 2026.

And LA Galaxy Head Coach Greg Vanney pointed to a lack of intensity from his players as one of the main reasons his side came up short in a must-win match. He was noncommittal when asked if his players let him down in a crucial fixture.

"I don't know. I don't have a great answer for it. I feel like we all let more let the fans down because they, you know, they were riding this ride with us, and when we come here, we need to put that intensity in, and we need to compete and play," Vanney explained to assorted media in the Galaxy's post-match press conference after the loss.

"They came out ready tonight, and they were flying, and we didn't meet the moment," a somber Galaxy boss said.

LA Galaxy Defender John Nelson agreed with Vanney and expressed his disappointment at the Galaxy's performance as well.

"That's what we talked about all week. Coming out here, having a good start," Nelson shared. "When you go two nil down, pretty much it is a playoff game at this point. You go two nil down, it makes everything harder. And yeah, like the gaps between us in the midfield, it was just way too open. We lost the ball way too many times in the first half. It felt like they were coming at us and coming at us, but yeah, two nil down the first 15 minutes is so hard, especially on the road in a playoff game. Then we're just behind the eight ball."

"Yeah, there's really not much to say. Honestly, it's just, it's just not good enough. It's not good enough."

The visitors needed all three points to lock in the seventh seed in the Western Conference, but before some fans had even taken their seats, they found themselves behind.

The only blemish in LA's 4-1 win over Austin FC last week, which set up this must-win match, was a goal conceded after giving the opposition too much space, and the Galaxy found themselves punished for the second straight week for their lack of intensity in the press.

RSL Center Back Noel Çalışkan got Real going with a surgical ball out of the backfield.

The defender's pass found Sergi Solans on the wrong side of Galaxy Center Back Emiro Garces. Solans took the ball in stride and did well to control the ball under pressure. RSL's talisman showed the patience to delay his shot, drag Galaxy Goalkeeper JT Marcinkowski out of position with a clever touch, and finish calmly for the 1-0 lead on 10 minutes.

The ease with which RSL picked the Galaxy defense apart was a sign of things to come.

"We were so wide open, it didn't make any sense," Vanney said in an almost beseeching manner. "We were so spread out and far apart, and every attack felt like it could have been a goal."

"So yeah, the start, it's again, didn't make sense. Our distances didn't make sense. They capitalized on it. They could have capitalized on it more in the first half, to be fair."

Just three minutes later, RSL did, and it made the Galaxy's job even harder.

After misplaying a clearance on an RSL corner kick, the rebound fell to ex-RSL player Pablo Ruiz. Ruiz's pass out fell to Zach Booth, who took the ball in himself on the left channel and toe-poked it home for the two-goal lead inside 13 minutes.

The Galaxy stemmed the flow of goals after conceding again, but their midfield was still largely overrun due to the space afforded the hosts. RSL created several chances but spurned them, with Nelson blocking a shot off the line to keep the deficit at two goals.

Marcinkowski, who made six saves in the loss, kept LA alive with three one-on-one saves.

Hirving "Chucky" Lozano took advantage of it to give the G's a lifeline in the 40th minute.

The winger drove the ball toward the right touchline and sent in a fizzed cross. The pace of the ball wrong footed Salt Lake Defender Luc De Fougerolles, who saw the ball cross the line for an own goal, and to give the Galaxy hope at 2-1.

They spurned several chances to tie the game, and ultimately ended with zero shots on target.

In truth, Real let them off the hook more than once, by missing several great looks at goal. In the end, the loss is a fair reflection not only of the game, but of the Galaxy's season as a whole.

"What is there to say? I mean, we can say a lot of things, but it just wasn't good enough," Nelson said plainly.

"And like I said, it didn't just come down tonight because it was just one point. But tonight, when we're down two on the on the road in first 15 minutes, it's just really really hard to come back. But no, for the fans, I feel for you. Just not good enough. But no, there wasn't much to say. What can you say?"

The defeat and another barren postseason are sure to prove Nelson right. This result might have shocked some Galaxy fans into silence.

Worse yet, it might've shocked them into indifference.

The loss officially eliminates LA from postseason play for the fourth time in the last six years. It is sure to prompt questions about Vanney's future with the team.

The coach was bullish about his future. For Vanney, it is clear he considers there to be unfinished business.

"I still think personally, I still think I can win championships in this league. It's just a matter of the moment and the opportunity, and getting our group together and everybody working together consistently. So, but again, not my decision."

That decision lies with the Galaxy front office, and they have tough decisions to make this offseason regarding Vanney and returning players heading into the 14-game sprint season. Both Vanney and Nelson claimed that the fans were let down and the team "fell short."

Galaxy fans are sure to agree, but despite that, it is they who will pay the price after another season of lowered standards and failure on the pitch.
