SpaceX Stock Is Trading Below Its $135 IPO Price. History Says What Other Giant IPOs Did From There.

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Most of these stocks climbed back to their offer prices within weeks. A year later, they ranged from 79% below to 197% above.

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 27, 2026 at 11:07PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

SpaceX (SPCX +4.83%) priced its initial public offering (IPO) at $135 a share in June. As of this writing, the stock trades near $134, and it's closed under $135 every trading day since Nov. 20.

SpaceX has been here before. It first closed below its IPO price on July 16, stayed there for a little over three weeks, and closed back above it on Aug. 10. Since then, the stock has dipped under $135 five more times, including the current stretch.

What does a fall below the IPO price tell investors? Not much by itself, if other big IPOs are any guide. I looked at five from the last 15 years that later slid under their offer prices: Alibaba Group (BABA +3.96%), Uber Technologies (UBER +0.42%), Rivian Automotive (RIVN +4.01%), Arm Holdings (ARM +6.22%), and Facebook, which now trades as Meta Platforms (META +3.13%). Most climbed back to their offer prices fast. What decided the following year was how the business did.

For three of the five, the first trip under the offer price was short. Alibaba, which priced its IPO at $68 in 2014, first closed below that level on Aug. 24, 2015, the day Shanghai's stock market saw its worst session since 2007. It closed back above $68 the next day. Arm, which priced at $51 in 2023, needed just one trading day, too. And Uber closed below its $45 offer price on its first day of trading in May 2019 but closed right at $45 around four weeks later.

Facebook took far longer. It priced at $38 in May 2012 and didn't close at or above that level again for around 14 months. Rivian, priced at $78 in November 2021, hasn't closed above its IPO price since it first dropped under it in January 2022.

But a quick trip back didn't say much about what happened next. A year after its first close below, Arm stood 197% above its offer price. Uber, which had gotten back over $45 in June 2019, was 27% below. Rivian was 79% under.

The gap, I think, boiled down to what each company reported in the months that followed.

Alibaba's revenue climbed 59% year over year in the quarter ending June 30, 2016, which its chief financial officer said was its fastest growth since the IPO. Arm's revenue rose 14% year over year in the quarter ending Dec. 31, 2023, to $824 million, a company record, and its licensing revenue got a boost from companies spending more on artificial intelligence (AI). Facebook closed back above $38 a bit over a week after reporting that mobile was around 41% of its ad revenue in the second quarter of 2013.

Uber and Rivian, though, were losing lots of money. Uber lost around $8.5 billion in 2019, and after June that year, its shares didn't close above $45 again until November 2020. Rivian lost around $4.7 billion in 2021. Then, in March 2022, it said supply-chain constraints left it with enough parts to make around 25,000 vehicles that year.

SpaceX has some of both groups in it. Its revenue climbed 161% year over year to $14.6 billion in the third quarter, almost twice the second quarter's $7.8 billion, and its operating income swung to $2.8 billion from a loss of $273 million a year before.

But SpaceX is spending much more than its operations bring in. This year through September, its operating activities produced around $13.1 billion in cash, while its capital expenditures totaled about $48.1 billion, mainly for its AI business. On its October earnings call, management said it expects first-quarter 2027 revenue between $33 billion and $37 billion, which could start to show whether that spending pays off.

A possible combination with Tesla, which special committees at both companies are reviewing, is another unknown.

At about $134, SpaceX is valued at roughly $1.8 trillion, or about 30 times sales if you annualize its third-quarter revenue. At that price, a lot of success for the AI spending is already baked in.

Does slipping under $135 make SpaceX stock a buy?

I don't think so. Being under $135 doesn't change much for me, and at around 30 times sales, I'd want to see more of that spending turn into cash before buying the stock.
