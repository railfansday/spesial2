Futu (FUTU) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Malaysia and Singapore led account growth as overseas expansion accelerated.

By Motley Fool Transcribing – Nov 19, 2026 at 1:39PM EST

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Futu (FUTU +5.33%) reported financial results for the third quarter of 2026 that demonstrated growth in international markets and a shift in revenue composition. Management stated that Malaysia and Singapore were the primary contributors to account growth as the company expanded its presence outside of Mainland China. The company reported a strategic emphasis on wealth management products, including new tokenized assets and mutual funds, to diversify beyond brokerage commissions. Management noted that the integration of artificial intelligence tools is intended to support self-directed investors in strategy creation and portfolio monitoring.

During the quarter, we further deepened our client relationships, expanded our multi-asset product offerings and further diversified our geographic mix and revenue base. These developments strengthened our confidence in our long-term strategy, while we remain focused on disciplined execution.

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] Let me take you through the quarter. As of quarter end, our total funded accounts reached 4 million, up 27.9% year-over-year. Despite challenging equity markets and mixed news across certain regions, we added 163,000 net new funded accounts during the quarter. Excluding Mainland China and Japan, net new funded accounts would have been approximately 173,000. International markets remain the primary growth engine with Malaysia contributing to the largest number of net new funded account additions, followed by Singapore and Hong Kong, reflecting geographically diversified growth. Our average client retention rate remained above 98%, demonstrating our ability to support clients through changing market conditions and evolving investment needs.

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] Now turning to client assets. We delivered resilient net asset inflows of approximately HKD 34.5 billion during the quarter despite challenging market conditions, supported by our expanding product suite and deepening customer engagement. Total client assets were HKD 1.33 trillion as of quarter end, up 7.6% year-over-year and down 4.6% quarter-over-quarter. The sequential decline was primarily due to lower market valuations. Margin financing and securities lending balance declined 22.3% quarter-over-quarter to HKD 73.8 billion, largely reflecting a high comparison base in the prior quarter, while remaining higher year-over-year.

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] In Wealth Management, our strategic transition towards a broader wealth management platform gained traction during the quarter. Client assets in wealth management increased 11.8% year-over-year and 9% quarter-over-quarter to HKD 196.4 billion. During the quarter, clients rotated into money market bonds amid a more cautious investment sentiment. We further enriched our product offering with 2 Hong Kong market firsts: a weekly dealing public mutual fund and a fully tokenized gold ETF. To meet the increasingly sophisticated trading and asset allocation needs of high net worth clients, we launched over-the-counter derivatives trading in Hong Kong and introduced the wholesale fund to professional investors in Malaysia.

Our AI assistant has also expanded into wealth management, supporting clients throughout the investment process from portfolio analysis and fund selection to ongoing portfolio monitoring. As a result, wealth management penetration climbed to 28.2%, representing 1.13 million customers.

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] Turning to trading volume. Total trading volume was HKD 5.01 trillion, up 28.4% year-over-year and down 22% quarter-over-quarter, as softer equity market conditions, particularly the sharp pullback in semiconductor and AI-related names, dampened client trading activity. U.S. stock trading volume declined 24% sequentially to HKD 3.81 trillion. Hong Kong stock trading volume declined 19.2% quarter-over-quarter to HKD 942.5 billion.

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] On IPO services, amid continued strength in Hong Kong's IPO market, we provided IPO-related services to more than 70% of new listings and served as joint bookrunners for several prominent IPOs, including those of Momenta, Longsys and Mech-Mind Robotics.

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] Despite near-term volatility in global equity markets, we remain confident in our long-term outlook. Rising investable wealth across Asia, growing demand for multi-asset allocation and continued rising use of digital financial services remain our long-term growth drivers. Our priorities remain clear: deepening client relationships, expanding our wealth management capabilities and strengthening our international franchise, laying the foundation for sustainable growth.

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] Now I'd like to invite our CFO, Arthur, to discuss our financial performance.

Arthur Chen: Thank you, Leaf and Leon. Please allow me to walk you through our financial performance in the third quarter. All the numbers are in Hong Kong dollars, unless otherwise noted. Total revenue was HKD 6.7 billion, up 4.5% from HKD 6.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025. Brokerage commission and handling charge income was HKD 2.8 billion, down 4% year-over-year and 17% Q-o-Q. The year-over-year decrease was mainly due to the decrease in blended commission rate from 7.5 basis points to 5.6 basis points, partially offset by the 28% year-over-year increase in the total trading volume, while the sequential decline was due to lower trading volume as market sentiment softened from the previous quarter.

Interest income was HKD 3.2 billion, up 4% year-over-year and 1% Q-o-Q. The year-over-year growth was mainly driven by higher interest income from margin financing and bank deposits, while the sequential growth benefited from higher interest income from bank deposits and securities lending business. Other income was HKD 735 million, up 65% year-over-year and 3% Q-o-Q. The year-over-year increase was primarily attributable to higher currency exchange income and fund distribution service income. The Q-o-Q increase benefited from higher fund distribution service income. Our total cost was HKD 1.1 billion, up 34% compared to the third quarter of 2025. Brokerage commission and handling charge expenses were HKD 239 million, up 49% year-over-year and down 4% Q-o-Q.

These movements were broadly in line with the year-over-year increase and the Q-o-Q decrease in trading volume, respectively. Interest expenses were HKD 586 million, up 24% year-over-year and 14% Q-o-Q. The year-over-year increase was mainly due to higher expenses associated with margin financing. The Q-o-Q increase was mainly driven by higher interest expenses associated with our securities lending business. Processing and servicing costs were HKD 236 million, up 48% year-over-year and 5% Q-o-Q. Both the year-over-year and the Q-o-Q increase were primarily driven by higher product service fees. As a result, total gross profit was HKD 5.6 billion, broadly stable year-over-year. Gross margin was 84.2% as compared to 87.6% in the third quarter of 2025.

Operating expenses were HKD 1.6 billion, down 7% year-over-year and 9% Q-o-Q. R&D expenses were HKD 484 million, down 16% year-over-year and 3% Q-o-Q. The year-over-year and Q-o-Q decrease was mainly attributable to effective cost control measures. Selling and marketing expenses were HKD 545 million, down 7% year-over-year and 17% Q-o-Q. The year-over-year and Q-o-Q decrease was mainly driven by fewer funded accounts. G&A expenses were HKD 561 million, up 3% year-over-year and down 5% Q-o-Q. The year-over-year increase was primarily due to higher office rental expenses, while the sequential decrease was due to lower professional expenses. As a result, income from operations was HKD 4 billion, up 3% (sic) [ 3.5% ] year-over-year.

Operating margin was 60.4%, largely stable compared to the third quarter of 2025. Our net income was largely stable year-over-year at HKD 3.2 billion. Net income margin was 47.6% in the third quarter compared to 50.2% in the same quarter last year. Our effective tax rate for the quarter was 15.8%. That concludes our prepared remarks. Leon Qi: [Interpreted] Let me translate Leaf's first part of the answer briefly. We think about this development at 3 levels. First, from a global industry perspective, we think NASDAQ's move toward 23/5 trading reflects a broader structural shift in global capital markets. As more investors are getting used to allocating assets across time zones, global capital markets may gradually evolve from competing across geographies to competing around time zones. That's why we don't view 23/5 as simply extending trading hours. We see it as an important step in the evolution of the U.S. capital market into a truly global marketplace. It reflects the long-term trend of digitization, globalization and cross-time-zone investing. NASDAQ's own data also supports this view.

Extended trading hours, including pre-market, after-hours and overnight sessions, already account for about 11% of total U.S. equity trading volume, with overnight trading making up about 4%. That's a clear indication that the demand is real.

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] And the second layer is what does it mean for Futu? We see it as a structural positive. Our client base is global and across time zones, with a significant portion in Asia Pacific. U.S. equities have also long been one of the most important asset classes on our platform. Over the past few quarters, we've already seen clients gradually building trading habits outside regular market hours. Overnight U.S. equity trading has consistently accounted for a high single-digit to low teens percentage of our total U.S. equity volume. If you include pre-market and after-hours sessions, extended hours trading now represents more than 20%.

That said, the bigger opportunity is not just about near-term trading, but what's more important is client engagement and asset accumulation over the long run. 23/5 further lowers the barrier to global investing by allowing Asian investors to access U.S. markets during their local daytime hours. We believe the long-term implications of that are potentially more significant. From a product perspective, Futu and Moomoo have already supported overnight U.S. equity trading through ATS for some time. We have built experience in execution, clearing and risk management, and we will prepare our infrastructure ready to ensure clients have a seamless experience as market hours expand.

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] The U.S. market is expanding its global investor reach by extending trading hours. Asian markets may eventually face a similar challenge. For international financial centers like Hong Kong, Japan and Singapore, the question becomes how to improve the accessibility for investors across time zones, how to deepen international investor participation, how to balance liquidity and quality and how to use digital infrastructure to better serve global investors. These topics all deserve our ongoing observation. Thank you.

Then on the AI side, we further extend our capabilities of FutuBull and Moomoo AI into the wealth management front, covering the full investment cycles from portfolio selections and fund screening to position monitoring to enhance both service efficiency and our user experience.

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] The reason we start disclosing net asset flow is to help investors better understand the quality of our client assets. As you may know, client asset balance is driven primarily by 2 factors. One is mark-to-market changes in asset valuation. The other is actual client cash flows. Simply looking at period end client asset balance alone doesn't always tell you how much the growth came from, whether it's coming from market movements versus client behavior. Therefore, we think net asset flows gives investors a clearer view of our ability to attract and retain client assets. In other words, it helps investors to better distinguish between the market beta and the company alpha behind our client asset movement.

Back to the third quarter, we've generated HKD 34.5 billion of net asset inflow. This was mainly driven by 3 factors. First, as both Leaf and Arthur just mentioned, we continue to expand our product offering, covering client demands in different market environments. Second, we see more and more clients are using Futu as a long-term wealth allocation platform rather than only a trading platform. Third, our international expansion has resulted in a more diversified client base. One additional point worth highlighting is the composition of these net flows. Over 60% of net inflow in the third quarter came from existing clients, which are defined as clients acquired prior to the current month.

In fact, this ratio has remained above 55% in every quarter over the past 2 years. To sum up, we view net asset inflow as one of the most important indicators of our long-term competitiveness. It reflects our strategic evolution from focusing primarily on the number of client growth to high-quality client growth, and it gives more visibility of our capability to attract and accumulate client assets. Operator: Our next question comes from the line of Shuo Yang with Goldman Sachs.

Shuo Yang: [Foreign Language] This is Shuo Yang from Goldman Sachs. We have seen that accelerating adoption of AI across the sector. Could you share management's view on how AI is impacting your products and also the operational efficiency? And the second question is on interest income. We noticed that interest income delivered a strong print this quarter, which helped to underpin revenue growth in a challenging macro backdrop. So the question is, could management break down the key drivers of net interest income and also provide a forward-looking outlook for the net interest income and also the underlying drivers?

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] We tend to view AI as a tool that enhances capabilities rather than simply a tool for cost optimization. First, AI lowers the barrier to investing. In the past, many retail investors had limited access to professional knowledge, which made it difficult for them to fully understand derivatives, quantitative strategies and sophisticated trading tools. AI significantly lowers the barriers to strategy creation. Investors can use natural language to obtain market insights, generate trading ideas and even build and optimize strategies. We believe this can structurally increase market participation and trading activity. AI is also making investing and wealth management services more accessible. Historically, many tools and research resources were primarily available to institutional investors.

AI creates an opportunity for more retail investors to access information, understand markets and make investment decisions more efficiently. As these barriers come down, more people are likely to take a more active role in making their own investment decisions rather than relying on mutual funds or traditional advisory models. This could further expand the self-directed investing population. Given that Futu is a one-stop platform built around self-directed investors, we are naturally well positioned to benefit from that trend. Second, Futu has an ecosystem advantage. Over the years, we have built a relatively comprehensive ecosystem that spans market data, content, community, trading tools, open API, derivatives and wealth management.

With the emergence of AI, these capabilities can work together in combinations. AI connects previously separate functions, further improving user experience and stickiness. Looking ahead, we see agent-based trading as an incremental opportunity. For Futu, a more important opportunity is to use AI to upgrade the user experience of information, tools and trading, helping self-directed investors make better investment decisions more efficiently. Over the long run, we believe AI will not change the fundamental elements of our success in financial services. Technology innovation, product capabilities, compliance standards and brand influence will continue to be the keys of long-term competitiveness. That's why our strategy is always embracing technological innovation while continuing to invest in compliance, risk management and user experience.

Leon Qi: [Interpreted] In the third quarter, around 40% each of our interest income came from client cash and margin financing, with the remainder mainly from our securities lending business. For cash interest income, the increase was mainly driven by a higher interest rate environment in both Hong Kong and the U.S. during the quarter. Client net asset inflows remained resilient, although some client funds continued to migrate into money market funds and other wealth management products, which had some impact on cash balances, but we were still able to manage to realize positive cash interest income quarter-on-quarter growth. For margin financing interest income, the decline was mainly attributable to market volatility and pullbacks in both Hong Kong and U.S. equity markets.

For securities lending interest income, growth was primarily driven by market demand, while the higher interest rate environment also supported lending yields during the quarter. Looking at the longer term, as clients continue to increase their U.S. equity holdings, the pool of securities available for lending should expand accordingly. At the same time, we continue to optimize our lending inventory and broaden our counterparty network, which should provide structural support for the long-term growth of this business. Longer term, we would encourage investors to think about our interest income through 3 factors: client asset balances, client risk appetite and the interest rate. Among them, client asset scale is the most important and sustainable long-term driver.

Ultimately, what matters most is the continued growth of client assets and the increasingly diversified ecosystem across trading, financing and wealth management provided by our platform. More broadly, we have observed that over 20% of our total revenue today comes from businesses that are less correlated with equity markets. We see these as recurring revenue streams or low beta revenue, which primarily include client cash interest income, fund distribution income, market information and data services, trust services and structured products. In the third quarter, such low beta revenue continued to deliver sequential positive growth and achieved double-digit year-on-year growth. We believe this demonstrates the noncyclical side of our revenue model. Thank you for your question.
