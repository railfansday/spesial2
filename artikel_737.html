The Deals: Yamaha Acquires Visual Technology Company Disguise for $280 Million & More

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Also: Ovation Rights buys the copyrights to Jeff Wayne's Musical Version of The War of the Worlds, among other recent deals.

Yamaha Corporation, the musical instruments and audio products company, acquired Disguise, which provides visual technology for live events, location-based experiences and virtual production spanning software, hardware and creative and technical services. A public notice published by Yamaha on Friday (Nov. 13) pegs the price of the transaction at 215 million pounds ($281 million).

According to a press release, Disguise technology has powered the visuals for tours by Beyoncé, Taylor Swift and Bad Bunny, along with locations including the Burj Khalifa in Dubai and the Stade de France in Paris.

“Welcoming Disguise marks a major step for Yamaha as we move beyond the traditional boundaries of musical instruments and audio equipment to create new experiential value centred on sound and music,” Atsugi Yamaha, president/representative executive officer of Yamaha Corporation, said in a statement. “As part of our long-term growth strategy, we are expanding our audio business into adjacent areas, including entertainment. We will combine the strengths of Disguise and Yamaha while respecting the brand, technology, and global relationships Disguise has built with customers and partners. Together, we will make new waves in live entertainment.”

Following the acquisition, the Disguise “brand, identity and relationships with customers and partners will be preserved,” according to a press release, with Disguise becoming “a new pillar alongside Yamaha’s existing business.” — Chris Eggertsen

Entertainment rights company Ovation Rights acquired the underlying copyrights in Jeff Wayne’s Musical Version of The War of the Worlds and struck a long-term creative partnership with Wayne, who composed and produced the music for the best-selling 1978 double concept album that spawned a multimedia franchise, including an immersive experience. The deal will see Ovation Rights working closely with Wayne and his family to create new opportunities for the property across live entertainment, music and digital formats ahead of its 50th anniversary in 2028. There are also plans to relaunch the immersive experience.

According to a press release, Jeff Wayne’s Musical Version of The War of the Worlds has sold more than 16 million records worldwide since its 1978 release. It adds that over nearly 50 years, the album “has evolved into a global entertainment property spanning spectacular arena tours and is a Guinness World Record ™ title holder as the world’s longest-running musical immersive theatre production.” — Chris Eggertsen

Schubert Music Group acquired the group of indie publishing and recorded music catalogs of French music publisher Cyril Dohar, which were built over three decades. The group consists of Éditer à Paris, Grounded Music, Modjo Music and Editions Cyril Dohar, which collectively include song copyrights and master rights to works by global stars David Bowie, Fergie, Goldfrapp, Joe Strummer, Jarvis Cocker and Florence + the Machine, along with a French roster of works by Orange Blossom, Dope Saint Jude, Sandra Nkaké and Disiz. The repertoire additionally includes works by reggae star Pablo Moses, world music acts Groundation, Yotam Silberstein, Sam Mangwana and Touré Kunda, and the Beautiful Songs catalog. — Chris Eggertsen

Nonprofit record label family Smithsonian Folkways Recordings acquired two record labels: Voyager Recordings, which released fiddle music and other traditional music of the Pacific Northwest and elsewhere; and Bay Records, which focused on Northern California old-time, traditional folk, bluegrass, fiddle and string band music. Selections from both labels’ catalogs are now available to download or stream.

“Smithsonian Folkways is honored to bring Voyager and Bay Records back into circulation as part of our collection,” said Maureen Loughran, director/curator of Smithsonian Folkways, in a statement. “These labels both have important roles in documenting the folk music history and traditions of the West Coast, a history that is often overshadowed by the long- storied folk revival of the East Coast. What is found here are vibrant music traditions, fierce fiddle tunes, old folk songs, and a story of two labels with deep connections to the labels already within the Smithsonian Folkways Recordings collection.”

A full discography for Bay Records is available here and for Voyager Recordings here. — Chris Eggertsen

AYO Music Group, an invitation-only music copyright administrator, announced investments from Wu-Tang Clan founder RZA, Atlanta music executives and Disturbing The Peace Records founders Chaka Zulu and Jeff Dixon, family office CIH Group and New York entrepreneur David Goldberg. Founded by Deborah Mannis-Gardner, Pär Almqvist and Stacey Haber, AYO offers global administration of publishing, neighboring rights and recordings. Its clients include MC Lyte, Pete Rock, LeToya Luckett and Lil Mama. — Chris Eggertsen

Rendezvous Sound, a new electronic music label and publishing venture, officially launched. The company’s label component will operate as a joint venture with broke Records, with longtime industry exec Alexander Bonavia leading the company from London. Bonavia takes on the position after previous roles at  Hipgnosis, Warner Music Group and Wavo. After operating for less than a year, the company reports that it’s averaging 100 million streams a month.

“We are agile, aggressive and modern,” Bonavia says in a statement. “We bet that the left of center electronic artists selling out venues would start to cross over to streaming, and that bet has already paid off with such artists as Kolter, Beave, Adriatique, Beltran, Ben Sterling, Ayybo and Joshwa.”

“Alex is a sharp dealmaker, consistently able to find and close the records and artists who succeed in both club culture and streaming,” added broke CEO Andre Benz. “His international marketing experience has also been invaluable to our wider team.” — Katie Bain

Dubai-based independent music and entertainment company Afterpeak, founded by Avery Chauhan, signed an exclusive multi-year global distribution deal with Too Lost for both catalog and future releases. Too Lost has additionally provided Afterpeak with “multi-seven-figure financing” to support the company’s catalog growth, artist development, new music releases and international expansion.

According to the release, Afterpeak has more than 25,000 releases and over 3 billion Spotify streams under its belt since its founding 2020. The company describes itself as being focused on “a high-volume, internet-native approach to recorded music, combining creative development with technology, data and catalog management,” adding that it “has built thousands of recordings of popular works with producers and studio musicians around the world while increasingly expanding its focus on original music, artist development and licensing.” — Chris Eggertsen

Warner Music France invested in Délicieuse Musique, the indie company that operates a portfolio of labels including Délicieuse Records, Nice Guys, Noire & Blanche, Egoplanet, Crosswalk and Hip Dozer. Through the deal, Délicieuse labels will enjoy access to Warner Music Group’s global distribution, marketing, synch licensing and brand partnerships resources, in addition to the creative and studio resources of Warner Music France.

Délicieuse will continue operating independently, led by co-founders Mathieu Léglise and Gautier de Bosredon, with the company’s existing team continuing to operate out of its headquarters in Bordeaux, France.

Founded in 2012, Délicieuse encompasses 10 specialized imprints and manages a multi-genre roster of more than 1,200 artists. — Chris Eggertsen

Generative AI audio company Sonilo, which is building video-native music and sound models, struck a licensing deal with Symphonic. Under the agreement, Sonilo will license opted-in recordings from Symphonic to train its AI music model. A press release says Sonilo “will provide attribution reporting to support transparency and compensation for the rights holders behind the music.” — Chris Eggertsen

Lyrics provider Musixmatch and DistroKid renewed their global partnership via a new multi-year agreement. The DIY distributor will continue offering Musixmatch’s lyric distribution via its Lyric Blaster service, which allows artists to deliver their lyrics to major streaming and social platforms.

Musixmatch also announced a new partnership with music royalty collection and distribution platform Notes.fm. The deal will allow Notes.fm to bring synchronized lyrics capabilities to its distribution offerings. — Chris Eggertsen

Believe‘s b:electronic and new electronic music label STILL BE AROUND signed a partnership that will see both labels align on A&R scouting, playlisting, DJ promotion, performance marketing and more. Led by Andreas Weitkämper, STILL BE AROUND operates out of Hamburg, Germany, and Lisbon, Portugal. It has upcoming releases from artists including Roger Sanchez, KI/KI, Interplanetary Criminal and Jaden Bojsen. — Chris Eggertsen

Symphonic signed a deal with Roblox that will bring hundreds of thousands of curated Symphonic-distributed songs to the gaming platform for use in games and virtual spaces. Symphonic songs are now available in the Roblox Creator Store; the deal also covers use of the music in Roblox’s new Moments section, where players can watch gameplay captures from the Roblox community and discover new games to play. — Chris Eggertsen

Universal Music Canada (UMC) signed an A&R and label distribution partnership with First Class Music Group, launched earlier this year by Leo Strang and Jake Pretorious — 24-year-olds who co-founded the Toronto underground rap festival First Class Fest. The two companies will jointly sign and develop Canadian artists under the deal. The partnership also covers distribution. First Class Fest will remain separate from the UMC agreement. — Chris Eggertsen
