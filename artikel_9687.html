Nowadays, having enough product material is rarely the main problem for e-commerce operators. Amazon listings may already contain product images, descriptions, key features, pricing information, and other commercial assets. The harder part is turning those materials into enough video creatives for advertising campaigns without spending hours on scripting, editing, and producing variations.

Therefore, we tested Vizard Agent from this perspective, focusing on a common scenario in which an e-commerce operator has plenty of product information but needs to produce a large number of promotional videos. The test looked particularly at its Amazon Product Link to Promo Video workflow, as well as its UGC-style and advertising function.

The starting point was quiet simple. Instead of preparing a complete video production package, the workflow begins with an Amazon product listing URL. Vizard's Ads mode allows sellers to paste an Amazon listing URL and have Vizard Agent extract product visuals and product details as the basis for video creation. The workflow can also start from uploaded product images, descriptions, and key features when a URL is not the preferred input.

This approach fits the broader design of Vizard Agent. The Agent can work with links, existing footage, files, or combinations of user materials and generated assets. It interprets the request, plans the video, selects or creates assets, makes creative decisions, assembles the result, and reviews the finished video. For an e-commerce operator, that changes the starting point considerably. The product listing itself can become the creative brief rather than simply a reference that someone has to manually read before beginning production.

The workflow is useful because it connects product information with the actual video production process. After providing the listing, the user can specify the desired video type by giving the prompts, such as a product showcase, lifestyle-oriented video, or promotional advertisement. Vizard Agent then adapts the visual treatment to the requested format. Product images and descriptions provide the foundation, while subtitles, voiceover, visual elements, and other creative components can be incorporated into the finished video. This is where the workflow becomes more practical than simply converting an image into a slideshow. The purpose is to turn product information into a structured promotional story. Instead of deciding manually which product feature should appear first, writing the script, selecting supporting visuals, and arranging the scenes one by one, the operator can give the Agent the product and the intended advertising goal.

Vizard's documented product-ad workflow also covers product pages to video ads, product images to UGC-style ads, multiple advertising angles for one product, variations from a winning advertisement, and seasonal campaign variations.

For high-volume e-commerce campaigns, the ability to move from one product to several creative directions is arguably more important than producing one polished video.

The UGC mode was one of the more practical parts of the test. We started with the same product materials rather than preparing a separate batch of creator footage, and used the product images as the basis for a UGC-style ad. The result was noticeably different from a conventional product showcase, with the product positioned within a more creator-oriented advertising format.

What worked particularly well here was the amount of preparation Vizard Agent handled on its own. We did not have to build a separate UGC script, work out the structure manually, or assemble the product visuals scene by scene. Vizard Agent allows product images to be turned into UGC-style ads and treats the creative process as a broader video-production task rather than a sequence of individual editing operations.From a testing perspective, the more interesting point was that the same product assets could be taken in different directions without restarting the production process. We could use the product information for a conventional promotional video, then move toward a UGC-style treatment when a different advertising angle was needed. For an e-commerce operator producing creatives at scale, that flexibility was more valuable than simply having another editing option.

The biggest advantage we observed was not a single editing feature. It was the reduction in the number of production decisions required from the operator. A conventional workflow might require a marketer to read the listing, identify selling points, write a hook, develop a script, gather product visuals, edit the footage, add captions and voiceover, and then repeat much of the process for another advertising concept.

Vizard Agent approaches these activities as one broader production task. Its documented workflow covers understanding the request, planning the video, selecting or creating assets, making creative and editing decisions, assembling the video, and reviewing the result. That becomes particularly valuable when one product needs ten creative variations rather than one finished advertisement. The platform's documented support for multiple ad angles, variations from winning ads, and seasonal versions directly addresses this type of production volume.

Vizard Agent is most relevant to Amazon sellers, DTC brands, e-commerce marketers, agencies, and performance marketing teams that already have substantial product information but need to increase their video output. It is also useful when the same product needs to appear in different creative formats across short-form channels. Vizard's Amazon product video workflow is not limited to Amazon itself. Generated videos can also be used across social and advertising channels such as Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts.

We should declare that AI’s work does not remove the need for human oversight. Product claims, pricing, brand requirements, campaign objectives, and platform policies still need to be checked before publication. The quality of the source listing also matters because the Agent is working from the information and assets available to it. That limitation is important for Amazon sellers in particular. Automating production does not automatically make an advertisement accurate or commercially appropriate. Human review remains necessary, especially when a video makes specific product claims.

For the e-commerce scenario we tested, Vizard Agent is more useful to think of as a video production agent than as another AI editing tool. The Amazon Product Link to Promo Video workflow provides a particularly straightforward entry point. An existing product listing can become the starting material for a promotional video, while UGC-style creation and multiple advertising angles allow the same product information to support different creative approaches. For e-commerce teams that already have plenty of product material but struggle with the volume of video they need to produce, that workflow reduction is the main value. The interesting shift is not simply that AI can edit a video faster. It is that the product listing, creative brief, asset selection, scripting, editing, and variation process can increasingly be treated as one production task.
