2026-Humanoid-IKEA-Assembly-Challenge: 35 Motors, Seven Cameras, in FiftyOne

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Posted on Nov 11 • Originally published at employabledatascientist.com

A humanoid assembling IKEA furniture logs 35 motors at once, and for each motor it records position, velocity, acceleration and torque. The 2026 Humanoid IKEA Assembly Challenge adds seven camera streams per episode, including infrared pairs from both wrists, and a human-annotated list of the subtasks the robot is working through. This FiftyOne version is a 6-episode, 30.7-minute subset, parsed so the torque plots sit on the same timeline as the wrist cameras.

The 2026 Humanoid IKEA Assembly Challenge subset comes from the BitRobot release. It keeps the shortest episode from each of six recording days spanning the collection window, and converts them to native multimodal MCAP episodes. Each of the 6 episodes runs 7,368 to 11,099 frames.

Each episode also has the fields episode_name, task, recording_day, subtasks, num_subtasks, num_frames and duration.

The subtask sequence is the part that makes the telemetry readable. The labels run in this order: move to table, move table base, pick table leg, insert table leg to table base, rotate leg to tighten, rotate table base, flip table.

Open an episode and play it. The wrist RGB views, the infrared pairs and the motor plots share one timeline, so you can scrub to the instruction change for "rotate leg to tighten" and check what the wrist camera shows while the arm torques move.

Six episodes is a small sample. Each one is the shortest episode from its recording day, so the subset leans toward short runs and says little about how long assemblies behave. Use it to check the stream layout, build a viewer or test a parser. For a policy-training claim you need the full BitRobot release.

The camera payloads are the source JPEGs passed through unchanged. The conversion from ROS 2 CDR to the FiftyOne MCAP flavor made one repair: the source recordings declare a SportModeState schema that omits its trailing path_point array, and the field is restored at decode so the odometry stream reads. If you decode the original files with a strict schema, expect that mismatch.

Six MCAP episodes of 7,368 to 11,099 frames each, 30.7 minutes in total. Each episode has seven camera streams, telemetry for 35 motors, gripper and end-effector streams, base odometry, two IMUs and the annotated subtask sequence.

The cameras are a stereo head camera, left and right wrist RGB cameras, and the infrared pairs from both wrist depth cameras. The body streams carry all 35 motors with position, velocity, acceleration and torque, and each has a timeline plot.

Call load_from_hub with Voxel51/2026-Humanoid-IKEA-Assembly-Challenge and persistent=True, then launch the App. Sort by num_frames to start with the longest episode.

The instruction stream holds a human-annotated sequence: move to table, move table base, pick table leg, insert table leg to table base, rotate leg to tighten, rotate table base, and flip table. Each episode also stores the sequence in a subtasks field and a num_subtasks count.
