Nebius vs. SoundHound AI: Which AI Tech Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Nebius's revenue leapt past $2.2 billion in a year, while SoundHound's sales growth is slower but its balance sheet carries almost no debt.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 20, 2026 at 12:30PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Nebius Group ›

The artificial intelligence boom has created massive demand for both infrastructure and software solutions. Choosing between Nebius Group (NBIS -13.63%) and SoundHound AI (SOUN -8.25%) depends on your preference for cloud scale or specialized voice interfaces.

Nebius provides the underlying cloud infrastructure and model training tools that power modern AI applications. SoundHound AI specializes in conversational intelligence, helping brands interact with customers through voice across various devices and industries. While both operate in the tech stocks landscape, their business models offer very different ways to capture the AI growth curve.

Nebius functions as an AI cloud company, offering a full-stack platform that covers everything from data processing to model deployment. The company says it has over 1,300 employees and provides a platform that spans everything from model training to production runtime. This full-stack approach allows it to serve specialized markets like physical AI and media entertainment, seeking to carve out a distinct space against the largest cloud providers.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $2.2 billion, representing explosive revenue growth of approximately 535.8%. The company also reported net income of roughly $124.5 million, reflecting a net margin of approximately 5.7%. This dramatic climb in sales indicates a rapidly scaling operation that is already showing the ability to generate a profit.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 1.1x, which compares total debt to shareholder equity. The current ratio, a measure of short-term liquidity, is a healthy 3.1x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly negative $3.7 billion, and stock-based compensation represented roughly 21.6% of operating cash flow.

SoundHound AI focuses on conversational AI, providing platforms that allow businesses to build voice-enabled agents for cars, TVs, and restaurants. Major partners include LG and Ultraviolette Automotive, reflecting a strategy of integrating its tech into high-volume consumer hardware. The recent acquisition of LivePerson further strengthens its ability to handle digital engagement and messaging for high-volume enterprise clients.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $223.6 million, marking revenue growth of approximately 50.7%. The company reported a net loss of around $74.2 million, resulting in a negative net margin of roughly 33.2%. While revenue growth shows steady adoption, the net loss reflects ongoing investments in research and the costs of integrating multiple new acquisitions.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was roughly 0.0x, indicating a very low reliance on borrowed funds. The current ratio stands at a strong 4.6x, providing a significant cushion for short-term operational needs. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was approximately negative $103.1 million.

Nebius operates in a highly capital-intensive industry where infrastructure costs can outpace revenue growth. The company faces intense competition from global cloud giants that possess much larger financial resources and established market positions. Rapid expansion into multiple sectors, such as life sciences and robotics, also creates operational complexity and execution risks.

SoundHound AI faces significant risks from its history of net losses and negative operating cash flows. The company is in a highly competitive market against both established big tech firms and specialized AI vendors, requiring constant product innovation to avoid obsolescence. It also faces ongoing integration challenges and potential asset impairment risks following major acquisitions such as LivePerson and Amelia.

SoundHound AI appears to be the more conservatively priced option based on its P/S ratio, which measures price against sales over the past twelve months. It does not have a forward P/E ratio because it is not projected to turn a profit.

AI is now so ubiquitous in our everyday lives and so popular in the stock market that it can be difficult to gauge long-term prospects for related stocks.

Nebius Group, N.V. is a relative newcomer to AI. It used to be part of the Russian Internet giant Yandex. It was split from Yandex to separate the Russian (Yandex) parts of the business from the non-Russian (Nebius) parts. That left Nebius with a lot of cash and a few unconnected businesses. Just last year, Nebius decided to go all-in on AI, using its large cash pile to buy up thousands of top-tier chips from Nvidia Corp (NVDA -10.83%) and turn itself into a hyperscaler. The audacious move paid off, with annual sales hitting about $530 million last year. The year prior, before it dove into AI, it generated less than a fifth of that.

To SoundHound AI's benefit, it's the more established of the two businesses, having been involved in speech recognition since its founding in 2005. In the past year, the company has chalked up impressive results, including counting 12 of the world's 15 largest banks, 4 of the 5 largest automakers, and 4 of the 5 largest airlines among its customers.

It's fair to question what competitive moat an AI speech recognition business has in an age where AI's capabilities are growing by leaps and bounds. It's not beyond the realm of possibility that larger competitors like Alphabet Inc. (GOOG -0.61%) or Apple Inc. (AAPL -1.27%) could take over the speech recognition space by leveraging their massive war chests and the popularity of their other products.

If you can justify NBIS's relatively high valuation, then Nebius Group and its aggressive push to be counted among the giants of AI make it the choice. The lessons of AI, so far, are that size matters. SoundHound's lack of profit for the foreseeable future is also a red flag. For 2027, go with Nebius Group.
