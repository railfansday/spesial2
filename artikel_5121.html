Coinbase stock sits near $108.78 after dropping roughly 40% over three months, yet CEO Brian Armstrong is pitching a $5 trillion future.

He believes autonomous AI agents will handle over 99.9% of all financial payments within ten years.

While Wall Street frets over sluggish spot trading, Coinbase is building bank accounts and payment tools specifically for software bots.

Personal assistant bots can use credit cards to buy plane tickets or shoes. Yet bot-to-bot teamwork requires instant, tiny global transfers that card networks simply can’t process well.

So Coinbase is building USDC stablecoin payment tools on its Base network, backed by $22 billion in platform USDC balances. Industry estimates also project this bot payment market will hit $3 trillion to $5 trillion by 2030.

Live data from CoinGecko at the time shows the AI Agents token category sitting near a $2.8 billion market cap today, which undoubtedly shows how early this trend remains.

Handing software real cash sounds risky, and it honestly is. Armstrong admitted he gave three or four AI agents $100,000 to trade different portfolios, and results were mixed.

To stop bots from blowing up accounts, Coinbase enforces strict spending caps, separate wallets, and practice trading modes. Behind the scenes, over 99% of user funds stay offline while security tools scan every line of code.

COIN stock fast approaching $80 and could keep printing lower lows until a clear catalyst sparks a bounce. Yet traders obsess over daily charts while Coinbase quietly builds revenue beyond spot Bitcoin fees.

Its Prediction markets recently crossed $200 million in annualized revenue, helping derivatives and prediction contracts generate 25% of trading fee revenue. Tokenized stocks on Base also just passed $4 billion to $5 billion.

Meanwhile, Armstrong blames recent Bitcoin selling on cash rotating into AI IPOs like SpaceX and last week’s Anthropic listing, insisting the cycle low is already in.
