Why Intel Stock Just Dropped

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Sure, Nvidia sold 25% of its Intel stock -- but it kept 75%.

Pity Intel (INTC -2.87%) investors. Not only have they had to deal with the fallout of an Nvidia CEO scandal not of their making this month -- one that's contributed to an 18% drop in stock price in November alone.

Not only are they now struggling to parse a new SemiAnalysis report warning that capital spending on AI chips in 2027 could be 19% below previous estimates, 2028 spending might be 28% below, and 2029 spending could fall 41% below estimates.

Today, Intel investors are facing yet another negative catalyst for their stock. Nvidia (NVDA -1.57%), it seems, is selling down its stake in its semiconducting rival -- and it's costing Intel stock a 2.3% decline through 1:30 p.m. ET today.

Nvidia just confirmed in an SEC filing that it has sold 25% of its Intel stock position, reducing its stake from 214.8 million shares to 161.1 million. At the same time, as StreetInsider.com reports, Nvidia has increased its ownership in SpaceX (SPCX +3.03%) by 6%, to 130.5 million shares.

All of these numbers are as of Sept. 30, before the Jensen Huang scandal broke.

Nvidia's moves proved prescient. Although SpaceX stock has fallen slightly since the report date (down about 6%), Intel shares are down much, much more -- roughly 55%. So Nvidia kept a lot of its Intel profits by cashing out before Intel crashed (a crash that Nvidia's own CEO scandal helped cause).

The question for Intel investors now is: should you follow Nvidia's example and sell out of Intel, too? Before you do that, recall that if Nvidia sold 25% of its Intel shares in September, then it also kept 75%.

So Nvidia hasn't abandoned all hope in Intel yet. Maybe you shouldn't either?
