Nvidia stock is heading for its longest losing streak since 2008

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The stock was under pressure even before Jensen Huang's ouster as investors question whether demand for the company's AI chips can be sustained

Nvidia $NVDA stock is poised to finish its seventh consecutive week in the red. That would mark its longest slide since 2008.

The stock changed hands at about $147 on Thursday, a 2.6% drop for the day. The VanEck Semiconductor ETF, whose largest positions include Nvidia, lost 2.3% that day.

The decline has continued despite third-quarter revenue that rose 103% from a year earlier. The slide worsened once Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO on Nov. 12, with CFO Colette Kress taking over as interim chief executive that day. The stock was under pressure before then as investors questioned whether demand for artificial intelligence can be sustained.

Nvidia reported third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion on Tuesday, in its first earnings report since Huang resigned. Data Center revenue reached $107.6 billion, up 110% from a year earlier. For the fourth quarter, the company expects revenue of $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, with the outlook excluding any Data Center compute revenue from China. Nvidia stock climbed by up to 4% in after-hours trading following the report.

Huang resigned after admitting to an affair with a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer. U.S. intelligence officials suspect she is an agent of Beijing's spy agencies. The board has retained law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz for an independent probe into whether Huang shared corporate or national security secrets.

Nvidia has also hired Heidrick & Struggles to lead the search for a permanent CEO. Board Chair Harvey Jones said on Tuesday's earnings call that directors will weigh insiders and outsiders. Jones also said the board "does not intend to reinstate" Huang.

Huang said Tuesday that he would be open to returning if directors asked him to. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent pushed the board over the weekend to hire someone from outside the company. "They need a dramatic shakeup," Bessent said.
