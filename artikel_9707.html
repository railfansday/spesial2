One button sent people to the wrong page. We checked all 41, and 14 didn't go where they said.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

On Tuesday morning I opened the page a small clothing shop was about to get from us: a short video of one of their products, and under it a single button that said "Make one with your own character."

It was a product, not a character. And the button didn't open anything that makes a video. It opened the page where you build a new character from scratch.

This post is about that one button, the 40 others we checked because of it, and the rule we wrote so it stops happening.

TL;DR We audited all 41 calls to action on our site and in our emails. 14 didn't go where their words said. We fixed 7 the same day, 7 sit on pages we froze on purpose, and a test now fails the build if a button drifts.

I build RefVid, a small tool where you upload a few photos of a product or a character once, then make short videos that keep it looking the same in every shot. Every finished video gets a public share page, and that page has one job: let whoever watches it make their own.

The share page picked its words from a fallback. When a video wasn't made from a template, the page assumed it was about a character, wrote "Keep your own character the same in every shot", and pointed the button at the character builder (we call saved characters and products "elements").

So a shop owner watching a video of their own product would read "character" and land on a form asking them to create one. Nothing on that page says "video".

The obvious patch: read the subject's type. If the clip shows a product, say "Make one with your own product". That went in at 11:15 UTC.

Then I looked at where it actually led. With no template recorded, the new button opened our marketing page for product videos. The words were right now. The destination was a page that explains the thing instead of starting it.

That fix lasted 22 minutes. The second one, at 11:37, is what this post is really about, because by then the question had changed from "why is this button wrong?" to "how many buttons are wrong?"

We went through every page type and every email we send, and wrote down each call to action as a row: the file, the words, where it goes for a signed-in visitor, and where it should go.

41 rows. 27 were fine. 14 were not. These are the 7 we fixed that day:

The part that surprised me: the same action, "start a new video", was written four different ways in the code. One helper went to the builder, one component opened a photo picker first, one link went to the marketing page, and the email linked to the subject's own page. Every one of them made sense on the day it was written. Together they meant a visitor couldn't predict where "make one" would take them.

Not one label changed in the fix. The words were already right in all 7 languages we ship. Only the destinations moved.

We wrote it down as four rows, and every button on the site now has to be one of them:

And each row is built by exactly one function. "Start a new video" is one helper call; nothing else in the codebase is allowed to assemble that link by hand. If you want to see the "make one like this" row working, any card in our template gallery goes straight into a new video with that template filled.

The same morning a support email arrived. A user had signed up on their phone, added their own photo, and somehow never started a video. Our follow-up email said, more or less: the photo you added is already saved; open the link and press Generate.

The link carried their saved photo in the URL:

They opened it in their mail app, signed out. Our middleware redirected them to sign in and rebuilt the destination from the path only:

The subject was gone. After sign-in, the template page did what it does when you have no photo: it loaded the template's sample product, a perfume bottle. They pressed Generate, exactly as the email told them, and 124 of their 129 signup credits went into a sample perfume ad they never asked for.

We refunded the credits and added extra, and fixed the redirect so the intent keeps everything it was given:

In the 30 days before, 72 people got that exact "your photo is already saved" email. One of them hit this. That's a small number, and it is also every credit that person had.

It's the same mistake as the button, one layer down: the words promised a destination, and something between the click and the page threw part of it away.

A second test follows the email link as a signed-out visitor through the sign-in redirect and checks that the saved photo is still in the URL where sign-in lands.

7 of the 14 wrong buttons are still wrong, on purpose, for now.

They sit on pages we froze on 8 November: our home page, our pricing page and our cartoon series hub. Those are the pages an AI assistant had started sending visitors to, and we decided not to touch them at all while we watched whether that traffic held. We are now unfreezing pages one at a time, each change measured against its own before-numbers. These three haven't had their turn.

The guard test knows about the three that open the builder: their files sit on its allow-list, each marked "frozen page". When a page thaws, its line comes off the list and the test forces the fix; the other four are on the audit list waiting for the same day. Until then, if you land on our home page and press one of those, you'll see the old behaviour.

If you searched your own app today for every button that says "start", "try" or "make one", how many different places would they lead, and would you be able to say which one is right?
