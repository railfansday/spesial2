Matthijs de Ligt is Fast Becoming Man Utd's New Paul Pogba

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Mauricio Pochettino faces an uphill battle to reverse Manchester United's fortunes this season, and improving some of his players' performances seems like a lost cause at the moment.

The Red Devils were thumped 4-0 by rivals Liverpool at Anfield in their first game under their new boss. Leny Yoro's early red card put the away side in a perilous position, and goals from Dominik Szoboszlai, Florian Wirtz, Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz ultimately punished them.

The result leaves United in the Premier League's bottom three, just a single point above rock-bottom Crystal Palace. Things look precarious for the north-west side, and they desperately need some of their senior players to stand up in this period of adversity.

It's up to Pochettino to elicit performances from these more experienced stars, although the players themselves need to take some accountability.

Bruno Fernandes' petulance and misguided leadership techniques are derailing United, while Lisandro Martinez's recent outburst against the club's hierarchy shows he's not the man Pochettino can turn to.

Diogo Dalot is in horrendous form, Youri Tielemans' declining physical attributes are putting him in trouble, and Harry Maguire is at the eleventh hour of his career.

This leaves Matthijs de Ligt as one of the remaining senior players Pochettino might hope will step forward and lead this dysfunctional dressing room, but there are unfortunately huge question marks around the £195,000-a-week Dutchman as well.

When United signed De Ligt for around £43 million back in the summer of 2024, there was still considerable hope that he could live up to the potential he displayed in his early days at Ajax.

The Netherlands international was viewed as Europe's best centre-back prodigy when he broke through in Amsterdam, so much so that he won the Kopa Trophy in 2019. A respectable but uninspiring three-year stint at Juventus was followed by a slightly underwhelming spell at Bayern Munich.

United offered him the chance to revitalise his career two years ago, after it'd become clear that he had no future at the Allianz Arena. A promising debut season at Old Trafford suggested that there was still life in him, but a turbulent last 12 months have raised serious question marks.

Between November 30th 2025 and October 21st 2026, De Ligt didn't play a minute of football due to a back injury. He's returned to the fold over the last month, but only managed his first Premier League minutes back on Sunday at Anfield, and his dire performance was a reminder of why his career has stalled over the last three or four years.

The 27-year-old struggled to deal with the intensity of Liverpool's press, being caught out in possession multiple times, while his positional play defensively was all at sea.

While this was certainly a difficult environment for him to come back into, and Pochettino's decision to throw the defender into this game was questionable, it's still difficult to envisage a world where De Ligt re-establishes himself as a dependable option in this United squad.

In many ways, there are comparisons to be made between the former Ajax man and Paul Pogba. The Frenchman was deemed one of Europe's most promising young talents at some point, had a spell at Juventus and then was largely disappointing after making the switch to United.

Like De Ligt, Pogba was hindered by injury issues, although it's undeniable that neither player displayed the consistency their elite talent as a youngster suggested they should've.

The midfielder was scrutinised far more than his defensive counterpart, partly because United forked out significantly more money to bring him to Manchester. However, Pogba arguably delivered much more for the Red Devils than De Ligt has at any point, contributing to a couple of relatively encouraging seasons where the north-west outfit secured top-two finishes.

The Argentine quickly learned the mess he's tasked with cleaning up.

Ultimately, however, the parallels are there. Teenage sensations who moved to United to fulfil their potential, but failed to do so, with injuries playing their part in their failure.

De Ligt can still resurrect his career at Old Trafford, but it looks increasingly unlikely. It wouldn't be a surprise to see him plying his trade at a club of Monaco's stature when he enters his early 30s, just like Pogba.
