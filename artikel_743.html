Governors Awards: Glenn Close, Ridley Scott and Floyd Norman Accept Honorary Oscars, Christine Vachon and Pamela Koffler Receive Thalberg Award

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

"I'm flattered and proud to accept this award, but I also accept it for Tony," Ridley said of his late brother. "His great work would certainly have earned this recognition."

By Scott Feinberg, Brande Victorian November 15, 2026 8:57pm

Five giants of the global film community were feted on Sunday night at the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences’ 17th annual Governors Awards: actress Glenn Close, animator Floyd Norman and — in absentia due to a recent medical procedure — director/producer Ridley Scott with bestowed honorary Oscars, and longtime indie producing partners Christine Vachon and Pamela Koffler with the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award, which recognizes “creative producers whose bodies of work reflect a consistently high quality of motion picture production.”

The black-tie gathering, which took place inside the Ray Dolby Ballroom at Ovation Hollywood, was, as always, packed with Academy members — including most of the 55 members of the organization’s board of governors, who host the event — and dozens of current Oscar contenders. Among them: You Can See Everything’s Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, The Odyssey’s Christopher Nolan, Zendaya, Tom Holland and Charlize Theron, The Debut’s Julianne Moore, Paul Giamatti and Emma Stone, Obsession’s Curry Barker and Inde Navarrette, Elsinore’s Andrew Scott, The Invite’s Olivia Wilde and Penélope Cruz, Wild Horse Nine’s John Malkovich, Artificial’s Andrew Garfield and Monica Barbaro, and Dune: Part Three’s Timothée Chalamet, accompanied by Kylie Jenner. Seated with Scott were his family, along with Lucy Fisher, Doug Wick, Jim Gianopulos, Tom Rothman, Richard Lovett, Simon Halls, Steve Asbell, Matt Damon and Jerry Bruckheimer, whileNorman was notably joined by Marlon West and Travon Free. Close’s table consisted of Malkovich, Kevin Huvane, Franklin Latt, Rodrigo Garcia and Ram Bergman. Vachon and Koffler were accompanied by John Sloss, Wash Westmoreland, Marcus Hu and Liz Karlsen.

The first awardee was Norman, 91, who began his career in 1956 at Walt Disney Animation Studios as the company’s first Black animator. He contributed to numerous Disney classics including Sleeping Beauty, The Sword in the Stone, Mary Poppins, The Jungle Book and Robin Hood, as well as other notable films made outside of the Mouse House including The Hunchback of Notre Dame, Mulan, Monsters, Inc. and Toy Story 2. A friend and mentor to younger animators and storyboard artists, he was the subject of a 2016 documentary feature, Floyd Norman: An Animated Life.

The presentation of the honorary Oscar to Norman — only the fourth animator to ever receive that accolade, after Walter Lantz, Chuck Jones and Hayao Miyazaki — was led by Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother star Mahershala Ali, who stated, “Floyd has played a pivotal role in our lives without any of us even realizing it, one [animation] cel at a time.”

In his acceptance speech, Norman reflected on his beginnings in the industry, offering “a special thank you to the best mentor a filmmaker could ever have, Walt Disney.” He concluded his speech remarking, “On occasion, even movies have a happy ending. This special evening? Here’s my happy ending.”

The evening’s next honoree was Close, one of the great stars and character actresses of the past 50 years. The 79-year-old has received eight Oscar nominations, four for leading performances (1987’s Fatal Attraction, 1988’s Dangerous Liaisons, 2011’s Albert Nobbs and 2018’s The Wife) and four for supporting turns (1982’s The World According to Garp, 1983’s The Big Chill, 1984’s The Natural and 2020’s Hillbilly Elegy). She could just as easily have been nominated for 1985’s Jagged Edge and Maxie, 1990’s Reversal of Fortune, 1994’s The Paper, 1996’s 101 Dalmatians and 1997’s Air Force One.

Close’s tally of eight noms for performances is higher than that of all but three other actresses, dead or alive — Meryl Streep (21), Katharine Hepburn (12) and Bette Davis (11), each of whom won multiple times — and more than any other living man or woman (Peter O’Toole, who died in 2013, also went 0-for-eight and received an honorary Oscar).

The presentation of the honorary Oscar to Close — who previously presented honorary Oscars to the National Endowment for the Arts in 1985, Deborah Kerr in 1994 and Carol Littleton in 2023 — was kicked off by John Malkovich, who described her as “my friend and treasured colleague.” Close’s Dangerous Liaisons co-star then went on to state, “She is a true master… she’s got the goods, she can do the big stuff, the little stuff, and everything in between… Glenn is unafraid to ‘go there,’ and I love that about her,” Malkovich added. “She loves the world, she is relentless, very smart, a little cagey and a great actress who knows exactly what she’s doing and gives it 100 percent every single time.”

Following a lengthy standing ovation, Close, who dedicated the award to her daughter, Annie, declared: “As an outsider, I have never worried about whether I was going to win or not, though some years I thought I might have a chance. I have always felt that the great honor is to be in the room. Over the past 43 years, I’ve been in the room eight times… so to be in this room tonight, embraced by the people who have consistently inspired me, whose work has sustained and challenged me and only made me want to be better is the greatest honor of all.”

Next up was Scott, whose films, collectively, have been nominated for 45 Oscars and won nine, but who himself has never won a competitive Oscar. The 88-year-old, who was nearly 40 when he made his directorial debut, has made up — and continues to make up — for lost time, consistently pumping out action-packed blockbusters, many now considered landmarks of cinematic achievement.

Indeed, he is behind two of the most significant sci-fi films of all time, 1979’s Alien and 1982’s Blade Runner, as well as 1991’s Thelma & Louise (for which he received a best director Oscar nom), 2000’s Gladiator (which won the best picture Oscar, while he lost best director), 2001’s Black Hawk Down (for which he received his most recent best director Oscar nom) and 2015’s The Martian. He has also produced many of his films, as well as a ton of TV series.

The presentation of the honorary Oscar to Scott was led by Matt Damon, who described him as “a master at the helm” of two films they’ve made together, The Martian and The Last Duel, stating, “Thank you to the Academy on behalf of everyone who has ever worked with Ridley Scott. It is about time we all saw him with an Academy Award in his hand.” Scott accepted his Oscar via a pre-recorded video from London, which included a clip from his first movie, Boy in a Bicycle, featuring his late brother, Tony, in which the narrator states, “I’ll remember this moment when I’m 80, and it’ll all seem like yesterday.”

“That was a moment for both of us,” Scott stated. “Now I’m well past 80, and I’ve thought about those days and moments. It does resonate just like yesterday. Both of us sharing our first film together that led to 20 years of advertising, five decades of filmmaking… I’m flattered and proud to accept this award, but I also accept it for Tony. His great work would certainly have earned this recognition.”

The final awardees of the night were Vachon, 63, and Koffler, 60, two of the most important producers in the history of American independent film. The co-founders of New York-based Killer Films have guided to fruition all 10 of Todd Haynes’ features — including Poison (1991), Safe (1995), Far from Heaven (2002) and Carol (2015) — plus Larry Clark’s Kids (1995), Todd Solondz’s Happiness (1998), Kimberly Peirce’s Boys Don’t Cry (1999), John Cameron Mitchell’s Hedwig and the Angry Inch (2001), Wash Westmoreland and Richard Glatzer’s Still Alice (2014) and Celine Song’s Past Lives (2023, for which they received their sole Oscar noms).

Vachon, the more public-facing of the duo, has been nicknamed “the Queen of New Queer Cinema” and is such a fixture of the indie scene that she was affectionately referenced alongside Spike Lee, Michael Moore and Richard Linklater in the title of the classic 1996 tome Spike, Mike, Slackers, & Dykes: A Guided Tour Across a Decade of American Independent Cinema. Her own 2006 book A Killer Life, which she co-wrote with Austin Bunn, was included on The Hollywood Reporter‘s 2023 list of the 100 greatest film books of all time.

The presentation of the Thalberg to Vachon and Koffler was kicked off by Neil Patrick Harris with a performance from Hedwig and the Angry Inch, which Vachon and Koffler produced. Todd Haynes and Julianne Moore then conducted the actual handoff of the statuettes, with Haynes saying, “From the outside, they couldn’t see more different, and of course they are. But the ways they trade and riff off each other, divide and share their unique abilities. It’s a shimmering example of how the world might actually be if it were run by adults, otherwise known as women.”

Added Moore, “These women have never been driven by profit and even recently admitted to me that no matter how hard they tried to be commercial, they’ve had to make peace of the fact that they’re just not. That doesn’t mean that they haven’t had commercial success. They are just not driven by it. They are interested in radical voices, queer voices, new voices, personal points of view.”

Vachon, in her acceptance speech, dedicated the pair’s honor to their “fellow hardworking producers,” stating, as she recalled a recent interaction, “A producer came up to me in New York and said, ‘I know this is really your Oscar, but it kind of feels like all of ours,’ And it is.”
