Celtics mailbag: Worried about Robinson? White's 3-PT shooting and more

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Celtics mailbag: Worried about Robinson? White's 3-PT shooting and more originally appeared on NBC Sports Boston

When we ripped open the Celtics Mailbag for the first time last week, optimism was high. Boston had just swept a four-game road trip and owned the best record in the Eastern Conference. The questions centered largely on whether Boston was a legitimate title contender after a fast start to the 2026-27 campaign.

The first losing streak of the new season has a way of reeling things in a bit. It's not exactly time to break out the panic meters after stumbles against Orlando and Denver, but let's just say there are a few lingering areas of concern.

Let's start with one concern that was there even when the Celtics were winning as we tackle a worry-themed collection of questions in this week's bag:

How worried should we be that Mitchell Robinson still hasn't turned a corner in Boston?

BOSTON, MASSACHUSETTS – OCTOBER 23: Mitchell Robinson #4 of the Boston Celtics celebrates after blocking a shot from Og Anunoby #8 of the New York Knicks during the Celtics Home Opener at TD Garden on October 23, 2026 in Boston, Massachusetts. The Celtics defeat the Knicks 107-103.

By now you've surely seen the stat. The Celtics have outscored opponents by 157 points this season, but Robinson is a team-worst minus-37 in his 246 minutes on the floor through 15 games. Plus/Minus can be a flukey stat but, somehow, the eye test suggests it might actually be even worse than the numbers indicate.

What's wild is that Robinson has spent more than 70 percent of his floor time playing alongside Jayson Tatum and yet the team has been outscored by 8.5 points per 100 possessions in those 174 minutes. Boston owns a +20 net rating in Tatum's 303 minutes without Robinson.

Some growing pains could be expected. The Celtics demand a lot of their big men and it can take time for players to settle into the system. Robinson has admitted at times that his conditioning is not where it needs to be, which is slightly frustrating given Boston's hefty offseason investment and the fact that a full month of the regular season is already in the rearview.

Robinson put together one of his more encouraging halves of the season in Denver on Wednesday night — and he even made consecutive free throws — but then produced a less-enthralling five-minute stretch in the third quarter as the Celtics came unglued. Boston leaned heavily on 19-year-old rookie Chris Cenac Jr. in the fourth frame instead of going back to Robinson.

We didn't like Robinson's energy in the third quarter, whether it was being unprepared for the ball when Tatum got blitzed, or glued to the ground on available rebounds on the defensive end.

It's much too early to make any wholesale declarations about the Robinson signing but undeniably both the team and player need to evaluate the reasons for his slow start. Do the Celtics tone down how much screening they ask him to do on the offensive end and keep him closer to the rim, where his offensive rebounding can tilt possessions? Does Robinson simply need to amp up his energy level and embrace all that the Celtics are tossing on his plate?

The fact that Neemias Queta has produced some of the best on/off splits in the NBA has made it easier to let Robinson ride the rollercoaster. Luka Garza struggled out of the gates last season but eventually settled into his role. Alas, Garza leaned into his natural motor and Robinson needs to crank up his own.

Robinson does feel vital to Boston's ceiling. The Celtics need Robinson to be a defensive anchor against the league's best bigs. They need his typical offensive rebounding rates to dominate the margins on the offensive end.

The Celtics don't have a lot of concerns despite their recent stumbles and getting Robinson activated feels like priority No. 1.

How worried should we be about Derrick White's jumper?

Feb 4, 2026; Houston, Texas, USA; Boston Celtics guard Derrick White (9) shoots the ball as Houston Rockets guard Amen Thompson (1) defends during the second quarter at Toyota Center. Mandatory Credit: Troy Taormina-Imagn Images

Even as White's shot has defied him for a season-plus now, we still expect him to make everything he hoists in the fourth quarter. So it was jarring that, back in his native Colorado and with his parents watching behind the Boston bench, White misfired on his last five shots against Denver. We were *certain* his 3-point look from the right wing with 27 seconds to play would drop.

It didn't. White is now shooting 30.6 percent beyond the 3-point arc through 15 games, even lower than last season's output (32.7) and well below his career mark (35.9).

White has played 99 games since the start of the 2025-26 season and has made 250 of the 780 3-point attempts — regular season and postseason combined — that he's hoisted in that 13-month span. He's reeled in his attempts from deep this year, dropping down to 5.7 per game this season after 8.3 per game last year.

The concerning part: White is getting better looks this year. Last season, his expected 3-point percentage was 35.3 percent, so he finished about 2.6 percent below expected overall. This season, NBA tracking has him at 36.3 expected on all 3-point attempts — right around league average, and his career mark — but he's nearly 6 percent below expected on tracked attempts.

The positive spin: White continues to impact winning. The Celtics have a +12.9 net rating in his 448 minutes on the court. Boston is 7-0 when White registers multiple STOCKS (steals and blocks). White is averaging 13.6 potential assists per 100 possessions, ranking in the 94th percentile for his position. His 1.2 offensive fouls drawn per 100 possessions ranks in the 96th percentile. He's still doing a lot of the things that make him a winning player.

Getting those shooting numbers up is likewise vital to Boston's overall ceiling.

How worried should we be that Denver delivered a blueprint for slowing down Tatum and the Celtics offense?

Oct 30, 2026; Boston, Massachusetts, USA; Boston Celtics head coach Joe Mazzulla talks with forward Jayson Tatum (0) from the sideline as they take on the Chicago Bulls at TD Garden. Mandatory Credit: David Butler II-Imagn Images

Tatum and the Celtics certainly didn't respond well when the Nuggets got blitz-happy in the second half. Tatum did the right thing trying to move the basketball when an extra body shuffled his way but it also led to a series of turnovers that allowed Denver to erase its 17-point deficit.

Tatum's teammates didn't aid the cause, though. Tatum finished with a team-high 16 potential assists but that translated to a mere three actual assists and six assist points overall. By comparison, White's 14 potential assists translated to eight actual assists and 20 assist points. Boston players need to shoot better when Tatum is creating open looks with the attention he draws. We'll have our eyes on how Boston responds if Sacramento starts sending blitzers on Saturday night.

Foul trouble didn't help matters, either, for Boston on Wednesday with Paul George playing just 11 minutes, 21 seconds in the second half. Tatum and White were a combined 3-of-14 shooting after the intermission. Boston was 2-of-19 (10.5 percent) on all second-half 3s.

The 11 second-half turnovers was still the most jarring number. Boston can't get sloppy when teams blitz. Tatum's potential assists number suggests he still often made the right play, the Celtics simply didn't make the opponent pay. And they compounded matters with the giveaways.

How worried should we be that opposing defenses will help off Jordan Walsh when he's running with the starting group?

The White-Walsh-George-Tatum combo is Boston's most frequent 4-man combo (113 minutes) this season and owns a +20 net rating in their floor time. That's encouraging. Still, it's clear that opponents will cheat away from Walsh and then challenge him to shoot if it means getting the ball out of the hands of Tatum or George. He's made strides with his offensive confidence but the offense grinds a bit when he's hesitant.

Walsh did not play in the second half in Denver. The Celtics have no shortage of options with that fifth spot. We've only seen 14 minutes with the core starters (White, George, Tatum, Queta) alongside Sam Hauser, but that group has a 150 offensive rating and +64.8 net rating overall. We'd probably like to see how Hauser holds up, defensively, in longer stretches with that group. Size wise, it's a super intriguing mix, though speedy guards could stress that 5-man pairing.

We've also only seen five minutes of Baylor Scheierman with that core group. Boston's offensive rating dips to 109.5 in the 22 minutes that Hugo Gonzalez has played with the White-George-Tatum-Queta combo.

How worried should the rest of the league be about Chris Cenac Jr. being this impactful as a 19-year-old rookie?

We'll continue to scream it: The biggest silver lining in the two recent losses was getting to throw Cenac Jr. into the fourth-quarter fire against both Orlando and Denver, and for him to hold up well in both situations.

The Celtics can trot out a bunch of double-big combos with Cenac Jr. able to pair with any of the other bigs on the roster. Cenac Jr. uses his length, even against shifty guards like Jamal Murray, and doesn't look overwhelmed against switching. Opponents are shooting 38.1 percent against him (8-of-21 overall), or seven percent below expected output. Those numbers plummet to 28.6 percent shooting inside six feet, or 34.5 percent below expected, albeit on a small sample (2-of-7 overall).

If Robinson finds his footing, we suspect Cenac Jr. will still see much of his development up north with Maine in the G-League. Reps are important at his age. But what a luxury to be able to throw him into NBA crunch-time situations as a teenager and have him look confident. His length and athleticism can be game-changers for this team.
