Throw a Kids’ Birthday Party at Home – Invite Fewer Guests and Skip Goody Bags

Host your child’s birthday party at home or in a park instead of booking a trampoline park or play place. Keep the guest list to your child’s age plus one, serve pizza and a homemade or grocery-store cake, and trade rented entertainment and goody bags for a game the kids play together.

The Sky Zone trampoline park in Evanston, Illinois, prices its Epic party package at $380 for 10 jumpers and $38 for each child past 10, with two large pizzas and drinks but no cake.

That makes the guest list the first thing to cut, since every child you add also means more pizza and another favor. Cap the guests at your child’s age plus one, so a 5-year-old invites six. If your child’s school bans party invitations in class unless every classmate gets one, text the other parents instead.

When the grown-ups on the list outnumber the kids, plan it as a big party on a small budget instead.

Then choose the cheaper version of each part of the party:

Say your son turns 8 and invites nine friends, his age plus one, to the backyard. Two large pizzas ($30), a homemade cake ($15), drinks ($12), paper goods ($8), and dollar-store prizes for a scavenger hunt ($25) come to $90 before tax. The same 10 kids in Sky Zone’s Epic package would cost $380 before tax, plus the same $15 cake. At $90, the backyard party saves $305 of that $395, and it gives up an hour on the trampolines and a host who sets up and cleans up.

Skip the goody bags. Hide one small prize per child in the scavenger hunt, and let each guest keep what they find.
