Are Bank Bonuses Worth It? Subtract Tax, Fees & Lost Interest First

Most bank bonuses are worth taking, but you keep only what’s left after three costs. Before you open the account, subtract the tax on the bonus, the interest the required deposit would have earned in a high-yield account, and any monthly fee you can’t waive. Skip any offer you’d need your emergency fund to meet.

Cash earns little at a typical bank. The FDIC puts the national average savings rate at 0.39%, which works out to about $38 a year on $10,000.

Chase and KeyBank both say in their offer terms that they report the bonus on Form 1099-INT, so it’s taxed as interest. Keeping a required balance in the new account also costs you what that money could have earned in a high-yield savings account, and any monthly fee you can’t waive comes out of the bonus.

Say a bank pays $300 for keeping $15,000 in a new account for 90 days, the account pays no interest, and you’re in the 22% federal tax bracket. In a high-yield account, the same money could earn 4% a year. After federal tax, the bonus leaves you $234. The $15,000 would have earned about $146 over those 90 days, or $114 after the same tax, so the bonus puts you $120 ahead. Three months of a $15 monthly fee you couldn’t waive would cut that to $75.

A direct deposit bonus costs little in lost interest, since your paycheck passes through the account on its way to your bills. Chase and KeyBank also limit how often you can collect. Chase pays one new checking bonus every two years, and KeyBank’s offer leaves out anyone who was the primary owner of one of its personal checking accounts in the past 12 months. Each pays only into an account that’s still open, so a fee you can’t waive keeps running until the bonus is paid. Then count the time spent moving deposits, the paperwork, and one more account to close later.

Skip a bank bonus whose required balance would come out of your emergency fund or your investments, or one whose fee waiver needs a balance you can’t keep.

A bonus paid for direct deposits is worth close to its after-tax amount, while one paid for a parked balance has to beat what that balance would earn in a high-yield account.

Test each of the best bank promotions and bonus offers the same way before you open one.
