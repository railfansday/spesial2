Japan’s Nikkei and South korea's KOSPI gain 1%, after Nvidia reports strong third-quarter revenue growth.

Hong Kong’s Hang Seng falls 1.12% as its technology index hits four-month low.

U.S. restrictions on advanced AI chips add pressure to China’s technology stocks.

Nvidia’s record earnings gave Asia’s chip stocks another boost, but the rally did not reach every market. Japan’s Nikkei and South Korea’s KOSPI index posted strong gains.

In contrast, Hong Kong’s Hang Seng index fell nearly 2 to hit a four-month low, as China tech stocks faced pressure from U.S. chip restrictions.

Japan’s benchmark Nikkei 225 index has climbed around 1% to trade over 59,334.28. The rally came after Nvidia reported third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier.

SoftBank Group and Tokyo Electron each gained around 3%, while Trend Micro jumped 6%.

Meanwhile, South Korea’s benchmark KOSPI index also gained around 0.79% to 5,079.54. Chipmakers led the rise, with SK Hynix up 4.19% and Samsung Electronics gaining 2.31%.

Nvidia added to the positive mood with its fourth-quarter revenue forecast of around $130 billion, giving semiconductor investors another reason to buy chip stocks.

Hong Kong is showing the other side of the AI trade. The Hang Seng Index fell 1.12% to 22,816.43, while the Hang Seng Tech Index dropped 2.5% to a four-month low.

Big tech stocks also came under heavy selling. Alibaba fell 2.63%, Tencent dropped 6.5%, Baidu declined 2.7%, while AI company MiniMax fell around 10%.

The pressure has grown after Washington expanded restrictions on advanced AI chip exports and remote access to high-end computing servers and overseas data centers.

Investors are also watching a meeting between U.S. President Donald Trump and Xi Jinping at APEC in Shenzhen. Technology and trade are expected to be key topics, especially as Shenzhen remains one of China’s major technology hubs.

Ahead of his meeting with Xi on November 19, Trump told APEC leaders that the U.S. opposes “any form of robust regulation” that could control or limit the development of superintelligence.

Beyond technology and trade, Trump also wants to discuss the wars in Ukraine and Iran. He could seek China’s help in pushing Russia toward peace.
