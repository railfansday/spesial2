There has been a buzz around Luca Williams-Barnett and in the 2-0 win against Ipswich, Tottenham fans could see why, writes MATT BARLOW

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Published: 12:22 EST, 21 November 2026 | Updated: 13:32 EST, 21 November 2026

Luca Williams-Barnett will score better goals. That was the consensus on a day when the Tottenham starlet marked his first Premier League by scoring the first and creating the second.

First his goal, driving in at the back post, full of youthful determination and the desire to get across his marker and be first to the ball, a delicious teasing cross delivered by Pedro Porro.

Williams-Barnett slid and stretched past Abdoul Ouattara with his right foot as the ball skipped and hit him high on the left thigh and rebounded towards the bottom corner of the Ipswich net.

The Spurs crowd erupted. They have been waiting to hail another of their own and maybe this is the one, with a goal on his first start like Harry Kane. And at 18 years and 51 days, the club’s youngest Premier League scorer since Gareth Bale.

There has been a buzz around Williams-Barnett as he emerged through the ranks.

Roberto De Zerbi rates him as ‘one of the best’ young players he has worked with acknowledging both his talent and attitude, with a dash of arrogance on display in pre-season when he dinked in a Panenka-style penalty in a friendly shootout against Sydney in Australia.

De Zerbi threw him into the role wide on the left which no player has made their own since Heung-min Son left and Williams-Barnett made his pitch for more opportunities.

Not only with his goal, but with the threat he carried and the buzz that rippled around the stadium when he picked up the ball.

He went close to a second before half time, first with a sharp chance miskicked in front of goal and then a header turned over by Christian Walton.

His impact was clear though when he collected the ball on halfway early in the second half.

With acres of space ahead the home fans roared him forward and he obliged, at first a cautious trot then a gallop as he drove into the Ipswich penalty box without meeting a challenge.

Then, as Darnell Furlong tried to hold him up, he made a yard on the outside and whipped over a cross with his left foot, which fizzed past Walton at the near post and was turned into an empty net from close range by Dominic Solanke.

It was Solanke’s second of the Premier League campaign and his first in the competition at home since March, and yet the moment still belonged to the teenager who booted the ball high into the crowd in celebration and was shown a yellow card by killjoy referee Rob Jones.

Williams-Barnett left the stage to a standing ovation when he was replaced by Savio in the 64th minute.

Spurs led 2-0 and the points were coasting by this stage on a comfortable return to the Premier League.

De Zerbi, in the stands serving a touchline ban, made changes to accommodate those returning from international duty. Sandro Tonali was absent after a couple of days feeling sick.

The home side were solid at the back and bristled with threat up front and peppered the Ipswich goal with 18 shots. Mohamed Kudus struck a post from a first-half free-kick, won by Williams-Barnett.

Walton made an excellent save to deny James Maddison in first half stoppage time and another to prevent Dara O’Shea turning into his own goal to make it 3-0.

A Savio cross hit a post late on with a deflected cross before he was struck down by an injury in a disjointed closing period. Ipswich defender Leif Davis was also off with an injury.
