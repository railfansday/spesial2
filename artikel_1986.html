Newcastle United could soon face a blow to their revenue.

It has been a difficult opening few months to life in the Premier League for Matthias Jaissle.

The German came in to replace Eddie Howe as the Englishman walked out after a failure to secure top transfer targets.

Things did start well with five unbeaten in all competitions, but results and performances have soon unravelled.

The Magpies are winless in three, with their only win in the last six games coming against a struggling Crystal Palace side.

Jaissle is facing problems of his own, but they could soon be felt in the pockets of the Newcastle owners.

After years of building towards the top of the Premier League under Howe, frustration and toxicity is beginning to settle in at St James' Park.

There has been plenty of annoyance aimed towards key individuals, with players like Yoane Wissa and Jacob Murphy facing the brunt of that.

It isn't truly a major change from last campaign as the Toon Army watched a 12th place finish last time around.

Finishing just 10 points clear of the relegation zone wasn't the greatest look.

It ended with the Magpies picking up £154.5million in domestic prize money, which may seem positive in itself, but does mark a £13.7m drop off compared to Sunderland.

Everyone associated with St James' Park will want that figure to be increased, but under Jaissle, that might not be possible.

The Magpies currently sit 12th, just as they finished last season, but it feels as if a drop down the league could be possible in the very near future.

That won't reflect well on the Newcastle boss, nor will it help the financial issues being felt in the north east.

PSR continues to frustrate, but should prize money drop further, the strain will have a further impact.

Consider that Tottenham finished 17th last season and secured £135.8m in prize money, they made £18.7m less than the Toon.

Should Jaissle drag his side towards that area of the table, it will hit Yasir Al-Rumayyan and Co. in the pocket.
