Fantasy Football Trade Tips: Why it might be time to buy into the Broncos passing game

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Creating realistic trade offers that lead to a deal being accepted is always challenging. Throughout the season, Justin Boone will be publishing his rest-of-season trade value charts, which you can use as a guide to help make moves and manage your roster.

Below he highlights some of the players you should be buying or selling, before their value shifts in the coming weeks.

Note: The default Yahoo Fantasy trade deadline is Nov. 28.

Prior to Chicago's Week 10 bye week, Swift had his worst fantasy outing of the season with just 31 scoreless scrimmage yards against a tough Buccaneers' front. Now, with Kyle Monangai's return looming, the Swift managers might be concerned about his rest-of-season ceiling.

Hopefully, that doubt opens the door enough for you to acquire him this week. After all, Swift is the RB8 in fantasy points per game this season and posted at least 70 scrimmage yards in every outing before that down game against the Bucs. That includes four contests where he shared the backfield with Monangai.

Not that Ben Johnson's offense needs the help, but the Bears have one of the 10 easiest fantasy schedules for running backs the rest of the way — starting with a Saints defense this week that's allowing the second most fantasy points to the position.

Swift will also get favorable draws against the Dolphins (Week 14), Bills (Week 15) and Packers (Week 16), giving him a nice runaway for the beginning of the fantasy playoffs. The veteran will continue to be a top-10 fantasy RB even when Monangai gets back in the lineup.

Warren is another talented ball carrier who's fresh off his worst fantasy performance of the season, with just 41 scoreless scrimmage yards versus the Bengals. It was a game where the Steelers' offense was unable to find success in any form, which is even more disappointing when you consider they were coming off a bye week.

Prior to the bye, Warren had averaged 141 yards over his previous six outings. Even if you only focus on the games where Rico Dowdle was active, Warren still averaged 119 yards in those three contests. Needless to say, he's going to be fine.

You should expect a bounce-back effort against an Eagles defense that hasn't been as strong as in past years. Philly has allowed double-digit fantasy points to five of the last six starting RBs they've faced, including at least 95 scrimmage yards to four of them. That was also before they traded away Jordan Davis at the deadline.

If you're trying to gauge his value, Warren is a high-end fantasy RB2 with obvious RB1 upside moving forward. His schedule in the fantasy playoffs is also enticing with the Ravens, Panthers and Titans.

In an effort to replace Drake Maye in multiple leagues, I've had to get creative with trades and pickups. The player who's led me to the most has been Nix.

It's understandable why managers would be disappointed in his QB22 fantasy production this season, after he finished as the QB8 and QB10 in his first two NFL campaigns.

However, things are pointing in the right direction. Prior to the Broncos' Week 10 bye, Nix was a top-12 fantasy QB in three straight games.

Nix will also benefit from a softer schedule. Denver dealt with the most difficult collection of opponents in the first six weeks, but from Week 7 on they have one of the 10 easiest slates. That includes the Raiders (fourth most fantasy points to QBs) twice, both this week and in the first round of the fantasy playoffs.

If you're looking for a discount fantasy QB1 the rest of the way, Nix is the quarterback to target.

While wrist and chest injuries prevented Hockenson from suiting up in Kirk Cousins' return, the arrival of a familiar veteran presence under center raises the fantasy potential for everyone in Minnesota.

Before getting hurt, Hockenson was heating up with double-digit fantasy points in three of his previous four outings. He also has one of the five easiest fantasy schedules the rest of the season, which is highlighted by matchups with the Lions and Commanders in the first two weeks of the fantasy playoffs. Detroit and Washington are first and second in terms of most points allowed to tight ends.

We'll need to monitor Hockenson's practice participation to see if he'll be available this week, but with Jordan Addison missing time due to a thumb injury, there is a massive opportunity to be the No. 2 pass-catcher behind Justin Jefferson.

After capitalizing on a friendly schedule in the opening month, the Panthers have fallen back to earth in recent weeks. Though McMillan's stat lines haven't been as bad as his teammate Jalen Coker, Carolina's No. 1 target has failed to reach 60 yards in any of his past four outings.

McMillan has also gone from being the ninth most targeted wideout (9.6) in the first six weeks to 20th (7.5) in that category over the last month.

To make matters worse, the Panthers have the most difficult fantasy schedule for receivers from Week 11 to Week 17. There isn't a single plus-matchup remaining on McMillan's calendar. While he can still return top-24 fantasy WR numbers, I'd at least float some offers out there to see if someone in your league values him higher.

Samuel has been an overachiever this season, maximizing his fantasy stats with the help of touchdowns and big plays. Expecting the 30-year-old playmaker (turns 31 in January) to maintain this level of production is extremely risky.

The veteran has put up at least 15 fantasy points in two-thirds of his appearances, despite being held to four targets or less on four different occasions.

The 49ers' receiver room is also about to get a lot more crowded after they just traded for Keenan Allen and will be getting Mike Evans back from injury soon. Given Samuel's narrow path to production, more target competition is concerning.

While Samuel is a decent boom-or-bust flex option, there will be more down weeks in his future as the schedule tightens up against the Vikings, Seahawks, Rams and Chiefs in four of his final six games. Take advantage of his WR5 overall finish in Week 10 and find a more dependable starter.

Goff is QB13 on the season, averaging 19.1 fppg. However, that's heavily influenced by his first six weeks when he was the second-highest scoring passer with 23 ffpg. Over the last month, Goff was the QB21, averaging just 14.2 fppg.

That doesn't project to be a death sentence for his fantasy value, especially with games against the Falcons (Week 13) and Titans (Week 14). Unfortunately, he gets a tough draw this week with the Buccaneers and the most difficult matchup possible in the first week of the fantasy playoffs on the road against the Vikings.

Staring down a battle with Brian Flores' defense in Week 15 is worrisome, so this is the time to move Goff after his 303-yard, three-touchdown game.

The injury to Drake Maye is expected to sideline the star quarterback for a long time and possibly the rest of the season.

That puts Tommy DeVito in position to start when they return from their Week 11 bye. DeVito is one of the worst backups in the league and this offense will grind to a halt for however long he's under center. Even if the Patriots sign a free agent like Jimmy Garoppolo, the outlook isn't much better. New England has one of the toughest schedules remaining, including dates with the Vikings, Chiefs and Broncos in the final month.

Fantasy managers who have any Patriots starters like A.J. Brown, Rhamondre Stevenson, Demario Douglas or Hunter Henry should be selling for whatever they can get. Take 50 cents on the dollar. Throw them in as additional pieces in bigger trade offers. Do whatever you can to get off this ride immediately, because the next step is outright dropping them.
