Chiefs’ Andy Reid defends KC special teams despite epic fail in Bills loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Kansas City Chiefs did almost everything needed to beat the Buffalo Bills on Thanksgiving night, except execute on special teams. Despite a string of costly kicking and punting blunders in a 13-10 loss at Highmark Stadium, head coach Andy Reid refused to throw special teams coordinator Dave Toub or kicker Harrison Butker under the bus.

Speaking with reporters after the game, Reid acknowledged the unit's struggles while shutting down any talk of a roster shakeup, according to ESPN Chiefs writer Nate Taylor on X.

"Overall, we've got to do better. I'm not going to make any decisions (on Harrison Butker)," Reid said.

That loyalty will test fans' patience after special teams squandered a dominant defensive effort. Kansas City outgained Buffalo 241-230, averaged 4.8 yards per play to the Bills' 3.5 and won the turnover battle 2-0.

The Chiefs' defense held Buffalo quarterback Josh Allen to 10 of 21 passing for 81 yards and two interceptions, yet Kansas City still walked away 8-3.

Missteps in the kicking game haunted the Chiefs all night in the rain. Butker converted a 26-yard field goal in the second quarter, then pushed a go-ahead 46-yard attempt wide right with under six minutes left, marking his eighth missed field goal this season. Punter Matt Araiza also endured a rough evening across seven punts for 291 yards, including a bobbled snap and a late kick that Bills linebacker Joe Andreessen tipped. Wide receiver Greg Dortch returned that deflected punt 17 yards to the Kansas City 39-yard line, setting up Bills kicker Tyler Bass for a walk-off 57-yard field goal.

Those miscues wasted strong nights from running back Emmett Johnson, who rushed 17 times for 72 yards and a 1-yard touchdown, and tight end Travis Kelce, who caught four passes for 71 yards from quarterback Patrick Mahomes (16 of 29, 174 yards). If the Chiefs want another deep playoff run, Toub's group must clean things up fast.
