Noah Okafor piles on pain for Alonso as rampant Leeds demolish dismal Chelsea

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Xabi Alonso is straying towards crisis territory. Here’s a mind-bending statistic: Chelsea are eighth but have now lost more league games than Manchester United. They were bullied by a rampant Leeds, who fully merited their first win at Stamford Bridge since returning to the top of the table with a 2-0 victory in December 1999, and can look forward to another season with no European football if they do not sharpen up soon.

It is not really worth dwelling on the fact that the second Leeds goal, a Dominic Calvert-Lewin penalty early in a one-sided second half, came after Morgan Rogers was harshly penalised for handball. That was a minor detail given the wider picture. Leeds, superbly coached by Daniel Farke and up to sixth after two clinical strikes from Noah Okafor, Calvert-Lewin’s sixth goal of the season and a late effort from Brenden Aaronson, were so dominant that it was easy to conclude they would have won even without a helping hand from the officials.

Farke’s side were tactically superior, better coached and far more determined than their flimsy opposition. Chelsea, who briefly led through Emmanuel Emegha, were comprehensively outplayed and could not complain about being jeered off at the end of their fifth defeat in 11 games.

These are worrying times for Alonso. Chelsea lost Moisés Caicedo to an early injury and have not developed a clear way of playing since appointing the Spaniard – unless, of course, the identity is supposed to revolve around a defence completely addicted to calamity.

It has been difficult to work out exactly what Alonso wants Chelsea to be. Are they a passing side or a counterattacking one? Do they mix it up and do both? One quality absent from their defeat against Sunderland was physicality. Chelsea were chewed up and spat out at the Stadium of Light and the recent lack of flow has not been helped by the ongoing absence of João Pedro in attack.

Something different was required and initially there was a more balanced feel to Chelsea with Emegha, making his first start since joining from Strasbourg, leading the line. They made a fast start but their equilibrium was disturbed by Caicedo making way for Jordan Henderson after injuring his back in a collision with James Justin. The shift in momentum was instant and it was against the run of play when Leeds fell behind to a sharp finish from Emegha in the 25th minute.

• Leeds have won their first game against Chelsea at Stamford Bridge in all competitions since December 1999, ending a run of 10 away games against the Blues without victory (D2 L8), while they’ve scored 4+ goals away at Chelsea for the first time since January 1970 (5-2).

• Leeds have won their first Premier League game in London since May 2022 (2-1 against Brentford), ending a run of 15 matches in the Capital without victory (D4 L11), while it’s their biggest winning margin in London since April 2003 (6-1 against Charlton).

• Leeds’ Ao Tanaka (pictured with Cole Palmer) committed three fouls inside the first six minutes against Chelsea; no player has ever fouled more often inside six minutes in a Premier League game on record (since 2003-04). Opta

The visitors had threatened in the air, Calvert-Lewin sending one header against the bar and another straight at Emiliano Martínez, but they were undone by poor defending at the other end. There was nothing clever from Chelsea, simply a hopeful punt down the left, and Leeds would have been fine if Justin had timed his header properly. Instead the defender’s clearance hit Rogers and ran nicely for the Chelsea forward to release Emegha, whose vicious shot flew past James Trafford at the goalkeeper’s near post.

The goal should have settled Chelsea’s nerves. Yet Leeds stayed calm and levelled within three minutes. Chelsea have no leaders at the back, especially with Maxence Lacroix absent with a hamstring injury. Wesley Fofana is too easily ruffled in the air and was partly at fault for the equaliser. He sent a routine header back into dangerous territory and Leeds were in business when that mistake was followed by Josh Acheampong mistiming his jump, leaving Calvert-Lewin to advance through the middle and give Okafor the chance to slip a low shot past Martínez.

Chelsea’s response was feeble. There were times when Leeds simply ran through them. Tarik Muharemovic charged in from the left, away from a lazy Cole Palmer, and saw his cross flicked wide by Calvert-Lewin. Ao Tanaka went close, Calvert-Lewin missed another header and Muharemovic grimaced after having a goal disallowed for offside.

Ethan Ampadu was running the game for Leeds in midfield. Chelsea were being outmuscled again. They were losing all the 50-50s and Levi Colwill, low on confidence since returning from a lengthy knee injury, looked like he wanted the ground to swallow him up when he sent a simple pass out for a throw.

Chelsea were booed off at half-time. Colwill was toiling against Calvert-Lewin and Leeds continued to dominate at the start of the second half, raging when Michael Oliver elected not to award a penalty for Martínez’s clumsy challenge on Calvert-Lewin.

The luck did not last long. Back came Leeds, causing havoc with one of Ampadu’s long throws. The ball dropped loose, Jayden Bogle cracked it goalwards and this time Oliver pointed to the spot after deciding that Rogers had blocked the wing-back’s shot with an arm. It was a controversial call given that Rogers had his arm close to his body and the ball hit him close to the shoulder. Yet the decision was upheld and Leeds were ahead when Calvert-Lewin sent Martínez the wrong way from the spot.

Leeds were relentless and did not have to wait long for a third goal. Ampadu delivered another long throw. Chelsea cowered and Sean Longstaff knocked the ball out to Ampadu, who was unchallenged as he crossed for the unmarked Okafor to beat Martínez with ease.

The closing stages were miserable for Chelsea. Palmer was an irrelevance, Estêvão Willian was awful on the right and Leeds turned the screw in added time. Aaronson, a late substitute, sped down the right, turned past Malo Gusto and smuggled a shot through Martínez’s grasp to complete the humiliation.
