Why Chris Doering claims Tennessee football roster is 'perfectly constructed'

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Injuries continue to pile up for Tennessee football as the regular season winds down.

The Vols lost leading receiver Mike Matthews to a season-ending knee injury in their 22-7 win at Texas A&M on Nov. 14. Matthews became Tennessee's 10th player to suffer a season-ending injury.

The setbacks have made the Vols' success even more impressive, according to SEC Network analyst Chris Doering.

"I think this Tennessee team is perfectly constructed," Doering said Nov 16 at the Knoxville Quarterback Club. "I know you'd like to have a little more production in the passing game. Losing Mike Matthews is devastating. I think Tennessee has sustained 10 season-ending injuries this year and has been able to keep the train on the tracks."

The No. 9 Vols (8-2, 5-2 SEC) play No. 23 LSU (6-4, 3-4) on Nov. 21 (3:30 p.m. ET, ABC) at Neyland Stadium.

Through 10 games, Matthews, a junior, caught 39 passes for a team-high 608 yards and four touchdowns.

Doering was confident, though, in Tennessee's ability to adjust. He heaped praise onto coach Josh Heupel for flipping the offense from its usual fast, pass-heavy approach to a slower pace.

"I think that Josh Heupel deserves to be the SEC Coach of the Year," the former Florida wide receiver said. "Look at where this team was supposed to be heading into the season, look at the youth and inexperience at the quarterback position and the way that he's changed how he goes about coaching offensive football.

"I really appreciate coaches that don't have to do it one way, that can do it differently based upon guys that are on the roster and I think he's done a tremendous job."

Doering credited Heupel for hiring coordinator Jim Knowles, who turned the Vols' defense into the top scoring defense in the SEC this season. Offensively, leaning on running back DeSean Bishop has given Tennessee a new identity while the young quarterbacks George MacIntyre and Faizon Brandon continue to split time.

Bishop is seventh in the country with 1,163 rushing yards and 13 touchdowns on 230 carries.

"I thought if there was anybody that could get into the Heisman race from the SEC, it would be DeSean Bishop because of what he's done consistently," Doering said. "He's like an old-school back. A grinder. I don't know if that's flashy enough for the national folks. I think he's the SEC offensive player of the year, no doubt."
