Dan Ballard’s Return Will Be A Godsend For Sunderland

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

There's no doubt that prior to Sunderland's gutsy and potentially pivotal victory over Chelsea, something had been missing from the Lads' performances on too many occasions this season.

The narrow 1-0 home victory over the Blues hinted at something stirring within the ranks; something far more wholehearted and unified that had been on display during many of our previous fixtures, but as we edge nearer to — and let's be brutally honest here — an absolutely savage late November and December schedule, it really will be a case of "all hands on deck" as we aim to climb away from the relegation zone and ensure that Christmas is a time of renewed optimism in Sunderland circles.

With that in mind, one player's return to the fold — and with fingers crossed, his recovery is progressing well and he's edging ever nearer to a return — will be nothing short of a godsend for Régis Le Bris.

During the early weeks and months of 2026/2027, Sunderland's defensive woes have been a defining factor, with the likes of Omar Alderete and Kevin Danso periodically struggling for form and Nordi Mukiele occasionally having to hold the entire thing together through sheer force of will and supreme ability.

Hope that someone big, bad and "f***ing hard" is currently on the road to recovery, watching on from the outside and with a raging fire burning inside. Does such a player exist? You bet he does.

Dan Ballard is essentially Sunderland's answer to the former Leicester and England flanker Lewis Moody.

He's harder than that twenty year old pack of nails that remain unused in your utility drawer and he's wholehearted to the point of borderline recklessness. He's also utterly tireless in defence, a useful option at attacking set pieces and of course, the scorer of possibly the most iconic header netted by a Sunderland player since Niall Quinn against Newcastle in 2000.

Of course, the Northern Ireland international's injury record since his 2022 arrival at Sunderland would give even the most cheery club doctor palpitations.

Setbacks and knockbacks have been part and parcel of Ballard's Sunderland career and it's fair to say that he looked a little sluggish before his latest injury, but to my mind, he's already well on the way to becoming a modern great and his return to the fold can't come quickly enough.

Alderete, who's never looked quite right in terms of both form and fitness since returning from a gruelling World Cup, and Danso are by no means lost causes — far from it. They've got international and domestic experience to burn and will both have key roles to play at various stages this season but they need calming presences alongside them in order to thrive, and Mukiele and Ballard can fulfil those roles perfectly.

Ballard is the kind of player that, in the pursuit of victory, won't just make that extra tackle or extra block.

Instead, he'll make five tackles, three blocks and casually ask what all the fuss was about. He has form for returning from injury and slotting back in seamlessly — he did so during our 2024/2025 end-of-season run — and his no-frills approach is probably exactly what we need.

That's my kind of defender and given that Sunderland have looked far too ropey at the back for the bulk of the season, the thought of a fit, firing and freshly motivated Ballard returning to the backline and hopefully giving us some extra clout could be an ace card for Le Bris. He might have a little bit to prove in terms of fitness but in every other sense? No chance.

In four years at Sunderland (it feels like far more, given what he's seen and experienced since arriving on Wearside), few players have embodied the notion of "leaving it all on the pitch" to a greater degree than Ballard.

If one of those rolling skull-and-crossbones bombs from Crash Bandicoot was heading for your rowing boat, you'd want him aboard to cushion the impact of the detonation.

Likewise, if one of your main threats is from an aerially-strong centre forward, who better than him to win a couple of thunderous duels in order to set the tone for what's coming next? Climbing the table won't just require physical application — it'll demand 100% commitment from every player on the park and Ballard has undoubtedly become one of our true leaders in that sense.

Ballard proved his ability at Premier League level on multiple occasions last season — even if he didn't play what would've been a deserved role in our final-day victory over Chelsea. The next time his name appears on a Sunderland starting eleven, it'll be with a different degree of expectation, but one that he'll undoubtedly be ready for.
