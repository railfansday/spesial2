Alibaba vs. Uber Technologies: Which Tech Platform Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Alibaba and Uber both lead their markets, but one trades at a far lower earnings multiple while the other is priced for sustained high growth.

Global platforms like Alibaba Group (BABA +0.02%) and Uber Technologies (UBER +0.83%) dominate their respective markets through massive scale. Choosing between these giants requires weighing deep value in Asia against high-growth mobility services.

Alibaba operates at the heart of the Chinese digital economy, while Uber connects the physical world through rides and logistics. While both rely on network effects to maintain their leads, they face different regulatory landscapes and geographic drivers. This comparison examines their financials and risks to help you decide which stock is the better buy.

Alibaba is a global technology leader focused on cloud computing and commerce. The company operates massive marketplaces in China, such as Taobao and Tmall, which are core components of the broader consumer discretionary stocks market. Its strategy centers on integrating artificial intelligence into its cloud infrastructure to serve enterprises while expanding its international digital commerce operations in Southeast Asia and Europe.

In the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, revenue reached around $153.7 billion, representing growth of approximately 2.7% compared with the prior fiscal year. The company reported net income of about $15.6 billion for this period, although this was a decrease from the year before. This trend reflects a competitive retail environment in China and significant investments in new technology initiatives and international market expansion.

As of its March 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.2x. This ratio divides total debt by shareholder equity to show how much a company relies on borrowing. The current ratio stands at approximately 1.3x, which measures the ability to cover short-term obligations by comparing current assets to current liabilities. According to the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026 report, free cash flow was a negative $7.6 billion, representing cash from operations minus capital expenditures.

Uber Technologies operates a massive technology platform connecting riders with drivers and consumers with merchants. The company relies on significant commercial partnerships, including work with McDonald's (MCD -0.61%) for global delivery and Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN -0.29%) to provide services for Prime members. Uber also maintains a critical mapping partnership with Alphabet (GOOGL -0.98%) to ensure its platform remains functional for millions of monthly users worldwide.

In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, revenue reached approximately $52.0 billion, which was an increase of more than 18% over the prior year. The company reported net income of roughly $10.1 billion, continuing a trend of improved profitability following years of heavy losses. This growth was driven by increased demand for mobility services and a maturing food delivery business that has become a staple for urban consumers.

According to its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is nearly 0.4x. This metric helps investors understand the company's financial leverage by comparing its total debt to the value of its equity. The current ratio is approximately 1.1x, indicating that the company has slightly more current assets than liabilities due within one year. Free cash flow for the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, was about $9.8 billion, which is the cash remaining after subtracting capital expenditures from operating cash flow.

Alibaba faces substantial risks related to the regulatory environment in China and ongoing geopolitical tensions. Changes in government policy can abruptly affect its domestic e-commerce operations or its cloud computing business. Additionally, the company faces intense competition from local rivals such as PDD Holdings (PDD +1.86%) and JD.com (JD +1.53%), which put pressure on its net margin. Any slowdown in Chinese consumer spending also directly impacts its core revenue streams.

Uber Technologies is subject to significant legal and regulatory risks, particularly concerning the classification of drivers as independent contractors rather than employees. A shift in these regulations could materially increase operating costs and force changes to its business model. The company also competes in a crowded market against Lyft (LYFT +2.23%) and DoorDash (DASH -0.62%), which limits its ability to raise prices. Furthermore, it faces potential liabilities from ongoing litigation regarding safety incidents on its platform.

Alibaba trades at a significantly lower earnings multiple than Uber, suggesting it is the value play while Uber is priced for continued high growth.

Uber revolutionized the taxi industry when it rolled out its ride-sharing network, allowing anyone with a car to earn a side gig. Both the industry and the companies have come a long way.

Uber Technologies is by far the largest player in the developing ride-share market in terms of revenue. Wall Street expects Uber's sales to increase $6 billion from last year to $58 billion this year, bringing with it net income of $6.4 billion. But as a sign of the expenses Uber faces in expanding its business model, that would be roughly $3.6 billion less income than in 2025. In the long run, its global ride-sharing, delivery, and autonomous solutions business will make for a strong business.

Alibaba focuses on defending its cash-rich China e-commerce marketplaces, Taobao and Tmall, while funneling capital into higher-growth AI cloud and AI services. Alibaba is the public cloud leader in China and the owner of the Qwen open- and closed-source models. Yet its retail businesses are losing market share to Chinese rivals, which crimps the business's ability to fund money-losing AI model efforts. That means its AI cloud and compute services are the one impressive growth center for the business. It grew 45% year over year in that sector. One metric in its favor is that the current valuation of Alibaba is attractive relative to U.S. cloud and AI peers.

Alibaba is forecast to continue growing, but some one the Street are skeptical of the company's ability to win in the hyper-competitive Chinese market and to expand its fast-growing AI services business beyond the China-dominated region, especially given the capital investment needed for AI.

BABA looks like a value play, but UBER is hardly priced at a premium, trading just over the average forward P/E of the S&P 500. Buying Uber Technologies at current levels makes for a better buy in 2027, given the volatility of AI and the Chinese consumer.
