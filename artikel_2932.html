Rangers new injury concern, though Durzi likely game-time decision vs. champs

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

When it comes to injuries here during their five-day break in the schedule, it feels a bit like one step forward, one step back for the New York Rangers.

On the positive side, defenseman Sean Durzi was a full participant for the second straight day at practice Friday. Coach Mike Sullivan sounded very pleased with where Durzi's at after missing the previous eight games, and hinted that the 28-year-old is on the cusp of returning to the lineup.

"Yeah, we're encouraged with Durz's progress," Sullivan said Friday. "I think he's had two real good practices. I think he looks way more mobile out there, the way he's moving, stronger, more assertive … I would anticipate him being a game-time decision."

That decision's coming up, with the Rangers next game a Sunday matinee at Madison Square Garden against the defending Stanley Cup champion Carolina Hurricanes.

The other side of things is that forward Oliver Bjorkstrand may not be able to play Sunday after missing practice and becoming the latest of New York's walking wounded.

"He's being evaluated for an upper-body injury. It was just something he's been dealing with. I don't know specifically when it occurred, but obviously just his discussion with our medical team prompted more evaluation," Sullivan explained.

When asked if there's a chance Bjorkstrand won't be available to play against the Hurricanes, Sullivan answered, "Yes, I think that's a fair statement."

The 31-year-old is tied for second on the Rangers with seven goals, playing up and down the lineup in a variety of roles this season. During a 3-1 win over the Montreal Canadiens on Monday, Bjorkstrand logged 10:56 TOI on the third line. Following two complete off days for the Rangers, Bjorkstrand took part in practice Thursday, before surprisingly failing to do so again the next day.

At practice Friday, fourth-liner Jaroslav Chmelar moved up and skated in Bjorkstrand's spot alongside Noah Laba and Eeli Tolvanen. With that, the fourth line consisted of Glenn Gawdin, Luke Glendening, and Matt Rempe.

If Bjorkstrand is unable to play Sunday, and Pavel Dorofeyev remains out, the Rangers could recall top prospect Liam Greentree from the minors. The 20-year-old leads Hartford with nine goals and 16 points in 19 games. He's second in the AHL with eight power-play goals, and leads all rookies in goals and power-play tallies.

Dorofeyev skated on his own before practice for the second straight day. The 26-year-old, who has six goals and six assists, has a lower-body injury and missed the home game Monday against the Canadiens.

Flipping back some good news, Joe Veleno skated on his own Friday for the first time since sustaining a lower-body injury Oct. 4 against the Utah Mammoth in New York's fourth game of the season.

"We're real encouraged that he's on the ice. He still has a timeline in front of him, but obviously the fact that he's back on the ice is just, I think, an indication of progress, and it's great for Joe," Sullivan stated.

Veleno's not expected back before the New Year, per his original diagnosis.
