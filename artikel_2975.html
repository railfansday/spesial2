Dollar Tree's Customer Traffic Jumped 3.8% as Comparable Sales Beat Its Outlook. Here's What's Behind the Gains.

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The retailer's pivot to a multipricing strategy is paying off handsomely.

By Isac Simon – Updated Nov 25, 2026 at 12:55AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Dollar Tree ›

As inflation continues to squeeze household budgets, consumers are increasingly becoming selective about where to spend their money. As a result, discount retailers are back in vogue. Shoppers are looking to squeeze every last bit of value from every dollar spent.

Even middle- and higher-income households are looking for better value on daily-use and household essential items. And that's where Dollar Tree (DLTR +11.18%) has taken advantage of the circumstances, attracting more customers with an expanded assortment of merchandise and pricing it competitively.

The discount retailer's latest quarterly results for the 13 weeks ending Oct. 31 strongly suggest that it is more than just an occasional bargain-shopping destination. Dollar Tree is now part of customers' regular shopping routines.

Dollar Tree's multipricing strategy has increased customer traffic. Image source: Getty Images.

Customer traffic rose a solid 3.8% over this period, up from the 0.4% increase in the second quarter. Comparable-store sales also rose a healthy 4.6% while total revenue increased nearly 7%, to about $5.1 billion.

Adjusted earnings per share (EPS), the core profit marker for Wall Street, crushed estimates by more than 25%, coming in at $1.16 versus average estimates of $0.92. Stronger-than-expected sales, improved merchandise costs, better expense management, and lower shrink all contributed to the bottom line.

Essentially, management's turnaround strategy is working. When customers respond with higher footfall, it means there's an improved shopping experience, usually driven by a superior merchandise assortment.

More importantly, the dollar store witnessed growth across customer income groups. In the earnings call, CEO Mike Creedon explained that while lower-income shoppers constitute the largest share of the customer base, middle- and higher-income customers (known as trade-in or trade-down customers in retail parlance) have contributed to over half of the growth. These customers may visit Dollar Tree stores only four to six times a year. Yet their seasonal or holiday shopping cumulatively generates $1 billion in sales for every additional visit, according to Creedon.

Traditionally, Dollar Tree operated on its hugely popular single $ 1.25 price point model. But moving to a multi-tier pricing strategy (or multi-price strategy) over the last few quarters has helped the retailer expand its merchandise assortment, particularly in discretionary and seasonal goods, which has proved popular with higher-income customers.

That said, the loyalists -- the retailer's traditional, price-sensitive core customers who visit its stores more than 70 times a year -- continue to drive the business's foundational revenue.

The bigger investor takeaway is that a multi-price strategy enabled customers to combine traditional single-price-point items with multi-price products in a single transaction. These mixed baskets accounted for 25% of transactions and half of total sales. So, essentially, this strategy does more than increase average transaction values (or "average ticket"). It's encouraging shoppers to purchase a wider array of products during each visit.

Dollar Tree has done its homework to make shopping more convenient and encourage repeat visits. The retailer completed 3,000 store refreshes and renovations across its store base, focusing on "brighter, cleaner, and more inviting stores."

This includes raising store operating standards, improving merchandising and product availability, and simplifying routines for store employees.

Additionally, the company's "It's All in the Tree" nationwide marketing campaign, which communicates its expanded assortment while reinforcing the value and convenience available at its stores, has played a major role in increasing traffic.

Not surprisingly, Dollar Tree's stronger showing in the third quarter prompted management to raise its fiscal 2026 earnings forecast to $8.05 to $8.25 per share from a previous range of $7.70 to $8.05, a significant 35% increase at the midpoint. The company also reported solid cash generation, with free cash flow reaching $122 million on an operating cash flow totaling $375 million.

However, investors might overlook that overall profitability declined this quarter, with gross margin declining 10 basis points to 35.7%, while adjusted operating margin fell 120 basis points to 6.1%. Management acknowledged that continued inflationary pressures, higher fuel and freight costs, and product cost inflation have weighed on profitability.

Importantly, management expects the fuel- and freight-driven inflationary environment to persist into 2027. But will the company be equipped to handle these pressures? I think so. The third quarter was a demonstration of just that.

However, growth rates could still slow down. Mixed baskets generated nearly half the sales despite representing just 25% of total transactions, proving that capturing more sales in higher-priced items is the clearest path to sustained growth and profitability.

So long as households continue to look for ways to make their budgets go further, Dollar Tree appears well-positioned to benefit as it becomes a more relevant everyday shopping destination for consumers across income levels.
