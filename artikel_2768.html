Mowbray's 'soldiers and artists' march up the table

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Since the lengthy international break across September and October, Blackburn Rovers have taken 12 points from a possible 21.

The only real blemish was the 4-0 hammering at Swansea City, a day on which Rovers never turned up. Beyond that, there has been plenty to like.

With just over a third of the season gone, it feels like the perfect moment to take stock.

Most importantly, Rovers are looking up, not down.

Twelve months ago, supporters were nervously checking the bottom end of the table. Now the conversation is about the play-offs. That alone tells you how much has changed.

That revival began under Michael O'Neill, but Tony Mowbray has kept the momentum going.

Matchdays feel like events once more. The big away followings have helped. Not many clubs can hand over more than 7,000 tickets to travelling supporters, and fans have responded in numbers.

Crowds nudging 20,000 for games against Middlesbrough, Coventry City, Leicester City and West Ham over the past seven months have brought a buzz back to the place.

The only defeat among them came in a dead-rubber against an already relegated Leicester side.

The positivity extends beyond the stands.

Most of this squad bears the scars of last season's struggles. Apart from Sam Morsy, Jayden Fevrier and Gady Beyuku, these players have lived through difficult times at Ewood. Yet they look liberated rather than burdened by the experience.

That's where the Mowbray factor comes in.

His "soldiers and artists" philosophy sounds simple, but right now it's working a treat.

Todd Cantwell and Dapo Afolayan have become central figures. Both have stayed fit. Both along with centre back Sean McLoughlin are ever-presents. Both are proving they can be difference-makers.

Afolayan managed just one start after his February arrival and was hooked at half-time.

Cantwell started fewer than half of Rovers' league games and never really had the freedom to express himself.

This team is playing with far more adventure, which suits both players perfectly.

Anyone familiar with Mowbray's career should not be surprised.

For all his reputation as a no-nonsense centre-half, his teams have always tried to attack. That's in his DNA. It always has been.

Morsy remains the heartbeat of the side. Mowbray calls him his coach on the pitch and it is easy to understand why.

His influence seems to be rubbing off on academy graduate Kristi Montgomery, who looks increasingly comfortable at Championship level.

Together, they give Rovers a solid foundation.

The next phase of the season now feels crucial.

There are 24 league matches and at least one FA Cup tie before the next international break.

By March, we'll know whether this team is genuinely capable of sustaining a play-off challenge. Staying healthy will be key.

The long-term absences of Ryan Alebiosu and Adam Forshaw are significant blows. Both were hugely important last season.

Rovers registered 23 players in September and Lewis Miller's return from long-term injury adds another option. There is room to manoeuvre, and every chance that the squad will evolve during the transfer window.

Mowbray expects backing. We'll soon discover exactly how much.

And then there's the question of Andri Gudjohnsen. Can he stay fit? Can he keep scoring?

Ten Championship goals from 11 starts is an extraordinary return. It puts him among the most clinical forwards in Europe statistically.

The bigger concern for Rovers might be whether they can keep hold of him if the goals continue to flow. Because strikers like that do not stay under the radar for long.

Still, if you'd offered Rovers supporters this position after 15 games, most would have snapped your hand off.

Three points outside the play-offs. Two behind West Ham. Momentum building.
