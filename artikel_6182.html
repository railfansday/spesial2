Golden Week gave Thailand its first real sign that Chinese travelers were coming back. Now a single embassy advisory has put that at risk, and Lunar New Year 2027, coming in early February, will show whether the damage is a dip or something more prolonged.

Chinese arrivals to Thailand fell to 49,494 in the week of October 18–24, down about 24% from the week before, The Nation reported. Thai officials attributed the drop partly to post-holiday seasonality and partly to weaker confidence after a Chinese embassy safety warning.

Chinese arrivals to Thailand fell to 49,494 in the week of October 18–24, down about 24% from the week before, The Nation reported, with Thai officials attributing the decline partly to post-Golden Week seasonality and partly to weaker confidence after a Chinese embassy safety advisory. The October 16 advisory cited cases of Chinese nationals who went missing, were unlawfully detained, or were lured toward the Thailand–Myanmar border, and urged travelers to tighten safety precautions. Thai Hotels Association president Thienprasit Chaiyapatranun told Skift daily Chinese arrivals dropped from about 15,000 during Golden Week to about 9,000, while the Association of Thai Travel Agents said cancellations have pushed Chinese group-tour business more than 30% below last year, with a warning it could halve over Lunar New Year 2027 without government action. The reversal lands after a period of rebuilding: Minor Hotels had told Skift Chinese room nights across its Thailand portfolio were 78% ahead of last year heading into Golden Week, and China had retaken the top source market spot with about 4.2 million arrivals through October 31. Thailand has since cut its 2026 China target to 5.1 million and its full-year visitor forecast to 33 million from 36.7 million, after total foreign arrivals fell 4.1% to 24.4 million from January 1 to October 17.
