Prediction: This Quantum Computing Stock Will Be the Biggest Winner of 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Pasqal is down more than 80% from its peak, but it could be 2027's big comeback story if its next machine shows up on time.

By Micah Zimmerman – Nov 20, 2026 at 11:47AM EST

If you only looked at Pasqal Holding (PSQL +5.67%) stock's chart, you'd assume something broke. The French quantum computer maker hit $24.69 a share within days of its Nasdaq debut in late August. Today, it trades at around $3.

But when I dug into what the company has actually been doing, I didn't find a broken business. I found one that's ahead of its stock. That gap is why I think Pasqal could be the biggest winner in quantum computing next year.

There are numerous ways to create the quantum bits — aka, qubits — that sit at the heart of every quantum computer.

Pasqal is taking the “neutral-atom qubit” approach, creating each qubit from a single rubidium atom held in place and manipulated by lasers. One advantage of this method is that its machines don't need powerful cryogenic cooling systems to bring them to temperatures near absolute zero, which some more commonly pursued qubit technologies require.

That makes it easier to install them in the same rooms as ordinary server racks, and Pasqal has seven of them deployed across three continents, plus factories in France and Canada built to turn out up to 13 machines a year. CEO Wasiq Bokhari says that its hardware design will scale to at least 10,000 qubits per machine by 2029.

"Customers don't care whether it's a quantum computer or a GPU," he told analysts in June. "They just want the problem solved."

The best example among its current customers is energy megacap Saudi Aramco, which formally launched a 200-qubit Pasqal machine at its data center in May and now offers cloud access to it for other companies.

"Aramco is not just waiting for quantum computing, it is helping to shape it," Bokhari said. Pasqal has also signed a partnership with LG's tech arm in South Korea and a joint venture agreement to deploy systems across Saudi Arabia. It even launched a plug-in that lets AI coding assistants turn a research idea into a running experiment on its cloud machines.

The big catalyst due to arrive in 2027 is Vela, the next machine on Pasqal's roadmap, which will have 200-plus qubits. CTO Loïc Henriet described Vela as the machine that will "bring the first quantum advantage use cases to our clients." In plain English, that means it should be able to solve real business problems faster than a traditional GPU-powered server cluster can. Pasqal also aims to have systems providing 20 error-corrected "logical" qubits in production by 2027.

The science is already getting interesting. In October, its 256-qubit processor revealed magnet behavior that theory didn't predict. "We are not simply reproducing what we already know, but uncovering behavior we did not expect," Henriet said.

The catch is that roadmaps slip. Vela is a target, not a product on a shelf, and if it arrives late or underwhelms, the 2027 story will fall apart.

The business’s financial numbers are small and bumpy. In the third quarter, revenue fell to 3.3 million euros, from 5.7 million euros a year earlier, because Pasqal sold no machines during the quarter. Revenue from services like cloud access, upgrades, and maintenance did jump 53%, but that wasn't enough to compensate for the lack of new quantum computer orders. However, Bokhari said: "Although the order book and revenue do not show it yet, we have made important progress in our technology roadmap."

In September, Pasqal also exited a French defense quantum program to focus on commercial work. The cash story is mixed, too. In June, CFO Stéphane Rougeot said the SPAC merger that it was using to join the public market could leave Pasqal with "north of $600 million." In August, the actual deal netted it about $360 million. During its analyst day presentation in June, Rougeot also mentioned that the company had secured a $250 million convertible note, which could turn into new shares and dilute today's owners.

Here's why I still like the setup. Pasqal ended September with 312.8 million euros in cash and equivalents on its balance sheet, and its market cap is only about $681 million. Rougeot said annual cash burn has been between 30 million euros and 50 million euros, should rise only modestly, and that "we have plenty of runway." At this price, investors are paying very little for the technology itself.

That's what makes a "biggest winner" call possible. A stock already priced for disappointment doesn't need perfection to soar. Pasqal just needs Vela to show up and work. If it delivers its first system providing a quantum advantage to customers in 2027, I expect the market will stop pricing it as a failed experiment.
