Steph Curry ‘not afraid' to admit recent Warriors contract could be his last

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Steph Curry 'not afraid' to admit recent Warriors contract could be his last originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

Steph Curry still is playing at an elite level for the Warriors. The question is, how much longer does he intend to pla?

Curry signed a two-year, $116 million extension in September with a player option for the 2028-29 NBA season, keeping the star in the Bay Area through at least his 20th season. But it is possible that could be the end of the line for the superstar guard.

Curry admitted that at 38 years old, he is on borrowed time as a basketball player, and while he might find it in him to play past the end of this current deal, he is treating this as the last contract he ever signs.

"I assumed that it would probably be one of, if not the last, contract I signed," Curry told ESPN's Anthony Slater. I'm not afraid to say it. But maybe I'll surprise myself and want to keep going."

"Against Detroit, it took everything in me down there on the training table just to be like, 'Oh, I can go out here tonight and play,'" Curry said, referencing a game against the Pistons in late January. "And I was just faking it for the first whatever [25 minutes]."

Curry opted for a pay cut in his new contract, allowing for the Warriors to recoup some salary cap space next summer to make moves, while ensuring he still got what he is financially worth. The idea is for Golden State to remain competitive and in the championship hunt while Curry still is there.

The Warriors started this season on the wrong foot, dropping their first three games, but elite play from Curry and players like rookie Yaxel Lendeborg, helped the team go on an eight-game winning streak and eventually improve to 8-3.

However, Curry suffered a right oblique abdominal tear in a loss to the Houston Rockets on Nov. 11 and is out for at least two weeks. The Warriors have not won a game since, and are hoping he can return soon. Curry was averaging 27.8 points, 3.7 rebounds and 3.9 assists per game before the injury.

Curry still is a focal point in Golden State, even at his age, but as is the case with many aging superstars, circumstances could demand that he slide into more of a supporting role. He came off the bench in two regular-season games last year for the first time since 2011-12, and played well.

There might not be plans for that to happen in the immediate future, but if it does become a reality, he will embrace the new role.

"It would not kill my ego," Curry told Slater. "I want to win. Whatever that means, you make decisions based on what's in front of you."

The Curry-less Warriors lost to the New Orleans Pelicans 115-94 on Monday night, dropping them to 8-6 to begin the season. Golden State will look to get back into the win column against the Dallas Mavericks on Wednesday night at American Airlines Center.
