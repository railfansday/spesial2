Abdul El-Sayed rebranded ‘Medicare For All’ to get Trump’s attention. It worked

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Democratic Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan campaigned on passing Medicare For All, a universal single-payer national health insurance system. He believes in the initiative so much, he’s even willing to rebrand it to an unlikely name.

In mid-November, El Sayed posted an image on social media of a gold card emblazoned with name “TrumpCare.” The mockup was a convincing depiction of what a TrumpCare insurance card might look like. Trump’s name and signature is in the bottom right-hand corner, and there’s a presidential-style seal on it as well as the tagline “Great Health Care For Every American.”

“Donald, this is on the table. Guarantee healthcare for every American, from cradle to grave,” El-Sayed wrote on X on November 12.

The card was designed to get President Donald Trump’s attention—and it did. On Saturday, Trump reshared a screenshot of the card on his social network without comment on Saturday. Vice President JD Vance’s office reached out to El-Sayed’s team to set up a meeting NBC News reported Sunday.

Republicans and others learned long ago that the key to Trump’s attention and heart is flattery, especially when it comes to design and branding. There’s already Trump passports, the “Trump Gold Card,” for visas, and “TrumpRx,” a prescription drug website. A proposal for children’s investment accounts previously called “Money Accounts for Growth and Advancement” for “MAGA” were renamed simply as “Trump Accounts” to be included in last year’s One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Architectural firms that submitted a proposal to Trump’s Department of Transportation for a new terminal at Dulles Airport just outside Washington, D.C., slapped Trump’s name on it in their renderings.

El-Sayed’s proposal to turn Medicare For All policy into pictures that Trump understands, is part of larger democratic playbook. Recall when New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani showed up to the Oval Office with an imagined New York Daily News front page with the headline “Trump to City: Let’s Build” to encourage Trump to support federal grants for housing construction in the city.

El-Sayed’s rebranded proposal shows that Democrats have now picked up on how to win over Trump’s favor when it comes to passing your agenda. Meet Trump where he’s at. In an Instagram video, El-Sayed appeals to Trump’s low approval ratings and pitching passing “TrumpCare” as a chance to redeem himself, and on Meet the Press Sunday, he called it “the ultimate Trump card.”

Medicare For All is popular, but winning over Trump and Republicans could be key to actually getting a proposal passed. An October KFF poll found 66% of U.S. adults “strongly” or “somewhat support” a national health plan, but it’s far more popular among Democrats (84%) and independents (72%) than it is among Republicans (34%). Trump has previously said Medicare For All “doesn’t work” and called it “a socialist takeover of our healthcare system.”

Though Democrats will have control of the next Congress beginning in January, Trump will still have the power of the veto to block legislation. To override that veto, Democrats will need Republican support to reach a total of 290 votes in the House and 67 votes in the Senate. Or they could avoid a veto altogether by getting Trump on board. A shiny gold card could be all it takes.
