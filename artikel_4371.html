The Chevrolet Corvette ZR1X is often touted as a budget hypercar, but perhaps this Arkansas-based dealership didn’t get the memo. The dealer reportedly placed a staggering $696,445 price tag on a 2027 Corvette ZR1X 3LZ Convertible finished in Red Mist Metallic, which is more than double the vehicle’s original sticker price.

Social media user Abomination shared photos of the car and its pricing sticker, showing a $296,445 MSRP and an additional $400,000 “Exclusive Market Value Adjustment.” The latter is attributed to limited dealer allocations, extreme exclusivity, and a hypercar demand premium. The listing states that the “market adjustments reflect authentic real-time secondary and primary market demand.”

To be fair, the 3LZ is the top trim of the Corvette ZR1X. This particular example also comes with a convertible roof, which costs about $10,000 more than the coupe. Moreover, while the ZR1X isn’t a limited-production model per se, dealers have received limited allocations, making availability scarce.

Its hypercar status is also warranted, given that the vehicle has shown in different tests that it can go head-to-head with more expensive and exotic models from brands like Lamborghini. The ZR1X is the flagship model of the Corvette range, coming with a 5.5-liter twin-turbocharged LT7 V8 paired with a front axle-mounted electric motor, producing a combined output of 1,250 horsepower.

However, whether the reported “Exclusive Market Value Adjustment” is justified is up for debate. At the $696,445 asking price, buyers could instead get a brand-new Ferrari 849 Testarossa at its starting MSRP and still have about $130,000 to spare.

Dealer markups will likely remain a common practice, as they also help keep the lights on. That’s why buyers should understand a vehicle’s market value to negotiate better deals and avoid overpaying, especially for exclusive cars like the Corvette ZR1X that are prone to hefty premiums.

For reference, the 2027 Chevy Corvette ZR1X has a starting MSRP of $227,800 following two price hikes this year. Buyers who don’t fancy hybridization can opt for the Corvette ZR1, which uses the same twin-turbocharged V8 but without the front-mounted electric motor and all-wheel-drive system, and starts at $198,000. Budget-conscious Corvette buyers can also consider the entry-level Stingray, which starts at $71,300 but is already capable of reaching 200 mph in its base configuration, thanks to a new 6.7-liter LS6 small-block V8.
