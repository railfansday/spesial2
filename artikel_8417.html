It's not "chances" as in "pick a winner" - it's about buying the future profits of companies.

The USA us currently 72.77% of developed world market cap (MSCI World).

Current PE for MSCI USA is 24.74, for MSCI World ex-US it is 17.62 - so the ratio in terms of current profits (comparing 72.77/24.74 with 27.23/17.62) is 65.56% US to 34.44 ROW. Those are real profits currently being made in these "stagnating economies with aging population, high gov debts, more regulations, high taxes". The current valuations seem to be a bet that the US will become even more business friendly than it already is, and/or the ROW will become even more business unfriendly. Otherwise why would someone use 24.74 dollars to buy the rights to a dollar's-worth of US company profits, when the same money could buy $1.40 of company profits in ROW?
I generally agree with your response to bogle_user, but I think we should not forget a little detail: Equity valuations are usually a function of (expected) return on equity, which I think is a measure of expected profit growth, which in turn is correlated to aggregate economic growth (all other parameters being equal). So a slower growing economy would justify lower price/earnings valuations, to equalize expected returns in both countries or regions.
I think a more business friendly economy is commonly and rightfully associated with higher growth, even when it does not become even more business friendly than it currently is.
