Who's Michael Comer? Dad Charged With Attempted Murder After Baby Buried Alive

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The South Carolina case that shocked the country — an 11-month-old baby buried alive in a dog crate for roughly 24 hours — has put his father, Michael Comer, at the center of an attempted murder investigation in Lexington County.

Comer’s arrest in Batesburg-Leesville, South Carolina, the dramatic rescue of baby Koah Sharp and the child’s custody situation that reportedly preceded the incident have drawn national attention to a case authorities say could have ended in tragedy if not for a search party that spotted freshly disturbed dirt.

Here’s what to know about Comer, the charges against him and the family at the heart of the case.

Michael Comer is charged with attempted murder, unlawful conduct toward a child, custodial interference and burglary, according to court records.

The Lexington County Sheriff’s Department says Comer took his 11-month-old son, Koah, while the boy was in the care of Comer’s mother on November 9, 2026, triggering an urgent search near Batesburg-Leesville.

Comer was found later that evening at a local outbuilding — without Koah — and was initially taken into custody on a burglary charge.

He was denied bond days later on November 12.

According to Tyler Douglas, the lead investigator with the Lexington County Sheriff’s Department, Comer showed no remorse for the attempted murder of his son while appearing before a judge, the Associated Press and CBS News reported.

On November 10, 2026, a search party spotted an area of “freshly disturbed” dirt near the home of Comer’s father, Lexington County Sheriff Jay Koon said at a news conference. Investigators began digging and discovered Koah inside a dog crate wrapped in a towel. Officials believe the towel and colder temperatures likely created an air pocket that allowed the baby to breathe during the roughly 24 hours he spent underground.

Koon described finding the infant “clinging to life, crying,” calling the sound signaling he was alive “the most joyful sound.”

The sheriff said Koah was “screaming at the top of his lungs” when he was pulled from the crate. The boy was rushed to a hospital and released later that evening. “Koah Sharp is alive and well,” the sheriff’s department said in a November 11 news release.

Koah’s grandfather, John Comer, told the Associated Press he witnessed the rescue. “I became ecstatic, my hands straight in the air. I’m looking in the sky, jumping up and down, praising God,” John said, noting his grandson was crying and covered in dirt. “And at the same time I hit my knees and I’m just crying.”

Court records and reporting from the New York Times paint a troubled picture of Comer in the months before Koah was buried.

Comer was arrested in connection with a domestic violence incident against Koah’s mother, Dezarae Sharp, in May and pleaded guilty in October, records reviewed by Us show.

Sharp told the New York Times that Comer tripped and kicked her before pushing her into a dryer while she held Koah, after she confronted him about pornography on his phone. She broke up with him and moved out of the mobile home they shared.

Comer was also recently released from jail in a separate case stemming from a July incident, in which he pleaded guilty to an aggravated breach of peace charge, the newspaper reported.

According to the New York Times, in the weeks leading up to the alleged burial, Comer had been drinking “excessively,” was not taking medication prescribed for schizophrenia and was unhappy about becoming estranged from Sharp and Koah.

Comer’s mother, Patricia Comer, told NBC News her son is “where he needs to be.” She added, “Honestly, I’m his mother, and I never expected this from him.”

Dezarae Sharp  gave her first interview to the New York Times on November 13, 2026, holding Koah as she described her disbelief. “It’s something you would see in a movie. It’s something you would see on TV. And the fact that it happened to him — I don’t know,” she told the newspaper. Of Comer, she said, “I didn’t think he could go that far.”

Sharp is currently 15 weeks pregnant with her second child and said she started dating Comer in August 2024, after the two had known each other since childhood. She recalled that ahead of Koah’s birth in December 2025, Comer was “nervous but excited” to have a baby.

After his most recent release from jail, Comer began living with his mother, who told Sharp he had stopped drinking, was managing his mental health with medication and wanted to be with his son. Sharp allowed Comer and his mother to see Koah on weekends.

With Sharp pregnant by a different father, Comer allegedly grew jealous over the custody arrangement. In texts reviewed by the New York Times, Sharp wrote to him a few weeks ago:

“U threatened it, u the one that wants to take him. I said what I mean. Plz stop texting me.” Comer reportedly replied, “I don’t want to take him. I want us to be a family.”

After the rescue, Sharp said Koah had several scabs on his forehead when she was reunited with him at the hospital. “I’m tired,” she told the New York Times, adding, “But better now that I know he’s alive.”
