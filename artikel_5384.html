Ford is shipping its first U.S.-made iron phosphate battery cells from Michigan plant

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The cells are bound for Fathom electric pickup prototypes in Kentucky, and Ford plans to ramp up cell production next year

Ford $F Motor has started shipping the first lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery cells made at its BlueOval Battery Park plant in Marshall, Michigan, according to CNBC. Production at the $3 billion facility began last month.

The cells are headed to Ford's Louisville Assembly Plant in Kentucky, where they will power prototypes of the Fathom electric pickup, according to Electrek. Ford said the Fathom is the first vehicle on its Universal EV Platform.

Ford said workers at the plant assemble full LFP prismatic cells through every stage, from slurry and coating to formation, aging and final inspection. The company said it has moved past pre-production cells and is testing batteries for quality, following practices from Chinese battery maker CATL. Ford said its goal is to hold anomalies to parts per billion.

The plant is expected to turn out 40 cells every minute, which adds up to 20 gigawatt-hours of yearly capacity to supply both Ford's EV programs and its energy storage unit, according to CNBC. Initial plans called for up to 40 gigawatt-hours a year. Ford is only beginning to certify how it makes the cells and intends to increase output next year, before the Fathom is expected to launch in the fourth quarter of 2027.

Alan Clarke, Ford's vice president of advanced development projects, spoke about the CATL arrangement during a tour of the plant on Tuesday, according to CNBC. "If we want to get to being able to produce affordable EVs in the United States, we need to also do it the fastest way possible," Clarke said.

Ford said the plant has more than 500 employees, with plans to reach 800 by the end of the year and a goal of 1,700 jobs. Ford will begin taking Fathom reservations in early 2027, with the base standard-range version priced from $28,350, according to Electrek.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy urged Ford to sever ties with two Chinese companies, CATL and Geely, in a September letter to CEO Jim Farley. The letter cited Ford's use of licensed CATL battery technology at the Marshall plant. Ford responded that the deal is "a limited technology-licensing and services agreement, not a joint venture or foreign-owned manufacturing operation." The company said it owns the facility, directs its day-to-day operations and is the employer of record for its workers.

Some lawmakers have also criticized Ford over its ties to CATL, according to CNBC.
