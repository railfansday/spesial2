Timberwolves 113, Thunder 106: Seatbelt Stops the Drivers (Happy Thanksgiving)

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Ahead of Thanksgiving, the Minnesota Timberwolves were faced with a challenge: how could they work up an appetite that would let them eat with reckless abandon tomorrow night? As good as the cardio is for getting the stomach going, they had other ideas.

In honor of the most American tradition of overeating and arguments among family, the Wolves decided to challenge the Oklahoma City Thunder with a football game.

This one had everything: injuries, a singular Minnesota defensive talent, and an Oklahoma legend driving himself into trouble.

While we'll get to the first and third items on that list, it's important we start with the most important takeaway from tonight…

Any Thanksgiving needs a very specific menu. We have cornerstone items, favorite recipes that become traditions, and the reluctant inclusions that are only at the table because they have to be. As the holiday dishes are being prepped and baked (I had a friendsgiving last week where I made two roast chickens, potato gratin, mashed potatoes, and an apple pie, and just finished making mac and cheese and apple strudel for my girlfriend's family celebration tomorrow), let's make a menu of our own.

Love it or hate it, we need to have turkey on the table, and we are getting to the point where the team cannot survive without Jaden McDaniels. It's not perfect. Sometimes he has an odd turnover, or he turns down a three for a long midrange.

Well, that was before the last two games. McDaniels has dropped back-to-back 30-point games and is either reaching a new height in his career or in the midst of a leap that will define the player he becomes for the next decade or so. Either way, it's a pretty big moment.

While the focus will understandably be on Jaden's scoring jump – and for good reason – but I want to focus on his playmaking on the ball and on the short roll.

Over his last three, Jaden is averaging 30.3 points, 8.3 rebounds, and five assists. Tonight's four assists came without a single turnover as well.

Looking back on this game, one in which McDaniels outplayed MVP Shai Gilgeous-Alexander down the stretch, this was the play that stuck out to me. This is what the offense has looked like without Jaden McDaniels and it looked a whole lot like something I've been thinking for a while.

We talk about fit a whole lot in regard to the way teams are built, but we often back seat it to talent comparisons when we try to make historical equivalents. People have been trying to call Anthony Edwards and Jaden McDaniels the new-age Michael Jordan and Scottie Pippen, but while the aesthetics of a star shooting guard and small forward talking smack and hitting artful middies are there, the actual playstyle isn't.

Especially with the introduction of LaMelo Ball to the mix, I think we need to make a different comparison…

I think these Wolves mirror the early dynasty Golden State Warriors in playstyle. LaMelo Ball (Steph Curry) uses his gravity to draw out trapping defenders, finding the splitting screener rolling to the rim in Jaden McDaniels (Draymond Green), who ultimately dumps it off on the short roll to Rudy Gobert (Javale McGee).

Maybe that's a bit too far, as Gobert would love to have McGee's hands.

No, in all seriousness, Jaden is a far more dominant on-ball player than Green ever really was, but Draymond's playmaking within the high-movement system that the Warriors ran was such an easy boon to push those teams' offense to greatness. If McDaniels can take his more creation-centric role and combine it with the playmaking reads he's been able to make, he will continue to be an All-Star-level player even when Edwards returns and limits his shot diet.

I don't know when mac and cheese became a Thanksgiving food, honestly. I am born and raised in Harlem, New York, so I know that the Abyssinian Church near me always offered it as part of their free plates, but that felt more like "everyone always wants this, so let's just add it to the plate."

And you know what, they were exactly right. I have never been unhappy to see a really crunchy-crusted, soul food baked mac and cheese.

And I have never, never been unhappy to see an Ayo Dosunmu moment.

Ayo has been absolutely unconscious over the past few weeks, but it's his timeliness that has been more impressive than the actual volume of his scoring. 15 points per game is not a massive number – there are 72 players averaging more than that so far this season – but it feels like the Wolves have relied entirely on Dosunmu as fourth quarters hit.

Tonight, it was a singular conversion of a blocked layup to a made three from Ayo that prevented a Thunder comeback from getting a real foothold as the two teams went back and forth.

Just like I don't know just when mac and cheese became a Thanksgiving staple, I don't know when I realized that the Wolves had pulled off another heist the way they had for Nickeil Alexander-Walker. All I know is that I am incredibly happy that it has, and I am filling my plate with a decent chunk of mac and cheese if I want to have an awesome holiday.

I don't want to talk about this, and you don't want to hear it. I don't want to be the one to bring green bean casserole, and you don't want to eat it. It's why I, well, don't bring green bean casserole and why I have never seen anyone enjoy it or eat it.

And yet, every Thanksgiving, there it is, glooping in the corner of the table, filled with cans of string beans and fried onions in sheer defiance of the cornucopia of delicious foods. Believe me, I have made some culinary attrocities, but the way this has become and unavoidable part of the holiday is beyond me.

In the same way, the way that injuries became such a necessary part of the conversation of this wonderful sport is so miserable to me. But, here we are.

Yes, LaMelo Ball, whose value has been discussed alongside the state of his lower body for years, tweaked his ankle on the foot of Isaiah Hartenstein. He headed to the locker room, and did not return for the remainder of the game.

Good news, though. It doesn't seem too bad. Maybe I should give green bean casserole another try.

The Wolves will close out the NBA Cup Group Stage against a Memphis Grizzlies team they faced very recently. A win would mean advancing to the Cup playoffs for the first time in the franchise's history since the event started just a few years ago.

That may not sound important, but it feels pretty massive to us Cupheads.
