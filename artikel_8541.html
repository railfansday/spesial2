I've never seen anyone on BH seriously arguing 2% is necessary. There may be folks who only end up spending 2% because frugal habits die hard or because they don't have anything they want to spend on, but that's not the same thing. The consensus is somewhere around 3-3.5% for perpetual withdrawal (early retirement or legacy) or 4% for a normal 25 year retirement.
Stick around the SWR threads for a while. 4% becomes 3.5% then 3% then 2.5% then 2% then nothing is really safe and so forth. It gets a little nutty by about the 50th post. Historically a blind 6% withdrawal technique with a 75/25 portfolio worked 97% of the time for 15 years and 60% of the time for 30 years. But you want me to only spend half of that.

Multimillionaire retirees like most Bogleheads don't need to be told to spend less. They'll do that just fine on their own. They need to be told to spend more. Studies show 6 out of 7 retirees are spending less than they safely could....by any measure. Many of them are spending only the income or even less. My parents just transfer RMDs from the IRA to taxable.

People who are financially literate and disciplined enough to build a multimillion dollar nest egg pretty much never run out of money. It just doesn't happen. So when they ask "How much can I safely spend?" the answer is 4%ish and adjust as you go. Trying to be more precise than that is a fool's errand. If you're lying awake at night worrying about a 4%ish withdrawal rate, buy more SPIAs and TIPS ladders until your portfolio is so boring it puts you to sleep.

A 4% rate historically left an average of 2.7X the original portfolio after 30 years. That should do for other 30 years. And another 30 years. And another 30 years. Almost all the time. But if you want a guarantee, then buy some guarantees. They're out there.
