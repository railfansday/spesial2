Moves to Make Before Week 11 Fantasy Football: Buy Josh Downs, sell Jonathan Taylor

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Last week, I suggested buying Ollie Gordon before his role in the passing game expanded and, sure enough, that's exactly what happened. I also suggested that you sell DJ Moore, and then he scored a touchdown, so we'll just ignore that part of the article. This week, I've got a whole new batch of players that you should consider buying, selling, or stashing before Week 11.

Xavier Hutchinson, Ja'Kobi Lane highlight Week 11's Regression Files

Denny Carter takes a close look at players who might be due for positive or negative regression headed into Week 11.

I know this sounds crazy, but Josh Downs legitimately makes the Chiefs Super Bowl contenders again. The offense opens up when he's on the field. Patrick Mahomes can actually play real quarterback and throw to real routes because there's a real receiver doing real receiver things downfield. Downs' 6.63 yards per route run last week were the second-highest among NFL wide receivers with at least four catches. Downs ran all eight of his snaps from the slot, an area of the field where he's lined up for nearly 80 percent of his career. It was remarkable to see Downs get targeted on five of his eight routes run. For context, those four receptions and 53 yards are the most Downs has had since Week 3, and this was just an early sample. We've seen a massive fantasy ceiling for receivers with Mahomes. Now, I'm not suggesting that Downs will immediately become the top target on the team and turn into Tyreek Hill or something, but we could see Downs turn into a must-start WR2 with touchdown upside every week. Looking forward, the Chiefs receivers have the fourth-easiest remaining fantasy schedule with no bye weeks and a Week 17 fantasy championship matchup against the Chargers, who are allowing the sixth-most fantasy points to receivers. This will probably be the last opportunity to buy Josh Downs, let alone buy at some sort of discount.

McBride is coming off his lowest output of the season due in large part to a tough opponent and a rookie quarterback making his debut. The McBride manager is likely a little bit nervous after last week's game and what the future may hold for this offense. That makes this week essentially the only time you can make an advantageous deal for the top tight end in fantasy. With fantasy managers fighting for the playoffs, every week becomes a must-win scenario. Over the next two weeks, McBride and the Cardinals will face the Chiefs and Commanders, who allow the seventh-most and second-most fantasy points to tight ends, respectively. The Week 14 bye for Arizona is frustrating, but that's a problem for future-you. Then, once you're in the playoffs, the Cardinals face the Jets, Saints, and Raiders, who are all in the top 12 in fantasy points allowed to tight ends. With no real target competition and a young quarterback just looking to win games, McBride becomes an easy buy-low target at a position that is perennially frustrating for fantasy football.

Over the last few weeks, Taylor's workload has decreased. In Weeks 1–6, Taylor averaged an 82 percent snap share and a 74 percent rush share. Since Week 7, those numbers have dropped to a 67 percent snap share and a 49 percent rush share.

At first, we all had the same thought: "the Colts are just protecting Taylor to trade him." Well, now the NFL trade deadline has passed, and Taylor is still with the Colts. So what gives? What we're likely seeing is a team that knows its season is over and is seeing what it has in some younger guys while keeping Taylor healthy for next year. Even in a competitive game against Miami in Week 10, rookie backup running back Seth McGowan handled 12 carries on 17 snaps compared to Taylor's 16 carries on 40 snaps. My worry here is that the Colts not only want to preserve Taylor for the future, but that the rest of the season could get ugly fast, leaving Taylor with a limited role because of game script. At first glance, it would appear that the Colts are the underdogs in each of the next two games against the Texans and Giants, with a bye week coming in Week 13. Taylor managers can expect crowded boxes along the defensive front and entire defensive game plans devised around stopping Taylor. We also have to ask the question: at what point would the Colts shut down Taylor for the season? Maybe that doesn't happen, but it's a risk I'd rather not carry on my team given the minimal upside Taylor has displayed over recent weeks.

This is a sell-low move where you hope you can get anything for him. Stevenson couldn't do much of anything against the Lions in Week 10, totaling seven touches for five yards. He's playing through a knee injury that was obviously a big factor for him. Injuries have become the identity for the Patriots at this point in the season, but at least with Stevenson, he's technically active and could have perceived RB2/flex value to one of your leaguemates. The truth is that the value simply isn't there. In fact, we may see the bottom fall out for the Patriots very soon. Drake Maye's ankle injury could spell disaster for the offense with a switch to Tommy DeVito and potentially bringing in Jimmy Garoppolo off the street. On top of the Maye injury, A.J. Brown returned only to have his appearance cut short with a hamstring issue. What's even more worrisome is that Stevenson is now in a three-headed backfield, with Corey Kiner and Lan Larison handling more than 56 percent of the snaps in the Week 10 loss. At this point in the season, I'm sure Stevenson knows that the Patriots would save more than $6 million in cap space by cutting the 29-year-old running back in the offseason. Would Stevenson consider benching himself? I know that's a wild concept, but we've seen players preserve their health for their next contract. Even if he plays, the schedule isn't very favorable, the offense is a mess, and the injury issues create a perfect storm of "I do not want this player on my fantasy team."

Burks is a really fun player. He's got speed for days and has an outside chance at being the Colts WR1 for the rest of the season in a lot of pass-heavy game scripts. In Week 10, Burks jumped from a 27 percent snap share to a 58 percent snap share. Now, there are obvious concerns. The Colts offense is not good, Daniel Jones has not been able to lead a fantasy-friendly passing attack, and Burks is a relative unknown. But looking forward in the season, the Colts receivers have the easiest remaining fantasy schedule in the NFL, and the circumstances leave an opening for someone to catch passes in this offense.
