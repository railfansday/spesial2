Aaron Rodgers says Mike McCarthy slept through meetings, held a grudge

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Buried in Aaron Rodgers' tirade against Pittsburgh Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy is a "concerned friend," the quarterback said.

On the Nov. 25 edition of "The Pat McAfee Show," Rodgers accused McCarthy of "unhealthy" behavior that led to the coach missing walkthroughs and falling asleep in meetings.

"How many quarterbacks around the league are dealing with the same type of stuff?" Rodgers said "… This guy's calling the plays!"

Rodgers said he told McCarthy "we got to make a change" about the offensive play caller and voiced that opinion on behalf of team, who talked about McCarthy's behavior and absences, according to Rodgers.

"I covered for him for a lot of years and we worked around it," Rodgers said. "And if you're standing on the truth and the facts … it's all firsthand accounts of what actually happened."

Rodgers said he has known about McCarthy dating back to their time together with the Green Bay Packers from 2008-18. The only time it became contentious, he said, was when Rodgers confronted McCarthy about his habits after the 2014 NFC championship game and after the a loss to the Buffalo Bills during the 2018 regular season. McCarthy was fired midway through that campaign; Rodgers said a staff member told him that McCarthy had said he had not forgiven Rodgers for "getting him fired in 2018."

"What he's saying and what he's actually doing is completely different," Rodgers said. "Anybody in the facility with nothing to lose would tell you the exact same thing.

"Nobody knew that this was going on for ten years in Green Bay."

Rodgers lamented that the Steelers are 6-4 and currently occupy the seventh seed in the AFC playoff race. Pittsburgh has lost two games in a row during which the offense looked borderline incompetent.

The final straw and impetus to go public with his qualms, Rodgers said, was an incident at halftime of the most recent loss to the Philadelphia Eagles. Trailing 13-3, according to Rodgers, McCarthy broke down the huddle with "one, two, three ... Packers!"

Rodgers told McAfee that he told McCarthy that he wants him to step away and work on his health or focus on the high-level jobs of a head coach and leave the offense and play calling to the assistants and Rodgers.

Their conversations did not necessarily end on a good note, he said. Tight end Pat Freiermuth called Rodgers to express his bewilderment that Rodgers was inactive when the four-time MVP learned of the news himself. Mason Rudolph will start Friday Nov. 27 against the Denver Broncos.

Other individuals who have talked to McCarthy "about some of these habits," Rodgers said, and did not respond well.
