BNB Chain’s new blockchain could make the same PancakeSwap trade 27% more expensive

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

BNB Chain’s proposed gas rules would raise the network fee for a sample PancakeSwap trade by 27% at the same gas price.

On BNB Chain’s proposed network, a trader would pay for all the computing capacity a swap reserves. Applying that rule to one PancakeSwap trade on BNB Smart Chain would raise its network fee by about 27% at the same gas price.

BNB Chain published its new layer-1 litepaper on Nov. 26, describing a separate network being developed alongside BNB Smart Chain (BSC). The design keeps BNB as the gas token, and a public testnet is in preparation.

For trading apps, the proposal pairs early feedback about where a transaction might land with reserved capacity for eligible activity. The devnet bills ordinary transactions for the room they request in a block.

A gas limit is the maximum amount of computing work a transaction can use. On BNB Smart Chain, the network fee for an ordinary transaction reflects the gas it actually uses.

The new devnet orders transactions before execution, using the declared limit to allocate room in the block. An ordinary transaction with a valid sequence number and sufficient funds at execution pays for that full amount, even if it finishes with gas to spare.

On June 26, a successful swap through PancakeSwap’s v3 router used 129,397 gas at 0.05 gwei per unit and paid 0.00000646985 BNB.

At the same price and the same 163,957-gas limit, the new devnet rule would charge 0.00000819785 BNB. The 34,560 units of unused capacity would add 0.000001728 BNB to that one swap’s fee.

At a 150,000-gas limit, the modeled fee would be 0.0000075 BNB, about 16% above the observed BSC fee. At 200,000 gas, it would be 0.00001 BNB, about 55% higher. A router that lowers its limit can save on transactions that fit and still run out of gas when a route takes more work than expected.

An included transaction that meets those conditions still pays the full limit if it reverts or runs out of gas under the devnet fee rules. At execution, an invalid transaction sequence number incurs no gas charge, while insufficient funds can limit what the network collects.

The network’s protected lanes reserve a portion of block capacity for transactions sent to governance-approved contract addresses. A swap sent to an unregistered router cannot use the reservation of a contract that the router calls.

The declared limit counts against a lane’s quota, so a larger buffer also leaves less room for other eligible transactions. Eligible transactions still compete for space within a lane.

The devnet’s preconfirmation documentation lists roughly 20 milliseconds for the block producer to stream a signed update showing a transaction’s intended position.

The transaction’s result is final after execution is committed and the block carrying that commitment has finalized. An app settling a trade needs a successful receipt and must confirm the receipt’s block is on the canonical chain and at or below the execution-finalized height.

A market maker might delay a quote update after seeing that a swap likely to change its reference price has provisional placement.

If the signal prevents costly stale quotes often enough, those savings can cover the recurring charge for unused gas. If the signal is withdrawn, the market maker has to reassess. A router that needs a swap outcome before making its next move still has to wait for execution.

BNB Smart Chain recorded $484.13 million in 24-hour decentralized exchange volume in DefiLlama’s Nov. 27 snapshot.

For any of that activity to move, the proposed network would have to make economic sense for traders at real gas prices. Public testing can show which lanes are available, how often placement signals are withdrawn, and how much spare gas different routes need.
