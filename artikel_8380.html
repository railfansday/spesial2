You can use $3K/year to offset ordinary income if you have no realized gains.

You can sell any equity/etf that has appreciated and buy it again at the higher price to give you a higher basis for a future cheaper sale.

If you don't sell the bonds, they will likely continue returning what they are returning which might be enough for your needs.

It sounds like you have too many bonds for your comfort level and perhaps for your desired asset allocation. The only way to fix that is to sell them.

If they were mine, I would review them all individually and decide if this is a bond I would but today at 5% less than the supposed market value of the bond today.

If the answer is yes, at that price I would rush out and buy it, then keep it.

If the answer is no, at that price I would not buy it, then sell it even if you have to take a big LTCG loss (or even a short-term loss). It doesn't really matter if you can't take advantage of the tax loss right now. The real loss happened when interest rates rose. You can try waiting for interest rates to fall , but I suspect you will be waiting quite a while before they fall enough for your individual bonds to return to the value they had previously. With an individual bond, you are not going to get increased payments like you might from a bond fund. You will get the payment that the bond promised when it was purchased.

I am not an expert on muni bonds but you have gotten some good advice on how to check which bonds might be more valuable to others.

As far as the $80K in loss goes, be glad it is available to you as a taxable loss. It could be they were worth $80K more the day before the basis was set so that the bonds were worth the same today but you had a small taxable gain to pay to sell the bonds.

This is also the kind of thing that can happen when people fixate on not wanting to pay taxes. They make choices that complicate things while only providing a small tax benefit that might or might not return more after taxes are taken into account.
