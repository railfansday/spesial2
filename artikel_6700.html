Will There Be Another <i><em>Hunger Games</em></i> Movie—and Which Character Will It Be About?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Sunrise on the Reaping details the story of Haymitch Abernathy’s Hunger Games and sets the stage for his involvement in overthrowing President Snow in the Second Rebellion, but there are so many stories within Suzanne Collins’s rich universe that are begging to be told. But is there another movie in the works, and if so, who would it be about?

“It’s interesting, because it’s always a surprise when she writes a book. She never tells us ahead of time, and Suzanne always surprises me and Nina [Jacobson], the [franchise’s] producer, with usually a pretty close-to-finished manuscript,” Francis Lawrence, Sunrise on the Reaping’s director, told ELLE in a September 2026 interview.

Here’s everything we know so far about a possible next installment in the Hunger Games movie franchise. Warning: Book and movie spoilers ahead.

There are currently no details about any future Hunger Games movies, but if Collins releases another book in the series, that has the potential to change.

It’s possible that Lawrence and Jacobson would return if another film is greenlit. Lawrence has directed every movie in the franchise since Catching Fire, and Jacobson has produced each installment.

There have been no details on a plot for a future Hunger Games movie, but there are already more than a few popular characters and topics that fans would like to see, should the franchise receive another installment:

First introduced in Catching Fire with the arrival of the Third Quarter Quell, Finnick quickly became a fan favorite thanks to his confidence and charm, which was only amplified when Sam Claflin starred as the victor in the book’s respective movie adaptation. But Finnick’s backstory is much more tragic than meets the eye. After becoming the youngest Hunger Games winner at 14, he was forced into sex trafficking and prostitution by President Snow.

Instead of perpetuating that pain, Finnick chose to protect those around him, notably his mentor, Mags, his romantic interest, Annie Cresta, and Katniss. If Finnick gets a movie of his own, it would provide details on the 65th Hunger Games, which takes place nine years before Katniss volunteers to participate in the 74th Hunger Games.

Also introduced with the Third Quarter Quell, Joanna (played by Jena Malone) became a beloved character in the franchise for entirely different reasons than Finnick. She’s outspoken, fearless, publicly opposes the Capitol and President Snow, and is never afraid to make others uncomfortable (see: the infamous Catching Fire scene in which she undresses in front of Katniss and Peeta in the elevator)—and she has every reason to be that way.

Joanna won the 71st Hunger Games by pretending to be a “sniveling, cowardly fool” and later proving that she was incredibly skilled with an axe. Like Finnick, she was also forced into sex trafficking by President Snow, who killed or tortured her loved ones if she didn’t cooperate; by the time fans meet her in the Third Quarter Quell, all of her loved ones are dead. A movie could dive into Joanna’s strategy for the 71st games, and how she became radicalized enough to participate in the Second Rebellion.

A movie about Mags could offer a unique bridge between the events that took place in Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, President Snow’s ascent to power, and the revamping of the Hunger Games system as we know it. Mags won the 11th Hunger Games—a year after Lucy Gray did in Songbirds, though she was later erased from history—and was the first victor in the history of the games to experience monetary prizes and life in the Victor’s Village, in addition to being the first to do a Victory Tour after winning, changes that were included at Snow’s insistence.

In Sunrise on the Reaping, Mags (Lili Taylor) works as Haymitch and Maysilee’s mentor, offering him a shoulder to cry on after Louella McCoy dies. By the Third Quarter Quell, Finnick revealed that Mags (Lynn Cohen) essentially raised him, and she volunteers in Annie’s place, ultimately sacrificing herself in Catching Fire when it becomes too difficult for her to move on.

Several clues are given about Wiress’s time in the 49th Hunger Games when fans meet her in Sunrise on the Reaping. During her games, the arena was completely covered in reflective and shiny mirror-like surfaces, making it difficult for the tributes to fight their way out of the arena. She won her games by hiding out in a blind spot until it was over and did not kill a single tribute. Visually, a movie with Wiress’s arena could be a feast for the eyes.

Wiress (Maya Hawke) appears in Sunrise on the Reaping and acts as Haymitch and Maysilee’s mentor; she later returns to the franchise in Catching Fire (Amanda Plummer) and uncovers that the arena works like a clock, inspiring the rebellion to make a new plan to break the arena.

Plutarch, a Capitol citizen with decades of wealth and influence to his name, has never been selected to participate in any Hunger Games, but his presence is deeply felt within the franchise.

In Sunrise on the Reaping, he (Jesse Plemmons) works on props for District 12 and lets Haymitch know that he’s actively working on the rebellion by giving Haymitch tips on how he can advance in the games and show resistance to the Capitol. While Haymitch is unsuccessful at being the spark needed to kick-start the Second Rebellion, Heavensbee stays on and works his way up to head gamemaker by the 74th Hunger Games with Katniss; he ends up becoming the brains behind the rebellion and leads it to victory. A Plutarch prequel could give longtime fans of the franchise a look into how a character with such proximity to the Capitol could become turned against it.

While fans are always hoping that Collins expands upon the existing Hunger Games characters, some are also looking for a fresh story that details a Hunger Games with a brand-new tribute that has no existing backstory in the franchise. Similarly, the exact events of The Dark Days—also known as the First Rebellion, which drove District 13 underground and started the Hunger Games after their years-long civil war with the Capitol—were never shown on-screen, and that could be a compelling story to give context to the Second Rebellion.
