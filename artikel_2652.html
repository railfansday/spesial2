Justin Herbert remains in concussion protocol as DJ Uiagalelei prepares for start

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

EL SEGUNDO, Calif. — The Los Angeles Chargers are coming off a disappointing loss to the New York Jets last Sunday at SoFi Stadium, but as they turn their attention toward the New England Patriots, the biggest concern in Los Angeles is no longer just finding a way to win. It is making sure the team stays healthy heading into Sunday's matchup.

Justin Herbert remains in concussion protocol after entering the league's process following the loss to New York. Herbert was not participating during the portion of Thursday's practice open to the media, leaving his availability for Sunday uncertain.

Chargers offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel addressed the situation, acknowledging the challenges that come with preparing for a game without knowing who will be under center.

"This isn't my first rodeo with regard to starting quarterbacks having an injury you don't know at the beginning of the week when we start game planning," McDaniel said.

McDaniel, of course, has experience navigating quarterback injuries and concussion protocol from his time as head coach of the Miami Dolphins, where Tua Tagovailoa dealt with multiple concussion-related situations.

With Herbert unavailable for practice and Trey Lance not available as a backup option, the Chargers made a move by signing quarterback Jake Browning to the active roster ahead of Sunday's matchup. Browning was on the practice field Thursday, taking reps alongside DJ Uiagalelei.

All indications currently point toward Uiagalelei potentially making his first start for the Chargers against New England if Herbert cannot get cleared. However, there is still hope that Herbert can progress through the protocol in time for Sunday.

McDaniel spoke highly of Uiagalelei's preparation and the work he has put in over the past couple of days.

"He's done a great job in the day and a half of work we've had," McDaniel said.

McDaniel also made it clear that Uiagalelei has been preparing as though he could be called upon to lead the offense.

"Justin Herbert is working his way through the protocol, DJ will get the start if he's not available," McDaniel explained.

"Regardless of whether or not he gets the start, whether or not Justin gets to play, that's an incredible valuable rep for DJ, who thus far two days into the plan, has done a fundamental job. I haven't been left hoping for more."

As for Herbert, McDaniel emphasized that the quarterback remains engaged with the team and that his health is the priority.

"Justin has been locked in," McDaniel said. "He will continue to do so. The biggest thing for him is his health and working through the protocol."

While the quarterback situation remains the biggest storyline heading into Sunday, McDaniel also reflected on the offense's performance against the Jets. Despite the disappointing result, he saw some encouraging signs from a unit that has struggled to find consistency throughout the season.

"As proud as you can be in a loss," McDaniel said when discussing the offensive performance.

"I thought getting points on four of our six drives was a step in the right direction."

The Chargers have shown some improvement offensively in recent weeks, but translating those flashes into victories remains the challenge.

One area that continues to develop is the running game, particularly with second-year running back Omarion Hampton becoming a bigger part of the offensive identity.

McDaniel addressed questions about Hampton's usage and whether the young running back should have received more opportunities earlier in the season.

"I've approached every game with an open mind," McDaniel said. "He's the starter and gets every opportunity to have a firm majority of the carries. We distribute the ball so he can have that effectiveness each game."

McDaniel pushed back against the idea that Hampton's role has significantly changed, pointing out that his playing time has remained relatively consistent.

"He's played about 29 plays a game," McDaniel said. "Consistently has been on the field the same amount of time," McDaniel explained.

McDaniel also referenced Hampton's performance against the Raiders in Week 2, suggesting that the coaching staff has consistently believed in what the young back brings to the offense.

"He's who we always thought he was," McDaniel said.

More importantly, McDaniel highlighted Hampton's ability to create yards after contact, something that has made him increasingly valuable as the season progresses.

"Seeing him get two, three, four, five yards after contact where other running backs wouldn't, it gets very easy to keep feeding him the ball. It becomes one of my main focuses in the game," McDaniel said.

For Hampton, that development could be especially important as the Chargers look to establish a more consistent rushing attack down the stretch.

The Chargers hosted a Thanksgiving Day practice as they continued preparations for New England. Now, the focus shifts toward Sunday's matchup at SoFi Stadium, with Herbert's availability, Uiagalelei's potential opportunity, and Hampton's continued development all worth monitoring.

For Los Angeles, the hope is that Herbert can get healthy in time. But if he cannot, the Chargers will need Uiagalelei to be ready for the biggest opportunity of his season.
