3 key adjustments Eagles must make out of bye week vs. Steelers

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

3 key adjustments Eagles must make out of bye week vs. Steelers appeared first on ClutchPoints. Add ClutchPoints as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

As crazy as it sounds, the Philadelphia Eagles leave their bye just 3.5 games out of first place in the NFC East after the New York Giants and Dallas Cowboys came up short to the San Francisco 49ers and Washington Commanders in comedic fashion in Week 10.

Could this be the year Nick Sirianni turns in his most impressive coaching performance to date, winning out in one of the most winnable divisions in all of football to take a backup quarterback to his third Super Bowl in five years? Philly does have a good track record of backup QBs playing in the "big game" after all, with a statue outside of Lincoln Financial Field forever highlighting one of their accomplishments thanks to the Anheuser-Busch Company.

Unfortunately, this team simply doesn't have that level of chutzpah, as after dropping seven games in a row, betting on anything but a close loss feels like a fool's errand at this stage of the season. Still, just because the Eagles are bad and will most likely close out the season with a top-5 pick doesn't mean they can simply throw in the towel and not show up for the final eight weeks of the season, even if they may want to.

No, with eight more games left to play whether they like it or not, the Eagles must use this time to make some changes, even if they don't immediately improve things in the win-loss column. Why? Simple: these last eight weeks need to be an audition for everyone to see which players can be pieces for the future, and which are better left in the past.

Unlike their first overall pick in the 2026 NFL Draft, Makai Lemon, Eli Stowers really hasn't seen much run for the Eagles as a rookie, which has to change coming out of the bye.

Widely considered the second-best tight end in this year's class behind Kenyon Sadiq, the Eagles clearly liked Stowers enough to make him the 54th overall selection in the 2026 NFL Draft, but between injuries and a very underwhelming training camp, Stowers has remained last on the depth chart for Philly, to the point where they just signed Zach Ertz to the 53-man roster off their practice squad to presumably fill the TE2 spot moving forward.

To his credit, Sirianni addressed Stowers' short-term future during his first media session since the bye, noting that he's very talented and is improving but was more interested in talking about his role on special teams than offense, which isn't ideal given the direction Philly is heading.

"We'll see how the game plan unfolds, but obviously he's super talented, and I think he's getting better each week. He's getting some vital opportunities to play on special teams as well," Sirianni said. "I think I've said this to you guys before: Guys that play on special teams and do a good job earn more and more reps through the trust that they build there. That's the ultimate portion of football where they have to block in space, tackle, and block destruct. As an offensive guy having to do that, that shows a lot about a guy, and I think he's doing a nice job there. I like how he's developing, and we'll see how that continues to progress."

Now granted, considering Stowers' biggest knock coming out of college was his inability to block, getting some run on special teams isn't the worst idea, especially on return teams where he has to engage with would-be tacklers in the open field. Still, Roseman didn't waste a mid-second-round pick on a John Mackey Award winner just to play on special teams, and if Stowers is ever going to live up to expectations, the Eagles need to find a way to utilize his supreme athleticism on offense, where he already has one long catch to his credit over 32 total snaps.

By trading away Davis at the deadline, the Eagles have all but solidified Jalen Carter and Moro Ojomo as their starting defensive tackles not just in 2026 but for the foreseeable future. But behind them? Well, the Eagles have to at bare minimum identify a new DT3 who will play a considerable chunk of snaps each game coming off the bench.

Asked about who will step up and play more now that Davis calls Jacksonville home, Fangio named off the usual suspects plus Leki Fotu, who was just signed to the practice squad on Tuesday, before explaining what he's seen from Ty Robinson so far as a second-year player.

"Yeah, obviously BY has been playing a fair amount. His snaps will obviously go up. Same with Ty, and then we'll have to decide who the fifth guy will be, either [DT] Gabe [Hall] or [DT] Leki [Fotu], who we just signed," Fangio said before turning his attention to Robinson's play in 2026. "He's better than he was last year, but he's improving. Still needs to improve some more."

From a pure technical standpoint, Robinson's game is far closer to Ojomo's than Davis', as at 6-foot-5, 288 pounds, he has the build of a classic 3-tech rusher, but Fangio did note that he's been practicing at the nose tackle spot already, which, in his nickel defense, is more of a 1-technique or even a 2i-tech than a true 0-tech alignment.

On obvious running snaps, sure, give the nod to Byron Young, Hall, or even Fotu, but if the Eagles are going to field the sort of deep, multi-dimensional fronts they have become synonymous with for years to come regardless of who is running the defense long-term, they need to see if Robinson can be part of the future as an interior pass-rushing, or if he's just another body who can be replaced with a Day 3 draft pick.

And last but not least, after letting Sean Mannion call plays from the booth for the first half of the 2026 NFL season, the Eagles need to move their offensive coordinator onto the field, where he can better communicate with his players and fellow coaches.

From a purely football standpoint, it makes sense why some coaches prefer to call plays from high above the sidelines, as they get a better view of the field and can use that extra information to adjust their play calls accordingly. But so far in 2026, that simply hasn't worked out in Mannion's favor, with plays rarely building upon one another to stress opposing defenses and the first-year coach nowhere to be found to talk to quarterbacks about what did or didn't go right from snap to snap.

With Joe Milton more likely than not locked in as QB1 for the rest of the season, Mannion owes it to him to be as hands-on as possible, which is hard to do when he can't look him in the eye at the end of every drive.

Sure, the Eagles just released Hollywood Brown, who never really worked out as a deep threat, but they will likely have DeVonta Smith back in Week 11, and his deep ball connection with Hurts was legendary. Considering Milton doesn't like the "Bazooka Joe" nickname because it too narrowly describes his arm strength, Mannion needs to dial up far more vertical passing concepts to stretch the field and lighten up the boxes for his run game. And to do that, Mannion needs to be on the same page as his quarterback, which is easier to do on the sidelines than in the booth.
