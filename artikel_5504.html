Marketplaces Connect Traders. BLACKSHEEP Connects Makers.

BLACKSHEEP is putting the world's biggest e-commerce players, ultra-fast-fashion platforms and direct-to-consumer retailers on notice.

Traditional marketplaces are built to connect consumers with sellers. BLACKSHEEP is built to go one step further: connecting consumers as close as possible to the manufacturers actually making the products.

By shortening that chain, BLACKSHEEP aims to remove layers of trading, distribution, inventory and traditional retail markup that are normally built into the final price.

Its proposition is simple: challenge consumers to rethink what everyday products should actually cost.

"Retail has spent decades convincing consumers that the price they see is simply what a product costs,"said Pierre Wizman, Founder of BLACKSHEEP.io. "We want to expose the difference between the cost of making something and the price consumers have been conditioned to pay."

True Factory-Direct Pricing: Category Disruption

BLACKSHEEP is now extending the same pricing model across multiple categories:

With $40 cashmere, $9 noise-canceling headphones and apparel starting at just $2, BLACKSHEEP is making an aggressive play for consumers tired of traditional retail markups.

The principle remains the same: bring selling prices as close as possible to the underlying economics of manufacturing.

"We are not trying to sell cheap products," Wizman said. "We are exposing what products actually cost before the traditional retail machine adds its layers."

Beyond pricing, BLACKSHEEP's manufacturing model is designed to address one of retail's biggest structural costs: unsold inventory.

Traditional retailers forecast demand, manufacture large quantities in advance, warehouse them and absorb the cost of what does not sell.

BLACKSHEEP uses demand-led production wherever manufacturing allows it, triggering or guiding production from real customer orders and reducing speculative inventory, warehousing and dead stock.

"Consumers are paying for much more than the product," Wizman added. "They are paying for middlemen, inventory risk, marketing structures and unsold goods. Today, technology allows us to remove a significant part of that obsolete architecture."
