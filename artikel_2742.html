Who Will be the Next USMNT Manager?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

From legitimate candidates to longshot outsiders, we examine which managers have a shot at becoming the next to lead the USMNT.

It was all fun and games when Manchester United were constantly stepping on rakes in their own backyard. I chuckled, I chortled, and I even occasionally laughed out loud at the massive club's ineptitude. I delighted in watching United mire themselves in an inextricable cycle of hiring big name managers only for the players to turn on them, replacing them with sympathetic former stars who started brightly, only for them to demonstrate their lack of tactical and managerial chops. It was objectively hilarious stuff.

But now they've trained their sights on this side of the Atlantic and lured away our precious Mauricio Pochettino, and my schadenfreude has turned to ash in my mouth!

Officially announced as the next man to helm the Red Devils, Pochettino had his farewell with the United States men's national team in its two-legged CONCACAF Nations League thrashing of Haiti. As soon as the curtain fell on the second leg at TQL Stadium in Cincinnati, Poch whisked away to the sunny climes of Liverpool to take the reins of Manchester United in a clash with their fiercest rivals.

Bon voyage, Poch, and don't let the door of Sir Jim Ratcliffe's private jet hit you on the way out.

Now begins the all-too-familiar process of selecting a new head coach. Truthfully, this is a prospect that doesn't fill me with the same level of hopeful optimism as it once did. Part of that owes to being a lifelong New York Jets fan, a sort of inescapable purgatory in which the team selects a coach seemingly at random, and we, the fans, know that whoever is foolhardy enough to take the job is almost guaranteed to land like a wet fart.

Whether you believe that Pochettino was a smashing success or an underachieving idealist, the truth of the matter is that he leaves behind sizable shoes to fill. His hiring represented a home run for the United States Soccer Federation, and he surfed into town on a wave of optimism and possibility. There were some growing pains early on, and some (notably America's favorite problematic uncle Alexi Lalas) wondered publicly if Pochettino ought to be replaced before the World Cup.

In spite of that, the tide turned considerably. Pochettino deserves a massive measure of credit for resetting what had become a toxic culture in the squad following the disastrous Gregg Berhalter 2.0 administration. Vibes at the 2026 World Cup on home soil were fun, at least until BalogunGate turned us into the villains. Pochettino's selections in the last couple of international windows represented an exciting turning of the corner as he sought to incorporate fresh, young, exciting faces of the American game, and gradually phase out the old guard.

So who's the right person to step in and capitalize on the momentum created by the Poch regime? Does the USSF swing once more for the fences with a big-name international manager, or do they revert to type, so to speak, and hire someone with a background in Major League Soccer?

Below, we'll take a look at some of the speculation surrounding this highly-coveted managerial position, and rate the likelihood of each individual getting the gig.

Very few available names in international soccer would represent a marked improvement on the managerial reputation of Pochettino, and Pep Guardiola is one of them.

It looks like the Catalan knew the exact right time to abandon the sinking ship that is Manchester City, but he did it (at least for now) as one of the most successful managers in the history of English soccer. He's accomplished basically all there is at the club level, and it has been widely rumored that his next managerial position would be in the comparatively slow-paced world of international soccer.

The USMNT, full of exciting young up-and-comers as well as a solid core of performers in their primes, would seem to be an enticing prospect for Pep. The other rumored nations like England, Brazil, and Spain have already locked down top-level managerial talent for the long term, so Pep would be tasked with choosing a second-tier position, such as that of the USMNT, if he's looking for a big payday.

What an opportunity this would be for Guardiola! Given a full World Cup cycle to work with, he could mold a young squad in his image, instill in it a mix of tactical awareness and swagger, and finally lead it into its rightful place as a bonafide soccer nation!

The only problem is that we've never seen what Pep is capable of when he's not given virtually unlimited resources to work with. He only really chooses jobs where he's almost guaranteed to succeed, and even then he's not exactly blown the doors off in European competition. Bayern hired him to win them a Champions League (if you fail to win the Bundesliga with the Bavarians you run the risk of being put in the stocks in the town square) but he never managed it. He only accomplished the feat with a Manchester City squad that was financially doped up to its ears, and a Barcelona squad that boasted the likes of Lionel Messi, Xavi Hernandez, and Andres Iniesta.

There's little doubt that Guardiola would be massive for the USMNT. He'd draw in countless soccer casuals, and bring a level of confidence and respect to the squad that has occasionally been missed. But I don't think the position is prestigious or well-compensated enough for him, so I doubt we'll be seeing the Bald Fraud on the USMNT sideline anytime soon.

You know when you interview for a highly-specific job as a young professional, and they still want to see years of experience on your resume? "I'm 22, how can I have six years of programming experience?!?"

Well, Big BJ Callaghan has got a level of experience with the USMNT that all these other candidates can only dream of. During that weird little interregnum after the 2022 World Cup when we weren't sure if Berhalter would be reappointed as coach, Callaghan took the reins of the senior U.S. squad on an interim basis.

He posted a record of four wins, three draws, and zero losses in his three-month stint with the USMNT. His team whooped Mexico 3-0 in the semifinals of the 2023 CONCACAF Nations League, and easily handled a feisty Canada 2-0 in the final. His only loss came on penalties against Panama in the semifinals of that year's Gold Cup.

Callaghan's Nashville SC took MLS by storm this year, setting a single-season record for most points earned. Their defensive solidity has been beyond impressive, with the Honky Tonk Boyz conceding a paltry 25 goals over the 34-game season.

A product of the Jim Curtain/Berhalter coaching tree, Callaghan emphasizes high-tempo, possession-based soccer, well-suited to the physical acumen of the current crop of USMNT players. Big BJ also seems to be well-liked as a manager. On the sideline he cuts an unemotional figure in the vein of Pochettino, exuding quiet confidence and stability to his players.

But would hiring Callaghan be seen as a step backward for the USMNT? Pochettino was regarded as an established manager with an impressive pedigree, and he used that cache to reset a culture that was in decay. He publicly feuded with Christian Pulisic, arguably the U.S.'s most influential player. Would Callaghan have the chutzpah to go to war against the big names in the U.S. player pool if the situation calls for it?

I have a feeling that Callaghan might be No. 2 on USSF's list of candidates to replace Pochettino. But, for his sake, I would hope that Callaghan's signing won't feel like a tremendous letdown to those who were dreaming bigger.

Beware, the false power of name recognition! For a long time, Spanish manager Roberto Martinez traded on his improbable FA Cup title with Wigan Athletic in 2013. He eventually traded his way up to coach Everton, Belgium, and most recently, Portugal.

Martinez was also a regular on ESPN's coverage of the 2010 and 2014 World Cups, making him a more recognizable name to U.S. soccer fans. But there are plenty who would doubt the assertion that this notoriety translated to on-field success. Case in point: Martinez is widely-regarded as having wasted Belgium's golden generation, as the Red Devils boasted the likes of Romelu Lukaku, Eden Hazard, Vincent Kompany, and Kevin DeBruyne, all in their primes during Martinez's 2016-22 reign.

The Spaniard gained a reputation as a coach who could breeze through qualifying for major tournaments, but faltered at the final hurdles. His Belgium side famously notched a 2-1 win over Brazil at the 2018 World Cup before falling to eventual champions France in the semifinal.

The third-place finish in 2018 was followed up with Belgium even achieving a No. 1 world ranking for long stretches of the cycle leading up to the 2022 World Cup. But Martinez failed to incorporate fresh players into the tactical setup and were, embarrassingly, eliminated in the group stage of the 2022 tournament.

Martinez must be really good at job interviews, since, on the back of this colossal failure, he talked himself into becoming Portugal's newest manager in January 2023. He led the squad to victory in the 2025 UEFA Nations League, and his side became the quickest ever Portugal team to qualify for the World Cup in 2026, managing the feat in only four games. But the World Cup went as the World Cup tends to go for Bobby Martinez: Portugal were unceremoniously bounced by a resilient Spain in the Round of 16.

Some claim that he let himself be bullied by the aging superstar that is Cristiano Ronaldo, that the team would've performed much better had it not been beholden to a 41-year-old who limped around the front line, waiting haplessly for service.

As Pochettino has made hay blooding exciting youth players in recent windows with the USMNT, one wonders if Martinez would fall back on the old guard, waiting too long to phase them out until they all run out of gas. The U.S. lacks individual superstars to make the most of the positional fluidity that Martinez emphasizes in the final third. There is no DeBruyne or Ronaldo-shaped "get out of jail free" card waiting in the USMNT roster.

While it wouldn't shock me for the USSF to give Martinez an interview, I hope beyond hope that the decision makers will see through the long, flimsy resume and not give Bobby his third high profile international gig.

LET HER DO BOTH! This idea has circulated online in the last few days and was notably posited by former USMNT player Kyle Martino in a television appearance.

Emma Hayes represented a massive get for the U.S. women when she signed from Chelsea in May 2024. Her coaching career actually began in the United States, dating back to when she coached the Long Island Lady Riders of the USL-W in 2002, before coaching the Iona Gaels women's college team from 2003 to 2005.

While men coaching women's teams is incredibly common, the opposite is exceedingly rare.

Marie-Louise Eta was appointed interim coach of Union Berlin in April of this year, becoming the first woman ever to coach a senior men's team in one of the "Big Five" European leagues. Despite the barrage of sexist abuse that came her way online, she acquitted herself well, winning two, losing two, and drawing one of her five matches in charge.

The United States has long been at the vanguard of developing women's talent in soccer, so it's only right that it becomes the first country to appoint a woman as the head coach of their senior men's team. Hayes more than has the tactical chops the job, and her no-nonsense, measured attitude would represent continuity with Pochettino's stoic managerial style.

But the idea of one person coaching two major programs at the same time is plain ridiculous. These are both high pressure, full time gigs that require 110% of a coach's attention and effort. Evaluating the player pools alone requires tons of travel, interaction with countless coaches and scouts, and hours of watching matches from obscure leagues, kicking the tires on multitudes of promising youngsters.

I for one would love to see this happen. But unless Hayes was inclined to leave the U.S. women in the lurch and coach the men exclusively, it seems more like a pie-in-the-sky than an actual possibility.

Old Wilfried, once one of MLS's hottest managers, has got a little bit of stink on him after a failed tenure at Scotland's Celtic FC earlier this year. That said, he's demonstrated that he's got tremendous tactical chops by converting the Columbus Crew into a possession-heavy juggernaut, as well as one of MLS's most entertaining teams.

I think he would have been in a better position for the USMNT job had he not gone to Europe, but his ill-fated time in Scotland should not sour our opinion about the Frenchman's managerial capabilities. The dynamism and proactiveness of Nancy's teams would continue the momentum of the Pochettino era, and his personal charisma would be easy to sell to the American soccer consuming populace. Besides, anyone who gets in a public altercation with Joey Saputo is automatically cool in my book.

The former Bayern Munich and Germany boss would represent a Pochettino-level swing from USSF. Having impressed at both Hoffenheim and RB Leipzig as a young man, Julian Nagelsmann never quite lived up to his wunderkind billing when it came to leading Bayern and the German national team.

Nagelsmann is also a reportedly prickly character, never shying away from public feuds with top players or assistant coaches. His personal life has also been a little messy over the past few years, as he divorced his wife and then began to very publicly date a journalist for one of Germany's major soccer publications, BILD. Players reportedly grew suspicious that the team's tactics might be leaked to the press as a result of Nagelsmann's romantic involvement.

Nagelsmann is an adept tactician, surely, but it's safe to say that USSF might prefer someone a little less cantankerous in the role.

I would do my best Sir Alex Ferguson impression and kick a boot across the room as hard as I could if this appointment came to pass.

Much as Martinez is accused of having squandered Belgium's Golden Generation, so too has Gareth Southgate been panned for failing to win anything as the coach of a seriously stacked England team. He helmed the Three Lions from 2016 to 2024, and did alright by getting England to a World Cup semifinal and two European Championship finals, but failed when push came to shove.

Critics point to his "handbrake on" style of risk-averse management, his failure to make tactically-astute substitutions, and his stale personality as evidence of his inability to lead England to the promised land. His sort of defense-first, milquetoast soccer is the very last thing we need on this side of the Atlantic.

Jesse Marsch wants you to know that he's NOT interested in the USMNT job. Oh, you didn't ask? Well, he wants you to know anyway.

And he's definitely NOT still mad about having been passed over for the job twice. Don't say or tell anyone that he's mad, OK?

Many idiots like me assumed that Marsch was shoo-in for the job in 2023. His resume was about as well-rounded as any American coach going, but Berhalter was, oddly, rehired as coach. This left a sour taste in Marsch's mouth, as he reportedly turned down a job offer by Leicester City to keep himself available for the gig.

Marsch then took the top job for the Canadian men's national team. In doing so, Marsch must've sworn a blood oath to renounce everything and everyone American, besmirching the name of his home country and letting the world know that he now bleeds maple syrup and Timbits.

But I, for one, think it would be tremendously funny if Marsch took the USMNT job and had to backtrack on his years of potshots at the USA. Come on, Jesse, do it for the memes!
