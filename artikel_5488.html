‘Half philanthropy and half protest’: Noah Wyle and Figs pair up to erase $200 million in real patients’ medical debt

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

After decades of playing a doctor on the long-running medical drama ER and today’s The Pitt, Noah Wyle has learned a thing or two about what it’s like to be a health care professional and the challenges patients face.

Now, he is taking that passion off-screen, partnering up with popular scrubs maker Figs with a goal to relieve at least $200 million of medical debt in the U.S.

“In my countless interactions with health care professionals over the decades, one thing that stays with me is how they try to do everything right for a patient while the system makes it harder than it should be,” Wyle said in a statement. “Medical debt follows people home, creates long-term devastation, and changes the whole experience of care.”

Americans owe at least $220 billion in medical debt, according to a 2024 KFF-Peterson analysis of federal survey data, and having insurance offers less protection than many assume. In a new Urban Institute study backed by the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation, more than one-quarter of U.S. adults said they or a family member owed medical debt at the end of 2025, including 26% of those with health coverage. Hospitals were the most common creditor.

Separately, a Commonwealth Fund survey released in September found that roughly one in three working-age adults with private insurance are paying off medical bills over time, with some pulling from savings or spending less on food and rent to keep up. The issue has also come up on-screen in Wyle’s shows. In one episode of The Pitt, a construction worker who already owes $100,000 in medical bills lands in the ER with serious diabetes complications after rationing his insulin because he can’t afford it.

“Trust is the bedrock of the relationship between patients and clinicians like nurses, pharmacists, and doctors, and that trust is increasingly undermined by the burden, and even fear, of medical debt,” Allison Sesso, president and CEO of nonprofit Undue Medical Debt, said in a statement.

During the next three years, Wyle and Figs will each donate $1 million to Undue Medical Debt, which wipes out, on average, $100 of qualifying medical debt for every $1 donated. The commitment is projected to relieve at least $200 million of medical debt for over 150,000 Americans. The first round of relief is set to take place in time for the upcoming holidays, according to the release.

Wyle, Figs, and Undue Medical Debt over the same time frame will work to develop and share practical resources for health care professionals to help patients navigate affordability. This builds on Figs’ and Wyle’s broader “Healthcare Is Human” advocacy work, focused on addressing systemic pressures making it challenging for health care workers to care for their patients.

“This is an act of half philanthropy and half protest, to underscore what the need is, and see if we can’t get our arms around some better solutions,” Wyle told CNN.

Wyle also serves as Figs’ global brand ambassador and has joined the company on Capitol Hill to push for policies supporting health care workers. He wore the brand’s first black-tie scrubs to the Emmy Awards last year.

Figs, a publicly traded company cofounded by CEO Trina Spear, reported third-quarter revenue of $190.7 million earlier this month, up nearly 26% from a year earlier.

Sydney Lake is an associate editor at Fortune, where she writes and edits news for the publication's global news desk.
