Mario Van Peebles, Abderrahmane Sissako Set for Ivory Coast’s SICA as African Content Economy Takes Center Stage

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Financing, distribution and Africa’s creative economy are set to focus discussions at 2026's SICA market in Abidjan

American actor-director Mario Van Peebles (“New Jack City”), Oscar-nominated Mauritanian filmmaker Abderrahmane Sissako (“Timbuktu”), Nigerian filmmaker Kunle Afolayan (“Aníkúlápó”) and French actor-director Jean-Pascal Zadi (“Simply Black”) are among the industry figures set to attend the fourth edition of Ivory Coast’s International Salon for Audiovisual Content of Abidjan (SICA).

Taking place Nov. 26-28 at the Sofitel Hôtel Ivoire, the market expects to welcome more than 5,000 visitors and professionals from 40 countries, with a focus on the economics of content creation and cultural diplomacy. The gathering comes amid considerable upheaval in Africa’s media landscape. Pay-TV subscriptions in sub-Saharan Africa fell 2.8% to 33.2 million in 2025, according to research firm Omdia. Following its acquisition of South African pay-TV operator MultiChoice in September 2025, French media group Canal+ shut down MultiChoice’s streaming platform Showmax in April, citing financial losses.

At SICA, a panel on adapting to streaming platforms and new distribution models will bring together representatives of Ivorian networks RTI, NCI, A+ Ivoire, Life TV and 7info alongside Netflix and broadcasters from Senegal, Cameroon, Benin and Canada. A separate discussion will examine how regulation can strengthen the market for local content on national television channels.

The theatrical sector, meanwhile, offers encouraging signs. According to the European Audiovisual Observatory’s Focus 2026 report, national film releases in Anglophone West Africa doubled from 58 in 2024 to 117 in 2025, with Nollywood’s regional market share rising from 46% to 50%.

Mobilizing capital to sustain local production will be central to SICA’s program this year. Producer Virginie Besson-Silla (“Lucy”), distributor Claire Diao and Meta’s Balkissa Idé Siddo will join Ivory Coast’s Minister of Culture and Francophonie Françoise Remarck for a panel titled “The Creative Economy: Financing, Production and Distribution.”

Besson-Silla and Idé Siddo will also be part of a panel with Ivorian filmmaker Philippe Lacôte (“Night of the Kings”) and representatives of financial institutions, including the African Export-Import Bank, to discuss financing Africa’s creative economy.

A memorandum of understanding aimed at facilitating financing of the audiovisual sector is set to be signed between Ivory Coast’s Agency for Media Support and Development and the commercial NSIA Banque Côte d’Ivoire, while in a masterclass, Sissako and South African producer Joel Phiri will tackle financing and co-production in challenging contexts.

SICA’s pitching competition, with Besson-Silla and Zadi as its patrons, will span film, television series, documentaries, animation and student projects. Running alongside the competition, the SICA Business Hub, launched last year, will connect African audiovisual creators and producers with international broadcasters, distributors, buyers and investors.

With Japan as this year’s country of honor, Kazuki Sunami of CoMix Wave Films, the animation studio behind Makoto Shinkai’s “Your Name” and “Suzume,” will deliver the inaugural conference address. Japanese media specialist Masayoshi Isago will join Ivorian filmmakers Abel Kouamé and Adja Soro for a discussion on animation, while digital entertainment specialist Masato Tanaka will participate in SICA Campus, the market’s educational program for students.

Congolese animation and visual effects specialist Sidney Kombo-Kintombo and Van Peebles will deliver keynotes on Africa’s creative economy and cultural diplomacy, respectively, with dedicated sessions exploring animation, VFX, gaming and emerging technologies.

Afolayan, Senegalese broadcaster 7TV’s Maïmouna Faye and Ivorian producer Charly Kodjo will explore ways to strengthen South-South cooperation. Discussions on the final day will focus on Africa-Asia partnerships and how film and television can strengthen Africa’s global profile.

In Ivory Coast, where cinema admissions surpassed 400,000 in 2025, according to the Ministry of Communication, connecting local productions with domestic audiences remains a challenge. American films accounted for 82% of admissions in 2024, compared with just 6% for Ivorian films, according to the country’s National Film Office.

Accordingly, seeking to engage a wider audience, the parallel SICA OFF program runs Nov. 25-Dec. 6, with Sissako serving as patron of its opening ceremony. It features a Japanese film festival opening with “Your Name,” premieres including Sissako’s immersive film “Artou” and Ivorian-Congolese production “Naïké,” and heritage screenings of Van Peebles’ “Baadasssss!” and a newly restored copy of Henri Duparc’s 1972 Ivorian drama “Abusuan.”

More than 100 screenings will be offered at a reduced ticket price of 2,000 CFA Francs ($3.50) from Dec. 2-6 to encourage cinema attendance.

Van Peebles will serve as patron of the market’s closing ceremony, featuring a tribute to Ivorian cinema pioneer Timité Bassori, the SICA d’Or awards recognizing excellence in television programming and presenting, and the pitching competition awards.
