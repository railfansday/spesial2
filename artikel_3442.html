11 Best Historical Fantasies Since Outlander

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It's been five years since the last new Outlander book released, and historical fantasy readers will likely be waiting a bit longer for Book 10. The series has had an impressive run, with the first book released back in 1991. Interest in Diana Gabaldon's time-traveling work of historical fiction remains strong, but it's far from the only phenomenal historical fantasy book out there.

Publishing has changed quite a bit since Outlander first hit the scene in 1991. Not only has the TV series brought a whole new level of attention to Outlander itself, but historical fantasy as a genre has continued to grow and expand. There are so many excellent books that blend magic and historical settings, giving Outlander ​​​​​​fans plenty of reading material between books.

Since Claire first journeyed to the past, we've seen countless stories that blend historical settings with fantasy elements. Some, like This Is How You Lose the Time War, feature their own wibbly-wobbly time travel goodness. Others stay tethered to one historical setting, but infuse it with magic and mystery. Regardless of what you're looking for, there's a historical fantasy book that will tide you over until the next Outlander book and beyond.

Like Outlander before it, this Hugo Award-winning novel has been adapted for TV, giving the story a whole new level of popularity and cultural relevance. But before BBC brought its characters to the screen, Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell had already captivated readers with its charming rival magicians and fast-paced storytelling. And believe it or not, this was Susanna Clarke's debut novel!

Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell is set during the Napoleonic Wars, in a world where magic is thought to be a thing of the past. But Mr. Norrell reveals that magic is alive and well in him, followed not long after by Jonathan Strange. What follows is a story steeped in magic, humor, and compelling world-building that brings its alternate historical setting to life. With over 4 million copies sold, this is an absolute must-read in the historical fantasy space.

The Napoleonic Wars, but with dragons, is one of those pitches that doesn't sound like it should work. Yet somehow, His Majesty's Dragon proves that it does. In this alternate history, Britain and France duke it out atop the backs of dragons. A New York Times bestseller and winner of the Locus Award, this is the beginning of Novik's beloved Temeraire series.

In this first installment, readers meet Captain Will Laurence, who suddenly finds himself thrust into the world of dragons and their riders. It offers a fresh take on the bond between dragon and rider, giving readers beautiful writing, engaging characters, and a page-turning plot. Masterfully blending historical fiction and fantasy, His Majesty's Dragon is a must-read for historical fantasy enthusiasts and dragon lovers alike.

You can purchase His Majesty's Dragon here.

When it first released in 2011, it felt like The Night Circus was everywhere. And given its New York Times bestseller status, that was at least partly true. The book pulls on the star-crossed lover trope, adding in a mysterious traveling circus and rival magicians for a story that spans time, place, and genre.

Set in the late 1800s to early 1900s, The Night Circus spans much of the lives of its core protagonists. Two rival magicians each select and train a young pupil, destined to one day battle one another. As their stories unfold, they fall in love, despite the circumstances that would pit them against each other. Packed with romance, longing, and wonderfully surreal magic, The Night Circus feels like a fairytale and a Shakespeare tragedy all rolled into one.

Before it became a viral social media sensation, This Is How You Lose the Time War snagged a Hugo, Nebula, and Locus Award for best novella. It's not easy to craft a compelling time-travel story in a short space, but El-Mohtar and Gladstone pulled it off in this historical sci-fi/fantasy hybrid.

Told through letters between rival agents on opposite sides of the titular time war, this story is part epic time-travel saga, part love story. Though more heavily science fiction than fantasy, this book defies exact genre confines with its magical, lyrical adventure through space and time. Lyrically written and surprisingly action-packed, This Is How You Lose the Time War continues to captivate readers who love historical fiction, fantasy, and sci-fi... not to mention a good star-crossed love story.

You can purchase This Is How You Lose the Time War here.

Practical Magic is, of course, an iconic story about witches, sisterhood, and love gone wrong. So it's not surprising that the prequel, released in 2020, became an instant bestseller. But many readers also consider this the best book in the entire series due to its combination of stunning prose and powerfully rendered characters. Magic Lessons offers a trip back to the 1600s to uncover how the Owens women became cursed in the first place, shedding light on the magical legacy that would bring us the beloved movie.

Maria Owens is a compelling protagonist, a young witch abandoned first by her birth family and later by her first love. By the time she lays the curse that will devastate generations of Owens women, Hoffman deftly makes you understand exactly what brings her to that place. It is beautifully written, full of nuanced characters and just the right touch of magic.

Whether you were browsing the bestseller lists or scrolling Bookstagram, The Invisible Life of Addie LaRue was impossible to miss. Released in 2020, this book landed a truly staggering number of Best Books mentions and continues to pop up in bookish circles to date. Schwab's impressive writing and the book's heartbreaking, time-traveling premise are central to this book's ongoing longevity.

Our story begins in the 1700s, when Addie LaRue first makes the deal that lets her live forever, but be remembered by no one. Spanning both time and location, this is a unique story of identity, connection, and longing. It's a page-turner in the truest sense, but also crafts an ironically unforgettable character in the titular Addie. If you've yet to pick up this historical fantasy, it's still well worth revisiting.

You can purchase The Invisible Life of Addie LaRue here.

This historical fantasy is set in an alternate version of the late 1800s. It centers on sisters who join the suffragette movement to help women gain a fraction of power, but wind up working to reawaken the lost magic that made them powerful all along. During its release year, it snagged several Best Books of the Year nominations and hit the New York Times bestseller list, and for good reason.

The Once and Future Witches is a beautifully written, compelling story that grips you from start to finish. A powerful homage to feminine power and the men who seek to crush it, this book is steeped in magic and packed with plot twists you won't see coming. If you love Outlander for Claire's spirited efforts to stand her ground in her past and present, you'll root for the sisters who strive to reclaim lost magic and the old ways in this historical fantasy novel.

You can purchase The Once and Future Witches here.

Though it's often classified as more traditional historical fiction, there's no denying the tinge of magic in Emilia Hart's stunning debut, Weyward. Set across three different generations of women haunted by their links to witchcraft, this book was an instant bestseller and winner of two Goodreads Choice Awards.

The story takes place in three timelines, set in the 1600s, 1940s, and modern day. Each woman faces unique challenges that harken back to the Weyward women's magical roots. The fantasy elements here might be a light touch, but this is nevertheless a beautifully crafted story about female power and resistance that is right at home on any historical fantasy reader's shelf right alongside Outlander.

There's a reason readers were beside themselves when Penner finally confirmed a sequel to The Lost Apothecary. This book is part historical fiction, part mystery, and part magical realism, weaving a story that left many readers desperate for more. Its status as a historical fantasy is debatable, as our apothecary is more of a traditional healer than a full-on witch, but the book very much reads like a story of witches.

The Lost Apothecary uses a dual-timeline setup, with part of our story in 18th-century London and the other half following a present-day historian. As Caroline uncovers Nella's past, the two women's stories become linked and echo off one another in surprising, magical ways. A beautifully written and bestselling debut, The Lost Apothecary is a must-read for Outlander fans and anyone who loves a good story about women's wrongs.

You can purchase The Lost Apothecary here.

She Who Became the Sun is the first book in the Radiant Emperor duology. It was both an international bestseller and a finalist for several major fantasy awards during its release year. Set in an alternate China during the rise of the Ming Dynasty, this book follows a young woman who takes her brother's identity to join the rebellion against the Mongols.

This story is an epic reimagining of history, one steeped in political intrigue and questions about fate, family, and identity. Its writing is memorable and poetic, tempering the absolutely devastating, epic story that doesn't pull punches. Its magic lies largely in its reimagined history, but She Who Became the Sun is nevertheless both a solid epic fantasy and a powerful work of historical fiction.
