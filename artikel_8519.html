Interview at least three buyer’s agents, and negotiate the written agreement with the one you pick before you tour a single home. That agreement sets your agent’s pay, how long you’re committed, and what it takes to get out, and agents now ask for your signature before any showing.

Unless the seller agrees to cover it, you pay the fee that agreement sets. The time to bargain over it is before you sign, while the next agent on your list is still an option.

There’s no standard fee. Under National Association of Realtors rules in force since August 2024, the agreement must state the fee as a set amount or rate, never as whatever the seller happens to offer. The agent can’t collect more than that from anyone, the seller included, and the agreement must say that commissions aren’t set by law and are negotiable.

Redfin’s sales data put the average buyer’s agent commission at 2.42% of the price in the third quarter of 2025, so an agent who opens at 3% is asking above that average.

Get names from friends who recently bought homes, then pick three agents who can name several homes they helped buyers close on in your neighborhood and price range in the past year. Ask each how they’d handle a bidding war, an inspection that turns up problems, and a lender’s appraisal that values the home below your offer.

Picture a buyer shopping for a $400,000 home who passes on the first agent’s six-month agreement at 3%, a $12,000 fee, and signs with the third for 60 days at 2.25%, or $9,000, which is $3,000 less.
