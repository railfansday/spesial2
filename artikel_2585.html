Early struggles haunt Garaway football in Final 4 loss to South Range

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

MINERVA — What could have gone wrong, did for Garaway in its Division VI state semifinal with Canfield South Range.

For starters, running back Matthew Frey re-aggravated his left knee injury in the first quarter and didn't return. In the second, receiver A.J. Miller took a hard hit to the head — which drew a 15-yard penalty — on a catch over the middle that sidelined him.

Then there were defensive struggles against sizable and multi-talented South Range offense, led by 6-4, 205-pound quarterback Nick Toy, top target Gavin Hiscox and four running backs weighing between 185 and 200 pounds.

Those were compounded by three first-half turnovers that proved the most detrimental of all in a 42-6 loss on a windy, wet and frigid night on Nov. 27 at Minerva High School.

Garaway's magical postseason ended one win shy of the school's first state final berth.

Garaway senior safety Wyatt Reifenschneider, a two-time All-East District pick and part of seven playoff wins the past two seasons, said the team failed to match South Range's physical play.

The 6-4, 195-pound Toy, Offensive Player of the Year in the Northeast Inland District, showed his dual threat ability throughout the half.

His 48-yard completion down the sideline on the opening drive set up his 8-yard touchdown run; a 36-yard completion to a double-covered Hiscox, who made a leaping catch, set up the Raiders' third score.

A strip of Grady Miller and ensuing Raider recovery occurred two plays later at the Pirates' 21-yard line. It set up Toy's 14-yard scoring strike on a slant to Tommy Titus. Suddenly, it was 28-0 with 8:46 still showing in the half.

Toy lived up to his play-making reputation, but Garaway's offensive struggles didn't control the bleeding. Five first-half possessions ended in turnovers and punts.

"It's very hard (to stop momentum)," Reifenschneider said. "You just try to stay connected with the defense, but that's a really good team."

Miller, who was intercepted twice in the first half, admitted he struggled with the raw conditions, especially with his grip on the ball.

"It kind of just unraveled," Miller said. "We've just got to prepare better next time and just do what we've got to do."

Garaway coach Jason Wallick and Pirate players alike lamented the struggle to sustain drives, protect the ball and tackling the biggest backfield it has faced. All were points of emphasis during what Wallick considered a productive week of preparation.

But the turnovers and lack of production were the killers. So were two punts covering a combined total of 20 yards. The first, on Garaway's opening possession, resulted in Hiscox's 29-yard return that led to the second touchdown. The second boot, from the South Range 30-yard line in an attempt to pin the Raiders deep, came with the score 14-0.

All told, it was the only time in 15 games the Pirates scored less than 13 points.

"And that's the lesson to be learned from this," Wallick said. "You can't make those kind of mistakes and expect to win at this level."

Garaway, which finished 11-4, trailed 35-0 and faced a running clock to start the third quarter. Its lone scoring chance in the first half was thwarted by a red zone interception by Harley Booms deep in South Range territory just before half.

The writing was on the wall by then. But a 65-yard scoring drive, capped by a 4-yard Miller touchdown run, on Garaway's first possession of the third, showed the team's mettle on an otherwise miserable night.

It was emblematic of the team's resiliency, something of which Wallick was sure to remind his players in their final postgame gathering on the field.

"These guys have fought all year," Wallick said. "That (drive) was something we thought we would be able to do more. We just didn't execute in the first half."

The Pirates, with several underclassmen in key roles to start the season, won five straight games after a 6-3 start. That came with an asterisk, however — two losses came in succession to unbeaten Indian Valley and Claymont. The other came to a Girard squad that finished 12-1.

Nonetheless, the team needed to rally. It did just that in a 35-34 comeback win against backyard rival Ridgewood in the regular-season finale, when it fended off a late General scoring drive in the final minute.

It gave the team valuable confidence entering the Region 23 postseason. The Pirates never scored less than 29 points in those games, as its last five wins came against teams with a combined 43 wins. Two were on the road in consecutive weeks.

Miller, Co-Offensive Player of the Year in the East District, said the team became more unified when it was most needed. That begam with accountability, he said.

"That's what we were supposed to do," Miller said. "(The Ridgewood win) was really big. It was the boost we needed to get us going."

Reifenschneider said he hopes the underclassmen learn from the mishaps against the Raiders and lean on that experience in the future. Miller, top target Trey Miller, running back Lewey Soehnlen and inside linebacker Thomas Grimm are among a talented core of key players due to return.

"These guys have no quit," Reifenschneider said. "I love them and wouldn't want to do it with anybody else. God gave us this great ability, this great opportunity."
