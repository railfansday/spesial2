North Carolina TE Jelani Thurman gives away 45 turkeys before Thanksgiving, pays tribute to late mom

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

North Carolina tight end Jelani Thurman is honoring his late mother, former WNBA All-Star Kara Braxton, this Thanksgiving holiday. The Carolina star gave away 45 turkeys ahead of the big day Thursday, honoring his mother's old jersey number. Braxton wore No. 45 with all three WNBA organizations she played for (Detroit/Tulsa Shock, Phoenix Mercury, and New York Liberty) over the course of her nine-year professional career.

Braxton, who was just 43 years old, passed away in February following a tragic car accident. Her son, Thurman, is in the midst of his fourth collegiate football season (his first at North Carolina). The Fairburn, Ga. native is enjoying a career-best campaign, as he's hauled in 51 catches for 610 yards and eight scores this season.

"She taught me to always go hard. There's one goal. You know what you need to do," Thurman said earlier this year, when asked about advice his mom would give him. Honestly, with her going to a championship, she told me about some of the experiences she had and some of the things she had to do to get there.

With one regular season game remaining, Thurman is second on his team in receiving yards, and leads the team with eight touchdowns. No other Carolina receiver has hauled in more than three touchdowns this season. North Carolina heads into its regular season finale with a 5-6 (2-5) record, needing a win over rival NC State to clinch bowl eligibility for the first time under head coach Bill Belichick.

Thurman isn't the only college football player giving back this Thanksgiving. Florida WR Vernell Brown III also gave away turkeys to families ahead of the holiday in partnership with his VB3 Family Foundation. Sunday afternoon, Brown gave away 100 turkey dinners ahead of the holiday at an event at the Martin Luther King Jr. Multipurpose Center. The event was due to run for two hours, but 100 families claimed the turkeys in just 13 minutes, according to the Gainesville Sun.

USF WR Mudia Reuben is making an even bigger difference, as he is helping fund the Nigeria Water Project to build water wells and help deliver clean drinking water. So far, the project has completed four wells – known as boreholes – with 45,000 liters of water on per borehole. All told, they have brought cleaner water to more than 10,000 people.

The funding comes from both donations and Reuben's NIL earnings. Two more boreholes have also been drilled and are nearing completion, according to the Nigeria Water Project's website, as part of the initiative.
