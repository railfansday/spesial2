Mauricio Pochettino endured the worst possible start to life as Manchester United manager as his side were thrashed 4-0 by fierce rivals Liverpool.

United set the tone for a disastrous afternoon early as they went a man down inside eight minutes, with Leny Yoro bundling into Alexander Isak as the last man to be shown a straight red card.

Dominik Szoboszlai fired in from the resulting free-kick to ensure Yoro’s blunder was fully punished, and Liverpool doubled their lead three minutes later when Florian Wirtz excellently improvised a volley to score.

Rio Ngumoha got a deserved goal after the break to put the game to bed, converting from Bradley Barcola’s cross, before Victor Munoz put the cherry on a ruthless performance for the hosts late on.

Mauricio Pochettino endured a dismal start to his tenure at Manchester United as Liverpool ran riot in a 4-0 victory against their relegation-threatened rivals.

A whirlwind week for the former Argentina international ended in familiar disappointment for the struggling visitors, whose hopes of a new manager bounce were decisively crushed during a nightmare opening 13 minutes at Anfield.

Dominik Szoboszlai put the hosts ahead with a fierce free-kick after defender Leny Yoro was sent off for denying a goalscoring opportunity inside just eight minutes, before Florian Wirtz’s smart finish quickly put Pochettino’s side further behind.

“Sometimes when we are two goals up we can get a bit complacent really. We kept our heads in the game and started controlling it and in the second half we took the game by the scruff of the neck and showed the mentality. We didn't slack off,” said Ngumoha on Match of the Day.

“As fans they always want us to score chances and we know we should have scored more. Creating those chances is important as well. It's about keeping a cool head and keeping composed and they will come."

“I just like exciting other people really, when I show everyone what I can do and get everyone off their seats. Hopefully I can keep going and keep my head on the floor and stay grounded,” he added.

“It's 10 vs 11 isn’t it, you come to a place like Anfield, it's a tough game anyway. Then you lose a man, it makes it hard. I can't fault the boys for their fight in the game. We take the pain and move on,” continues Mainoo.

“I wouldn't say it all goes out the window. You can still take the things you did in training into the game, but it makes it 10 times harder with one less man in a situation like that.

“It's hard, it's painful. But no-one's coming to save us. We have to work hard, keeping digging, keep working, keep fighting. It will all come round,” he added.

A quick word from Rio Ngumoha on Sky too...

“When I come up against any defender really, I like to take them on. A bit of fearlessness really. It was great to get the three points.

“It's good to get a goal. Liverpool vs Man Utd is the biggest game in England. To do it over someone like Michael Owen, I'm very happy,” he adds, referencing the fact that he’s become the youngest player to ever score in this fixture.

“This doesn't define us you know? The game's been taken out of our hands by a red card that I don't think can be a red card,” says Kobbie Mainoo at full-time.

“We have to come together, the games are coming thick and fast. We have to use the pain to bring us together.

On the red card, he adds: “No chance. The ball's already with the goalkeeper. They're both running full speed. What can we do? It is what it is."

“When the red card happened you take advantage of it immediately and score. Then it feels like a jab and a knock-out punch straight away,” says Daniel Sturridge on Sky.

“Liverpool took advantage and took over the game straight away when the red card happened.

"Liverpool will be happy, the red card completely changed the game but ultimately it was a very good performance,” he added.

Pochettino is the first manager in Premier League history to see his team concede twice and receive a red card in the first half of his first match in charge of a club.

He also suffered the joint-heaviest defeat by a Manchester United manager in his first game (0-4), along with a defeat by the same scoreline for Lal Hilditch in October 1926.

“Really good, the atmosphere after the game was so good. We could enjoy it and just a nice afternoon,” said Florian Wirtz on Sky at full-time.

“I feel very good. It's also because the team develops and gets better. being part of this you also get better and I'm enjoying that,” he explained.

“It was definitely important today, also the table situation. A lot of teams at the top didn't win this weekend so it's good we can use the chance. The red card influenced the game of course, but we take that. We earned that because of the run of Alex [Isak], so it's very good.

“Everybody knew it was a chance to make some points good. I'm happy we used this chance,” he added as he finished.

Both teams will play their next games in the Champions League in midweek.

Liverpool will face Club Brugge at home on Wednesday, while United take on Sporting in Lisbon at the same time.
