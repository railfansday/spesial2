Billionaire Collector Ken Griffin Loans Three Dinosaur Fossils to Pittsburgh Museum

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Kenneth C. Griffin, the billionaire collector known for buying everything from Abstract Expressionist paintings to rare copies of the US Constitution, will loan three dinosaur fossils to Pittsburgh’s Carnegie Museum of Natural History.

The fossils—nicknamed “Daisy,” “Lucky,” and “Lucy”—are all sauropods, according to the Financial Times, which also reported that Griffin will gift the museum $5 million in support of its education programming. The gift will also enable the museum to launch a paleontology lab that will be viewable to the public every day. “Daisy, Lucky and Lucy offer an extraordinary window into our planet’s history,” Griffin said in a statement. “I am proud to partner with the Carnegie Museum of Natural History to share these remarkable dinosaurs with the public and give visitors a front-row seat to the work of paleontologists.”

All three fossils date to the Jurassic Period and were found between 2023 and 2025 in Washakie County, Wyoming, per the Financial Times. No financial value for these fossils has ever been publicly reported, but it’s possible that they are not even the most expensive ones owned by Griffin, a mainstay of the ARTnews Top 200 Collectors list since 2007.

In 2024, he memorably purchased a stegosaurus nicknamed “Apex” at a Sotheby’s auction in 2024. When it appeared there, the house billed it as the second-most complete Stegosaurus fossil ever discovered. Griffin bought it for $44.6 million, a record at the time for the most expensive dinosaur fossil ever brought to a public auction. Not long after buying “Apex,” Griffin loaned the fossil to the American Museum of Natural History in New York.

“Apex” is no longer an apex predator in the fossil market, however. Its record was undone this year by “Gus,” a Tyrannosaurus rex fossil that Christie’s sold for just over $50 million in July. Yet the sales of “Apex,” “Gus,” and others have suggested to many that the market for dinosaur fossils is red hot at the moment.

Griffin’s philanthropy has increasingly come to reshape universities and museums in the US. His recent endeavors include a $3 billion donation to Carnegie Mellon University, in what was reportedly the largest-ever financial gift in the history of higher education, and the loan of a Leonardo da Vinci painting to the Metropolitan Museum of Art.
