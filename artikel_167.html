MPs urge Miliband to force UK overseas territories to publish public company registers

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Improvements in corporate transparency ‘essential’ amid ‘outright disregard for the will of parliament’, says cross-party group

Ed Miliband is being urged by MPs to use his powers to force overseas territories such as the British Virgin Islands to publish public company ownership registers – almost a decade after parliament voted for transparency.

Ahead of a meeting between the foreign secretary and overseas territories this week, a cross-party group of MPs from Labour, the Tories, Lib Dems and Greens wrote to Miliband calling on him to mandate public registers of beneficial ownership of companies.

The group, coordinated by the all-party group on anti-corruption (APPG), said an improvement in corporate transparency in the overseas territories was essential after “years of broken promises and missed deadlines from certain jurisdictions, and despite repeated commitments to ending secrecy”.

They said some territories such as Gibraltar, Montserrat and St Helena had successfully opened corporate registers to the public.

But they called out the British Virgin Islands, saying it “in particular continues to let the British family down”. They said the Cayman Islands has raised its search fees to “prohibitive levels”, and Turks and Caicos have introduced huge fines for disclosing information from the register which they said was “undermining the entire purpose of open registers”.

They said that persistent engagement in good faith from the UK government has yielded few results, in the face of “outright disregard for the will of parliament”.

The BVI has a register launched this year which allows people to apply for access to ownership information if they have a “legitimate interest” such as investigating money laundering. A BVI Government spokesperson said: “We are proud of the BVI’s Legitimate Interest Access register introduced earlier this year, which allows for prompt, proportionate access, in line with international standards.

“We continue to work closely with the UK Government, delivering a major governance and regulatory reform programme to support global efforts combatting illicit finance, enforcing sanctions and recovering criminal assets - with significant progress recently recognised by the Financial Action Task Force.”

However, ahead of the meeting, the MPs including former Conservative Foreign Office minister Andrew Mitchell and Labour anti-corruption expert Phil Brickell are asking Miliband to lay an order in council mandating fully public registers of beneficial ownership in jurisdictions that continue to fail to meet their obligations.

They said it was important for UK national security “because continued secrecy undermines our ability to enforce sanctions and leaves us open to hostile state actors and criminal networks”. Second, they said developing countries continue to be destabilised and looted by kleptocrats using Britain’s own offshore financial centres to clean and stash their ill-gotten gains.

Parliament voted for open registers in 2018 and the UK government had expected public access registers to be in place in all overseas territories and crown dependencies by 2023. However, this deadline was not met.

While an order in council to mandate open registers was drafted in 2020, the government said that because there were “firm commitments” from the overseas territories to introduce public registers, the order would not be used. It said it would keep this decision under review.

Brickell, Labour chair of the APPG, said “it should never have come to this but every year of deliberate delay is another year in which kleptocrats, tax dodgers and money launderers can hide behind a British flag”.

“That is a stain on the reputation of our global family. This joint ministerial council must be the moment we finally draw a line under it.”

Mitchell, a Conservative vice-chair of the APPG and former development secretary, said: “When parliament voted for public registers in 2018, it was not making a polite request. It was the clear, cross-party will of the House of Commons, and it should have been respected.

“Instead, money stolen from some of the poorest people on earth is still being hidden behind anonymous companies in British jurisdictions. That money should be building hospitals and schools. It is paying for yachts and mansions instead, and the price is measured in lives. The British family has been patient for long enough. The foreign secretary has the power to act, and I urge him to use it.”

A Foreign Office spokesperson said: “Security is the first duty of any government. Hostile states and malign actors can exploit opaque corporate structures like those in the British Virgin Islands to hide their assets and support activity that threatens our national security. “Ministers are actively considering how best to ensure that, where the risks are greatest, law enforcement agencies have the information they need to progress critical investigations. We will continue ongoing engagement with Overseas Territories on these issues.”
