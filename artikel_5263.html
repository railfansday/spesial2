U.S. filed forfeiture complaint to recover more than $1 billion from Binance hack

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Prosecutors allege some of the money sits at identified crypto addresses after a hack that took about $6.3 billion

The U.S. Justice Department lodged a complaint in Washington's federal court on Monday asking to forfeit over $1 billion in crypto that hackers tied to North Korea took from Binance Holdings, according to Bloomberg.

According to Bloomberg, investigators and prosecutors traced some of the money to specific virtual currency addresses, where they allege it was held after the attack. The attack netted roughly $6.3 billion, one of the biggest hacks ever recorded.

"Today's action underscores the critical role of public-private partnerships in combating cryptocurrency-enabled crime," Tysen Duva, head of the Justice Department's criminal division, said in a statement, according to Bloomberg. "By working closely with the digital asset industry, we can disrupt illicit financial activity, protect victims, and recover stolen assets before they move beyond the reach of law enforcement."

Binance publicly confirmed the intrusion and blamed North Korea for it. The company said the breach was a "sophisticated, multi-stage campaign" that put malicious software on the laptop of one of its engineers, according to Bloomberg. Someone claiming to hold the role of project director had approached the engineer on a website for job seekers.

A Binance spokesperson said in a statement, according to Bloomberg: "We are coordinating closely with the DOJ as well as all of our industry partners that have assisted in freezing assets impacted by the hack. We expect to continue to cooperate with DOJ throughout the asset forfeiture process with the goal of securing the return of those funds as expediently as possible."

Earlier this month, the Justice Department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the stolen cryptocurrency. The department attributed the theft to members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls "TraderTraitor," and said they stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets on or about Oct. 29. Government and private-sector efforts had frozen more than $2.7 billion, the department said, and the $1.2 billion was part of that total.

The department said at the time that efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets were ongoing. It said TraderTraitor actors continued to launder the funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

Binance has since made customers whole for the entire $6.3 billion taken in the hack, the exchange said. North Korea has denied the claim that it was responsible.
