Child Predator Deemed “Extreme Danger” Sentenced to Life in Prison For Relentlessly Sexually Exploiting Multiple Minors in Hawaii

Darren Patrick Riley, 46, of Honolulu, Hawaii, was sentenced to life in prison by Senior U.S. District Judge J. Michael Seabright following his conviction by a jury of 12 counts related to his trafficking and sexual exploitation of five minor boys in Oahu in 2019 and 2020.

“Darren Riley is a predator in every sense of the word,” said Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “He identified and preyed on vulnerable victims, lured and enticed them with gifts and money, drugged them, raped them, and then recorded their sexual abuse. This sentencing speaks to the egregiousness of Darren Riley’s predatory abuse of children. Thanks to the bravery of the victims who testified at trial, along with the good work of law enforcement and the prosecutors, Riley’s exploitation was exposed and justice in some measure was obtained. Riley will never be a threat to other children in the future.”

“Darren Riley systematically committed some of the most egregious and reprehensible sexual abuse of minor children we have seen in Hawaii by grooming multiple young boys with drugs, money, and electronics and then sexually assaulting them,” said U.S. Attorney Ken Sorenson for the District of Hawaii. “As we have repeatedly admonished those who prey upon our children, when you act upon your deviant sexual desires and abuse children in Hawaii, you will face swift and punishing justice in federal court.”

“Riley systematically exploited vulnerable minors, taking advantage of their age, trust, and personal circumstances to facilitate and conceal his abuse,” said Special Agent in Charge Tom Ryan of the FBI Honolulu Field Office. “The courage displayed by the victims in this case was instrumental in holding Riley accountable and protecting other children from future harm. This life sentence demonstrates the relentless work of our agents and partners, and it serves as a warning to anyone who exploits children: no matter how carefully you hide your crimes or how long it takes, justice will find you.”

According to evidence presented at trial and sentencing, Riley used a dating app to find minors who ranged from 14 to 16 years old. After contacting victims through the app, Riley relentlessly pressured minors into sexual encounters by contacting them constantly and exploiting their particular vulnerabilities. At sentencing, District Judge Seabright commented that Riley “had a very sophisticated way of grooming” the minor victims.

Riley engaged in a pattern of systematic sexual abuse, which included drugging minors to the point of incapacitation and drugging a minor without his knowledge, as well as engaging in forced sexual encounters with multiple minors. He also filmed and later distributed videos he took of their sexual acts. In one video, Riley directed a victim to announce that he was 14 years old, his age at the time. Riley abused his victims in multiple locations, including his apartment, car, the beach, and hotel rooms. On one day, Riley abused two victims during separate assaults in the same hotel room. The five minor victims included in the charges bravely testified at trial, and the court considered information at sentencing that Riley had engaged or attempted to engage in similar conduct with twelve additional minor victims.

Riley knew the victims he exploited were vulnerable and preyed on their financial needs. One victim was struggling to afford consistent meals, and another was saving money because he feared being expelled from his family home. During his abuse, Riley supplied several of his victims with controlled substances. After Riley provided one victim MDMA and other pills, the victim described drifting in and out of consciousness while Riley abused him. Multiple victims tried to refuse Riley’s sexual advances, but Riley persisted. Riley’s crimes were finally uncovered when he was arrested by the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) at the Los Angeles airport for attempting to traffic methamphetamine into Hawaii. A search of his phone revealed a collection of child sexual abuse material, including images and videos of infants and toddlers being sexually abused, which prompted his charges.

Riley engaged in obstructionist conduct throughout the proceedings. He refused to come to court for his arraignment, trial, and sentencing. When he was forced into court, he engaged in a variety of disruptive tactics, including slumping over non-responsively, refusing to walk, disrobing, and screaming repeatedly at full volume. During at least one attempt to bring Riley to court, he assaulted multiple prison personnel. At sentencing, District Judge Seabright found that Riley perjured himself in filing affidavits under oath and in testifying twice during his motions to suppress evidence.

Prior to being charged in the current case, Riley was on federal supervised release in Honolulu, Hawaii for a drug trafficking conviction in the Central District of California. Within approximately one month after beginning supervised release, Riley absconded from supervision after being caught by U.S. Probation with methamphetamine. Other evidence gathered in the investigation, showed that Riley was engaging in drug distribution. After approximately two and a half months, law enforcement located Riley. He struggled with law enforcement in an attempt to avoid arrest.

In sentencing Riley to life in prison, District Judge Seabright stated that “the public must be protected from Riley” because “he has no respect for the law, the system of justice, or other people” and is “an extreme danger.”

The FBI Honolulu Field Office investigated the case with the assistance of the DEA.

Trial Attorney Gwendelynn Bills of the Criminal Division’s Child Exploitation and Obscenity Section (CEOS) and Assistant U.S. Attorney Rebecca Perlmutter for the District of Hawaii are prosecuting the case.

This case was brought as part of Project Safe Childhood, a nationwide initiative launched in May 2006 by the Department of Justice to combat the growing epidemic of child sexual exploitation and abuse. Led by U.S. Attorney’s Offices and CEOS, Project Safe Childhood marshals federal, state, and local resources to better locate, apprehend, and prosecute individuals who exploit children via the internet, as well as to identify and rescue victims. For more information about Project Safe Childhood, please visit www.justice.gov.
