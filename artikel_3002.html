Nvidia Has a $118 Billion Promise Tucked Inside Its Blowout Quarter. Here's What It Means for the Stock

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nvidia crushed expectations again. But its report also shows a growing pile of promises to help customers pay for the data centers that run its chips.

Nvidia (NVDA -0.44%) delivered another huge quarter on Tuesday. Revenue rose 103% from a year earlier to $115.8 billion, and the company forecast $130 billion for the current quarter, according to its earnings release. Shares rose about 5% after hours to around $163.

It was Nvidia's first report since co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO last week. But for long-term investors, one of the most important details sits in the fine print of its CFO commentary.

Nvidia disclosed up to $118 billion in guarantees, meaning it may have to pay third parties "if certain of our partners default on their obligations."

Almost all of that, $105 billion, supports SB Energy's PORTS-Pike Technology Campus in Ohio, which will "exclusively host Nvidia infrastructure under 20-year leases to OpenAI." The remaining $3.5 billion is for neocloud data centers, and $9.5 billion is for power and systems.

Neoclouds are smaller companies that rent out AI computing power. Nvidia also has $32 billion in separate commitments to them, which let it share in their rental revenue.

For scale, $118 billion is more than twice the $53.5 billion in cash and marketable debt securities Nvidia held at the end of the quarter.

Nvidia was direct about the reason. Neoclouds and AI labs "are seeing extraordinary demand for AI infrastructure, yet many are growing faster than their balance sheets and long-term credit profiles can support," it said in the Tuesday filing.

In other words, some customers want more Nvidia computing power than they can finance on their own. By backing the land, power, and buildings, Nvidia helps them raise money, and the chips that fill those buildings come from Nvidia.

The guarantees come with conditions. The Ohio guarantees "take effect phase by phase" as each building is finished, with the first expected in fiscal 2029. Exposure then shrinks over each 20-year lease as OpenAI pays rent. Nvidia said it has not made any payments on its guarantees.

On the earnings call, interim CEO Colette Kress said that if a partner is delayed or runs into trouble before a building is ready, "our guarantee doesn't exist anymore." She added that Nvidia is "rigorously evaluating future investments, commitments and guarantees" as other sources of capital become available.

The same pattern appears on the balance sheet. Accounts receivable, the money customers owe Nvidia, reached $81 billion, more than double the $38.5 billion at the end of January. Nvidia said that this was partly reflected in "extended payment terms on large, multi-quarter agreements with certain investment-grade customers."

None of this changes the core story. Nvidia expects revenue to grow about 75% next fiscal year, and Kress said demand is "far exceeding supply."

But it does change the risk. If AI spending cools, the damage may not stop at slower chip sales. Nvidia could also be on the hook for some of its partners' obligations, and it could take longer to collect what it's owed.

For investors in AI stocks, the guarantee total is worth watching every quarter. If it keeps climbing faster than sales, that could be a sign Nvidia is increasingly paying to create its own demand.
