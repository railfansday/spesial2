5 Reasons Why 76ers Fell Flat In Blowout Loss To Cavaliers

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Back in October, the Philadelphia 76ers crushed a Cleveland Cavaliers team at full strength, overcoming a 29-point deficit to pick up their second win of the season. So, going into tonight's game, the 76ers had full confidence in their ability to stave off a Cavs squad without their starting center. Instead, they suffered a brutal defeat as the Cavaliers successfully delivered revenge, cruising to a blowout victory (119-95) at Rocket Arena.

Starting with the winning side, Donovan Mitchell led the way with 23 points, seven rebounds, eight assists, two steals, and one block on 38.9% shooting from the field (1-6 from three). Evan Mobley, who stood in at center, put up 22 points, six rebounds, four assists, one steal, and one block on 61.5% shooting from the field and 75.0% shooting from three. Finally, there's James Harden, who finished with 17 points, six rebounds, and three assists on 36.4% shooting from the field (3-7 from three).

For the 76ers, Jaylen Brown was one of the few bright spots. In 34 minutes, he tallied 20 points, five rebounds, five assists, and one steal on 47.4% shooting from the field (2-9 from three). V.J. Edgecombe also deserves a mention with 19 points, three rebounds, two assists, and four steals on 61.5% shooting from the field (3-5 from three). Tyrese Maxey, meanwhile, managed to contribute 17 points, three rebounds, four assists, and one steal on just 33.3% shooting from the field 2-9 from three).

In what was supposed to be a highly competitive NBA Cup matchup (between two Conference rivals), the Cavaliers set the tone early and never looked back. They won all but one quarter and had the star-studded Sixers backpedaling all night. While it can be easy to pin the blame on one particular player, there were many factors at work that led to this loss, and some are more obvious than others.

Star big man Joel Embiid has been having a good season overall, and his activity on the interior has been huge to the 76ers' early success. That's why his performance in this disaster was so unexpected. Not only was he off his game tonight, but he was unrecognizable on the floor and a borderline liability to his team. In 25 minutes, he finished with just six points, seven rebounds, one assist, two blocks, and two turnovers on just 0-9 shooting from the field. It's unclear if he was injured, tired, or just having one of those tough shooting nights, but the 76ers won't get very far if he's playing like that.

What's confusing is that Embiid looks good physically. He's in shape, moving well, and has earned praise from teammates and coaches alike. Tonight, though, he looked painfully slow, and Nick Nurse had no choice but to keep him benched in the final quarter of play. What made it worse for Embiid, and hard to justify keeping him in the game, is that he wasn't making his usual impact defensively. He allowed Evan Mobley to go off for the Cavs, getting outplayed in a matchup where he had every advantage.

Embiid's stat line is the most glaring on the box score, but he wasn't the only 76er who had a rough night. In fact, just about everyone other than Jaylen Brown and V.J. Edgecombe struggled to find a rhythm. Tyrese Maxey, who is averaging 28.0 points per game this season, was held to just 17 points on 33.3% shooting. LeBron James also couldn't hit shots, and the bench failed to deliver any relief. Altogether, the 76ers shot just 36.5% as a team and 25.0% from three. They even struggled at the charity stripe, going 23-29. When a team is unable to hit shots like that, it completely narrows the margin for error, leading to the kind of results we saw today.

Of course, it didn't help that the Cavaliers were playing active, aggressive defense throughout the game. While their performance was far from perfect, the players did a good job contesting shots, denying second-chance opportunities, and not allowing the stars to catch any type of rhythm. They applied constant pressure, even when leading, taking advantage of a reeling Sixers squad.

At 41 years old, it's a miracle that LeBron James is still playing in the NBA, and even more so that he's starting for a title-contending team. At this stage of his career, the 76ers aren't asking him to play a leading role, but they count on his scoring and playmaking on the wing. In both those areas, he failed to deliver today. In 31 minutes, the King posted an uncharacteristic stat line of eight points, seven rebounds, three assists, two steals, one block, and six turnovers on 25.0% shooting from the field.

LeBron was still able to impact the game in other ways, but the Cavaliers had him on lock, limiting his effectiveness on the floor. In this game, at least, he didn't have his usual touch, and he wasn't able to make up for it at the three-point line. From the opening tip, LeBron looked sluggish and tired, showing his age in a way we haven't seen before. Whether it's a one-time thing or a glimpse of his new reality is unclear, but LeBron isn't showing any signs of panic (yet).

The 76ers' role players have been streaky this season, and they needed a big performance from them tonight against a stacked Cavaliers team. Unfortunately, nobody off the bench provided a spark, leading Nick Nurse to look deep in his rotation for an answer. In the end, between Dean Wade, Jabari Walker, Kentavious Caldwell-Pope, and Anfernee Simons, the 76ers' second unit combined to score 19 of the team's 95 points, vs. 47 points by the Cavaliers' bench. Without a true point guard to run that second unit, it makes everything so much harder.

If there's one area in particular that stood out for the Cavaliers tonight, it's the backup center spot — for all the wrong reasons. Tacko Fall made a rare appearance, but it only shows how desperate the situation became. With the Cavaliers dominating the paint and controlling the boards (56-42 rebound differential), it really exposed how limited their center options are after Embiid and Adem Bona, who missed tonight's game with neck soreness.

The 76ers are a team of competitors, but they didn't bring the fire against Cleveland. Careless turnovers, poor shot selection, and early defensive lapses are the signs of a team that was mentally checked out, and that's exactly how they played. From the first quarter, fans noticed that something looked off with their team, beyond just the inability to hit shots. Guys were disconnected and going through the motions offensively, almost like they were on the second night of a back-to-back.

From Embiid to LeBron to everyone in between, the 76ers did not play their usual brand of basketball, and it gave the Cavaliers a perfect opportunity to take advantage. Ultimately, it's unclear if the shooting woes caused the lackadaisical effort from the 76ers, but it could just be growing pains after a summer of so many new additions. Whatever the case, the Sixers were a step behind all night, and by the time they found a spark, they were already facing an uphill climb.
