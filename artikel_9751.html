How We Dashboard a Cinema Chain: Scheduling, Footfall and Concessions

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

By Ananya Krishnan, Operations, CineVerse Theatres.

CineVerse Theatres runs nine theatres and 12,400 seats in Hyderabad. Our dashboard problem is not producing more charts. It is getting a duty manager, film programmer, and concession lead to the same next action.

We organise the operating view around three questions:

Shared dimensions—such as theatre, screen, film, business date, daypart, and item—make the views comparable.

One trap is joining raw POS lines directly to shows and then summing admissions. Every receipt line can duplicate the show-level measures. We aggregate each fact separately before comparing them at a common theatre, business-date, and daypart grain.

The chain’s 12,400 seats describe our scale; they are not the denominator for a day’s occupancy. Each show has its own sellable capacity after holds and closures.

Alongside occupancy, the schedule view surfaces:

A chain-wide average is useful for a weekly review, but intervention usually happens at show or screen level. Film-scheduling research similarly treats film, screen, start time, per-show demand, and operating constraints together. An optimizer is not required for these first useful checks.

Door counters, ticket scans, and POS transactions answer different questions.

We plot entries in 15-minute buckets alongside ticket admissions and align them with upcoming showtimes. This helps distinguish a lobby arrival surge from a show that simply has low admissions.

We deliberately do not call entries / admissions a conversion rate. Depending on the installation, counter events can include staff, re-entry, and people who do not attend a screening. Counter outages are shown as no data, never as zero footfall.

If transaction rate falls while average receipt holds, we investigate arrival bunching, queues, staffing, and stock availability before drawing conclusions about the menu or pricing. If one receipt serves a group, transaction rate is a proxy—not a measure of unique buyers.

“Net sales” must have one documented meaning across theatres, including the treatment of taxes, refunds, and voids.

We reuse one dashboard with theatre, business-date, film, and daypart filters rather than maintaining separate copies for each location. Every panel displays the last successful source timestamp.

Operational alerts focus on things someone can act on:

Low occupancy alone goes to programming review rather than automatically paging a duty manager.

Refresh frequency follows the source. Refreshing a panel every 30 seconds does not improve a 15-minute extract. Business dates use the Asia/Kolkata timezone and an explicit rule for post-midnight shows.

Our basic rule is that every operational KPI needs an owner, grain, denominator, freshness expectation, and next action. If a panel cannot tell someone what to check next, it probably belongs in an exploratory report rather than the operations dashboard.
