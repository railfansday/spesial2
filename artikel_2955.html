Where Will SpaceX Stock Be in 2030?

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The company is growing fast and has tons of opportunity.

By Jennifer Saibil – Nov 19, 2026 at 2:43PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

There may have been enormous demand for Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -4.82%) stock when the company went public this past June, but the excitement has petered out. SpaceX stock is down 15% from its first-day closing price despite a fantastic third-quarter performance.

Is there any hope that the trend will change? Let's consider where the stock might be in 2030.

SpaceX's three segments all demonstrated outstanding performance in the third quarter, and both the Starlink satellite broadband business and the artificial intelligence (AI) business, which it now calls “super intelligence (SI),” reported operating profits, leading to the company's first quarter with a net profit.

The space business lost $7 million, but it recently achieved an important milestone when it successfully landed a Starship rocket on its return from orbit last week, catching the rocket with giant mechanical arms on its launch pad. This was the first time SpaceX had performed that maneuver with the Starship. SpaceX expects to have fully reusable rockets eventually, and plans to have Starship making two trips weekly by the second half of 2027, which will result in tremendous cost savings.

Management announced that it's developing a second Starbase in Louisiana, and it expects to have 20 launch towers and fly thousands of flights a year from there starting in 2029. It flew 165 flights in 2025, and by 2030, with thousands of flights, revenue should be much higher so long as there's demand for the flights.

The company's most established business is Starlink, the leading global satellite broadband business. It had 13.6 million customers as of the end of the third quarter, up 1.6 million from three months prior, and more than 11,000 satellites in low Earth orbit covering 160 countries.

It plans to have 100,000 communications satellites to meet growing broadband demand and offer coverage all over the world, including on airplanes and over water. It also plans to eventually have a million Starmind mini data centers in orbit, powered by the sun, to meet the world’s growing AI compute needs.

While there are big ambitions for the space business, including trips carrying 100 passengers for interplanetary travel, the biggest opportunities are in SI.

SI revenue increased 830% year over year in the third quarter, driven by new cloud agreements for compute power from its Colossus I and Colossus II data center clusters, the acquisition of AI coding company Cursor, and high use of the company's Grok AI agent.

Management has said that it sees a $26.5 trillion addressable market for AI, and with more than $7 billion in revenue in the third quarter, it already accounts for roughly half of the total.

There's uncertainty in this business, since several governors of states that are in the midst of the most intense data center build-outs have pledged to put construction of new data centers on hold while they review the impacts of those developments, and SpaceX is still far from being able to deploy solar-powered satellite data centers profitably or at scale. However, the likelihood is that AI will continue to grow and generate higher revenue. In fact, AI is already highly advanced from what it was when ChatGPT launched four years ago, and four years from now, it should be doing things people can't even imagine right now.

As for the stock, what's holding it back from further growth right now seems to be its astronomical valuation. It trades at almost 56 times trailing-12-month sales, and that ratio keeps getting lower.

I think you'd have to imagine SpaceX's growth slowing down over the next four years and its valuation getting lower. Revenue increased 161% year over year in the third quarter, and if it continues at a compound annual growth rate of 50% over the next four years, it will have $165 billion in annual sales. At a price-to-sales ratio of 23, or half of today's, its market cap would be $3.8 trillion, or just more than double today's value, in 2030.
