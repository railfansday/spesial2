California has pulled the plug on Waymo’s driverless freeway rides across the entire state, citing potential safety hazards.

In a statement, the California Department of Motor Vehicles said that the ban went into effect on November 11th. The crackdown follows two recent high-profile Bay Area incidents, in which Waymo robotaxis drove into an active construction closure on US-101 near Burlingame, and a separate run-in involving California Highway Patrol vehicles on Highway 280 in San Francisco.

In its statement, the DMV explicitly labeled Waymo’s freeway operations as an “imminent hazard to public safety,” noting that this isn’t an isolated glitch. Waymo has experienced “recurring incursions into active construction zones, the DMV said, arguing the company was putting road workers at risk.

Waymo has struggled with freeway operations over the years. The company has been testing its vehicles on California’s freeways since at least August 2024, before eventually opening up to public passengers in November 2025. Waymo voluntarily paused freeway operations in May 2026 over concerns about construction sites — but then resumed rides in late July.

Operating on freeways is crucial to Waymo’s robotaxi business; without freeways, the company will be forced to take longer routes on local roads that will force customers to pay more for their fares. On average, Waymo rides are already more expensive than human-driven Uber or Lyft trips. A spokesperson for Waymo did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

In order to get back on the freeway, Waymo will need to demonstrate it has taken sufficient action to improve its operation around construction zones, the DMV said. The company will need to submit evidence to the agency for review, at which point regulators will determine whether it can safely resume operations.
