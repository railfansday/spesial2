Mike McCarthy: Loss to Bengals ‘not to the standard of the Pittsburgh Steelers’

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

An unsurprisingly brief Steelers postgame press conference following a 38-18 loss to the Bengals on Sunday Night Football yielded similar answers from Pittsburgh's head coach and players: One team executed. The other did not. One team got out to a fast start. The other did not. And the final result, no matter how ugly, wouldn't define the rest of the season.

"That's very dramatic," quarterback Aaron Rodgers quipped postgame when asked how to keep the 20-point loss from damaging the Steelers' trajectory.

Still, no football platitudes could mask what was an undeniably disappointing end result for the once high-flying Steelers, who entered Sunday's game with the same record as the 6-2 Bengals. Pittsburgh was coming off a bye week with a chance to take sole possession of first place in the AFC North on the momentum of a four-game winning streak.

Instead, the Steelers fell flat in the biggest game of their season thus far.

"Handling success is the biggest challenge in this league, and you come off the bye week, you worry about a letdown — I've seen it happen before, been a part of it," Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy said postgame. "But give credit to the Bengals, they played extremely well — they played the game they wanted to play, and we didn't get the game headed in the direction we wanted to play."

While the Steelers had already lost two games in 2026 entering the Bengals matchup, including a 20-3 loss to the New England Patriots in Week 2, the result in Cincinnati was by far the most lopsided defeat the black and gold have endured all year. By halftime, the Bengals had already pulled ahead, up 24-7.

"This is the first time we've been through one of these (losses) as a football team," McCarthy said. "So, it's gonna be an experience to go through it, correct it, and (see) how fast we move through that process and get on to (Week 11's matchup versus) Philadelphia, a game where we have to get on a winning track."

Pittsburgh has rebounded from both of its prior losses this season with wins, but each time, a tough road loss was followed by a matchup at home. Next week, Pittsburgh will face a team with a 2-7 record in the Eagles, but it will be on the road in Philadelphia, a city the Steelers haven't won in since the 1960s.

But will there be any carryover, good or bad, from the Steelers' Week 10 to Week 11?

"No … It starts over every single week," Rodgers said.

However, McCarthy emphasized that Sunday's result wasn't what he wanted his team looking like at midseason with playoff seeding looming.

"That's clearly not to the standard of the Pittsburgh Steelers; it's not to any of our expectations," he said following the loss. "You had two teams that were playing good football over the month of October that came in here tonight. And being on the road, there were some things mechanically and operational that we were not on top of — we're too far in the season for that."
