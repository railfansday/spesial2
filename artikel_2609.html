10 Eagles things I think I think after incredible comeback vs. Cowboys

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

There's no other way to describe the Philadelphia Eagles' comeback victory over the Dallas Cowboys — which was way more enjoyable than any meal you could consume on Thanksgiving. This is an Eagles game that's going to be remembered forever, just like the Christmas Day win over the Cowboys in 2006 and the Miracle at the Meadowlands comeback in 2010.

The victory is that improbable, especially when the Eagles were down 31-10 with 3:35 left in the third quarter. They had no business being in this game, yet they found their way back to make it a game — and stole a victory away from the Cowboys on Thanksgiving.

This was a game the Cowboys should have won handily. Instead they choked it away, thanks to the hands of a resilient Eagles team that didn't let their 3-7 record stand in the way of what seemed to be an insurmountable deficit.

No matter how the rest of this season goes, that was one heck of a win. This victory will be talked about amongst generations of Eagles fans.

A Thanksgiving miracle — that may have ended the Cowboys season. The Eagles are good at ruining seasons all of a sudden.

Milton delivered one of the greatest performances my a quarterback in Eagles history. What Milton did against the Cowboys was absolutely incredible, and this was more than a quarterback showing off his ridiculous arm talent.

The final stat line: 21-of-31, 311 yards, 3 TD, 1 INT, 10.7 YPA — 121.8 rating. 3 carries, 15 yards, TD.

There are only two quarterbacks in Eagles history with 300+ passing yards, 3+ passing TD, 1+ rushing TD, and average 10+ YPA in a game — Milton and Michael Vick (2010 at Commanders). That's how well Milton played on Thanksgiving — in just his third career start.

Once the Eagles trailed 31-10, Milton went 11-of-16 for 181 yards with 2 TD, 1 INT and a 120.1 rating (averaging 11.3 YPA). He also had 15 yards and a rushing touchdown in that stretch to bring the Eagles back from three scores.

This level of play was throughout the game. Milton used his arm strength to keep the Eagles offense moving, going 11-of-19 for 278 yards with 3 TD, 1 INT and 14.6 YPA on throws that traveled 10+ air yards — finishing with a 120.1 passer rating. He was 2-of-6 for 95 yards and 2 TD with 0 INT for a 121.5 passer rating on throws that traveled 25+ air yards. Any completion on passes that traveled 25+ air yards was a touchdown.

The Eagles had some exciting performances from Jalen Hurts over the years, but that was arguably the most exciting passing performance by a quarterback since Nick Foles threw for 471 yards against the Houston Texans in 2018 (and that was an Eagles' win too).

Impressive performance by Milton, finally giving life to an Eagles offense that badly needed it.

Milton had a touchdown pass that traveled 64.4 yards in air distance, and that wasn't the most impressive aspect of his performance. He threw an interception into triple coverage with 7:14 to play when the Eagles were trailing 38-3, which seemed to be the backbreaker with how the defense was playing.

The Eagles got the ball back and Milton goes 4-of-4 for 70 yards and a 24-yard touchdown to Makai lemon on the ensuing drive — netting a perfect 158.3 passer rating. The poise and ability to forget that interception immediately following the interception spoke more volumes about who Milton is as a player than anything else he accomplished on Thanksgiving.

When Milton made a boneheaded play, he responded in the way winning quarterbacks respond. He hasn't even been on his team for two months and was able to shake that play off and perform at an even higher level.

Since the interception, Milton finished 6-of-9 for 84 yards with a touchdown and a 133.6 passer rating. He led the Eagles to victory with a touchdown drive and set Jake Elliott up with his game-winning field goal.

Want to know how to stick around as a starting quarterback in the NFL? Moments like that.

One of the few things I wanted to see in the remainder of the season was if Milton could play well enough to be the QB2 next year. Judging Milton through two starts was tough, given the state of the offense and the changes the Eagles were making to accomondate his game.

Milton didn't get to throw the deep ball much, and he missed when he had his opportunity last week to DeVonta Smith. He didn't miss any of those opportunities on Thanksgiving, hitting two of them for 95 yards and two touchdowns.

On throws that traveled 20+ air yards, Milton went 6-of-11 for 188 yards and 3 TD with 0 INT — a 139.2 passer rating. The arm strength was clearly on display, along with the accuracy on the deep ball.

Milton was relentless facing pressure too, finishing 7-of-10 for 154 yards with 3 TD and 0 INT for a 152.1 passer rating — while also being sacked five times. He was 6-of-8 for 145 yards with 3 TD and 0 INT on throws from outside the pocket — 156.3 rating.

Backup quarterbacks don't have games like this. QB5's — which is what Milton was when he signed — really don't have games like this.

This performance may have locked up QB2 for Milton next year. If Milton can stack up good-to-solid performances, he'll be the QB2 on the Eagles (at least).

Milton may get a starting opportunity elsewhere too (he is a restricted free agent), but the Eagles can control his fate there as well. The situation needs to play itself out, but Milton is certainly on the radar around the league.

When Milton was making incredible throws against the Cowboys, I couldn't help but think of his journey to get to this point. Over the last four months, Milton was cut by the Cowboys after losing the QB2 job to Sam Howell —toiling on the practice squad for five weeks.

Any team could have claimed Milton when he was waived, and no team did. Any team could have signed Milton from the Cowboys practice squad to the active roster, and no one did.

The Eagles signed Milton to the active roster when Jalen Hurts was lost for the season due to blood clots in his lungs. If Hurts wasn't done for the year, the Eagles wouldn't have brought in Milton.

He literally was their QB5, the fifth quarterback to make the active roster. Tanner McKee got three starts before the Eagles turned to Milton, Andy Dalton told the Eagles he had a better opportunity with the Atlanta Falcons.

Milton wasn't considered by the Cowboys and 31 other teams until the Eagles needed a quarterback. To have a performance like that against a team that cut him and chose Sam Howell over him had to be bittersweet — especially since he led the Eagles to a 21-point comeback on the road against that team on Thanksgiving in just his third start.

Certainly Milton will soak that all in at some point, but the battle to succeed and have a moment like that are why sports are so great. Even when Milton was counted out, he wasn't.

There were questions whether DeVonta Smith was a WR1 earlier this season. There shouldn't be any following Thanksgiving.

Smith finished with 11 catches for 200 yards and two touchdowns in the win over the Cowboys, the first Eagles' player to get 200+ receiving yards in a game since DeSean Jackson in 2010. He had the most receiving yards on Thanksgiving since 1945 — and the second-most by any player on the holiday in NFL history.

Smith had two touchdown catches of 45+ yards, the first player to do that on Thanksgiving since Randy Moss in 1998. He had more receiving yards on Thanksgiving (200) than A.J. Brown has the whole season (128).

How good was the Milton-Smith connection? Milton was 11-of-14 for 200 yards with 2 TD, 0 INT and a 158.3 passer rating when targeting Smith — 14.3 yards per attempt. He hit Smith on throws of 24, 50, 45, and 23 yards of those 11 completions, and also hit him six times on throws of 10+ air yards.

When Smith is healthy, he's very good. He wasn't even fully healthy in this one, injuring his lower back trying to make an insane catch in the fourth quarter on a Milton deep ball. Smith could barely run at full speed the rest of the way, yet still was able to go out and make plays.

Smith has 42 catches for 551 yards and 4 TD in six games. He's averaging 91.8 receiving yards per game, a 17-game pace of 1,561 yards. With the injuries, Smith is still on pace for 851 receiving yards (and those numbers will go up if he's good enough to play).

Milton has an excellent chance to stick around with Smith on the field. That connection was brilliant on Thanksgiving.

Hard to state the claim the Eagles have given up on Nick Sirianni based on the last two games.

A win against the Pittsburgh Steelers to end a seven-game losing streak is a gut-check victory. Stealing a game on Thanksgiving against the Dallas Cowboys is in unchartered territory.

The Eagles were down 21 points with 3:35 left in the third quarter. They had eight plays in the quarter that went for 1 yard at that point, while the defense allowed 31 points on seven possessions and 6.7 yards per play.

They could have packed it in and allowed Dallas to continue to roll. They didn't.

The Eagles not giving up in a season that has experienced injury after injury (including the multiple starters that left the game), team captains and long-time starters getting traded, the handling of Jalen Hurts' blood clots, and the uncertain future of the coaching staff speaks volumes of how Nick Sirianni prepares his team during the week.

No matter what Sirianni does during a game (and after), his players are still playing for him. The message is still getting across to the players in that locker room and the Eagles don't believe their playoff chances are dead yet.

If Sirianni is back after this season, this is the reason why. This locker room has no checked out on him, and they are demonstrating that with their performance on the field.

Any team could have called it a day down 31-10 late in the third quarter. Not the Eagles, who had their biggest comeback in 16 years.

I understand why Sirianni acts like he does. Sirianni is a very emotional individual and a competitor who puts his heart and soul into coaching.

Going on a seven-game losing streak took its toll on Sirianni, who spent weeks trying to figure out what was going on with his football team. This is a head coach who was 59-26 (.694 win percetatge) after his first five seasons, making the playoffs all five years and winning a Super Bowl while appearing in another one.

Sirianni is fighting for his team — and his job. The last two months have been brutal.

The Eagles head coach gets caught up in the moment with fans a little too much. He lets his emotions get the best of him. I'm not even sure if he was interacting with Eagles fans or taunting Cowboys fans, but it was enough where he had to be pulled away by Dom DiSandro.

Sirianni shouldn't be putting himself in that situation, and he's done it far too much. I like that he's authentic, but that is taking it too far.

The Eagles head coach shouldn't care what the fans think of him, good or bad. It's clear Sirianni hears what's said of him and acts on it when he wins.

Sirianni gets over the top with this stuff, a silly distraction after the massive win. The Eagles have enough issues to worry about inside the Jefferson Health Training Complex.

Can't speak enough on how good Jake Elliott has been all season. Elliott's two field goals on Sunday were the reason the Eagles won, and he gave the Eagles their first lead of the game on a 59-yard field goal with two seconds left — and the kick would have been good from 65 yards.

Elliott had ice in his veins when making that kick, just like the majority of clutch kicks he's made in his career. Elliott is 14-of-14 on field goals in the final minute of a game in regulation or overtime, 5-of-5 on kicks of 50+ yards in one-score games.

He's 2-for-2 in those situations this season, and 1-of-1 on game-winning field goals.

Elliott has improved to 5-of-5 on 50+ yard field goal attempts, after being 5-of-15 (33.3%) on those kicks over the previous two seasons. Elliott is 8-of-9 (88.9%) on 40+ yard kicks this season after going 20-of-34 (58.9%) on those kicks over the previous two seasons.

The comeback is incredible, and why the Eagles chose to adamantly defend Elliott this offseason. Just an amazing season from Elliott, who might be in line for an All-Pro campaign.

This Eagles comeback doesn't happen without Robinson, who made the most of his opportunity when his number was called. Robinson had three pressures in seven pass-rushing snaps, an insane pressure rate of 42.9%.

One of those pressures was the fourth-down sack on Dak Prescott on 4th-and-1 with 55 seconds left — the first one of Robinson's career. This was a player who wasn't even guaranteed to make the roster once training camp started, and earned his way onto this team with his play in training camp.

That play set the Eagles up to steal the game, and set off a fire in Dallas on whether Brian Schottenheimer should have punted or went for it — enough where his job is in question. Robinson was able to make that massive of a presence as a pass rusher, and deserves more playing time going forward.

Oh, and Robinson also blocked a punt that was recovered by Kenny Moore for a touchdown. That play was massive in the Eagles' comeback, and cut the deficit to 31-24 with 13:53 left in the game. Robinson should have been credited with a forced fumble in this game too, but I digress.

Robinson is the first player in Eagles history to record a sack and a blocked punt in the same game. This is a player that logged just 73 defensive snaps this season prior to Thursday.

The Eagles have something with Robinson. They traded Jordan Davis for multiple reasons.

The Eagles entered this game with the worst second-half offense in football. The 4.6 point average in the second half was last in the NFL. So was the yards per play (3.6), average yards in second half (110.1), and net yards per attempt (4.3). The only major statistic that wasn't 32nd was yards per carry — 30th at 3.3.

This week was significantly different. The Eagles scored 22 offensive points in the second half (29 total), averaging 6.9 yards per play and gaining 200 yards of offense. They averaged 5.4 yards per carry and 8.5 net yards per attempt — all this while only converting 20% of third downs.

This was the worst team in football in the second half, and they're still last in the league in offensive points scored (6.2), yards per play (3.9), and average yards in second half (118.3). yards per carry moved up to 29th (3.4) and net yards per attempt improved to 31st (4.8).

The Eagles even scored in the third quarter! Milton's touchdown pass in the third quarter was just the second touchdown scored by the Eagles in the third quarter all season — the other one was against the Cowboys. They improved their third quarter scoring average to 30th in the league (2.1 points).

This offense in the second half underwent a complete 180, and it didn't start out that way (first eight plays went for a yard). Amazing turnaround once Milton hit Smith on that 45-yard touchdown with 24 seconds left in the third quarter.

Can the Eagles build off this? Hopefully.

Bonus: Enjoy this win (enjoy all of them). This is one the city of Philadelphia will talk about for a long time, even better since this season has been a disaster and this matchup seemed like a easy victory for the Cowboys (I didn't even think the Eagles could score 20 points when I was watching Dallas film on Monday).

You'll remember where you were and who you were with when the Eagles pulled off this comeback.

The Eagles are 4-0 against the Cowboys on Thanksgiving and Christmas — all games in Dallas. Happy Thanksgiving everyone!
