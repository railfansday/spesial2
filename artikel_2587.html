The Good, the Bad and the Ugly: Bengals get revenge vs. Steelers

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

That's how you answer a September loss. With first place in the AFC North on the line and the country watching on Sunday Night Football, the Cincinnati Bengals jumped on Pittsburgh early, led 24-3 at halftime and 31-3 late in the third quarter, and cruised to a 38-18 win at Paycor.

Cincinnati is 7-2, has won five straight, and sits alone atop the division. It's hard to find much to complain about after a night like that, but we'll try. Let's get into it.

Joe Burrow, MVP front-runner. Burrow went 35-of-44 for 409 yards and four touchdowns with no turnovers, a 135.7 passer rating. He had 302 yards and three touchdowns by halftime. His 27 touchdown passes lead the NFL. He also became the second quarterback in league history with at least four total touchdowns in five straight games, joining Peyton Manning (2004).

Ja'Marr Chase on the big stage. Chase had 11 catches for 231 yards, including 146 in the first half. That's the most receiving yards by any player in a game this season, the fourth-most in Bengals history, and the fourth 200-yard game of his career. His 53- and 43-yard catches both set up touchdowns.

Chase Brown, red zone weapon. Brown caught touchdowns from 2 and 4 yards out, his second career two-touchdown game, and finished with eight catches for 39 yards and 10 carries for 44 yards. Drew Sample (3 yards) and Dohnte Meyers (15 yards) also caught touchdowns, and Samaje Perine ran one in from a yard out.

A first-half defensive clinic. Pittsburgh had 40 total yards and four first downs at halftime. Aaron Rodgers started 0-of-8 and didn't complete a pass until late in the second quarter. By then, the Bengals had completed 21, which ties the most by one team before its opponent's first completion since 1978. Boye Mafe (his team-leading 6.5th sack), Jonathan Allen and Myles Murphy each sacked Rodgers.

History. Cincinnati has now scored 30-plus points in five straight games. That's the longest streak in franchise history, breaking the old mark of four set in 1997, and no other NFL team has done it this season. The 66,860 fans at Paycor were the fourth-largest home crowd in team history.

"The Dirty Work" Guys. Drew Sample, Samaje Perine, and Brown all did a number of things in this one. Sneakily effective receiving options, effective runs, and pass protection galore. Upon a rewatch of this game, Perine and Brown were seldom knocked down on first contact.

The Beatdown on Primetime TV. How many times have we seen the Steelers impose their physical will upon the Bengals—especially under the brightest of lights? Cincinnati absolutely dominated their divisional foes in their own trademark way, dominating the trenches, and utilizing their amazing offensive weapons. And they did it with the nation watching. "Satisfying" doesn't quite cover it.

The streak is over. Evan McPherson opened the game with a 24-yarder, his 25th straight make, the longest active streak in the NFL. Then he pushed a 43-yard try wide right in the second quarter, his first miss of the season. He's still 13-of-14 on the year, and nobody should panic, but it was a reminder that nothing lasts forever. I mean, let's give him a pass—he has been outstanding this entire season.

The run defense, still leaky. Pittsburgh ran for 102 yards on 23 carries, 4.4 yards per carry. That's better than the last two weeks, but it's still more than you'd like against an offense that couldn't throw a lick in the first half.

The flags. The Bengals were penalized eight times for 80 yards, double Pittsburgh's four for 46. Against a team that was already beaten, it didn't matter. Against Kansas City, Buffalo and/or a playoff opponent, 80 yards of free field position will matter in a big way.

The fourth-quarter fade. Up 31-3, the defense let Rodgers throw touchdown passes to DK Metcalf (22 yards) and Pat Freiermuth (27 yards), plus a two-point conversion. That was 15 points in about five minutes. It's garbage time, sure, and the final margin was never in doubt. But it turned a historic beatdown into a 20-point win, and it's a habit worth breaking before the games get tighter.

That's about as nitpicky as it gets. On a night that featured the best quarterback in the league and arguably the best receiver, the Bengals looked every bit like a contender.
