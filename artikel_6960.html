30 Breakup Texts to Send Instead of Ghosting Someone

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Figuring out how to tell someone you're not interested shouldn't be harder than the actual breakup.

You went on a date. They were perfectly nice, you had a decent time, and you even said you'd do it again. Except now they're texting you about Friday, and you're realizing you'd actually rather spend the evening doing literally anything else. So you consider taking the path of least resistance: simply never responding again. No awkward conversation, no hurt feelings (at least on your end), and no need to figure out how to tell someone you're just not that into them.

Unfortunately, there's a reason sending a breakup text feels harder than disappearing. “We ghost because we would rather avoid the interaction completely,” explains Kayden Kross, founder of Gasplighting and author of Deeper. And while ghosting might save you an uncomfortable interaction, it leaves the other person wondering whether you're busy, uninterested, or just never planning to text them back.

To be clear, we're talking about casual dating and early-stage connections here, not ending a years-long relationship with a two-sentence text. And if someone makes you feel unsafe or repeatedly disrespects your boundaries, you don't owe them an explanation.

But if the only thing stopping you from ending things is that you don't know what to say, we've got you. From the person who won't stop asking for a second date to the one who only texts after midnight, here are 30 breakup texts to send instead of ghosting.

Short answer: Yes, but context matters. If you've only been on a date or two, there's no need to schedule an in-person breakup conversation with someone you're still getting to know. “If the majority of the interaction has been by text or DM then ending it by text is within bounds,” says Kross.

But if you've been seeing someone for months, have developed real feelings, or are in an established relationship, a phone call or face-to-face conversation is generally more considerate. The more time and emotional investment involved, the more care the breakup deserves.

Of course, there's one exception to any breakup etiquette rule: your safety. If someone has made you feel unsafe, threatened you, or repeatedly ignored your boundaries, you don't need to meet up, get on a call, or even respond just to be polite.

The best breakup texts are short, honest, and leave very little room for interpretation. You don't need to write a five-paragraph essay about why you're not feeling it, but you do need to make it clear that you're ending things. “It’s more about being clear that we’re not interested in continuing so the other side isn’t left in a position of uncertainty about what’s going on,” Kross explains.

One easy approach? Acknowledge the time you've spent together, briefly explain that you don't see things moving forward, and wish them well. No need to list everything you didn't like about them or invent an excuse to soften the blow.

And whatever you do, don't leave the door open if you know it's closed. “To me, the most painful thing in relationships is false hope,” says Kross. Skip the “maybe someday” and “let's stay friends” unless you genuinely mean them. Being clear might feel uncomfortable now, but it's a lot kinder than keeping someone guessing.

Seriously. If someone has made you feel unsafe, harassed you, or continued contacting you after you've asked them to stop, you don't owe them another explanation. Protecting your peace and safety is more important than following breakup etiquette.
