Mauricio Pochettino issued an emphatic response after pundit DaMarcus Beasley, who spent time on loan at Man City, told him live on air: “Manchester will always be blue”.

Pochettino, who replaces Michael Carrick as Manchester United head coach, took charge of the United States Men’s National Team for the final time on Tuesday night.

The Argentine helped the USMNT record a 2-0 victory over Haiti, with the goals coming from Christian Pulisic and Julian Hall.

USA subsequently advance to the CONCACAF Nations League semi-finals after defeating their quarter-final opponents 8-0 on aggregate. But they will enter the final four without their influential coach, leaving US Soccer to appoint the next USMNT manager.

Pochettino, the former Tottenham and Chelsea manager, now heads to Manchester. His first game in the dugout is a daunting trip to Liverpool’s Anfield on Sunday.

The South American, who has signed a two-and-a-half-year contract with the Red Devils, will be under pressure to get an immediate tune from his new squad.

Man Utd find themselves inside the Premier League relegation zone, having won just one of their opening 10 league fixtures. Even Sunderland and Crystal Palace, the two teams lower than United in England’s top division, have won more fixtures during the opening three months of the 2026-27 campaign.

United endured their worst start to a season under Carrick, winning three of their opening 15 matches in all competitions.

Pochettino’s initial task will be to steady the ship before guiding United back up the Premier League table as quickly as possible.

West Ham, Wrexham, and Blackburn Rovers have all been predicted for huge seasons, but each have different fates in next season's Championship.

Before flying to Manchester after his final game in charge of the USMNT, Pochettino conducted a live televised interview with TNT Sports.

Asked what will be going through his head as he flies to Manchester, Pochettino replied: “We’re excited because it’s a big challenge, but at the same time it’s the adrenaline… that you want to feel… to have a different job. We need to add our common sense in the way we’re going to approach everything.

“We know the situation isn’t a situation that a club like Manchester United deserve to be in. The fanbase is amazing. In America, every 10 people I cross out here on the street, nine are Manchester United fans. It’s amazing.”

One of the pundits on the panel was DaMarcus Beasley, who played 126 times for the USMNT between 2001 and 2017.

He spent the 2006-07 season on loan at Manchester City, scoring four goals in 22 appearances.

The 44-year-old didn’t miss the opportunity to tease the new Manchester United boss: “I said it before: Manchester is blue - and it will always be blue!”

Pochettino replied: “No way. You will see. Give me time. Give me time and that is going to change, for sure.”

It’s fair to say Pochettino’s emphatic response will help to further endear him to Manchester United supporters, who are hoping the Argentine is finally the manager who can help reverse the club’s fortunes.

Pochettino’s second game in charge is away at Sporting in the Champions League on November 25 before he makes his Old Trafford bow for Man Utd vs Brentford three days later.
