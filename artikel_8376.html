On my very rough planning spreadsheet I assume 4% real return for global equities, so this is in line with my conservative prior
Is it? Vanguard's forecast average annual return numbers are all nominal returns, not real returns.

If we assume 3% / yr CPI inflation, lower than inflation today & a little lower than US long term averages, then Vanguard's forecast nominal returns for global ex US equities of 5% / yr -- 7% / yr end up being a range of 2% / yr -- 4% / yr after subtracting 3% / yr inflation.

At the upper end of Vanguard's forecast band, it agrees with your 4% / yr real assumption. At the lower end of Vanguard's forecast band, you're only getting half of your assumed real return.

I'm assuming a 50% drop in US stocks followed by lower returns than the Vanguard report you cite, so I'm not worried.
It sounds like your plan has a comfortable safety factor! Out of curiosity, is a 50% drop in US stock prices your baseline expectation (if so, do you hold any US equities at all?) , or is it more one of a handful of scenarios - a plausible worst case scenario - that you want your plan to be robust against?



From a quick check using the historical S&P price & earnings data in Shiller's CAPE spreadsheet, here are rough peak-to-trough drops in S&P price,
  • 1929 - 1932: 85% drop in S&P index price
  • 2000 - 2003: 45% drop in S&P index price
  • 2007 - 2009: 50% drop in S&P index price
  • 2000 - 2009: 50% drop in S&P index price (spanning dot com peak to GFC trough)
So, having a plan that can deal with a possible 50% decline in stock market prices seems prudent. These things have been observed to happen & a 50% drop in stock prices is neither an observed historical worst case nor a theoretical worst case.
