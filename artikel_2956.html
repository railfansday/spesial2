Daniel Jones landing spots: Lions and Rams among best fits for QB after being released by Colts

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Daniel Jones, for the second time in three years as an NFL starting quarterback, has been released in Week 11 after being demoted by his team. His time with the Colts came to a shocking midseason end, with the team not even continuing to roster him after re-promoting starter Anthony Richardson Sr.

In 2024 with the Giants, after being benched for Tommy DeVito, Jones asked for and was granted his release. He landed with the Vikings as a backup for the rest of that season before the Colts took a chance on him. He was playing at a high level for offensive-minded coach Shane Steichen for the first half of last season, but a torn Achilles derailed that success.

Jones returned healthy to start in Weeks 1 through 11 for the Colts, but he had been struggling. The final straw for Jones was yet another dismal performance in Thursday night's rough 26-3 loss to the Texans in Houston.

Where does Jones go from here, either this season or next, as he tries to land with a fourth NFL team at age 29? Here's a breakdown of his top options for short- and long-term fits.

A Giants reunion with Titans offensive coordinator Brian Daboll and quarterbacks coach Shea Tierney won't be happening, with Mitchell Trubisky signed to be Cam Ward's veteran No. 2 through 2027. But Jones' former OC in New York, Mike Kafka, is now the Lions' passing game coordinator. Jones needs a team loaded with blocking and skill support to be good.

The Lions have been shaking up their backups for Jared Goff of late. When Teddy Bridgewater retired, they signed 31-year-old journeyman Josh Dobbs to be the No. 2, only in August. Dobbs is only on a one-year deal. Jones would be an upgrade there because of his overall starting experience. This might not happen this season, but watch for it as a logical signing after the season.

Jones could also find a good home as a top backup for the division rival that just routed him with its defense. Jones' early QB coach with the Giants was Jerry Schuplinski, who now manages that position in Houston. Davis Mills remains the clear No. 2 to C.J. Stroud, but he's a pending free agent playing on only a one-year, $7 million deal.

Jones' connection with the team he just helped the Colts beat in Week 10 is former Giants wide receivers coach Tyke Tolbert. Tolbert is watching over the wideout roster rebuild in Miami and has Brady Cook backing up Kyle McCord, with Malik Willis (groin) on injured reserve. In addition, passing game coordinator Brian Callahan used to work with Shane Steichen in Philadelphia.

The Rams make sense for next year, as Stetson Bennett is a free agent and Matthew Stafford's retirement decision might depend on how far the Rams go in the playoffs. If Stafford hung it up, then it would be time to elevate his rookie first-round stashed successor, Ty Simpson. If Stafford stayed and Bennett didn't re-sign, it would make sense to have a more dedicated, experienced true No. 2 in Jones behind Stafford rather than a projected future starter in Simpson. Jones spent a short time in a Sean McVay-adjacent offense with Kevin O'Connell's Vikings. Former Steichen Eagles colleague Brian Johnson is also a senior offensive assistant to McVay.

Would it be weird for former Giant Jones to join Saquon Barkley in Philadelphia? Not at all. The only question is whether it would make more sense now to try to save a lost 2-7 season or look at Jones as an upgrade to the backup situation behind Jalen Hurts. Hurts' former top backup, Tanner McKee, is a pending free agent and was terrible filling in during three games. Joe Milton III is just a makeshift flyer. The Eagles may not feel comfortable elevating current rookie Cole Payton to No. 2 behind Hurts, but the experienced, mobile and strong-armed Jones is a good fit for former McVay mentee Sean Mannion.
