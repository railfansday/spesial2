3 Investing Habits That Can Build Life-Changing Wealth, According to Decades of Stock Market History

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Keep it simple: save hard, early, and often; buy and hold for the long term; and reinvest to enhance compounding.

Investing can be shockingly simple if you set your investment approach up the right way. Investment icon Warren Buffett, the former CEO of Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -0.37%)(BRKB -0.54%), has provided all of the advice you need: buy an S&P 500 index (^GSPC -1.09%) ETF like Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -1.13%) and hold for the long term. But there's a little more to the story than that, including saving, buying and holding, and reinvesting. Here's how these three habits can help you build life-changing wealth with a single exchange-traded fund.

The first step to building wealth is to live below your means so you have money to invest. This key step sounds obvious, but it is truly shocking how many people fall short before they even have a chance to start building wealth. Saving is really just delaying spending today so you can, hopefully, spend more in the future. Moreover, if you can create a habit of saving, you can continue to add to your investments over time.

So saving is actually two key wealth-building steps in one. First, it allows you to start your investment journey. Second, you can buy more of your chosen investments each month, which is known as dollar-cost averaging. Dollar-cost averaging not only allows you to grow your investments with additional cash but also tends to result in a lower average purchase price over time.

Once you have the cash to invest, you have to choose an investment path. As noted, Warren Buffett has suggested that most investors keep it simple and buy a low-cost S&P 500 index fund. Vanguard S&P 500 ETF, noted above, has a tiny expense ratio of 0.03%, which is as close to free on Wall Street as you are likely to get.

Notably, the S&P 500 index is basically what most investors consider "the market." It is a committee-selected group of roughly 500 large companies that are representative of the U.S. economy. If you buy it, you are guaranteed to get average results, which is more than enough to build life-changing wealth over time. As the chart below highlights, the S&P 500 index has trended steadily higher, even though it has experienced numerous downturns, known as bear markets, along the way.

This is why buying and holding is so important. Even if you bought right before a bear market, history shows that you'll end up OK if you don't panic and sell. Every bear market so far has been followed by the market going on to post new all-time highs. In fact, buying right through a bear market is what makes dollar-cost averaging work. And Buffett's suggestion to buy the S&P 500 is also important because it keeps your investment plan simple. It is likely to be easier to stick to a simple plan than one that is overly complex.

If you do buy an S&P 500 ETF, you'll be collecting dividends along the way, and there could even be some capital gains distributions. Put all of that cash right back into the S&P 500 ETF you've chosen. That allows you to buy more without having to save more, which is basically what's known as compounding.

At some point, you may want to use the dividends and other distributions you get to pay for living expenses. That's a great way to help supplement your Social Security checks in retirement. But until that point, you should resist the urge to spend that money and put it back to work.

For some people, buying an S&P 500 index ETF won't be the right fit. That's OK; you need to find an investment approach that suits your personal needs and temperament. However, the three key steps don't change: Save, invest for the long term, and reinvest. If you get those three down, you will be well set up to build life-changing wealth.
