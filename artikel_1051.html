'Special' teen Williams-Barnett scores to dazzle in Spurs' win over Ipswich

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Tottenham Hotspur vs Ipswich Town. Premier League.

Report and free match highlights as teenager Luca Williams-Barnett stars in Tottenham's 2-0 home win over Ipswich; the 18-year-old made Spurs history with his 15th-minute opener; England U19 international then involved in Dominic Solanke's second-half goal as Ipswich go five without a win

Teenager Luca Williams-Barnett scored on his first Tottenham start to make history in Spurs’ 2-0 home win over Ipswich.

The 18-year-old bundled home the opener at the back post in the 15th minute to become the youngest Spurs player to score on his first Premier League start for the club.

The Spurs academy graduate then turned provider in the second half as he terrorised the Ipswich defence from his left-wing position to tee up Dominic Solanke with a low cross for a simple 51st-minute finish.

But Ipswich goalkeeper Christian Walton's diversion into Solanke's path meant Williams-Barnett was not credited with the assist officially.

It meant Williams-Barnett, at the age of 18 years 51 days, just missed out on becoming the second-youngest player to both score and assist in a Premier League game (Michael Owen in December 1997 at 17y 364d).

The teenager was substituted in the 64th minute to a standing ovation and rapturous cheers from the Spurs fans, who had serenaded Williams-Barnett with Harry Kane's 'He's one of our own' chant - raising hopes that the club may have unearthed a new academy hero.

The England U19 international's dazzling display inspired Tottenham to a third consecutive home league victory for the first time in two years, lifting Roberto De Zerbi's side to ninth and within three points of the top five.

But the Italian had to watch the deserved win from the stands as he served a one-game touchline ban after receiving three yellow cards this season.

His opposite number Gary O'Neil, meanwhile, saw his side's winless run extend to five games as Ipswich rarely threatened the home side to leave the Tractor Boys in 16th and just two points above the relegation zone.

Spurs head coach De Zerbi believes Williams-Barnett is a "special player" and is a similar talent to Barcelona teenage sensation Lamine Yamal.

De Zerbi said at his post-match press conference: "He is a special player. I'm not surprised because his talent is bigger than you think.

"He played very well. He's lucky because he's playing in a top club with top team-mates.

"Yesterday evening, when I gave the first 11 to the players, I said, 'remember your debut and help the kid tomorrow, because he's one of us and if he plays well, we have more chances to win the game'.

"He's very young but at the same time he's a mature guy. He understands the right behaviour to keep in the dressing room - and he's older than his age."

He added: "The first day I spoke with Luca, I asked him two things: to play like he plays on the park with his friends, with the same freedom and to play and to work hard without the ball, like [Ousmane] Dembele, like Raphinha, like [Lamine] Yamal, because football is going in a different way and the first defenders are really the attackers even if they are Ballon d'Or or now the wingers or any players with talent, they have to work on the pitch."

Then asked whether Williams-Barnett is a special talent like PSG's Ballon d'Or winner Dembele and Spain's World Cup-winner Lamine Yamal, De Zerbi replied: "Yes, I think so.

"One of the best things in my career as a player, I've seen today on the bus from the training ground to the stadium. He was very close to me on the bus, and while we were coming to the stadium, he was watching Ronaldinho and Hazard on YouTube.

"I thought he was a special guy because, one hour and 30 minutes before your debut in the Premier League, you are watching Ronaldinho, [Eden] Hazard, Neymar, all this type of talent.

"Football is magic, no? The qualities, okay, but also the passion - you understand football, how football can be."

What a memorable full debut for Williams-Barnett.

After a handful of encouraging substitute displays this season, the 18-year-old was deservedly handed his first Spurs start - and boy did he take it.

He dazzled on the left wing to become the youngest Spurs player to score in the Premier League since Gareth Bale against Fulham in September 2007 (18y 47d).

Bale would go on to become a Spurs great, and there will be optimism that Williams-Barnett can do the same.

Every time he touched the ball in the win over Ipswich, there was real excitement at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, which has been in short supply in recent times.

He was a constant threat to the Ipswich defence as defenders backed off him, which exactly led to Solanke's goal.

The stats back up his impressive performance - he had the most touches in the opposition box (8) and big chances (2) of any player, while no player had more than his four shots on target.

For all of Spurs' big summer spending, their new hero looks to be from within.
