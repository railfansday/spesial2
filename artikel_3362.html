Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders Drop My Chemical Romance Lawsuit, but War of Words Rages On

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders have formally dismissed their lawsuit claiming My Chemical Romance infringed on the cheer squad’s trademarked uniforms during its recent run of concerts at the Hollywood Bowl. The dismissals, filed Monday in Texas federal court, came after the band wrapped up its final shows at the famed venue celebrating the 20th anniversary of its 2006 classic, The Black Parade, whose anniversary reissue reached the top of the Billboard 200.

But even with the dismissals, Live Nation is still calling a foul. In a filing Monday responding to the dismissals, the concert promoter accused the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders and their lawyers of twisting the facts by claiming they had extracted a “concession” from My Chemical Romance to “stop wearing the DCC uniform.” Live Nation, which said it was the only defendant formally served with the lawsuit, insisted that neither it nor the band had made any such concession. Instead, it said, the cheerleading squad and its parent company, Pro Silver Star, had been told more than a week earlier that the costumes were already retired before the lawsuit was officially filed and would never be used again.

Live Nation’s position remains that use of the uniforms in a satirical rock opera that was performed on Oct. 21, 23, and 24 was a parody protected by the First Amendment. According to the new Monday filing, Live Nation and the band still “explicitly contest that the shows were infringing.”

“There is no merit to plaintiff’s assertions that an agreement was reached between the parties to discontinue the performances,” Live Nation’s filing states. “My Chemical Romance’s performances of the third part of the rock opera ended October 24, 2026, not because of any agreement, but because they were always scheduled to end that day.”

The iconic cheer team filed its lawsuit last month, claiming the knockoff versions of its signature blue-and-white uniforms were used without authorization during a mock gun battle onstage. They said the costumes infringed on the squad’s protectable trade dress and “degraded” the DCC brand, so their lawyers sought an emergency restraining order ahead of two more shows set for Oct. 30 and 31. In a filing last Friday, Live Nation and its lawyers defended the lookalike uniforms as “artistic expression” and said Pro Silver Star was trying “to use trademark law to line edit theatrical staging at a Los Angeles concert.” They called the performance a “successful parody” where no viewers believed the dancers were real Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders.

“Recognition is not confusion,” the Friday filing said. “Observers understood the performance as an allusive, exaggerated, theatrical reference within a My Chemical Romance show.”

Lawyers for DCC did not respond to multiple requests for comment, including on Monday.

No lawyer for the band appeared at what was expected to be a courtroom showdown last week, and a note on the court’s docket in Collin County, Texas, said that an agreement had been reached. Live Nation’s lawyers said in the new filing that My Chemical Romance was never served, so it had no obligation to appear.

“In sum, neither Live Nation nor My Chemical Romance ever agreed to alter any of the planned shows in response to plaintiff’s lawsuit, nor do they concede any of their defenses to the claims in this lawsuit,” the concert giant’s new filing, which was submitted Monday in federal court after Live Nation sought to move the case there last week, argues.
