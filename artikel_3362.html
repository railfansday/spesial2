Oregon-Based Laika Set To Give Thanks To Portland For Success Of ‘Wildwood’ By Offering Free Screenings At Theaters Citywide Over Thanksgiving Holiday Weekend

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

EXCLUSIVE: Portland-based Laika‘s critically acclaimed animated feature, Wildwood, has grossed $50 million so far after its first month in release and still going strong. In an unprecedented move Laika is giving back to the Portland Oregon community where the film not only was made, but also is set.

This Thanksgiving weekend , specifically on November 29, the studio will open the doors and invite local audiences to complimentary screenings all across town. So far 15 theaters, adding up to 7900 seats and 37+ showings, have already signed on and that number is expected to grow substantially. The idea to do this citywide free event actually came from its legendary six time-Oscar nominated cinematographer Caleb Deschanel who spent a good deal of time in Portland making the stop motion epic directed by Travis Knight who is also CEO of Laika. Portland has been one of the top grossing markets for the film – seven of the top ten markets are in the city – but rather than continuing to make residents pay to see their moment of cinema glory, Laika is offering the chance for everyone to see it for free. The screenings are part of a wider Thanksgiving homecoming celebration, which also includes a special Wildwood soundtrack event featuring Portland musician Colin Meloy at the Bagdad Theater on Friday evening, November 27.

“Portland isn’t just where we make our films. It’s part of who we are,” said David Burke, LAIKA’s Chief Marketing and Operations Officer and Executive Producer on Wildwood. “Wildwood was inspired by this city, brought to life by an extraordinary community of artists here, and embraced by hometown audiences in a way that has been incredibly meaningful to all of us. This Thanksgiving, we want to say thank you by bringing Portlanders together to share something special at the movies.” Watch on Deadline

Created by more than 1,200 artists and technologists, Wildwood represents an ambitious achievement in handcrafted filmmaking. From its intricate puppets and costumes to its elaborate sets and fantastical landscapes, the film reflects the devotion to the unique art of Stop Motion animation that has defined Laika’s work in Oregon for more than two decades. The company’s previous five animated Stop Motion features – Coraline, ParaNorman, The Boxtrolls, Kubo and the Two Strings, and Missing Link – have each been nominated for the Best Animated Feature Academy Award and current odds favor Wildwood to likely make it six in a row.
