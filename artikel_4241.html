This spooky weekend I am finally playing The Blood of Dawnwalker on PS5. What. A. Game. I have been pleasantly surprised by what this game offers, and its unique experience with time management.

I like how personal the entire narrative feels. It manages to accomplish what other open world games fail to do: provide a sense of urgency. More often than not, there are games set in a sprawling world that threatens the end of existence but allows you to explore and take your time, undercutting the story. Dawnwalker doesn’t skimp on this at all.

It’s been a long while since I’ve played an RPG that had me genuinely questioning my actions. With its unique time mechanic, I’m thinking of what quests — main and side — I should prioritize. You would think this would be a hindrance, and restrictive, but it actually enhances the experience because it respects your time.

The writing is top-notch because each quest feels important, thus the feeling of missing content due to time doesn’t seem like a loss. You never feel like you’re making the wrong choice in what to focus on.

Moreover, I find all of the characters to be really intriguing. I’m invested in their stories and curious to see how my decisions impact them. There have been a few reactions that have caught me off guard. Even minor, typical open world tropes such as “clear out this encampment” make a meaningful difference. Once again, the world feels alive and significant because I’m making decisions that can alter the outcome in drastic ways, to people whom are struggling to make a living, but also living in constant fear.

The combat is also really fun to me. It’s not for everyone, but once you get a grasp at the parrying and the many abilities at your disposal, it becomes an intense battle of nutrition. That’s not to say it’s not without its jank. You can get stuck on corners. And once you are in a confined area you are committed. The lock on can also be finicky at times, but it also adds to the adrenaline.

Overall, I’m really impressed by this game. It obviously has The Witcher DNA in terms of graphics, combat, and world design, but that’s not a bad thing, is it?
