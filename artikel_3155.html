Lightbridge (LTBR) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

DOE LaunchPad selection accelerates pilot factory development at Idaho National Laboratory.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Lightbridge Corporation (LTBR -8.46%) reported that its selection for the Department of Energy's LaunchPad program establishes an accelerated path for manufacturing lead test assemblies through a dedicated pilot facility at the Idaho National Laboratory. Management stated that this facility, designated as SHED, will operate under Department of Energy authorization to potentially shorten commercialization timelines. Executives noted that the recent agreement for loop irradiation testing in the Advanced Test Reactor allows for fuel rodlets to be tested under conditions representative of commercial pressurized water reactors. The company also reported progress in licensing its intellectual property, including a patent allowance in Japan for its multi-zone fuel design and additive manufacturing techniques. Management confirmed that the current liquidity position provides flexibility to fund expanded research and development as the company enters a more resource-intensive phase of its development program.

Seth Grae: Hello, Matt, and thank you all for joining us to discuss Lightbridge's Third Quarter 2026 business update. The third quarter brought what we believe is the most significant advance to date in Lightbridge's path from fuel testing to commercial development. In August, Lightbridge was selected to participate in the Nuclear Energy LaunchPad INL program administered by the U.S. Department of Energy's National Research Innovation Center (sic) [ National Reactor Innovation Center ] at Idaho National Laboratory. Selection provides an accelerated pathway toward DOE authorization for us to design, build and operate a pilot facility at INL to manufacture lead test assemblies of Lightbridge Fuel for demonstration in U.S. commercial power reactors.

We now call that facility, the Lightbridge Fuel Pilot Facility, which we informally referred to as SHED. By establishing a dedicated manufacturing facility under DOE authorization, we expect to shorten the time line for producing lead test assemblies for operation in commercial U.S. reactors. That gives us a two-pronged approach. The pilot factory is designed to produce lead test assemblies under DOE authorization while we continue to develop a conceptual design for our future commercial scale plans, the Lightbridge Fuel Factory, formerly referred to as the Lightbridge Expandable Fuel Facility. The Lightbridge Fuel Factory will still be expandable even though it doesn't have that word in its name. Alongside that milestone, we advanced in other key areas during the quarter.

In August, we entered into a new Project Task Statement with Idaho National Laboratory that gives us access to the Loop Facility of the Advanced Test Reactor where we will irradiate clad Lightbridge Fuel rodlets under the conditions of a fuel rod would experience in a commercial light water reactor. And in September, we received notice of -- allowance of a patent from the Japan patent office covering our multi-zone fuel element and 3D printing method. I will now turn the call over to Andrey Mushakov, Executive Vice President and Chief Operating Officer, to walk through our fuel development program highlights in detail. Andrey?

Andrey Mushakov: Thank you, Seth. As Seth mentioned, our focus since the launch pad selection has been on preparing the pilot factory. We're currently working with NRIC and INL to negotiate the applicable agreements and project scope. The authorization to construct and operate the pilot factory remains subject to additional DOE and INL agreements, reviews, authorizations and other prerequisites before construction can start. And as of September 30, 2026, we had not entered into any material binding commitments for its construction. We plan to design the pilot factory as a multipurpose fuel manufacturing and R&D platform rather than a facility dedicated solely to the single fuel research project and it will have a dedicated research and development laboratory.

The pilot factory will operate under an NQA-1 Nuclear Quality Assurance Program from the outset. I also want to be clear the DOE authorization of the pilot factory does not constitute authorization for lead test assemblies to be used in commercial power reactors. Any such use would remain subject to U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission licensing, review and approval of lead test assemblies as well as any required approvals by the host utility. The pilot facility will only ever be DOE authorized and not subject to NRC licensing. Development of the pilot factory and our planned fabrication of lead test assemblies will require qualified source of uranium feedstock, including high-assay low-enriched uranium and uranium metal.

We are evaluating potential supply sources, alternative supply arrangements and related infrastructure requirements to support these activities. Separately, we continue to build the organization at Lightbridge. Idaho Falls is now home to more Lightbridge employees than any other location, and we expect to hire additional engineering and technical personnel as the pilot factory and our fuel development work progress. Approach remains disciplined and aligned with program needs so that we attract top talent and build a world-class team while maintaining capital efficiency. With that, I'll turn the call over to Scott.

Scott Holcombe: Thank you, Andrey. I will start with the Advanced Test Reactor Loop, which is the milestone Seth mentioned. In August, we entered into Project Task Statement #2 under our cooperative research and development agreement with Battelle Energy Alliance, the DOE operating contractor for Idaho National Laboratory, which we announced in September. It gives us access to the loop testing facility of the ATR. Unlike other in-core positions, a loop irradiates fuel within its own independently controlled environment, the coolant chemistry, temperature, pressure and flow a fuel rod experience in a commercial light water reactor. There are a limited number of positions in the world where that is possible, and we're now able to place our fuel into one of them.

Under this task statement, we will test enriched uranium and zirconium fuel rodlets with cladding, both cylindrical and multi-lobe geometry. The scope of work includes experiment design, provision of materials, assembly, irradiation and shipment and disposition of materials. Samples from this irradiation are later intended for further testing under transient conditions in INL's transient reactor test facility. The rodlets for that test build on our fabrication work with INL under SPP Project Task Statement #6 which supports the development of co-extruded fuel rod components for future loop irradiation testing in the ATR and transient testing. That program begins with depleted uranium and upon successful demonstration is expected to move to enriched uranium to produce fuel rod segments for irradiation experiments.

Turning to the first batch of fuel material samples removed from the ATR in May, postirradiation examination has begun. That examination will collect data on fundamental material properties at various burn-up levels which is what is required to validate and verify our field performance models, and it becomes part of the basis of our licensing work. Back to you, Seth.

Seth Grae: Thank you, Scott. The policy environment continues to move towards the market Lightbridge fuel is designed for. In June, I represented Lightbridge at the White House, launch of UPRISE. The Department of Energy initiative targeting approximately 5 gigawatts of additional nuclear capacity from reactors that are already operating. Lightbridge Fuel is designed to increase the power output of an existing reactor by up to 10% with no change to the plant's steam generators and up to 20% for plants that move to a high-capacity steam generator. New build reactors equipped with higher capacity steam generators could see an increase of roughly 1/3. Our testing and licensing program exists to confirm those design targets.

Commercial deployment also requires fuel material, and that supply must be arranged years in advance. In July, we signed a Memorandum of Understanding with Quadrant Nuclear Industries to establish a framework for discussions regarding potential long-term supply of HALEU from QNI's planned Vanguard facility at Idaho National Laboratory. The memorandum is nonbinding and does not establish pricing, quantity or exclusivity commitments and any binding terms will be subject to a definitive agreement. We continue to believe the prospects for growth in nuclear power are the strongest they have ever been.

There are approximately 440 operating power reactors worldwide today to triple nuclear power globally and quadruple it in the United States by 2050, the world will need more power from reactors already operating, not only from those still to be built. Lightbridge Fuel is designed to deliver exactly that within the same-sized cores of new reactors and plants that exist today with even greater safety and also in new reactors.
