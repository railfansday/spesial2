I totally get this and have the same problem. I'm spending 1% and that's with some aggressive spending compared to what I had done most of my adult life.

2% is below the SWR level, but opinions vary, but it's not super lean. Also recognize that we've had fabulous returns in recent years, so at some point that will likely change and your 2% could become 3%. Not predicting that necessarily but it could happen and things will feel different without the cushion of earned income.

Some random ideas on spending:

Upgrade your house and furnish it using an interior decorator. I did this and not sure if I'm happier, but my place is nice!

Get a sports car if you always wanted one. I got a Mustang, which is an affordable entry level, and it's pretty fun. Sits in the garage..

Get some custom clothes. I always wanted to do this, and should have done it 10 years ago, but finally did. Spent $10k. It's nice to have some fine threads.

Travel more. This is hitting the easy button. Plan some trips, work the bucket list. I still can't justify flying first class..but I've had some great guided tours where everything is provided. Just visited Italy and had a wonderful time. More destinations are planned.

Support charities. Here, I've struggled to find a cause I really feel passionate about. But everyone has different values. Depending on what it is, you might be able to make a large enough donation to get more actively involved, performing arts for example.

Get a vacation home. This is the #1 recommendation I get. I've considered it and just don't want the trouble of it. I think it would be a burden.

Get a professionally installed stereo system for your vehicle. This is a small one, but I recently did it. I'm thumping now rolling down the street!

Help relatives. Another common suggestion. I'd like to, but not sure if I'd do more harm than good. I just can't do it.

Doordash it. Don't feel like cooking or going out? Order it in and don't worry about the outrageous markups. I do this all the time.

In any event, enjoy your circumstance. Everyone says spend on experiences. I agree with this. But also get you some "things" you always wanted. Why not? Interested in super fancy knives or guns? Get a few. Just don't create burdens on yourself. Burdens that come to mind are the vacation home, an RV, or a boat. If you want those, get them. Just think about the upkeep and ongoing expenses.
