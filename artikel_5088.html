Ripple CTO Reminds: XRP’s Got No Coercive Mechanism

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Home » Ripple (XRP) » Ripple CTO Reminds: XRP’s Got No Coercive Mechanism

David ‘JoelKatz’ Schwartz reminds the crypto crowd that the majority desire still can’t force anyone to play along.

David Schwartz, CTO Emeritus at Ripple and one of the original architects of the XRP Ledger, dropped a clear-eyed reminder on X today about how XRPL consensus actually works.

In response to a community discussion about whether an 80%+ supermajority of nodes could push through a change (in the context of ongoing chatter about Ripple’s large escrow holdings), the famed computer virtuoso JoelKatz spat out:

“If the majority of nodes want something, nothing can stop them from having it. They just can’t force those who don’t agree to go along with them. But the same is true for a minority of nodes too. There’s no coercive mechanism at all.”

David Schwartz’s comment cuts straight to the design philosophy of the XRP Ledger. A group of nodes (even a large majority) can coordinate, run the software they prefer, and activate features among themselves.

What they cannot do is compel every other participant to accept those rules. Dissenting operators simply continue running the version and Unique Node List (UNL) configuration they choose.

No. They'd just disagree with everyone else who didn't want that change and be counted as disagreeing validators, effectively isolating themselves from the network.

The result is either continued coexistence or, if the disagreement is deep enough, a clean split.

This is the same principle that has allowed previous XRPL-related networks to emerge. There is no on-chain “force” button, no automatic override of node operators who decline an update, and no mechanism that lets one side confiscate or rewrite the state of the other side.

The timing is not accidental. Segments of the XRP community have repeatedly floated the idea of burning or otherwise neutralizing large portions of Ripple’s escrowed XRP.

Some have argued that a sufficiently high validator threshold (the ledger currently requires roughly 80% support sustained over two weeks for amendments) should be enough to make such a change binding.

Schwartz’s reply undercuts that assumption: even overwhelming validator support does not create the power to compel non-consenting nodes. Any side that wants different rules must convince node operators to run the code that implements them.

If the majority of nodes want something, nothing can stop them from having it. They just can't force those who don't agree to go along with them. But the same is true for a minority of nodes too. There's no coercive mechanism at all.

Validators coordinate activation; they do not govern the software choices of independent operators.

Schwartz has made versions of this argument for years. Today’s post simply restates it in the face of fresh pressure around escrow and governance. XRP Ledger’s architecture continues to prioritize the absence of coercion over the ability of any group to impose its will on everyone else.

In a nutshell: the majority can have what it wants. It just cannot make the rest of the network come along for the ride.
