Jets getting much-needed defensive reinforcement with David Onyemata’s return

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Three years ago, David Onyemata missed three games with an ankle injury. In 2020, the defensive tackle missed one game with a calf injury.

Those four games represented the entirety of his injury-related absences during his first 10 years in the NFL.

However, it has been nearly 10 weeks since the 33-year-old has taken the field — following core-muscle surgery — after just two appearances with the Jets.

"It's part of the business," Onyemata said. "Things like this happen. You try not to think too much about it. You just try to stay involved mentally and physically; just be present in the moment and present for the guys."

Though Onyemata is listed as questionable for Sunday's game at Miami, the veteran — who was a full participant in practice all week — told The Post that he will return to the field for the first time since Week 2.

Jets defensive lineman David Onyemata practices during training camp on Aug. 17, 2026, in Florham Park, N.J. Bill Kostroun/New York Post

"I feel great," Onyemata said. "Getting back out there, getting in the rhythm of things and being with the guys has been great."

Onyemata — who came from Atlanta on a one-year, $10.5 million deal — was a much-needed 300-pound addition, signed to help overhaul a defense that finished 29th in the league against the run in head coach Aaron Glenn's first season.

Last season, Onyemata — who played for the Saints when Glenn was a defensive coach there — ranked eighth overall and against the run, out of 127 interior defensive linemen, according to Pro Football Focus.

The Nigeria-born defender has played just 45 snaps with the Jets, who rank 30th in the league in rushing yards allowed per game (137.4) and tied for last in rushing first downs allowed per game (8.3).

"[He's] a veteran presence and someone that knows the system inside and out," Glenn said Friday. "Extremely tough, extremely violent, and there is a trust there."

Glenn's defensive line could also soon be boosted by the return of T'Vondre Sweat (knee), who was placed on injured reserve following the Week 7 loss to the Dolphins.

Jets defensive lineman David Onyemata speaks to reporters after a training practice on Aug. 17, 2026 in Florham Park, N.J. Bill Kostroun/New York Post

The 25-year-old, 366-pound defensive tackle — who was acquired from the Titans in an offseason trade for Jermaine Johnson — opened his 21-day practice window this week and was a full participant in every session since Wednesday, but he is listed as questionable.

Glenn made it clear how much it would mean for the defense to bring back the former second-round pick, who has 17 tackles and seven quarterback pressures in seven games (six starts) this season.

"When it comes to Sweat, when you build your defense, you want to build it through the middle, and he's a guy that we always felt could be that key cog in the middle of our defense," Glenn said. "Both of those guys we've talked about at length about what they bring to the table."

Their absences have been especially glaring in the past three games, as the Jets have allowed an average of 174 yards rushing on 5.6 yards per carry.

In last month's loss to the Dolphins, Ollie Gordon II ran for a season-high 103 yards and a touchdown. This weekend, Miami may lean on the running back even more, with Kyle McCord set to make his third career start at quarterback.

"I think they're doing a good job of keeping it simple and saying, 'Hey we're gonna do it this way and we're gonna beat you doing some simple things,' " Jets defensive tackle Harrison Phillips said. "They've found their rhythm running the football a little bit more … I think it's a little bit more of a hard-nosed, 'Hey, we might even be telling you what we're gonna do. Can you stop it?' "
