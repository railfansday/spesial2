Philip Rivers drops ‘this is home’ admission on new NC State football gig

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Philip Rivers drops 'this is home' admission on new NC State football gig appeared first on ClutchPoints. Add ClutchPoints as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

Philip Rivers is making it clear that taking over as NC State's head football coach was about more than just landing another opportunity. For the former NFL quarterback, returning to his home state and leading the Wolfpack was the opportunity he wanted.

Rivers appeared on The Pat McAfee Show on Tuesday and discussed his decision to take the job, making it clear that NC State was the place he wanted to be.

"I wasn't always around there but I was always pulling for the Pack," Rivers said. "There is no place in college football that would have been intriguing for me. This is home."

He added, "I'm fired up to be leading the Pack."

Rivers has already held his introductory press conference, but he won't officially take over the program until after the current season. Interim head coach Todd Goebbel is finishing out the remaining games of the 2026 regular season. That hasn't stopped Rivers from getting around the team and beginning the transition, although there are limits to what he can do before officially stepping into the role.

He even had to be careful when discussing his son, Gunner Rivers, during his appearance with McAfee. Since Gunner has not officially signed his commitment papers, Rivers couldn't even mention his name.

That gives a glimpse into how unusual this transition period can be for a coach who is eager to get started but still has to wait for the season to conclude.

Rivers is certainly an intriguing hire for NC State. He brings a recognizable name and a deep connection to the state, but the bigger storyline will be what he can build once he officially takes control.

It will be interesting to see how Rivers approaches rebuilding the Wolfpack and what the program looks like once he gets to put his vision into action. For now, he has made one thing clear: he believes NC State is home.
