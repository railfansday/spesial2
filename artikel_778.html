Wale on Triumphant Pair of 2027 Grammy Nominations for ‘Everything Is a Lot’: ‘I Hope It Teaches a Lot of People About Resilience’

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

"Vote for Wale. I'm gonna draft up the greatest speech of all time," the DMV rapper tells Billboard.

Wale is still training his mind to have a positive outlook when it comes to musical accolades. As a longtime fan of the snakebitten Washington Commanders and Wizards franchises, one would understand his built-in hesitancy.

However, Monday (Nov. 16) is a different story. Fresh off a flight to NYC, Folarin is riding high after earning a pair of 2027 Grammy Award nominations tied to his 2025 LP Everything Is a Lot.

The DMV rap veteran picked up his first Grammy nomination for 2011’s top 40 Billboard Hot 100 hit “Lotus Flower Bomb.” But even after a handful of multiplatinum Hot 100 hits and critically acclaimed projects, the Grammys never came calling back.

Fifteen years and six albums later, that changed Monday morning with Everything Is a Lot. notching a best rap album nomination, along with “Watching Us” — which topped the Rhythmic Airplay chart — featuring Grammy darling Leon Thomas being nominated for best melodic rap performance.

“I’m just grateful, full of gratitude,” Wale says on a call in the car, his voice a bit hoarse. “15 years was my one and only nomination. I always wondered how I would feel if I got any of these things. There’s a part of my brain that was like, ‘Man, that sh– ain’t gonna happen.’ But then there’s another part of me that was like, ‘Bro, do you realize like the one and only joint was 15 years ago?’ I don’t know if too many people could say that.”

The 42-year-old wordsmith continues: “To get it 15 years later, and then one of them to be for [best] rap album, it just it means a lot, bro. It’s like the white whale, I guess they call it, you just can’t never catch. So it just, it’s a lot of emotion. … I’m still kind of taking it all in. Like I don’t want to be that guy that’s going crazy just for a nomination, but I am.”

Wale is used to brushing off compliments about his work, but this time around he knew something felt different, pointing to a conversation with DaBaby, who showed his latest album love. “[DaBaby’s] like, ‘Man, that album.’ And I’m like, ‘OK, this is different,'” he recalls. “This ain’t just people that know me. There’s people that don’t have no reason to say this to me. But again, I still have my guard up because I didn’t want to wake up with nothing and then just be dejected. But we don’t have to worry about that no more.”

The pair of Grammy nominations fittingly came just two days after the first anniversary of Everything Is a Lot., which Wale championed as the “most important” project of his career. His first release with Def Jam, the 2025 album debuted inside the Billboard 200‘s top 20 and “Watching Us,” which reached No. 76 on the Hot 100, was the last track to make the LP’s cut.

“I can’t wait to put him in a mother—-ing headlock,” he quips about the next time he sees Thomas. “Golly, that’s a buzzer beater right there. That was the last song we did, like two, three days before we submitted the album. Me and Leon are studio mad men. We we kept changing s–t and then brought it back to how it originally was. Just it’s good to get one with my dog. When we dropped it, all the fans was like, ‘Oh man, this is gonna go for next year.’ And I’m like, ‘Bro, it’s way too early, like chill out.’ But they was right too.”

There have been times when it’s felt like Wale hasn’t gotten to enjoy the fruits of his labor compared to some of his 2010s rap contemporaries when he’s been counted out by hip-hop pundits. But it was his persistence that’s allowed him to continue raising the bar two decades into his journey, and let these Grammy nominations serve as validation for his trials and tribulations. “I hope it teaches a lot of people about resilience and also having the right people around you,” he adds, giving his team much-deserved flowers.

As a streetwear savant and one of rap’s fashion-forward thinkers, his mind’s already cycling through possible looks for the red carpet come Grammy night on Feb. 7 in Los Angeles. “Vote for Wale. I’m gonna draft up the greatest speech of all time. The fit, we already thinking about it. I’m mentally already there. I got good seats this year. I might show up with a Caesar, man,” he jokingly teases. “I might cut it all off.”
