Washington Spirit provide hope and joy in a city that badly needs it

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

In the moments after the Washington Spirit's 2-1 semi-final victory over the Utah Royals last weekend, all of Audi Field was in rapture. In the stands, around 20,000 Spirit supporters had filled the stadium to capacity, the sixth time they've done so in 2026. They filled the place with the type of wondrous noise that's become alien in DC sports in recent years, the type of gleeful adoration reserved for the city's rare winners. The Spirit will return to their home stadium to face the Kansas City Current in the NWSL championship this Saturday.

In short order, the Spirit's players made their way to the north end of the stadium, taking their celebrations into the crowd with their supporters. Perhaps no player has done more to push the Spirit into not only local but national relevance than US women's national team forward Trinity Rodman, and Rodman herself grabbed a flag from a fan, jubilantly waving it above the crowd. Moments later, she bolted back onto the field and track down the club's owner, Michele Kang, cajoling the businesswoman into a celebratory dance.

In the echo of all of this, many media members covering the match in the press box received an email from DC United, the local Major League Soccer club that operates and rents out Audi Field to the Spirit. It was a press release announcing the dismissal of their head coach, René Weiler. As the Spirit celebrated a third consecutive championship appearance on the pitch below, some unfortunate soul in a nearby office was tasked with sending that notice, the final nail in yet another dreadful season. While the women have thrived, the local men's team has not made the playoffs in nearly a decade.

The Spirit, who share not only United's stadium but also their training facility, are not asking to be compared to them. In reality no NWSL team is, as the audiences for men's and women's teams in the same city frequently prove to be quite different. Yet the differing fortunes of the two clubs and the stark difference in atmosphere at their games makes it nearly impossible not to do so. Once-mighty United, four-time MLS Cup champions, have become a laughingstock. That has not stopped the club's ownership from seeking hundreds of millions of dollars from the DC government to renovate their eight-year-old stadium. While they lobby for that assistance, their matches have become borderline funerary affairs.

The Spirit? The club has become a beacon of hope and joy in a city that badly needs it. The District is a wildly diverse, deeply progressive place and its residents have struggled at times to come to terms with the political realities of hosting the current presidential administration. Thousands of National Guard troops roam the streets, more than a year into an anti-crime deployment that many believe hasn't helped the city at all. In the shadow of all of this, Spirit matches have become a refuge. And while much of that relief comes from soccer the club plays – technical, entertaining – much of it comes from a deeper place.

"I think it's the way that we bring the fans into everything that we do," Rodman told the Guardian. "I think the way we interact with them after games, it's not just a meet and greet. We truly do have conversations, and it feels like they're our friends in a way. I think that's amazing. The way that we celebrate, we're always celebrating and interacting with them in the stadium - I feel like it's a very safe, inclusive environment.

"There is always new people coming to each and every game and what I've heard is that everybody feels comfortable when they come to a game, there's so many friends made. The comfort in it, the safety, the inclusivity of it at all."

The Spirit have been mentioned as potential benefactors of United's push for renovations at Audi Field, but Spirit ownership has long harbored bigger ambitions. The club have strong ties to the NFL's Washington Commanders, who are moving into a new 65,000-seat home as part of a $4bn deal in the coming years, and "opportunities for women's soccer" made up part of the pitch for that stadium's public funding. Kang has spoken about aspirations of constructing a standalone training facility and moving the club out of the one it shares with United, a glorified collection of shipping containers in exurban Loudoun county, Virginia.

"I'm hoping in the future we do have that," said Rodman. "Especially with how great we've done and how this organization is as a whole, that would be the cherry on top. Sharing facilities isn't the greatest for anybody; to be able to have our own facilities with our own logos on it, our own space, not worried about timing would be great and I'm hoping it happens soon."

All of it feels plausible. Should the Spirit remain competitive, and continue to endear to the District's residents, it almost feels probable.
