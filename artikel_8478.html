Personal Investments • Re: Cold feet [When can we securely retire?]

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Hi. My wife and I have over the last few years collected data and sought advice to inform the "when can we securely retire" question. The data and advice suggest retirement within the next two years is reasonable but as we're getting close enough to start making plans on that understanding there’s some “cold feet" happening. Below is a summary of our situation and advice thus far. Any further input very appreciated - thank you.

Ages: 56 (me), 52 (W)
Kids: One in college, one HS
House: Own (no mortgage)
Retirement Target: Mid-2027 (me); Early 2029 (W)

Education

College: For an average cost of $70k/year we saved $560k (in 529s and a dedicated brokerage, from which we have started spending for first kid).
Graduate School: We want to have available $100k for each kid if they choose further education. We've saved $67k thus far in a brokerage toward this goal. (The remainder would need to come from retirement investments.)

Investments

$4.9M (80/20% allocation; 401ks, IRAs, brokerages equities; mostly in low-cost passive Vanguard funds; house, education funds not included; $400k+ in Cash/Laddered CDs/Bonds)

Expenses

We've tracked actual expenses (by item and category) for the last 4 years and have modeled expenses in Boldin and PlanVision based on those actuals but with costs tapering down gradually as our kids start their own lives. Yearly expenses (excluding taxes, as Boldin and PV handle):
  • $170k/year (current)
  • $160k/year (when last kid leaves house)
  • $145k/year (when both kids are economically independent a ~decade from now)
  • Medical: $20k/year (while on employer plan, more than current actuals); $40k/year between working and Medicare
  • Additional modeled expenses: $50k * 2 = $100k for weddings; $100k *2 = $200k for graduate school; $150k in new cars over the years (all current dollars)
Boldin / PlanVision Assessments

Boldin
We paid a few thousand dollars ealier this year for an assessment from Boldin. Results: 97% probability of success (not requiring cuts; with Boldin default rate assumptions per our allocations).

PlanVision
Successful (with no cuts and PV rate assumptions) but with smaller legacy than indicated by Boldin.

Plan for Retirement

Further education and potential adjunct teaching (me); mulling options (W); longer and more relaxed (but reasonably priced) vacations (with or without kids).

Concerns
  • Our kids are still young and will depend on us for some years. I am confident we could control and adjust spending as needed were it just the two of us but am uncomfortable retiring early then needing to cut back if needed while our kids remain economically dependent.
  • Quite the bull-market… SORR seems pretty real.
Net worth includes all assets and all liabilities. Include the house.


Without the house the 4% rule is met. You are good to go. Congratulations.
