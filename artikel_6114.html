Ryanair Cancels 16 Routes From 3 Busiest Airports: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

In November 2026, Ryanair and its various units will fly to 190+ destinations in Europe and Africa, reduced from 220+ during the summer. The ultra-low-cost carrier will be seen in 34 countries, with Italy, the UK, and Spain having the most flights.

Ryanair's three most-served airports remain London Stansted, Dublin, and Milan Bergamo. OAG data for the current month indicates the trio collectively have 404 daily departures, up 1% compared to November 2025. They're so critical that 30% of Ryanair's services touch them. Like everywhere else, they'll have no Ryanair flights on Christmas Day.

All the following routes, and those from Dublin and Bergamo, were served at some point between January 2026 and March 2027, with no flights scheduled beyond then. The increasingly popular destination of Rovaniemi, famous for Santa Claus, is not included. For obvious reasons, the route to Lapland only operates during the winter.

When writing, flights from Stansted to Rovaniemi are bookable through next March but not beyond then. Every year since the market's launch in 2022, services have been available at the end of October. But when writing, they do not exist for October 2027. They are likely to be added later, or the route's seasonal return has been pushed back to November, beyond the current schedule and booking periods.

When Ryanair's entire network is considered, the airline has entirely pulled out of serving Bodrum, Dalaman, and Vigo, with the ultra-low-cost carrier no longer serving Bremen from April next year. It has stopped flying to Turkey, with development hindered by a lack of open skies. Jet2.com continues to fly from Stansted to Bodrum and Dalaman, with multiple other operators doing so from other London-area airports.

It appears that Ryanair began flying from Dublin to Hahn, located nearly equidistant between Luxembourg and Frankfurt am Main, in May 2005. The airport is about 120 kilometers (75 km) from downtown Frankfurt.

That route's flights existed through 2019, when the carrier switched to Frankfurt International, influenced by that airport's then-reinvented financial incentive package. Service continued through 2022, when the airline pulled out of International, which was then Europe's fifth-busiest airport for flights. As such, Ryanair reintroduced Dublin to Hahn.

In the current month, Ryanair has an average of 129 daily departures from the Irish capital. Its offering exceeds that of Aer Lingus' short-haul activity by 55%, with Ireland's flag carrier having about 83 such departures. Given Ryanair only uses the Boeing 737-800 and 737 MAX 8-200, and that Aer Lingus Regional deploys ATR 72s on some Irish, UK, and French routes, the difference is much starker for seats for sale.
