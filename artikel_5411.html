Rocket, Mr. Cooper to pay $6.75M over cybersecurity lapses in New York

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Rocket Mortgage and Mr. Cooper Group agreed to pay $6.75 million to the New York State Department of Financial Services to settle alleged violations of the state’s cybersecurity regulations.  AI Summary

Rocket Mortgage and Mr. Cooper Group have agreed to pay a combined $6.75 million to the New York State Department of Financial Services (NYDFS) to resolve allegations of cybersecurity violations tied to data protection, risk assessments and incident reporting, the parties announced Thursday.

Rocket will pay $2 million to settle NYDFS claims that the company failed to report cybersecurity incidents in a timely manner, did not implement multifactor authentication, and lacked sufficient policies and procedures to safeguard customers’ nonpublic information. Mr. Cooper, meanwhile, will pay $4.75 million related to a November 2023 cyberattack that exposed sensitive borrower data and disrupted online operations.

In a statement given to HousingWire, a Rocket spokesperson said the company “has strengthened our information security program, adopted a formal data disposal policy, enhanced monitoring, team member training and testing.”

“Rocket Mortgage today entered two separate consent orders with the New York State Department of Financial Services,” the statement read. “One concerns a 2023 cybersecurity incident at Mr. Cooper, two years before Rocket’s acquisition. Rocket, as Mr. Cooper’s successor, will adhere to the required information security controls and audits. The second consent order concerns a 2021 on-site examination that identified gaps in Rocket Mortgage’s processes and procedures related to multi-factor authentication, secure data disposal and incident notification.”

NYDFS found that Rocket Mortgage failed to notify the department of cybersecurity incidents within the required 72 hours on seven occasions between December 2019 and August 2021. In six of the seven cases, NYDFS only learned of the incidents after examiners uncovered them during a 2021 on-site examination.

That exam also identified gaps in Rocket’s processes and procedures related to multifactor authentication, secure data disposal and incident notification, according to the consent order.

The NYDFS action against Mr. Cooper stems from a widely reported cybersecurity incident in November 2023 that forced the company to lock down certain technology systems, including access to its online payment portal.

According to the consent order, hackers impersonated employees on phone calls to the IT help desk to fraudulently obtain temporary passwords. They then used the credentials to gain access to company systems and bypass multifactor authentication controls.

NYDFS said Mr. Cooper had not completed a comprehensive cybersecurity risk assessment for almost four years leading up to the attack, leaving the servicer unprepared for the threat.

The department found that Mr. Cooper’s failure to protect nonpublic information stored within its systems resulted in the exposure of millions of individuals’ Social Security numbers, dates of birth and other nonpublic information, including data affecting hundreds of thousands of New York residents.

The incident also disrupted Mr. Cooper’s online business operations for five days, temporarily cutting off many customers from access to their accounts.

“New York’s cybersecurity regulation requires covered entities to maintain strong safeguards to protect consumer data, understand evolving risks, and report cybersecurity incidents to regulators,” NYDFS Acting Superintendent Kaitlin Asrow said in a statement. “The Department is committed to rigorous oversight to ensure regulated institutions protect consumers and adapt their cybersecurity programs to evolving risks.” Beyond the monetary penalty, Rocket is expected to strengthen its controls to protect its information systems and consumers’ private information.
