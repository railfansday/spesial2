State lawmaker pay in 20 states, from New York's $142,000 to New Mexico's $0

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

State legislator salaries run from $142,000 in New York to $0 in New Mexico, and these 20 states show the range in base pay and per diems

A New York state lawmaker earns $142,000 a year. A New Hampshire lawmaker earns $100 a year and gets no per diem. New Mexico sits outside that scale entirely. It is the one state that sets no legislative salary, though its legislators collect a per diem of $202 a day. Those figures come from Ballotpedia's 2025 comparison of state legislative salaries. They show how far state legislator salaries spread across the U.S.

All five legislatures that Ballotpedia names as its top five by pay operate full time. Every state in the lower half of this selection runs a hybrid or part-time legislature.

States also differ in who decides the pay. Commissions play that role in 19 states. Some of those commissions produce reports that bind no one. Others make recommendations that take effect unless the legislature, the governor or voters block them. In Arizona and Nebraska, voters must approve any recommended change. Some states tie legislative pay to what other state employees earn. Others let lawmakers set their own pay. The National Conference of State Legislatures calls the political risk of voting yourself a raise the "pay problem." Inflation can then outrun that pay. When it does, real salaries shrink over time.

This slideshow covers 20 of the 50 states. It includes 10 from the upper end of the annual base salaries in Ballotpedia's 2025 table and 10 from the lower end. Ballotpedia names only a top five. The rest of the order here comes from sorting the table's annual figures. States that pay lawmakers by the day, week, month or session are left off both lists. Ballotpedia gives no session lengths, so their pay cannot be placed on an annual scale. Those states are Kentucky, Montana, Nevada, Utah, Wyoming, Vermont, North Dakota and Maine. Washington state and Wisconsin fall just below Alabama and outside this selection. Each slide gives a state's base salary, its per diem and whether its legislature is full time, hybrid or part time.

New York pays its state legislators an annual base salary of $142,000, according to Ballotpedia's 2025 comparison of state legislative salaries. That is the largest annual base salary among the 20 states covered here.

New York lawmakers also receive a per diem of $203 a day. The two payments work differently. The salary is a fixed yearly amount. It arrives regardless of how many days the legislature meets. The per diem is a daily payment, and the 2025 table lists it separately from base pay.

The table does not give the length of New York's legislative session. It also does not say how many days members claim the per diem. Without those figures, the daily rate cannot be turned into a reliable yearly total. The $203 is a separate daily payment, not an addition to the $142,000 salary.

Ballotpedia classifies the New York legislature as full time. The National Conference of State Legislatures (NCSL) uses the same category in a report dated July 28, 2021. In that report, lawmakers in full-time legislatures devote about 84% of a full-time job to legislative work. That share means legislative work takes up most of a standard working year for members in this group. Average pay in that group was about $82,358.

New York's $142,000 salary sits well above that full-time average. The two figures come from different years, however. Ballotpedia's number reflects 2025 pay. The NCSL average dates to its 2021 report. Pay in other full-time states may have changed since then. A current average could sit higher or lower than the 2021 figure. The comparison shows the scale of New York's pay rather than a precise difference.

The full-time label and the salary describe the same job from two angles. One measures how much of a working year the role takes. The other measures what the state pays for it.

California pays its state legislators a base salary of $132,703 a year. Lawmakers also collect a per diem of $236 a day, according to Ballotpedia's 2025 table of state legislative salaries.

That salary places California second among the 20 states in this selection, behind New York. California is one of the five legislatures that Ballotpedia names as its top five by pay.

The two leading states differ on the daily payment. California's per diem is larger than New York's, even though its base salary is smaller.

The per diem sits outside the $132,703 figure. It is set as a daily rate rather than an annual sum. Ballotpedia's table does not say how many days it applies. That means there is no annual per diem total to report. A California lawmaker's full yearly pay cannot be read from the table alone.

Ballotpedia classifies California's legislature as full time.

Pennsylvania pays its state lawmakers an annual base salary of $110,015.54, according to Ballotpedia's 2025 comparison of state legislative salaries. Members also receive a per diem of $198 a day.

Pennsylvania is one of the five states Ballotpedia names as its top five by legislative pay. Within this 20-state selection, only New York and California set a higher annual base salary.

Pennsylvania's legislature is classified as full time.

Ballotpedia lists the salary and the per diem as separate items. The $110,015.54 base figure does not include the daily payment. That payment comes on top of the salary.

Ballotpedia gives a single salary figure for Pennsylvania. It does not list separate amounts for each chamber, as it does for some other states.

Pennsylvania's lawmakers serve in two chambers. The General Assembly is made up of a House of Representatives and a State Senate.

Members of the General Assembly are not subject to term limits. They can keep seeking reelection without a cap on how many terms they serve.

Illinois state legislators earn a base salary of $93,712 a year. That places Illinois fourth among the five legislatures that Ballotpedia's 2025 comparison of state legislative salaries names as its top five by pay.

Within that group, Illinois sits below Pennsylvania's $110,015.54 and above Alaska's $84,000. Those two states hold the third and fifth spots.

Illinois lawmakers also receive a per diem of $178 a day. Ballotpedia lists this daily payment separately from the annual base pay. The table gives it as one flat rate, with no distance tiers or residency conditions attached.

That flat rate differs from the per diem rules in several other full-time legislatures in this selection. Massachusetts and Michigan pay no per diem. Ohio pays none during session. Alaska and Hawaii tie their daily payments to where a lawmaker lives.

Ballotpedia classifies the Illinois legislature as full time.

The table does not say how many days a year Illinois lawmakers collect the per diem. Without that count, the daily rate cannot be combined with the $93,712 salary to produce a yearly total.

Alaska pays its state legislators a base salary of $84,000 a year. Its legislature is classified as full time. Lawmakers from outside Juneau also collect a per diem of $307 a day. Lawmakers who live in Juneau receive no per diem.

That salary puts Alaska fifth among the 10 higher-paying states in this selection. It sits behind New York, California, Pennsylvania and Illinois. It sits ahead of Massachusetts, Hawaii, Ohio and Michigan.

The per diem rule creates two levels of compensation inside one legislature. Every member draws the same $84,000 base salary. A member from outside Juneau adds $307 for each day the per diem applies. A colleague from Juneau draws the salary alone.

Alaska's $307 daily rate is the largest per diem among the 20 states in this selection. South Carolina's $240.07 and Virginia's $237 come next.

The $84,000 figure covers base salary only. The per diem is a separate payment made on top of it. Eligibility for that payment depends on where a member lives.

Massachusetts pays its state legislators an annual base salary of $82,044.31. Ballotpedia's 2025 table lists no per diem for them and counts the legislature as full time.

That salary places Massachusetts sixth among the 10 higher-paying states in this selection. It sits below Alaska and above Hawaii.

Ballotpedia's own named top five ends with Alaska. Massachusetts is therefore the highest-paying state here that falls outside the source's named top five. Its sixth-place position comes from sorting the table's annual figures, not from a ranking Ballotpedia publishes.

A per diem is a daily allowance some states pay on top of a lawmaker's salary. In those states, yearly pay can rise above the base salary on the days a legislator qualifies for the allowance. The table shows no such rate for Massachusetts. Its base salary is the only pay figure the table lists for the state's lawmakers.

Massachusetts is not alone in this among the higher-paying states in this selection. Michigan also lists no per diem. Ohio lists none during session. New York and California, by contrast, pair their salaries with daily rates.

The full-time label puts Massachusetts alongside every other state in the upper group except Alabama. Alabama runs a hybrid legislature.

Hawaii pays its state legislators an annual base salary of $74,160. Members who live off Oahu also receive a per diem of $225 a day.

Ballotpedia's 2025 salary table lists both figures and classifies Hawaii's legislature as full time.

Whether a member gets the per diem depends on where that legislator lives. A member whose home is on an island other than Oahu qualifies for the $225 daily rate. A member who lives on Oahu gets no per diem and draws the base salary alone.

Under that rule, two Hawaii lawmakers with the same base salary can end up with different total pay. The difference comes from their home island.

Among the 10 states in the upper half of this selection, Hawaii's $74,160 salary places seventh. It falls below Massachusetts and above Ohio.

Ohio pays its state lawmakers an annual base salary of $72,343, according to Ballotpedia's 2025 comparison of state legislative salaries. Ohio legislators receive no per diem during session.

That salary ranks eighth among the 10 higher-paying states in this selection. When the source's annual figures are sorted from highest to lowest, Ohio sits just below Hawaii and just above Michigan.

Ballotpedia classifies Ohio's legislature as full time. Nine of the 10 higher-paying states in this selection carry that label. Alabama, which the source lists as hybrid, is the only exception.

Ohio's per diem entry sets it apart from most of the higher-paying group. Seven of those 10 states list some form of daily allowance on top of base salary. In Alaska and Hawaii, that allowance goes only to some members. Massachusetts and Michigan are the other two that list no per diem.

The wording of Ohio's entry differs from those two states. Massachusetts and Michigan are listed with no per diem at all. Ohio's entry specifies no per diem during session. The 2025 table does not say what, if anything, Ohio lawmakers receive outside session.

For session work, that leaves the base salary as the only pay the table records for an Ohio lawmaker. In California and Pennsylvania, by contrast, a daily allowance comes on top of a larger base salary.

The table also gives Ohio a single salary figure. Virginia lists different amounts for its delegates and its senators.

Ohio's salary exceeds every base salary in the lower half of this selection. That includes Rhode Island's, the highest in that group.

A missing per diem is not limited to the higher-paying states. Rhode Island and New Hampshire, both part-time legislatures in the lower group, also list none. In those states, no daily allowance sits on top of a much smaller base salary.

Ohio's legislature is the Ohio General Assembly. It has two chambers, the Ohio House of Representatives and the Ohio Senate.

Michigan pays its state legislators a base salary of $71,685 a year, with no per diem on top. Its legislature is full time.

That salary puts Michigan ninth among the 10 higher-paying states in this 20-state selection, which is sorted from Ballotpedia's 2025 table. Ohio ranks just above it, at $72,343. Alabama ranks just below it, at $62,212.

Michigan is also the lowest-paid full-time legislature among the 20 states here. Alabama, the next state down, runs a hybrid legislature. Every state in the lower 10 is hybrid or part time.

Without a per diem, the $71,685 salary is all of the pay Ballotpedia lists for a Michigan lawmaker. A per diem is a daily payment made on top of salary. New York, California, Pennsylvania and Illinois all pay one to their full-time legislators. Michigan does not.

Two other states in the upper group take a similar approach. Massachusetts lists no per diem. Ohio pays none during session.

The absence of a per diem also means Michigan's listed pay does not change with where a lawmaker lives. In Alaska, the daily allowance depends on whether a member lives in Juneau. In Hawaii, it goes only to members who live off Oahu. Alabama's depends on distance and trip length. Michigan's entry carries no such variation. A member from any part of the state receives the same listed figure.

Alabama pays its state legislators a base salary of $62,212 a year. It is the only hybrid legislature among the 10 higher-paying states in this selection. The other nine are all full time.

The hybrid label comes from the National Conference of State Legislatures. NCSL sorts legislatures into full-time, hybrid and part-time groups based on how much of a full-time job the legislative work takes.

NCSL found that hybrid legislators spend about 74% of a full-time job on legislative work. Average pay for the hybrid group was about $41,110.

Alabama's base salary sits above that hybrid average. The two figures come from different years, though. The NCSL average dates from 2021, while the $62,212 salary comes from Ballotpedia's 2025 table.

The hybrid label does not settle where a state's pay lands. Texas, South Carolina, Nebraska, North Carolina, Louisiana and Virginia also run hybrid legislatures, and all six sit in the lower half of this selection. Alabama shares their classification, yet its salary places it among the higher-paying states.

Alabama does not pay a single flat per diem. The daily allowance depends on distance and trip length, including whether a trip involves an overnight stay.

Trips of under six hours carry no per diem at all. A trip lasting six to 12 hours pays $12.75.

A trip of more than 12 hours without an overnight stay pays $34. A trip that includes one night away pays $85.

For trips of two or more nights, the rate rises to $100 a day. The structure ties the allowance to time spent away. A short trip earns nothing, while a multi-night stay draws the highest rate.

Among the 10 higher-paying states here, Alabama is the only one whose per diem varies with trip length. New York, California, Pennsylvania and Illinois each pay one daily rate. Massachusetts and Michigan pay no per diem. Ohio pays none during session.

Rhode Island pays its state lawmakers an annual base salary of $19,817, with no per diem on top. Ballotpedia's 2025 salary table classifies the Rhode Island legislature as part time.

With no per diem, that salary is the only regular pay the table records for a Rhode Island legislator. Days spent in session do not add a separate daily payment to it.

Among the 10 lower-paying states in this selection, Rhode Island has the highest base salary. It pays more than Virginia pays either its delegates or its senators. It also pays more than Louisiana's base salary, before Louisiana's separate expense allowance is considered.

The comparison changes once daily payments enter the picture. Several states in the lower group pay smaller salaries and then add a daily payment. Texas, South Carolina, North Carolina and Nebraska all follow that pattern. Rhode Island takes the opposite approach and puts all of its recorded pay into the salary itself.

Rhode Island is one of two states in the lower group with no per diem at all. New Hampshire is the other. In the upper group, Massachusetts and Michigan also pay no per diem, and Ohio pays none during session. All three run full-time legislatures.

Rhode Island is also one of four part-time legislatures in this selection. The others are South Dakota, New Hampshire and New Mexico. Of those four, Rhode Island pays the highest base salary.

Politico reviewed 2018 financial disclosures from state legislators whose forms were available, and Ballotpedia cites that review. Of 117 men, 42, or 36%, had other jobs. Six of 49 women also had other jobs.

As Ballotpedia presents them, those counts are not specific to Rhode Island. They show that a share of state legislators held other jobs in 2018. They do not show how many members of the Rhode Island legislature hold outside work.

Virginia pays its delegates $17,640 a year and its senators $18,000, according to Ballotpedia's 2025 salary table. Virginia legislators also collect a per diem of $237 a day.

Virginia is the only state among the 20 in this selection with two base salaries in its table entry. Senators receive the higher amount. Delegates receive the lower one.

Sorting the states requires one figure for each. This selection uses the delegates' $17,640 to place Virginia.

The choice of figure does not move Virginia. On either salary, the state sits above Louisiana and below Rhode Island. That makes Virginia the second-highest payer among the 10 lower-paying states covered here. Even the senators' $18,000 falls short of Rhode Island's base salary.

Ballotpedia classifies Virginia's legislature as hybrid. Six of the 10 lower-paying states in this selection run hybrid legislatures, and Virginia is one of them. The other four in that group are part time.

The table lists Virginia's $237 per diem as a single daily rate. Some states in this selection scale their per diem by how far a legislator lives from the Capitol. Virginia's entry attaches no distance or residency condition to its rate.

The per diem is a separate payment from base pay. It is not part of the $17,640 paid to delegates or the $18,000 paid to senators.

Louisiana pays its state legislators a base salary of $16,800 a year. Each lawmaker also receives a $6,000 annual expense allowance, which Ballotpedia's 2025 comparison of state legislative salaries lists apart from the salary.

The two payments serve different purposes. Salary is compensation for holding the office. The $6,000 is an expense payment, and it is unvouchered. Legislators do not have to back it with receipts or itemized claims to receive it.

That distinction shapes how Louisiana compares with other states. Its position in this 20-state selection rests on the $16,800 salary alone. The allowance plays no part in the ordering.

Louisiana also pays a per diem of $178 a day. A per diem is a third kind of payment, separate from both the salary and the expense allowance. It is paid for days of legislative work rather than as a fixed annual amount. The per diem grows with the number of working days, while the salary and the allowance stay fixed.

Illinois uses the same $178 daily rate. Illinois runs a full-time legislature with a far higher base salary, so an identical per diem can sit inside very different pay structures.

Louisiana's legislature is classified as hybrid.

Among the 10 lower-paying states in this selection, Louisiana's base salary ranks third from the top. Only Virginia and Rhode Island pay more in base salary within that group. South Dakota, at $16,348, sits just below Louisiana.

South Dakota pays its state legislators an annual base salary of $16,348. Its legislature is classified as part time, and members who live more than 50 miles away also receive a per diem of $178 a day.

The salary is the fixed part of the package. Every member receives the same base amount, whether home is a short drive from the statehouse or at the far end of the state.

The per diem works differently in South Dakota. The $178 daily rate goes only to legislators whose homes sit more than 50 miles away. Members who live closer receive the base salary alone.

That split means two South Dakota lawmakers serving in the same session can take home different totals from the job. The difference turns on where each member lives.

The per diem is also a daily rate rather than a yearly sum. What an eligible member collects depends on how many days that member is paid for. Ballotpedia's table does not give that count. Without it, the per diem cannot be converted into an annual figure.

The part-time label comes from the National Conference of State Legislatures, which sorts legislatures into full-time, hybrid and part-time groups. Its report dated July 28, 2021, found that part-time legislators devote about 57 percent of a full-time job to legislative work. That is more than half of a standard workload.

The same report put average pay for part-time legislators at about $18,449. South Dakota's base salary sits below that average. The two figures come from different years, though. The NCSL average dates from 2021, while South Dakota's salary comes from Ballotpedia's 2025 data.

The NCSL report also found that part-time legislators typically need another source of income beyond their legislative pay. That finding describes part-time legislatures as a group, not South Dakota's members in particular.

North Carolina pays its state legislators an annual base salary of $13,951 and a per diem of $104 a day, according to Ballotpedia's 2025 table of state legislative salaries. Ballotpedia classifies the state's legislature as hybrid.

The two figures measure different things. The salary is a fixed yearly amount. The per diem is a separate daily payment on top of it.

Ballotpedia does not say how many days a year the per diem covers. A North Carolina lawmaker's full-year pay cannot be worked out from these two figures alone.

The daily rate is lower than the salary ranking might suggest. Several states in this selection pay every member the same flat daily rate, without distance or residence conditions. Among those states, North Carolina's $104 is the lowest.

The base salary sits near the middle of the selection's lower group. Among the 10 lower-paying states here, North Carolina ranks sixth from the bottom. Its salary sits above Nebraska's $12,000. It falls below South Dakota's $16,348.

The hybrid label does not settle where a state's pay lands. Three other hybrid legislatures in this selection, Texas, South Carolina and Nebraska, pay lower base salaries than North Carolina. Virginia, Louisiana and Alabama, also hybrid, pay more.

The two states below Texas in the lower group, New Mexico and New Hampshire, both run part-time legislatures. North Carolina's hybrid legislature pays more in base salary than either of them.

Nebraska pays its state legislators an annual base salary of $12,000. Any recommended change to that salary must win voter approval before it can take effect.

That rule means the salary cannot rise through a commission report or a legislative vote alone. Nebraska voters hold the final say over what their legislators earn.

Ballotpedia's 2025 salary table classifies Nebraska's legislature as hybrid. Hybrid legislatures fall between full-time and part-time bodies.

Within the 10 lower-paying states in this selection, Nebraska sits in the middle. Its salary is higher than South Carolina's and lower than North Carolina's.

The salary is only one part of a Nebraska legislator's compensation. Lawmakers also collect a per diem. The daily rate depends on how far they live from the Capitol.

Legislators who live within 50 miles of the Capitol receive $68 a day. Those who live farther away receive $178 a day.

The distance line splits Nebraska's lawmakers into two groups for daily allowances. The $12,000 salary is the same for all of them. A legislator who lives far from the Capitol draws the higher rate for each day the per diem applies.

South Dakota also draws a line at 50 miles. There, the distance decides whether a legislator gets a per diem at all. In Nebraska, every legislator gets one, at one of two rates.

South Carolina pays its state legislators an annual base salary of $10,400, according to Ballotpedia's 2025 salary table. Lawmakers also collect a per diem of $240.07 a day.

That salary places South Carolina near the bottom of this 20-state selection. Among the 10 lower-paying states covered here, only New Mexico, New Hampshire and Texas set a smaller annual base salary.

The daily allowance points the other way. South Carolina's per diem of $240.07 is the largest daily rate among those 10 states. Virginia pays $237 a day, and Texas pays $221.

The South Carolina rate also tops California's $236 a day. California pairs that per diem with a base salary of $132,703. Across all 20 states in this selection, only Alaska's rate for legislators who live outside Juneau is higher.

A per diem is a daily payment made on top of salary. Ballotpedia's table gives no session lengths. It therefore does not show how many days qualify for the payment. Any yearly per diem total for a South Carolina legislator would be a guess.

That gap shapes how South Carolina compares with other states on this list. The salary is a fixed annual amount. The per diem varies with the number of qualifying days. Ordering by base salary captures only the fixed part of the pay.

Rhode Island pays a larger base salary than South Carolina and no per diem. Salary order alone therefore cannot show which of the two states pays its lawmakers more over a year. Texas sets both a smaller salary and a smaller daily rate than South Carolina. New Mexico sets no salary at all and a lower daily rate.

The state's legislature is classed as hybrid, a category that sits between full-time and part-time legislatures. Six other states in this selection share that classification: Alabama, Virginia, Louisiana, North Carolina, Nebraska and Texas. The salary table does not say how South Carolina sets its $10,400 figure.

Texas pays its state legislators an annual base salary of $7,200 and a per diem of $221 a day, according to Ballotpedia's 2025 comparison of state legislative salaries. Ballotpedia classifies the Texas legislature as hybrid.

That salary puts Texas third from the bottom among the 20 states in this selection. Only New Mexico and New Hampshire list a lower annual base salary.

The per diem ranks higher. Among the 10 lower-paying states in this selection, only South Carolina and Virginia list a higher daily rate than Texas.

The $221 is paid per day, separately from the salary. The annual salary on its own therefore leaves out part of what Texas pays its legislators.

Ballotpedia's table does not say how many days a year Texas legislators collect the per diem. Without that number, the source does not support a yearly total for it. It also does not show how the per diem adds up against the $7,200 salary over a full year.

New Hampshire pays its state legislators $100 a year and no per diem. Its legislature is classified as part time.

With no daily allowance added, that $100 is the full amount the state pays a member for legislative service in a year.

In this 20-state selection, only New Mexico ranks below New Hampshire on annual base salary. New Mexico's place at the bottom comes from setting no salary at all. New Hampshire does set one, but it is a nominal sum.

Those lawmakers serve in the General Court, the name for New Hampshire's legislature as a whole. It has two chambers, a House of Representatives and a Senate.

Members of the General Court face no limit on how many terms they may serve.

New Mexico pays its state legislators no salary. It is the one state in Ballotpedia's 2025 salary table that sets no base pay for lawmakers. Its legislators receive a per diem of $202 a day instead.

With a base salary of $0, New Mexico sits at the bottom of the 20 states in this selection. The $202 figure is a daily allowance, not a salary. Ballotpedia lists it separately from base pay.

The absence of a salary is written into state law at the highest level. New Mexico's constitution provides that legislators are not paid a salary.

Ballotpedia classifies New Mexico's legislature as part time.

The per diem is set as a daily rate rather than a fixed yearly sum. Ballotpedia's table does not show how many days that rate is paid in a year. It therefore does not show what a New Mexico lawmaker collects in total.
