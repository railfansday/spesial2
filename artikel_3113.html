Nokia vs. Verizon: A Contrast in Revenue Scale and Seasonality

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Verizon's revenue runs 6x larger, but Nokia's year-end surges reveal a pattern that could signal timing opportunities for contrarian investors.

Nokia Oyj (NOK +2.17%) earns its revenue by supplying extensive network infrastructure, optical routing equipment, and comprehensive cloud services to communications service providers and digital enterprises globally. Between August and October 2026, it completed the planned sale of its fixed wireless access business and entered a separate agreement to develop satellite communications capabilities.

Verizon Communications (VZ +0.42%) generates its revenue primarily by delivering wireless connectivity, high-speed internet access, and specialized business networking solutions to diverse consumer and enterprise customers. Between August and October 2026, it launched a joint venture to eliminate mobile coverage dead zones and finalized the sale of a wireless carrier unit.

Revenue represents the total cash brought into a business before any operating expenses are deducted. This metric helps investors measure a company's overall size, market footprint, and long-term trajectory.

Nokia and Verizon are two giants of the telecommunications industry, although both are looking toward capitalizing on the artificial intelligence boom. This could prove transformative to their revenue growth trajectories in time.

Verizon's AI Connect offering is an integrated suite of network infrastructure, connectivity, and data center solutions launched by Verizon Business to help enterprises deploy and scale artificial intelligence workloads.

Nokia formed a partnership with AI giant Nvidia to support the development of AI radio access network (AI-RAN) technology. AI-RAN differs from today's 5G network in that artificial intelligence transforms it from a passive data pipe into one where AI intelligently manages traffic for improved speed, coverage, and capacity.

How these AI endeavors pan out will reflect in revenue totals eventually, if they prove successful. For now, Verizon's income trajectory reflects the stable sales growth inherent in its cellphone service subscriptions, while Nokia’s represents cyclical technology spending. The revenue spikes in Nokia's fourth quarter reflect the use-it-or-lose-it IT spending by its customers, who have to use up their budgets before the end of each calendar year.
