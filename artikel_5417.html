At 17, South Carolina-bound GG Banks is building a brand before her first college game

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Jezelle “GG” Banks doesn’t turn 18 until Nov. 27, but she already has a logo.

The senior at The St. James Academy, just outside Washington, D.C., is a five-star guard ranked No. 4 in the class of 2027 and committed to South Carolina, the No. 1 team in the AP poll. She’s as at home slicing past defenders as she is hosting long livestreams on social media.

Banks is also building a business. In a growing number of states, high school athletes can now be paid for their name, image, and likeness (NIL), just like college athletes. In an exclusive interview with Fortune, she revealed her personal logo publicly for the first time.

“I love it! It really fits me,” Banks said. “Everything in the logo has special meaning to me. I think the logo speaks to my personality—spikes of energy boosting everything, and then towards the bottom you’ve got a dollar sign, because, of course, banks.”

The dollar sign plays on her last name. Banks worked on the design with her agency, THE·TEAM, the sports and entertainment firm formerly known as Wasserman. Coming just weeks before her 18th birthday, it’s a birthday present of sorts.

“This was a dream,” Banks said. THE·TEAM showed her mockups of the logo on sneakers and in Times Square. “That’s what I envisioned when I was younger, me having the logo.”

Custom logos are still rare among high school players, but Banks has been dreaming big for a while.

“I saw this coming a little bit, not just for me, but I think just the women’s era—people paying more attention to women’s basketball,” she said. “And I think when I was 14 or 15, I honestly started to, I wouldn’t say build my brand, but build who I was on the court… I learned pretty early on how to manage myself in a mature way.”

Her agent, Lindsay Kagawa Colas, executive vice president of talent representation at THE·TEAM, has represented some of the game’s biggest names, including Diana Taurasi, Paige Bueckers, and 2026 WNBA Finals MVP Gabby Williams. “In establishing an elevated mark like this, I think our goal, with (the logo) and her business, was to make sure that she was building something big enough to match her potential,” Kagawa Colas said.

In viral highlight videos, Banks is equally adept at dribbling past opponents to score and dishing to teammates for easy buckets. She finishes with a flourish, usually with a winning smile. That’s a combination brands notice.

She’s clearly been working on her handle off the court, too. She has more than 120,000 followers on TikTok and about 84,000 on Instagram.

“She had a real clear understanding about the value of building her audience as she was building her resume,” Kagawa Colas said.

Last month, Banks partnered with Amazon-owned Twitch on an exclusive livestream of her commitment to South Carolina. The Saturday-afternoon stream followed her through the day and ended with her announcement in her high school’s gym, packed with friends and family.

“For me to be the first high school hooper to have this on Twitch, it just shows what I’m trying to build, not only for me, but for the younger generation behind me, anything is possible,” Banks said. “I think it was awesome!”

Kagawa Colas sees a generational shift. “I think she is certainly emblematic of a generation where she’s becoming the media we used to wait for, where she knows that fans believe in her and will invest emotionally if you let them get close and provide that sense of connection,” she said.

Under coach Dawn Staley, South Carolina has become a powerhouse. The Gamecocks have won three national titles since 2017, most recently in 2024, and finished runner-up in each of the past two seasons.

“It’s clear that GG wants to be the best and that she is not afraid of high expectations and hard work. So that was really the first focus,” Kagawa Colas said. “We’ll be looking for partnerships that build on that, but establishing who she is in that direction, through that (college) choice, we thought was very clear.”

Kagawa Colas knows what it takes for athletes to reach the top, on and off the court. “With GG, it’s been rewarding to see her plan gain traction so quickly, because it’s driven by her joy, her love of the game and her natural ability to connect with people,” she said.
