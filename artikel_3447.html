Tom Waits’ Rare ‘Orphans: Bonus’ EP, Previously Vinyl-Only, Now Streaming

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Six Tom Waits songs that were previously available only on vinyl have been added to streaming services to commemorate the 20th anniversary of the artist’s Orphans: Brawlers, Bawlers & Bastards box set. The EP, titled Orphans: Bonus, originally came out when the Orphans set got upgraded from CD to vinyl in 2009.

The Orphans foundlings are a mix of originals, cover songs, and collaborations. Waits recorded the soulful, jazzy “Pray” with the Book of Knots, an avant-garde rock group also on his record label, Anti-. Another jazzy recording is Waits’ funky, piano-driven cover of Fats Waller’s “Crazy ‘Bout My Baby.” Meanwhile, he sings his rendition of Kurt Weill and Bertolt Brecht’s “The Cannon Song,” from The Threepenny Opera, to samples of huffing and puffing voices, a perfect backdrop to singing about eating hamburgers raw.

He sings his original, “Diamond in Your Mind,” to a lush backdrop of orchestral strings, fretless bass, and tambourine, which supports the chorus, “Always keep a diamond in your mind.” He wrote the song with songwriter (and his wife) Kathleen Brennan for Robert Wilson’s Woyzeck; Solomon Burke later recorded it. Waits wrote the EP’s penultimate track by himself. “No One Can Forgive Me but My Baby” is like a blues recitative set to percussion and sparse instrumentation as he begs for forgiveness. The album’s last song, “Mathie Grove,” is an old murder ballad that Waits sings a cappella.

The original Orphans box set came out on three CDs. The first, Brawlers, contained harder blues and rock songs, while Bawlers featured ballads, and Bastards was more avant-garde. Among the tracks were songs written for soundtracks and several covers including Ramones’ “The Return of Jackie and Judy” and “Sea of Love.” “At the center of this record is my voice,” Waits commented on the box set at the time of its release. “I try my best to chug, stomp, weep, whisper, moan, wheeze, scat, blurt, rage, whine and seduce.” It was nominated for the Best Contemporary Folk Album Grammy. In April, Waits released his first new music in 15 years, “Boots on the Ground,” a gruesome song protesting war that Waits he recorded with Massive Attack; the B side was a spoken-word piece called “The Fly.”

He also recently contributed a rendition of the Pogues’ “Summer in Siam” to the tribute compilation, 20th Century Paddy – The Songs of Shane MacGowan. He gave Rolling Stone an exclusive statement about MacGowan for an article about the compilation: “His voice can sing about a well, and I’ll be sure to fall into it,” he wrote, “and sing about a factory wall, and I’m weary leaning and warily wooing that girl taking a drag off my smoke, and he can sing about a battle, and my head will be bloody and bandaged at the end of the song.… His songs come from the earth, the wind, the birds, and the bees, the gone and forgotten but for their songs.”
