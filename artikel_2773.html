Sal Licata ‘sick to my stomach’ about SNY layoffs: ‘I would never have left’

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Sal Licata joined SNY in 2010 and has been a mainstay of the regional sports network since.

That ended this week, as he was part of mass layoffs on Friday. SNY also canceled Baseball Night in New York and Sportsnite effective immediately.

It's a feeling Licata has unfortunately gotten used to recently, as he was pushed out at WFAN in 2025 following Craig Carton's return to the station.

The longtime radio show host livestreamed Friday night in a video titled "Sal Got Fired, Again," saying that although he is going to be fine, he is "sick to his stomach" for his former coworkers.

"The real reason I'm sick to my stomach is because there are a lot of superb people who are great at what they do," Licata said. "I've worked at a lot of places over the years, part-time and things like that, and none of them compare to SNY.

"It was the most fun environment you could possibly be in. It's not like you walked in and it was this picture-perfect world, but walking into that place was a special environment."

He added that if it had been up to him, he would have stayed there for his entire career.

"I would have never left SNY," Licata said. "It was not about the money for me … I truly loved doing that show. I would never have left."

Licata suggested the timing of the layoffs may be tied to Major League Baseball's impending lockout, which could delay the 2027 season.

The Mets are owned by Steve Cohen, one of the richest owners in Major League Baseball, but Sterling Equities, an investment firm owned by the Wilpon and Katz families, still controls SNY. The Wilpons were notoriously cheap during their Mets tenure. Last year, SNY reportedly explored a possible sale, listing Cohen as a potential buyer. SNY has a current broadcast agreement with the Mets that extends through 2035, making Cohen seem like a strong candidate.

"As part of our regular review of our business, operations and programming, SNY has decided to cease broadcasting Baseball Night in New York and SportsNite," an SNY spokesperson told The New York Post. "We are grateful to those team members that helped bring these programs to fans for many years. Ongoing coverage of the Mets and New York sports are at the heart of SNY's business, and we remain focused on continuing to deliver high-quality content to our passionate fans."
