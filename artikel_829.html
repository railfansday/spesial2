'He's got to get 14 more to catch me!' - Taylor on Littler, crowds, and comeback

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Phil Taylor speaks to Sky Sports on his remarkable comeback to darts alongside Adrian Lewis for the MODUS Super Series; fans can catch every match of the MODUS Super Series from 23-28 November for free on the Pluto TV app or online at www.pluto.tv

Luke Littler has been the star of the darting show for what feels like an eternity - but Phil Taylor is in no doubt as to who still holds the crown.

The most decorated player in history will make a sensational return to the oche when Taylor joins up with long-time friend Adrian Lewis at the MODUS Super Series in his first competitive tournament in eight years, starting November 23 in Portsmouth.

The 16-time world champion has inevitably been compared to teenage sensation Littler - who already has two Alexandra Palace titles at 19 - but remains calm about the prospect of his world title record being overtaken.

"I have more publicity [now] than when I was winning it!" Taylor joked, speaking to Sky Sports. "Everywhere I go, people ask me 'do think he'll beat your record? It's all debating all the time.

"So just to qualify for the next 14 years isn't easy. He's got to keep his form going, he's got to work hard, and even then, it'll take 14 years - that's if he wins it every year!"

"If the crowds keep on at him, then he might just walk away."

Littler - the favourite in almost every tournament he participates in - has become something of a target of hostile darts crowds across the tour, even threatening to boycott future tournaments in the Netherlands after being booed and whistled during his defeat in World Series of Darts final in Amsterdam.

This is a feeling Taylor - who reached 14 consecutive World Championship finals between 1994 and 2007 - can relate to.

"I'm not a fan of it. I didn't like it when I was playing - it's the whistles," Taylor said.

"When you're in the venue, some of them are screaming and whistling and calling your every name under the sun. I have to go and get a dictionary when I get home to see what they were calling me!

"It is affecting him. He doesn't like it, and he doesn't deserve it in fairness.

"It's just something they keep saying you've got to get used to, but it's very, very difficult."

Taylor left professional darts in show-stopping fashion, finishing runner up in the 2018 World Championship after a sensational run to the final as the No 6 seed.

Could the adrenaline of competitive darts tempt Taylor into a sensational return to the Pro Tour?

"I've still got my own teeth, I've got my hip done, got my eyes done - but no, not a chance.

"It would be strange for me anyway, because I don't play competitive anymore. Adie [Adrian Lewis], still plays and it's going to be different. I'm just trying and trying and trying to pick my form up.

"Age has caught up with me, that's the problem."

Taylor and Lewis representing England is an image which will bring back fond memories for fans of a pairing that has won four World Cup of Darts titles - the most by any two players in the tournament's history.

It was the opportunity to share the stage with Lewis once again that was the deciding factor in tempting "The Power" back to the oche for one last dance.

"It was the owner of MODUS, Jason Thame, who said I was playing with Adie, and I said 'yeah, I'd enjoy that' - that's why I'm doing it!

"I'm not as good as what I used to be. He's going to have to really play well on this tournament, but I'm going to go and enjoy it. That's the main thing. We'll have a laugh."
