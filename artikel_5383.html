Bitcoin Braces for a $10.9 Billion Options Expiry With Max Pain at $78,000, Two Days After the Fed’s Split Hold: Can BTC Hold $79,000?

About 135,700 Bitcoin options worth $10.9 billion expire on Deribit at 08:00 UTC on Friday, October 30, a third of the exchange’s open contracts, two days after the Federal Reserve held rates in a 10–2 vote that left a December hike open.The price is sitting almost on top of the expiry’s pain point. BTC trades at $79,976, down 1.8% in 24 hours, after giving back its whole post-Fed bounce overnight. Max pain for Friday’s Bitcoin options expiry, the price at which buyers of those contracts would lose the most, is $78,000, and the October low that has held for a week is $79,221.

So what exactly is expiring, how did Bitcoin take the Fed, and can it hold $79,000 through Friday?

Mostly bullish bets that are now far out of reach. Deribit’s public order book data at 09:01 UTC on Thursday shows 135,676 BTC in contracts dated October 30, out of 397,442 BTC across all Deribit expiries:

Deribit BTC open interest expiring October 30, by strike. Source: Deribit

The Deribit Volatility Index (DVOL), which tracks the 30-day volatility priced into Bitcoin options, fell to 36 from 38 over the past two days. Options traders are paying less for protection even with an expiry this size a day away. Ether adds a smaller expiry of about 338,000 ETH, roughly $780 million.

A two-hour rally, then a slow leak. The Fed’s statement on Wednesday kept the federal funds rate at 3.75% to 4%, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack voting for a quarter-point hike instead. Chair Kevin Warsh declined to signal the next move: “I don’t believe in forward guidance, absent exigent circumstances, absent a crisis.”

ETF buyers stayed in. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs took in $137.3 million on Wednesday, their third straight day of inflows, nearly all of it into BlackRock’s IBIT, SoSoValue figures show.

A soft day after the Fed is normal for Bitcoin. On Binance data, BTC was higher 24 hours after only 33 of the Fed’s 71 decisions since January 2018, and after holds the bigger moves tended to come weeks later.

It has so far, but with less room each time. On the 4-hour chart, BTC sits at $80,130, below both its 50-period EMA at $80,978 and its 200-period EMA at $81,635. The faster line crossed under the slower one around October 23, and Wednesday’s post-Fed rally stalled right at the 200 EMA.

The floor is the October 24 low at $79,221. Bitcoin has dipped into the $79,200–$79,850 area on five of the last six days and bounced each time, most recently at 02:00 UTC on Thursday.

Max pain is only a reference point. It marks where option buyers would collect the least on Friday, and the price is under no obligation to go there.

Friday’s settlement clears a third of Deribit’s open bets in one go, and most of them were placed for a market well above $80,000. After the Bitcoin options expiry, the next tests come from data, not derivatives: the October jobs report on November 6 and consumer prices on November 10, both on the road to the Fed’s December 9 decision.
