Binance says stolen crypto has been replaced but some customers have to wait

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The 198-token reserve snapshot expands Binance's disclosed scope, while proof availability, phased withdrawals and SAFU restoration remain separate tests.

Binance says it has replaced the customer assets lost in its security incident and reopened withdrawals on 87 networks this week. The exchange aims to restore the remaining networks and assets by the end of next week, leaving some customers waiting despite its completed-coverage announcement.

The announcement, signed Nov. 13, accompanies a reserve snapshot covering 198 tokens. For customers, the next question is whether covered balances can be verified and moved off the exchange.

In its Nov. 4 update, Binance put replenishment at approximately 98%, with purchases of less-liquid tokens still continuing. The latest notice says that replacement process is complete.

CryptoSlate's earlier coverage of the sourcing delays described the difficulty of acquiring thinly traded tokens alongside progressive withdrawal reopening. The new announcement changes that reported coverage position while retaining the phased withdrawal rollout.

Binance says the new snapshot was taken at 00:00 UTC on Nov. 12 and spans 198 tokens, compared with 44 in the Nov. 4 snapshot. The earlier snapshot was taken at 08:00 UTC+8, equivalent to 00:00 UTC.

The expanded snapshot covers more tokens, but does not establish whether each asset and network is available for withdrawal.

The latest notice says the snapshot is available on its proof-of-reserves website. It separately estimates approximately 40 to 50 hours to generate the full zero-knowledge proof, after which affected holders would be able to verify minimum one-for-one backing.

A published snapshot and an available verification result therefore describe different stages. The announcement does not confirm that proof generation is complete. The notice gives no starting point for the estimate, leaving current proof availability unestablished here.

The earlier 44-token round carried a different estimate of 50 to 60 hours. Both notices separate taking a snapshot from producing its full proof. For affected holders, verification would address backing at the specified snapshot, while access to funds remains an operational question.

In its Nov. 14 recovery update, Binance said withdrawals had reopened on 87 networks this week. It aims to reopen all remaining networks and assets by the end of next week, while warning that rollout traffic could cause delays or longer processing times.

Customers moving covered balances off Binance still need to check the withdrawal page for their chosen asset and network. The reopening announcement is not a guarantee that every route is available or every transfer will process immediately.

A small number of affected tokens that Binance no longer supports will be covered through its delisting process. The notice does not specify how or when those holders will be compensated.

Binance says it committed more than $6 billion in company funds, drawing on corporate treasury, partial deployment of its Secure Asset Fund for Users and a portion of Futures insurance resources above its stated risk-management requirement.

It separately commits to returning SAFU to approximately $1 billion. The announcement does not report that restoration as complete.

Replacing affected customer assets and restoring an emergency reserve serve different purposes. The first addresses losses from this incident; the second concerns the resources available for future emergencies. The announcement provides no allocation breakdown among the three funding sources, and the fund-restoration pledge remains distinct from its completed-coverage assurance.

Binance's announcement changes the reported coverage position. The next practical milestones for customers are accessible verification for their covered assets, confirmed withdrawal availability and delivery of the remaining fund-restoration commitment.
