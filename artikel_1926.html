Noussair Mazraoui will be hoping to impress new Manchester United manager Mauricio Pochettino.

The Moroccan defender has rarely started for the Red Devils this season, with Michael Carrick handing him just two starts.

Now, with the Englishman no longer manager, there is a clean slate for the whole Man United squad.

The international break has handed those on the fringes a chance to effectively audition before linking up with their new boss.

And Mazraoui appears to be taking his chance away with Morocco.

His performance against Niger could therefore hand Pochettino an unlikely option for the rest of the season.

Mazraoui has faced questions over his long-term future at Man United, but his latest international display offered a timely reminder of his qualities.

Against Niger, the 29-year-old showcased that he still has plenty left to give.

And has given new manager Pochettino something to think about ahead of his first Man United starting XI.

As per FotMob, Mazraoui was heavily involved in the game, with him competing in more duels than anyone else.

The Man United defender came out on top in a majority of them, winning 12 out of 17, including 100 per cent of his four aerial duels.

He also only misplaced three passes, coming in with a 93 per cent passing accuracy.

Mazraoui also had more defensive contributions than any of his national side teammates, although he did concede a penalty.

Overall, other than that penalty incident, his performance against Niger proved that he still has plenty to give, especially from a defensive point of view.

And perhaps under the incoming Argentine, he could still have an Old Trafford future.

Mazraoui's aggression and commitment will surely appeal to Pochettino,

According to Sport Le 360 in Morocco, they share the Man United man "put in a tremendous effort" in order to contain Niger.

And his duelling stats certainly portray that exact story, showing he is not afraid or hesitant to stick a foot in.

Man United will need those types of personalities to turn things around.

Having players willing to put their bodies on the line and compete for every ball is essential, particularly when results are not going their way.

And that international performance may point Pochettino's eyes in his direction ahead of facing Liverpool at Anfield.
