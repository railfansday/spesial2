Google made changes to AI in schools as backlash grows

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Free daily briefing on global business news.

K-12 student accounts will have Gemini and Search off until administrators opt in, but Search's AI features come with it

David Paul Morris/Bloomberg via Getty Images

Alphabet $GOOGL's Google will turn off Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search on accounts held by K-12 students, and they will stay dark until a school's administrators enable them, the company said Tuesday.

Schools that want students to have Search will get its AI features along with it, according to Bloomberg. Google provides no setting that switches off Search's AI features on their own, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Google said it will share specifics with schools in the coming months, ahead of the rollout. The company said the decision came after it consulted policymakers, researchers, teachers and schools.

Google is also adding Guided Learning, a study tool from its Gemini app, to Search for student accounts. When Google's systems sense that a student is seeking particular kinds of homework assistance, Search will guide the student through the problem in stages. If a student pastes in a physics problem or asks for help with a book report, the student will see prompts that push them to work through the concepts rather than getting the answer.

Google plans to test the feature at a limited number of U.S. schools later this month. It will expand to other districts in the coming months after the company gathers educator feedback.

Guided Learning rolled out in Google Classroom to users globally as of Tuesday. Students start the activities after a teacher assigns them, and the activities draw on class material. Teachers receive live insights showing where a class may need added assistance.

Google said its research shows Guided Learning leads to measurable learning gains. The blog post did not cite independent evaluations, according to Bloomberg.

"Our goal is simple: provide school leaders the control and confidence to use AI products in classrooms as they see fit," Google said.

Google is meeting mounting pushback over AI from parents as well as school districts, according to Bloomberg. In August, Google made its chatbot active automatically in Classroom for schools allowing students to use Gemini, a move that school leaders criticized. In early September, New York City banned generative AI for students through eighth grade for the current school year, while Los Angeles Unified shut it off on devices issued by the district.

Sens. Richard Blumenthal, a Connecticut Democrat, and Josh Hawley, a Missouri Republican, sent a letter to Google CEO Sundar Pichai asking what the company knows about how its AI products affect children's mental health and learning. They set a Nov. 2 deadline for a reply.

Students have grown emotionally and academically dependent on Google's AI tools, according to a September investigation by The Wall Street Journal that drew on conversations with over 60 people, including teachers, school officials, parents, children and clinicians.
