Frick targets Premiership top-six for Aberdeen with 'heavy metal football'

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The 52-year-old has replaced Stephen Robinson at Pittodrie; the Dons are 11th in the Scottish Premiership; his first game in charge is at Kilmarnock on Saturday; watch Rangers vs Aberdeen on November 24, live on Sky Sports

Aberdeen's new head coach Mario Frick will not just be happy with Scottish Premiership survival this season, insisting he wants a top-six finish.

The 52-year-old takes over with the side sitting 11th in the table following Stephen Robinson's dismissal after their 4-0 defeat at Hibernian before the international break.

Frick, who played 125 times for Liechtenstein, previously took charge of the national youth teams in his homeland before spells with Vaduz and Luzern, helping the latter to avoid relegation.

"I had a very long career as a professional, so I know the pressure and I'm always optimist," he said.

Asked why he was confident of success, he added: "[Luzern] they were on the bottom of the table with 18 matches and 11 points and the whole environment was sad and we saved them.

"I'm pretty sure we can do this also in this place and I'm very ambitious.

"It's very important to get out of this situation but we want to go in the top six."

Frick will take charge for the first time on Saturday at bottom side Kilmarnock and he is determined to make an immediate impact with his style of football.

"Heavy metal," he said. "That's high pressing, that's counter-pressing vertical play creating many goalscoring chances."

Aberdeen have scored just 12 goals in the league this season while conceding 18 and Frick, who spent most of his playing career as a centre-forward, wants better.

"I'm known for high-scoring clubs. With Vaduz we had the goal record," the former St Gallen player said.

"Everything is lacking to score and so I'm here to change that.

"We have to improve now. We need time but we don't have, so it's more important to show the basics.

"Body language, willing to work, intensity. That's what I want to see."

The Aberdeen supporters booed Robinson off the pitch at the end of what was ultimately his final game at Pittodrie.

With just two home league wins this season, the morale of the Red Army is at a low.

Chief executive Adrian Bevington revealed Frick was the "unanimous decision," although his appointment took most by surprise.

"I'm not here to win with the fans, I'm here to win with my team.

"The supporters are amazing here I know that and but I'm not here to win them. The most important thing is the team and not me."

Frick faces an immediate relegation battle, starting with a run of five fixtures in just 15 days.

His first match is a trip to bottom of the table Kilmarnock. A win would move them five points clear, a defeat would see the Dons drop to 12th.

Next is a tough test against Rangers and former Aberdeen boss Derek McInnes at Ibrox, live on Sky Sports. The Dons have failed to win any of their last eight games against the Gers, losing to them in the Premiership and League Cup already this season.

There is no doubt Frick has taken charge of a team low on confidence, with four straight defeats ultimately leading to his predecessor's dismissal.

He has said he is "very hungry and ready to level up players" and, after playing most of his career as a centre-forward, he wants his team "playing forward and trying to score".

Time is not on the new manager's side. Robinson took four games before his first win, but with the prospect of ending the weekend bottom of the table, Frick will be aiming for a faster start to his Dons career.
