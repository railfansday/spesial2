Unfortunately, at that stage, that is still the patient's choice to make. The person who will become POA can discuss options and try to convince them to take a different course of treatment (or lack of treatment), of course.
That basically would be telling a loved one to turn down care that a specialist is saying is all he or she has to offer (though it did not seem to be helping). The PCP eventually suggested a less aggressive approach, but the specialist was considered to be among the top docs in the state in the given specialty, and held sway.
I don't have a problem with telling a top specialist that their treatment plan isn't something we intend to follow after thanking them for their input. Or, less confrontationally, to simply say we will think about how to proceed. (I have done this for my own medical care at times.) Nor do I have a problem with declining to consult with a specialist at times, for that matter. Just because something is all they have to offer doesn't make it a good idea in all circumstances, of course, as you seem to agree. But I can understand in some family dynamics that giving advice to a family member to consider going against specialist advice might not go over well. So again, in these situations, YMMV. And again, I am sorry for your experience.
