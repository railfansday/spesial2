Tesla (TSLA) Q3 2026 earnings preview: flat revenue, falling profits

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Tesla reports its third-quarter 2026 financial results tomorrow, October 21, after the market closes. The earnings call follows at 5:30 p.m. ET.

Wall Street expects revenue to come in flat and earnings per share to drop 10% from a year ago, even though Tesla just beat its own delivery consensus by more than 24,000 vehicles.

We already have the volume numbers. Tesla delivered 486,532 vehicles in Q3, down 2.1% from its all-time record of 497,099 in Q3 2025, but well above the 461,974 consensus Tesla compiled from 24 analysts.

It built only 464,391 vehicles during the quarter. So Tesla delivered 22,141 more cars than it produced, the second quarter in a row it has worked down inventory after building roughly 50,000 excess vehicles in Q1.

It’s still a two-car company. Model 3 and Model Y made up 478,237 of those deliveries, while “other models” (Cybertruck, Semi and what’s left of Model S and X) fell 48% year-over-year to 8,295 units.

Energy storage was the weak spot. Tesla deployed 13.7 GWh, up 9.6% from 12.5 GWh a year ago, but well short of the roughly 15.9 GWh analysts expected and below the 14.2 GWh record it set in Q4 2025.

The Wall Street consensus calls for $28.06 billion in revenue, with the Estimize crowd a bit more bullish at $28.36 billion. Either way, that’s flat against the $28.1 billion Tesla reported in Q3 2025. Wall Street estimates range from $25.1 billion to $30.6 billion.

Flat isn’t as bad as it sounds. Q3 2025 was inflated by US buyers rushing to grab the $7,500 federal tax credit before it expired on September 30, 2025, so matching it without the credit would be a decent result.

But look at the sequential picture. Tesla posted $28.24 billion in revenue in Q2 2026 on 480,126 deliveries and 13.5 GWh of storage. Q3 had more of both, yet Wall Street is modeling slightly less revenue and Estimize only about $120 million more, which means both are baking in lower average selling prices.

Analysts expect non-GAAP earnings per share of $0.45, down 10% from $0.50 in Q3 2025. The Estimize consensus is a little higher at $0.48, and Wall Street estimates range from $0.30 to $0.70.

Take that number with a grain of salt. In Q2, Tesla came in at $0.33 against a $0.53 consensus, as its operating margin collapsed to 1.4%. Operating income fell 57% to $398 million while operating expenses jumped 47% to $4.35 billion.

Tesla has now missed the Street’s EPS estimate in two of the last four quarters. And its EPS has declined year-over-year in five of the last seven.

The number we’ll be watching first is automotive gross margin excluding regulatory credits. It dropped to 16.3% in Q2 from 19.2% in Q1, which Tesla blamed on a $230 million warranty true-down and tariff relief that helped Q1 and didn’t repeat.

Regulatory credits won’t bail it out either. They brought in only $146 million in Q2, down 67% from a year earlier, and that pure-profit revenue keeps shrinking now that automakers no longer face federal CAFE penalties.

Then there’s cash. Tesla burned $1.09 billion in free cash flow in Q2 as capital expenditures more than doubled to $5.8 billion, and management guided to more than $25 billion in capex for 2026, with spending weighted to the second half. Another negative free cash flow quarter wouldn’t be a surprise.

Energy is worth a look too. The storage business ran a 20.4% gross margin in Q2, and Tesla says it expects the low-to-mid 20% range long term. With Q3 deployments missing estimates by more than 2 GWh, energy won’t offset much.

Tesla’s Say Technologies Q&A for the call closes today at 4 p.m. ET. It drew 832 questions from about 2,860 participants representing 3.85 million shares.

That’s more people than last quarter (about 1,710) but less than half the shares, which were 8.6 million in Q2.

The auto business is flat. Deliveries are up 8.8% year-to-date at 1,324,681, which is a real recovery from two years of decline, but Q3 came in below last year and Tesla still sells almost nothing outside of Model 3 and Model Y.

Shareholders mostly listen to Tesla bulls like Cathie Wood. Notice the stock was up regardless of the news. As long as people like Wood say the stock may go up to $5000 per share, investors will continue to put in money.

So the quarter comes down to margins and spending. If auto margins ex-credits hold around 16% and opex stops climbing 40%+ a year, the Q2 miss was a one-off. If not, Tesla is spending more than $25 billion this year on AI, Optimus, Cybercab and chips while its core business barely breaks even at the operating level. That’s a car company funding a very expensive bet out of a shrinking profit pool.

And shareholders mostly don’t care. Look at the questions: Cybercab, FSD, Optimus, HW3. The top question about Tesla’s consumer lineup was a request for a van.

They are completely delusional, waiting for questionable new products to bring in money while watching their cash cows stop growing, something that was completely avoidable if Musk hadn’t bet the whole company on autonomy too early.
