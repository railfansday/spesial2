5 up, 5 down from Miami’s 36-25 win over Duke

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

And with it being Monday, it's time again to look at who did the best and the worst among Miami Hurricanes who logged significant playing time on Saturday during the Canes' 36-25 win over the Duke Blue Devils – at least according to Pro Football Focus. And see how those performances matched up with my brain without the benefit of a rewatch. As usual, I'm looking at PFF's grading system based on players with at least 20 snaps.

I thought perhaps Mallory might be the highest-graded player, but he came in third. Still, he was undoubtedly one of Miami's best players on the day and certainly the most impactful with the 63-yard touchdown catch, 39-yard game-sealing touchdown run, and 150 scrimmage yards. He was a difference maker, although it's interesting that Mario Cristobal said this morning on "The Joe Rose Show" that Mallory would be mad about some other opportunities he missed during the game. The future is bright for 21, y'all.

Lucas had the big fourth quarter pick, and he was credited with allowing no catches on four targets and no missed tackles. Solid solid solid. I saw Blount get held BADLY in the first half in front of two officials. How those aren't called is mind blowing. But he still had three QB pressures credited to him and generated at least some form of a rush where others haven't (looking at a player wearing #10).

Mo Toure was all over the field with a team-high 11 total tackles, so you knew he was going to be a possibility. PFF also has him with four catches allowed on six targets for 20 yards, so the damage wasn't bad in the passing game.

Miami's guard play is generally leaving something to be desired, and that was on display on Saturday. I saw this and to check for myself, and PFF indeed had this grade for Alofaituli in true pass sets.

I can't say that I remember seeing him getting beaten on EVERY true pass set blocking rep, so I'm not sure where they're getting that number from. Off the top of my head, I'm honestly not sure how fair that is.

Congdon gave way to Rhys Woodrow at one point. But go back and watch that Mallory 39-yard touchdown run. I didn't notice this immediately live, but when I caught a replay on Twitter, I saw this (and I should have noticed/remembered this live). Watch Congdon block two Duke defenders and give Mallory space to get outside to the left and off to the races. Terrific work by him there.

So, where guys get some bad grades, I think that when they deliver on impact plays like that, it doesn't nearly accurately reflect in the grades they receive. Therefore, the grades sometimes don't fully reflect a player's own respective impact on the game, and I find that problematic.
