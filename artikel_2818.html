Struggling Colts should start Anthony Richardson Sr. at QB the rest of the 2026 campaign

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The consensus among Indianapolis Colts fans seems to be that this current rendition of Daniel Jones should no longer be the starting quarterback for the franchise.

After leading the Colts to an 8-2 start last year, at the helm of a historically elite offense at the time, the wheels have quickly come off for Jones and the franchise. Cracks of vulnerability were beginning to show even before Jones suffered critical injuries late last season, including a fractured fibula and season-ending torn Achilles. Most notably, opposing defenses began to blitz Jones at a higher rate, and started having a greater deal of success.

Jones continues to be pass pressured at a high rate, and while there may be some expected physical limitations, still working his way back less than a year removed from the torn Achilles, Jones' regression doesn't appear to be all physical—as his mental processing and decision-making in-game has also relapsed. He much more closely resembles the turnover prone former failed franchise quarterback hopeful for the New York Giants, than the breakout Pro Bowler that Colts fans saw firsthand for the first half of the 2025 campaign.

ESPN's Jeremy Fowler recently reported that Steichen sticking with Jones, despite his continued struggles, may speak more to the lack of viable quarterback options behind him:

Colts head coach Shane Steichen's loyalty to Daniel Jones might be less about Jones' poor performance and more about the options behind him. As it was told to me, Steichen simply is not motivated to play Anthony Richardson Sr., the 2023 fourth overall pick who fell out of favor after a strong start to his rookie year. Richardson and No. 3 quarterback Riley Leonard did not have strong training camps in the Colts' view, and it seems that has carried over. Richardson, a 2027 free agent, badly needs a fresh start elsewhere but appears poised for spot duty in Indy at some point this year, given the team's 3-7 record and its trading away of four key players before the deadline. But I just don't see Steichen benching Jones right now unless absolutely necessary.

That being said, if Steichen doesn't believe that Richardson can even supplant this clearly spiraling version of Jones, then what was the point of even keeping him on as the primary backup? They should've just moved him.

Furthermore, even if Richardson Sr. didn't have a particularly strong 2026 training camp, neither did Jones in 2025 Colts training camp when he won the starting job from Richardson Sr. and was named the new starter just weeks before the regular season started.

If anything, Richardson Sr. was arguably the stronger on-the-field performer in 2025 camp, whereas Jones won the job because of his off the field study and practice habits, and mental understanding of Steichen's offense.

I'm not saying Richardson Sr. is going to be the savior either, but at the very least, he could potentially provide this stalling passing offense a much needed spark, especially with his big arm and willingness to throw the ball downfield—which at the very least, is more exciting offense than the "Charlie Checkdown" Jones we've seen.

For a team on a short week missing its two starting offensive tackles, and facing Houston Texans All-Pro pass rusher Will Anderson Jr. on Thursday night, Richardson Sr. could also provide much needed mobility and escapability in the passing pocket, while making the Colts ground game more dynamic and versatile, and harder to defend and predict for the opposing defense.

Coming off the Achilles, this current version of Jones hasn't looked nearly as explosive or comfortable running with the football, so Richardson Sr.'s improved mobility would be a significant upgrade in that regard.

I'm just having a tough time with this one because Jones starting for the 2026 Colts has clearly run its course, and it's hard to see how starting Richardson Sr. could be any worse. The status quo at QB isn't working.

At the very least too, it provides the Colts, who are clearly going nowhere fast, one last extended look at the 24-year-old Richardson Sr., to see if he's made any significant jumps in passing accuracy and progression of reads, and safely close the chapter on his time here—should he falter again.
