Man Utd to contact PL and refs over 'totally wrong' Yoro red card interpretation

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Man Utd to contact ProRef and the Premier League over 'totally wrong' interpretation of Leny Yoro's red card; In the latest Match Officials Mic'd Up, ProRef chief refereeing officer Howard Webb analyses decision as audio of how officials handled incident is revealed

Manchester United will contact ProRef and the Premier League directly over what they believe to be a 'totally wrong' interpretation of Leny Yoro's red card against Liverpool.

Yoro was sent off after eight minutes of Sunday's 4-0 defeat at Anfield and was due to face a one-match ban for denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity when he brought down Alexander Isak outside the box as he was in on goal.

But Man Utd have had the suspension lifted after a successful claim of wrongful dismissal, the FA said on Tuesday. The independent commission will publish the written reasons in "due course".

Referees' chief Howard Webb backed the process of the officials who sent off the Manchester United defender.

Sky Sports News' Kaveh Solhekol: “I've looked back at past decisions of these commissions to see the make-up of the panel. "In the case of Brentford striker Igor Thiago - who was sent off for elbowing an opponent against Brighton earlier this month - the panel was made up of two former players in Tony Agana and Francis Benali, and a former referee, Steve Bennett.”

However, United boss Mauricio Pochettino questioned why the VAR did not intervene to overturn the on-field decision.

Speaking at a press conference ahead of his team's Champions League match against Sporting in Lisbon on Wednesday, Pochettino said: "Very disappointed because I think this type of thing never can happen.

"My question was always the same - we can accept the referee can make a mistake because of the emotional relationship with the game.

"But if we have VAR, what were they doing? What were they doing? No, because now we are going to repeat the game? We're going to have the possibility to play with the similar tools, 11 against 11?

"The problem is that when we were there, I think they need to take their time to review the situation.

"We have an unbelievable car but we need to be in the level to drive, we need to drive in the best way."

In the latest edition of Match Officials Mic'd Up, Webb, chief refereeing officer at ProRef, described the on-field decision from referee Samuel Barrott as "decisive" and said the point of contention was around whether Isak would beat United goalkeeper Senne Lammens to the ball.

Audio from the officials reveals that the VAR, John Brooks, who backed Barrott's decision, determined Isak was "likely" to get on the end of the ball before Lammens. His assistant VAR, Paul Howard, agreed.

Webb was non-committal in his verdict but backed the process of the officials by saying "most people would see that as a correct decision".

"There's a question mark, I guess, around whether or not he would get onto that ball before Lammens," said Webb. "Now, in the moment, the referee looked at it and felt he would. That was the referee's call made on the field.

"The VAR checks that to see whether that decision made on the field was clearly and obviously incorrect and formed the opinion that it wasn't. If there was any doubt, then it was doubt.

"It wasn't going to be something that was clearly and obviously wrong and therefore allowed that decisive on-field decision to stand. And I think by and large most people would see that as a correct decision with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball.

"The alternative view is that Lammens might get there first, but I think it's a little bit debatable, I guess. Certainly not clearly wrong."

Manchester United strongly believe the incident was a result of a process error and a failure to interpret the rules correctly.

Club sources believe on the VAR audio there is a quick look and consensus to support the referee with no meaningful challenge or consideration there was an error.

United are also understood to be unhappy there has been no recognition from ProRef that the red card was incorrect after the disciplinary panel overturned the decision.

United believe it is not a subjective decision. If Isak had maintained his top-recorded speed, he still could not have reached the ball before Senne Lammens and the club believe it is objectively provable and they could have shown this on the data within minutes.

ProRef did not want to comment further when contacted by Sky Sports News.

Earlier this season, Pro Ref admitted an error in awarding Manchester City's winner at Manchester United in their 1-0 victory at Old Trafford and contacted the club to explain how the VAR "should have recommended an on-field review" for Erling Haaland's winner.

Sky Sports News understands United received an apology over the incorrect VAR decision.
