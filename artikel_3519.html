Country Singers Name the Musicians They're Most Thankful for This Thanksgiving

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Country music is so much more than an industry — it’s a community filled with supporters and cheerleaders.

Need proof? Just look what happened when Us decided to celebrate Thanksgiving by asking artists to share a fellow musician they are grateful for this holiday season.

Time and time again, country singers shared stories of their peers who have offered encouragement, inspiration and friendship in times when it mattered most.

9-1-1: Nashville star LeAnn Rimes will never forget the opportunity she got to learn from Reba McEntire during a Dr. Pepper commercial.

As for Lady A’s Dave Haywood, he continues to look up to Tim McGraw for hitting all the right notes when it comes to “work-life balance.”

Keep reading to see who your favorite country singers are grateful for this Turkey Day:

I’m thankful for Dolly [Parton]. I am thankful for the light she shined on this world, for all the goodness, for her heart. There will never be anyone like Dolly, and I think she showed us what real heart looks like, and she radiated goodness. I just think we could all learn a thing or two from Dolly. I’m just grateful that we got to have her in all of our lives in this lifetime, and I’m grateful that I got to have the time with her that I did, and she was impactful and inspiring to me as not just an artist, but just as a woman in this world. And I always, like everyone else, I always think, “What would Dolly do?” And she would always meet everything and everyone with kindness.

I’m thankful for my buddy, Thomas Rhett. I’ve known Thomas for a long time now, and I have always been a fan of the way he has approached music and his career while keeping his family first; I’ve always respected that. He’s been a role model to me not just in music, but in life. I love him to death and I’m proud to call him a friend.

I would absolutely say Willie Nelson. He has had such a huge influence on me, being from Austin, that I just released a Willie Nelson tribute album. He is a true one of one, a legend, an amazing songwriter, and being on stage with him a few times has been a highlight of my career so far.

I’d have to say Carrie Underwood! I remember early on, when I first got to Nashville, we swapped numbers, and I ended up calling her from the side of the road one day just to ask for advice. She was so gracious with her time and encouragement. She’s continued to be such a kind, steady source of encouragement over the years, even from afar. This industry can be a lot to navigate, especially when you’re young and figuring things out, so I’ve always been really grateful for the kindness she’s shown me. I grew up listening to her music and have looked up to her for a long time, so it’s been really special to experience that same kindness from her personally.

I would say Reba. She is one of the sweetest people I’ve ever known, and in my early, early years — and still to this day — if I see her, she’s just incredibly kind and supportive. I would definitely have to say she kind of tops my list. We did a Dr. Pepper commercial together in my early 2000s and she has always been someone who uplifts everyone around her, including myself. I just love her to death. I can’t say enough good things about her.

I’ve always looked up to Hilary Duff, and she was actually my very first concert! Even though she grew up in the spotlight as a child star, she has always kept a strong head on her shoulders and remained true to herself. I love her message of ‘strong, not skinny’ and the way she continues to promote confidence, self-love and a healthy perspective on body image.

I’m thankful for Stevie Nicks. I’ve always been drawn to women who create an entire world around their music, and Stevie is the ultimate example. Her songs are romantic, haunting, feminine and fierce all at once. What inspires me most at this chapter in my life is how unapologetically herself she has remained. She reminds me that there’s power in your individuality, beauty in your scars and that a woman’s creative spirit only gets more interesting with time.

We are thankful for Jeffrey Steele. There’s something extra special about someone who believed in you back when you were just two guys chasing a dream. Jeffrey has been that person for us, mentoring us through the ups and downs of this industry. We wouldn’t be where we are today without him.

I’m thankful for Tim McGraw and the example he has set for us as a band. His commitment to continuing his legacy through music and touring, while prioritizing his wife and three daughters, is such a beautiful picture of a successful work-life balance. He was actually one of the first to invite us out on a big run, and hopping on his stadium tour this summer was something special. We also were so honored to surprise him during his induction into the Country Music Hall of Fame this year with our take on “Live Like You Were Dying.” He is so passionate about pouring into other artists, and having Tim be a mentor and friend all these years now means so much.

I’m thankful to Vince Gill because he was the very first person who agreed to sing on my very first album, and I hadn’t even met him yet. This act of generosity has taught me to pay it forward with other artists or songwriters. He also told me that using wedges over in ear monitors allowed for the experience of being immersed in music fully. Nashville is the foundation for my songwriting. I’m so deeply grateful for this community.

I’m thankful for Dolly because her talent and legacy have shown me so much. When I was 16, I convinced my parents to move me to Tennessee so I could sing in the country show at Dollywood. I guess you could say I’ve always been grateful for her!

I’m a country singer with California roots, so the west is in my DNA. The Bakersfield sound changed my world. It’s always felt like home to me, and Dwight Yoakam has carried that torch.

Before I had a record deal, Dwight took a chance on me and recorded “Heart Burn” with me. That duet changed my life. Singing with one of my musical heroes was a dream come true, and I’m just as grateful for the friendship that grew out of it with Dwight, his wife Emily, and their son Dalton. Dwight has become a mentor and someone I can call when I’m trying to make sense of this crazy industry. My favorite piece of advice from him is one I still carry with me. He told me, “Go with God.”

Honestly, I’m thankful for Riley Green. There’s been a full-circle story behind our relationship that has shown me grace, humility and what it means to be a true friend. It showed me that a true song wins. Songwriting is King and to treat others the way you wanna be treated.

I’ve known and loved Sheryl Crow’s music since I was a kid. But over the past two years, while writing my last record and going through a deep season of transformation, I found my way back to her albums. Songs I’d known for years took on a completely new meaning. “Strong Enough,” “My Favorite Mistake,” “Maybe Angels,” “The First Cut Is the Deepest”. It felt like I was hearing them for the first time, with enough life behind me to understand them differently.

To me, Sheryl’s music is what embodied art sounds like. There’s something so instinctive about it, like the words and melodies could move straight from whatever she was feeling into the music. You hear the ache, the humor, the desire, the contradictions. It all gets to be there.

In this season of my own life, I’ve been trying to remove as many filters as I can between myself and the audience. That’s made me appreciate the openness in her music even more. It reminds me how powerful a song can be when you let someone close enough to recognize themselves in you. I can get so caught up in finding the right words that sometimes I forget to trust the real ones.

I’m forever grateful for her songs, but just as grateful for her example: the dedication to her craft, the commitment to saying things that matter, and the courage to let the truest parts of herself find a place in the music.

This year, I had the privilege of opening for Kacey Musgraves on her Middle of Nowhere tour. After meeting her and getting to know her, it is clear to me why she is as successful as she is. Not only is she incredibly talented, but she pays attention to details and is very hands-on in everything she does. I witnessed her talking to the lighting director for an hour after her sound check until they got the colors exactly the way she wanted them. She works on all levels of her career. That is how it should be, and that is something I would like to apply to my own career.

I’ll forever be thankful for my friend Keith Urban who was the first artist to take me out on the road as an opener. Watching him thrill crowds every night and run his entire tour like a well-oiled machine was a masterclass on how it’s done.

I’m thankful for Miranda Lambert because she has been supportive of me and my art from the beginning. She sends me long notes after binging my album, making me promise to keep going. She calls when she loves a song. When she asks how I’m doing, she genuinely means it. Miranda is as real and authentic as she is badass.

The fans! The way they show up for me and support me, it really inspires me to keep going and give them everything I have to give. I love The Lefties and I’m so grateful for them!

I’m thankful for Deana Carter. “Strawberry Wine” was one of my favorite songs growing up. It reminded me of my high school sweetheart back in the day. It was just such a nostalgic country song, and it’s still so good even today. I remember when I first kind of started in the country world, I told my producer how much I loved Deana Carter, and she came over and we wrote a song together, and she let me sing “Strawberry Wine” with her, and she didn’t have to do that, and she’s so sweet and such a kind person. I still remember that. I wasn’t known at all in the country world, and she still took the time to sit with me, write a song and let me sing “Strawberry Wine” with her, and it was a memory I’ll always remember. I’ll never forget that.

I’m thankful for Kristian Bush because he’s taught me to be bold, stay true to my roots and always enjoy the ride! A chance meeting at a songwriting festival in Hawaii turned into him producing my very first record, and now I’ve been out on the road opening for Sugarland on my very first arena tour! Getting to learn from one of my childhood heroes every night is a dream come true!

I’m thankful for Rhett Akins because of the support, mentorship, friendship and opportunities he has given me. When I moved to Nashville, I had never done a cowrite in my life. Rhett took a chance on me, and we have now written multiple songs together, and I was also lucky enough to watch him be inducted as a member of the Grand Ole Opry this year.

I’m thankful for Russell Dickerson because he and his wife, Kailey, have been two of my closest friends for almost 20 years. I still can’t believe that writing songs with one of my best friends is a part of my job. We’ve been so lucky to celebrate some pretty big wins together, but getting to live life and raise our kids alongside each other is my favorite part.
