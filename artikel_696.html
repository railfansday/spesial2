Indian Mythology Meets Mumbai Noir in Animated Feature ‘Bombay Eternal’ From ‘Rana Naidu’ Producer Locomotive Global, AI Outfit Studio Blo (EXCLUSIVE)

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Figures drawn from Indian mythology are headed for the streets of a stylized modern-day Mumbai in “Bombay Eternal,” an animated neo-noir crime fantasy from “Rana Naidu” producer Locomotive Global and AI-native film company Studio Blo, which have released a first teaser.

The project is planned as a feature film for eventual theatrical release in international markets, and the story will unfold across multiple parts. The partners are building it as a franchise from the outset and want the kind of worldwide reach achieved by “Ne Zha,” “Arcane,” “KPop Demon Hunters” and the “Spider-Verse” films. The story borrows characters and tales from Indian mythology and drops them into an original present-day setting where crime, magic and mystery mix with themes of redemption. The filmmakers have played freely with familiar archetypes, recasting them in a more modern story designed to be accessible worldwide. The teaser gives a first look at some of the main characters and locations, and shows off a visual style that pairs the bustle of the city with an animation approach that crosses genres.

Locomotive Global’s slate also includes a Tamil-language horror film with Trisha Krishnan.

“The world of ‘Bombay Eternal’ has been in the making for more than two decades. What began as an idea set in Manhattan has evolved considerably, but the core ambition has remained the same: to take characters and ideas inspired by our mythology and place them in a contemporary world in a way that feels cinematic, unexpected and globally accessible. Our partnership with Studio Blo is about finding the right creative and production ecosystem to finally realize that vision,” said Sunder Aaron, Locomotive Global’s founder and CEO.

Studio Blo, which recently set an international series with “Elizabeth” director Shekhar Kapur, was co-founded by Dipankar Mukherjee, a filmmaker who also works as a creative technologist. The company has devised a look for the film that the team calls “painterly cinematic,” setting illustrated, painting-like foreground elements against deep, film-style environments. It is also contributing its in-house technology, production pipelines and global tech partnerships.

Over the past year, teams from both companies have worked out how the film will look and sound. Recruits include U.K.-based musicians and voice actors, along with production designers, cinematographers and artists drawn from Europe and the U.S.

“What drew us to this project was the opportunity to build a world rather than simply produce a film. The mythology gives us an incredibly rich foundation, but the visual language, characters and environments we are creating are distinctly our own,” said Mukherjee, who is also Studio Blo’s CEO.

The partners said new production tools are being folded into the workflow to keep the film efficient and financially viable. They stressed, however, that craft, storytelling and design remain at the center of the project, along with the considerable human labor behind it.

More of the film will be revealed in stages. Much of its wider story world is being kept under wraps for now.

Indian artists and technicians have long worked on major international animation projects. With “Bombay Eternal,” the partners are betting India can now originate animated IP of its own, much as Korean and Chinese animation have shown that stories rooted in one culture can find audiences far from home.
