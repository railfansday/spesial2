Borrow the Toys Your Kids Will Outgrow – Libraries Lend Them for Little or Nothing

Toys for babies and toddlers fit a stage your child soon outgrows, so borrow them instead of buying. Start with your public library or a toy library, then try swap groups and rental services, and rotate the few favorites you keep so they feel new again.

Buying them new adds up. One $40 toy every three weeks comes to about $690 a year, and the toys stay in your closets long after your child has moved on.

A toy library lends toys the way a library lends books, for little or nothing, and each one goes back after a few weeks instead of piling up at home. Borrow the bulky, pricey toys made for one stage, such as ride-ons, activity centers, and age-specific learning toys, and buy only the few your child keeps reaching for.

Before your child plays with anything borrowed, wipe it down, make sure no pieces are missing, and search its name on the Consumer Product Safety Commission’s recalls page.

Rotate the toys you keep, too. Leave a handful out, put the rest away, and switch them every couple of weeks. In a University of Toledo study of 36 toddlers, children played twice as long with their toys when the room held 4 instead of 16. Once your child outgrows a toy for good, have them sell it or give it away.

This week, ask at your library’s children’s desk whether it lends toys.
