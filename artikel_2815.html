‘It Was So Bad I’m Now Wondering How Did They Win That Game?’ — Hall of Fame QB Refuses to Praise Chargers After Ending 11-Game Skid vs. Ravens

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It took nine tries, but the Los Angeles Chargers finally found the win column Monday night, edging the Baltimore Ravens 23-20 at M&T Bank Stadium for their first victory since Dec. 21 of last season.

However, two-time NFL MVP and Pro Football Hall of Famer Kurt Warner decided to give the game a second look days later, and he came away calling the Chargers' passing game "bad live, but even worse than I thought… AGAIN!" with Justin Herbert's name at the top of his list of problems.

Warner ran through his concerns one by one. "Just getting to watching back the MNF game, and I thought the Chargers' pass game was bad live, but even worse than I thought… AGAIN! Herbert missing reads… Spacing bad… WRs falling down… What appeared to be numerous miscommunications between QB & WR! It was so bad I'm now wondering how did they win that game?"

The answer to Warner's question lies largely away from the passing game, because the Chargers did most of their damage on the ground, piling up 160 rushing yards on 34 carries. Omarion Hampton ran for 105 yards and a touchdown on 22 carries, while Herbert chipped in 51 yards on seven carries, including a 22-yard touchdown run on the Chargers' first possession.

The defense held up its end of the bargain, too, limiting Baltimore to 16 rushing yards on 15 carries. Derrick Henry managed just 10 yards on 10 carries, though he punched in two short touchdowns.

Chargers head coach Jim Harbaugh credited the run defense after the game, saying, "Heck of a run wall, [a] big emphasis on that. Just trying not to let [Henry] get started … guys just battled the whole night."

Herbert credited his teammates after Monday's win, which snapped an 0-8 start to the season and an 0-11 losing streak going back to last season. "It's been a tough year. It hasn't gone our way this year, and for those guys to continue to fight, not point fingers, and stick together… they've earned it, and I'm really happy for them."

That leaves the passing game Warner zeroed in on. Herbert finished 12-of-18 for 129 yards without a touchdown pass, his 18 attempts a season low, and the offense converted just two of its 13 third downs.

According to Next Gen Stats, Herbert took an average of 3.46 seconds to throw in Week 10, the longest of 29 qualified quarterbacks, and his average intended air yards of 5.6 were tied for second-shallowest in that group.

Still, Herbert went without an interception for the first time this season. His completion percentage over expected of 9.9 ranked fifth of 29 qualified quarterbacks that week, and Tre' Harris caught all five passes thrown his way for 86 yards.

This was not Warner's first trip through Herbert's tape this season. Back in October, he said on "The Rich Eisen Show," "This is all speculation here, but when I watch Justin Herbert the last couple weeks drop back and throw the ball right to a defender numerous times, we're all looking at these plays like what is he looking at?"

He floated the offense's structure as a possible reason, adding, "Again, speculation, but I've seen it a lot in pure progression offenses where guys are looking at their guys as opposed to the opposing team's guys. And they're a bit oblivious."

The season-long picture has not been much prettier. Through nine games, Herbert has six touchdown passes against 11 interceptions and has been sacked 33 times, and his 71.6 passer rating ranks 30th of 31 qualified quarterbacks. Pro Football Network's NFL QB Impact Grade has him at 61.2 for the season, 35th of 35 graded quarterbacks.

Harbaugh's postgame message was a simple one: "Thrilled, let's do it every week. Good to see the smiles back on the faces."

The Chargers announced Saturday that they activated guard/tackle Trey Pipkins III from injured reserve. That adds a veteran with 66 career regular-season starts to an offensive line that started three rookies in Baltimore.

According to PFN's win probabilities for Week 11, Los Angeles has a 50.4% chance at home against the Jets, leaving the matchup on a knife's edge.

Now 1-8 and fourth in the AFC West, Los Angeles kicks off against the Jets at 4:05 p.m. ET Sunday at SoFi Stadium.
