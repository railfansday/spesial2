Australia’s Sandpapergate probe to include 2017/18 Ashes series

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Australia's conduct during the 2017/18 Ashes is back under the microscope after the independent forum set up to explore the 'sandpapergate' scandal was handed an expanded brief.

Earlier this month, Cricket Australia reacted to a barrage of claims and counter-claims from former players by announcing that all players and staff members would be invited to share their recollections of the ill-fated 2018 South Africa tour in a 'safe space'.

CA has now formalised the terms of reference and opted to add the previous home summer – when Australia beat England 4-0 to reclaim the urn – to its scope.

Usman Khawaja's book revived interest in the sandpaper scandal. (Rory Dollard/PA)

A statement confirmed the forum would hear accounts related to "ball-management practices in the Australian Men's Team during the 2017/18 season, all four Tests of the 2018 SA Tour, the events of 24 March 2018, (and) the conduct and scope of the 2018 CA investigation".

Usman Khawaja, whose autobiography reopened the historic drama, has alleged that ball-tampering methods were being utilised prior to South Africa and Cameron Bancroft – who was caught red-handed in Cape Town with sandpaper – has written about being urged to manipulate the quarter-seam on his Test debut against England in Brisbane.

Those claims have brought to light a passage in Sir Alastair Cook's own memoir, published in 2019, in which he alleges David Warner admitted during a post-series drink to using substances in his finger strapping to deteriorate the ball.

Warner has admitted his involvement in the sandpaper episode but responded to subsequent reporting about his using superglue and grit, by stating: "I would suggest establishing the facts rather than perpetuating speculation".

England have maintained near total silence on the subject during their recent white-ball tour of Australia. Ahead of the ODI series Joe Root, who captained the 2017/18 tour, stated: "We obviously have no idea (about it).

"It's all speculation. I wasn't in that dressing room. I don't know what went on."

CA has announced that senior barrister Fiona McLeod will oversee the forum process, which will invite but not compel squad members and support staff from the South Africa tour to share their recollections and experiences.

David Warner (left) and Sir Alastair Cook (right) shake hands (Jason O'Brien/PA)

CA has reiterated its previous commitment that no new sanctions or punishments will be handed down, while the confidentiality of all participants has been guaranteed.

After a six-week evidence period, a report will be published within 30 days covering "principal themes" and "recommendations for future improvements" rather than specific testimony or direct apportioning of blame.

CA chair David Boon said: "This is an opportunity to provide their accounts in a safe environment and to provide the CA Board with relevant information including about the events of that tour and resultant investigation.

"We are acutely aware of the enormous toll the renewed focus on this tour eight years ago has had on those involved and their welfare remains our main priority. This forum is the best opportunity for people to speak and be heard, and for CA to listen and learn.

"Publishing the report in full is part of our commitment to transparency, and to be open with cricket fans about what we learn. It is also very important to understand that it will not seek to sanction or recriminate."
