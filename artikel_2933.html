SES AI: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Software-driven accuracy gains could let battery customers buy less hardware, while M-Labs now targets manufacturers directly.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 19, 2026 at 2:01AM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 12, 2026, SES AI (SES -7.19%) announced a 25% sequential narrowing of its net loss to $13.4 million. It's pivoting from legacy energy storage hardware toward specialized defense and software markets where it owns production facilities. Three things management said on the call stand out.

Management is directing capital into drone battery manufacturing. The company completed the scale-up at its South Korean factory to satisfy National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) compliance rules, which mandate non-Chinese defense supply chains.

Energy storage systems (ESS), which supply stationary battery power, generate nearly half of current revenue. SES AI uses Edgebox, its on-site software device, to calculate state-of-charge metrics far more accurately than legacy tools, letting customers purchase less physical hardware.

Molecular Universe, the company's battery material discovery platform, expanded into a stand-alone software product named M-Labs. Battery manufacturers evaluate the air-gapped system to accelerate testing without exposing proprietary customer data to external networks.

Demand for drone batteries is accelerating as defense supply chains pivot away from China. The company relies on its Korean factory, proprietary software, and $137 million in debt-free liquidity to scale production. Operational priorities center on commercial conversion rather than lab testing.

Combining software intelligence, drone battery manufacturing, and material discovery creates an integrated engine for growth. Converting qualification trials into long-term purchase orders remains the primary test for the business.
