Crystal Palace must surely regret Justin Devenny deal, in-depth data says it all

Crystal Palace may well be regretting their £5million sale of Justin Devenny to Stoke City given his current trajectory and the way they have struggled this season.

Pierre Sage was handed the reins at Selhurst Park over the summer as Palace's post-Oliver Glasner era began.

The Frenchman allowed versatile young midfielder Devenny to leave towards the end of the window as he joined Stoke for an initial £5m.

He has impressed this season for his promotion-challenging club, and was key to Northern Ireland's great fortunes over the international break.

Palace have already sacked Sage, meanwhile, as they struggle to put a run of results together and sit bottom of the Premier League table.

They may have been better off keeping the homegrown 23-year-old around for this campaign rather than selling him for a relatively nominal fee.

Devenny made 60 appearances for the Eagles across two seasons in their first team, playing an underrated role in their most successful squad ever as they won the FA Cup, Community Shield and Conference League.

The 23-year-old, who can play across the midfield and as a full-back, started his Stoke career in explosive fashion with a brace on his debut against Charlton in September.

He has since continued to impress and mostly kept his place in Mark Robins' side, with the Potters sat third in the second tier, proving an important part of their team with tireless off-ball work and effective link-up play.

Devenny shone at left wing-back in the latest round of international fixtures for Northern Ireland, creating the joint-most chances (3), winning the most ground duels (6) and making the most tackles (5) of any player in their 1-0 win over Ukraine.

He followed that up with a stunning equaliser against Hungary on Tuesday to earn the Green and White Army promotion to Nations League A, while also having seven defensive contributions, five recoveries and four duels won.

Tyrick Mitchell has nailed that spot down at Palace in recent seasons, but they lack sufficient depth and Devenny was always a great option to have in several areas of the pitch, particularly with their European exploits needing a bigger squad.

It already looks like a mistake on the Eagles' part to have sold the 23-year-old for such a small fee when they will now need to invest heavily to bolster Thomas Frank's side in the new year.

The Northern Irish international penned a four-year deal in North Staffordshire and is already one of the most decorated players in the Championship right now.

Palace's decision to sell in September looks even worse when the CIES Football Observatory's latest data is taken into account.

Devenny is the Potters’ most valuable player as it stands, and by their calculations, a fair price for his next move would be between £16m and £18.7m.

Palace clearly could have made much more money if they had held out and given him more time to develop in the Premier League.
