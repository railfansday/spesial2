Ranking every 2026/27 EFL Championship club by average attendance

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The EFL Championship provides great wide-ranging diversity when it comes to average home attendances, and that trend has not been bucked one bit so far in the 2026/27 campaign.

Indeed, the season's half-way point is already swiftly upon us and all 24 sides have played 17 matches in front of their home supporters ahead of this weekend's round of fixtures, with average attendance figures taking shape by this stage.

There are a handful of second-tier clubs who can call some of the country's largest stadiums home, whereas others play in grounds that wouldn't look out of place in League One or even League Two. Some clubs, meanwhile, have large capacities which simply never come close to being filled, providing a number of nuanced reasons to the context of attendances.

With that in mind, then, Football League World has decided to rank EVERY Championship club so far this season by measure of average home attendances from worst to best.

Kicking off proceedings is Lincoln City, who are in the second-tier for the first time since 1961 after winning last term's League One title against all odds and, unsurprisingly, have the division's lowest average attendance at just 10,501.

The Imps, in all fairness, play their football in the Championship's smallest stadium, and the 10,780-seater Sincil Bank virtually always operates at near-full capacity.

It's a similar story with Wrexham, with vital context required as to why the Red Dragons are only posting the Championship's second-lowest home attendance figures at 10,544.

Their Stok Cae Ras home is currently operating at a reduced capacity of 10,700 from around 12,600 owing to the redevelopment of the club's Kop Stand since the beginning of the previous campaign which, when completed, will allow more than 18,000 supporters into North Wales' largest ground.

Wrexham fans, then, are doing rather well to ensure precious few empty seats at this moment in time.

Blackburn Rovers, by contrast, are housed in one of the Championship's largest grounds, yet that doesn't prevent some of the division's worst attendance figures being posted each season without fail.

Rovers supporters are disillusioned with the club's direction under the unpopular Venky's ownership group, which is the driving force behind frequent boycotts and low attendances at their 31,367-capacity Ewood Park home.

Blackburn's average attendance falls shy of the 15,000 mark in a stadium that rarely even fills out to 50% of its total capacity, with opponents able to take thousands upon thousands of fans to East Lancashire as a result.

Across the Lancashire divide, cross-county rivals Preston North End are hardly faring much better with an average home attendance of just 16,397.

The context, of course, is that Deepdale's 23,408 capacity makes it a considerably-smaller stadium and the Lilywhites' struggles this season have clearly kept some supporters away.

QPR, meanwhile, should perhaps be doing somewhat better, although Loftus Road is among the Championship's smallest and most tightly-packed stadiums.

Despite having hosted numerous campaigns of Premier League football, the homely and traditional Loftus Road is only capable of catering to 18,360 supporters at full capacity.

Therefore, QPR's average attendance of 16,632 certainly represents a respectable figure, even if a few more seats could be taken up.

Watford's 21,557 capacity means that the Vicarage Road faithful can only ever do so much, and an average attendance of 17,636 is nothing to be sniffed at in light of the Hornets' difficulties both on and off-the-pitch.

Other clubs, mind you, provide a decent atmosphere in spite of smaller numbers that Watford simply do not offer up.

The majority of the Championship's lesser-attended clubs can be contextualised with on-pitch struggles, but that's hardly been the case with Swansea City this season.

They have enjoyed an uplifting campaign despite the departure of Vitor Matos, yet their attendance figures never vary too much and the Jack Army are averaging just shy of 18,000 supporters for each home fixture at the 21,088-capacity Swansea.com Stadium.

Charlton Athletic's 18,180 average attendance certainly poses questions considering the large capacity of the Valley, which often features more than a few vacant seats.

Indeed, it can accommodate around 27,000 supporters, but the Addicks struggle to sell-out and do see attendance figures limited by the volume of competition within the South London area.

Millwall, however, are undoubtedly making the most of their more modestly-sized 20,146-seater home less than five miles up the road, which features an average attendance of 19,305.

That figure is even greater than that of the previous campaign, in which the Lions made it all the way to the play-offs, and there's little room for attendance improvement considering that The Den can only let in just over 20,000 supporters.

Attendances throughout Lancashire have been nothing to shout about this season, yet Burnley's figures relative to stadium capacity are much more impressive.

The Clarets have also endured their fair share of challenges since returning to the Championship this term, although supporters are doing a good job of filling out the 21,994-seater Turf Moor by returning average attendances in excess of 19,000.
