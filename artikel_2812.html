MVC NOTEBOOK: Sycamore women take big step in facing off against taller foes

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Aaliyah Youngblood and her Indiana State teammates had just finished a wild 86-74 victory over IU Indy in ISU's home opener Tuesday night, when she challenged assumptions that the Sycamores would be outsized this basketball season.

In doing so, Youngblood hinted at how ISU might match up with Missouri Valley Conference competition once those games begin next month.

Youngblood paused, realizing she's not yet played a MVC game. The speedy junior guard transferred to ISU from San Joaquin Delta College in California, as one of 13 newcomers for the 2026-27 season.

But her point remains valid. In Tuesday's win, the Sycamores' lineup — with six players 6-foot or taller — reversed a 32-14 first-half rebounding deficit. ISU grabbed 26 second-half boards to IU Indy's 17, exploded for a 38-5 run during the third and fourth periods and won the nonconference game handily.

"We're a little smaller than other teams, but that doesn't mean we're ever going to back down," said Youngblood, whose seven steals and 17 points off the bench helped ISU erase a 17-point deficit.

"I feel like we're one of the toughest teams — well, I'm new to this [Missouri Valley] Conference, obviously but — we're still going to put up a fight every game, no matter our size, no matter anything. We're still going to fight to the end every time."

That's music to coach Marc Mitchell's ears.

"I measure toughness with rebounding, and they know that," Mitchell said after Tuesday's win. "We were getting outrebounded minus-18 in the first half. I made that a point of emphasis at halftime. You've got to toughen up. Winning is very, very hard to do. At any level, winning is hard. And rebounding is a measure of toughness."

Illinois State and Drake currently lead the MVC in rebounding with 52 and 43 boards per game respectively. The Sycamores rank third at 39.8.

ISU's conference opener is Dec. 18 against defending MVC champ Murray State in Hulman Center.

• Around the Valley — Two other MVC women's basketball teams got nonconference wins Tuesday. Drake (now 2-1) beat visiting Cleveland State 76-71 and Valparaiso (1-2) won 89-47 at home vs. Culver Stockton. On Monday, Bradley (1-2) lost 84-56 to the Indiana Hoosiers in Bloomington's Simon Skjodt Assembly Hall.

The Sycamores' next outing comes at 7 p.m. Friday against Wright State in Hulman Center. The Raiders stand 1-2 for the season after a 109-52 loss at seventh-ranked Michigan on Sunday in Ann Arbor. WSU's 5-9 guard Jayden Terry led the Raiders with 24 points against the Wolverines.

• MVC men's hoops notes — Four Valley men's teams were unbeaten going into Wednesday night, including Murray State and Illinois-Chicago at 3-0 and Drake and Valparaiso at 2-0.

Valpo drew the most attention with its program's first-ever road victory over a Top 25 opponent, beating Purdue 83-80 in double overtime Friday in Mackey Arena. Beacons sophomore Rakin Chaney totaled 35 points, eight rebounds, six assists and no turnovers for the Beacons, becoming the first player nationally to notch such numbers since 2016, according to the MVC. He earned MVC Player of the Week honors, as a result.

Back in 2023-24, coach Roger Powell's debut season ended with a 7-25 record for Valpo. Since then, his Beacons have improved their marks to 15-19, 18-15 and now 2-0, so far.

Coach Matthew Graves' Sycamores play at Valparaiso on Jan. 27, then play host to the Beacons on Feb. 13.

ISU also got a significant in-state victory Friday night. While Valpo was winning at Purdue, the Sycamores rallied from 15 points down to win 69-66 at Ball State. A 20-point performance by ISU's Karmani Gregory against the Cardinals yielded him MVC Newcomer of the Week honors. His 29 points in a season-opening loss to Charlotte also mattered in that award.

The MVC's cumulative 17-5 nonconference record through Sunday's games ranks ninth best among Division I conferences in the country. The ACC tops that list at 36-3.
