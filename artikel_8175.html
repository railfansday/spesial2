The Pilgrims Hug

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Faced with a 21-mile mountain trek and no open accommodations in between, we decided to book a taxi at 7:30 AM to jump ahead to Vilaseirío. Although a minor miscommunication delayed our ride, we eventually got dropped off in the countryside, shortening our daily walk to a much more manageable 13 miles.

It was a glorious, bluebird morning on June 6th, with cool 56°F air and morning dew coating the fields. The path led us along dirt and paved farm roads through lush cornfields and managed eucalyptus groves. The dark, organic soil smelled rich and earthy, framed by vibrant white and purple wildflowers.

Reflecting on a YouTube video I had watched of someone hiking this same stretch in September, the contrast was striking. Where their autumn footage showed dry, brown, and scorched hillsides, our June hike was alive with vibrant poppies and rolling green pastures.

After passing several large dairy operations, we pulled over at Panadería As Maroñas—one of the few bakeries on the trail that bakes all their pastries fresh daily.

While enjoying our baked goods, we met Bernard, a traveler from Austria now living in Berlin. He was heading in the opposite direction after walking from Muxía down to Finisterre and back toward Santiago.

Bernard taught us the “Heart-to-Heart Pilgrim Hug” he learned on the Meseta: both people raise their left arms and embrace chest-to-chest so you can feel each other’s heartbeat. Following a deep and meaningful conversation, we said our goodbyes and hit the trail again around 11:00 AM.

The path briefly followed a busy side road where cars whizzed past without slowing down. Leaving the traffic behind, we began a steady climb through hillside farms. Near the crest, a fellow hiker in front of us suddenly collapsed face-first onto the pavement. Knockerz immediately jumped in to help him lie down, elevate his head, and control the bleeding from his face. Once a larger group of multilingual hikers stepped in to manage the situation, we quietly pushed on.

To avoid a steep, pointless up-and-down section (PUD), we opted for a brief road walk around the peak. As we crested the hill, a broad lake opened up in the valley below, flanked by pine forests, granite outcroppings, and distant mountain peaks.

Continuing along country dirt roads, we eventually crossed into Ponte Olveira. Suddenly, someone yelled our names from a second-story window—it was our tramily! We hadn’t seen them in days. After a quick catch-up, we pushed on for the final mile into Olveiroa.

When we checked into our albergue, we realized we had accidentally booked a shared dorm room—something we hadn’t slept in for weeks! We claimed our bunk beds, stowed our gear in the lockers, and headed straight to the attached bar for a hearty meal and drinks.

Back in the dorm, Knockerz showered and fell asleep instantly. Although the cramped room grew warm and noisy as late arrivals filtered in, the room quieted down early, allowing us to get a surprisingly deep night’s rest.
