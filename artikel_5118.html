Bittensor (TAO) has gained attention as a decentralized protocol focused on artificial intelligence. The network enables participants to build, share, and evaluate machine learning models through a blockchain-based system.

The project combines AI infrastructure with token incentives. This model positions TAO crypto within a growing segment of crypto tied to AI development. TAO price outlook depends on both technical patterns and network activity.

The daily chart for TAO/USDT reveals a structural shift that began in March when the price staged a temporary recovery from $140 to the $375 level.

This momentum quickly faded going into April, leading to a sharp decline that forced the token down to test major support around $240.

A brief relief rally followed in May as buyers attempted to push the price back up, but the move was swiftly rejected at the $330 resistance mark. This failure allowed sellers to regain complete control throughout the rest of May, and by early June, the price reached $190 support, where it staged another rally that even led to breakout from falling wedge pattern’s upper border to hit $335 by late September.

However, bears overpowered the market again, and by November, TAO’s price was pushed back down and has broken support at $190. If this sell pressure continues then TAO price might crash further lower toward the $160.

But, if the TAO price shows bullish strength then with a daily close above $200 level could revive the bullish trend again.

On November 5, 2026, Trishool AI (@trishoolai) and the competitive miners of Bittensor Subnet 23 (SN23) achieved a new open-source AI safety milestone with the release of HaloGuard 2.0, setting a new State-of-the-Art (SOTA) benchmark for streaming guardrail models.

On September 4, (Subnet 28) Good Morning launched OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra, offering private, TEE-routed access. The deployment provides early access ahead of OpenRouter alongside an 8.3% discount via SayGm. Moreover, the (Subnet 80) OpenRoboto brought its xArm 6 workstation online for real-robot teleoperation and model validation. The robotics team plans to publish live demo validation results featuring the simulation track’s winning model soon. Also, the (Subnet 23) Trishool was accepted into OpenAI’s Trusted Access for Cyber program for defensive cybersecurity teams. The selection provides advanced frontier AI capabilities to accelerate the commercial expansion of its Halo security model.

On September 3, the (Subnet 44) Score expanded its automated fuel-station rollout beyond Avia by adding Shell and Eni locations. Initial deployments focus on franchisees, with plans to negotiate direct contracts with Shell and Eni after the rollout.

On September 1, the (Subnet 60) Bitsec launched a unified public website for Sentios, its continuous AI-powered code-auditing product. The site centralizes pricing models, coverage details, and real-time vulnerability detection capabilities in one place.

On August 31, the (Subnet 74) Gittensor released a Qwen3.8-27B checkpoint optimized for a single RTX 5090, crossing 500K downloads on Hugging Face. It represents the most downloaded model release from a Bittensor subnet, with a Gittensor API set to launch this week.

On August 4, the Opentensor Foundation officially launched the Root Reborn upgrade on the Bittensor mainnet. This upgrade fundamentally rewrites how TAO yield and subnet emissions are handled, turning validators into active “fund managers” across the ecosystem.

On July 21, crypto exchange MEXC partnered with Digital Currency Group’s subsidiary Yuma to launch native Bittensor (TAO) staking for 40 million global users. The integration abstracts away complex validator setups, letting retail traders earn network rewards directly through the exchange via Yuma’s institutional grade infrastructure.

On July 18, Bittensor released its v4.3.1 network upgrade, introducing a comprehensive overhaul of its developer ecosystem, SDK security primitives, and Rust-based tooling. The update fully launches the “conviction” stake-lock mechanism to live infrastructure, setting the stage for a decentralized network architecture autonomously run by AI models and owned by humans.

On July 9, the Opentensor foundation announced that “Conviction” is fully live for Bittensor subnets, introducing a native onchain mechanism that allows participants to prove long-term commitment by locking subnet alpha tokens to a specific hotkey. Under this new upgrade, permanent ownership monopolies are eliminated as any challenger can contest subnet ownership after 365 days by achieving the highest aggregate conviction score on the ledger.

On July 3, fiat-crypto payment gateway Alchemy Pay announced the strategic integration of Bittensor’s native token TAO into its global fiat on-ramp solution. This collaboration enables uses worldwide to purchase the decentralized AI-focused token directly using traditional local fiat payment methods, including Visa, Apple Pay, and regional mobile wallets.

On June 15, Grayscale openly endorsed the TAO token and the Bittensor ecosystem, highlighting the robustness of its decentralized network and positioning it as a strong alternative to centralized AI companies like Anthropic, which are more vulnerable to government intervention.

On June 12, 2026, the US government forced Anthropic to abruptly suspend global access to its Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models over national security and export control directives, cutting off both international users and internal foreign staff. In response, Bittensor highlighted on June 13th that this incident is a critical turning point, arguing that centralized state intervention underscores why the world urgently needs open, decentralized, and permissionless AI networks.

On April 10th, Covenant AI a major project on TAO announced they are leaving bittensor and reportedly sold 37,000 $TAO worth over $10 million.

On March 16th, Grayscale posted about its Bittensor Trust for private placement, offering eligible accredited investors direct exposure to the TAO ecosystem. This move underscores growing institutional interest in decentralized AI, as Grayscale highlights the protocol’s role in leveraging economic incentives for open-source development.

The weekly chart for Bittensor (TAO/USDT) reveals a well-defined long-term range that has governed price action since the network’s explosive growth in 2024. This structural parallel channel is anchored by a significant accumulation floor near $160–$220 and a formidable overhead supply ceiling around $720–$760.

In Early 2024 saw TAO price reached its All-Time High (ATH) of approximately $760. Despite a volatile year, the price repeatedly cycled between the channel’s borders, demonstrating high demand at the lower bounds and aggressive profit-taking at the upper extremes.

But throughout 2025, market momentum shifted into a lower-intensity regime. The price largely remained capped under the $500 psychological barrier. A brief Q4 2025 rally attempted to reclaim the upper range but was rejected at $535, leading to a sharp retracement back to the primary demand zone by early 2026.

As evident in H1 2026, the price has successfully defended the $160–$200 support zone again. The price rose but collapsed to $190 by H1-end.

As H2 began in July, the price of TAO trended higher, retesting the $350.00 mark by September. With the final quarter of the year now active, a continued surge in demand could propel the price toward $400.00 or even $480.00.

Conversely, if selling pressure intensifies, TAO could face a sharper decline. A failure to defend current levels would likely lead to a retest of the $240.00 support zone, resulting in a prolonged bleed through the remainder of H2, unless buyers step in to establish a stable base for a potential trend reversal.

The total supply is capped at 21 million tokens, similar to Bitcoin.

Bittensor is a decentralized AI network where users share and train models. TAO tokens reward valuable contributions and enable access to AI services.
