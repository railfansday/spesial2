NBC’s New Detective Series Is Set To Beat ‘Law & Order’ at Its Own Game

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Police procedurals can be so much fun to watch. There are always plenty of thrilling storylines and characters that you love to root for. Typically, cases are solved within the hour, with a few storylines carrying over throughout the season. This provides a predictable framework for a series that also always feels inherently entertaining. But the problem with procedurals is that the genre can feel a bit stale at times. NBC's Law & Order franchise is the perfect example. These shows don't generally offer anything new, and even though they might still remain on your watchlist, you could be searching for something a little more exciting. You might be in luck with an upcoming NBC detective series, based on a classic novel, that is sure to crack open the police procedural in the best way possible.

F. Scott Fitzgerald's The Great Gatsby is one of the most well-known American novels ever written. Taught in most high schools, the 1925 book tells the fictional story of a man named Nick Carraway and his involvement in the glitzy enclave of Long Island during the Jazz Age of the 1920s. Nick becomes entangled with the enigmatic, wealthy Jay Gatsby, who in turn is obsessed with his ex-girlfriend, Daisy Buchanan. With rich writing that explores themes of the American dream, class differences, and gender disparities, each page of The Great Gatsby sings with captivating storytelling. The narrative unfolds in a constantly fascinating way, making it no surprise that it still holds up over 100 years later. The Great Gatsby has been adapted for the screen many times, including a 1974 version starring Robert Redford and Mia Farrow, as well as a 2013 Baz Luhrmann film with Leonardo DiCaprio, Carey Mulligan, and Tobey Maguire.

A great detective is really just a personality with a method attached, which is why we keep remaking the same four of them. Here is yours, what it says about you, and the case for them being the best in the business.

You let people think you are not a threat and then you simply never go away. Columbo is the only major detective show that tells you who did it in the first ten minutes, because the pleasure was never the puzzle, it was watching a rich man slowly realise he is in trouble.

You are the most interesting person in the room and you know exactly how to use it. Rian Johnson built Blanc as a gentleman detective who is also an outsider everywhere he goes, which lets him be charming at people who would never otherwise talk to him.

Everybody tells you everything, and you have never once asked them to. Jessica Fletcher is a crime novelist from a small town in Maine who is so warm and so obviously trustworthy that suspects confess to her essentially as a social courtesy.

You expect to be lied to and you are almost never disappointed. Chandler wrote Marlowe as a man with a rigid private code in a city that has none, which is why he takes the case, solves it, gets nothing out of it and takes the next one.

In some exciting news, NBC just announced that they'll be bringing The Great Gatsby to television, with an intriguing twist. The series, titled Gatsby, will be set in present day New York, and will follow police investigator Jay Gatsby as he solves crimes in the Big Apple. His partner, in a surprising gender-swapping twist, is a woman named Nicole "Nicky" Carraway. The procedural will likely be a fast-paced, action-packed show, with nods sprinkled throughout to the original source material.

There are so many compelling elements to this new series. Because the novel entered the public domain back in 2021, the IP for these characters can be shifted around to form a brand-new concept by gender-swapping roles and time period switch-ups. This allows the framework of the novel to still exist, but for anyone who didn't enjoy the book in their high school English class, the series offers viewers something altogether new. The gender swap also provides a unique twist on many other police procedurals with male characters as the focus. The change in timeframe allows the series to feel current, especially because an older setting might have deterred some viewers who like their police procedurals to be modern.

All of these alterations to the original source material suggest that Gatsby will be able to stand on its own as a detective series. It doesn't seem like you'll need any Great Gatsby references to enjoy the new series, which means that the show will be accessible to all audiences. Gatsby also already has a strong pedigree with its creative team. Creator Jake Coburn helmed NBC's recent series, The Hunting Party, which offered another twist on the police procedural genre, proving he has the chops to take on this show. And co-creator Jason Tracey is the executive producer behind R.J. Decker and Elementary (which also functioned as an adaptation of a beloved classic with gender-swapped characters from the Sherlock Holmes universe). With these brilliant writers, Gatsby will likely be able to combine the thrilling world of New York City detectives with an inventive take on the characters from the classic novel.

There is no cast or production info yet for Gatsby, but if its background is any indication, the series seems poised to be an exciting entry into the world of police procedurals. If the show is successful, it could even inspire other classic novels that are well-known to audiences to be revamped for television.
