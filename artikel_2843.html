The Wild Officially Outlasted an Unlucky Start and Excelled

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Minnesota Wild have experienced numerous injuries, snakebitten players, and mass media speculation about a delayed contract and an inchoate trade.

Nevertheless, they flourished on the ice and sat near the top of the standings in points and almost every statistic through it all.

Minnesota's defense seemed to have taken the brunt of these injuries: defenseman Brock Faber (34-95–129 and a plus-21 throughout his 245 career games) and defenseman and captain Jared Spurgeon (124-322–446 and a plus-139 throughout his 1,027 career games) have both suffered upper-body injuries that kept them from playing for at least nine games.

Additionally, goalie Filip Gustavsson (.911 SV% and 2.67 GAA in 219 games played) has not played a game for the Wild this season after undergoing off-season hip surgery.

Still, the Wild were undoubtedly strong defensively without them. They remained near the top of the league in goals against (GA), team save percentage, shots on goal (SOG) allowed per game, and GA/GP even though the injuries kept piling up.

Fortunately, Faber and Spurgeon have recently made their returns, and it looks like they haven't missed a beat. Additionally, Gustavsson is making his season debut for the Wild's AHL affiliate (the Iowa Wild) soon.

Unfortunately, in Thursday night's game in Calgary, Quinn Hughes took a hit from the Flames' Adam Klapka in the third period and did not return to the game.

One step forward, two steps back, a phrase the Wild are certainly familiar with regarding their injury log. There has been no update on Hughes at this time.

Hughes easily skated off the ice on his own. So, assuming he misses little to no time, the Wild are expected to become even more tight and rigid on the blue line with each of their top four defensemen on the ice. This would be a spectacular feat considering their stinginess when only two or three of these players were on the ice.

Before Matt Boldy's recent five goals in three games, he suffered a snakebitten start to the season. Boldy is coming off a 42-goal season, so his lack of production was certainly startling.

This wasn't for lack of effort, though. Boldy has consistently held a position as one of the top shooters in the league this year. He even led the league in shots on goal in the midst of his scoring drought.

Now, after his hot streak, he is back up to a shot percentage of around 13.4%. Previously, he was stuck around 8%. Moreover, he is now tied for seventh in goals. During his period of bad puck luck, he was outside of the NHL's top-25 scorers.

Even through his rough start, Boldy was a massive part of this team's success in other ways. These include increasing his average time on ice, keeping the puck in the offensive zone (94th percentile offensive zone time – 47.3%), and being a staple on Minnesota's penalty kill.

So, while his assistance to each of the Wild's early wins didn't always show up on the score sheet, the advanced analytics prove his worth to this team.

Luckily, this team had a leading goal-scorer to aid Boldy's slow start. Kirill Kaprizov has only played two games without a point (24 games played). Additionally, he sits near the top of the league in goals scored. And, in contrast to his linemate Matt Boldy, Kaprizov has consistently held an elite shot percentage far above 20% for the whole season.

Despite Kaprizov's early-season excellence, he too has left a gap in the Wild's well-roundedness. Known for his slick moves in three-on-three overtime, this year has been different. Minnesota lost its first four overtime games this season (no shootouts).

In fact, Kaprizov had just two SOG in overtime through these four games (both on November 7 against the Tampa Bay Lightning).

However, in their most recent overtime period against the Montreal Canadiens, Kaprizov scored on a four-on-three after Cole Caufield took a slashing penalty late in the third period. Still, the Wild walked away with two points and their first overtime win.

So, even though Kaprizov (and his linemates Boldy and Joel Eriksson Ek) often steals the show during five-on-five play, there's still work to be done in the occasional three-on-three. Still, Kaprizov's signature winner in the extra frame is a good sign that he's finding his overtime groove again.

Minnesota is hearing its name more than ever in the media this season. Furthermore, just because they're a top team doesn't mean all the speculation and coverage is positive.

Quinn Hughes (acquired in December 2025 from the Vancouver Canucks) has not yet signed a deal to stay in Minnesota. Dylan Larkin refuses to play for the Detroit Red Wings and has been pushing to be traded since the offseason. Minnesota is said to be one of his desired teams.

Also, Minnesota has been crafting massive packages (that include roster players and picks/prospects) in hopes of landing Larkin.

Still, nothing has been completed in Minnesota with Hughes or Larkin, who have played together on Team USA. Minnesota media is full of speculation surrounding Larkin.

However, there is a lack of speculation surrounding a commitment from Hughes. Rather, the media is full of possible scenarios of why he will or will not sign and the anxiety that comes with that.

But this doesn't seem to be affecting the team. The active players seem to be clicking, the injured are being activated off of (LT)IR, and the locker room doesn't have any apparent issues.

Hughes stated that it seems like the Wild's top line knows each other's thoughts on the ice. This is what makes playing with them so fun, seamless, and successful. So, until something happens, the media will likely speculate. However, the players know how to handle it, as proven in their 17-3-4 run.

The Minnesota Wild are far from a perfectly well-rounded team, especially given these early circumstances. Still, even through their unluckiness, lack of polish, and surrounding rumors, they proved that their clear-headedness and tight-knit locker room trump all "distractions."

Now, with many of these issues resolved, the Wild could become an even more competitive team. A scary thought for the rest of the Western Conference.
