Riq Woolen ‘annoyed' by benching but happy for an Eagles win

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Riq Woolen 'annoyed' by benching but happy for an Eagles win originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

The veteran cornerback was pulled out of the game in the second quarter of the Eagles' 19-16 win over the Steelers in the middle of a drive. He then sat for most of the second half until injuries thrust him back into action.

"It may be frustrating at times, it may be annoying at times, but at the same time, I still got to be a great player," Woolen said.

"I'm happy we won today because that's the overall objective when we go out there is to win. So overall, I'm annoyed, but I'm still happy we won. Wins are hard in this league and we ain't won since when? I don't know, Week 3 of September? So I'm happy but annoyed. But at the end of the day, we won and that's what everybody in here wants to see."

The 27-year-old Woolen, who signed a one-year deal with the Eagles in March, has had plenty of ups and downs this season. That continued in the first half against the Steelers.

During that second quarter drive, Woolen missed two tackles and was pulled from the game in favor of veteran Jonathan Jones. Woolen said aside from those missed tackles his felt like his game was "smooth." A few plays later, Jones had a huge pass breakup on third down to force a field goal.

Woolen said he went into Sunday's game knowing there was a chance he could be taken out in favor of Jones because Woolen had previously had a "couple mental lapses," but expected it to last for just a series or two. On Sunday, it didn't look like the Eagles were planning on going back to Woolen at all.

Late in the second half, Quinyon Mitchell and Jones suffered injuries. So Woolen and Kelee Ringo had to take over.

"It was just hard," Woolen said. "I didn't play the whole second half. To go in the last three, four minutes, it's tough. The DB position is a mental position more than physicality. So to be able to check back in mentally when you've been out so long, it's hard. But I got to be there for my guys no matter what. No matter how I'm feeling. It's a team sport. No matter if I'm getting my way or not, I still gotta go play ball for my guys."

Woolen's teammates appreciated that he stayed focused and engaged even after he was pulled from the game.

"A hundred percent," Cooper DeJean said. "He's a heck of a player. Just to have the mental ability to go out and then be able to step back in when someone goes down, you really appreciate that. S—, he almost made a turnover for us. As a teammate, you definitely appreciate that."

Shortly after Woolen got back into the game, he was tested by a deep ball from Aaron Rodgers to Roman Wilson down the right sideline.

Woolen held up in coverage and the pass fell incomplete.

"It was good to see him respond there and then go out there and cover his guys in those situations," head coach Nick Sirianni said. "I know that's always tough when you come out of a football game, especially there at corner. But I've got a lot of faith in him and the things that he can do. We're going to need him moving forward."

The Eagles are probably going to need Woolen on Thanksgiving against the Cowboys. Mitchell suffered a hamstring injury on Sunday and Jones was seen after the game with a walking boot on his right foot.

So Woolen is likely going to go from benched to starting against a Cowboys offense that scored 51 points on Sunday.

Before the trade deadline, Woolen's name seemed to come up quite a bit but the Eagles didn't move him. Woolen on Sunday confirmed there were teams that called about him, but he was happy to stay in Philly.

Woolen signed a one-year, $12 million deal with the Eagles this offseason. It was viewed as a prove-it deal that could lead to a long-term contract going into 2027. But after 10 games, it's hard to determine what kind of value Woolen will have on the open market.

He can boost that market with a strong finish in 2026. Because despite getting taken out of Sunday's game, he's likely going to get more opportunities the rest of the way.

"Whenever you strive for greatness, everything ain't going to be easy," Woolen said. "You're going to have hardships. And I strive for greatness every day, even if I may fall short a couple times, I'm still going to keep going for greatness. That's how I'm able to continue to play through all the adversity."
