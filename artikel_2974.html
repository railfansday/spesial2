If a Recession Hits in 2027, This Is the 1 ETF I Want in My Portfolio

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

While I can't predict whether there will be a recession in 2027, I do know that I want protection.

By Dana George – Nov 24, 2026 at 3:15PM EST

Unlike a bull or bear market, there's not always a clear sign that a recession has arrived. There may be subtle signs -- like slowed GDP growth or high unemployment -- but there's no single factor that sets off recession alarm bells.

In fact, a recession may be officially declared many months after it's already begun, or even after it's ended. And frankly, that bothers me.

As someone who prefers to plan ahead, I find it maddening that I can't always spot a recession before it hits my investments. Fortunately, I'm in the middle of rebalancing my portfolio, and as part of that process, I've been looking for an ETF that can serve as a hedge against the next recession -- whenever that may be.

I do believe I've found one that will work.

I have no way of knowing if a recession will hit in 2027, or how markets will respond if one does. However, I have decided that the one ETF to help steady my portfolio through the next economic storm should be the Vanguard Total Bond Market ETF (BND +0.00%).

Buying BND helps balance out some of the stocks in my portfolio by providing broad exposure to investment-grade U.S. bonds. I know it's not recession-proof or a replacement for cash, but it may help soften the ups and downs -- which matters to someone like me who worries about market swings.

I've come to view bond funds as a risk reduction strategy. In a recession, investors tend to be more cautious about company earnings and stock prices, and many will sell, hoping to avoid losses.  Rather than tying investors' money to a single company or industry, BND spreads the risk across many issuers.

Bonds don't always move in the same direction as stocks. If a recession brings lower interest rates, existing bonds with higher yields may become more valuable. That's not a sure thing, but it's one reason I'm drawn to BND.

There's certainly more to protecting a portfolio than balancing or diversifying. It's also important to keep investment costs low and match risk to when I'll need the money.

But in times like these, when I'm not sure when the next recession will occur, I feel good about a fund that can provide steady dividends now and help protect my portfolio when the market becomes volatile.
