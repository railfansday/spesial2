5 things we learned from Ohio State football's loss to Oregon

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Here are five things we learned from No. 12 Ohio State's 41-28 loss to No. 23 Oregon on Nov. 7:

The bubble likely burst for the Buckeyes.

The unraveling against the Ducks leaves Ohio State with a remote chance of reaching the College Football Playoff.

No one with three regular-season losses has ever made the playoff, and the Buckeyes were already out of the field when the selection committee revealed its first set of rankings earlier in the week.

The Buckeyes are not mathematically eliminated from making the Big Ten championship game. With two conference losses, they could get help over the last three weeks of the regular season in order to reach Indianapolis and vie for the league's automatic bid.

But the nature of their second straight loss, one in which they surrendered a double-digit lead and allowed 38 consecutive points, makes the prospect of an at-large berth rather improbable.

The Buckeyes have a range of issues that extend beyond the loss of starting quarterback Julian Sayin to injury, and it's likely they will plunge to the bottom of the committee's next rankings if not fall out entirely.

Unless they snap back over the coming weeks and things break their way at the top of the Big Ten standings, the Buckeyes look like they're poised to become the second straight preseason No. 1 team to miss the playoff entirely.

Ohio State's defense needed to step up following the loss of Sayin.

It's instead fallen apart, bearing significant responsibility for two straight loss losses to Oregon and USC.

The Buckeyes have allowed 71 points in a span of two weeks, surrendering at least 30 points in consecutive games during the regular season for the first time since 2021.

They were again vulnerable to big plays. After not giving up a play from scrimmage of 40 or more yards through seven games, they have allowed three in two weeks.

Running back Dierre Hill Jr. broke loose for a 48-yard touchdown run in the third quarter after he dodged an attempt at a shoestring tackle by linebacker Christian Alliegro and outran other defensive backs.

The Buckeyes allowed the Ducks to convert four times on third down when they needed at least 10 yards to move the chains.

The pass rush did little to impact quarterback Dylan Raiola, who was never sacked and hurried only twice.

There were missed opportunities for game-changing plays as well. Nickel back Jay Timmons dropped a potential pick-6 in the first quarter, and safety Jaylen McClain couldn't corral an interception in the second quarter.

It's been a sudden drop-off for a defense that had allowed just 9.3 points per game last season, the best in the Football Bowl Subdivision, as well as 13.4 points per game to start this year.

The absence of Sayin has made it more difficult for the Buckeyes to lean on superstar wide receiver Jeremiah Smith.

While Smith finished with seven receptions for 133 yards and one touchdown, he was a non-factor in the second and third quarters when the Buckeyes lost control of the game.

Freshman quarterback Luke Fahey never targeted Smith during the second quarter. It wasn't until the last play of the third quarter that Fahey connected with Smith, but the completion gained no yards.

Sayin was not without flaws before he went down at Indiana three weeks ago, but there was little doubt that the returning Heisman Trophy finalist could feed Smith with a variety of downfield passes.

It was an element that stressed defenses to a much greater degree and papered over the cracks of a roster that misses much of the talent that carried the Buckeyes to an unbeaten regular season a year ago.

Along with losing Sayin, the Buckeyes have also been stripped of the full effect of their dominant pass catcher.

Issues resurfaced on special teams a week after the Buckeyes committed three penalties on punt coverage and returns at USC.

The Buckeyes were caught off guard by Oregon's fake punt late in the second quarter.

When the Ducks faced a fourth down at their 31-yard line, they hiked the ball to Teitum Tuioti, who was lined up as one of the upbacks.

Tuioti ran about 3 yards as if he was prepared to barrel through the line of scrimmage before lobbing a pass to Matayo Uiagalelei, a fellow edge rusher who slipped downfield and was left uncovered along the sideline.

The pass to Uiagalelei gained 30 yards and marked the Ducks' longest play from scrimmage at that point.

Not only did it position the Ducks in Ohio State territory, but it also swung momentum in their favor. Oregon ended the drive in the end zone, the first of two touchdowns within the last 1:25 before halftime to erase the 14-0 lead the Buckeyes had built in the first quarter.

Ohio State's punt return unit never saw it coming.

There's been a familiar feeling in each of the Buckeyes' three losses.

When the Buckeyes stumbled at Texas in September, they saw a 20-point lead vanish in the fourth quarter of the 24-23 loss.

They fell behind 14-3 at USC last week. Then there was this week against Oregon when they came undone late in the second quarter and failed to stop the bleeding into the third quarter.

Following the fake punt, the Ducks would score a touchdown on four out of five drives as part of a stretch of 38 unanswered points.

"I'm not going to sit here and point fingers at one group of guys that are coming up short," Buckeyes coach Ryan Day said, "because we're all coming up short."

Neither side of the ball has been able to wrest control in these situations, a troubling trend for a team relying on so many veterans.

It's ultimately one of the biggest reasons the Buckeyes are heading toward their worst regular season in 15 years.
