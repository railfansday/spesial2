Derek DelGaudio and Frank Oz Reunite for ‘Unknown Caller’ at New York’s Public Theater (EXCLUSIVE)

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Performance artist Derek DelGaudio and director Frank Oz are reteaming for “Unknown Caller,” a new stage production that debuts next spring at New York City’s Public Theater. The two had collaborated on DelGaudio’s unconventional one-man show “In & Of Itself,” also directed by Oz, which explored themes of identity and illusion.

Veiled in secrecy, “Unknown Caller” is described as a “new encounter with mystery that asks what we can believe in a counterfeit world” and promises to “push the boundaries of storytelling and live performance.” It will world premiere at the Public’s LuEster Hall on March 30. “We are becoming more skeptical of what we see and hear,” DelGaudio said. “Understandably so, every day we are bombarded by new forgeries and falsehoods. But I have been thinking about what that skepticism costs us. What happens when our first response to something extraordinary is suspicion rather than wonder? That’s part of what led me to create ‘Unknown Caller.'”

Oz says working with DelGaudio is like “jumping off a cliff together into the creative air of uncertainty.”

“We’re grateful to have Oskar [Eustis, artistic director] and the wonderful people at The Public support our odd process in making Unknown Caller,'” Oz said.

“In & Of Itself” ran for more than 500 performances at the Manhattan’s Daryl Roth Theatre from 2017 to 2018 before a taped version streamed on Disney+ and Hulu. DelGaudio also created the two-hander “Nothing to Hide,” and he appeared in Steven Soderbergh’s thriller “Kimi,” starring Zoe Kravitz. Oz is best known for directing movies including “In & Out,” “Dirty Rotten Scoundrels,” and “Death at a Funeral” and voicing iconic characters on “Star Wars,” “Muppets” and “Sesame Street.”

“Derek DelGaudio is a magician of the soul. His performances bring us face to face not with illusions, which are just a vehicle, but with mysteries: the unanswerable questions at the heart of our lives,” said the Public’s artistic director Oskar Eustis. “Like his director and collaborator Frank Oz, he’s transcended any genre and become, simply, one of our most visionary artists.”
