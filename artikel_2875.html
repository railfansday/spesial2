Connor Zilisch Addresses Rumors of Early Hendrick Motorsports Move

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Connor Zilisch isn't hiding his interest in driving for Hendrick Motorsports. He just isn't planning to get there ahead of schedule.

The 20-year-old NASCAR driver addressed growing speculation about his future Friday at Homestead-Miami Speedway, where he confirmed that he intends to remain with Trackhouse Racing through the 2027 season before making his anticipated move to Hendrick in 2028.

And while Zilisch acknowledged that an earlier opportunity would be appealing, he offered a compelling reason for staying put: He doesn't believe he's finished learning at Trackhouse.

Zilisch's future has become a popular topic in the NASCAR garage since plans emerged for him to join Hendrick Motorsports in 2028.

The biggest question has been whether that timeline could change, particularly with Alex Bowman's future in the No. 48 Chevrolet drawing attention.

During Friday's media availability, Zilisch was asked directly whether there had been discussions about moving to Hendrick or a Hendrick-affiliated team as early as 2027.

"Yeah, it's been a lot of noise," Zilisch said. "Alex [Bowman] has a commitment to run one more year. I have sponsors to fulfill obligations for another season. I'm excited about that."

He acknowledged that joining Hendrick sooner would be an attractive opportunity, but suggested that remaining at Trackhouse could ultimately serve him better.

"I think it'd be great to go race for Hendrick Motorsports next year and get that opportunity quicker, but at the same time, I'm happy in the spot I'm in," Zilisch said.

His explanation went beyond contractual obligations.

Zilisch believes another season with Trackhouse will allow him to continue developing without the additional scrutiny that comes with driving for one of NASCAR's most successful organizations.

"I get to learn in a lower-pressure situation with less eyeballs on me," he explained. "I feel like I can just go out and focus on myself."

It's a notable admission from a driver whose rapid rise through NASCAR has already brought considerable attention and expectations.

For Zilisch, the decision isn't simply about waiting for an opening at Hendrick. It's also about how he wants to leave Trackhouse.

His rookie Cup Series campaign hasn't delivered everything he hoped, and he made clear that walking away after a disappointing season isn't how he wants to end his time with the organization.

"I would not want to leave Trackhouse after a season like this," Zilisch said. "I want to end on a good note."

That perspective adds another dimension to the speculation surrounding his future.

Rather than treating 2027 as a season to get through before joining Hendrick, Zilisch sees it as an opportunity to build on his rookie experience and help Trackhouse make meaningful progress.

He also recognizes that moving to Hendrick would bring a different kind of pressure, with heightened expectations every weekend.

For now, he believes there's value in having another year to prepare for that responsibility.

"Hopefully next year can go better; we can take something away from it and have a good last season together," Zilisch said.

The destination may already be decided, but Zilisch isn't interested in rushing the journey.

And for anyone expecting an early move to Hendrick Motorsports, his message from Homestead was clear: He still has unfinished business at Trackhouse.
