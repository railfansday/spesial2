Lakers biggest weakness exposed despite overtime win against Hornets

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Los Angeles Lakers have the best record in basketball, the NBA's best offensive player in Luka Doncic, and a perfect sidekick in Austin Reaves.

They also have a problem big enough to swallow their championship hopes whole.

They don't have a reliable backup center.

For three and a half quarters Tuesday night, the Charlotte Hornets exposed that glaring weakness, turning what should have been another routine victory for the league-leading Lakers into a desperate fight for survival.

Quentin Grimes eventually rescued Los Angeles with a career-high 47 points and 10 three-pointers, joining Kobe Bryant as the only Lakers to accomplish the latter feat, in a thrilling 118-114 overtime victory.

Quentin Grimes of the Los Angeles Lakers reacts after making a 3-point shot against the Charlotte Hornets, Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026, in Los Angeles. Getty Images

But beneath the celebration was an uncomfortable truth that general manager Rob Pelinka can no longer ignore.

The Lakers were in need of another big man as Kevon Looney's role diminishes. NBAE via Getty Images

Kevon Looney isn't the answer. And Tuesday night might have been his last opportunity to prove otherwise.

With starting center Walker Kessler sidelined by lower-leg soreness, Looney was handed something he hadn't received in weeks: a legitimate opportunity.

He started the game, played 17 minutes and delivered four points, eight rebounds, zero assists, zero steals and zero blocks.

His minus-7 plus-minus was the worst on the team.

For a veteran brought to Los Angeles specifically to provide dependable depth behind Kessler, it was a miserable audition.

Looney has always been a terrific rebounder, a physical presence who carved out a successful career doing the dirty work that rarely appears in highlight reels.

But the Lakers don't need someone who can simply collect rebounds. They need someone who can protect the rim, defend and also run pick-and-rolls and prevent opposing players from treating the painted area like a game of pop-a-shot.

Looney couldn't provide any of that Tuesday.

And the most damning indictment came from Lakers coach JJ Redick, who effectively decided he'd rather trust an inexperienced rookie than continue watching his veteran center struggle.

Two-way rookie Carson Cooper played more minutes than Looney, including the majority of the fourth quarter and overtime as Los Angeles mounted its comeback.

Cooper finished plus-6. Looney finished minus-7.

That's a 13-point swing in plus-minus between a rookie fighting for his NBA future and a veteran who was supposed to provide championship-caliber insurance.

"Carson was part of that group that played well in the fourth. We trusted him to go out there to start overtime," said Redick. "I think he understands the assignment. He's here to play a role. And he did that tonight for us."

Quentin Grimes, left, and Carson Cooper of the Los Angeles Lakers high-five during a game against the Charlotte Hornets in Los Angeles on Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

Despite Doncic averaging close to 40 points per game, and in a tight two-man race for the NBA MVP, according to DraftKings Sportsbook, the Lakers' most important player is Kessler.

The Lakers didn't hand Kessler a four-year, $130 million contract simply because he could catch lobs from Doncic. They paid him because he changes the geometry of the basketball court.

With Kessler protecting the paint, opposing players think twice before attacking the basket. Without him, the Lakers' interior defense looks about as sturdy as a screen door.

Just look at the numbers. The Lakers have a 112.8 defensive rating when Kessler is on the floor. When he sits, that number balloons to 118.1.

That's a difference of 5.3 points allowed per 100 possessions, a massive defensive drop-off for a team with legitimate championship ambitions.

Lakers center Walker Kessler (14) has once again been one of the league's best rim protectors. AP Photo/Bethany Baker

Rather than play Looney, the Lakers have leaned into their smaller lineups through the first 15 games of the season because frankly, they don't have any other choice.

Sandro Mamukelashvili offers valuable offensive versatility, while Jarred Vanderbilt provides energy and defensive effort. Neither is a true replacement for a 7-foot-2 shot-blocker. And as Looney demonstrated on Tuesday night, he isn't one either.

That's why Pelinka has until the Feb. 11 NBA trade deadline to find a solution, although waiting that long could be a dangerous gamble.

Potential options include Milwaukee's Jericho Sims, former Laker Jaxson Hayes or even Clippers veteran Brook Lopez, whose rim protection and outside shooting would provide a particularly intriguing fit.

The Jazz dominated the Lakers on the glass on Sunday. NBAE via Getty Images

But the Lakers' limited trade assets complicate matters, and none of those players is guaranteed to be available at an acceptable price.

Tuesday's victory was spectacular. Grimes deserved every ounce of the celebration that followed. Yet the biggest takeaway wasn't what the Lakers accomplished in overtime.

It was what the first three quarters revealed about the roster Pelinka has assembled.
