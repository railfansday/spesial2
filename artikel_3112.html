Because the disease and the mechanism may be new to many of you, I want to spend a moment on each. Hypophosphatasia or HPP is a rare genetic condition caused by mutation in the ALPL gene that codes for the enzyme tissue nonspecific alkaline phosphatase.

When this enzyme is deficient, inorganic pyrophosphate, or PPI, accumulates upstream and acts as a direct inhibitor of bone mineralization leading to fractures and exerting both direct and indirect effects on muscle leading to weakness. PPI reduction is known to track with clinical benefits on bone health and mobility in patients. BMN-440 is a direct inhibitor of the ENPP 1 enzyme, directly upstream of PPI and thus can be thought of as a substrate reduction therapy for HPP. Preclinical data support this approach. At ASBMR in October, data from a mouse model of later-onset HPP showed that oral treatment with BMN-440 reduced PPI, and normalized the phosphate to PPI ratio. It also improved bone mineralization muscle strength, and mobility measures.

The promise of this approach is to deliver an oral alternative to ERT therapy for pediatric and adult onset HPP with the added potential to better address systemic symptoms that bone selective ERTs cannot. This is especially important because adult onset patients are often affected by muscle weakness, pain, and fatigue more so than bone problems. At ASBMR in October, a study with adult HPP patients demonstrated that the burden of this condition in adults extends beyond the skeleton to physical functioning, fatigue, sleep, and pain. There are no approved therapies for adult onset HPP in the US or Europe. BMN-440 is now in a Phase 1/2a study in healthy volunteers and adults with HPP.

We expect data in 2027 that will characterize pharmacokinetics pharmacodynamics, and safety, establishing an initial human proof of mechanism and informing dose selection and design for our next study.
