Russell: I found Verstappen comments amusing

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

George Russell's long-standing feud with Max Verstappen reignited at the Las Vegas GP after the pair collided in final practice; watch the Qatar GP on Sky Sports F1 this weekend, with qualifying at 6pm on Saturday and the race at 4pm on Sunday

George Russell says he was "amused" by Max Verstappen’s criticism of him after their feud reignited at the Las Vegas Grand Prix but admits it was "avoidable".

After colliding on track in final practice last weekend, Russell publicly criticised Verstappen following the incident. The Dutchman won the race after an intense battle between them and said afterwards that the Mercedes driver is "far below my level".

The pair have clashed several times on track in the past, most notably engaging in a remarkable war of words following an incident during qualifying at the 2024 Qatar Grand Prix where Verstappen felt Russell had pushed unreasonably hard for him to be penalised.

Speaking on Thursday - back at the Lusail International Circuit almost exactly two years on from that incident - ahead of this weekend's Qatar Grand Prix, Russell said: "At the end of the day, I guess I stand by what I said after practice.

"But he was the one who went out and won the race. I didn't and I'm sure he had a bit of a laugh about that, and that was reasonably amusing. From my side, just a new race weekend, try and focus on the driving and move on."

Russell was pressed on the saga throughout the FIA press conference and revealed he was perhaps "guilty of being just too open and honest when I speak with you guys [the media]".

"It's all avoidable at the end of the day, and it's all a bit unnecessary. It's not something I'm looking to continue. I'm not looking to have poor relationships with people. That's just not really the type of person I am," he said.

"There are lessons just to be taken away from these situations. Maybe I am just too open and honest with the media because I feel that I'm having more of an open conversation with you guys as opposed to remembering there's a camera.

"There are more people in this room than there are cameras, and I feel like I'm talking to you, but I'm not actually talking to you. I need to remind myself that I'm talking to the camera there that's broadcasting to millions of people. That's the biggest takeaway."

Verstappen did not want to make any further comments about his Russell feud during his media sessions at the Lusail International Circuit.

"Everything else that has been said off track has been said. There's nothing else to add," he said.

Russell had been battling with Verstappen for the lead during most of the Las Vegas Grand Prix as a combination of a big slipstream effect and the new 2026 power units created a peloton-style race.

However, the Mercedes driver was third with three laps to go behind Verstappen and team-mate Kimi Antonelli, so went for a move on the latter at Turn 5 but crashed and eventually finished ninth.

Verstappen, who is likely to finish second in the Drivers' Championship this season, said it was "fantastic" when he saw Russell in the wall.

Russell said: "I don't think it's important to have a good relationship or no relationship in that regard to be honest. We're all fighting for race wins. My mistake in Vegas came when I was trying to overtake my team-mate.

"The truth is, my risk dial is probably a bit too great at the moment, because I'm just kind of wanting to go for these big results and get some highlights on the board towards the end of the season.

"If that was race one of the season, there's no way I would have gone for that move with two laps remaining. But two races to go in the season, I kind of wanted to go for it.

"I think the majority of us respect one another out there, and we have hard and good battles. and even the race with Max until that point was good and fair. It was very strategic."

Russell reiterated that he respects Verstappen "immensely for his driving talent and everything he's achieved on track" and feels he does not have to be friends with the four-time world champion.

"At the end of the day, we're all competitors out there. I think there's no right or wrong way about the mentality that everybody has, and I think we're all different in many regards, and you don't need to agree with one another," he said.

"I'm not looking to be best mates with anybody on the grid. I'm just working and looking and fighting to win. Simple as that."

McLaren's Lando Norris, who has fought Verstappen on track several times in recent years, agrees with Russell.

"You don't have to get on. Do you get on with everyone?" he said.

"It's just normal. It happens in life; not everyone agrees with everything. It's also a sport where you want to prove that you're better than everyone else.

"You can either do your talking on the track or you can do your talking in front of a camera. It normally always helps when you can back it up on track, that's what everyone, at the end of the day, really cares about, but I still respect both drivers a lot.

"Obviously, Max has proved to everyone [what] he's capable of and George is kind of still I think yet to prove what he's capable of. I still think he can achieve a lot in his Formula 1 career and look forward to seeing that. He's obviously had a tough year but I still believe he can have a good season and I look forward to more times when he's giving a good fight to Max."

Verstappen's former Red Bull team-mate Pierre Gasly half-joked he is "sitting and eating my popcorn and laughing" about the Verstappen-Russell spat.

"I think it was best with Max just to tell him right up in his face! He's someone that just takes anything," said the Alpine driver.

"You can have opinions, etcetera. I like both of them, obviously [they are] very different. We all know each other, we know each other since we were kids. It's not the first time, it's not the last time, these two are going to go at it."
