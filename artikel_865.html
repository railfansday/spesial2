Sarah Michelle Gellar Poses With Daughter Charlotte, 17, in Sweet Pic

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Sarah Michelle Gellar declared her and Freddie Prinze Jr.'s daughter Charlotte, 17, her “built in bestie” as they enjoyed a sweet mother-daughter outing.

All Sarah Michelle Gellar’s children are simply mini-mes.

One week after the All My Children actress shared a photo proving her and Freddie Prinze Jr.'s 14-year-old son Rocky was his dad’s twin, it’s clear the apple doesn’t fall too far from the tree for their 17-year-old daughter Charlotte either.

After all, the Buffy the Vampire Slayer alum shared a sweet snap with her first-born on Instagram Stories Nov. 2 of the pair sporting matching sunglasses and striking the same pose. The two enjoyed a mother-daughter outing together at the Los Angeles Rams vs. Los Angeles Chargers game.

As Gellar wrote over the pic, “My built in bestie @charlotteprinze.”

And just like her mama, the camera seems to love the teen as Gellar shared a pic of her little girl and a friend on the Jumbotron, writing, “I spy @charlotteprinze @cathykelley on the infinity screen."

Just one week earlier, the 49-year-old shocked fans when she shared a photo of her youngest child looking like the spitting image of Prinze Jr.

Indeed, Gellar and her son were all smiles as he posed in his racing uniform and a buzzcut, with the Ready or Not 2 star captioning the Oct. 26 carousel, "Race weekend with my boys."

Previously, the Scooby-Doo actress shared deeper insight into what she and her Mystery Gang get up to for family time.

"Dinners for me are really important," Gellar told E! News in 2024. "We're a food family, food friends, we love to cook."

"If we're all at home eating dinner together, there are no phones," she said. "It's a moment of connection."

"We play board games," she continued. "I puzzle-overloaded during Covid so I had to take a little break from puzzles, but we do a lot of board games and card games."

And when Gellar isn’t busy slaying family memories, she’s guiding her teens through setting boundaries on social media.

"We have all of the screen time limits on their phones, so they get whatever amount of time per day that we deem acceptable," Gellar explained. "And then it shuts off, and it's up to us to decide and say, ‘Hey, you've really been on it a lot. Go outside and go shoot some hoops or go see your friends across the streets.'"

As she put it, "The goal is that they will learn to self-regulate because they'll be used to thinking, ‘Okay, it's time to take a break.'"
