During the same period, Bitcoin’s price dropped approximately 24.5%, falling from $76,700 to around $57,900. Despite the reduction, these key stakeholder wallets still hold approximately 13.58 million BTC, accounting for 67.55% of the circulating supply.

This concentration makes their activity particularly important. Continued distribution from these holders could increase available supply in the market and limit the strength of any recovery. Conversely, stabilization in their balances followed by renewed accumulation would provide a stronger indication that selling pressure is easing.

Santiment’s data suggest that the next meaningful recovery may depend partly on whether these large-holder balances stop declining.

Bitcoin is trading near $60,100, following a steep decline that pushed the price below several previously established support levels. The latest rebound has helped BTC recover from its recent lows, but the broader technical structure remains weak.

The daily chart identifies the $64,000–$66,000 region as the immediate resistance zone. This area previously acted as a consolidation range before the latest breakdown. A sustained move above it could improve the short-term structure and open the way towards the $68,000–$70,000 region.

However, Bitcoin continues to trade below its declining moving averages, with the chart showing additional resistance around $71,400, $73,500 and $74,500. These levels could become important barriers if buyers regain control.

On the downside, $56,000 remains the major support area. Bitcoin’s ability to defend this region will be important for preventing another deeper correction. A decisive daily breakdown below it could expose the market to further selling pressure.

Whale distribution is not the only concern facing Bitcoin. Institutional flows have also remained weak, with US spot Bitcoin ETFs recording six consecutive sessions of net outflows through November 24.

The six-session withdrawal streak contributed to approximately $4.94 billion in net outflows during November through November 24, according to reported Farside data. The persistent withdrawals suggest that institutional demand has yet to provide a meaningful counterweight to selling pressure.

Bitcoin’s broader market structure has also deteriorated. Its recent decline pushed more than 11.6 million BTC into unrealized losses, a record level, while the supply in profit fell to 8.5 million BTC, below the previous low recorded during the November 2022 FTX collapse.

This combination of whale distribution, ETF withdrawals and elevated unrealized losses makes a sustained recovery more difficult. Buyers need to demonstrate stronger demand rather than rely on short-term rebounds following sharp declines.

Bitcoin’s next move will depend on whether large-holder selling begins to stabilize and buyers can reclaim the $64,000–$66,000 resistance zone. A sustained move above this region would improve the recovery outlook and potentially bring higher resistance levels into focus. However, continued whale distribution and weak ETF flows could keep the rebound vulnerable.

For now, the $56,000 support area remains critical. If it holds, Bitcoin could continue consolidating while the market searches for stronger demand. A breakdown below it would weaken the recovery case and increase the risk of another correction.
