Bitget Launches Open Custody Network to Separate Institutional Trading From Asset Storage

TL;DR: Bitget announced an Open Custody Network on November 5 intended to connect external banks and institutional custodians with its trading infrastructure. The arrangement aims to reduce the need to keep trading assets directly at an exchange, though the security benefits depend on the exact custody and settlement design.

The largest crypto exchanges have historically offered trading and asset custody within the same service. That is convenient for customers, but it creates a concentration of risk: a platform failure can disrupt access to both execution and the underlying assets. Bitget says it wants to offer another model.

In its November 5 release, the company introduced the Open Custody Network, or OCN, as a connection between external banks, institutional-grade custodians and Bitget’s trading infrastructure for eligible users. The stated goal is to make it possible to trade through the venue while relying on a separate custody provider.

When trading and safekeeping sit in the same institution, a customer is exposed to that institution’s technology, operational controls, governance and financial health. Independent custody can reduce some of those exposures, especially if a trading venue does not have unilateral control of deposited assets.

But the words ‘off-exchange custody’ are not a complete security guarantee. A structure can still contain settlement obligations, pre-authorised transfers, credit arrangements or shared service providers. Institutions need to examine who holds legal title, who can initiate a withdrawal and what happens if the trading venue suspends operations.

Those questions have become urgent after recent security incidents. NewsBTC’s reporting on Aave restricting new tBTC activity showed that risk often depends on how separate components connect, not only on whether an individual component is technically sound.

Fast trading platforms need to know that obligations can be settled. Traditional markets solve this with clearing, collateral and regulated intermediaries. Crypto venues have often simplified the problem by holding assets directly in exchange accounts. If assets remain with external custodians, the system needs a dependable way to represent collateral and complete settlement.

That creates design choices around collateral efficiency, counterparty exposure and settlement finality. A custody network can be commercially attractive if it combines external safekeeping with a straightforward trading workflow, but it can also become harder to explain to customers if the protections differ by asset or jurisdiction.

Bitget’s release is a company announcement of the network’s launch and intended model. It does not independently establish that every partner bank or custodian provides the same services, that all users are eligible, or that risk has been eliminated. Marketing claims should be assessed against contractual details and actual operating history.

Prospective clients should ask for the participating custodians, the jurisdictions where the service operates, the method used to secure trading obligations, how positions are reconciled and how assets can be retrieved if the venue or a partner fails. Independent security assessments and clear segregation terms are particularly important.

The industry’s direction is becoming clearer. As more institutions trade digital assets, they expect the separation of functions familiar from other financial markets. Whether OCN delivers that standard will be judged by its real-world safeguards rather than its branding. For customers, the key question is not simply where assets are stored, but who can move them and under what conditions.
