The Ford Ranger Raptor is the horsepower king among gas-powered midsize pickups, at least on paper, with its 3.0-liter twin-turbocharged EcoBoost V6 producing 405 horsepower. That exceeds the 326-hp output of the 2.4-liter i-Force Max turbo hybrid powertrain in the Toyota Tacoma, America’s best-selling midsize pickup. The Nissan Frontier takes a different approach with its naturally aspirated 3.8-liter V6, but the automaker is apparently exploring a more powerful formula.

At this year’s SEMA Show, Nissan has brought a new Frontier-based concept called the Ronin, which adopts twin-turbocharging hardware from one of the brand’s most iconic nameplates, the GT-R. Both may have the same displacement, but the truck retains its VQ38DD engine rather than swapping in the GT-R’s VR38DETT V6. The automaker didn’t say how much the forced induction boosted its output, but for reference, the regular model already makes 310 horsepower.

This concept is particularly noteworthy given that the production Frontier makes 310 horsepower across the lineup, including the off-road-focused Pro-4X. Some may argue that this is more than enough, even for off-road driving, while others may see the single-engine lineup as limiting its appeal, especially compared with the Ford Ranger, which offers multiple engine options.

Nissan paired the GT-R-derived turbocharging system with EcuTek engine management, a custom front-mounted intercooler, dual off-road air intakes, and a Nismo Off Road exhaust. The suspension also received extensive upgrades, including modified Titan components, a rear axle swap, Bilstein Black Hawk shocks, and custom racing control arms. CJD Racing’s long-travel suspension components provide a four-inch lift, a 12-inch wider track, and 14 inches of suspension travel.

Other notable modifications include a custom front fascia, grille, and bumpers; prototype Nismo Off Road fender flares and running lights; Nismo Off Road 6-inch driving lights, 4-inch A-pillar lights, rock sliders, and all-weather floor mats; custom Kinetic sport bucket seats; and a dashboard gauge pod displaying boost, oil pressure, and exhaust temperature.

As a concept truck, the Frontier Ronin has no confirmed production plans, though Nissan can gather feedback that could influence future products. The automaker cites the Rock Creek lineup as an example of how previous SEMA concepts have inspired production models. So, with enough interest, the Japanese marque might consider adding a new, more powerful trim above the Pro-4X, potentially matching or even exceeding the Ranger Raptor’s horsepower on paper.

Nissan has already collaborated with Roush Performance to build a more extreme version of the Frontier called the Pro-4X R, featuring upgrades such as a two-inch suspension lift, Öhlins 2.0 off-road shocks, and 17-inch titanium-finished wheels. However, it retains the standard powertrain.

Aside from the Frontier Ronin, the automaker has brought two other concepts to the 2026 SEMA Show: the Pathfinder XE-V6 Concept, which celebrates the model’s 40th anniversary, and the Armada Jungle Patrol Concept. The event runs from November 3 to 6 at the Las Vegas Convention Center.
