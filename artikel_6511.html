We Tried 8 Store-Bought Mashed Potatoes &amp; These Are Worth Bringing To Thanksgiving

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Supermarket shortcuts that (almost) taste homemade.

Making a full holiday menu can be a monumental task, so you can—and should—outsource some of the cooking. The Thanksgiving dishes that you can reliably buy from the supermarket are the ones that are almost impossible to screw up. Case in point: mashed potatoes.

The essential side requires peeling pounds of potatoes, meticulous mashing, and taking up stove space (a precious commodity on Turkey Day). But at its core, the recipe is as simple as it gets. So if you want to save time and energy this year, store-bought spuds are a shortcut absolutely worth taking.

To help you decide which one to add to your Thanksgiving menu, we tasted eight varieties of mashed potatoes to identify the best of the best. We only considered refrigerated versions that are ready to heat and eat (no instant potatoes, thanks!). Here’s the verdict from our blind taste test.

Wegmans offers a few varieties of mashed potatoes, but the classic Whipped recipe is our favorite. It also has the shortest ingredient list: just potatoes, milk, butter, salt, and a small amount of natural food preservative. That simplicity translates to a product that tastes close to homemade.

Our editors appreciate the richness, real potato flavor, and chunks that add a rustic texture. They’re well-seasoned and can be eaten on their own, while still basic enough to pair with the rest of your Thanksgiving plate. These were the potatoes News and Features Editor Amanda Mactas tried first, and she knew after one bite they’d be her favorite. (Spoiler alert: they were.)

Another top contender comes from Sam’s Club with its private-label Yukon Gold mash. The ingredients start similarly to Wegmans', with potatoes, milk, and butter, though the Sam's Club version also includes black pepper and natural flavoring. But notable differences show up when you taste them side by side.

The Member’s Mark mash puts butter front and center and is seasoned a bit more generously than our overall winner. The richness is dialed up to 11. While some of us enjoyed it, a few found the butter to be too overpowering. But if you’re a butter lover, you won’t be disappointed by this Sam’s Club recipe.

Mashed potatoes are a bit like peanut butter: Some like it chunky, while others prefer creamy. If you fall in the latter camp, the Traditional Mashed Potatoes from Simply Potatoes is your best bet. The brand is widely available at major retailers and is distributed by Bob Evans Farms (note that the other mashed potatoes bearing his name did not make this list). This recipe includes margarine and a few extra preservatives, but the main ingredients are still potatoes, milk, and butter.

Our editors agree that the mash from Simply Potatoes has “great potato flavor” and a silky-smooth texture. It’s looser than our other top picks, with a finish closer to a French pommes purée than a hearty mash. This is the brand to buy if you want to avoid chunky mashed potatoes this Thanksgiving.
