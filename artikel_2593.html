The Colts have changed. Now Anthony Richardson must show he has, too

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

For the Colts, it's largely been for the worse, with a still-unbroken playoff drought, an 8-2 start to 2025 that has been followed by a 3-15 run, a fired GM and a starting quarterback situation more uncertain than ever. Only two of the eight team captains from 2024 remain, including an oft-injured, once-benched and once-demoted quarterback who in the time since he last started an NFL game wondered if he'd ever play again.

The Anthony Richardson Sr. who last started for the Colts was a single game off a 44.7% (17-for-38) passing performance while targeting the likes of Josh Downs, Alec Pierce, Adonai Mitchell and Michael Pittman Jr. Earlier that season, Richardson struggled through three separate 50% completion percentage games, as well as three more far worse (11-for-28, 39.3%; 10-for-32, 31.3%; 10-for-24, 41.7%).

With Daniel Jones' benching (and subsequent waiving) on Friday, Richardson rejoins the starting lineup, replacing a QB he squarely lost out to in a QB1 battle in the summer of 2025 – but within a shell of the offense he once captained and without virtually any of the weapons he still routinely struggled to connect with that produced his lowly 50.6% career completion percentage to go with 21 total touchdowns and 17 turnovers.

Even with a league-worst 13 interceptions, Jones' interception percentage (3.7%) is identical to Richardson's career mark, and the now ex-Colt's struggle to connect in the passing game – as bad as it has looked (58.4%) – is still more than 10 percentage points clear of Richardson's during the 11 games he started as a second-year QB in 2024 (47.7%).

With a diminished offensive line to guard him and a much-depleted caliber of weapons, the Colts can only hope Richardson, too, has changed over the past 23 months – for the better.

"The proof of that's going to be in the pudding on Sunday a little bit," Colts offensive coordinator Jim Bob Cooter said Tuesday, noting that in the time since Richardson lost the starting job in Indianapolis, he's had time to learn and observe under Jones and Philip Rivers in how they prepare for games and attack opponents. "It can be nice to have that 10,000-foot view and be the backup quarterback, sitting at the other spot at the table.

"You've got your thoughts on how you would solve the problem, and maybe you bring those up in that QB meeting room, but you're able to watch somebody else attempt to do that and sort of see what you think, see how that worked. Maybe add a new tool or two to your game. And the more you learn, the better it can serve your career. Anthony's had a chance to learn for a while."

When asked Tuesday about Richardson's lowly completion rate while making 15 starts over 2023-24, Cooter said the issues ranged not just from inaccurate throws but a lack of timing with receivers and either incorrect footwork or footwork that didn't accentuate the routes he was trying to throw to.

Richardson could wow crowds – and coaches and teammates – with his occasional deep-ball connections, combined with a physical, fearless rushing attack, but too frequently would Richardson make mistakes on the simple, more mechanical things that can be practiced and refined by repetition.

"Those are things that young quarterbacks get better at as they get those reps, as they're able to work on the side," Cooter said. "I think it's something Anthony has put a lot of time into. His footwork within his drop, timing up just exactly with that receiver route that he's trying to time it up with, whether it's the first read – and he's trying to get it out on time and not be late, but also not be early – or it could be the second read, and how I'm sliding my feet, working through those progressions and then ultimately delivering the pass."

During Richardson's extended action during the preseason, as he competed with second-year QB Riley Leonard for the team's backup quarterback spot behind Jones, the former-turned-current starter completed 63.6% of his throws with 7.6 yards per attempt on average to go along with 68 rushing yards on 10 carries and two total turnovers.

Throughout training camp, Richardson proved himself to be more consistent than the at-times shaky Leonard over the long haul, but on an eye test alone, he looked steps behind Jones – who ultimately struggled for much of the season before registering his two worst performances on the year in his final two starts that came after the team traded away his top two receivers at the deadline.

"We're playing some different guys these days, maybe than Anthony played with a few years ago, so (he's got to get) on the same page with the timing of their routes, the timing of the offense and how each guy may be a little bigger or smaller with their strides," Cooter said. "The timing of certain routes with different guys can be different."

What follows on Sunday is anyone's guess, as Richardson gears up to target Laquon Treadwell, Anthony Gould, Deion Burks, Ashton Dulin and company. A ground game that had at times found its stride has taken a hit, as rookie running back Seth McGowan will miss some time due to a hamstring injury, leaving Jonathan Taylor the lone feature back. The Giants, coming off an impressive win against the AFC South-leading Jaguars, will have to contend with a quarterback that no team in the league has remotely recent, meaningful tape on. Perhaps that element of surprise could play into the Colts' hands, but surprises can work both ways, too.

"We'll start offensively with the teammates that are around him – what are their strengths? Anthony's played a few different stretches over the years with different talent around him, so we'll definitely try to put all those guys in a position to have success," said Cooter, repeating a refrain Colts head coach Shane Steichen has said on repeat for weeks up against a rudderless offense. "We'll evaluate the big picture of this thing and not simply throw plays out there that we've run before with Anthony."
