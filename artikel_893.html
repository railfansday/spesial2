Korda seeks LPGA Hall of Fame place and familiar success at The Annika

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Nelly Korda and Charley Hull speak to media ahead of start of LPGA Tour's The Annika at Pelican Golf Club; Korda seeking fourth title at event and LPGA Hall of Fame place; Watch The Annika on LPGA Tour from 4pm on Thursday, live on Sky Sports Golf

World No 1 Nelly Korda makes her long-awaited return to competition this week at The Annika, live on Sky Sports Golf, seeking a fourth title at the event which would earn her a spot in the LPGA Hall of Fame.

Korda will make her first competitive start on the LPGA Tour since the FM Championship in August, and first appearance since the Solheim Cup in September.

The 28-year-old is already a three-time winner in her six prior starts at The Annika driven by Gainbridge at Pelican Golf Club, and is chasing her fifth win of the season.

"Eight weeks - I don't remember the last time I took that long off," said Korda.

"It was nice but by the middle of it I was ready to get going. There is only so many hours you can go out and practise and beat balls.

"I'm very excited to be back. I think it's more the rejuvenation of being really motivated to come back out, and sometimes when you play a lot and you travel a lot, you start to get a little stagnant.

"I'm not saying you're not driven but you just get a little tired, a little worn out, your body gets a little drained.

"When you do take that time to have a little bit of time for yourself to re-energise your batteries, you're very motivated to come back and play against the best players in the world and test yourself.

"I wanted to give my body a little bit of a break. It wasn't feeling too great. Last time I put myself into that position I ended up injuring myself and was out longer, so I would say that as I gotten older I've learned to listen to my body.

"Sometimes I just have to take that break. A lot of people may not agree with it. I have to really listen to myself and know what's best for myself."

Korda recently clinched her second player the year award after another year of excellence.

"Obviously it's not just me but it's my entire team behind me. I'm the one hitting the shots and grinding, but there is so much that goes on behind the scenes.

"I'm very grateful and also so thankful for everyone that has been with me through the ups and downs.

"Winning it in 2024 and having a little bit of an off year in 2025 and getting back to it in 2026, to me that's a testament of the people that I have behind me through the ups and downs just lift me up.

"I'm honoured and hopefully that continues to push me forward."

Britain's Charley Hull has played well at Pelican Golf Club with top-five finishes the past two seasons, including a runner-up finish behind Korda in 2024.

"I just had one week at home after Malaysia [Maybank Championship] and I was pretty much in bed by 6pm every day," Hull said on Wednesday.

"The jet lag got the best of me. The weather has gone foggy and cold at home but I love that kind of weather, so we've just been playing nine holes and just prepping as much as I can for these two weeks.

"These are my favourite two weeks of the year.

"It would be nice to get a win. Obviously I've won round the golf course next week back in 2016 and come second here, so hopefully I can get it going because I like the two weeks.

"I just need to hole some putts. I played so well in Malaysia but didn't putt that well. Still resulted in a top-10 finish but if I just get the putts rolling it should be a good week.

"I just need to go out there and have fun. Play a bit of golf and enjoy it. Win or lose on a Saturday, Sunday."
