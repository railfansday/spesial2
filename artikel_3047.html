Don't Give Up on Realty Income in a Rising-Rate Environment; It Can Easily Survive Until the Property Market Adjusts

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Rising rates are a problem for Realty Income, but not the end of the world. Here's why you should buy it anyway.

By Reuben Gregg Brewer – Nov 22, 2026 at 11:18AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Realty Income ›

Rising interest rates are not good for real estate investment trusts (REITs). That's a major concern for investors today who own Realty Income (O +0.64%) or are considering buying it. Don't use interest rates as an excuse to skip past this stock and its attractive 5.6% dividend yield. Realty Income is prepared for the impact of rising rates, and history shows that the headwinds are likely to be temporary. Here's why this giant net-lease REIT is still a buy for conservative dividend investors.

Like most real estate investment trusts, Realty Income makes extensive use of leverage. When interest rates go up, debt becomes more expensive. That, in turn, puts pressure on profits. There's no easy way around that fact, but it isn't the end of the world. In fact, it's just the normal ebb and flow of the economy. And Realty Income has proven it can ride the wave, noting that it has increased its dividend annually for 32 consecutive years.

To be fair, Realty Income is much larger today than it was 32 years ago. At this point, it is the largest public net lease REIT with a portfolio of more than 15,500 properties. (A net lease requires tenants to pay for most property-level operating costs.) Given the company's size and its investment-grade balance sheet, it tends to have an advantage in access to capital markets.

However, that scale is important in another way because it has given the company the leeway to diversify. For example, it generates around 20% of its rents from Europe, where the net lease sector is still fairly small. Europe also has slightly different interest rate dynamics from those of the United States. Realty Income has also entered new areas, including asset management, partnerships, and lending. All of these help to offset the impact of rising rates as well.

So, from a business perspective, Realty Income has not only proven it can survive interest rate volatility but is also better prepared today than ever before. However, the real reason dividend investors should consider buying the REIT today, despite rising rates, is related to timing.

The big reason why rising rates are a problem is that they make buying new properties less profitable. That's not good, but rates don't operate in a vacuum. Think about it for a second: if rates rose to the point where it was unprofitable to buy property, then nobody would buy property. The property market would crawl to a standstill.

That can't happen for an extended period. Property owners with debt coming due and property developers will need to sell assets if they don't want to default on their loans. To avoid that fate, they will be forced to lower prices to the point where buyers are willing to step back in. In Realty Income's experience, such market adjustments take roughly a year.

That said, there are more players in the market today than historically, which Realty Income believes could shorten the adjustment period somewhat. But the real takeaway is that property markets will eventually adjust and buying new assets will become attractive again. Thus, Realty Income isn't facing a permanent headwind, but a temporary one. So all it has to do is get by until the property markets adjust. As noted above, it is better positioned than ever to do just that.

All of that said, Realty Income is meant to be a boring, slow-growth dividend stock. It is a foundational investment that allows you to sleep well at night and, perhaps, add lower-yielding but higher-growth businesses to your portfolio. This high-yield stock won't excite you, but that's the point. And its ability to support its dividend (and actually continue to grow it) amid the uncertainty of a rising-rate environment is actually evidence that it is the type of dividend stock you'll want to own for the long term.
