Banks offer dozens of services beyond checking and savings, from wire transfers and notaries to small loans and foreign currency, and many cost account holders nothing. Others cost less at your own bank than at a check casher or payday lender. Check what your bank includes before you pay anyone else for the same service.

Millions of households with a bank account still pay someone else for these services. In 2023, 14.2% of U.S. households, about 19 million, had a checking or savings account but turned to nonbank businesses for check cashing, money orders, or payday loans, the FDIC’s national survey found.

A bank does far more than hold a checking account. The same bank can guarantee a payment, wire money the same day, lend against a home, notarize a form, and order currency for a trip.

Plenty of its services cost an account holder nothing, from direct deposit and bill pay to the bank’s own ATMs and, at some banks, a notary. Others cost less than the usual alternative, such as cashing a check at your own bank rather than a check casher, or borrowing a few hundred dollars from a bank rather than a payday lender.

Deposits in these accounts at an FDIC-insured bank are insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, for each ownership category, such as individual and joint accounts.

Before you pay a check casher, a payday lender, or a coin kiosk, look up your bank’s fee schedule, which lists what each service costs account holders, or ask at a branch which of these your account includes.
