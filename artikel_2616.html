As Commanders stay in playoff race, Bengals' rivals rejoice: MNF winners and losers

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

LANDOVER, MD – On a drizzly, breezy night outside the nation's capital, the Washington Commanders put a damper on the Cincinnati Bengals five-game winning streak, prevailing 22-20 on Monday, Nov. 23, as Drew Stevens' fifth field goal of the night at the final gun provided the winning margin.

Not only did the Commanders wind up victorious, they executed to perfection in a late-game situation − a scenario that had been a major bugaboo throughout the season prior to their upset of the Stripes.

As with almost any NFL game, there is plenty to sift through on a night with several winners and losers beyond the final score registered at the end. They are as follows:

Week 3: Indianapolis Colts kicker Spencer Shrader (3) celebrates with long snapper Luke Rhodes (46) after Shrader kicked a 58-yard field goal against the Houston Texans at Lucas Oil Stadium. The Colts debuted their "Anvil Strike" uniform - featuring an unfamiliar gray uniform and glossy, metallic blue helmet with the familiar horseshoe logo in white. The Colts won, 19-17, for their first win of the season.

Week 3: Buffalo Bills quarterback Josh Allen (17) hands off to running back James Cook III against the Los Angeles Chargers at New Highmark Stadium. For the game, the Bills debuted their "Nickel City" alternate uniforms - the most striking element being the blue "Charge Helmet," adorned simply with an oversized cutout of the team's familiar logo. The Bills won the game, 24-16.

Week 2: Los Angeles Rams quarterback Matthew Stafford (9) hands the ball to running back Kyren Williams (23) during a 28-6 win on "Monday Night Football" against the New York Giants at SoFi Stadium. The Rams debuted their new "Classic Sol" uniforms, an homage to the team's look during its 1951 championship season.

Week 2: Arizona Cardinals quarterback Jacoby Brissett (7) hands off to running back Jeremiyah Love against the Seattle Seahawks at State Farm Stadium. The Cardinals wore their desert-themed "Rivalries" uniforms in the 31-7 loss to the Seahawks. Arizona's "Rivalries" look was one of the best to debut in 2025.

At 3-5-1, they remain two games behind the New York Giants in the NFC East but have the tiebreaker if they can catch them.

The undrafted rookie kicker out of Iowa shrugged off the elements to do the bulk of the scoring for Washington − perfect on all five of his field-goal tries and one PAT to account for 16 of the Commanders' 22 points. He drilled the game-winning 26-yarde on the game's final play. Stevens also held forth after the game in a captivating press conference and hilarious conference that probably only could have been pulled off by a green 22-year-old.

Trey who? The Cincinnati edge rusher, who entered the night with a team-leading 6½ sacks, bagged Washington QB Jayden Daniels three times – leaving Mafe among the league's top five in that department by night's end.

As speculation continues to swirl about the "Monday Night Football" tandem's partnership – and specifically the fate of apparent lame duck Aikman – the duo continue to deliver a professional broadcast every week that belies the apparent tension that exists behind the scenes and that the Hall of Fame quarterback continues to fuel when he's interviewed by other outlets between games.

It certainly wasn't a neutral voting party, but 100% of the three dozen tailgaters polled by USA TODAY Sports at Northwest Stadium prior to kickoff enthusiastically embraced Washington's new alternate logo, a gold spear infused through the Commanders' usual "W" sigil. The mostly black alternate uniform was nearly as popular amid its coming-out party … though Buck said the burgundy jersey numbers were hard to decipher from the "MNF" booth.

Cincinnati's loss reduced its lead over its nearest AFC North pursuers to one game while weakening the Bengals' playoff standing, where they are currently projected as the No. 4 seed.

Cincinnati's loss softened the blow of the Jags' Sunday loss to the Giants just a bit. By virtue of a superior record in AFC games, Jacksonville reclaimed the third overall spot in the projected AFC postseason field.

The field temporarily went dark three minutes into the third quarter – and with NFL commissioner Roger Goodell looking on from a suite. Fortunately, it was never a Super Bowl 47-adjacent situation, and the press box remained fully lit along much of the venue's secondary lighting, main scoreboard and ribbon panels throughout the interior of the stadium. After a short commercial break from ESPN, the field was again aglow … apparently after the building's lighting vendor re-enabled the main lights, which usually go down for maintenance on Monday nights.

Hardly ideal playing conditions given a slick ball and field, not to mention an inspired effort by Washington's defense – which prevented the presumed MVP frontrunner from setting an NFL record by accounting for at least four TDs (passing and/or rushing) in six consecutive games. Worse, the Bengals fell two game off the pace currently being set by the AFC-leading Buffalo Bills.

Cincinnati's receiver had a quiet night (5 catches, 31 yards) and lost the chance to match Hall of Famer Calvin Johnson's mark of snagging at least 10 passes in four consecutive games.
