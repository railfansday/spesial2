Inside Wales' dressing room - how Lake inspired history

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Inside the Wales dressing room at the Principality Stadium at half-time on Saturday, heads could easily have bowed.

Visitors New Zealand had just levelled the scores at 21-21 with the clock in the red at the end of the first half.

It was a sucker punch that could have knocked the wind out of Welsh sails.

But captain Dewi Lake summoned the words that inspired a first win over New Zealand in 73 years.

With a rousing speech, the captain reminded his players of the feeling in the same changing room 12 months earlier, when they trailed by just three points at the break only to eventually lose 52-26.

"Dewi made a very good point, the last time that we played them we had been in the contest, but let the dam break," Leicester and Wales wing Gabriel Hamer-Webb told the Rugby Union Weekly podcast.

"They slipped through and we promised ourselves that we weren't going to let that happen again, that we had an opportunity to really do something special.

"The heart the boys showed from then on, we wanted it more than they did. New Zealand were shocked and there was no way that we were going to let it slip."

Gabriel Hamer-Webb won only his second cap in the win over New Zealand [Huw Evans Picture Agency]

Hamer-Webb endured a tough time when making his Wales debut in the Six Nations defeat by Scotland in February.

He was rewarded for strong club form with a second cap against the All Blacks, coming into the side at the expense of experienced wing Josh Adams.

The former Cardiff back is a Test novice, but has already cemented himself as part of Welsh history, with the victory leading to post-match plans being ripped up.

Hamer-Webb said: "I had dinner planned with my family for 8.30pm and the boys were telling me that I had to cancel and that there was no way I was going!

"The changing room beers after a great win - they are the best moments.

"We went into the lounge at the stadium afterwards and there was so much singing and happiness.

"We absolutely made the most of it and we understood this was a moment that would go down in history and we had to celebrate properly.

"After the game I couldn't walk two metres without somebody congratulating me or someone saying that was one of the best moments of their life," he continued.

"My grandfather had never seen us beat the All Blacks, you now start to understand that meant a lot more than us just playing for 80 minutes and getting a win. It meant a lot more."

Wales got back to work on Monday as they hunt a first November clean sweep since Warren Gatland's side achieved the feat for the first time in 2018.

They take on Australia in Cardiff on Saturday (20:10 GMT) and then face another opponent, likely to be Japan, at Nations Championship finals weekend at Twickenham's Allianz Stadium.

"Having another game week definitely makes you park it up a little bit in your mind because we want to focus on Australia," said Hamer-Webb.

"The belief is massive. This is maybe turning point where people realise what we are doing as a team, and what Steve Tandy is providing for us, is working and we are going to go somewhere with it.

"The bar is set. We can't go into this week and the final game and not have that positive form of desperation not to let them score.

"That needs to come out every single time if we want to keep progressing. There are no excuses to not bring that same level of intensity that we did."
