Player grades: Thunder get much-needed 125-103 win over Kings

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Jamming on the brakes, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander had Malik Monk flying in the opposite direction. The two-time MVP went with the killer crossover move before he drilled the outside jumper. It was all fun and games in a much-needed low-stress night.

The Oklahoma City Thunder snapped a rare three-game losing streak with a 125-103 win over the Sacramento Kings. They were in firm control throughout the night in this almost wire-to-wire victory.

Against the literal worst team in the league, the Thunder had fun in rookie mode. It didn't take long for them to get ahead. The ball movement was crisp. Isaiah Hartenstein found Kenrich Williams on a cutting layup. They had a quick 13-4 lead to get things started.

Gilgeous-Alexander went right at several Kings defenders to get the nifty up-and-under layup. Domantas Sabonis played like a guy who is trying to sabotage his way out of Sacramento. The Thunder had a 32-24 lead after the first quarter.

If you thought the Kings didn't have at least one counterpunch in them, think again. They went on an 18-7 run. The Thunder struggled to get any offense going as Gilgeous-Alexander rested. Ajay Mitchell and Nikola Topic couldn't properly run the second unit.

After Sacramento tied things up at 47 points apiece with a little under four minutes to go, Gilgeous-Alexander took over. The Thunder had a strong finish to the first half. They finally figured out the Kings' gimmicky zone defense. On their defensive end, Alex Caruso turned into a ball-hawk as he disrupted passing lanes and generated steals.

The Thunder had 30 points in the second quarter. They had a 62-50 lead over the Kings at the halftime break. To show how disoriented Sacramento is, it fouled Micah Peavy on a heave before the buzzer. Considering how this season-long road trip had played out, you'd take that score in a heartbeat. It was badly needed as the OKC fanbase's morale was at an all-time low.

The second half was much the same story. The Thunder kept their distance on the scoreboard. Gilgeous-Alexander ensured that as he almost walked his way to an efficient 40 points. He completely dissected Sacramento's defense. Darius Acuff Jr. tried his best to go band for band, but to no avail. None of its youth or offense-first guards had a shot at slowing him down. Funnily enough, maybe Nique Clifford could've been their best shot.

The Thunder had an easy 32 points in the third quarter. They had a 94-80 lead over the Kings. The final frame turned into Clifford's revenge stretch as he had nine points. As Gilgeous-Alexander sat out the final dozen minutes, OKC's B and C squad actually ballooned their advantage to a game-high 27 points. They had 31 points in garbage time.

The Thunder shot 52% from the field and went 10-of-31 (32.3%) from 3. They shot 23-of-28 on free throws. They had 21 assists on 46 baskets. Six Thunder players scored double-digit points.

Gilgeous-Alexander led the way with an efficient 37 points and two assists. Aday Mara recorded a 16-point, 13-rebound double-double. Mitchell had 14 points and five assists. Caruso finished with 11 points and four steals. Jared McCain chipped in with 12 points and Williams had 10 points.

Meanwhile, the Kings shot 45% from the field and went 14-of-43 (32.6%) from 3. They shot 15-of-20 on free throws. They had 27 assists on 37 baskets. Six Kings players scored double-digit points.

Acuff Jr. put up plenty of shot attempts with 26 points on 25 shots. Sabonis had 11 points and 11 rebounds. Monk had 14 points and 10 assists. Dylan Cardwell tallied 10 points and six rebounds. Daeqwon Plowden went off for 13 points. Leaky Black finished with 12 points and nine rebounds.

Back to business as usual for the Thunder. Now, you shouldn't throw parades because they had a stress-free win over the worst team in the league, but it shows a pulse. Something you couldn't have proclaimed after a recent bad stretch. Gilgeous-Alexander helped them gain a little confidence. Also helped that Mitchell returned. The offense was awesome — something you've seldom been able to say since the Post-D.C.C. (Dub, Chet, Caso) era started.

On an island with Alex Karaban, Gilgeous-Alexander's eyes widened like a Looney Tunes cartoon. He toyed with the fresh-faced rookie as he yo-yo'd the ball. Going with the between-the-legs move, not even Acuff Jr.'s ill-advised help made a difference. The two-time MVP stepped through to go up for the layup in a circus-style finish.

Gilgeous-Alexander finished with 37 points on 13-of-20 shooting, two assists and two rebounds. He shot 4-of-5 from 3 and went 7-of-7 on free throws.

Vintage Gilgeous-Alexander showed up once again. The Kings had absolutely no hope of slowing down the two-time MVP winner. The dysfunctional bunch was at his mercy. He completely sliced them up like a surgeon. Barely needing to break a sweat, his shot diet returned to getting to the cup or drilling pull-up jumpers. When he was on the floor, the Thunder ran the Kings out of their own quarter-filled gym.

Gilgeous-Alexander had a dozen points through the first quarter. He sat at 21 at halftime. As the Thunder ran away on the scoreboard, he flirted with another 40-burger before sitting out the final frame. Against the worst and most disorganized defense in the league, things were too easy as he went at half speed. No need to go all out against a team where half-effort was adequate.

I mean, not much to say about this one. Gilgeous-Alexander has normalized these types of super-efficient scoring outings. Especially against a basement-dwelling team. He's mastered the art of scoring in basketball. At all four levels, too — at the rim, in the mid-range, from the outside and at the free-throw line. It speaks to his NBA excellence that nobody even blinks when he has these types of video-game-esque games.

Setting a screen, Mara received the ball at the high nail. He waited for Nikola Topic to run the backdoor cut to get the easy assist. The 21-year-old continues to accelerate his player development amid the Thunder figuring out what exactly they have left with their undermanned group.

Mara finished with 16 points on 8-of-11 shooting, 13 rebounds and three assists.

Naturally, lottery-picked NBA rookies are going to have plenty of ups and downs. A rollar-coaster-esque first year is expected. And after a couple of dips that exposed how Mara just isn't ready to anchor a defense at all times, he showed why his unique 7-foot-3 frame gives him such a high floor to work out in the NBA as a rotation player at worst.

The Thunder continue to feature Mara in their halfcourt offense. About every one of their ball-handlers knows by now his galaxy-sized catch radius. Just throw it up top and odds are, he'll come down with it for an easy alley-oop connection. Not just that, but he's also a mean putback machine. The Kings didn't possess the height or muscle to slow him down on the glass.

Mara flexed his rebounding with a double-double. That included eight on the offensive boards. As the game got out of hand, he experimented with his playmaking. That has been an underutilized aspect of his game. Even if he's been extremely green at times, he's been a blessing to OKC's current injury-depleted situation. Who knows where this team would be without his frontcourt depth? Probably at a much more dire spot.

Swiping at an unproven ball-handler, Caruso punked the ball out of Plowden's possession. He single-handedly created another steal and a fastbreak bucket as the Kings failed a simple inbounds play. The 32-year-old was on another level both with his savviness and active hands.

Caruso finished with 11 points on 3-of-6 shooting, three assists and two rebounds. He shot 1-of-3 from 3 and went 4-of-4 on free throws. He also had four steals and one block.

The activity from Caruso was off the charts. Even more so than usual. Could you tell the Thunder treated this as kinda a must-win situation? As they navigate their new grim NBA reality, he's been a bright spot. Anytime he's been on the floor, it's given OKC a glimpse into the past with an all-time defensive rampage. He bullied the poor Kings' ball-handlers all night.

This will get lost in his NBA career arc, but Caruso was at the peak of his defensive powers in this one. He helped the Thunder pile up on the scoreboard. They were a plus-21 in his 18 minutes off the bench. Nice to see him get to double-digit points, but any offense is just found money. His value is at the defensive end. And that has been more important than usual right now with all their absences.

Receiving Williams' pass, Mitchell had things going with a catch-and-shoot outside jumper. The Thunder desperately missed him during his two-game absence. The 24-year-old didn't even need to have a monstrous night to get this one — just his mere presence supplied breathing room to everybody on offense.

Mitchell finished with 14 points on 6-of-14 shooting, five assists and three rebounds. He shot 1-of-4 from 3 and went 1-of-1 on free throws. He also had two steals.

Over their last two losses, the Thunder needed somebody else whom they could trust with the ball. Jared McCain was too hit-or-miss and the other candidates just weren't NBA-level scoring talent, sadly. At least not yet with guys like Bennet Stirtz. In came Mitchell. He helped OKC get ahead in the early stages of this lopsided matchup. Just the threat of another go-to scorer helps run a much smoother offense that was previously gummed up.

Back to barreling into the rim. Mitchell won't ever stray away from that blue-collar style of hoops. Even if it means missing time every now and then. He also relied on his pull-up jumper to get buckets. The Thunder need him to suit up from here on out. He's scaled up in the scoring totem pole. And has become too important at work to miss time.

Jab-stepping his way to the baseline, Clifford had Black bite on the pump-fake. The 24-year-old hopped off the floor for a one-legged mid-range jumper that swished in. Revenge bucket to start garbage time. Let's throw the new guy a shoutout as he played against his old squad for the first time since being traded.

Clifford finished with nine points on 3-of-4 shooting and three rebounds. He shot 0-of-1 from 3 and went 3-of-4 on free throws. He also had one steal.

Heading into the night, Clifford's return to the Kings was a much-discussed storyline. After all, it had only been a week since the Kings traded him for two future second-round picks. Surely, the wounds were still fresh. But as the game progressed, it felt like he was going to have a quiet night but enjoy a win. That was until he scored nine points in the final frame of inconsequential basketball.

Funny enough, Clifford had his best stretch since he joined the Thunder. The offense went from being a black hole to at least intriguing with a few layups. The defense was solid. Still early in his time in OKC, he hasn't shown a ton of promise yet. But expect him to get more opportunities as the season progresses. The Thunder need NBA talent right now.
