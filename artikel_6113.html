TAP Air Portugal Adds Major New 9-Hour US Nonstop Route: See Map Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

TAP Air Portugal has big long-haul plans for next summer, with the Portuguese flag carrier and Star Alliance member set to launch a new transatlantic route from Francisco Sá Carneiro Airport (OPO) in Porto. This route will serve Dulles International Airport (IAD) in Washington DC, with three flights a week planned using the Airbus A321LR. All of its transatlantic flights from Porto use these narrowbody twinjets.

Their long-range capabilities and lower capacity than TAP's Airbus A330 family widebodies make them a good fit for secondary transatlantic routes like these, which have enough demand but not enough to consistently fill a twin-aisle jet. There are some comfort concerns, but at least in business class, TAP puts passengers at ease with spacious flatbeds, some of which are 'thrones.'

According to AeroRoutes, TAP will begin flying from Porto to Washington on March 30, 2027. This follows the switch to the IATA Summer Schedule on March 30, with these flights operating three times a week on Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Saturdays. Flying westbound from Porto to Dulles, the maximum block time is eight hours 35 minutes, with flights departing relatively late in the evening for this route.

On Saturdays, TP263 departs Porto at 5:10 PM and reaches Dulles at 10:45 PM local time. Meanwhile, the Tuesday and Thursday flights depart later, at 7:20 PM, arriving in Dulles at 10:55 PM. In both cases, TAP's Airbus A321LR has just a 90-minute turnaround, with the 7.5-hour return leg, TP264, departing at 10:15 PM on Saturdays and 12:25 AM on Wednesdays and Fridays.

The A321LRs that TAP is set to use on its new flights from Porto to Dulles are relatively premium-heavy for a narrowbody twinjet, with 16 business class flatbeds located at the front of the aircraft. This reflects their use on long-haul routes, with the seats laid out in an alternating 2-2/1-1 setup, with the latter featuring single 'throne' seats. There are also 155 economy seats in the classic 3-3 configuration.

The Airbus A321LR is also the jet of choice for TAP's two existing transatlantic routes from Porto, which also serve destinations on the East Coast of the United States of America. According to current scheduling data from Cirium, an aviation analytics company, the airline currently links Portugal's second-largest city with Boston Logan International Airport (BOS) and Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR).

The Boston route operates just twice a week during the winter, but in late March, the switch to the summer schedule will double its frequency to four flights a week. The Newark route is served more or less daily all year, albeit with a slight drop in frequency to six flights a week in February. With Newark being a hub for fellow Star Alliance member United Airlines, this route has particular strategic importance.

TAP's main hub is Humberto Delgado Airport (LIS) in Lisbon, the capital city of the western European country. Its transatlantic network is much more extensive, with nonstop flights to nine destinations in the US and another two in Canada. However, the airport's slot restrictions can limit growth opportunities, hence the importance of growing in Porto.

In terms of TAP's two Canadian routes this November, it serves Montreal three times a week and Toronto eight times a week. In the US, its top destination is currently Newark, with an average of 13 weekly departures, followed by Boston with 12 and Miami with 11. Less-than-daily flights serve Chicago, Washington Dulles, Los Angeles, New York-JFK, Orlando, and San Francisco.
