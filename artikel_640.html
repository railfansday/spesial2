Amanda Seyfried Reveals Secret Elizabeth Holmes Role in ‘You Can See Everything’ Required a “Five-Factor Authentication App” to Read Script

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

"How the hell did I manage to keep it under wraps?,” the actress joked.

Amanda Seyfried is revealing more about the secrecy surrounding her involvement in A24’s You Can See Everything and how she’s surprised she managed to keep her role in the film under wraps.

Directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, the documentary follows the 34 days the filmmakers spent with disgraced Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes and her partner, Billy Evans, leading up to Holmes’ imprisonment. While Fielder and Oppenheim have worked on the doc for the past three years, Seyfried joined a year ago, appearing as herself and playing Holmes in the film’s third act, after she leaves for prison. Until the film’s October release, Seyfried’s role was kept secret. During a new appearance on The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon, Seyfried opened up about just how secret her participation was.

“I didn’t know what the script would be until I got to set, or it would be, like, a five-factor authentication app where I could maybe read it the night before,” she said.

You Can See Everything marked Seyfried’s second time playing Holmes, following her portrayal in the 2022 limited series The Dropout. Holmes herself watched clips of Seyfried’s performance in the documentary. Seyfried won an Emmy for her portrayal, and now her latest turn as Holmes is generating Oscar buzz. If she wins the Academy Award, she would make history as the first person to win both an Oscar and an Emmy for playing the same person.

Although Seyfried kept her participation in the film private for the better part of a year, she did tell some of those closest to her.

“I’m the most indiscreet person I’ve ever met in my life. How the hell did I manage to keep it under wraps?” she said. “I told a lot of people. But I guess I could trust them all.”

YCSE has crossed $100 million worldwide at the box office and became the first documentary film to have one million Letterboxd members log it as watched.
