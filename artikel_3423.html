The three movies released by Paramount and Warner Bros. following their merger under the Skydance Corporation have delivered uneven results at the box office. While both Street Fighter and Clayface managed to surpass the coveted $100 million milestone at the global box office, the video game adaptation's hefty reported budget of $80 million somewhat tempered that achievement. Meanwhile, Clayface's relatively solid box-office run was overshadowed by the departure of DC Studios co-CEOs James Gunn and Peter Safran. W.B. has an ace up its sleeve with the upcoming Dune: Part Three, which is poised to break franchise records this December.

However, the studio's most recent release appears to be losing momentum after three weeks in theaters. The animated movie is facing fresh competition from Disney's new big-budget film​​​​​​​ Hexed this week, after competing with Laika's critically acclaimed Wildwood​​​​​​​. While the W.B. film wasn't as expensive to produce as Hexed — the Disney fantasy movie has a reported budget of $200 million — it wasn't as critically acclaimed as Wildwood either.

Directed by Travis Knight, Wildwood continued Laika's exquisite streak of releasing acclaimed stop-motion gems; the new movie holds a "Certified Fresh" 97% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes and has grossed around $80 million worldwide so far, against a reported budget of $100 million. The W.B. movie cost around $90 to produce, but it now holds a 49% score on Rotten Tomatoes.

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

We're talking, of course, about The Cat in the Hat, the second major adaptation of Dr. Seuss' famed children's book. The film features Bill Hader as the voice of the titular character, along with Xochitl Gomez and Matt Berry in supporting roles. Its Rotten Tomatoes consensus reads, "A high-energy sprint through the world imagined by Dr. Seuss, The Cat in the Hat's eagerness to please has some charm but could have benefitted from smaller stakes and a less frantic pace."

The movie has so far grossed a little under $75 million at the global box office, and comes on the heels of fellow W.B. movies such as Lee Cronin's The Mummy, Mortal Kombat II, Supergirl, and Clayface. It debuted in more than 4,000 theaters three weeks ago and is now playing​​​​​​​ in roughly 3,200 locations nationwide. With a terrific 89% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes, the movie has all the makings of a home-video hit. This is the second major adaptation of "The Cat in the Hat," following the infamous 2003 movie starring Mike Myers, which grossed $135 million worldwide against a reported budget of $109 million, and now holds an 11% Rotten Tomatoes score.
