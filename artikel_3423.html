Reading Hunger Games Books In Order: Complete Guide

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping hits theaters on November 20, the dystopian franchise returning at the best possible time. As of now, all five books in Suzanne Collins’ era-defining dystopian YA series—the original trilogy and its two prequels—have made it to the big screen. But whether you’ve been following along since the series began in 2008 or are just entering the world of The Hunger Games, your immersion doesn’t have to end when the film does.

In fact, if Sunrise on the Reaping is your first exposure to the world of The Hunger Games, there’s no time like the present to dive into The Hunger Games books. This is one of the best dystopian series of all time, and so many other authors have tried to create the next Hunger Games. But few literary worlds have been crafted with as much intimacy and care as Panem.

Whether you’re jumping into the books for the first time or simply planning to re-read the series when you get home from the theater, Collins’ propensity for sequels means there are multiple ways to approach this series. After all, The Hunger Games spans nearly a century of its fictional setting’s history, and every twist sets the stage for something even greater.

The original book trilogy—The Hunger Games (2008), Catching Fire (2009), and Mockingjay (2010)—centered on 16-year-old Katniss Everdeen, a reluctant Hunger Games contestant turned leader of a revolution. It was both impossible to put down and devastating to leave behind when it’s finished. Its two prequels—The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes (2020) and Sunrise on the Reaping (2025)—add even more context to Collins’ already rich dystopian world.

But they also function as intimate character studies of two pivotal figures from the original trilogy: dictator Coriolanus Snow and mentor Haymitch Abernathy. They work as both a setup for and an addendum to the trilogy. In short, there is (almost) no wrong way to read the series, but there are several different ways to do it correctly.

Whether you're planning to read the Hunger Games books in chronological or publication order, it will help to have a basic understanding of Panem, the dystopian nation where the series takes place.

In short, Panem is a version of North America in the distant, post-apocalyptic future. In this version, serfdom has made a major comeback: a dictator rules Panem from an isolated district of wealthy elites, while the rest of the country, divided into 12 Districts, struggles to survive.

By the time the original Hunger Games series begins, Panem is a fully-formed state that has existed for decades, as have the Hunger Games themselves. According to the original series, the Capitol formed in the immediate aftermath of the apocalyptic event that wiped out what had been North America, and held power for several years as a totalitarian state, ruling Panem in 13 districts. However, the residents of the ultra-impoverished District 13 eventually rose up against the Capitol, starting a civil war later referred to as “the Dark Days.” Minor spoiler alert: they failed.

The Capitol then organized the Games—aka the annual battle royale for children at the center of this series—in the aftermath of that war as a means of quelling any future rebellion. Another minor spoiler alert: this doesn’t go as planned.

The original Hunger Games series takes place 74 years after the first Hunger Games, meaning we have yet to actually experience the Games’ origin story from a firsthand perspective. However, Collins’ prequels do provide incredibly detailed context for other parts of Panem’s early history.

The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, which centers on President Snow’s dark path from destitute aristocrat to brutal dictator in his younger years, takes place during the 10th Hunger Games.

The next book, chronologically, is Sunrise on the Reaping, which takes place during the 50th Hunger Games and follows Haymitch Abernathy, Katniss Everdeen’s mentor from the original series, as he competes in the Games as a teenager.

From then on, the series runs in publication order. Collins’ original three Hunger Games novels take place in rapid succession, starting with the 74th annual Hunger Games, and center on Katniss Everdeen.

This method is even easier to track than reading the books in chronological order: Collins’ original series were published in rapid succession between 2008 and 2010, while her prequels came more than a decade later. The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes was released in 2020, while Sunrise on the Reaping hit shelves last year, in 2025.

While the prequels give some context to Panem’s history, as anyone who read the series in real time upon its release can tell you, saving them for after the original series won’t necessarily disrupt your ability to follow the story.

After all, the most significant “spoilers” you could encounter by reading the original books before the prequels are so unavoidable that you probably already know them, even if you’ve never touched one of Collins’ books. Coriolanus Snow becomes Panem’s dictator after The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, and, despite the best efforts of many tributes, no one successfully stops the Hunger Games before Katniss arrives on the scene.

Whether you do it before or after the original trilogy, one of the best parts of reading the prequels is meeting two all-new ensembles of beloved, tragic past residents of Panem who don't live long enough to see Katniss' rebellion. However, because Panem’s entire history is fairly short, several characters who experienced the first Hunger Games are still alive when the main series takes place. Aside from the obvious—President Snow and Haymitch Abernathy, the respective protagonists of the two prequels—there are a few other familiar faces you'll recognize in both the prequels and the original series.

From Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, Snow’s cousin Tigris appears in the main trilogy. From Sunrise on the Reaping, stylist Effie Trinket, TV host Caesar Flickerman, game maker Plutarch Heavensbee, and former tributes Wiress, Beetee, and Mags all appear in the Capitol at some point or another. President Snow is the only character to appear in both the prequels and the main series.
