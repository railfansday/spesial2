Snap vs. The Trade Desk: Which Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Snap's 474 million daily users meet The Trade Desk's open-internet ad platform, two very different bets on where digital ad budgets are heading.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 27, 2026 at 12:32PM EST

Snap operates as a mobile-first social media company that prioritizes visual communication and augmented reality experiences for its global community. The Trade Desk provides a self-service platform that allows advertisers to buy and manage digital campaigns across various publishers, websites, and connected television services. These companies represent two different ways to profit from the ongoing transition of advertising budgets to digital channels.

Snap focuses on its core Snapchat application, which includes features like Stories, Spotlight, and Snap Map to engage users. The company serves a diverse range of advertising customers, from small local businesses to major global brands. It relies on infrastructure partnerships with providers like Alphabet (GOOGL +0.68%) (NASDAQ:GOOG) and Amazon (AMZN +3.96%) to deliver its bandwidth-intensive video and augmented reality services.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $6.6 billion, representing growth of roughly 13.8% compared to the prior year. Despite this growth, the company reported a net loss of nearly $151.2 million for the period. Management has focused on diversifying revenue through subscription services like Snapchat+ and integrating conversational artificial intelligence to improve user interactions and ad targeting.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company maintained a current ratio of nearly 3.6x. The current ratio measures a company's ability to cover its short-term liabilities with its short-term assets. Its debt-to-equity ratio, which compares total debt to shareholder equity, is roughly 2.1x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached close to $437.2 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 155% of operating cash flow, meaning reported cash generation is heavily inflated by this non-cash add-back.

The Trade Desk operates an independent buy-side platform that emphasizes transparency and data-driven decision-making for advertisers. Unlike platforms that control both the ad space and the buying tools, Trade Desk focuses exclusively on the buyer side. This allows it to aggregate inventory from hundreds of ad exchanges and publishers, including partnerships with retail data providers to measure actual sales lift from digital campaigns.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue grew by approximately 7% to reach nearly $3.0 billion. The company achieved a net income of roughly $329.4 million during this timeframe, demonstrating its ability to maintain a positive net margin. As more consumers shift toward investing in social media stocks and streaming services, Trade Desk aims to capture the ad spending that follows them across the open internet.

Based on its December 2025 balance sheet, the company carries a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.2x. This low ratio suggests that the company uses relatively little debt compared to its equity. Its current ratio stands at nearly 1.6x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was approximately $795.7 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 49.4% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

I'd go with Snap. Attention is moving to video, and Snapchat sits right in the middle of it, with nearly a billion people opening it every month. More than 30 million people pay for subscriptions, and the company just turned a profit.

Sales are growing and almost every extra revenue dollar flows through to adjusted profit. Still, there are some question marks. Advertising, still most of its revenue, is growing slowly, and its profit is thin. Stock awards dilute shareholders, and regulators are watching teen use. Still, subscriptions are growing fast and don't depend on ad budgets.

The Trade Desk helps advertisers buy ads across the open internet and streaming TV, and it’s ramping up ad buying with Netflix. But the company is clearly under pressure. Next quarter's forecast points to lower sales than a year ago. Profit fell sharply partly on layoff costs, and Amazon and Google are fighting for the same ad dollars. To its credit, it is still profitable and keeps nearly all its customers.
