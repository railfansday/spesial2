Creator Deja Foxx says 2028 presidential hopefuls are already lining up for influencers like her and James Dumoulin

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Deja Foxx thinks the next presidential race will be fought in creators’ interview chairs.

Foxx, a political creator and host of Courier’s Candid, spoke Monday at the Fortune 500 Innovation Forum in Detroit alongside James Dumoulin, the 24-year-old cofounder of the business interview channel, the School of Hard Knocks. When asked by Fortune Editorial Director Kristin Stoller what the two see in store for traditional media as compared to the creator economy, the two said it’s all about relationship building.

“My predictions are that creators are going to take up the lion’s share of our interview space with many of these candidates,” Foxx said. She said she has already seen it at two conferences in Washington and Denver over the past two weeks.

“Presidential hopefuls line up for creators to come speak to them,” she said.

Stoller opened with Pew Research Center data showing that 21% of U.S. adults regularly get news from news influencers on social media, a share that rises to 38% among those ages 18 to 29. She asked whether the two see themselves as journalists.

“I think definitely to some degree,” Dumoulin said. “Traditional media is great at building audiences, but I think creators and influencers, if you want to call it, are great at actually building relationships.” Viewers who watch a creator regularly see “the successes and the failures,” he said, which makes them more likely to trust that person.

Foxx’s first viral video was about 10 years ago, when, at 16, she confronted her senator over his vote to defund Planned Parenthood, where she had gotten care while working at a gas station with “no money, no parents, and no insurance,” she said. Young people still stop her on the street to say they saw it in middle school.

“In a moment where institutional trust is at an all-time low, relational trust is at an all-time high,” she said.

Foxx said trust is why candidates come to her. During the midterms, she went live for 90 minutes a day, five days a week, for six weeks with Courier, which she called “one of the largest values-driven newsrooms” for Gen Z and millennials. She interviewed reporters from all 20 of its state and local newsrooms, along with candidates.

“Candidates, much like businesses or billionaires, can see the value in coming in and having a personal conversation,” she said.

Dumoulin said the billionaires he interviews talk because he asks what viewers actually want to know, such as how much money they have made and what they gave up to get there. Recent guests include Bill Ackman and Ray Dalio. While he said his openings, often featuring a security guard stopping him, can be seen as “a little orchestration,” the interviews themselves are not scripted, he added. Dumoulin aims for at least 25 million views per video, and he hasn’t posted a video with fewer than 1 million views since April 2024.

On track for $36 million in revenue this year, Dumoulin said he modeled the business on what he learned from billionaires like Aliko Dangote, whom he interviewed in Nigeria in June. Dumoulin pointed to how Dangote owns each step of his supply chain, from chemicals to fertilizer to refining.

“I look at media the exact same way,” he said. His company earns money from advertising, brand deals, a paid subscription community, a content agency, and a consulting business, out of an audience of 27 million followers.

Foxx told the business leaders in the room the era of the one-off sponsored post is over. When the women’s health startup Winx Health approached her, it couldn’t afford to pay creators at the scale it needed. So she took equity instead. She advises and holds equity in the brand, which is now sold in Walgreens.

“It has everything to do with being a spokesperson and a strategist and being in it long term,” she said.

AI is also changing the game, the two creators said on stage. For Dumoulin, the pro is it allows for quicker posting. The con is it allows for quicker posting, making platforms more crowded.

“The best time to start creating content and building that brand online is now before it gets harder and harder,” he said.

Foxx said when she hears “AI likeness,” she thinks first of deepfakes. She said most are pornographic and target women, often to drive them out of politics, business, and content creation. She also said Gen Alpha, now in high school, will be the first “generative AI natives.” As a result, what she sees young people asking for is the opposite: time offline at run clubs and junk journaling meetups.

“I think the truth is they’re looking for connection and they’re looking for those in real life experiences,” she said. “And it takes organizers to build them.”
