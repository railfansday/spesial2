‘The Hunting Wives’ Season 2 Is Even More Complicated and Just as Addictive | Review

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

If you haven't actually watched The Hunting Wives, chances are you've at least heard of its existence. The drama series led by Brittany Snow and Malin Åkerman propelled itself into the pop culture zeitgeist over the summer last year thanks to a blend of murder mystery thrills and unapologetic raunch, launching as many tongue-in-cheek parodies as it earned viewers who couldn't stop binging. It's also turned out to be one of Netflix's most successful acquired titles, with the streamer not only renewing the series for a second season but officially confirming a third before its sophomore installment has even premiered.

While this means The Hunting Wives is destined to go beyond the plot of the novel of the same name by bestselling author May Cobb, that could actually be a good thing. Season 2, which drops this week on Netflix, doesn't tie up every lingering narrative thread in a neat little bow; instead, it gets even twistier and more tangled, for both its characters and the various plotlines being flung at the wall. Not all of them stick, but the ones that do keep The Hunting Wives irresistibly bingeable, thanks in large part to the show's leading ladies and a story that actually sheds some of the silliness in favor of more lies, machinations, and an increasingly complicated sapphic romance.

When The Hunting Wives Season 2 picks back up in Maple Brook, it seems like everyone has made it their mission to move on from the past. Jed Banks' (Dermot Mulroney) campaign for governor of Texas is in full swing, and having his devoted wife Margo (Akerman) along for the ride is certainly helping sway undecided voters to his side of the ballot. Meanwhile, Margo's number two BFF, Callie Baskin (Jamie Ray Newman), has officially kicked off production on her new reality show, Married Justice. The hope is that it'll rival any version of Real Housewives, thanks to the show's new producer, but Nadia Kelly (Kim Matula) has her own reasons for wanting to get close to the sheriff's wife.

As for Sophie O'Neil (Snow), she's at least trying to repair her marriage, particularly with a young son in the mix. For the moment, she and Graham (Evan Jonigkeit) are living apart, attending weekly counseling sessions and trying to tackle their biggest problems from a place of brutal honesty. Yet with Margo's face popping up everywhere on the campaign trail, and her dead brother Kyle's (Michael Aaron Milligan) phone stashed under the floorboards, it's easier said than done for Sophie to leave the past behind.

The same holds for sheriff's deputy Wanda Salazar (Karen Rodriguez), who remains convinced that there's more to Abby Jackson's (Madison Wolfe) murder that may have been covered up, much to the consternation of her partner, Flynn (Hunter Emery). While continuing to investigate the case might raise the suspicion of their boss, Sheriff Jonny (Branton Box), Salazar's dogged determination means she'll stop at nothing to uncover the truth. Also struggling in the aftermath of last season is Brad (George Ferrier), now off at college and failing to cope with the loss of his mother, Jill (Katie Lowes), who most believe was responsible for Abby's death. When a spontaneous acoustic performance at a party goes viral online, he earns the unsettling attention of country music guru Chase Brylan (John Stamos), sending him down a dangerous drug-fueled rabbit hole that he may not be able to dig himself out of.

If The Hunting Wives' first season revolved around tension-filled target practice and illicit hookups, Season 2 is... well, certainly more of the same, but with an even bigger emotional minefield to navigate. Nowhere is that more true than with the ever-evolving relationship at the story's center. When the second season kicks off, Sophie is firmly determined not to let herself fall back under Margo's spell for multiple reasons, not the least of which is her direct hand in Kyle's demise in Season 1's finale. Despite her efforts to maintain a healthy distance, both for her own well-being and for the sake of her marriage to Graham, Sophie is repeatedly drawn back into Margo's orbit, and, as one might expect, these women are still harboring some undeniable attraction for each other. What The Hunting Wives improves on, however, is all the ways in which the show emphasizes that Margo and Sophie's dynamic isn't just a means of experimentation or the most physical form of rebellion — it's a romance, albeit one made immensely thornier by the skeletons in their respective closets.

Ditching their husbands and running off into the sunset isn't really an option, though, between Jed's gubernatorial campaign requiring Margo to be in the public eye and Sophie not wanting to slide right back into her most destructive habits. Yet, as The Hunting Wives reminds us time and time again, it's impossible for anyone to fully resist Margo Banks, and that sentiment remains wholly believable in Season 2 thanks to Åkerman's alluring performance. We've seen that Margo can put up a terrifically convincing facade when she needs to — or when she finds herself backed into a corner with seemingly no means of talking her way out of things — but we've also been privy to some major vulnerability, occasional evidence of the "Mandy" who still exists deep down.

Season 2's biggest lingering question, about who actually had a hand in Abby Jackson's death, results in Åkerman deftly balancing both sides of her character, particularly once Margo is put in the position of having to choose between the biggest political seat of power and the possibility of true happiness outside the spotlight. What actually proves most compelling are the moments when Margo doesn't actually have the upper hand, but here, her increasing desperation is partly driven by a desire to keep Sophie out of the line of fire too.

Snow, for her part, is dedicated to playing her character's gradual mental and emotional unraveling at the start, as Sophie attempts to keep her most irredeemable act secret from the one person she can't stop thinking about. Even more thrilling, however, is the turn her storyline takes as Season 2 progresses. Piece by piece, Sophie begins to strip away all remaining artifice, no longer confining herself within a specific box to please others. It's a refreshing change of pace from the East Coaster being dragged around by so many external forces the first time around, and it establishes a terrific path for her that the show's writers will hopefully pursue.

With its return, The Hunting Wives is dedicated to delivering twice as many twists and turns, but there are also even more wild cards in the mix. Some additions do give the show a fun jolt of new energy, including a blast from Margo's past — Dale Dickey is always a delight to watch, and gets to have a lot of fun as biker gang leader Zelda Moffitt — as well as Jed's slimy new campaign manager, Lincoln Trout (Alex Fitzalan), who has no problem cleaning up anything that might interfere with the election results. Meanwhile, Nadia's real motivation for producing Married Justice can be correctly guessed from a mile away, but her arrival in Maple Brook gives Callie more purpose and motivation to finally move on from her on-again, off-again affair with Margo.

Yet in many ways, Season 2 ends up feeling like a narrative tug-of-war, held in limbo between formally resolving the past and any kind of forward progression. The lingering mystery of Abby Jackson's murder defines almost too many arcs this season, though some characters do benefit from keeping one foot planted in the events of Season 1. Salazar's steadfast commitment to pursuing justice results in a really sweet development in her dynamic with Flynn, for instance, but Brad's self-destructive spiral isn't nearly as compelling as the show tries to convince you it is. In fact, every time the season jumps over to his methods of coping (or not), it's hard not to wish you were back watching Margo and Sophie's infinitely more interesting storylines instead.

Where Season 2 ends, with its most irresistible relationship somehow infinitely more complicated than it's ever been, leaves The Hunting Wives facing its most intriguing future direction so far. While the prospect of moving beyond the source material might have once been cause for trepidation, it actually represents the show's best chance to thrive while leaving the small-town setting its characters and story have since eclipsed. In other words, there are even bigger and better things on the horizon for Margo Banks and Sophie O'Neil, and thanks to the confirmation that there's still more to come with Season 3, the future of The Hunting Wives is looking very bright indeed.
