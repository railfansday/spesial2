Anthropic vs. SpaceX: Here's the Better IPO Stock to Buy With $1,000 Right Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

While Anthropic is growing at an extraordinary pace, Space Exploration Technologies offers investors a more diversified way to capitalize on the artificial intelligence (AI) revolution.

By Adam Spatacco – Nov 24, 2026 at 11:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

Two of the most highly anticipated initial public offerings (IPOs) in Wall Street history took place this year. Following the public debut of Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -5.65%) in June, artificial intelligence (AI) developer Anthropic (ANT -6.54%) listed on the Nasdaq just last week.

While both companies are capitalizing on insatiable demand for AI, their respective business models are considerably different. Interestingly, the relationship between Anthropic and SpaceX is much deeper than most investors realize.

Let's explore the investment thesis for each company and assess which AI stock represents the more compelling opportunity for investors with $1,000 to deploy right now.

Anthropic's Claude family of large language models (LLMs) has emerged as a leading platform across software development, enterprise productivity, and agentic AI. The company's ability to integrate its technology into Fortune 500 workflows presents an enormous opportunity to capture enterprise software spending across the market.

During the third quarter, Anthropic generated roughly $17 billion in revenue, representing a jaw-dropping 1,127% year-over-year increase. By the end of October, the company's annualized revenue run rate had reached nearly $81 billion.

Despite this impressive top-line growth, the rest of Anthropic's financial profile raises some concerns. During the third quarter alone, the company incurred $9.9 billion in computing and infrastructure expenses while recording an operating loss of $567 million.

Perhaps most concerning is that Anthropic has $901 billion in infrastructure commitments. Although these obligations span several years, they illustrate the enormous financial resources required to remain competitive in frontier AI development.

Anthropic also faces competition from OpenAI's ChatGPT, Alphabet's Gemini, SpaceX's Grok, and an expanding ecosystem of open-weight models. As competing models become more sophisticated, Anthropic will likely face pricing pressure at exactly the time it needs to generate returns on its infrastructure investments.

While SpaceX is best known for its reusable rockets and Starlink satellite internet service, the company's AI infrastructure business is swiftly becoming its largest growth engine.

During the third quarter, SpaceX generated $14.6 billion in total revenue, up 161% year over year and 87% sequentially. Unlike Anthropic, SpaceX also generated positive operating income of $2.8 billion and net income of $4.4 billion.

The table illustrates how SpaceX's revenue growth is unfolding across its three business segments.

Management expects SpaceX to reach a $100 billion annualized revenue run rate by December, driven in large part by accelerating demand for AI infrastructure. Interestingly, one of the biggest catalysts behind this milestone is none other than Anthropic.

Earlier this year, SpaceX disclosed that Anthropic had agreed to pay $1.25 billion per month for access to its Colossus 1 data center. This gave Anthropic access to more than 300 megawatts of new capacity, powered by more than 220,000 Nvidia GPUs.

However, Anthropic's S-1 filing revealed that the relationship with SpaceX expanded substantially. Anthropic may spend up to $120.5 billion on SpaceX's compute services through 2029. Once all contracted capacity is delivered, monthly payments could reach $3.1 billion. This equates to $37.2 billion in annualized revenue and nearly 2.5x the original monthly arrangement.

Of course, SpaceX isn't immune to risk. The company also faces substantial infrastructure expenditures and must compete with hyperscalers and specialized AI cloud providers. Moreover, there is uncertainty surrounding the company's ambitions to build orbital data centers.

In addition, Anthropic's computing agreements contain termination provisions. This means investors should not treat the entire $120.5 billion as guaranteed revenue.

The biggest difference between Anthropic and SpaceX is that Elon Musk's space empire offers investors more diversified exposure to technological innovation. Anthropic's success largely hinges on its ability to monetize AI models profitably. By contrast, SpaceX can capitalize on demand for AI infrastructure alongside satellite connectivity, commercial space exploration, government contracting, and its own AI software.

This distinction is important when considering how quickly the AI landscape is evolving. SpaceX doesn't necessarily need Grok to defeat Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini to build a lucrative AI business. The company can simply monetize demand for compute from competing AI developers, regardless of which platform emerges as the market leader.

Admittedly, both SpaceX and Anthropic command premium valuations. Nevertheless, SpaceX's combination of established businesses, emerging market opportunities, and improving profitability provides a more compelling foundation for long-term investors.

Rather than betting $1,000 on a particular AI model developer like Anthropic, I'd prefer to allocate that capital to SpaceX and gain exposure to several transformative markets all at once. Ironically, investing in SpaceX could prove to be one of the more attractive ways to capitalize on Anthropic's future success.
