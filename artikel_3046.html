Warren Buffett's Most Valuable Bear Market Wisdom Every Investor Should Hear

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Even when the market pundits thought he was wrong, he turned out to be right.

By David Dierking – Nov 18, 2026 at 2:30PM EST

10 stocks we like better than S&P 500 Index ›

In October 2008, Warren Buffett publicly told people that he was buying stocks. It made sense at the time. The S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.19%) had already fallen by around 40%. We know Buffett likes bargains.

The problem is that stocks fell another 20% to 30% after his op-ed was published. Investors who followed his advice saw their portfolios suffer (at least in the short term). Market pundits wondered if he'd lost his touch.

If the world's most famous investor can't find the market's bottom, why should anybody else think they can? Most people are familiar with his "Be greedy when others are fearful" quote. But perhaps the most interesting quote from that op-ed is one no one remembers.

During his "buy American" article, some people presumed that Warren Buffett was calling a market bottom. He wasn't. In fact, he didn't seem the least bit bothered that the S&P 500 had continued to fall.

He wrote, "I can't predict the short-term movements of the stock market. I haven't the faintest idea as to whether stocks will be higher or lower a month or a year from now." Buffett was essentially saying that sometimes he'll be early when investing in something, and that's OK. He followed his process of investing when the value presented itself, but he never promised that he was investing at the bottom.

It's a great example of Buffett's mindset. He's OK being "close enough," even if he isn't able to find the market's bottom.

With the benefit of hindsight, it's easy to look back now and see what a buying opportunity the S&P 500 really was in October 2008. It wasn't easy at the time, and it was counterintuitive for many to invest in stocks after they'd fallen 40% and were still falling.

In reality, he wasn't that far off. The S&P 500 ended up bottoming in March 2009 and spent most of the next year moving higher. Those people who thought Buffett had lost his touch got a lesson in why long-term vision usually beats short-term reaction.

That's why maintaining your investing process throughout even the most painful of bear markets yields long-term results. If you continue your monthly investments, you buy shares at discounted prices. Do that long enough, and you lower your overall cost basis enough that you can enhance your long-term returns.

Instead of trying to find the absolute bottom and trying to time that market, Buffett simply concluded that the level of value was good enough and took advantage of it to improve his returns even more.

The big takeaway is not to try to time the market or wait for a market bottom. Simply keep investing despite short-term conditions and take advantage when the value is there. Buffett's patience and disciplined approach is usually the right one to follow.
