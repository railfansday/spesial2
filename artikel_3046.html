Why Arm Holdings Plunged Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

A sector-wide sell-off happened as a major industry research firm cut chip growth estimates ahead of Nvidia earnings.

Shares of Arm Holdings (ARM -8.13%) fell on Monday, down 7.9% as of 3:45 p.m. EDT.

Arm certainly wasn't alone, as pretty much the entire sector fell hard on the day due to several factors, all of which relate to uncertainty over the AI build-out and specifically the fate of Nvidia (NVDA -4.12%) within the AI ecosystem.

Arm's downturn today may have been particularly severe due to its close relationship with Nvidia as a licensor for Nvidia's CPUs.

Semiconductor stocks were down sharply today, with some, like Arm Holdings, down more than others. There are likely a few reasons driving this sell-off.

First, a prominent semiconductor and AI research firm, SemiAnalysis, reportedly issued a forecast update to institutional clients over the weekend, downgrading its prior growth forecast for AI accelerators through 2030. These cuts were relatively significant, with cuts to accelerator revenue estimates of 19%, 28%, 41%, and 40% for 2027 through 2030, respectively, compared with prior expectations.

It should be noted that even with these cuts, SemiAnalysis still forecasts growth every year through 2030, just not as much. Furthermore, the cuts seem to bring SemiAnalysis' estimates more in line with Wall Street analysts’. However, the devil is in the details: the bulk of the cuts to estimates appear to be due to Nvidia chip pricing, while competitive vendors were less affected.

Combined with the fact that Nvidia reports earnings tomorrow in what will likely be a hugely consequential report without former CEO Jensen Huang, investors are understandably de-risking by selling the entire chip ecosystem today.

For Arm specifically, Nvidia licenses Arm's technology to produce its CPUs, the latest of which is named Vera. While CPUs weren't part of SemiAnalysis' report, which focused on accelerators and GPUs, the association with Nvidia could be affecting Arm more than other chip stocks today. Oh, and starting from a high-looking valuation of 77.5 times forward earnings, even after today's sell-off, likely didn't help.

To be sure, Arm has customers other than Nvidia. In fact, virtually all major hyperscaler cloud companies license Arm's architecture for their custom CPUs. Furthermore, Arm has its own AGI CPU coming, with initial sales forecast to begin at the end of next year.

That's all very promising; however, Arm's high valuation relative to some other semiconductor companies likely means there are better buys available, especially after the sector's major sell-off over the past two months.
