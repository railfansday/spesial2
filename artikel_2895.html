Chiefs-Bills: 5 questions with Buffalo Rumblings ahead of Week 12

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Tonight, the Kansas City Chiefs face the Buffalo Bills in a week 12 Thanksgiving's night showdown. We welcome Matthew Byham of Buffalo Rumblings— our sister SBNation site covering the Bills— for Five Questions with the Enemy.

Join fellow Chiefs fans to react to every explosive play and game-changing moment in real time.

It wasn't all that surprising to me that Josh Allen and the Bills struggled to convert red zone trips into touchdowns. The Miami Dolphins under Jeff Hafley field a top-10 defense and they put together a great game plan. Unfortunately, their surprising talent is overshadowed by the mess on offense. Divisional matchups are always unpredictable, no matter any lack of familiarity (i.e., Dolphins having new coaching/roster overhaul).

The team would never admit it, but I also have to wonder if Buffalo was holding some things back a bit with a very important game only a few days later. Not necessarily in an effort to hide plays (because at this point there's a lot of tape on every team), but rather pacing themselves for the marathon ahead.

Not to take anything away from a Dolphins team that seems to be on the right track, but the Bills never trailed in that game. Head coach Joe Brady probably felt that even accumulating field goals might be too much for Miami's offense to counter.

As to your question about what the Bills need to improve? First and foremost, it would be great if tight end Dalton Kincaid (questionable w/thumb, ankle) were able to play in this one. He missed last week, and it's pretty evident that Brady's offense is vastly superior with Kincaid involved — especially as a red zone target.

But drilling down further, the Bills struggle to gain separation on routes, and it's magnified in the red zone.

Buffalo would also do well to leverage their running game more near the goal line in this one. Sometimes I believe Brady gets a bit cute trying to outsmart a defense when, time and again, they've proven almost impossible to stop in the trenches this season.

I'd like to see Brady roll out with two running backs near the goal line. It's rare for him to do that anywhere on the field, even though Ty Johnson is the team's most reliable pass catcher. Pairing Johnson with James Cook III in goal-to-go situations, while accounting for Allen's dual threat, could create real havoc for a defense. Brady just has to be willing to shift out of a few 13-personnel plays, while finding the shortest path from point A to B.

Allen should run as instinct instructs, nothing more, nothing less. I wouldn't expect Brady to confine him to one thing or another, aside from asking him to avoid arm punts in a game like this, and with weather that's going to be suspect at best. Allowing Allen to play free from constraint is the best move, while giving him options to abort anything risky.

It is interesting that Allen didn't rush a single time versus the Dolphins, but I'm left wondering if that was to protect him from any potential injury. Early on, Buffalo's offense faced 4th & 1, and they ran a traditional handoff to Cook, who went right. It was a complete paradigm shift from what we've known of the Bills' short-yardage offense. I expect him to have at least a few carries in this game, both in short- and long-yardage situations.

James Cook is vital to the Bills' success on offense. You could place bets on Buffalo leveraging Cook early and often, even if the weather was trending pleasant. Buffalo is 14-2 when Cook carries 20 times or more in a game. Under Brady, the Bills now employ a smash-mouth brand of football where they'll remain committed to the run game even if it's only getting a few yards a clip in early action.

Outsiders have begun to catch on that Brady's system is heavy on the run, despite having Allen at quarterback. Somehow, Cook seems to be fairly overlooked in many circles despite leading the league in rushing last season and roughly 30 yards out of first place this season.

Cook is slight by typical NFL standards, but he's proven tough between the tackles and moves like a hummingbird in traffic. With Cook and the maulers along the offensive line, Buffalo has been able to wear out defenses in a four-quarter battle of attrition.

That's the question everyone wants answered right now. It's fair to say that Buffalo hasn't faced the NFL's best quarterbacks in the last four games, but there's reason to be optimistic about their trajectory. That's due to confidence, which is so important in this sport.

Early in the season, defensive coordinator Jim Leonhard's group was often out of position and visibly confused pre-snap. The pass rush was the only thing working, and mostly because Greg Rousseau has been a revelation as a 3-4 outside linebacker.

The secondary has played well of late, especially since moving on from safety C.J. Gardner-Johnson (first to Damar Hamlin and now Amani Hooker — the latter via trade). Cornerback Christian Benford has been elite, working to almost completely shut down tight end Brock Bowers and wide receiver Justin Jefferson, and holding wide receiver Zay Flowers to zero burger on the stat sheet.

Make no mistake, this is a huge challenge. Kelce will always be Kelce, right (he's always so open!)? It seems like this may be the most productive group of pass catchers KC's had since Tyreek Hill wore red. Will they hold the Chiefs under their 34-point average in the last three games? It's impossible to know, but at the same time the Bills are a different caliber of team compared to the New York Jets, Atlanta Falcons (what an epic defensive drop this season), and Arizona Cardinals.

It's similar to Buffalo, where the Chiefs are either more complete or far better than the Baltimore Ravens, Minnesota Vikings, Jets, or Dolphins. I think we find out a lot more about both teams Thanksgiving night.

I know that many Chiefs fans like to say that the regular-season matchup means little, and that Buffalo has yet to defeat Mahomes and company in the postseason. However, this is a massive game simply because of the playoff bye that's at stake.

Allen and the Bills have never earned the one seed, which I believe harmed prior rosters as it relates to injury recovery/availability in the playoffs under McDermott. Currently in control of the one seed, I'd venture to guess that Allen and company are going to be locked-in and ready to take a more firm hold on the lead.

I understand you're hoping for a final score prediction, but I never pick Bills games. I think they have as good a chance as any team to defeat KC, given their season-long consistency as a dominant throwback offense. Even if the defense gives up 34 points, Allen and Cook are fully capable of besting it with 35 or more.

If it's a low-scoring affair instead due to weather, I expect Buffalo and Kansas City to each try bleeding the clock through the run game. That might be both teams' plan A regardless of the situation. The Bills are built to both bully the clock and go toe-to-toe in a shootout.

Who wins? I'd say since both teams are once again playoff favorites, they each have a pretty fair shot at postgame turkey. It comes down to the red zone, and maybe a two-point conversion or two. We'll find out what's what soon enough in what should be a thrilling Turkey Day nightcap!
