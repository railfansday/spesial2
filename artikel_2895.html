Why Analog Devices Stock Fell Nearly 20% Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The chipmaker crushed fourth-quarter estimates, so why did Wall Street hit the panic button? Let's look under the hood at the guidance quirks and spending plans behind today's sell-off.

By Anders Bylund – Nov 24, 2026 at 4:26PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Analog Devices ›

Analog Devices (ADI -17.37%) reported fourth-quarter results early Tuesday morning. The analog and mixed-signal chip designer crushed Wall Street's consensus estimates and followed up with in-line guidance.

And investors hated it. The stock fell as much as 19.7% in the morning session, recovering slightly to a 16.6% drop as of 3:23 p.m. ET.

Analog Devices' headline figures were great in Q4 2026. Earnings rose 80% year-over-year to $4.06 per share. Revenues jumped 42% to $4.37 billion. The analyst consensus had called for earnings near $3.89 per share on roughly $4.32 billion in top-line sales. The guidance ranges for next quarter were broadly in line with current Street projections.

But the drop makes sense when you dig below the usual numbers. Q1 revenue guidance is in line with Street expectations, but it's still a disappointing slowdown after a fantastic Q4 result. Operating margins will fall due to high component costs and holiday-season factory shutdowns.

Moreover, Analog Devices' chip-making facilities are running at full capacity, limiting the company's ability to actually take on more orders. More manufacturing capacity is on the way with a 150% year-over-year increase in capital expenditures, but the new production lines won't make any chips until the second half of 2028.

Analog Devices is a victim of its own roaring success. When your factories are already humming at full tilt and channel inventories are finally back to normal levels, there simply isn't much room left for upside surprises in the near term.

Instead of another beat-and-raise quarter, shareholders are getting a massive $1.6 billion to $1.9 billion capital spending bill next year -- and they won't see a single chip roll off those new lines until fiscal year 2028. The stock entered this report priced for perfection, trading at a beefy 37 times trailing earnings after Tuesday's big drop. From there, in-line guidance and a long wait for the next growth spurt were all the excuse traders needed to hit the eject button.
