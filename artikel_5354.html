Before You Raise the Camera, Listen: A Photographer's Case for Slowing Down and Using All Five Senses

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Brand strategist Jeff Stern, whose career spans P&G and Hershey, introduces the Five-Sense Lens in A Change of Scenery, now available from Palmetto Publishing.

CHARLESTON, S.C., Nov. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Palmetto Publishing has released A Change of Scenery, a travel memoir and photography collection by Jeff Stern. The book traces a journey through all seven continents, beginning with a solo trip to the American Southwest in March 2020 after a photography conference was cancelled and much of the world stopped moving.

Stern structures the book continent by continent. Each photograph appears alongside the full sensory record of the moment it was made. Readers encounter the smoky, fermented taste of kymyz offered inside a Kyrgyz yurt, frost crystals falling as a tent unzips on the Sary-Jaz Mountains at sunrise, and robusta coffee two hours after dawn in the rice paddies of Northwest Vietnam. At White Pocket in Arizona, standing on rock that pulsed terracotta and ochre beneath a cobalt sky, Stern made himself a promise about the role creative expression would play in the rest of his life.

The book's central tension is one of self-definition. Stern resists the label of landscape photographer, describing it as a box that felt polished, precise, and oddly impersonal. He calls himself a scenery enthusiast instead, arguing that scenery is not a view but a sensory event. That renaming required him to set aside the technical measures by which photographers are usually judged and to trust a creative voice he spent much of his life believing he did not have.

Jeff Stern stated, "I kept asking myself the same questions after every scene, and none of them were about aperture or shutter speed. They were: What did I hear? What did the ground feel like underfoot? I wrote this book because those questions changed what I was able to see, and I think they can do the same for anyone who spends time in nature â with or without a camera."

The book closes with the Five-Sense Lens, a framework Stern built from those recurring questions. Readers can try an interactive version of the Five-Sense Lens at https://sceneryenthusiast.com.
