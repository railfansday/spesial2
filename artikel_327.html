Which Android Phones Are Most Similar To The OnePlus 16?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

While the OnePlus 16 isn't available in the US or Europe, there are lots of good alternatives.

The OnePlus 16 debuted in China last month, and unlike previous years, it won't be released globally. With the manufacturer exiting the US and European markets, the OnePlus 16 is limited to China, and may see an eventual release in India. This is an issue if you're in the US, as OnePlus played a pivotal role in the country; while it didn't have a sizable market share, it served as an alternative to Google and Samsung's flagships, delivering high-end hardware for less than its rivals.

Interestingly, another Chinese phone maker is taking up the slack: Motorola. The Lenovo-owned manufacturer is making another play at the high-end category, and Motorola's high-end Signature, released earlier this year, proved that Motorola can offer a reliable device with powerful internals and great cameras. While the Signature didn't make it to the US, the Signature 27 is now up for pre-order in the country and hitting store shelves starting November 26. It comes with Qualcomm's latest silicon, flagship cameras and the biggest battery of any phone sold in the US. Then there's the contingent of Oppo, Xiaomi and Vivo; with these manufacturers increasingly turning their attention to the west, the OnePlus 16 omission isn't as keenly felt. If you're a OnePlus user looking to switch, here are some strong alternatives.

Motorola chose the right time to return to the flagship category in the US. The Signature 27 has everything you'd look for in a flagship, and Motorola is touting the cameras as a key point of differentiation. The phone gets a 50MP Sony LYTIA L910 main camera that's joined by a 200MP telephoto with 3x optical zoom and the ability to scale up to 100x with AI (similar to what Google and Samsung do on their phones), and a 50MP wide-angle lens. Motorola also included an event-based vision sensor (EVS); the company says this is designed to help the Signature 27 take better photos of fast-moving objects.

The Signature 27 could be one of the best Android phones when it is released. It's powered by Qualcomm's latest 2nm Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 chipset, has 12GB of RAM and 512GB of UFS 4.1 storage, IP68 and IP69 dust and water resistance, Wi‑Fi 8 compatibility, sound by Bang & Olufsen, and a massive 7,000mAh battery that should last two days between charges. Interestingly, the phone gets a tandem OLED panel (similar to the iPad Pro and the new MacBook Pro PROLED) that Motorola says is among the brightest of any phone today. I used a few Huawei phones with tandem OLED panels, and it makes a genuine difference; the tech stacks two OLED layers, delivering higher brightness levels.

OnePlus and Oppo always shared manufacturing facilities and components, and the Find X10 is the closest alternative to the OnePlus 16 if you're in Europe. The phone is now available for €1,199 ($1,342), and while there are a few differences — it runs MediaTek's Dimensity 9600M instead of Qualcomm hardware, and is slightly smaller — it's the phone you should get if you used OnePlus in the past.

That's because the Find X10 has the exact same software as recent OnePlus phones. Sure, the ColorOS name is different to OxygenOS and the latter has a few aesthetic differences, but they're identical otherwise. The tweaks are so minute that you won't even notice a difference in daily use; having tested the OnePlus 15 earlier in the year and the Find X10 Pro Max in recent weeks, the interface is indistinguishable.

OnePlus stood out not just for the value-focused hardware, but also its software efforts; earlier iterations of OxygenOS had a clean interface with plenty of customizability, and it instantly attracted enthusiasts. While its merger with ColorOS in 2021 altered the UI quite a bit, it still had one of the most fluid interfaces around.

Having said that, the software is heavily inspired by iOS' Liquid Glass design, and you'll see translucent effects in the notification pane and lock screen. Alongside the software, the Find X10 has standout cameras with Hasselblad tuning and a new imaging engine that delivers true-to-life colors. Video recording has gotten much better this generation as well, and you get a big 7,150mAh silicon battery. If you need better cameras, the Find X10 Pro Max is undoubtedly the go-to choice, but it costs €1,699 ($1,902) as of writing for the 12GB/512GB model.

The Vivo X500 Pro is another intriguing option. It has a smaller 6.36-inch AMOLED panel, but it is powered by the 2nm MediaTek Dimensity 9600 Pro, has terrific cameras with Zeiss tuning  and a decent-sized 5,300mAh battery. The difference between Oppo and Vivo's Pro models comes down to camera tuning — and having used both phones, I prefer what Vivo is doing. It is costlier at €1,399 ($1,566) for the 12GB/256GB version, but you're getting better cameras and faster hardware.

Vivo's Android 17-based OriginOS 7 has a modern interface with plenty of features, but like Oppo, there are Liquid Glass-esque design elements that may not be to everyone's liking. Other than that though, I didn't see any issues with the software or hardware, and Vivo's X500 Pro and the Find X10 are good choices if you're switching from OnePlus.

Another option is the Poco F9 Ultra. Although it doesn't have the latest hardware, it targets the value flagship niche, and it's down to £769 ($1,030) for the 16GB/512GB edition. POCO did a much better job with the cameras this generation, and I put it on par with the OnePlus 15. You also get a gargantuan 8,040mAh battery, fast charging and the cleanest onboard audio of any phone, with sound tuned by Bose.

Then there's the Redmagic 12 Pro. It has a distinct gaming-focused design, but if you want a phone with the best performance, the 12 Pro is hard to beat. You get Qualcomm's latest silicon, a huge 8,400mAh battery, 6.85-inch OLED panel with up to 185Hz refresh, and a front camera that's hidden underneath the panel, giving you an all-screen design. It runs Android 17 out of the box, and the best part is that it starts at $899 for the 12GB/256GB model. The cameras are strictly average, and the software is rather plain, but it's significantly cheaper than some of these other options.

With OnePlus' exit, the list of flagship Android options has dwindled, but with the introduction of the Signature 27 and the Redmagic 12 Pro, there are decent choices available in the US. And if you're in Europe, the Find X10, Vivo X500, and POCO F9 Ultra offer a similar caliber of hardware and software feature-set, with better cameras.
