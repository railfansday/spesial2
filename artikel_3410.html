Convinced that filmmakers lose their mojo as they get older, Quentin Tarantino has chained himself to the promise that he will quit directing films after his 10th feature. He is already trying to find loopholes in this self-imposed limit, having written two books and a stage play. Tarantino is now preparing for the release of his latest project, on which he serves purely as a writer. Longtime fans will know that before he broke out with Reservoir Dogs and Pulp Fiction, Tarantino announced his arrival on the scene as the writer of True Romance and Natural Born Killers.

Tarantino has yet to win an Academy Award for Best Director, although he has won two screenwriting Oscars for Pulp Fiction and Django Unchained. He reportedly received $20 million for writing the upcoming movie, which serves as a sequel to his most recent directorial effort, Once Upon a Time in Hollywood.

The new film brings back star Brad Pitt in the lead role, and is directed by Pitt's longtime collaborator David Fincher. Pitt and Fincher have worked together on films such as Se7en, The Curious Case of Benjamin Button, and Fight Club. Pitt and Tarantino, on the other hand, also worked together on Inglourious Basterds.
