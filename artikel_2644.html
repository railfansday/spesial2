Steelers bench Aaron Rodgers in stunning move in QB's final season

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

In a stunning development late Tuesday, Nov. 24, the Pittsburgh Steelers announced that quarterback Mason Rudolph would start in Week 12's Black Friday game against the Denver Broncos. Team PR spokesman Burt Lauten gave no reason for the change but only revealed that Aaron Rodgers, the team's QB1 since the start of the 2025 season, would be inactive for a matchup with significant playoff ramifications for both teams.

However, according to The Ringer's Tom Pelissero, the pivot from Rodgers to Rudolph is a performance-based one that was made by head coach Mike McCarthy and effectively a benching of the 42-year-old four-time league MVP.

In Pittsburgh's most recent game, a 19-16 defeat to the largely decimated Philadelphia Eagles, who had lost seven games in a row entering last Sunday's contest, Rodgers completed 30 of 44 passes but only for 227 yards and a TD. Rodgers' 6.2 yards per pass attempt is the lowest of his 22-year career since he became a full-time starter in 2008 and discounting his 2023 campaign with the New York Jets, when he suffered a season-ending Achilles tear on the first drive of opening night.

The first-year coach and native Pittsburgher spoke to reporters hours before the team's late-night announcement about Rodgers. McCarthy gave no indication he would bench a player he's been paired with most of his head coaching career, the duo spending nearly 13 seasons together with the Green Bay Packers.

"I just think the first thing comes to mind is wealth of knowledge and experience. I mean, there's really not a spot that he hasn't been in, so I think that's clearly evident. Our conversation this morning, probably half of it was going through different games over the years that reminds you of this, reminds you of that, the team we're getting ready to play," McCarthy said earlier Tuesday when asked about Rodgers.

"So, he just has a tremendous amount of experience and expertise, and I think that's a tool that his teammates and coaches are fortunate to have."

McCarthy did say as recently as Monday that "Aaron Rodgers is our quarterback" when asked about the possibility of mixing some of the other teams passers into the gameplan.

The coach also lamented the team's recent lack of offensive balance on Tuesday and the resulting impact on the pass game.

"We need to run the ball better, but we need to run it more," said McCarthy. "I mean, it's just like anything − a good run game starts with attempts. So, but we definitely got to run the ball, and then with that, the action pass game opportunities I feel will increase."

After a midseason surge in production from running back Jaylen Warren and the ground game, the Steelers have only managed 175 rushing yards total while dropping their past two games. And despite being in the top quartile of passes thrown league-wide, Pittsburgh has not gotten many explosive plays through the air nor sufficient production from wideouts like DK Metcalf.

The coach and quarterback have mostly had a very successful NFL collaboration over the course of their relationship, notably teaming to take the Packers to victory in Super Bowl 45, that storied franchise's most recent championship, to cap the 2010 season.

However the relationship had frayed to a degree by the time McCarthy was fired in Green Bay with four games left in the 2018 season. Rodgers has largely plastered over that period in subsequent years and typically speaks highly of McCarthy.

However the quarterback was caught on Prime Video's cameras in a Week 4 loss at Cleveland while screaming at McCarthy from the field and demanding a play call. The coach was subsequently caught muttering an expletive with a frustrated look on his face.

"I think it is a positive," Rodgers said in the aftermath while downplaying the incident. "Player-led teams are the best teams. I think anytime you're seeing the fire and the passion from all those guys, it's a good thing."

McCarthy has not addressed Rodgers' deactivation for the upcoming game, and Lauten's statement provided no insight into the decision. No NFL rule would preclude the Steelers from reversing course or asking Rodgers to back up Rudolph. But a decision as seismic as this one, combined with Rodgers' stature, would seem to make that highly unlikely. The Steelers also did not specify if Rodgers could return as the starter in Week 13, but that also seems improbable, barring further information.

Now in his second year with the Steelers, Rodgers had already indicated the 2026 season would be his final one. His one-year contract is set to pay him $22.5 million, fully guaranteed.

A ninth-year veteran, Rudolph was selected by the Steelers in the third round of the 2018 draft. He's spent all but one season of his NFL career in Pittsburgh, mostly serving as a backup to Rodgers, Ben Roethlisberger and Kenny Pickett. Rudolph has been one of the league's better Plan B QBs, going 8-5-1 in 14 regular-season starts for Pittsburgh.

Yet this also isn't the first time the Steelers have called him out of the bullpen late in the season. Rudolph replaced then-QB2 Mitch Trubisky late in the 2023 season when Pickett was injured. However then-coach Mike Tomlin kept Rudolph in the lineup, including Pittsburgh's playoff game at Buffalo, even when Pickett was healthy enough to play. Rudolph ended the regular season by winning all three of his starts and compiling a gaudy 118.0 passer rating. However, the overmatched Steelers were soundly beaten 31-17 by the Bills in the wild-card round, though Rudolph passed for 229 yards and two TDs.
