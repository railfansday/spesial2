Vikings Football and the Fine Art of Catastrophizing

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

In Week 11, Minnesota Vikings fans waited all day for Sunday Night. . .and almost immediately wished that they hadn't.

In what had been billed as one of the games of the year in the National Football League, the Vikings managed to hang with the San Francisco 49ers for about a quarter and a half before a late second-quarter touchdown pass from Brock Purdy and a pick-six from Kirk Cousins essentially ended things before halftime. The scoreboard said 35-10 at the end of the night, but it was over long before that.

Of course, the internet being what it is, there was much weeping and gnashing of teeth and rending of garments and those sorts of things. The euphoria that had washed over Vikings fans following the return of Kirk-a-Mania just a week prior had been swept away and replaced with the lament that Brian Flores' defense had been "exposed," and that teams around the league now had the "blueprint" to beat the league's top defense going forward.

Because that's the way things are now. Everything is either the greatest or the worst. Hell, sometimes things are both of those things simultaneously, somehow. Don't ask me how it happens, because I don't know. I'm fifty years old, and everything hurts. . .I can barely walk and chew gum at the same time, let alone reconcile those two things simultaneously.

But yes, the Niners put forth a very specific blueprint on Sunday night on how to beat the Vikings' defense. Step one of that blueprint is, apparently, "Start Brock Purdy at quarterback." That eliminates a lot of other teams from being able to emulate it. Honestly, that guy is operating on a level that pretty much no other quarterback in the league is currently operating on. I'll listen to an argument for Joe Burrow, but at this point that's just about it. He handled Flores' scheme in a way that nobody has in a very long time.

But even if the Niners did put a "blueprint" out there. . .so what?

Let me illustrate this with a slightly different example. If you play video games and you get stuck on a specific part of the game, you can pause the game or turn it off, head on over to YouTube, and find no shortage of videos of people showing you everything you need to do in order to get past that one particular spot you're having trouble with. You can probably even find four or five different people who will show you four or five different strategies to get through exactly the same map or boss or whatever it might be. You can watch all the videos your eyes can handle.

Eventually, however, you reach a point where you have to step away from the video, go back to your console, pick up your controller, and do the job yourself. And if your reflexes aren't fast enough or you hit the wrong button at the wrong time. . .well, that "blueprint" that you got from watching all those videos really doesn't do you a whole lot of good, does it? Teams can know what they have to do in order to beat the Vikings' defense all they want. How many teams can actually go out and do it?

The Vikings have lost three games so far this season. In two of those three losses, they were competitive until the late stages of the game, despite Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz looking like they had never actually seen a football before, never mind having any idea what the heck they were actually supposed to do with one. In the other one, they happened to run into the one team equipped. . .almost uniquely so. . .to beat them at what they do best. And if the Vikings should happen to meet up with that team again at some point, Flores has earned enough of my trust at this point for me to believe he's going to make enough changes to not let it happen again.

Now. . .while I can understand the disappointment after watching a game like Sunday night's loss, there's plenty of reason to be positive about the rest of the season. Am I crazy? Possibly, but that has nothing to do with football. Allow me to explain what I'm talking about as far as the Vikings are concerned.

The Vikings have seven games left on the schedule. Only two of those games will be played away from U.S. Bank Stadium. Those will be the Thursday Night Football game in Week 14 at New England and Week 17 at the New York Jets. The Patriots will be without Drake Maye and, as of now, are starting Tommy "In a Gadda" Devito at quarterback. The Jets. . .well, I was always taught that if you can't say anything nice. . .

The Vikings also only have two games remaining against teams with winning records the rest of the way. Both of them are against division opponents, and both of them are at home. Those will be on Sunday Night Football in Week 15 against Detroit (coming off a mini-bye following the New England game), and the regular-season finale against Chicago. The other three games are against Atlanta (currently 3-7), Carolina (currently 5-5), and Washington (currently 4-5-1).

According to PlayoffStatus.com, despite currently sitting behind both Detroit and Chicago in the NFC North, the Vikings have the inside track to both the NFC North title and the #2 seed in the NFC playoffs. They also have the second-highest odds of making the playoffs overall in the NFC at 93%. According to Tankathon, in terms of remaining strength of schedule, the Bears, Lions, and Packers rank #1, #5, and #7, respectively. The Vikings rank. . .#16. So the sledding is much tougher for the rest of the division, at least on paper, than it is for the purple.

You can make a pretty convincing argument that the Vikings could (should?) be favored in every game they play from here on out. The Lions and Bears play each other on Thanksgiving, so barring a tie, one of them is going to come out of Week 12 with a third loss. The Vikings have swept the Packers already, so Green Bay would need a minor miracle to overtake Minnesota for anything at this point.

The bottom line is that there's still plenty of football to be played, and while, yes, the loss to the Niners was painful to watch, it's not going to ruin the season or anything. There are adjustments to be made and, hopefully, this team will make them. Now it's time for them. . .and for us, as fans. . .to get up, dust ourselves off, and get ready for the last seven games of the year, starting with Kirkomecoming (if that's even a word, and if it's not, I just made it up) on Sunday afternoon at U.S. Bank Stadium.
