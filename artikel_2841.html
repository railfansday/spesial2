Miami Dolphins offensive line review: Tracking the guards in Week 10

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

When you lose a close game to a bad opponent and your quarterback gets strip-sacked for a defensive touchdown, you might assume the offensive line had a terrible afternoon. The reality is more complicated. The Dolphins nearly completed a 14-point comeback but ultimately fell 24-22 to the Colts in Week 10, a game that highlighted a seemingly downward trend for the team as a whole.

That may be especially true for the offensive line, where rookie left guard Kadyn Proctor and second-year right guard Jonah Savaiinaea remain key pieces of the Dolphins' future. While the offense struggled as a whole, player development doesn't stop because of one ugly afternoon, and that's especially true for two young guards expected to be part of the foundation moving forward. Each week, we take a closer look at Proctor and Savaiinaea and examine what analysts are saying about their most recent performances.

When you think of the ideal offensive line, you're probably thinking of five guys who have played together for a long time and who understand what the quarterback likes to do in certain game situations. At this moment in time, the Dolphins have none of that. In Week 5, Savaiinaea misses most of the game due to injury. Proctor misses Week 8 while in concussion protocol. Against the Colts, Andrew Meyer played the entire game at center while Aaron Brewer is treated for his own concussion, and the team is led by a brand new quarterback in Kyle McCord. With all of these injuries and new faces, you can't really be surprised when plays break down and players don't know where to go.

A big reason why the offensive line held up as well as it did – even without Brewer – is Kadyn Proctor. There's an old line about offensive linemen: Strong o-line performance slips by the average viewer – it's when you make a mistake that you stand out. Proctor has been steady most of the season, but especially lately: Fewer bad reps as the season moves along, with steady, consistent growth. His PFF grades averages out to a slightly above league-average 61.9, which is remarkable for a rookie, and his pass protection over the past two games is bordering on elite. LockedOn Dolphins' Kyle Crabbs said this week that Proctor's last three games "have graded as the three best games that he has played this season."

As for Jonah Savaiinaea, the picture is a little murkier. Crabbs has been increasingly critical of his play since the bye week, arguing in the same video that the Colts game featured several examples of the inconsistency that plagued him as a rookie. One missed block late in the game potentially cost Ollie Gordon a significant gain during Miami's final drive. That said, PFF ranked his performance against the Colts as his second-best overall and against the pass. Maybe Savaiinaea is just slow to develop (remember how long it took Austin Jackson to turn into a serviceable starter?), and the last month has raised questions about how much of Savaiinaea's hot start was sustainable. Jonah for sure has more development to do, especially against the pass (PFF grades <45 in 3 of the past 4 games). I still have hope.

One last point we touched on before: Perhaps more than anywhere else on the team, offensive line performance is a team effort, especially as opposing defenses become more nuanced and creative. Offensive-line play is often evaluated as if the five linemen operate in isolation. They don't. A failed run can be traced to a guard losing a block, but it can also be caused by a tight end missing an assignment (cough Dulcich cough), a receiver failing to seal the edge, a protection call that never gets communicated or the absence of an All-Pro center who normally keeps everything organized. Against Indianapolis, we saw examples of all of the above.

That's why a strip-sack, a stuffed run or a pressure allowed doesn't always tell the full story. When looking at o-line scoring, I try to remember that context matters.

The tracker will be updated each week throughout the season. For a more in-depth breakdown of Proctor's and Savaiinaea's performance, check out offensive line Coach Eric Smith's film analysis of the game, which you can find on his YouTube channel, Focused on Fins.
