The Philadelphia 76ers are not as good as they think they are

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Two Fridays ago, the NBA treated us to an East Group B Group Play game between the Philadelphia 76ers and Cleveland Cavaliers. At the time, the 76ers were 8-1 and thriving, while the Cavaliers were a pedestrian 4-4. Conventional wisdom tells us this should have been a cakewalk for the team looking like a title contender against one that seemed to have more problems than they could handle. To make matters worse, the Cavaliers were without their metronomic starting center (Jarrett Allen) and a key bench piece in Jaylon Tyson, while the 76ers were only really without their backup five man (Adem Bona).

But something strange happened, the Cavaliers throttled the 76ers 119-95. However, when you look a little closer, maybe this outcome wasn't that strange at all.

Less than two minutes into the game, Joel Embiid was forced to play the cat and mouse game in pick and roll. The issue is that Embiid's body has diminished so much that he can account for neither the cat nor the mouse. James Harden has the option to shoot a floater or a lob to Evan Mobley, and of course, one of the greatest passers in NBA history chooses to further solidify his legacy.

A minute later, LeBron James gets a switch on the smaller Donovan Mitchell. His gravitas warrants a double, he finds the weakness, the ball finds his way back to him, but he isn't able to convert on the open jumper, and hasn't been able to all season (22.5% from three). Also, notice how no one on the 76ers makes a serious effort to crash the offensive glass (28th in offensive rebounding rate).

Here, Mobley hits a three. James tries pushing the pace with a hit ahead pass to Jaylen Brown, but for some reason, Mr. Anti-analytics decides not to use his Herculean frame to exploit the smaller Mitchell and starts backing him down at the 3-point line – only to get ripped by Spida (the team's fourth turnover in less than five minutes of game action).

At the 6:30 mark, we get more Harden pick and roll (with a little Spain wrinkle in there). If you pause the clip, Harden and Embiid are at the free throw line at the same time. Five years ago, Embiid is stamping that ball against the glass. Unfortunately, this isn't five years ago.

Last one, Tyrese Maxey floater doesn't go in. No problem, move on to the next possession. Well, he doesn't. So, that already puts them at a disadvantage. Dean Wade does what he is supposed to do, picking up the ball as early as possible, but, instead of sprinting to the paint to take away the most valuable real estate on the floor, James and Anfernee Simons are tagging guys on the perimeter. But that only accounts for four players? Where is the fifth guy? Take another look. By the time Thomas Bryant dunks the ball, Embiid is barely crossing the halfcourt line.

By the end of the first quarter, the 76ers trailed 33-22. And before you ask, the Cavaliers only shot 2.8% better from three (25%) than them (22.2%). So, variance isn't the explanation. This also wasn't part of a back-to-back, as evidenced by Embiid and James both being in the lineup, which rules out exhaustion.

No, this is who the 76ers really are. Their offense is filled with ball stoppers, and even when they create advantages, they don't have reliable enough shooting around them to truly punish people (25th in 3-point attempts per game). Since they have so many stars, no one feels the need to scrap, which hurts them in key areas like second chance points and transition (eighth-highest transition frequency).

With so few defensive specialists out there, the center position becomes even more important. But in an era of Victor Wembanyama's and double big lineups, Embiid – a once stout guardian – is now a moving target that teams like the Cavaliers choose to pinpoint rather than avoid. His block rate is in the 29th percentile among bigs, and teams are shooting four percent BETTER around the rim when he is on the floor (per Cleaning the Glass). We glossed over Bona's absence being insignificant, but he's actually weirdly important to this team, as his athleticism, youth, and tenacity are the attributes this 76ers' group desperately needs from their center.

Funny enough, the 76ers have only lost one game since that treacherous showing. They now sit at 12-3, and are first in the Eastern Conference. But, to me, this is a facade – a paper tower waiting for a gust of wind to come and knock it all over.

Despite having the third-best win percentage and third-best odds of winning the NBA Championship (per FanDuel), the 76ers are 11th in net rating. The reason for this disparity is that they have played in and won more clutch games than any team in the association, going a perfect 10-0 in those instances.

Yes, having four premium creators (Maxey, Brown, James, and Embiid) helps you close games. But like Avon Barksdale said, "And how you ain't gonna never be slow? Never be late?" Yeah, it's good to win close games, but the more often you put yourself in these situations, the more you are exposing yourself to getting caught up. That's why great teams don't play with their food. They blow people out.

Cleaning the Glass has a nifty stat that perfectly illustrates what is going on with the 76ers: Win Differential, which calculates the number of games a team has one versus what they are expected to win based on their offensive and defensive efficiency. The 76ers are first in this stat by a mile, with 3.6 wins above expected – double the number held by the team in second place (1.7, Los Angeles Lakers).

The 76ers deserve credit for coming through in the clutch as often as they have. But if a couple bounces don't go their way, this team could easily be 9-6, or even 6-9. Then, we're having a whole different conversation. In an 82-game regular season, living on the edge like this tends to catch up with you. If and when it does, the 76ers better hope they've figured out how to juggle the obvious flaws in their roster, or else they will be faced with a harsh reality.
