Markets have a way of eventually pushing investors out of their comfort zones, testing the bounds of their convictions and challenging them to separate signal from noise amongst a firehose of news and turbulent price moves. We thought the third quarter of 2026 provided a great example of that.

The quarter opened with a meaningful air pocket in AI-related equities, as a wave of hyperscaler earnings reports raised fresh questions about whether enormous AI-related capital expenditures would generate commensurate returns. These fundamental concerns were quickly compounded by crowded positioning, elevated leverage, and forced selling, producing one of the sharpest, most concentrated sell-offs the AI trade had experienced since the current cycle began in 2023, with semiconductor and AI infrastructure-related stocks bearing the brunt of the decline.

However, as July came to a close and it became more apparent just how much of the sell-off was mechanically driven, sentiment began to stabilize. In short order, continued evidence of robust hyperscaler spending plans, resilient enterprise demand, and strong earnings results helped restore confidence in the AI investment cycle, lifting the Russell Microcap Growth Index to a fresh high by mid-August. That strength proved short-lived, though, as attention pivoted sharply toward inflation and monetary policy. Hawkish commentary from new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh raised the odds of a 25bps rate hike, which ultimately came in mid-September. From mid-August through the end of the quarter, the Russell Microcap Growth entered an approximately -10% correction.

This volatility can be dizzying, and we think at worst can induce some to mistake signal for noise at the wrong times if you don't have a robust process for navigating it. It's something we have worked very hard at over the course of 26 years, continuously refining things in an attempt to get better. We think having three disciplines to draw onâfundamental, thematic, and technicalâprovides us with an opportunity here, especially in moments where the signal can become clouded over with a cacophony of noise.

In our view, the quarter's volatility largely reflected a resetting of elevated positioning and expectations that had built during the first half of the year, rather than a broad deterioration in fundamentals across AI infrastructure and its adjacent themes. While the volatility was at times significant, we believe the underlying demand drivers across much of the ecosystem remain supportive.

During the third quarter of 2026, the Polen 5Perspectives Ultra-Small Growth Composite Portfolio (the "Portfolio") returned -17.1% (net of fees) compared to the -12.2% return of the Russell Microcap Growth Index (the "Index").

The top contributors to the Portfolio's relative performance in the quarter were Kodiak Sciences, Flotek Industries, and Hyperliquid Strategies.

Kodiak Sciences (KOD) is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company developing therapeutics for retinal diseases, including wet age-related macular degeneration (wet AMD), diabetic retinopathy, and retinal vein occlusion. Shares outperformed during the quarter, nearly tripling in a single session in late September, after the company reported positive topline results from its pivotal Phase 3 DAYBREAK study in wet AMD, in which both Zenkuda and KSI-501 met their primary endpoints with vision gains non-inferior to aflibercept (Eylea). Kodiak reported that 54% of Zenkuda patients were on six-month dosing at one year and plans to seek regulatory approval in the fourth quarter, which could potentially bring it closer to commercialization if approved.

Flotek Industries (FTK) is a technology-driven specialty chemistry and data analytics company serving the energy industry that has increasingly pivoted toward higher-margin, recurring data and power services. Shares outperformed during the quarter following a second-quarter report in which revenue grew 70% year-over-year and earnings came in well ahead of expectations, led by record revenue in its Data Analytics segment, which became the largest contributor to gross profit for the first time. Management raised full-year guidance, raised it again later in the quarter, and announced a 10-year, roughly $400 million power services contract with Puerto Rico's power authority, reinforcing investor confidence in the company's transformation.

Hyperliquid Strategies (PURR) is a digital asset treasury company focused on accumulating HYPE, the native token of the Hyperliquid blockchain, and generating yield through activities such as staking and validator participation. Shares outperformed during the quarter as the HYPE token rallied sharply, with the stock more than doubling from its July low to reach a record high in September. The company's fiscal year-end results, reported in August, showed approximately $1.9 billion of HYPE holdings as of June 30, 2026, and no debt. The company also reported continued HYPE accumulation and share repurchases during the period.

The most significant detractors from the Portfolio's relative performance in the quarter were Vishay Precision Group, Babcock & Wilcox, and CareDx (not held).

Vishay Precision Group (VPG) is a provider of precision sensors, foil resistors, and measurement and weighing systems used across industrial, semiconductor, data center, and robotics applications. Shares underperformed during the quarter, falling sharply after the company reported fiscal second-quarter results in which profitability came in well below expectations as an unfavorable product mix, delayed shipments, and foreign currency losses pressured margins and pushed the company to a net loss despite double-digit revenue growth. The sell-off overshadowed record orders for precision resistors tied to AI-related semiconductor and data center demand and a book-to-bill ratio above one, suggesting demand remained resilient despite the weaker near-term profitability.

Babcock & Wilcox (BW) is a provider of boilers, steam generation, and environmental and thermal technologies serving utility, industrial, and, increasingly, AI data center power customers. Shares underperformed during the quarter, extending a pullback from first-half highs amid litigation related to the company's disclosures concerning its Base Electron data center power project and investor concerns following an earlier equity offering. During the quarter, the company also reported second-quarter revenue growth of 130%, net income of $14.3 million compared with a net loss in the prior-year period, and backlog of $2.6 billion, while increasing its full-year adjusted EBITDA target. Shares initially rose following the earnings release but subsequently gave back those gains.

CareDx (CDNA) is a precision medicine diagnostics company providing testing services for transplant patients, with a growing presence in specialty oncology and cell therapy. Although not held in the Portfolio, the stock is a constituent of the Index and detracted from relative performance as shares rallied sharply during the quarter. The rally followed a second-quarter report in which revenue grew 52% to $132 million, earnings exceeded expectations, and management raised full-year revenue and adjusted EBITDA guidance. Favorable final Medicare coverage decisions for its transplant surveillance testing and the completed acquisition of oncology diagnostics provider Naveris added to investor enthusiasm.

The salient changes to positioning this quarter included our increased exposure to Health Care and decreased exposure to Industrials. The additions within Health Care fall under a broader theme of Health Care Innovation which encompasses Biotech and Medtech, but more specifically, themes like precision medicine/diagnostics, genomics, next generation therapeutics, and innovations that transform the delivery of care and improve on the standard of care. By contrast, the reduced exposure to Industrials came as we pared back exposures within the Electrification and AI infrastructure-related themes. While we still see a meaningful opportunity for these picks and shovels "suppliers" to the AI build out, we have increasingly used these positions as a source of funds to add to the "appliers" of AIâthe companies that are seeking to leverage AI in powerful ways to drive efficiencies and bring innovation to many sectors of the economy. We feel Health Care Innovation is among the best examples of this, though there are other notable themes under the "AI appliers" banner. As a result of these shifts in positioning, Technology remains our largest sector exposure and Health Care has overtaken Industrials as our second largest sector exposure in the Portfolio by the end of the quarter.

The volatility we experienced this quarter is consistent with what we have come to think of as "the pause that refreshes." In our view, the quarter's volatility largely reflected a resetting of elevated expectations and related positioning that had built during the first half of the year, rather than a broad deterioration in fundamentals across AI infrastructure and its adjacent themes. While the adjustment was at times significant, we believe the underlying demand drivers across much of the ecosystem remain supportive.

At the same time, our primary objective has always been optimizing around performance. In other words, given the set of conditions present today, we seek to best position the Portfolio to pursue attractive long-term performance. In that vein, we believe we are in the early stages of seeing a transition from the "suppliers" of AIâproviding infrastructure and componentsâto the "appliers", the businesses leveraging AI to make their core businesses more productive and introduce new products and services. We recognize that while we see meaningful opportunities for the suppliers in the quarters and years ahead amidst this AI capex cycle, we believe the thematic timeliness (a distinct element of our process) has changed since Spring of this year. As a result, we have incrementally shifted our positioning towards the "appliers"âa powerful theme we believe has a more meaningful growth runway.

Taking a step back, we believe the small cap universe in the US is home to some of the most innovative, dynamic companies and entrepreneurs in the world. More than anything, we have found that our performance is fueled by the accelerating pace of change and orienting around this has provided us with opportunities largely irrespective of the prevailing market environment. We have sought to build a process that harnesses change and disruption to our advantage. Amidst this paradigm shift we've experienced with AI in recent years, this has only become more important for us and we expect that to continue into the future.

Thank you for your continued interest in Polen Capital and the 5Perspectives Ultra-Small Growth Portfolio. Please do not hesitate to reach out with any questions.
