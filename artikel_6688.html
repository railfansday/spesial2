Why Do We All Love a Small-Town Holiday Romance? <i><em>Guarding Stars </em></i>Might Have the Answer

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The fantasy of falling in love in a small town has surprisingly little to do with the small town itself.

I’ll find any excuse to cozy up with a holiday rom-com, so when Guarding Stars dropped on Netflix, it was a no-brainer. Gossip Girl and Supernatural alumni Leighton Meester and Jared Padalecki are embracing one of my favorite tropes: the good old “small-town holiday romance.” Leighton’s character Hannah goes with Jared’s character Jack to his hometown pretending to be his girlfriend, while actually serving as his bodyguard. It got me thinking: Whether we’re die-hard city dwellers or seasoned travelers, why do we still love the home-for-the-holidays romance trope?

For me, the answer lies in how this specific genre taps into our own deep-rooted desire for escapism. We’ve seen these kinds of fake girlfriend or friends-to-lovers storylines play out a million times before, so their predictability is incredibly comforting to watch. We have, quite literally, seen this film before. And during the heightened chaos of the holiday season, it places viewers in the safe bubble many people crave.

Plus, the small-town setting in Guarding Stars is perfectly cozy. The two head to Jack’s obnoxiously large family home in Montana that’s been pristinely decorated in Nancy Meyers–inspired aesthetics. And much like in one of my favorite holiday movies, The Family Stone, the house itself is just part of the fantasy. The idea of being welcomed into a close-knit community with acceptance might be exactly what we dream of the holidays being like. Being loved by a family that, in the case of Hannah, is not even your own is really intoxicating.

Even the dating looks more fun. Hannah and Jack spend the whole movie dating their way around town, doing cutesy romantic things like attending the local rodeo, getting caught on a kiss cam, and mucking out horses together in the family stables. These wouldn’t be your go-to date locations in a more urban, city-based setting, so their dating experience feels playful and different from everyday life.

And Guarding Stars finds a way to elevate this trope. Hannah isn’t actively looking for a man when all of this unfolds. She’s just doing her job as a bodyguard when she inadvertently falls into a snowball of festive shenanigans. Hannah is doing fine on her own, but there’s something sweet about watching her let her guard down. Guarding Stars gently suggests that by Hannah making herself a little more vulnerable, she’s making room for more people to love and support her. As someone who tends to handle the hard stuff alone (eldest daughter syndrome, anyone?), that really resonated with me.

So often, this genre leans toward women going to the small town and being “fixed” or changed by the relationship they encounter. Their lives in the big city are unsatisfying, actually, and they really would rather work at the Christmas tree farm. That doesn’t happen here. She ends up back in New York City, and that’s where the final action set piece of the movie happens.

Guarding Stars shows that the small-town holiday romance trope can change and evolve without losing its festive magic, because it’s about comfort and connection. We can be cozy and keep our apartments in New York City, and I’m thankful for that this holiday season.
