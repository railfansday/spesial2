Teacher Sobs in Court While Admitting She Sexually Abused 16-Year-Old Student

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A substitute teacher in Kentucky sobbed in court while admitting that she sexually abused a 16-year-old student and sent him compromising pictures over the course of their relationship.

Mary “Hanna” Mattingly pleaded guilty to first-degree sexual abuse of a minor and possession of material portraying a sexual performance by a minor during a hearing on Friday, November 13, according to WKYT.

Mattingly, 32, broke down in tears as she admitted to the crimes, which took place while she was working as a Bardstown High School substitute teacher and assistant soccer coach, per the outlet.

Her inappropriate relationship with the student began on March 1, 2025, and lasted until July 31, 2025. Mattingly’s relationship with the student began when the married teacher asked him to help her clean the girl’s locker room when she was a coach, according to statements obtained by The Courier Journal.

The victim claimed that he and Mattingly would “sit down and talk about her life and what is going on.”

As their relationship progressed, they began to communicate on Snapchat “more and more.” Police also said that Mattingly sent the victim nude photos of herself over the course of their communications.

The Bardstown City Schools learned about the incident when they received an anonymous letter that “alleged inappropriate conduct between a female substitute teacher and a student,” the district previously said in a statement.

Mattingly was fired from her job in March after the district launched an investigation into the allegations and notified police, according to the Lexington Herald Leader. She was then arrested that same month.

She is scheduled to be formally sentenced on January 21, 2027. Mattingly is expected to serve five years in prison as part of her plea agreement, according to WKYT.

Additionally, Mattingly will be required to register as a sex offender when she is eventually released from prison.

“This investigation came from an anonymous tip, so if someone in the community knows something that’s going on, let us know,” Bardstown Police Department Detective Eric Williamson said of the case, according to the New York Post.

“We can’t do anything about it if you don’t tell us. We’re here to support victims. We’re going to support you, listen to you, and walk beside you to do anything we can to bring you any form of justice in your case,” he continued.
