British Airways Finally Relaunches This Major 9-Hour Route: See Map Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

British Airways (BA) has finally resumed flights between London Heathrow Airport (LHR) and Dubai International Airport (DXB), ending an eight-month suspension following the outbreak of the Iran conflict in February this year. The airline's first returning service, BA105, departed Heathrow on Tuesday and arrived in Dubai the following morning. The reinstated route covers approximately 3,416 miles (5,497 km).

However, the return represents only a fraction of BA's previous operation. Instead of three daily flights, including Airbus A380 services, the airline is offering just one daily Boeing 787 Dreamliner departure. The reduction reflects the British carrier's much wider retreat from the Middle East, where scheduled winter departures have fallen by almost 65%. Skift reports that numerous other airlines have been slow to return to Dubai, or are skipping it altogether.

British Airways resumed its London Heathrow–Dubai service on Tuesday, after more than eight months of suspension amid the conflict involving Iran. BA105 departed Heathrow at 8:33 PM, arriving in Dubai at 7:34 AM the following morning. The approximately 3,416-mile (5,497-km) route is now operated daily by a 787-10, with a westbound flight time of up to nine hours.

The capacity reduction is even more significant than the fall in frequencies. In February, before hostilities in the region began, British Airways operated three daily Heathrow-Dubai departures: BA107 using a 469-seat Airbus A380, and BA105 and BA109 using 256-seat 787-10s. Together, these offered 981 daily seats. The reinstated service provides just 256 seats, representing a 74% reduction in daily capacity.

The airline plans to increase Dubai frequencies to twice daily in summer 2027, although this would still leave departures below February's level. British Airways has said its schedule changes are intended “to provide greater clarity” for customers while it continues assessing regional conditions, and the airline's cautious restoration comes as other international carriers also delay their returns to Dubai.

Dubai is not the only Middle Eastern destination affected by British Airways' regional restructuring. Doha Hamad International Airport (DOH) remains in its winter network, but at half the capacity of flights last winter. Amman's Queen Alia International Airport (AMM) returned yesterday with four weekly departures, which is also a significant reduction over a year prior. And Riyadh Airport (RUH) is scheduled to follow on January 11, initially with daily flights, although recent attacks on Saudi airport infrastructure cast considerable doubt on whether that date will hold.

Other destinations face much longer interruptions and won't be a part of the 2026-27 winter schedule. Bahrain International Airport (BAH) is only scheduled to resume later in 2027, while Abu Dhabi International Airport (AUH) is not expected to return until winter 2027–28, a year from now. Meanwhile, Jeddah Airport (JED) has disappeared from British Airways' network entirely. This means the carrier is only operating five of the eight Middle Eastern destinations included in its 2025-26 winter schedule, with only Tel Aviv's Ben Gurion Airport (TLV) operating at normal capacity (for now).

The net result is that across these destinations, BA's scheduled departures have fallen from 1,334 to 474, representing 860 fewer flights, and a 65% reduction in Middle East flying. And with smaller aircraft operating on the routes that are reinstated, such as Dubai, the available capacity has declined even more sharply.

British Airways is among several international carriers restoring Dubai connections. Philippine Airlines, Air Astana and Aegean Airlines resumed operations during October, while the Lufthansa Group is planning a larger restoration later this month. However, the airlines already postponed their original October schedules because of ongoing regional uncertainty.

Nevertheless, numerous airlines remain absent. Singapore Airlines has extended its suspension through February 28, while KLM Royal Dutch Airlines and Finnair have canceled Dubai services through March 27. Across the Atlantic, Air Canada and United Airlines are also expected to remain away until spring.

Also, as British Airways shows, even when returns happen, they're at reduced capacity. For example, Eurowings has abandoned its planned Cologne/Bonn and Stuttgart flights this winter, sticking solely with Berlin. So while Dubai's international recovery is clearly underway, restoring its former connectivity could take considerably longer.
