Veruschka Dies: German Supermodel Of The 1960s & ’70s Who Appeared In ‘Blow-Up’ Was 87

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Veruschka, the German supermodel of the 1960s and ’70s who appeared in Michelangelo Antonioni’s iconic Swinging London film Blow-Up and was christened by photographer Richard Avedon as “the most beautiful woman in the world,” has died. She was 87.

Her death was announced by Germany newspaper Bild. A cause of death has not been disclosed, but Bild reports that she died surrounded by friends and family at her home in Berlin. Various media reports indicate November 24 as the date of her death.

Born Vera Gottliebe Anna Gräfin von Lehndorff-Steinort into German aristocracy on May 14, 1939, in Königsberg, East Prussia, Germany, Veruschka was only 5 when, according to Vogue magazine, her father, Count Heinrich von Lehndorff-Steinort, was executed following a failed 1944 plot to assassinate Adolf Hitler.

By age 20, Veruschka had moved to Florence, where she began modeling after meeting photographer Ugo Mulas. Within a couple years she had adopted the Russian-sounding name Veruschka, a moniker befitting her statuesque frame and stark, even severe beauty. By the mid-1960s she had moved to New York, was modeling for the Ford Modeling Agency and on her way to becoming what was considered Germany’s first supermodel. She was a favorite of the era’s leading photographers including Avedon, David Bailey and Irving Penn.

A brief appearance in Antonioni’s 1966 Blow-Up furthered her status in a fashion world dominated by such British models as Twiggy and Jean Shrimpton, and she would soon make the covers of Vogue, Life and other major publications of the day.

Veruschka largely retired from the fashion world by the mid-1970s, often citing a regime change at Vogue as a cause. She said in a 2009 interview that the magazine’s new editor Grace Mirabella “wanted me to be bourgeois, and I didn’t want to be that. I didn’t model for a long time after that.”

Although she only occasionally returned to modeling over the decades, Veruschka remained an iconic signifier of glamorous fashion, such as when she appeared in 2006’s Casino Royale as a poker player sharing a table with Daniel Craig’s James Bond.

Information on survivors was not immediately available.
