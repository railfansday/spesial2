CBS Officially Confirms Major Schedule Change for ‘FBI’ and ‘CIA’

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

While not as long-running on CBS as NCIS or as storied as other Dick Wolf franchises, FBI is nearing a decade of patrolling the streets of the Big Apple and has built a following all its own. In that time, the elite agents of the New York field office have teamed up with other spin-offs, changed nights, and generally experienced a lot of shuffling as the Eye sets its schedule each season. This fall, both the flagship and its spin-off, CIA, saw another change, moving up an hour with no comedy block to account for, while Harlan Coben's Final Twist Season 2 took up the later 10 p.m. slot. Those used to the old lineup won't have to wait long for things to shift back, though.

CBS set midseason premiere dates for its entire primetime slate last week, beginning on Wednesday, January 6. For FBI fans, however, the date to watch is Monday, January 25, when the original series and CIA return to continue their ninth and second seasons, respectively. The procedurals will move back into a schedule more akin to last winter, taking up the 9 p.m. and 10 p.m. time slots once more to make room for a new hour of comedy. For the first time, Ghosts will be haunting two nights a week, taking up the 8 p.m. spot on Monday, in addition to its typical Thursday slot, during Season 6, while Joe Port and Joe Wiseman's new supernatural sitcom, Eternally Yours, takes up residence at 8:30 p.m. on Mondays.

The FBI franchise shuffling is on the lighter end of CBS' changes to the schedule this winter. Starting Sunday, January 17, Cupertino, the new legal thriller from The Good Wife creators Robert and Michelle King, will air at the very end of the weekend in the 10 p.m. time slot, making way for Matlock to return for Season 3 at 10 p.m. on Thursdays alongside Elsbeth. The all-NCIS block is also getting a shake-up, with Origins returning for two episodes to close out its shortened third season before NCIS: Sydney returns for its ten-episode Season 4 run on January 26. One new scripted series is also penciled in to premiere during the midseason — Einstein. Starring Criminal Minds alum Matthew Gray Gubler, the new mystery series will slot in on Sunday at 8 p.m. starting on February 12 following the Season 5 finale of Fire Country.

Three episodes of FBI Season 9 and CIA Season 2 remain before the holidays begin, and CBS' lineup takes off until midseason. Neither series will be taking it easy heading into the break, though. For the flagship, next week's episode, "22 minutes," will see the New York agents racing to stop a drug lord who murdered a DEA informant and now plans to take out the 16-year-old girl who saw everything. While that unfolds, Isobel (Alana de la Garza) pressures Maggie (Missy Peregrym) to rein in her relationship with OA (Zeeko Zaki) after his return from going undercover with Anna Vorpe left him in a troubled state. CIA, meanwhile, will air "Burned Rubber," following Colin (Tom Ellis) and Bill (Nick Gehlfuss) on a mission to save Colin's asset from an international crime group by uncovering a high-profile car theft ring that could lead to his whereabouts.

After next week, the two branches will once again link up in the CIA episode "Past Lives" on December 7. Nikki (Necar Zadegan) calls in some backup in the form of Jubal Valentine (Jeremy Sisto) after one of her former assets and their family suddenly vanish from their suburban block. However, as the team investigates further, the question becomes whether their allegiances have flipped or if their lives are all in danger. It'll be a welcome return for Jubal, who first introduced strait-laced FBI agent Bill Goodman to the joint operation and the more complicated world of spycraft in the series premiere.

FBI Season 9 and CIA Season 2 will make their midseason premieres on CBS on January 25 at 9 p.m. ET/PT and 10 p.m. ET/PT. Stay tuned here at Collider for further updates on both series as their winter break nears.
