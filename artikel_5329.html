Sanofi is weighing a takeover bid for U.S. respiratory drugmaker Insmed

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Insmed stock rose as much as 24% on Thursday, and any deal could be weeks away, according to Bloomberg

Sanofi is considering a purchase of U.S. respiratory drugmaker Insmed and is in talks with advisers about a possible transaction, according to Bloomberg.

Deliberations remain under way, a deal could take weeks to materialize, and rival suitors might yet surface, according to Bloomberg. Sanofi said its policy is to stay silent on speculation circulating in the market, according to Reuters. Representatives for Insmed declined to comment, according to Bloomberg.

Insmed stock jumped by up to 24% during Thursday's session and was trading 20% higher as of 11:44 a.m. in New York, which put the company's worth at $31.6 billion. Sanofi stock ended the Paris session roughly 2.9% lower, leaving the company valued at about €101 billion ($111 billion).

If a deal is struck, it could become the biggest transaction the worldwide biopharma industry has seen since Pfizer $PFE acquired Seagen $SGEN in a $43 billion purchase at the end of 2023, according to Bloomberg.

Bridgewater, New Jersey-based Insmed has two approved respiratory drugs: Brinsupri, which treats non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis, and Arikayce, which treats refractory mycobacterium avium complex lung disease. Insmed said third-quarter revenue rose 268% from a year earlier to $524.4 million.

Sanofi CEO Belén Garijo said during last month's earnings call that the company planned to lean on dealmaking to help drive its expansion, according to Bloomberg. Chief Financial Officer François-Xavier Roger told reporters on a call that "the amount at stake is bigger than what we sought in the past," according to Bloomberg.

Sanofi expanded its immunology alliance with Regeneron $REGN in October, under which Regeneron will receive $1 billion upfront and be eligible for up to $7 billion in milestone payments. Dupixent, the drug at the center of that alliance, is expected to begin losing patent protection in the early 2030s.

In July, Sanofi ended development of amlitelimab in atopic dermatitis after a series of late-stage setbacks. The decision came after Garijo replaced Paul Hudson as chief executive.
