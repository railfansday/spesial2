Investment casting is essential for many high-performance metal components, particularly in aerospace and defense, from rocket propulsion systems and turbine blades to complex structural parts. Industry estimates place the global investment casting market at approximately $20 billion annually, with the U.S. representing a significant share and benefiting from strong aerospace and defense demand. For decades, the casting industry has relied on hard tooling to create molded polymer patterns. Hard tooling is expensive, slow to produce, and creates barriers to design changes once production begins.

Additive manufacturing changes that equation by producing casting patterns directly from digital designs. Customers can avoid tooling costs, dramatically reduce lead times, accelerate design iterations, and manufacture geometries that are difficult or impractical with conventional tools. The transformation of the commercial space industry illustrates what becomes possible when advances in propulsion, reusable launch systems, and modern manufacturing come together. Over the past decade, launch cadence has increased dramatically, while the cost per kilogram of reaching low Earth orbit has fallen by more than an order of magnitude compared with earlier launch systems. Additive manufacturing has contributed to this progress by enabling complex propulsion components to be developed and produced faster, with fewer parts and greater design freedom.

Importantly, when additive manufacturing is used to produce casting patterns, foundries can achieve these benefits while continuing to use the alloys, casting processes, and manufacturing expertise they already trust, minimizing the need to requalify finished components. Equally important is the print material and process that we use. Historically, as production volumes rise, the economics at some point favor a transition to injection molding with hard tooling.
