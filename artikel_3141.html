A Bear Market Is Coming Eventually. History Says This Is the Best Investing Move You Can Make.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A bear market is coming eventually, but history suggests the bigger risk to your wealth is how you react when it arrives.

By John Ballard – Nov 18, 2026 at 1:15PM EST

Bear markets are a regular part of investing. Since 1928, the S&P 500 has fallen into one -- a drop of at least 20% from a recent high -- about every 3.5 years, on average, according to Hartford Funds and Ned Davis Research. The typical bear market has erased about 35% of the index's value.

That sounds frightening, but bear markets are usually short. The average one has lasted about 9.6 months. These downturns are less a disaster than they are the cost of admission to building long-term wealth. The bigger risk is how investors react when the next one arrives.

For anyone with many years until retirement, here's why I'd stay fully invested.

According to Hartford and Ned Davis, the S&P 500 has had 28 bull markets and 27 bear markets since 1928. Through all those ups and downs, it delivered a compound annual return of about 10%, according to The Motley Fool's research. The market rises more in a bull market than it loses in a bear market.

It pays to keep investing when things look worst. Capital Group tracked the 18 biggest market declines since the Great Depression and found the S&P 500 was higher five years later every time. MFS found that after the 10 worst calendar years since 1975, a $100,000 investment was worth about $207,000 on average five years later.

Don't think that you'll be better off waiting to invest until the market moves higher. In fact, history says you'll be wealthier by staying fully invested through every down year.

J.P. Morgan Asset Management found that $10,000 invested in the S&P 500 at the start of 2006 grew to $80,619 by the end of 2025 for someone who stayed fully invested. However, missing just the 10 best days cut that to $35,866.

Take 2025: The S&P 500 fell nearly 19% in the spring, yet its best day came on April 9, in the middle of the turmoil. Missing that one day would have cut the year's 17.9% return to 7.6%.

Now, some will think: "I'll just sit in cash and wait until a down year to buy bargains and make a fortune." That's not a smart strategy either. Bear markets are infrequent. Over the past 95 years, stocks have been rising about 78% of the time, according to Hartford.

Bear markets are also hard to predict. A normal pullback of 10% or so can easily be mistaken for the start of a bigger decline.

Waiting for the perfect moment tends to backfire. Morningstar found that the average dollar in U.S. mutual funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) earned 8.7% a year over the decade ended 2025, versus 9.9% for the funds themselves, largely because of when investors bought and sold.

A bear market is, indeed, coming, but we don't know when. The best move is to stay invested and not sell when the market goes down, because missing the biggest up days after the bottom will cost you a lot more money down the road than if you just stayed invested.

Time in the market is far more important than market timing. From 1970 through September 2026, every 20-year rolling period for the S&P 500 produced a gain, according to Mellon data published by Ave Maria Mutual Funds.

Even the worst timing can still make money for patient investors. Hartford calculated that investing $10,000 at the peak just before each of the six bear markets since 1995 would have still turned $60,000 into $364,774 by the end of 2025. That beats staying in cash and earning almost nothing.

As Warren Buffett wrote in his 1991 shareholder letter, "The stock market serves as a relocation center at which money is moved from the active to the patient."

The best time to start is always when you have money available. Pick a low-cost index fund such as the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -0.21%), which tracks the S&P 500, or the Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF (VTI -0.15%), which covers the whole U.S. market.

Set up an automatic purchase of a fixed amount each month or with each paycheck, and keep it running no matter what happens in the markets. Keep money you'll need in the next few years out of stocks, and you'll be on your way to growing your net worth.
