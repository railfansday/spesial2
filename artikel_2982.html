Michael Burry Just Added to His Adobe Stake. I'd Buy Adobe Stock Now, Too.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The famous investor paid around $246 per share on Monday. Is he right about the software giant?

The next day, the creative software company's shares are even cheaper, down about 4% on Tuesday to roughly $235 as of this writing. This leaves it under what Burry paid, and about two-thirds below the stock's record close in November 2021.

Burry listed two risks to his own case: the cost of using outside artificial intelligence (AI) models, and slower growth in new subscriptions. Adobe's latest numbers show signs of both.

But I think he's right anyway, and the lower price only makes his case stronger.

Michael Burry. Image source: Getty Images.

Burry didn't just find the stock. In an April post on his Substack, he put Adobe near the top of the software and payments firms he tracks.

On Monday, he said he still thinks Adobe will adjust to AI. He acknowledged that Adobe is often held up as the software company most threatened by AI, pointing to ArtCraft, a free AI-coded clone of seven Adobe apps that has been getting attention.

His answer, according to reports on his paywalled post, is that free alternatives to tools like Photoshop have been around for decades. But Adobe has grown revenue at least 10% in each of the last four years. He credited the company's standard file formats, its big base of current users, its enterprise contracts, and its information security -- not better AI models.

Adobe's own results back up the growth claim. Revenue rose 10% to $19.4 billion in fiscal 2023, then 11% to $21.5 billion in fiscal 2024 and 11% to $23.8 billion in fiscal 2025. What's more, growth has sped up a bit this year. Revenue climbed 13% year over year to a record $6.76 billion in the fiscal third quarter of 2026 (the period ended Aug. 28, 2026). And management's target for the full fiscal year, which ends this month, points to growth of around 12%.

Burry's first risk shows up more clearly in the numbers. Adobe offers its own AI models along with partners' models, and it doesn't share what it pays outside providers. But the cost of its subscription revenue, which is mainly third-party hosting and data center costs (including the cost of running AI features), is rising fast.

Granted, that cost started from a low base. Around 90 cents of every subscription dollar was still left after covering it in fiscal Q3, compared to about 91 cents a year before. But if the acceleration continues, it might keep chipping away at Adobe's profit margins.

Slower subscription growth, Burry's second risk, is showing up too. Adobe's annualized recurring revenue (ARR), a measure of what its subscriptions bring in over a year, climbed 11.2% year over year to $27.5 billion in fiscal Q3.

But that number includes around $480 million in ARR from Semrush, a marketing software company Adobe bought in April. Without it, ARR growth comes out closer to 9%, down from 11.5% growth in fiscal 2025. Still, slowing from around 11% to about 9% is far from a stall.

At around $235, Adobe stock trades for about 10 times its non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings over the last 12 months. The S&P 500, in comparison, trades at about 25 times its earnings over the same period.

Burry argued that the valuation more than makes up for both risks, and I agree. At 10 times adjusted earnings, the market looks to be pricing Adobe as if its growth is about to stall. Nothing in the company's numbers suggests that yet.

AI costs might keep weighing on margins for a while. But revenue is still rising by double digits.

I'd buy Adobe stock at today's price, a little under where Burry bought it on Monday. I'd think about buying in a few chunks over the coming months, though, while watching whether the cost of delivering its subscriptions keeps rising faster than subscription revenue.
