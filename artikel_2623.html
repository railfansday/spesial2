Aaron Rodgers' greatest moments as Steelers release clouds future

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Aaron Rodgers has been a central figure in the NFL for more than two decades, dating back to when he was the last player left in the green room during the 2005 NFL Draft. The Green Bay Packers selected him with the No. 24 pick as Brett Favre's heir apparent, setting the stage for one of the most accomplished quarterback careers in league history. He found himself at the center of controversy long before he was viewed as a controversial figure.

Rodgers eventually lived up to the on-field expectations in Green Bay, taking over for Favre after a tense changing of the guard and leading the Packers to a Super Bowl title while winning four MVP awards. He's now facing down a difficult ending of his own, finding a measure of success with the Pittsburgh Steelers only to be confronted with the reality that few NFL careers conclude on a player's terms.

The 42-year-old was benched ahead of the Steelers' Week 12 Black Friday game against the Denver Broncos, with his declining performance cited as one of the reasons for the decision despite the team remaining in the thick of the AFC playoff race. Rodgers followed the news of his benching with a Wednesday appearance on the "The Pat McAfee Show," which featured several accusations from Rodgers launched at head coach Mike McCarthy. Rodgers was released shortly after the show aired.

Regardless of how this final chapter unfolds, Rodgers' dazzling arm talent, unforgettable comebacks and remarkable individual achievements make him one of the best quarterbacks of all-time and a defining player of this era.

Here's a look back at some of the greatest moments from his legendary career.

Rodgers' first great postseason performance occurred in a 48-21 win over the Atlanta Falcons in the 2010 NFL divisional round. Then just 27 years old and in his third full season as the Packers' starting quarterback, Rodgers torched the NFC's top seed by completing 31 of 36 passes for 366 yards and four total touchdowns (3 passing, 1 rushing) to ignite a magical Super Bowl run that included three road wins.

It seemed Rodgers might be a Super Bowl regular when he won MVP honors in 2011 after leading the Packers past the Steelers in Super Bowl XLV. Rodgers completed 24 of 39 passes for 304 yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions in the 31-25 win, carrying the Packers with their running game stalled against a stout defense. Rodgers was lauded after the game for his precision altering plays at the line of scrimmage and joined Favre and Bart Starr as Green Bay quarterbacks to earn Super Bowl MVP honors.

There's an argument that Rodgers' best regular season as an NFL quarterback occurred during the season after he won the Super Bowl. Rodgers finished the 2011 regular season having thrown for a career-best 4,643 yards, 46 touchdowns and just five interceptions over 15 appearances. He cemented his status as NFL MVP with a five-touchdown performance in a win over the Bears on Christmas day. Rodgers received 48 of the 50 possible first-place votes in the MVP race.

Rodgers came back after missing seven games due to a broken collarbone and threw a 48-yard touchdown pass to Packers wide receiver Randall Cobb on fourth down in the final minute of a 33-28 win over the Chicago Bears to clinch the NFC North title.

Rodgers completed a 61-yard Hail Mary to tight end Richard Rodgers for a touchdown on the final play of regulation, evading multiple defenders before throwing the pass. It's considered the longest Hail Mary in NFL history and was later named the play of the year by multiple national outlets. The touchdown capped off a 20-point comeback win for the Packers over the Detroit Lions on the Dec. 3, 2015 edition of "Thursday Night Football."

A little more than two months after that first Hail Mary, Rodgers had another one that was arguably more dramatic since it occurred in the playoffs and the Packers were trailing the Arizona Cardinals, 20-13. The memorable sequence began with Rodgers connecting with wide receiver Jeff Janis on a 60-yard completion for a fourth-and-20 conversion. Rodgers then uncorked another Hail Mary pass after spinning away from a pass rusher and Janis leapt in the air to catch a game-tying 41-yard touchdown on the final play of regulation. The only problem with this highlight is the Packers wound up losing the game in overtime.

Rodgers got the third Hail Mary of his career the very next postseason after inspiring a late playoff push by the Packers. Green Bay had a 4-6 record when Rodgers told reporters he thought the team could "run the table" the rest of the way. The Packers did just that, reeling off six wins in a row before facing the New York Giants in an NFC wild-card game. Rodgers then completed another 41-yard Hail Mary to Cobb in the back of the end zone just before halftime to help spark a comfortable win.

One week after another Hail Mary, Rodgers completed a throw that was arguably more improbable. Tied with the Dallas Cowboys late in an NFC divisional round playoff game and facing third-and-20, Rodgers threw on the run while rolling left and found tight end Jared Cook for a toe-dragging 36-yard pass along the sideline. It set up Mason Crosby for a game-winning 51-yard field goal on the next play as time expired as the Packers advanced to the NFC Championship game.

Perhaps the gutsiest performance of Rodgers' career happened during Week 1 of the 2018 season when he returned from a left knee injury to throw three second-half touchdown passes and beat the Chicago Bears. Rodgers had been taken off the field on a stretcher during the first half and wound up throwing a 75-yard, go-ahead touchdown to Cobb in the final minutes of the fourth quarter.

No team has been tormented more by Rodgers than the Chicago Bears, who are 5-25 facing their longtime nemesis when he played for the Packers. One of his final wins in the rivalry also produced a memorable soundbite after Rodgers scored on a 6-yard touchdown run to seal a 24-14 win over the Bears on Oct. 17, 2021. Cameras caught Rodgers taunting fans at Soldier Field by telling them, "I still own you."

Rodgers enjoyed a career resurgence under current Packers coach Matt LaFleur to start this decade. He was named league MVP after the 2020 regular season when he threw for a career-high 48 touchdowns and completed a career-best 70.7% of his passes. He then won MVP honors again in 2021, leading Green Bay to a 13-3 record and the No. 1 seed in the NFC for the second season in a row. Rodgers also passed Favre as the Packers' all-time leader in touchdown passes during the 2021 campaign.
