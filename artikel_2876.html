Xavier Hutchinson, Ja'Kobi Lane highlight Week 11's Regression Files

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Air yards, whether we like it or not — and most fantasy folks definitely do not like it — are a good measure of a pass catcher's opportunity and the type of passing volume in a given offense. That air yards don't always translate into real, edible receiving yards is just part of our frustrating little game. We make small bets here and there and hope things break right from week to week and prove our process correct more often than not. It's not any more complicated than that, even if we desperately want it to be.

Over the past few seasons I have used this space to talk mostly about guys with exceptionally high or low touchdown rates. Air yards has mostly been a side issue in the Regression Files. That will change in 2026: I want Regression Files to be your weekly go-to for (freely available) air yards data and what it means for your various fantasy football teams.

Thanks to my friend and fellow football analytics nerd Brad Beatson, we have a more organized way of not only tracking air yards data, but a measure of which pass catchers are seeing accurate targets and which guys are not. The latter phenomenon is known in nerdy football circles as Prayer Yards, or inaccurate passes that technically award a bunch of air yards to a given receiver who rarely has a chance of converting those air yards into real yards as the football sails over their head or out of bounds or wherever else.

In this space I'm going to look powerfully into total team air yards trends, players seeing plentiful prayer yards, and those who are outperforming their air yards profile and (perhaps) due for the bad kind of regression.

10 Fantasy Football Takeaways from Week 10: Emmett Johnson and Bhayshul Tuten on the rise

Emmett Johnson and Bhayshul Tuten turned in RB1 performances on the back of elite workloads in Week 10. Both running backs are poised for encores in Week 11.

Below are the teams averaging the most total team air yards per game headed into Week 11.

The fun might be over for our Panthers. They started the season as heroes for those of us who like fantasy production. They were fast-paced, they were way over their expected pass rate, and they regularly ranked among the teams putting up the most total team air yards.

That's faded of late. The Panthers over the past month rank 25th in total team air yards per game. In a Week 10 game against the Saints — a matchup I fully expected to shoot out — Carolina had a humble 246 total air yards, the 17th-most on the week. They were 5 percent below their expected pass rate, the seventh-lowest in the NFL. No Carolina pass catcher got there for fantasy purposes. It makes me question whether Jalen Coker and Darren Waller can be started in the coming weeks.

Naturally, the Panthers take on a Broncos defense this week that allows the fifth most team air yards per game.

Kirk Cousins is living proof that pass attempts (and the air yards that come with them) are a quarterback stat. Cousins poured on the air yards for most of his stint as Raiders starter this season before being traded to Minnesota last week. With Cousins gone and Fernando Mendoza under center — albeit in the worst conceivable matchup — the Raiders had a measly 219 total air yards, the seventh fewest on the week. They had averaged 317 total air yards per game coming into Week 10.

The Raiders in Week 10 against Seattle were only 1.3 percent below their expected pass rate. That wouldn't be so bad if Mendoza didn't average just seven air yards per attempt. Maybe that will rise as the rookie gets more comfortable, or when he doesn't face elite defenses.

But with Brock Bowers potentially out with yet another injury and the Raiders operating at a slow offensive pace, I'm not sure if Tre Tucker, for one, is viable in this new environment.

The Jets have trailed on 82 percent of their offensive snaps since Week 5 and their air yard totals show it. Over that miserable stretch for New York, only the Texans, Cowboys, and Packers average more total air yards per game. The Jets, in other words, are inching up on elite air yards territory.

This past week against Buffalo — a defense that regularly gives up lots of air yards — Garrett Wilson saw a whopping 193 air yards on his way to 106 yards on seven receptions. Regression Files deity Adonai Mitchell was second on the team with 119 air yards (he had three catches for 59 yards). If Omar Cooper (ankle) misses time, look for Wilson and Mitchell to split an increasingly silly number of weekly air yards in a Jets offense constantly chasing points in the second half.

The problem here is that the Jets still have the Dolphins, Chargers, and Cardinals on their schedule. Those, for now, don't look like hugely negative game script situations for the Jets. Even so, Mitchell and Wilson could be in a quietly good spot for the stretch run. Mitchell, as you might know, is running ice cold: Since Week 7, only 12 receivers have more expected fantasy points per game than the speedy deep threat. Mitchell ranks 53rd among wideouts in actual points per game over that span. So it goes.

Jacoby Brissett is living, breathing proof that pass attempts are a quarterback stat. There was no universe in which Brissett arrived in Baltimore and conducted a run-first, low-volume offense. And so on Monday night against the Chargers, the Ravens attempted a season-high 49 pass attempts while losing to the worst team in living memory.

I don't care about the Ravens as a team or a franchise. Probably you don't either. I only care about the volume their offense creates for fantasy-relevant players. We might have hit on something in Week 10: A Baltimore offense that is not limited to 20-25 throws per game and somewhere around 220 air yards per game. The Ravens on Monday night posted 480 total air yards, second only to the Cowboys in Week 10. That's right: The Brissett-led Ravens more than doubled the team's previous air yards average. It's a beautiful thing.

This could very well be a season-changing development for Ja'Kobi Lane and Mark Andrews (that it's a boon for Zay Flowers goes without saying). Lane saw a stunning 247 air yards against the Bolts; he came into the game averaging 29.5 air yards per outing. Probably he's never going to see another 200-air yard game, but Lane could have the makings of a high-variance flex play if this kind of pass volume keeps up for Baltimore. Incredibly, Ja'Marr Chase and George Pickens were the only wideouts in Week 10 with more expected fantasy production than Lane, who ranked WR44 in actual points.

This is objectively a better fantasy environment for Ravens pass catchers than it is when Lamar Jackson is healthy and under center.

With Josh Downs running 21 percent of the team's routes — and being targeted on 61 percent of those routes — the KC offense cranked up the air yards machine in Week 10's destruction of the Falcons. Patrick Mahomes, who entered the week with a league-low 5.7 air yards per attempt, averaged 9.3 air yards per throw against Atlanta. The Chiefs' 396 team air yards — a season-high — ranked fifth in Week 10. They were 11 percent over their expected pass rate and now rank second only to the Bengals in pass rate over expected.

This would, if it holds, change the prospects of a few KC pass catchers. Rashee Rice, Cyrus Allen, and Xavier Worthy each saw about 19 percent of the team's air yards in Week 10. Downs, meanwhile, had a 17 percent air yards share on his limited route participation. Worthy was the only Kansas City receiver to run more than 63 percent of the team's routes. It's a bit of a mixed bag.

If Downs' addition to the KC offense means more downfield passing for Mahomes, that could be enough to push Downs into WR3 territory and it could make Allen a deep-league flex, however high variance. Allen does have a 44 percent air yards conversion rate, a fairly high mark for downfield guys.

Cooper, in Joe Milton's first game as Eagles starter, was the unofficial air yards eater in the deleted Philly offense. He had 113 air yards (a 47 percent share) in Week 9 while leading Eagles pass catchers with an 84 percent route rate. Cooper ranked top-10 in air yards that week. It's good stuff.

Milton's haywire accuracy led to a horrifyingly low 17 percent air yards conversion rate for Cooper in Week 9. My position here is that if Cooper retains his air yards eater role in Week 11 — and DeVonta Smith remains sidelined — he should see at least a little positive regression.

Hutchinson is on a heater in a Houston offense that produces more total air yards per week than any team not quarterbacked by Jordan Love. He has touchdowns in three straight games, running 78 percent of the team's routes and leading the Texans in air yards over their past two games.

Yards have been harder to come by for Hutchinson. He has just three games with more than 50 receiving yards this season, and he's averaging 13.8 fantasy points per game over Houston's past two outings (while averaging 10.9 expected points per game). It's not a bad role, per se, but for now it requires touchdowns. That is, if Hutchinson's air yards conversion rate remains miserably low. That makes some sense for a wideout with an average depth of target of 12.5 yards.

Hutchinson profiles as an interesting option in an air yards-rich Houston offense that should be able to support at least a couple fantasy-viable pass catchers every week. And yes, he seems to be due for a visit from the (positive) Regression Reaper in the coming weeks.

When a guy sees 299 air yards in a single game, I am required by federal law to write about him. Look it up if you don't believe me. The Supreme Court has been very clear on this. I have no choice.

By now you surely know Pickens went berserk against the Niners in Week 10 to the tune of 145 yards and three touchdowns. A week after Jerry Jones said the reason the Cowboys suck this season is because Pickens isn't seeing enough looks, Pickens had an otherworldly 35 expected fantasy points (and 43 actual points). Pickens, who had averaged a mere 87 air yards per game coming into Week 10, now ranks sixth among all receivers in air yards.

The Cowboys in Week 10 were 4 percent over their expected pass rate. It was the first time they had been over their expected pass rate since Week 6. They went run-heavy in a few games and NFL pundits called it "real football" or whatever. There's always incentive (in the form of media praise) to establish the run, even though it's usually stupid and ineffective. So maybe Dallas is done with that silly experiment and we can get back to the only reason the team exists: To fill up our fantasy box scores.

This week the Cowboys take on a Titans defense that profiles as a pass funnel and allows the eighth most total team air yards per game. Pickens should have a real chance to stay red hot here. I just needed the good Regression Files readers to know how ridiculous and unsustainable his air yards profile was in Week 10.

The above bullseye rate — also known as air yards conversion rate — is over the past four games. In Week 10, Flournoy turned a 10 percent air yards share into six catches for 71 yards and a touchdown. His expected fantasy production over the past four games is about four points per game lower than his actual production. That's a red flag, as you probably know.

Flournoy has run extremely hot and has so far avoided a visit from the (negative) Regression Reaper. I'm not sure how much longer that can hold up unless the Dak Prescott logs 50 drop backs a week, every week.

WAS total team air yards allowed per game: 314 (6th) CIN total team air yards allowed per game: 295 (15th)

It's not exactly bold for a football blogger to tell you a game with a 52.5-point total is a potential shootout. This one could have an absurd amount of air yards on both sides, however, putting a few secondary and tertiary guys in play.

That includes Chig Okonkwo — coming off his hideously awful Week 10 performance — and Treylon Burks, the Commanders' best downfield threat and a verified air yards eater. On the Cincy side, consider Mike Gesicki as a deep league tight end play against a Washington defense allowing the third most fantasy points per target to tight ends this season.

TEN total team air yards allowed per game: 313 (8th) DAL total team air yards allowed per game: 301 (12th)

The Tennessee defense is going to bleed fantasy production to all comers in the coming weeks after the team traded away their only decent defensive players ahead of the NFL's trade deadline. Since Week 8, the Titans have given up the league's highest yards per play, the second-highest EPA per play, and the third-highest offensive success rate.

This is the kind of thing that forces an otherwise conservative offense to increase play volume and pass attempts as they chase points. That's exactly what we're going to get from the Titans down the stretch, including this week against the awful Dallas defense.

Since Week 7, the Titans are allowing a league-high 404 total air yards per game. That puts everyone in play on the Dallas side. Carnell Tate, if he returns from injury this week, could see a boatload of air yards, along with Elic Ayomanor, who in Week 10 ranked 16th among all receivers in air yards (87) and has accounted for 45 percent of the Titans' air yards over the past two games.
