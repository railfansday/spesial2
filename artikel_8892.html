Solveig Løvseth Shares How She Kept Going After Calamitous Kona Collapse

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

With just a kilometer to go, Solveig Løvseth (NOR) looked like she had the 2026 Ironman World Championship win in the bag. For nearly eight and a half hours, she had seemed calm, even happy, out on the brutally hot and humid course, methodically working her way through the field on the bike and surging past Taylor Knibb with five miles left on the run to move into the lead. Now, with over a minute and a half gap, she was less than five minutes away from becoming the first woman to defend her title since all-time great Daniella Ryf in 2018.

That is, until seemingly out of nowhere she started to weave back and forth across the road before finally stumbling to the ground.

“I really wanted to stop,” Løvseth told Triathlete with a giant smile on her face. “I’ve been saying in a lot of interviews that I’m not that competitive. But I guess I am a little bit competitive since I didn’t want to DNF, but I still did prioritize my health because I did lay down.”

After that first moment meeting with the pavement, Løvseth found the strength to get back up. But after a few more steps, she was back on the tarmac. She waved off medical, trying to regroup to make it to the finish line despite watching her competitors stream past her.

At the pre-race press conference, Løvseth said she didn’t really have a race plan and would just see what happens. Collapsing so close to the finish line is certainly something you can never plan for.

Her will to continue resulted in an eighth-place finish, 25 minutes and 39 seconds after Knibb broke the tape. Making it to the line ensured Løvseth also stayed in the Pro Series hunt, ultimately placing second behind Laura Philipp, who took second at the 2026 Ironman World Championship.

Not winning the race cost her $182,000 in prize money between the championship title and the Ironman Pro Series title — not to mention multiples more in sponsorship bonuses and future contracts. But speaking with Løvseth at the finish line, it’s clear she doesn’t do triathlon for the money. You’ll be hard pressed to find a competitor more gracious in defeat.
