Steelers fans spare no one amid Aaron Rodgers, Mike McCarthy feud

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

PITTSBURGH – Steelers fans expressed broad disgust with the state of the franchise in the hours before their team's Black Friday matchup with the Denver Broncos on Nov. 27 at Acrisure Stadium.

Aaron Rodgers' decision to air out his grievances with the coaching in an explosive interview on ESPN's "Pat McAfee Show" on Wednesday, Nov. 25 didn't sit well with many.

That didn't mean they were taking the side of coach Mike McCarthy either, though. Most seemed to believe Rodgers' assertions that the first-year coach was falling asleep in meetings, missing them entirely and failing to conduct basic film study.

"It's wild, man," Mark Hirschfeld said as fans gathered outside Acrisure Stadium ahead of the game. "I've just been trying to stay awake, unlike Mike McCarthy.

"People are mad, and I don't think they're mad only at Aaron Rodgers, I think they're mad at the organization as a whole. People don't like that the Steelers are a laughingstock."

The weather wasn't helping the mood. It was a raw, gray and soggy November morning in the North Shore parking lots, which were filling lazily on the morning after the Thanksgiving holiday.

Tailgates were sparse beyond some hardcore zones. And many fans were reluctant to talk to USA TODAY about the state of the Steelers franchise.

Call it a citywide hangover from 48-or-so hours of chaos that began late Tuesday, when the Steelers announced they were benching Rodgers, making him inactive and starting longtime backup Mason Rudolph for a crucial game against the 8-2 Broncos.

Rodgers responded by appearing with McAfee to rhetorically torch the organization, asserting that general manager Omar Khan and owner Art Rooney II were aware of McCarthy's alleged shortcomings and chose not to act on them by granting his preferred changes to the play calling structure. For those reasons, few were feeling sympathetic toward the front office.

"When you sign a guy like Aaron Rodgers, you are signing up for this show," Hirschfeld said. "I don't think anybody thought it would end any other way besides wild success or some short of giant show like this."

Apparently, Rooney did. He told the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette on Thursday that, because of his positive experience with Rodgers in 2025, he wasn't expecting things to go off the proverbial rails in the way they did.

Rodgers led the team to a division title a year ago. He did not play well in a playoff loss to Houston in January, but there was belief that he was the right quarterback to start the McCarthy era after he was hired to replace Mike Tomlin, because of their more than a decade of previous success together with the Green Bay Packers.

Matt Spielvogle's feelings were mixed about who was most in the wrong.

"Whenever you have a head coach actually doing this, if it's all true, the public deserves to know," the Altoona, Pa., native said. "We're paying $500 a ticket, I think the fans deserve to know what's going on inside the locker room.

"I agree with a lot of things that [Rodgers] is doing, but I also disagree with a lot of things that he's doing. I understand the whole secrecy. You can't take it outside. I played college football. I get it. But at the same time, how long is it going to go on? There's a reason why we're failing."

The awkward reality is that the Steelers haven't failed yet. Pittsburgh entered Week 12 at 6-4 and was the seventh seed in the AFC playoff race without a ton of pressure coming from other teams behind them.

The two-game losing streak that prompted the furor with Rodgers followed a four-game winning streak that had fans dreaming of the franchise's first playoff win since 2016.

The offense had shown gradual improvement and defensive coordinator Patrick Graham's soft zone coverage scheme had helped the highly-paid defense led by Cam Heyward and T.J. Watt regain some footing compared to last season.

NFL discourse tends to exist in the present tense, though. When asked whether McCarthy should remain in place as coach beyond this season, most fans demurred or expressed a desire for the franchise to move on.

"Personally, I think let him finish out the year, then fire him," fan Santino Fischio said just outside the North Shore's Kamin Science Center, adjacent to Acrisure Stadium. "But do I think we're going to do that? No."

In fact, Rooney told the Post-Gazette that he has "100% confidence" in the coach, despite Rodgers' assertions that he had largely lost the locker room with his conduct.

"I figured that's how they were going to do it," Fischio said. "I feel like they were kind of using [Rodgers] like a scapegoat."
