Ranking West Brom’s 5 biggest rivals from least to most fierce

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

West Brom are one of the most historic clubs in English football, and having spent the majority of their existence competing in the top two divisions, there have been plenty of rivalries formed along the way.

Based in the Black Country, the Baggies have several local opponents who they have developed a strong dislike for, with geographical proximity, historic encounters and some particularly hostile moments between supporters all playing their part in creating such intense rivalries.

Of course, there is one particular club that stands above the rest when it comes to Albion's biggest rivals, although there has historically been some debate among supporters over who is considered their biggest enemy.

So, with this in mind, FLW has ranked the five biggest rivals of West Brom, starting with the least fierce and working towards the club that Baggies supporters have the most hate for.

First up are Coventry City, who perhaps have the least fierce rivalry with West Brom out of the five clubs on this list.

The Sky Blues are another West Midlands outfit, and with both clubs having spent plenty of time competing in the Championship over recent years, there is certainly an element of competitive tension whenever the two sides lock horns.

However, unlike some of Albion's other rivals, there isn't a particularly defining event that has created a huge amount of animosity between the two fanbases.

Instead, the relationship is largely geographical and the history of the two clubs competing against one another so often.

There have been some memorable encounters between the two, with West Brom enjoying a particularly strong record against Coventry in recent seasons, winning each of their three league meetings before December 2024.

And while the Sky Blues have enjoyed their own spells of success, there is arguably more attention placed on their encounters with Leicester City and Aston Villa than there is with the Baggies.

The same can be said from an Albion perspective, with Coventry not necessarily the first club that supporters would name when discussing their most disliked opponents.

Nevertheless, there is always a little extra spice when two West Midlands clubs come together, and with Coventry having now secured promotion to the Premier League for the 2026-27 campaign, there is an added incentive for West Brom to eventually join them back in the top-flight.

Next up are Stoke City, who have a slightly more heated relationship with West Brom.

The two clubs are separated by a reasonable distance, but having spent considerable periods competing against one another in both the Premier League and Championship, there has been plenty of opportunity for a rivalry to develop.

One particular aspect that Albion supporters will recognise is the chant from Stoke fans which mocks West Brom's famous 'Boing Boing' celebration, adding to it with a rather less complimentary version, as shown from an old tweet by darts icon and Stoke fan Adrian Lewis.

Such taunts have stirred up Albion’s hatred towards Stoke fans, while a historical reason is also at large for such hostility.

It has been a fixture that has often been a difficult one for Albion to come out on top in. Stoke have generally had the upper hand in the overall head-to-head record, winning 65 of the 159 meetings between the clubs, compared to West Brom's 53 victories, with the other 41 games ending in draws.

However, the balance has shifted considerably in more recent years. Albion are unbeaten in their last eight league meetings with the Potters, recording four wins and four draws since November 2022. That includes victories at the Bet365 Stadium in April 2023 and August 2024, as well as a 1-0 away win in August 2025.

While Stoke have historically been a difficult opponent for the Baggies to overcome, there is a strong argument that West Brom have wrestled back control of this fixture in recent seasons, therefore dampening down some of the heat for these clashes.

Moving into the top three, Birmingham City are undoubtedly one of West Brom's biggest rivals, with the two clubs sharing a particularly fiery relationship.

The Hawthorns and St Andrew's are separated by only a handful of miles, meaning there is plenty of local pride at stake whenever the two sides meet.

However, unlike Coventry and Stoke, there have been some particularly significant moments between the two fanbases that have helped intensify the rivalry.

One of the more notable recent encounters came in February 2024, when the two sides met at The Hawthorns just a week after the infamous FA Cup clash between West Brom and Wolves.

And while the Birmingham fixture passed without the same level of crowd disorder, there was still plenty of needle from the supporters, with Birmingham midfielder Juninho Bacuna reporting racial abuse during the game.

There have been plenty of other competitive meetings between the clubs throughout their histories, including West Brom's victory over Birmingham in the 1931 FA Cup final.

However, the rivalry is perhaps slightly less fierce than the two clubs further up this list, with Birmingham's biggest enemy being Aston Villa rather than the Baggies.

Nevertheless, given the close proximity of the two grounds and the history of competitive meetings, there is no doubt that Birmingham are a club Albion supporters always want to secure a victory over.

Moving into the top two, there can be little doubt that Aston Villa are one of West Brom's biggest rivals.

The two clubs first met competitively back in 1882, and have since shared plenty of memorable encounters, including three FA Cup finals between 1887 and 1895.

The rivalry really intensified during the late 1970s and early 1980s, when both clubs were competing towards the top end of English football and regularly battling for Midlands supremacy.

There have also been some particularly memorable modern encounters, notably the 2015 FA Cup quarter-final at Villa Park, and perhaps one of the most painful recent moments for Albion supporters in the 2019 Championship play-off semi-final.

A defeat on penalties was the sort of result that Baggies supporters would have hated to experience against any opponent, but having it happen against Villa made it all the more difficult to stomach.

Some West Brom supporters will still consider Villa to be their biggest rivals, particularly given the historic significance of the fixture and the regular meetings between the clubs at English football’s highest level.

However, with the two sides having spent lengthy periods apart in recent years, the rivalry has perhaps lost some of its previous intensity.

This is undoubtedly the biggest rivalry of the lot, with Wolves and West Brom sharing one of the most fiercely contested derbies in English football.

The Black Country derby has been a major part of the region's footballing identity for well over a century, with the two clubs separated by just a handful of miles.

The rivalry dates back to the 1880s, and with both clubs having been founding members of the Football League in 1888, there has been no shortage of history between the two sides.

Perhaps one of the most significant periods in the rivalry came during the 1953-54 campaign, when Wolves and West Brom finished as the top two clubs in the country.

Albion enjoyed a memorable season by winning the FA Cup, but Wolves ultimately pipped their Black Country neighbours to the First Division title, finishing four points ahead of the Baggies.

And while that particular campaign is now several decades in the past, the rivalry has continued to produce some memorable moments in more recent times.

One of the most famous encounters for West Brom supporters came in February 2012, when the Baggies travelled to Molineux and produced a stunning 5-1 victory in Wolves’ backyard.

Peter Odemwingie was the star of the show that afternoon, scoring a hat-trick to help Albion secure one of their most memorable derby victories in the Premier League era.

However, it is perhaps the behaviour of supporters that has contributed most to the intensity of this particular rivalry.

There have been longstanding tensions between sections of both fanbases, with chants, confrontations and incidents away from the pitch all adding to the hostility surrounding the fixture.

One of the most infamous examples came in January 2024, when the two sides met in the FA Cup fourth round at The Hawthorns.

Wolves eventually secured a 2-0 victory, but the result was overshadowed by serious crowd disorder which saw the match suspended for 38 minutes.

Fighting broke out in the stands after Matheus Cunha doubled the visitors' advantage, with missiles thrown and supporters spilling onto the pitch as police and stewards attempted to restore order.

It was an incredibly ugly episode in the history of the Black Country derby, but it also demonstrated just how much emotion and hostility surrounds this fixture, which was also on show in their opening meeting at Molineux this season, with the Baggies suffering a gut-wrenching 1-0 defeat.

Albion will be hopeful of getting their revenge later on in the campaign when the pair meet next February, with The Hawthorns expected to be a cauldron of noise when they welcome their bitter rivals to B71.

There is simply no other opponent that brings the same level of hostility, local pride and anticipation as Wolves, with most West Brom supporters circling the clashes with them when the fixture list drops.
