Hull City secured a huge three points in the Premier League by snatching a 2-0 win late on in their clash against Brighton.

The Tigers scored two late goals, courtesy of Ollie McBurnie, to dent their opponents' unlikely title challenge and secure a valuable win at the MKM Stadium.

Brighton were on top throughout and had numerous big chances to score, but failed to beat Hull goalkeeper Konstantinos Tzolakis, who enjoyed an outstanding performance.

Joao Costinha was sent off shortly before added time after receiving a second yellow card for a foul on Sorba Thomas, which may have changed the game.

Hull took advantage, with their goals coming in the 92nd and 95th minutes to snatch all three points after a hard-working display.

McBurnie ended the game in ecstatic fashion with two late goals, but it was not all plain sailing for the striker, who was forced into a battle with the Brighton defence.

His individual battle with Luka Vuskovic was highlighted as the Brighton defender risked a red card, but it was Pascal Gross who got under the striker's skin.

Shortly before half-time, the midfielder went down inside the six-yard box and was adamant he should have been awareded a penalty.

Instead, Brighton had a corner, and Gross' protests clearly rubbed McBurnie up the wrong way as he ran over to have a stern word with the Seagulls' captain.

The two players stood face-to-face, exchanging words, before being ordered to separate by the referee and both ultimately went without punishment.

Thomas was brought on at half time by Sergej Jakirovic, who believed the winger would change the game, and he was involved in earning a foul and getting Costinha sent off.

However, his introduction to the game and the second half was delayed slighly by a disgareement over his shin pads, with Tony Harrington reluctant to let him on.

The Wales international eventually called over a teammate to show his own protection in comparison, as he attempted to talk the fourth official around.

Ultimately, with his small shin pads now more on display, the winger was allowed to head onto the pitch and get on with the game despite the nit-picking of the officials.

Ultimately, the goalkeepers both played a significant part in this game, and it came against a backdrop of the pair's last meeting while on international duty.

Verbruggen's Netherlands side put seven goals past Tzolakis' Greece during the break, but the tides had turned after both returned to their club sides.

This time, it was the Greece international who came out on top with a man-of-the-match performance at the MKM Stadium, as he outshone his opposite number.

Hurzeler, meanwhile, has been criticised for his role in Hull's second goal which put the game to bed after he was slow coming off his line and allowed Mohamed Belloumi to break through.

Belloumi then squared it to McBurnie who slotted home, winning the game for his side, and ensuring his goalkeeper would receive the plaudits for what was a huge win.

Brighton ended up on the wrong end of the scoreline at full-time, and it proved to be a frustrating game throughout, with the coaches displaying their grievances.

The Seagulls missed chance after chance, largely due to the goalkeeper but also due to their own wastefulness, and eventually failed to find the net.

There were also a number of decisions which went against Brighton, adding to their frustrations, both in the stands and in the dugout.

Hurzeler's number-two Jonas Scheuermann ultimately received a yellow card for barking in the referees ear, with things refusing to go in favour of his side.

Missed chances and 50-50 decisions utlimately went against Brighton, and the assistant coach was eventually sanctioned for his protests.
