Why CoinShares Stock Jumped 14% Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

CoinShares sold its Ethereum to buy back its own stock. Investors seem to like that trade.

By Anders Bylund – Nov 18, 2026 at 12:47PM EST

10 stocks we like better than CoinShares Plc ›

CoinShares (CSHR +11.90%) stock jumped as much as 14.3% on Wednesday morning, peaking at 10:50 a.m. ET. The digital asset management company reported third-quarter results early in the morning, crushing some analyst estimates and missing others. Overall, investors liked what they saw.

The average analyst expected unadjusted earnings of $0.17 per share on roughly $31.1 million in revenues. CoinShares reported earnings of $0.28 per share, up from $0.25 per share in the year-ago period. Revenues fell 45% year-over-year to $27.7 million.

The top-line figure looks bad, and it reflects substantially lower prices in the cryptocurrency market. But the quarter ended on a much brighter note than it started. A late crypto rally in the reporting period and $125.0 million in net inflows into CoinShares funds pushed assets under management (AUM) to $7.4 billion, up from $5.5 billion at the end of June.

The capital markets segment did the heavy lifting. Revenue and gains from lending and trading rose to $17.8 million, up from $4.9 million in the second quarter. A $14.4 million gain on the company's own crypto holdings also padded net income, which came in at $39.6 million.

CoinShares also sold its entire corporate Ethereum (ETH -2.03%) position and half of its Solana (SOL +0.29%) holdings. The move raised $19.3 million to fund the company's $40 million share buyback program.

A crypto company selling crypto to buy back its own stock is a pretty clear statement about where management sees value. Spoiler alert: it's not in altcoins right now. CoinShares' corporate treasury is now mostly Bitcoin (BTC -1.72%) and classic cash with a light sprinkle of Solana tokens.

It's hard to argue on paper. CoinShares' stock is down 67% over the past year, and its $470.5 million market cap still trails its $492.4 million in net assets. A Russell 2000 listing after the Dec. 11 close should put the stock on index funds' shopping lists, too. It makes sense to buy back the discounted stock before the expected index bump.

The core business remains tied to crypto prices, though. Asset management fees fell 48% year over year, and a further slide in digital assets since quarter-end could weigh on fourth-quarter results. The big buyback is still a gamble.
