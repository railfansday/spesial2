Littler values 'titles and legacy' over money as he launches new initiative

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Luke Littler and The Kings Trust join forces to help young people find employment through practical job skills and work experience; Littler to defend World Darts Championship title live on Sky Sports from December 11 after second-round exit at Grand Slam of Darts

Luke Littler says "titles and legacy" are more important to him than money after joining forces with a youth unemployment charity to help those in his native north west of England.

Two-time world champion Littler, 19, and The Kings Trust have established the 'Luke Littler Opportunity Fund', which aims to assist young people in finding employment through practical job skills and work experience.

The world No 1, who will look for a hat-trick of titles at Alexandra Palace live on Sky Sports from December 11, told the Big Issue after becoming its youngest guest editor: "I just love what I do. Once that love for the game goes, then I'll obviously stop, whenever that is.

"It's just the drive, the momentum you get when I'm going on stage, knowing that if I hit this double, you're into the final or I'm world champion.

"Many people are like, 'oh, money's got to his head', but it's never been about the money. I've never grown up with a lot of money, so now I've got it, I'm not going to go and use it all.

"I want titles and legacy in the game of darts."

On his new initiative to help reduce youth unemployment, Littler, who has become an ambassador for The King's Trust, added: "Help is needed all across the country. I'd like to focus on the north because I've seen many people struggling.

"The King's Trust gives opportunities to people, whether it's to build a bit of confidence or to find their feet and find a job.

"I just want to get out there, be an ambassador and help people out. Darts has given me a path in life and opened up so many amazing opportunities for me at a young age.

"Not everyone gets the same start and I want this fund to be something we can build over the coming years and hopefully get lots of people behind it so we can make a real difference to young people across the north west."

Littler's friends also contributed to the Big Issue, including Manchester United and England footballer Ella Toone and fellow darts player Beau Greaves.

Littler said: "I've really enjoyed guest editing and getting some familiar faces involved to share their own stories.

"Hopefully, together we can get more people talking about the importance of giving young people opportunities, whatever their background."
