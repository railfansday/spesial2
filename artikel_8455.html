Personal Finance (Not Investing) • Re: Defined benefit (pension) versus defined contribution

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

  • Inflation adjustment maxes at 1.5%/year
  • If she leaves service early she can cash out her contributions + a modest (~2%) appreciation
For me these two are reasons against the pension plan. The inflation protection is weak. Some plans may have similar annual increases but in parallel provide an inflation-adjusted floor, for example the LA Schools which promises that after inflation the pension will be at least 85% of the starting value. Since this one does not, in a 20-30+ years retirement even moderate inflation would erode the pension significantly.

Second, the 2% inflation if she leaves and cashes out may be break-even or worse after inflation. While markets are not guaranteed to have positive returns over any length of time, historically the index-fund alternatives in the Defined Contribution choice would have better returns. But to walk that back a bit, even for a 30 year holding period which averages real returns of about 6.5-7% a year the worst return in the US data is about 1.7%. And past performance does not guarantee future results so it is possible the returns over the next 30 years could be below the worst previously seen.

If it was me I'd take the Defined Contribution choice, invest 100% in stock until within a few years of retirement, and put 40% of the investment international.
