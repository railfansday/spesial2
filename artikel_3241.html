Hollywood Heavyweights Urge Dems to Dump Schumer

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

A choir of celebrities and donors are urging Democrats to jettison New York Sen. Chuck Schumer from his leadership position as the party prepares to retake control of the Senate in January.

More than two dozen actors and Democratic donors signed a letter calling on members of the incoming majority to replace Schumer with his challenger, Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, according to The New York Times.

“The Senate Democratic leader will be chosen behind closed doors, by secret ballot,” authors of the letter wrote, per the Times. “No senator will ever have to tell voters, or us, how they voted. We believe a decision this consequential deserves public accountability.”

After a long stretch in either the minority or holding the very slimmest majority in the chamber, Democrats will return to the Senate with a commanding 56-seat majority, the largest the party has had in more than 20 years.

But despite Democrats’ impressive electoral gains, the party — and Schumer in particular – remain deeply unpopular. According to a recent Economist/YouGov poll, 50 percent of Americans surveyed held a negative view of Schumer — a higher unfavorability rating than Jim Jordan, Tucker Carlson, or Stephen Miller. (Only JD Vance was viewed more negatively, with 52 percent of respondents holding unfavorable views of the vice president.)

Liberal activists are particularly sour on Schumer. Indivisible, MoveOn, the Working Families Party, and Our Revolution are among those backing Van Hollen over Schumer, who they say has been an ineffective opposition leader.

In an interview with the Times, the comedian Chelsea Handler, who signed the letter, described the 2026 election as an inflection point. “This is an opportunity. This is a window… We need to, like, get the ball and run with it. And I don’t believe that Chuck Schumer is going to do that.”

It’s unclear who specifically the letter was addressed to; the Times does not say in its story. Van Hollen’s office was unable to provide a copy or any additional information about the letter, including the names of the more than two dozen other celebrities and donors who are said to have signed it.

Schumer’s bid currently boasts the public support of at least 16 senators, compared to the six who have openly pledged their support to Van Hollen. Incoming Sens. Abdul El-Sayed and Peggy Flanagan, whose respective rivals Schumer backed in their primaries, have both pledged to support Van Hollen. Schumer, for his part, was recently filmed on Capitol Hill affirming that he had the 29 votes necessary to secure the position. That video, however — in which the New York senator is captured hunched over, slightly hobbling — invited a fresh round of questions about the 76-year-old’s fitness for the role of the face of the Democratic Party in the Senate.

The vote is set to take place at 9:30 a.m. on Dec. 1, as early as allowed by Democratic Conference’s rules. Schumer has denied prior reporting that he was trying to move the vote up.
