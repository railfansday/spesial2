Duran Duran’s Andy Taylor Was the Wildest of the Wild Boys

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Farewell to Andy Taylor, the bad boy in Duran Duran, the loose cannon, the purest rocker in the band. He was the guitarist who brought his own style of glam-rock flash to their classic Eighties hits, from “Hungry Like the Wolf” to “Girls on Film” to “Rio.” He gave them an aggressive edge that no other New Romantic band had — part of the reason these songs never fade into the past. The band announced his death today, at 65, after a long fight with prostate cancer.

The tragic news comes at a tough time for Duran Duran fans, just days after original drummer Roger Taylor announced he was stepping down because of his tinnitus. That leaves bassman John Taylor as the last of the band’s three Taylors. (None of whom were related.)

“He brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music,” Duran Duran said in a statement. “His playing drove the songs that took us around the world and with a dry wit that was matched only by his wonderfully unsentimental outlook on life he kept us grounded and gave the band its edge.”

Andy Taylor was the original guitarist from their early days, but he quit in 1986, at the peak of their fame. “It’s like one of those rat wheels, isn’t it?” he said in the 2018 documentary Duran Duran: There’s Something You Should Know. “And you just gotta be King Rat sometimes and say, ‘Bollocks! I’m jumping off!’”

He grew up in a fishing town near Newcastle and played his earliest gigs at military bases around Europe. But he took the train down to Birmingham when he saw a “guitarist wanted” ad in the British music paper Melody Maker. In the band’s first Rolling Stone cover story, in 1984, he told the story of his audition for Duran Duran. “They were quite blatant,” Andy said. “They said, ‘We’re poseurs. We want a good-looking poseur band.’” But for Andy, that was no problem. “I said, ‘Good, because I like dressing up and I love wearing makeup.’”

These were fighting words, calculated to shock and outrage any old-school rock traditionalists who might be reading. And it worked — Duran Duran were a massively divisive band, especially in America. But it sums up the brazen attitude he brought to the band. Brash, Andy was. In the classic 1984 documentary Sing Blue Silver, a film that Duran Duran fans watch the way cineastes watch Citizen Kane, there’s a famous scene at a press conference. An innocent question: When did they start playing their instruments? The reporter unwisely asks Andy to answer first; naturally, he turns it into a crass dick joke. “I learned to use my instrument,” Andy says, pausing as the room breaks up in laughter, “when I was five, actually, but it was plastic at the time. I got a real one when I was 11.”

Duran Duran were the band who combined disco and punk, at a time when it simply wasn’t done — as they always said, their mission was to fuse Chic with the Sex Pistols. Andy had more old-school tastes than the others — he was more into the Doors and Thin Lizzy. “Andy didn’t look like one of us,” John Taylor once recalled. “He didn’t talk or think like one of us.” But his guitar brought edgy aggro into the mix; he made sure nobody would mistake them for just another synth band. He’s loud and clear right from the intro of their debut single, “Planet Earth,” and brings a sense of danger to “The Wild Boys” and “Union of the Snake.”

This guy was trouble, no doubt, but fans loved him. He was notoriously the combative one in the band. “Spikey hair & spikey personality,” Simon Le Bon recalled in a loving tribute on the band’s website. “Andy didn’t mince his words; he didn’t suffer fools gladly. Andy never took the easy musical route. Andy played up his feisty reputation in his salty memoir Wild Boy: My Life In Duran Duran. He takes pleasure in recounting his battles with his bandmates, like when he claims Nick Rhodes got seasick on the yacht making the “Rio” video. He also takes their videos apart. “‘New Moon on Monday’ was our least favorite video of all,” he wrote. “Everyone in the band hates it, particularly the dreadful scene at the end where we all dance together. Even today, I cringe and leave the room if anyone plays the video.”

But he was always a rock star. In the video for “A View to a Kill,” Andy’s a terrorist out to blow up the Eiffel Tower, but he poses as an anonymous blind tourist, with his walking cane. He does his best to blend in with the crowd unnoticed. It’s hard not to laugh: if there’s one thing a member of Duran Duran should never attempt to do, it’s play anonymous.

He formed the Power Station with bassist John Taylor, along with Chic drummer Tony Thompson and English smoothie Robert Palmer. It was a vehicle for him to play all the long, flashy guitar-hero solos that didn’t fit on Duran Duran records, in the hit “Some Like It Hot” as well as a sleazed-up headbanger bash at T. Rex’s “Bang a Gong.” Andy also played guitar on Palmer’s giant solo hit “Addicted to Love,” getting greasier than ever. (Before Palmer joined the Power Station, he was a polite soft-rocker best known for hits like “Every Kinda People”; Andy helped teach him to loosen up.)

The other three members of Duran Duran went off to form their own band, the far artier Arcadia, making the excellent So Red the Rose. But Duran Duran was splitting apart — when they got together to play Live Aid in the summer of 1985, he could barely stand being in the same room as the others. He made his big move for rock stardom with “Take It Easy,” a giant MTV hit from the soundtrack of the gymnastics-themed teen flick American Anthem, a song that took the “Bang a Gong” guitar riff to hair-metal levels. He dropped a 1987 solo album, Thunder, with the single “Don’t Let Me Die Young,” working with the Sex Pistols’ Steve Jones, but had more success behind the scenes, hooking up with Rod Stewart. He played on Rod hits like “Forever Young” and “Lost in You,” which he also co-wrote. His 1990 solo album Dangerous was a weird mix of bar-band standards by the likes of Bad Company, AC/DC, and Thin Lizzy. It was definitely strange to hear the guitarist from “Girls on Film” sing “Feel Like Makin’ Love.”

Taylor made a welcome return to Duran Duran in 2001, when the band did their wildly successful reunion. It gave longtime fans a chance to see the original Fab Five lineup together for the first time since Live Aid. He played on the 2004 album Astronaut before leaving again; he briefly returned to play on their excellent 2023 Danse Macabre, including their version of Billie EIlish’s “Bury a Friend.” He announced his prostate cancer diagnosis in 2022, on the same day Duran Duran were inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame. But the music he made will live on — as the Duran Duran song says, wild boys always shine. Shine on, Andy Taylor.
