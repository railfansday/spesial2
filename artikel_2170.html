6 Steve Evans replacements Bristol Rovers must consider as sack calls grow

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

After a nightmare few weeks, it is fair to say that pressure is increasing on Bristol Rovers manager Steve Evans.

Evans took over at the Memorial Stadium in December with Bristol Rovers sitting in the League Two relegation zone, but the 64-year-old led his side to a 14th-placed finish last season after a stunning second half of the campaign that included a run of eight consecutive victories.

The Gas' momentum continued into this season as they won four of their opening five games to top the table in early September, and as Evans' impressive work started to attract attention from elsewhere, he was said to have been on the radar of Scottish Premiership side Hibernian.

However, since a 1-0 win over Rotherham United on 5th September, Rovers have won just one of their last nine league games, and they slumped to a seventh defeat during that run as they were beaten 3-1 by Fleetwood Town at Highbury Stadium on Saturday.

As they continue to slide down the table, the Gas currently sit 17th, but while they are just four points clear of the relegation zone, they are also the same distance from the play-off places in what is an incredibly congested division.

After their latest defeat at Fleetwood over the weekend, growing numbers of Rovers fans are calling for Evans to be sacked, and we have looked at some of the potential replacements the club should consider if they decide to make a managerial change.

Stuart Maynard has been out of work since his shock departure from York City in July, just three weeks before the start of the season.

Maynard led the Minstermen to the National League title last season with an incredible total of 108 points, but owner Matt Uggla made the surprise call to part company with the 46-year-old over the summer, much to the dismay of the fanbase.

While Maynard's last managerial role was in the fifth tier, he previously had an 18-month spell in charge of Notts County in League Two, guiding the Magpies to a sixth-placed finish in the 2024-25 season before being dismissed following defeat to AFC Wimbledon in the play-off semi-finals.

It was reported that Maynard rejected an approach from National League side Hartlepool United in September, which suggests he may be eyeing up an EFL job, and he should be high on Bristol Rovers' wish list if they were to sack Evans.

If Bristol Rovers feel that Maynard is too much of a gamble, they could go for a manager with more recent experience of the division in former Salford City boss Karl Robinson.

Robinson led the Ammies to a fourth-placed finish in League Two last season, only missing out on the automatic promotion spots by a single point, but he was sacked in June after a 3-0 defeat to Notts County in the play-off final at Wembley.

The 46-year-old has achieved one promotion during his managerial career, having guided MK Dons to the Championship in 2016, while he also oversaw two play-off finishes in League One during his five-year spell in charge of Oxford United.

Robinson held talks with Peterborough United in September prior to the appointment of Robbie Savage, so he may be keen to hold out for a League One job, but the Gas would surely have a strong chance of tempting him back to the fourth tier.

Another manager with an excellent recent record at League Two level is ex-Bromley boss Andy Woodman.

Woodman took over at then National League side Bromley in March 2021, and after leading the Ravens to promotion to the EFL for the first time in their history in 2024, he defied the odds once again as he inspired his side to the League Two title last season.

However, after a tough start to life in League One that included a number of heavy defeats, Bromley parted company with Woodman last month, and the 55-year-old broke his silence on Monday to express his disappointment at his exit from the CopperJax Community Stadium.

Given that he has only just departed Bromley, Woodman may wish to take a break before returning to the dugout, particularly with emotions still running high about the way he was treated by his former employers, but he may find it difficult to turn down a club the size of Bristol Rovers.

Like Woodman, former Peterborough United boss Luke Williams is another manager to have been sacked by a League One club this season.

Williams saved Posh from relegation last season after taking over at the Weston Homes Stadium last October, but after an underwhelming summer transfer window, his side made a slow start to the new campaign, resulting in his departure in September.

While Williams' time at Peterborough did not go to plan, the 46-year-old led Notts County to promotion from the National League in 2023, and he steered Swansea City to a 14th-placed finish in the Championship in the 2023-24 campaign before being dismissed midway through the following season.

Williams' career is at something of a crossroads after his exit from Posh, which could mean that he may have to consider taking a job at a lower level than he would ideally like, but Bristol Rovers could be the perfect place for him to rebuild his reputation.

Although his stock has not fallen quite as low as Williams, it has been a tough few years for ex-Luton Town and Oxford manager Matt Bloomfield.

After taking his first steps into management with Colchester United, Bloomfield then made the move to Wycombe Wanderers, where he spent much of his playing career, and his side emerged as serious automatic promotion contenders in League One in the 2024-25 season.

Despite the Chairboys sitting second in the table at the time, Bloomfield opted to depart Adams Park to take over at Luton in January 2025, but he was unable to prevent their relegation from the Championship, and he was dismissed last October after an inconsistent start to life back in League One.

Bloomfield was handed another opportunity in the Championship by Oxford in January, but he suffered his second consecutive relegation after failing to save the U's from the drop, and he was sacked in June after just over five months in charge at the Kassam Stadium.

The 42-year-old's recent record does not make for good reading, but it could be argued that he was rather unfortunate in each of his last two managerial roles, and he would certainly arrive at Bristol Rovers with a point to prove.

Many of the above candidates are on the market, but if Bristol Rovers are willing to poach a manager who is currently employed, Worthing's Adam Hinshelwood could be an exciting option.

After a successful first spell at Worthing, Hinshelwood took over at then National League side York in February 2024, and after keeping the Minstermen in the division in his first season, he led them to a second-placed finish in the 2024-25 campaign before losing to Oldham Athletic in the play-off semi-finals.

Despite making an unbeaten start, Hinshelwood was sacked by York just four games into last season, and after making a surprise return to Worthing just over a month later, he guided the Rebels to the fifth tier for the first time in their history after winning the National League South title.

Hinshelwood's managerial reputation has continued to flourish this season, with Worthing currently sitting sixth in the table after an outstanding start to the campaign, and the Gas should consider swooping for the 42-year-old.
