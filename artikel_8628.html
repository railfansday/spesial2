Personal Consumer Issues • Re: Is anyone stocking up supplies?

Tanggal: 2026-11-07



You can't. The two basic choices are (1) take reasonable precautions against reasonably possible scenarios for your situation, which, while the future is unknown, can be informed by what's happened in the past to your area and those in similar situations; and (2) really lean into it and let your imagination run wild. In the latter case, you're primarily getting value from your spending and effort because you have a hobby of fantasizing about trading precious metals in between shootouts with bandits or zombies.

I don't have a hobby of fantasizing about trading previous metals in between shootouts with bandits or zombies but having a few supplies on hand sounds reasonable in case of extended power outage which we lose all our refrigerated goods and or a pandemic with supply chain shortage which makes everyday food items incredibly difficult to obtain. Both of which have happened to us personally within the last 6 years!

It's worth some thought about whether your preparation measures have any correlation to the likely risks. How does your month of drinking water help with a Flint scenario? In all of the above combined, how many people died due to being stranded without food or drinking water? Who needed two weeks of food in Southern California last year? Anyone starve to death or die of dehydration in Sedona last week?

In my day-to-day life, I don't need to stockpile anything other than for my own convenience or the opportunity for savings, because there are ample resources that keep my necessities on hand to be picked up or delivered to me. I call these "stores" and "utilities" and thus emergency preparation only needs to be targeted toward covering the periods where those are likely to be unavailable to me due to inaccessibility or supply problems or whatnot. If I lived in an area where power outages were more common, maybe I'd have a generator to be more comfortable when that happens. But given its rarity for me, I'm not trying to guard against inconvenience, only disaster.

It takes pretty darn unlucky circumstances to be stranded or lack the ability to acquire necessities for more than a week. Even if the water supply becomes unreliable for a lengthy period of time, water is available for purchase and alternative living situations are available. Outside of temporary situations rarely lasting more than a few days, the emergency fund is much more powerful than the emergency stockpile. And that's a good thing, since a lot of these things will destroy your emergency stockpile too.

But if you get enjoyment or comfort from spending your time and treasure on emergency preparedness, go for it.
