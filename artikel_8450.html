How would you know if it is in a will or trust and why would it matter to the OP? The amount is relatively small for each child but it will probably seem large to the children.
Presumably if it were in a trust, the OP wouldn't be having to deal with it if the trust wording specifies this grandchild is not get get a dispursment until age 25. Since the OP is being sent the money I pointed out the difference between a will & a trust.

The OP later said "Keeping the funds in the trust would generate costly fees", so presumably the funds are in a trust and should not be dispersed prior to the grandchild reaching age 25 if that's spelled out in the trust wording. It seems like that was not a restriction in the trust and was a desire of the grandparent expressed in some other written document. The attorney is advising to disperse now to reduce fees and avoid dealing with a fussy financial planner. That also makes me think there's no clause in the trust requiring a delay on disbursement to recipients below age 25. Finally, the OP also later said "the executor and the attorney are ok with this and will be sending me release documents", which is confusing to me if the assets are in a trust with a "not before age 25" clause (there's could be both a will and a trust, but the assets in the trust by-pass anything stated in the will so typically the will is irrelevant for the OP's consideration).

Like I said, I'm not a lawyer, but I do like adhering to the rules. That really was my point about identifying differences between money passed from an executor through a will vs money passed from a trustee through a trust (the trustee is supposed to be legally bound to execute the restrictions identified in the trust; the will is typically just "split the residual assets by % as written" with no other restrictions like "not before age 25").

Anyway, the OP seems to have a path forward (and if there's a legal issue it would seem to be on the trustee, not the OP if they're putting their child's inheritance in a UTMA).
