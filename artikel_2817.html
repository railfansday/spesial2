College football draft targets for the Cowboys: Week 12

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

We're looking ahead to see who may be on the Dallas Cowboys' draft card in 2027.

Georgia enters Williams-Brice Stadium as the nation's top-ranked team, but South Carolina has enough speed to make this a useful scouting assignment. The Bulldogs' front will test LaNorris Sellers and the Gamecocks' offensive line. On the other side, South Carolina has a corner and return specialist whose athletic ability should be apparent whenever the ball comes his way.

Gabe Harris Jr. has the type of frame NFL teams are always willing to develop. He has the length and mass to play as a traditional defensive end or stand-up outside linebacker. He is not a small speed rusher who has to win before an offensive tackle touches him. He can take contact and hold his ground.

His best work starts with his hands. Harris can lock out an offensive tackle and control the outside shoulder. Against the run, that lets him set a firm edge and forces the ball back toward Georgia's pursuit. He also has enough strength to squeeze a gap when a tight end tries to block him down. There is useful position flexibility here because Georgia can move him inside on passing downs and ask him to attack a guard.

Dallas needs young defensive ends who can survive on early downs. The roster needs another player who can set an edge without coming off the field whenever the opponent gets heavy. Harris would give the Cowboys a power complement in the rotation. He can align outside a tackle in an even front, play the strong side against a tight end and reduce inside in selected passing packages. That versatility would help Dallas keep its fronts less predictable.

Vicari Swain is one of the more entertaining cornerbacks in this class because his athleticism appears in two phases. He's listed at 6-foot and 193 pounds, and he brings genuine speed and excellent open-field instincts. Those traits help him in coverage, but they have also made him one of the country's most dangerous punt returners.

Swain is comfortable when he can play with vision on the quarterback. He closes quickly from off coverage and has the acceleration to recover when a receiver creates early separation. His movement skills also fit man coverage. He can mirror releases and carry vertical routes.

Swain fits the most important item on Dallas' shopping list. Joey Porter Jr. is the best Dallas has at corner and he's no guarantee for next season. Swain has the feet to compete outside and the quickness to receive work in the slot, giving the Cowboys options as the rest of the secondary changes around him.

His return value could help him dress immediately. A rookie corner selected outside the first round may begin as the fourth defensive back, but Swain can justify a game-day place through punts and coverage units while competing for defensive snaps. Dallas would gain an explosive special-teams player rather than waiting for a developmental corner to become useful.

Tennessee is trying to stay in the playoff race after grinding out a road win at Texas A&M. LSU has slipped out of the AP Top 25 at 6-4, but its roster still gives evaluators plenty to study. This matchup puts one of the SEC's quickest cornerbacks against Tennessee's vertical passing game and one of the nation's busiest running backs against LSU's defensive front.

P.J. Woodland is built differently from Swain. He's smaller at 5-foot-11 and 164 pounds, so there is no hiding the concern about his frame. What keeps him in the draft conversation is the quality of his coverage movement and his confidence playing the football.

Woodland is at his best in man coverage. His feet are quick at the line and he changes direction without wasted steps. He also has enough recovery speed to remain connected when a receiver threatens vertically. LSU trusts him on an island because he rarely panics when the ball is in the air. He tracks it and competes at the catch point despite giving away weight.

Dallas needs coverage talent, and Woodland's tape supplies it. His quick feet and man-cover ability could translate to the Cowboys' sub-packages, especially when the defense wants to pressure and cannot afford a corner losing immediately at the line.

DeSean Bishop has gone from a walk-on with one major college offer to the center of Tennessee's offense. The redshirt junior is a compact runner and his game is built around vision, balance and decisive cuts rather than rare size or track speed.

Bishop presses the line with patience. He allows blocks to form and gets vertical without extra movement. His low center of gravity helps him absorb glancing contact, while his footwork lets him redirect when the initial lane disappears. He is particularly effective on inside and split-zone concepts because he understands how to manipulate a linebacker before making his cut.

Javonte Williams can remain the lead back next season, but Dallas should keep adding inexpensive talent behind him. Bishop profiles as a complementary runner who can handle a series or take on a larger workload if the starter misses time.

His running style should fit the Cowboys' interior concepts. Bishop reads blocks cleanly and gets north quickly. That reliability would make him a sensible Day 3 selection, where Dallas can address the position without spending the premium capital needed at corner, tackle or edge.
