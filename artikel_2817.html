How to Watch: Michigan vs Ohio State, other Rivalry Week matchups

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

In what has been a game between two titans of college football this decade, the 2026 version of The Game is lackluster thanks to two underperforming teams. Both have wounds that cut deep this year, as the Michigan Wolverines continue through a debacle on and off the field that span beyond this team. Meanwhile, the Ohio State Buckeyes lost starting quarterback Julian Sayin more than a month ago and have lost three of their last four games heading into Saturday.

Less to play for does not mean this game is for naught. Ryan Day is still trying to shake the born on third base comments off his back, and Kyle Whittingham is desperate to save his job. The result on Saturday will still be meaningful for the future of either program. Here's how you watch all the action in The Game:

It feels weird to not have everything on the line for either team. The 2020s have provided some historically great matchups between these programs, both finding their way to national championships in between. Even one of the biggest upsets in the history of the rivalry, as Michigan knocked off Ohio State as three-touchdown underdogs in the Buckeyes' eventual run to a national title two years ago.

That was the last time these two teams met in Columbus, and at the time it was four straight wins for Michigan. From then to now, Day has had an up-and-down relationship with Ohio State fans. Even with the course-correct in 2025, as Ohio State blew out Michigan in The Big House, there are still pockets that question if Day is the right man for the job in Columbus.

Every day, the situation at Michigan looks worse. THe administration can't stay out of the headlines with the athletic director debacle. Kyle Whittingham continues to put his foot in his mouth in front of the media. And on top of all that, there are concerns about a potential Bryce Underwood injury from the upset win over UCLA. A loss for Ohio State amidst all of the chaos would be as inexcusable considering the season Michigan has had.

Michigan is playing with house money, just like last week. You felt the sense of pride from the veterans in the room who showed up and showed out in their last game in The Big House. Now, this is the last time they'll play Ohio State. Will we see the same level of pride? With the way Whittingham has taken the importance away from this game, at least to the outside perspective, it will be interesting to see how things play out.

Vegas odds have shifted significantly since open. The Buckeyes were two-touchdown favorites and the total was at 46.5. Since then, the line has crept in favor of Michigan, now down to just 10.5. Staggeringly, the total has dropped by nine full points to 37.5. Both numbers favor the Wolverines and the game they'd want to play against the potential power the Buckeyes have on the offensive end with Heisman hopeful Jeremiah Smith.

I'm not sure I'm fully on board with a Michigan win, but I do think Ohio State is going to struggle to move the ball against a Jay Hill-led defense that has thwarted strong rushing attacks throughout this season. Ohio State wins, but give me the Wolverines covering the spread and this one being close heading into the fourth quarter.
