Building Round: All-or-nothing USDC escrow for micro-raises on Arc

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

A technical deep-dive for developers. Code snippets, architecture, and the lessons learned building a trust-minimized crowdfunding escrow on Arc Testnet.

Round is a crowdfunding escrow where backers commit USDC to a funding goal + deadline. The founder is paid only if the goal is reached; otherwise every backer gets a full refund. No admin, no pause, no upgrade, no fee — each raise is its own independent contract. The whole thing runs on Arc Testnet, where USDC is both the native gas token and the ERC-20 being escrowed.

Traditional crowdfunding platforms (Kickstarter, GoFundMe) are custodial: they hold funds, charge fees, and can freeze or refund at their discretion. For micro-raises under $10k, that overhead and trust assumption is disproportionate. Round removes the middleman entirely: the escrow logic lives on-chain, anyone can finalize an outcome, and refunds are pull-based by each backer.

Every chain read goes through src/lib/rpc.ts, and every export makes exactly one JSON-RPC request. A JSON-RPC batch counts as one. This keeps the home page (which loads many rounds) to a single private round trip:

No viem http() transport is used anywhere in src/. wagmi's transport is a custom one layered over anonRpc, so all traffic is anonymized through a null-origin iframe + WebRTC worker.

Contracts are decimals-agnostic — they operate on raw token units. The 6-decimal USDC view is enforced only in the frontend via @/onchain-money. On Arc, USDC is both the native gas (18-decimal view, used only for gas/msg.value) and the ERC-20 (6-decimal view, used for all balances/transfers). Never sum the two views.

Factory is deployed at 0xd6c786d3eabddd791e9ad9554e56e808075a3a95 on Arc Testnet (constructor arg = USDC 0x3600…0000). Rounds are created by users through /new. Seeded rounds (real txs) exercise both outcomes.
