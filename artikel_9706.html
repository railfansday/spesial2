Why Standard PDF Taggers Turn LaTeX Matrices Into Gibberish (and 4 Math TTS Traps)

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

With ADA Title II (WCAG 2.1 AA) deadlines coming up for U.S. public universities, most accessibility discussions focus on web pages and body text. In math, physics, and engineering departments, the real bottleneck is legacy lecture PDFs: compiled LaTeX where the .tex source is long gone, old Word/MathType exams, and scanned handwritten notes.

Even when a campus runs standard PDF auto-taggers over those handouts, the output often looks fine visually while completely breaking for screen-reader users.

Here are the failure modes we kept hitting while building EqScribe and testing real open STEM courseware (OpenStax CC BY 4.0 chapters, multivariable calculus handouts, and a 0-text-layer 1903 handwritten page from Srinivasa Ramanujan's notebook).

Automated pipelines can reliably produce verifiable HTML5 + MathML3, EPUB 3.3, and Tagged ISO 14289-1 PDF/UA-1 (backed by unaltered veraPDF, EPUBCheck, and axe-core reports), plus a browser-based Side-by-Side Audio QA Sheet so sighted instructors or TAs can spot-check flagged equations next to original PDF crops without installing NVDA or JAWS.

Where automation stops is spatial diagrams: circuit schematics, free-body force diagrams, and 3D plots. Guessing alt text on those risks feeding wrong physics to a blind student. The honest approach is flagging every spatial figure explicitly for human alt-text or tactile graphics review.

We put four open, commercial-safe sample packets online (including a genuine 1903 handwritten Ramanujan page with zero text layer) at https://eqscribe.netlify.app/.

If you work with LaTeX, MathML, or higher-ed accessibility and have seen other tricky notation patterns break in screen readers, we would love to hear about them in the comments.
