What a small business needs on a one-page website: a simple checklist

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Most local businesses do not need a big website. One well-organised page is enough for a customer to decide whether to call you. Here is a checklist for what to put on it, in the order people usually look for it.

At the very top: your business name, what you do and the area you serve. Someone landing from a search should know in a few seconds that they are in the right place.

Put your phone number, address and email near the top, and again at the bottom. Make the phone number a tap-to-call link so it works from a phone:

List what you offer in plain words. If you can share prices or a starting price, do. If prices vary, say what they depend on. People leave when they cannot find a number.

A short table or list. Keep it accurate, and update it when your hours change for holidays.

Photos of your own work or your shop say more than stock images. Keep file sizes small so the page loads quickly on mobile data.

Answer the questions you get asked all the time: do you take walk-ins, how do I book, what are your payment options, do you offer a guarantee. Only state what is true for your business.

A contact form is useful, but a plain HTML form does not send email by itself. Connect it to a form service such as Formspree or Netlify Forms, or just link to your email and phone.

Most visitors will see it on a phone. Open the page on one before you publish and tap every link.
