Chris Cenac Jr. flashes promise, growing pains in early minutes

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Chris Cenac Jr. entered the NBA as a raw, uber-athletic prospect, who was expected to get little run early in his career.

But 14 games into the season, the 19-year-old has already played some meaningful minutes — and showed quite a mixed bag in that time.

So far, Cenac Jr. has played in three G League games for the Maine Celtics and 5 in the big leagues, with his biggest opportunities coming in the past couple home games against the Dallas Mavericks and the Orlando Magic.

Boston drafted the 6'11" power forward/center with the 27th pick after the New Orleans native averaged 9.5 points and 7.9 rebounds in 37 games for the University of Houston Cougars, where he displayed impressive athleticism and rebounding talent.

Cenac Jr. was widely viewed as an unrefined prospect who wouldn't meaningfully contribute to Boston's title hopes for years, and that may prove to be true, but he may also bring some value to the table immediately.

Over his 3 games in Maine, Cenac Jr. has averaged 13.7 points, 9.3 rebounds, 1.7 assists, and 1.7 blocks in 26 minutes per game. While he has struggled with shooting efficiency, he has been a net positive player in the G League, with a 122.2 offensive rating and 98.2 defensive rating that result in a stellar 24.1 net rating.

In the G League, he has also showed off many of the reasons why the Celtics drafted him, such as his springy athleticism, the ability to shoot from beyond the arc, create his own shots, grab boards, and block shots.

At the NBA level, Cenac Jr.'s first 4 stints all came in garbage time. Over a little more than 16 minutes of play time, he scored 14 points on 6/9 shooting from the field (including 1/4 from three-point range), 6 rebounds, and 4 turnovers. Each stint was the result of a hefty Celtics lead, especially against the Mavericks, when he played nearly 7 minutes (his longest stretch of play time through that point) to conclude the blowout win. And he definitely made his presence known with 3 emphatic dunks.

Mazzulla must have liked what he saw because he immediately played Cenac Jr. for his most significant minutes of his young career in the next game, against the Magic. It was the second night of a back-t0-back, and the team was without starting center Neemias Queta, so there was a void in the paint for Cenac Jr. to fill.

He played a little more than 12 minutes, with his first stint coming at the start of the second quarter. That 4-minute stint was solid but unspectacular, as he was mostly used on offense to set screens and serve as a lob threat, even though that threat went unrealized. He did play smart with the ball in the little time he had possession of it, and dumped the ball down to an unguarded Derrick White in the paint for a nice assist. He also spotted up for a corner 3, which he missed. Defensively, he was an active and bouncy paint deterrent in drop coverage, but had a hard time getting in the right position to box out Wendell Carter Jr. on rebounds.

Cenac Jr. returned to the court for the final 43 seconds of the half, but was uninvolved in any real action. His next run came early in the third quarter, when he played almost 3 minutes and grabbed an easy defensive rebound, but it wasn't until his second stint of the quarter when the mixed bag of his performance was put on full display.

During that stretch, from 3:47 to 0:38, Cenac Jr. had both his best and worst moments of the night — almost consecutively. He threw down an effortless alley-oop on a pass from Payton Pritchard when Carter Jr. went to help stop Pritchard's drive, then finished another alley-oop (this time assisted by Jayson Tatum) on a fast break soon after. A couple plays later, Cenac Jr. turned the ball over by dribbling it off his foot in what should have been an easy handoff to Tatum, then made a bad pass on Boston's next offensive possession that led to another turnover.

To be fair, as Austin Rivers pointed out during the broadcast, Baylor Scheierman passed it off to Cenac Jr. as he was streaking down the middle of the court and into 3 Magic defenders who recovered in time to cover him. He should have passed it out to Pritchard or Tatum, but it's hard to fault him for trying to drive to the rim when it initially appeared that he had a lane to attack.

Unfortunately, his play (and the Celtics' as a whole) only went downhill from there. Despite playing passable defense through the first three quarters, Cenac Jr. looked rough on defense in his final real stint. He let Jalen Suggs steal what should have been an easy rebound from him when the game was still a contest at 104-99 with a little less than 3 minutes to go, which allowed the Magic to widen their lead at a crucial moment. The next time the C's were on defense, Franz Wagner cooked him with a couple nifty moves, forcing Sam Hauser to come over to help stop Wagner. That left Carter Jr. wide open to throw down an easy alley-oop. The game went from a 5-point contest to a 9-point contest in less than a minute in the clutch, and it was largely because of the rook. He was pulled after the next play, and it's easy to see why. He came in for one more play with less than 40 seconds to go in the game, but the damage had already been done.

Overall, Cenac Jr.'s big league stats haven't been too impressive. He has averaged 3.6 points per game on 66.7% from the field and 20% from three-point range, along with 1.6 rebounds, 0.2 assists, 0.8 turnovers and 0.2 personal fouls per game. He has yet to get a block or a steal. The advanced stats are also rough. While he has recorded an excellent 70.8% effective field goal percentage and a decent 21.2% defensive rebounding percentage, according to NBA.com, he has also accumulated a 28.6% turnover percentage, an offensive rating of 91.4 and a defensive rating of 106.6 — resulting in an ugly -15.2 net rating.

Nevertheless, Cenac Jr. has displayed some of the potential that got him drafted, and has plenty of time to build upon his weaknesses. No one should expect a 19-year-old rookie to have a solid, consistent grasp of defensive positioning on the professional level, especially only 14 games into their career. Luckily, the Celtics sport an elite developmental staff, led by assistant coach Craig Luschenat. Pritchard, Hauser, Walsh, Scheierman, and Gonzalez are all proof of that. If Cenac Jr. is going to become an impactful, net-positive player, there are few teams in the league better equipped to make it happen.

But, in the meantime, his growth will undoubtedly be fun to watch.
