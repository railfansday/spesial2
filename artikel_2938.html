Colette Kress Just Delivered Excellent News for Nvidia Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nvidia passed its earnings call with flying colors.

AI investors and beyond were eagerly anticipating Nvidia's (NVDA -0.44%) earnings call Tuesday night. The earnings report was the company's first since Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO amid controversy last week. Nvidia named CFO Colette Kress as the interim CEO as it conducts a search for a permanent replacement, considering both internal and external candidates.

Kress passed her first test with flying colors, as she delivered a confident pitch for the company and even raised guidance for fiscal 2028 from 70% revenue growth to 75% revenue growth. Nvidia also beat estimates on the top and bottom lines, and the stock was trading higher after the call, up 3.3% as of 8:23 p.m.

However, amid the focus on the headline numbers and parsing the call for hints into the company's future, there was one key comment from Kress that investors may have missed.

It's understandable why Nvidia is down a third from its peak last month, given Huang's sudden exit, but with Nvidia stock now trading at a forward P/E of just 11, the stock is as cheap as it's ever been and ripe for share buybacks.

In response to a question about the company's capital allocation and its buyback plans, Kress said that the "super top priority" for returning capital to shareholders was to use the company's current share repurchase authorization, which was raised by $150 billion in September and sat at $227.3 billion at the end of the third quarter. Kress added, "So thinking about all of that together, I think there is definitely room to continue all of the stock repurchase plans that we want to do and complete those before the end of fiscal year '28." She also noted that the top priority for its capital spending is supporting its suppliers in building out additional capacity.

If Nvidia executes on those buybacks and the stock price stays roughly the same, it would reduce shares outstanding by roughly 6% by Jan. 2028, when fiscal 2028 ends.

The share repurchase authorization and Kress's commitment are a positive sign, not just because it will lift Nvidia's earnings per share, but because it shows management's confidence that the stock is undervalued after the recent sell-off and signals to investors that it's worth buying.

For investors worried that Nvidia would struggle during the transitional period, Kress gave no indication that anything has changed, saying, "it's business as usual," and that customers, suppliers, and partners are all "100% behind us."

Demand for the company's products continues to outstrip supply, and Kress spent much of the call reiterating the company's commitment to securing adequate supply, which allowed it to raise its revenue guidance for fiscal 2028.

In just one quarter, its supply commitments increased from $279 billion, primarily due to memory supply. Kress said, "We feel confident in terms of both the demand that we have in front of us as well as the supply and additionally that we can do the 75%," referring to 75% revenue growth next year.

Nvidia also addressed the controversy around Jensen Huang, and new Chairman of the Board Harvey Jones made it clear that the board has no intention of reinstating Huang. Regarding the CEO search, Jones said, "We want to install this CEO, the permanent CEO, as quickly as possible, but we won't expedite the CEO before finding exactly the right person." Ian Buck, Nvidia's vice president of hyperscale and high-performance computing, who has been seen as a possible replacement for Huang, was on the Q&A portion of the call, but did not answer any questions.

Nvidia did what it needed to do on the earnings call and then some. Its commitment to using up its $227 billion share repurchase authorization and the guidance hike for fiscal 2028 show investors the company continues to excel. Nvidia is on track to bring in more than $700 billion in revenue next year. Given the deep discount, Nvidia looks like a buy, even without Jensen Huang.
