Beasley (BBGI) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Record digital margins as Beasley trims debt and expands its sports push.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Beasley Broadcast (BBGI +2.20%) prioritized the expansion of its digital operating margins and the reduction of its debt obligations during the third quarter. Management reported that while the overall advertising environment remained challenging, particularly within national and local agency channels, structural cost reductions implemented over the past two years have begun to fundamentally improve the economics of the business. The company successfully reduced its first lien debt to $3.6 million through asset sales and equity financing while achieving record profitability in its digital segment. The company stated that it is evolving its sales organization from a traditional radio company into an omnichannel marketing platform with a focused emphasis on sports media and local direct relationships.

While profitability remains below prior year levels, our results reflect the significant work underway to fundamentally improve the economics of our business. On a same-station basis, revenue declined approximately $4.8 million year-over-year, while operating expenses were down $3.7 million, limiting the decline in station operating income to approximately $1.1 million. These savings reflect the structural changes implemented over the past two years, including our most recent actions in May. Importantly, not all revenue carries the same value to our business, and our strategy increasingly reflects that distinction.

Within digital, we have deliberately shifted our focus away from lower-margin third-party products and toward higher-margin O&O offerings, where we have greater control over our inventory, pricing, customer relationships, and profitability. That shift has contributed to lower digital revenue in the near term, but the improvement in profitability demonstrates why we believe it is the right approach. Despite a $1.4 million decline in same-station digital revenue, digital operating income increased nearly $300,000 year-over-year, reaching $3.1 million, and our digital operating margin expanded from approximately 26% to a record 32%.

We are generating more digital operating profit on a smaller revenue base, and that is an important distinction and reflects the progress we are making towards building a higher-margin, more scalable business. At the same time, we continue investing in the content, technology, and commercial capabilities that support our O&O platforms, strengthen our local advertiser relationships, and position us for sustainable, profitable growth. Our brands remain deeply connected to their communities, and our content attracts audiences across broadcast and digital platforms. Our focus is on converting that engagement into higher-quality revenue. Rebuilding revenue remains our focus. Our stations reach nearly 17 million consumers every week.

That decline is not unique to Beasley, and we are not planning for it to reverse, which is exactly why the model must change. We are evolving our sales organization from being a radio company to a local omnichannel marketing platform, one that combines the trust and scale of broadcast radio with the precision of streaming, podcasting, social, live events, and community engagement.

A local business owner does not wake up wanting to buy radio first thing. They want customers, and they want their cash registers to ring. Our mission is to help brands and businesses reach consumers at every touchpoint in the local journey, in the car, on the phone, in the feed, and at the event, and then to show that advertiser what we did for their business. That part really matters. We are deploying attribution and measurement across the platform now. Because brand growth earns the first order and accountable results earn the renewal, our investment here is really making a difference. We get the trust and scale from broadcast, the precision from digital, and proof from measurement.

That is a platform that we have and we are continuing to add to. The clearest example of that platform today is sports. In Boston, 98.5 The Sports Hub is the model for that. It is the home of the New England Patriots, the Boston Bruins, the Boston Celtics. It is not a radio station that just carries games. It is broadcast, streaming, podcasts, social, and live events, built around teams a city cares about deeply and sold to advertisers as part of a relationship. We have the same foundation in Philadelphia with the Philadelphia Flyers and the Philadelphia 76ers, along with The Fanatic, and in Detroit with the University of Michigan Athletics.

During third quarter, we added to that roster. Beasley is now the official year-round audio partner of the Vegas Golden Knights. Under a multi-year agreement, we have the English language and the Spanish language flagship. We signed this partnership less than three weeks before the NHL season, and advertiser response has already exceeded our expectations for the window we had. We are not sharing figures today, but what I will tell you is that it gives our Las Vegas team something every advertiser in the city wants to stand next to and be part of. Sports is appointment listening in an on-demand world. Fans show up live, they show up loyal, and advertisers follow them.

Based on what we have built in Boston and what we are seeing already in Las Vegas, we intend to keep building and expanding our sports portfolio. A platform only matters if we can sell it, and we are also making changes with how we sell and how we execute in all of our markets. The first is structure. We are moving to one selling organization with audio and digital in a single funnel, every seller carrying the same full portfolio, and market leaders whose job is defined around revenue generation. One team, one funnel, one number. The second is technology.

In September, we announced a multi-year agreement with Futuri and completed the deployment of their TopLine Enterprise to our sellers. TopLine combines AI with our own sales and audience data, as well as outside market intelligence. It supports prospecting, account research, meeting preparation, proposals, and campaign reporting. We did not buy a tool. We rebuilt the sales process around one. Our sellers review and approve everything it produces. AI does the research. Our sellers own the relationship. It is early, and I will report back on adoption and results when we have more data. The third part is talent. For decades, the way this industry found sellers was to hire them from a competitor across the street.

That is expensive, and it does not add one new seller to the business. We are building our own farm team. Our sales development representatives, our SDRs, are early career hires with one job, prospecting. They find and qualify new local business and hand those leads over to account executives in our markets. The best of them earn an account executive seat down the road. During the quarter, we brought on another group, and members of our first group have already moved into account executive roles and are closing business and participating in their local markets. Digital represented roughly 23% of total company revenue during the quarter, or 24% excluding political. On a same-station basis, digital revenue declined roughly 12% in the third quarter, while year-to-date revenue remains up roughly 4% compared with the prior year period. Advertising demand remained mixed during the quarter. Strength in several key verticals was offset by softness in more discretionary and consumer-facing categories. Gaming generated roughly $3 million in revenue, up 17% from the prior year period.

This remains an important category for Beasley, and its growth is encouraging amid broader pressure on traditional advertising. Automotive performance was mixed within the category, but the broader auto category experienced modest growth this quarter versus prior year period. Domestic auto dealer spending was relatively flat year-over-year, while import auto dealers grew roughly 13%. Although the broader environment remains challenging, these core dealer categories stabilized this quarter and are showing early signs of sustained improvement heading into 2027. Other areas of growth included insurance, banking, and food advertising. Our focus is on building on these areas of strength, rebuilding underperforming categories, and diversifying revenue through local direct relationships and digital capabilities.
