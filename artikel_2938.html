‘She Knows More Ball Than a Lot of So-Called Football People’ — Patriots Super Bowl Champ Backs Carlie Irsay-Gordon to Steer Colts Out of the Storm

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Indianapolis Colts' 26-3 loss to the Houston Texans on Thursday night dropped them to 3-8. As the game slipped away in prime time, owner Carlie Irsay-Gordon, working the sideline with a notepad as she so often does, faced the brunt of criticism once again.

However, one former player is firmly in her corner. Stephon Gilmore, a Super Bowl champion and former New England Patriots and Colts cornerback, pushed back Friday, writing, "Y'all have to chill on Carlie Irsay." He then made a prediction for a franchise stuck in a long slide.

Irsay-Gordon has been facing a barrage of criticism, especially since the firing of former general manager Chris Ballard, which was followed by a fire sale of key players.

The Colts traded defensive tackle DeForest Buckner and wide receiver Keenan Allen to the San Francisco 49ers, guard Quenton Nelson to the Seattle Seahawks, and wide receiver Josh Downs to the Kansas City Chiefs.

Surprisingly, the Colts chose to hold on to their three-time Pro Bowl running back Jonathan Taylor on trade deadline day, despite being one of the team's most valuable remaining assets.

Meanwhile, the Colts' decision to release quarterback Daniel Jones came just 10 days after the November 10 trade deadline. Indianapolis decided against trading him, only to cut ties after a disappointing 3-8 start in which Jones threw seven touchdowns against 13 interceptions across 11 starts.

Jones ranks 34th in Pro Football Network's NFL QB Impact Score with a grade of 62.1 through Week 11.

The timing raised further questions about the front office's handling of the situation, particularly after the Colts committed to Jones with a two-year, $88 million contract in March.

Jones also had roughly $10.5 million protected for injury in 2027 — $6.5 million in base salary plus a $4 million roster bonus — creating additional risk if he suffered a serious injury. The release clears the way for Anthony Richardson Sr. to take over as the starting quarterback for the remainder of the season.

Gilmore, who played cornerback for Indianapolis in 2022, answered the criticism of Irsay-Gordon with what he has seen for himself up close.

"I've seen her in meetings. She knows more ball than a lot of so-called football people. She's very passionate about the game and the Colts."

"She will get the Colts right in due time. Watch," Gilmore predicted.

Gilmore wasn't the only former player to step in Friday. Mitchell Schwartz, a Super Bowl champion with the Chiefs, had done so earlier, making the case for the sideline habit.

"I do not get all the hate. She stays out of the way, she listens to the headsets, and she takes notes."

Schwartz then compared Irsay-Gordon to what one Dallas Mavericks owner used to do at NBA games. "Mark Cuban sat right behind the bench for half a century and no one batted an eye."

The routine is not new, and neither is her reason for it. In a June 2025 interview with the team's website, Irsay-Gordon explained what pushed her to learn more about the game. "I need to be able to say, 'Is this person full of BS? Do they even know what they're talking about?'"

The scrutiny comes as more of the football operation runs through ownership. The Colts fired general manager Ballard on Oct. 19, one day after a loss to the previously winless Tennessee Titans dropped them to 2-4.

Ballard went 72-82-1 with two playoff appearances over parts of 10 seasons. Assistant GM Ed Dodds is overseeing personnel for the rest of the season, while head coach Shane Steichen and his staff report to ownership.

Indianapolis is 1-4 since Ballard's firing and sits third in the AFC South, and the skid runs deeper than this season.

Since an 8-2 start in 2025, the Colts have gone 3-15. That run began with a seven-game losing streak, making them the first team to reach six games over .500 and still finish with a losing record.
