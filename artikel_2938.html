Alphabet Has $251 Billion Invested In 2 of Its Biggest Artificial Intelligence Partners

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Alphabet's successful investments leave it with substantial equity value on its balance sheet.

By Adam Levy – Nov 25, 2026 at 2:09AM EST

Alphabet (GOOG -1.05%) (GOOGL -1.08%) has long invested in new technology and start-ups. Not only does it operate multiple moonshots under its Other Bets segment, but it also invests in start-ups via two venture capital funds (GV and CapitalG) and makes larger strategic investments at the corporate level. The strategy has produced significant gains for Alphabet and its shareholders, and it now holds around $251 billion in value across just two stocks.

That's quite an achievement considering Alphabet's marketable and non-marketable securities totaled less than $200 billion at the end of 2025. But the continued rise of AI stocks has led to some huge winners in 2026. As a result, two of Alphabet's biggest AI partners account for $251 billion of its portfolio as of this writing.

Here's how Alphabet built large stakes in Anthropic (ANT -6.54%) and Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -5.65%).

Alphabet first invested in Anthropic by committing to buy $2 billion worth of convertible notes in September 2023. It entered another note purchase agreement in 2024, adding up to $1.3 billion to its commitments. It then laid out $10 billion in early 2026, with an additional $30 billion in commitments.

Alphabet fulfilled $10 billion of that remaining commitment in a private placement at the time of Anthropic's IPO. With the conversion of all of Alphabet's notes, it holds approximately 13.7% of Anthropic's capital stock, which is worth approximately $174 billion as of this writing.

Alphabet isn't just an investor in Anthropic; it's also one of its biggest suppliers. Anthropic has committed to spending $121.3 billion on Google Cloud Platform between April 2026 and July 2033. Additionally, it committed to buying Alphabet's Tensor Processing Units (TPU) chips, with aggregate future payments totaling between $125 billion and $150 billion.

That makes Anthropic one of Alphabet's most important customers for the foreseeable future. Combined with its equity stake, it's heavily exposed to the AI lab's financial future. In the near term, Anthropic looks well-positioned to meet its commitments, with a huge pile of cash, financing options in place, and very strong revenue growth. The long-term outlook for both its stock price and its ability to meet its commitments relies on increased enterprise adoption and usage, which will fuel substantial earnings growth.

Alphabet will be able to sell its Anthropic shares, with periodic lock-up expirations over the next year. Taking some cash off the table seems smart, as it can use the proceeds to fund its AI build-out, and it already has significant exposure to Anthropic.

Alphabet initially invested $900 million in SpaceX in 2015 for around 9% of the company. SpaceX's merger with X.AI diluted its share of the business, but Alphabet still holds a substantial stake in the combined company. In fact, Alphabet increased its share count in the third quarter, adding 3.9 million shares to bring its total to 555 million. Those shares are worth $77 billion as of this writing.

Alphabet has close ties with SpaceX, relying on the company for additional AI infrastructure capacity. It signed a contract worth $920 million per month for 32 months, for a total potential value of $29.4 billion. That contract started in October, freeing up capacity for other developers using Google Cloud Platform.

Alphabet CFO Anat Ashkenazi described Alphabet's deals with SpaceX and other third-party compute providers as a "bridging strategy." CEO Sundar Pichai explained on the company's second-quarter earnings call: "There are very, very large customers of ours on Cloud ... And the incremental opportunities they are bringing to us, while a short‑term cost over a few months may be very high, in the lifetime of the deal, as we bring more capacity on, is highly ROI‑positive." He appears to have been talking about a deal with Anthropic.

Alphabet and SpaceX both have the right to cancel their contract with 90 days' notice. That means that once Alphabet builds out enough supply to meet its demand, it no longer needs to keep paying SpaceX.

But canceling its contract would likely also negatively impact its SpaceX shares, as much of SpaceX's stock value is tied to its AI build-out. Alphabet faces an extended lock-up of many of its shares until next summer, so it's likely to keep paying for SpaceX's compute through mid-2027 at the very least to help preserve the value of its shares before it can liquidate them.
