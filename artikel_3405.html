Forget Yellowstone, Taylor Sheridan's New Show Is Officially Less Than A Month Away

Taylor Sheridan's directorial debut instantly made him a force of nature in one main genre, with the Yellowstone franchise widely credited with reviving the Western genre for a modern audience. Since then, Taylor Sheridan's TV shows have gone from strength to strength, with the Yellowstone TV shows reaching into the past through prequels, and with spinoffs like Dutton Ranch being hits with both critics and audiences. However, while Sheridan's Westerns might have made his career, he has been slowly expanding into another genre, with his biggest leap coming next month.

Mayor of Kingstown had elements of a great neo-Western TV show, but it marked Sheridan's departure into the crime thriller genre. Then, after a return to Yellowstone's world with its prequel,1883, Tulsa King then continued his crime thriller run but brought comedy into the mix, with Sylvester Stallone's performance as the relocated mafia capo Dwight Manfredi bringing new viewers to Sheridan's TV shows. Now, the director is building on this success with Frisco King, which arrives on Paramount+ on December 18. This continues an extraordinary career streak, as Sheridan has several upcoming TV shows in the pipeline.

The only Taylor Sheridan TV show to receive negative Rotten Tomatoes ratings from critics and audiences is Marshals. Considering the fact that the director has been central to making 14 TV shows in the past eight years, this is a remarkable achievement. 1923 and Landman polarized Rotten Tomatoes critics and audiences, with critics awarding both shows 94% and 80% ratings respectively, and audiences being considerably less impressed. That said, even this indicates that these shows are fuel for discussion and debate, rather than being forgettable, and both 1923 and Landman still won Satellite Awards.

Sheridan's prestige TV shows follow a formula that allows for creativity without forcing any to feel same-y. His characters tend to be morally ambiguous but deeply connected to their setting, allowing for extensive worldbuilding and requiring nuanced performances from the cast. The acting in Taylor Sheridan's TV shows is consistently excellent, as his shows have attracted some of the most accomplished actors working today, from Helen Mirren to Zoe Saldaña. Now Frisco King is being headed by another megastar, with Samuel L. Jackson in the main role as a gangster who relocates to the suburb of Frisco, Texas.

Frisco King is a sequel to Tulsa King, which is a fish-out-of-water crime comedy centering on Dwight "The General" Manfredi, who is released after a 25-year prison sentence and sent to Tulsa, Oklahoma to set up a new life away from his mafia contacts. However, old habits die hard and before long, Manfredi is setting up his own criminal enterprise. Tulsa King's third season introduced Russell Lee Washington Jr (played by Jackson,) as an old friend and prison mate of Manfredi, now working as a New York hitman. After refusing the order to kill Manfredi, he also decides to relocate.

Samuel L. Jackson's best movies and TV shows have been crime staples, and his performance was one of Tulsa King's standouts. This makes Frisco King feel like a logical next step for the story and characters, and Stallone is reprising his role as Manfredi for a few episodes, acting as a familiar face to help introduce the new series and its tone, which will be different from Tulsa King. Rather than repeat the original show's story with a different character, the sequel is set to move further toward comedy, marking an even more drastic genre change from Sheridan's neo-Western beginnings.

Tulsa King's Dwight Manfredi is Sylvester Stallone's first role in a scripted TV series.

While Frisco King does not directly connect to any more of Taylor Sheridan's TV shows other than Tulsa King, it shares directors Christina Alexandra Voros and Michael Friedman with Yellowstone, as Sheridan has split directing duties across the show's eight episodes, while writing each of the episodes himself. With this new show on the horizon, Sheridan's Tulsa King franchise is finally taking shape, and Frisco King marks another major change for the director, establishing him as a multi-genre powerhouse with another potential hit franchise on his hands. ​​​​​
