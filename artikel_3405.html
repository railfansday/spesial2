Hunter Schafer Breaks Down the Meaning Behind 'Blade Runner 2099's Final Scenes

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Editor's Note: The following contains spoilers for the entire season of Blade Runner 2099.Blade Runner 2099 is officially out — the thrilling eight-episode series from creator/showrunner Silka Luisa drops today on Prime Video. The series takes place 50 years after Blade Runner 2049 and 90 years after the Ridley Scott film. 2099 introduces us to a version of Los Angeles we've never seen before — one run by replicants. We meet Cora (Hunter Schafer), a human working undercover as a replicant Blade Runner. Hiding in plain sight, Cora slowly learns the truth behind this replicant-run world while partnered with Olwen (Michelle Yeoh), a Blade Runner who is dying within a matter of days.

The series takes Cora and Olwen across Los Angeles, ultimately leading them to discover that what they're hunting for is the truth of the Nexus-1 replicants. Cora learns that her adoptive mother, Helena (Sheila Atim) was one of the original replicants, born alongside Julius (Tom Burke), who eventually killed her while hunting for Tyrell's memory, which hold the secret to creating more Nexus-1 replicants. The series ends ambiguously, with Cora returning to the LAPD after Julius' death and Olwen journeying off to space, uncertain whether she's set to discover new worlds or simply finish off the last remaining days of her life.

Collider spoke with both Schafer and Yeoh about that surprising Blade Runner 2099 finale. The two actors discuss their characters' changing relationships and what leads Cora to protecting Olwen in that final fight against Julius. Yeoh also breaks down that thrilling fight with Clotilde (Amy Lennox) and why that scene was so important for Olwen. Schafer contemplates Cora's final conversation with Greta (Katelyn Rose Downey), and what it might mean for the two humans caught in a world full of replicants.

COLLIDER: One of the scenes I really liked in the finale is seeing Cora protecting Olwen from Julius; I thought that was such a great scene to show the development of their relationship. Can you guys talk about developing that bond between these two characters and going from enemies to these close friends?

HUNTER SCHAFER: Yeah. I mean, it was so magical, as we were getting to know each other as we were getting to know our own respective characters better throughout the filming process; it sort of coincided with the growth of Cora and Olwen's relationship. I think it was a matter of exploring it; I think moments would reveal themselves as more human or intimate with us, depending on the nature of the scene. But I really loved... I mean, especially by Episode 6 when they're really seeing each other for who they are; from then there's a beauty to the mutual understanding.

MICHELLE YEOH: I think the most important thing is that there were no more secrets. Because at the beginning, she had her secrets. She was like a bloody human trying to pretend to be a replicant! That's horrifying for Olwen, who is the replicant who thinks she knows everything and knows what it takes to be a replicant and to be lied to by someone like that.

But, at that point, when we came to confront each other and then finally understand that we both had secrets that we kept from each other, and that secrets freed us and made our bond stronger and made us work together, because we were on the same mission, right? We needed to find what was the answer that could destroy this very fragile place that we have. So I think it was important as a human and as a replicant, we have to work together; otherwise, everything will go to hell.

Along the way, they have learned in very strange ways to care for each other, and that bond was beautiful, because, right at the end, it was like, we fought for each other, not just with each other, but at the end it was how to defend ourselves. So I don't think Cora at any point would not jump in front if she felt Olwen was in trouble.

SCHAFER: Yeah, there's a real kinship by the end.

Michelle, you have this great scene where Olwen's fighting Clotilde, and she has the chance to let her fall to her death. Why do you think that she takes that moment and saves her? Because, obviously, that comes in to benefit her later on, but, at that moment, she could just let her die.

YEOH: I know, I think this is almost like a flashback to Roy Batty and Deckard at that point, where it's like, what is humanity? Does it mean that if you were born as a replicant, you can't find the humanity, you don't understand it? And, at that point, you're right, Olwen could have let her go, because she was getting so badly beaten up by Clotilde. I mean, she could have just [been] like, "Die, you bitch," you know? [Laughs] And then I can just move on to do other things. But then that's what it takes to be human. To save another, whether it's someone who doesn't have a soul or has a soul or whatever it is, but not to just let someone die. And I think that was the humanity that suddenly struck Olwen, and she didn't let go, which then also transferred to Clotilde, because otherwise she would not have had clarity in her head.

SCHAFER: Yeah, they both had desires to live.

YEOH: Yes, and also desires to know the truth. Like, what is this guy Julius selling us? Because he's the one that's like, "I gave you this. This is what you could have. You would come back, and you'd be stronger, and you wouldn't have to die." It was a very special moment in that scene which just turned things around.

Hunter, for you, Cora has that final scene with Greta, and I think it's just such an intriguing moment, especially since I really love Greta in the show. What's your read on what's happening there between these two people?

SCHAFER: I think Blade Runner is famous for its open-ended conclusions in that they are open to interpretation, but I think Cora sees herself in Greta. And Greta is such an intriguing character, in another way, I think, as a human who has infiltrated the LAPD in that world of Blade Runners. Greta is a human as well. So, I think there's a kinship that she feels there, even though they've barely spoken. They've only really done a few empathy tests together and had a few interactions, but human-to-human, there still is that connection together.

YEOH: Yeah, that poor human was actually quite a sad figure because she's forced to be there because she has a gift of being able to detect. And I think she detected that in Cora at the beginning because she sort of reached out to call for help, like help me get away from this.

SCHAFER: Well, yeah, I love that because you're not just part of the test, or if it's really her.

YEOH: Yeah, exactly! And that was like, whoa! Is Cora going to "fall for it"? Is it real? That was so cool.

SCHAFER: And then she drops her face, and Cora passes the test; it's awesome.
