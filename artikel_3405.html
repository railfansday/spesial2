Forget The Clone Wars, Disney+'s 25-Episode Anakin Skywalker Series Is Star Wars' True Masterpiece

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Though Star Wars: The Clone Wars is rightfully considered a masterpiece, there's another Star Wars show on Disney+ that serves as a better version of Anakin Skywalker's story. The Clone Wars is often considered one of the greatest Star Wars shows ever made, and only partly because it tells a massive and incredibly nuanced epic saga of war. It's also because The Clone Wars helped make the prequel trilogy of Star Wars movies more cohesive, ultimately leading to the "prequel renaissance" of fans reevaluating Episodes I - III and judging them less harshly than they originally did.

The Clone Wars is undeniably important to Star Wars, which makes it all the more impressive that there's another show in the franchise that handles Anakin's story better. While the show did focus on dozens of Jedi like Ahsoka Tano and Obi-Wan Kenobi, Anakin was the main character of The Clone Wars. Much of the impact the show had on Star Wars at large also revolved around Anakin. He was perceived as a whiny and annoying person in the films, and The Clone Wars transformed him into a much more empathetic and tragic figure.

Even with everything The Clone Wars accomplished for Anakin's story and the Star Wars prequels at large, its direct competitor still has it beat. Genndy Tartakovsky's Star Wars: Clone Wars, a 2D animated series released between 2003 and 2005, chronicles the same stretch of the Star Wars timeline, between Attack of the Clones and Revenge of the Sith. While it's now considered a non-canon part of Star Wars Legends, Tartakovsky's Clone Wars is still better than The Clone Wars.

Based on their own merits alone, Genndy Tartakovsky's Clone Wars is a better show than Dave Filoni's The Clone Wars. As iconic as The Clone Wars is, it has its fair share of problems. The first two seasons of The Clone Wars, for example, featured unpleasant 3D animation, had dozens of filler episodes with weak storylines, and was built around a very kid-friendly "adventure of the week" format.

There's no denying that The Clone Wars became a masterpiece for its final five seasons. By the end of the series, the animation had drastically improved, the episodic stories had gotten far more mature and focused, and the characters featured in the show got a lot more nuanced, relatable, and enjoyable to watch. However, over a quarter of The Clone Wars' seven seasons were just alright at best.

Genndy Tartakovsky's Clone Wars, on the other hand, never had those growing pains. Right from the start, Clone Wars featured beautiful and fluid 2D animation, a coherent story focused on connecting Attack of the Clones to Revenge of the Sith, and some of the best action in any animated Star Wars story, even after more than two decades. It may not have had the narrative depth and scope of the later seasons of The Clone Wars, but it was a master class in efficient visual storytelling.

It's the efficiency of Tartakovsky's Clone Wars that really sets it apart from Filoni's The Clone Wars. In just 25 mini episodes totaling up to about two hours, Clone Wars provides some incredible insights into Anakin's inner turmoil, highlights epic moments from the biggest battles of the Clone Wars itself, and sets up everything from General Grievous' cough to Palpatine's abduction. Most importantly, it's just fun to watch from start to finish. There are no filler episodes of Clone Wars, which is more than The Clone Wars can say.

Importantly, Genndy Tartakovsky's Clone Wars is also a much better story for Anakin than Filoni's The Clone Wars was. Again, that's not to say that The Clone Wars didn't handle Anakin's story well. As previously mentioned, The Clone Wars did an immense amount of work in rehabilitating Anakin's image, showing the signs of Darth Vader that were always in him, and making him both more empathetic and less annoying.

The problem with The Clone Wars' treatment of Anakin is that it did too much for him. The animated show basically features a completely different character from the movies. In animation, Anakin was kind, a good teacher to Ahsoka, and he performed enough feats to have a genuine problem with being passed up for the rank of Master. None of that is in George Lucas' films; the whole point of Anakin was that he was supposed to be whiny, arrogant, and quick to anger, a cautionary tale. The Clone Wars stripped that all away in favor of a more likable but distinctly different protagonist.

Tartakovsky's Clone Wars, however, brought depth to Anakin's character without sacrificing his key personality traits. The micro-series highlighted Anakin's anger during his duel with Ventress and showed how he gained power through the dark side. It foreshadows his transformation into Darth Vader without redeeming him. It also actually depicts some of the changes Anakin went through between movies, like becoming a Knight.

Clone Wars also serves as a better interlude about the three years between Attack of the Clones and Revenge of the Sith. The Clone Wars jams an almost unbelievable number of battles and events into those three years, while Clone Wars lets all of its events breathe. That decision alone makes Anakin's lack of personal development a lot easier to believe, and it also helps support the idea that he was far too immature to be a master. As both a standalone show and a show about Anakin Skywalker, Clone Wars has The Clone Wars beat.
