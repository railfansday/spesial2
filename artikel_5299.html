Woodway Assurance finds European Commission anonymization measures need to be updated to ensure Google search data has low privacy risk

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

BRUSSELS, Nov. 18, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Woodway Assurance, the company behind EviDataâ¢, announced today that Google's end-user search query data could be considered anonymized under established contextual practices, provided certain gaps in the European Commission's anonymization measures are addressed first. Those gaps include specifying explicit criteria for the performance of tools to detect direct identifiers, such as names and email addresses, in search queries, and providing further measures to protect against sensitive information being inferred about individuals. Woodway's quantitative risk assessments have an important role to play in the anonymization process, as they allow risks to be assessed and reported in a consistent, standardised and auditable way. The findings come from Woodway's independent assessment of the European Commission's approach to sharing the data.

Woodway, working with the University of Ottawa and other collaborators, independently designed and conducted its assessment and retained sole responsibility for analyzing the data, interpreting the results and reporting the findings. The company critically analyzed the Commission's anonymization requirements and carried out an empirical motivated intruder testingâ controlled attempts to re-identify individuals â using search and structured data made available by Google. Woodway conducted the testing under a protocol approved by a research ethics board, with defined safeguards for re-identification testing, access to sensitive data and reporting of findings.

Motivated intruder testing, in which testers act like someone determined to identify people in the data, gives real evidence of where weaknesses remain. On its own, however, it cannot show that the risk of re-identification is low across all users and under all the ways the data might be accessed. Woodway therefore complemented the testing with quantitative risk modelling, which combines the test findings with explicit assumptions about attackers, how likely attacks are, and how well the recipients' controls work. A defensible assessment needs both kinds of evidence, with uncertainty and untested scenarios stated openly. Woodway used elements of this modelling to set the threshold against which it interpreted the results.

The main test targeted 98,501 individuals across the six countries covered by the data. Testers gathered publicly available information about named people, such as their professions, interests or activities and, often, contact details like email addresses, and used it to locate those individuals' search records. Google's team verified every match. The attacks re-identified 0.094% of the targeted individuals. Each verified match took an average of 3 hours of effort, equivalent to â¬105. Costs this low would not realistically deter a re-identification attack. In a quantitative model using a predefined residual risk threshold of 0.47%, the observed rate of 0.094% for identity disclosure falls below that threshold.
