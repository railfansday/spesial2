Jiri Prochazka vs. Navajo Stirling prediction, pick: Can ex-champ stop rising star at UFC Qatar?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

MMA Junkie analyst Dan Tom goes in-depth to break down the biggest fights in the UFC. Today, he takes a closer look at UFC Fight Night 294's main event between Jiri Prochazka and Navajo Stirling.

The main event in Qatar features a ranked light heavyweight affair between two men who embody the warrior spirit.

Walking a fine line between self-proclaimed samurai and martial arts madman, Jiri Prochazka brings a unique brand of offense that has become a hallmark of his game.

Seldom taking a back step in the spirit of retreat, Prochazka traditionally comes forward with an unpredictable mix of sporadic feints and shifting footwork that he uses to create offensive opportunities. Whether Prochazka's charging his chakras on the resets or using his long arms for karate-style frames, the fighter from the Czech Republic can quickly and deceptively close distance off of odd looks and tempo changes.

Prochazka also seems to pack a solid spread of dynamic attacks in his arsenal, almost always appearing to be primed to launch flying knees in the face of level-changing threats. And despite his amateur muay Thai accolades, Prochazka typically tends to lean into a crouch variation for his attacks and counters.

But with counters being a potent two-way street in these sorts of matchups, Prochazka will need to respect the powerful shots that Navajo Stirling can generate.

A product of the City Kickboxing stable in New Zealand, Stirling shows all the striking fundamentals you'd associate with a Eugene Bareman-trained fighter. From an educated lead hand to the classic CKB jab and low kick interplay, Stirling's offense appears to be well-schooled in almost every phase.

When throwing in combination, Stirling is not afraid to throw away a shot or two in order to land with something bigger in mind. And when feeling in stride, the 29-year-old is good about punctuating his presence with kicks or knees from either stance (working particularly well off of his sneaky-fast left side).

Although I see left-sided strikes serving Stirling well against someone like Prochazka, he'll need to be mindful of his own defense given the lack of layers and responses we've seen from him in past fights.

Considering the chaos that could be taking place on the feet, I'll be curious to see if either party looks to change things up by interjecting some grappling into the mix.

Prochazka, who is far from a fish out of water on the floor, proved how dangerous his ground game was back when he first won the title opposite Glover Teixeira.

The product of the Czech Republic has always been an urgent scrambler and opportunistic threat, but Prochazka has made clear efforts to sharpen up his wrestling in recent years. Even though most of those improvements have shown themselves in the defensive realm, one can't help but wonder how much of Prochazka's offense has improved.

That said, fighters who have tried to take Stirling down in the UFC have not found it as easy as some might expect.

Akin to many muay Thai-based kickboxers we've seen enter MMA, Stirling shows a natural and semi-surprising predilection toward tie-ups and ground striking from topside.

When fighters clinch with Stirling, the four-year pro is quick to establish an over-under variation in order to turn his opposition into the cage. From there, Stirling will either frame off with a knee or elbow on the exit or engage for a takedown of his own.

Although you could question some of Stirling pursuits in this phase given his on-paper strengths, the City Kickboxing fighter shows solid positional instincts as far as wrist traps and riding are concerned, and his clinch striking also translates seamlessly into vicious ground-and-pound.

The oddsmakers and the public are favoring the undefeated fighter, listing Stirling -172 and Prochazka +140 via FanDuel.

Despite not disagreeing with who is favored, I can't help but feel like this is a trap spot for Stirling.

I know I said the same thing in Stirling's last outing opposite Jan Blachowicz, but Stirling is still awfully young in his career to be making this big and fast of jumps up the ladder. So, if you told me on Monday morning that Prochazka was able to punish Stirling's propensity to dip with a flying knee and follow-up punches for the win, then I honestly wouldn't be surprised.

However, when breaking down this fight from a bird's-eye view, I can't help but see some built-in edges for the Stirling side of the equation.

For one, Stirling, akin to his City Kickboxing stablemates, carries a solid base-level understanding of footwork and cage positioning, utilizing consistent leg kicks and lead-hand interplay. Stirling has also quietly sharpened his wrestling and grappling chops in recent years, so don't be shocked if he exercises the option of changing levels with takedown threats anytime Prochazka comes in hot (threats that strikers like Jamahal Hill and Khalil Rountree Jr. did not have to offer when facing a marauding Prochazka).

As I've stated in previous breakdowns, left-sided strikes, in general, have been Prochazka's common culprit due to his propensity to heavily crouch and dip to his power side when operating out of orthodox stances. And though this only strengthens my reasoning for picking fighters with dangerous left hooks like Alex Pereira to beat him, left hands could be a potent two-way street in this matchup.

But tactical options aside, it's the dynamic of this matchup that makes it so difficult to call.

Does Prochazka's pressure prevail? Or does Prochazka's pressure get him pummeled?

Although I'm more confident in my prediction that this fight will end in the second frame, I'll ultimately side with the younger man in Stirling, whom I suspect will be well-prepared for this opponent considering his camp's recent history of preparing Carlos Ulberg to beat Prochazka earlier this year.

The official pick is for Stirling to find Prochazka's off switch with a left hook and follow-up ground shots in Round 2.

Prediction: Prochazka inside the distance

As the main event, Prochazka and Stirling are expected to walk out to the cage at approximately 3:10 p.m. ET. The fight streams on Paramount+.
