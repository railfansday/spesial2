Dutch Bros' 2027 Outlook: Accelerating New Store Openings to Drive Growth

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

This under-the-radar consumer discretionary stock boasts a Superscore of 80 from our Hidden Gems Primary database, part of The Motley Fool's Moneyball Database system. Here's why.

By Robert Izquierdo – Updated Nov 25, 2026 at 1:43PM EST

Picture the average morning at a Dutch Bros (BROS +4.13%) location. A line of cars wraps around the building, yet the flow remains steady as staff deliver drinks with an energy that feels like a choreographed performance rather than a transaction.

This drive-thru-only beverage retailer has scaled from a regional curiosity to a national powerhouse with over 1,200 locations in 27 states. The stock, trading at $34.27 as of Nov. 19, has faced a volatile year with a 31.8% decline over the past 12 months as investors weigh its rapid expansion against broader market pressures.

Our proprietary Hidden Gems scoring system assigns Dutch Bros an overall Superscore of 80 out of 100, placing it in the Strong category. The Superscore is an AI-powered score that evaluates a company's overall strength by combining financial performance, product market position, technological capabilities, leadership quality, and relative valuation. It represents the unification of all our scores into a single score for public companies, with five rating bands: Exceptional (90-100), Strong (75-89), Above Average (60-74), Average (40-59), and Cautious (0-39).

An 80 places the company in the Top ~10% of every company we score. This score serves as a data-driven signal for further investigation, and the following analysis highlights the tension between its operational growth and the risks that keep the score from moving higher.

Dutch Bros is a highly capital-efficient business, as evidenced by its strong return on net tangible assets. Because it earns a significant profit on a relatively small base of hard assets, each point of revenue growth has the potential to translate into outsized returns, which partially explains why investors are willing to pay a premium for the company's shares despite the inherent execution risks.

With impressive 30% year-over-year sales growth to $549.8 million in the third quarter of 2026, Dutch Bros has delivered blistering top-line expansion. It aims to continue the trend by opening at least 185 stores this year, and accelerating to about 250 new locations in 2027.

Management has established a real estate pipeline that puts it on a path to 2,029 shops by 2029. This development positions Dutch Bros to continue business expansion for the next several years.

While its stock sports an elevated trailing P/E ratio of 45.6, this represents a low point for the past year. The share price fell after the company reported Q3 results on Nov. 4, which showed slowing same-store sales growth. Dutch Bros reported a Q3 year-over-year increase of 4.4% in systemwide same-shop sales compared to 5.8% in Q2. Management also forecasted a softer 2026 full-year outlook to the low end of prior guidance amid customer choppiness as gas prices rose.

These short-term headwinds are likely to ease over time once the conflict in the Middle East winds down. With a multi-year roadmap of store openings, a capital-efficient operation, and a popular menu that extends into proprietary energy drinks, Dutch Bros is well positioned for years of business growth.
