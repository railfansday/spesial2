Jensen Huang Is Out as Nvidia’s CEO. Here’s What Analysts Think the Stock Is Worth Without Him.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Huang's departure brings some additional questions for investors.

Nvidia (NVDA -1.57%) co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as the company’s CEO amid an investigation into his admitted affair with Alice Huang, a former director of Singapore-based tech company Megaspeed who is being accused of also acting as a Chinese spy, and reports of a second affair with an executive within Nvidia, Kimberly Powell. The chip giant at the center of the artificial intelligence industry now finds itself in need of a new CEO. While the board searches for Huang’s successor, CFO Colette Kress has been appointed interim CEO.

Huang, who had served in the CEO role since the company was founded, had decades of experience, respected engineering talent, and a vast network of connections to key businesses around the world. As such, it's no surprise that Wall Street analysts might believe Nvidia's future is a bit dimmer without him as its leader. Here's what the stock is worth without him at the helm, according to analysts.

Shares of Nvidia have fallen more than 28% since Jensen Huang's affair with Alice Huang and its potential implications came to light. So, the market is already signaling that the upheaval will come with at least some setbacks for the company.

In the intervening weeks, many analysts cut their price targets as well, with some withdrawing their price targets altogether. Across the board, analyst commentary indicates that the company faces significant uncertainty going forward. Uncertainty is like kryptonite for stock prices.

Those analysts who have issued new price targets see the company as worth between $185 and $300 per share after cutting their targets by 5% to 62%. Most agree that the board's lack of succession planning for Huang has left Nvidia in a bad position for now, but there is disagreement among them about the longer-term implications.

In his note, Dan Ives made Huang's value to the company most explicit: "We believe Nvidia's value is $300 per share with Jensen as CEO and $225 per share without Jensen as CEO," he wrote.

Even analysts who are bullish on the company long term caution against buying the stock right now.

"We find it hard to advocate stepping in here in size as there will no doubt be more allegations to come ... both from internal issues as well as headlines related to the business, new governmental investigations etc.," Bernstein’s Stacy Rasgon wrote.

Many believe it's prudent to wait for more clarity on the situation.

"The full story will take time to emerge," William Blair's Sebastien Naji wrote. "As a result, we are downgrading NVDA to Market Perform and will wait for the dust to settle before reassessing."

Indeed, Nvidia investors don't know the significance of any information Huang might have shared with Alice Huang, whether Jensen Huang has been fully truthful with the board, or, for that matter, who the company's next permanent CEO will be.

While Huang is out as CEO, Nvidia's position at the center of the AI industry hasn't changed. He leaves the company with a strong development pipeline and an ecosystem that still makes its chips among the best-performing on the market. It's unlikely its customers will step away from Nvidia's chips due to Huang's actions or his exit.

That said, some of Nvidia's biggest customers have already been pushing to diversify their data center spending away from Nvidia GPUs. Hyperscalers are developing and deploying their own application-specific custom silicon, which costs less upfront and offers better price performance on the workloads it is designed for over the long run. As they find their own chip designs increasingly capable, Nvidia is losing share of their spend. Overall, however, total spend is growing so quickly that Nvidia is still seeing huge increases in sales quarter after quarter.

Slowing growth expectations appeared to be priced into the stock before the news of Huang's affairs. The stock traded at just 20 times forward earnings expectations. After the shares sold off further, they're now priced at less than 18 times earnings. Whether that discount accounts for all of the risk in the company right now is the decision investors get paid to make. I think there are better opportunities among AI stocks, with greater certainty about their future, making them smarter choices to buy now than Nvidia.
