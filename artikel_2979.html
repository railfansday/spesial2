Warren Buffett Has Backed This Consumer Stock for Decades, and Greg Abel Is Still Holding It. Here's Why It's a Buy in November.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It's a shareholder-friendly company that has stood the test of time.

By Stefon Walters – Nov 19, 2026 at 2:30PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Coca-Cola ›

For decades, Warren Buffett was synonymous with Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -0.85%)(BRKB -0.69%), the company he helped turn from a modest textile manufacturer into a trillion-dollar conglomerate. Berkshire has dozens of businesses under its umbrella, but its stock portfolio under Buffett became a true moneymaker and a source of investment inspiration for everyday investors.

One of Berkshire's long-standing holdings is beverage giant Coca-Cola (KO -1.89%). It's Berkshire's third-largest holding, accounting for 11% of its stock portfolio as of Nov. 18, and that's likely to continue. Even with current CEO Greg Abel taking over for Buffett at the beginning of this year and adjusting Berkshire's portfolio, Berkshire has kept its Coca-Cola stake.

Here's why I'd double down on the stock for the long haul.

The main reason it's a buy in November is the same reason Buffett has loved the stock, and why Berkshire has held onto its 400 million shares for decades: its dividend. Coca-Cola is one of the stock market's premier dividend stocks. It's a Dividend King (a company with at least 50 consecutive years of dividend increases), with 64 consecutive increases under its belt.

When Coca-Cola pays its next $0.53 quarterly dividend, that will be the last payout from its $2.12 commitment this year. That means this year alone, Berkshire would've received $848 million in dividend payouts from Coca-Cola. That's more than many mid-sized companies make in a year, and with Coca-Cola consistently increasing its annual dividend, it's only a matter of time before the payout reaches the billion-dollar mark.

The everyday investor obviously won't be in the same position as Berkshire to receive that much, but Coca-Cola can be a great dividend stock for most portfolios. With Coca-Cola's stock up over 41% this year (as of Nov. 18), its dividend yield has shrunk a bit to around 2.1%, but its average over the past decade has been around 3%.

Coca-Cola has one of the world's most notable brands, and distribution is a major reason why. Its products are in over 200 countries, and its flagship Coca-Cola soda is one of the most successful consumer products ever.

Instead of trying to do the groundwork in all parts of the world, Coca-Cola relies on third-party bottlers and distributors, selling them syrups and concentrates, which they then package and sell to local retailers. This asset-light business model is why it routinely makes close to half of PepsiCo's (PEP -2.23%) revenue while bringing in more net income.

At its size, Coca-Cola won't produce eye-popping growth, but the third quarter was impressive. Revenue grew 7% year over year, operating income (profit from core operations) grew 12%, and earnings per share (EPS) grew 8% to $0.93.

The solid third-quarter results were mainly due to higher volume, but when that's not the case, Coca-Cola has used its pricing power to keep cash flowing. Many consumers are brand loyal when it comes to their go-to beverage, so slight price hikes won't necessarily make them stop buying it. However, that should be more of a nice-to-have for Coca-Cola than a growth driver.
