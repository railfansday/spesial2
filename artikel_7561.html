3 Mexican Nationals Plead Guilty to Laundering Millions of Dollars in Drug Proceeds from the United States to Mexico

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Three Mexican nationals pleaded guilty for their roles in the collection of millions in drug proceeds in the United States and the return of those proceeds via cryptocurrency or wire transfers to Mexico as part of a money laundering conspiracy.

According to court documents, Eliomar Segura Torres, 37, offered contracts to “money brokers” to pick-up drug proceeds in U.S. cities, including Lexington, Kentucky. The money came from the sale of kilogram quantities of fentanyl, cocaine, heroin, and methamphetamine. Money brokers, including Juan Carlos Michel Martinez, 41, and Manuel Ignacio Correa, 45, then personally arranged for the pick-up and laundering of the drug proceeds through a network of co-conspirators throughout the United States. After delivery of the bulk cash, Michel Martinez and Correa provided instructions to transfer those funds via cryptocurrency or wire transfer. Michel Martinez and Correa received a “commission” based on the percentage of money that they laundered successfully.

Segura Torres, Michel Martinez, and Correa pleaded guilty to money laundering conspiracy. Segura Torres and Michel Martinez are scheduled to be sentenced on Feb. 18, 2027. Correa is scheduled to be sentenced on March 4, 2027. All three face a maximum penalty of 20 years in prison. A federal district court judge will determine any sentence after considering the U.S. Sentencing guidelines and any other statutory factors.

Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division, U.S. Attorney Jason Parman for the Eastern District of Kentucky, and Chief of Operations Matthew W. Allen for the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) made the announcement.

The DEA Detroit Field Division and IRS Criminal Investigation Detroit investigated the case, working closely with the DEA Austin Resident Office and Lexington Resident Office with assistance from DEA’s Special Operations Division and DEA offices in Mexico, Denver, Houston, Atlanta, Chicago, Tulsa, and Youngstown, Ohio.

Deputy Chief Elizabeth R. Rabe of the Criminal Division’s Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section (MNF) and Deputy Chief Gary Todd Bradbury of the Eastern District of Kentucky prosecuted this case.

MNF’s mission is to take the profit out of crime, eliminate drug cartels, and protect the U.S. financial system. MNF pursues criminal prosecutions and criminal and civil asset recovery actions involving: financial facilitators who launder profits for criminals; financial institutions and their officers and employees whose actions threaten the U.S. financial system and financial institutions; international money launderers who support transnational organized crime; and the top command and control of international drug trafficking organizations.

MNF’s Money Laundering and Forfeiture Unit investigates and prosecutes sophisticated money laundering schemes involving financial facilitators, gatekeepers, and other individuals and entities laundering criminal proceeds, and litigates complex civil forfeiture cases to recover assets on behalf of victims.

This prosecution is also part of the Homeland Security Task Force (HSTF) initiative established by Executive Order 14159, Protecting the American People Against Invasion. The HSTF is a whole-of-government partnership dedicated to eliminating criminal cartels, foreign gangs, transnational criminal organizations, and human smuggling and trafficking rings operating in the United States and abroad. Through historic interagency collaboration, the HSTF directs the full might of U.S. law enforcement towards identifying, investigating, and prosecuting the full spectrum of crimes committed by these organizations, which have long fueled violence and instability within our borders. In performing this work, the HSTF places special emphasis on investigating and prosecuting those engaged in child trafficking or other crimes involving children. The HSTF further utilizes all available tools to prosecute and remove the most violent criminal aliens from the United States.
