How many tickets Sheffield Wednesday have officially sold for Barnsley away

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Dave Challinor reign at Sheffield Wednesday finally got going on Saturday, as the Owls won their first league game under their new boss, after a couple of losses in cup action the week prior.

The 3-0 win over fellow play-off side Plymouth Argyle wasn't perfect, by any means, but it's a good starting point, and Challinor will be looking to maintain a 100% winning record in the third tier with his new side in his first away game in charge on Friday night.

Sheffield Wednesday travel to Barnsley for a South Yorkshire derby and one that Owls fans are looking forward to much more than their hosts.

Only goal difference is keeping Daniel Stendel's side off the bottom of the table, with Saturday's defeat at Wigan Athletic taking their winless league run to nine games, picking up just two points in that time. The last time they went 10 without a league victory was back in the 2021/22 campaign, which saw them relegated from the Championship.

Sheffield Wednesday head to Oakwell as strong favourites, and will be hoping to have a party in the away end, which will be full to the brim on Friday night.

Tickets for the game sold out within two days, with 4,810 packing into what is one of the biggest away ends in the EFL.

With the game broadcast live on Sky Sports, it presents a good opportunity for Sheffield Wednesday to showcase their following, which has been reborn under the Arise ownership. This season, attendance records have been broken at Hillsborough, with an average higher than it was back in their Premier League days in the 1990s.

Away from home, Owls fans have packed out away ends all across the country already this season, and for this, their shortest trip of the season, it won't be any different.

However, while it's important to credit Sheffield Wednesday's following, and admit that it will be an impressive visual to see an away end which doesn't always get filled to the rafters do exactly that, this game will have more of a focus on Barnsley.

There have been murmurs online amongst Reds fans about potential planned protests against chairman Neerav Parekh and the direction that the club is sliding dangerously towards, with the proud South Yorkshire side staring down the barrel of a first fourth-tier season since the 1978/79 campaign.

Those who are going due to having a season ticket aren't planning on spending money in the ground, with those who haven't committed to the season ticket put off by the £35 price for adult tickets in the home end, anyway.

Whether there'll be a protest during the game which halts play is still to be seen, but with this being broadcast on Sky, it presents a good opportunity for an angry set of fans to make themselves heard.

Barnsley's average attendance at Oakwell is decreasing, but more concerning is the lack of away fans travelling to watch games, too, which arguably speaks more damningly to the current state of the club.

Only 447 travelled to Reading a few weeks ago, and just 711 were at Wigan on Saturday on a much shorter away day. For a club that, last season, had an average away following of over 1,100, it is quite concerning.

Daniel Stendel has had to put his faith in young stars this season, with the Tykes having the lowest average age in the EFL. While some of those, such as Reyes Cleary, Patrick Kelly, and 18-year-old Arley Kay, who hadn't played a professional game until this past summer, have shone, it's evident that the lack of experience is killing them.

Neerav Parekh is no Dejphon Chansiri, but like Sheffield Wednesday fans in the latter stages of their former owner's reign, Barnsley fans are sick, tired and near-apathetic about their club at the moment.

There's a big chance on Friday that over 33% of the crowd at Oakwell will be away fans, and while that will be a massive negative for Barnsley fans, they'll hope that, under new direction, perhaps they could rediscover the excitement that Wednesday fans clearly have this season.
