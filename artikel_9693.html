Copywriters: revision vs. rewrite — get the brief approved first

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

"Two revision rounds included" sounds clear until round two arrives with a new audience, a new offer and a new stakeholder. That isn't a revision. It's a rewrite, and rewrites are new work.

The line that makes this easy to hold: a revision refines the approved brief; a rewrite responds to a new brief. That only works if there is an approved brief, in writing, before you draft a word.

Add this clause to your services agreement:

Brief approval: Drafting starts after Client approves the messaging brief. Changes to the brief after approval are billed as new work.

Then list the brief as its own deliverable in the SOW, priced flat, so the client sees it as a real step and not admin.

And one more clause worth having: the client is responsible for verifying factual, medical, financial or legal claims in final copy.

When "extra round" is already a priced line, asking for one stops being awkward.

For the first, ask before you touch the draft:

Subject: Quick details on [REQUEST SHORT NAME]

Hi [NAME], thanks for sending this. Before I place it in the plan, could you confirm what "done" looks like, the format/audience, and who has final say? Once I have those, I'll confirm whether it fits the current revision round or outline the smallest adjustment needed.

Subject: [PROJECT] — treating [REQUEST] as a scope change

Hi [NAME], thanks for the note on [REQUEST]. Compared to our approved brief ([DATE]), this looks like a change because [ONE CLEAR REASON]. I'm pausing on that piece until we pick a path, so we don't burn timeline or budget by accident. I'll send options shortly: trade scope, move the date, add a fee, or defer.

Log every one of these in a simple revision log (date, request, revision or change, decision). It's the thing you point to when the invoice gets questioned.
