Prediction: SpaceX Stock Could Go Parabolic After Anthropic's IPO. Here's Why.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Anthropic is expected to list on the Nasdaq on Nov. 19.

Anthropic is preparing for what is expected to be one of the largest initial public offerings (IPO) of all time. The company behind the Claude chatbot is expected to price its IPO on Wednesday, Nov. 18, before beginning trading on the Nasdaq Stock Market on Thursday, Nov. 19.

Much of the attention around Anthropic's IPO has focused on Amazon and Alphabet, two companies with meaningful financial and strategic relationships with the AI developer. However, I see Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +3.03%) emerging as an unexpected winner of Anthropic's public debut.

A closer examination of Anthropic's S-1 filing reveals that its relationship with SpaceX is much larger than previously thought. This potentially creates an overlooked catalyst for Elon Musk's aerospace and AI infrastructure company.

Anthropic's public debut will be an important test of Wall Street's appetite for AI stocks. The offering comes during a period of heightened volatility among technology stocks.

Amazon and Alphabet are obvious candidates to benefit from Anthropic's IPO because both companies have invested heavily in the start-up and provide cloud computing infrastructure to Anthropic's models. An enthusiastic reception could increase the market value of their respective Anthropic positions while reinforcing investor confidence in their AI road maps.

SpaceX presents a different opportunity. Following its IPO back in June, SpaceX has increasingly positioned itself as a diversified technology company spanning reusable rockets, satellite connectivity, and AI infrastructure. Anthropic's upcoming listing could draw renewed attention to the scale of SpaceX's computing business, particularly as investors scrutinize the AI developer's spending commitments.

A few months ago, SpaceX disclosed that Anthropic had agreed to pay $1.25 billion per month for access to computing capacity across its Colossus I supercomputer. The arrangements involved access to 220,000 Nvidia GPUs, with payments scheduled through May 2029. At the full monthly rate, this deal represented $15 billion in annualized revenue potential for SpaceX.

However, Anthropic's amended S-1 paints a much different picture. Between May and October, Anthropic entered into an amended agreement with SpaceX.AI, a subsidiary of SpaceX. According to the filing, Anthropic agreed to spend up to $120.5 billion on SpaceX computing services through 2029, with aggregate monthly payments reaching about $3.1 billion once the expanded capacity is fully scaled.

This represents an annualized revenue of approximately $37.2 billion, nearly 2.5 times the previously disclosed amount. Anthropic expects the additional compute to be delivered by December, suggesting the expanded arrangement could begin contributing to SpaceX's revenue in the near future.

Importantly, this agreement demonstrates that SpaceX is swiftly becoming a meaningful supplier of AI compute to major frontier labs rather than relying exclusively on its own AI applications such as Grok.

The interesting aspect of Anthropic's relationship with SpaceX is that investors may not fully appreciate its significance. Although the original agreement was publicly disclosed, the new, larger arrangement appeared within Anthropic's lengthy IPO registration statement. It's highly likely that most investors haven't yet accounted for the expanded economics in their expectations for SpaceX's AI infrastructure business.

I think Anthropic's IPO will change that. As analysts pour over the company's financial statements, computing obligations, and infrastructure partners, SpaceX's role may receive substantially more attention after Thursday. As such, this could strengthen the argument that SpaceX is quietly evolving into a new kind of hyperscaler, capable of serving external AI developers alongside its aerospace, satellite, and proprietary AI applications.

In my eyes, Anthropic's IPO could become an unexpected catalyst for SpaceX stock if investors begin to assign greater value to its fast-growing AI infrastructure segment. The difference between the originally disclosed agreement and the expanded contract is too meaningful to overlook. If Anthropic's IPO brings that relationship into sharper focus, SpaceX could easily attract renewed investor enthusiasm as one of the AI industry's increasingly important infrastructure providers.
