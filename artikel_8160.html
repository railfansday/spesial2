Running from Another Rain Storm on the Arizona Trail

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

This morning we were up and hiking right as the sun came up. I slept really well last night at our tent site. Which is great, because we have a long day of hiking ahead of us today. As of today we have about 100 miles to get to the highway crossing where we can hitch into the town of Pine. It’s Monday morning and a big storm is going to start rolling in late Tuesday night or early Wednesday morning. Our hope would be to be as far south as possible before the storm hits so we can head into town and avoid the imminent flash flooding on trail.

Right off the bat once we got walking this morning we got to enjoy an absolutely incredible sunrise. There was a thick layer of fog rolling in along the surrounding mountains and it settled just below the line of the sunrise. That was beautiful and was a fantastic way to start off our day. The plant life is so lush here right now and the deep greens of the nearby pine trees were complemented well by the fog and glow of the sun coming up. The section of trail that followed had some tall rock walls which were bright orange. We’ve been told that the bright orange rock is actually notably more bright because of the moisture from recent rain. How nice for us!

This sunrise this morning was unbelievable. The trail went right past this cliff and the valley below was filled with fog. The layer of fog rested so perfectly beneath the rising sun.

As we continued along the trail the water sources were super limited. Luckily we filled up last night and each still had a good amount of water this morning. Because the first couple of water sources were cow ponds that looked like chocolate milk! I love chocolate milk, but not chocolate milk-colored water. We bypassed those sources and just hoped for some fresher water up ahead along the trail. For a lot of the morning we were continuing through shaded pine forest. There were some adorable little wildflowers adorning the trail, some of which we hadn’t seen yet on our hike!

I loved the way this red cliff looked, surrounded by pine trees.

In the late morning the trail changed a bit and felt a lot more like a desert environment. The ground was more of a clay/dirt and there were hardly any trees at all. Around this time we saw a massive tarantula crawling along the trail. It was really cool standing there and watching it make its way by us. Then not long after that we saw another huge tarantula! Apparently they are migrating this time of year for mating season. It’s wild to picture tarantulas migrating any sort of distance considering how tiny they are. Perhaps that explains why we saw more than one tarantula though in such a small vicinity.

A fairly large tarantula on the trail. It might be the biggest tarantula I’ve ever seen.

In the afternoon the trail passed by another one of the cool rusted trail markers that it has. The marker is a map of the trail and is engraved, noting how far the distance is to both Utah and Mexico. As of around noon today we had 558 miles remaining of the trail and have traveled over 243 miles. Not too shabby considering we had to take off 3 days for the storm that hit our first week on the trail.

An adorable fluffy little flower alongside the trail.

At this point in the day we were approaching the Mormon Lake area. There is a campground and some sort of restaurant and store there by the lake. Some of our friends popped in there for food and drinks when they passed through. If we wanted to end our day there we would have had to hike a little more than 40 miles today. But there’s no need to do that. We have plenty of food and are actually going to see Gorf tomorrow morning at a road crossing past Mormon Lake. She let us leave some food at her place and offered to drop it off to us tomorrow morning. That way we didn’t have to carry as much for the bigger stretch of mileage between towns.

I really love these Arizona trail signs because they actually indicate where you are at on the trail!

Over the course of the day I was looking at the map and trying to figure out exactly how far we should go and what our options were. I checked the weather and saw that the heavy rain was expected to start in the early morning on Wednesday. Then I started to look around on Far Out at the map to see about some alternate options to the original plan. We have to hike around 35-40 miles today and tomorrow to make the following day even remotely close to 30 miles. Which means that likely we will get to the highway sometime Thursday evening, even if we push out massive mileage the next few days. When I looked on the map though I saw another trailhead about 30 miles before the one we were intending to stop at. If we got off there to go into town instead, then we would only have to do big miles today and tomorrow and maybe be able to get into town Tuesday evening. Otherwise we’ll be hiking for probably a full day in the heavy rain which does not sound like fun.

Only 558 miles remain to the Mexican border! At least as of this morning.

After thinking about all of that I felt a bit of relief about our plan. Sure, we’ll still have to hike big days today and tomorrow. But originally we were thinking that we might have to hike in a full day of rain in order to get to town. Doing 35 miles in heavy rain can be pretty brutal. Usually the biggest issue for me is either being far too wet and cold, or getting major chafing. Plus, all of our friends are going to be in town! 9 Lives messaged me the other day when they got into Pine. Another hiker she was hiking with got injured and wanted to take a couple of days off. So they made the decision to just stay in town for a handful of days to let him heal and then wait out the storm. They got an Airbnb in the town of Strawberry which is right by Pine. Which means if we can get a hitch late tomorrow night, we have a place to stay! It will be a blast waiting out the storm with friends. And when we get back on the trail we will only be 30 or so miles behind them. Originally we started a week after everyone! But thanks to bad weather and big miles, we have caught up significantly. Then when we do hike out after the storm we will hopefully be able to catch up quickly and finally hike with everyone. We’ve been chasing our friends for this whole trail practically.

A large bone just off the trail. Perhaps from the shoulder or collar bone area of a cow?

In the afternoon we finally reached our first decent water source of the entire day. It was around 2PM and we actually had another handful of miles before the marked source that we were aiming for. But we crossed under a bridge that had a pretty clear pool of water beneath it and we couldn’t resist. It looked so much better than any other water we’d seen all day. Getting water earlier meant not having to worry that the next source was gross or dry. We took a nice break there and filtered water. I knew we had a long way to go for the night so we also pulled out more food to each snack on. Then we continued along, enjoying our last few hours of daylight left.

Grabbing water from a delicious puddle along the trail. Somehow this was a much better source than any of the actually listed water sources earlier on in the day.

The next couple of hours went by quickly as we continued along a dirt and pine-covered trail. There was hardly any climbing at all and the trail wasn’t rocky or tough to navigate. It was about as smooth of sailing as it could possibly be. Then the sun slowly began to go down and we enjoyed golden hour as it coated the trees and surrounding brush in its glow. All of the small wildflowers and the tall wheatgrass have such a lovely glow in the early evening. I’ve really enjoyed watching the plants dance around in the breeze, especially as the sun is beginning to set. There was also a dense cloud covering the mountains off in the distance. It sat perfectly on top of them and made for a great view as the sun went down.

The way that these clouds looked resting on top of the mountains in the distance was only made even better thanks to the setting sun.

Before it got dark, the trail crossed over a dirt road and we walked right through a small campground. It appeared mostly closed for the season and the water had been turned off. The privy’s were also locked, which is always a disappointment. But there was actually a really lovely creek flowing through the area though which we were able to enjoy. It was nearly 6PM at this point so we just decided to fill up for the night at this source. That way we could hike as far as we wanted to this evening and not have to worry about getting water later. It was also a good excuse to take another break. The final break of the night before it would get dark.

Lovely views as the last bit of sunlight lit up the trail.

Lucky for us, our early start this morning meant that it didn’t have to be a wildly late night tonight. Our goal was to go around 36-37 miles, and the terrain graciously accommodated us. After passing through that campground we continued on into pine forest for most of the remainder of the evening. Before 8PM we had already made it around 36 miles for the day. Around then we started keeping our eyes peeled for campsites and wound up walking a bit longer before we found a nice one. In the end we did 37.2 miles for the day and I actually felt really good.

Once we got to our site I got situated, made hot chocolate, and snacked a bit. Then I did my stretching and a little bit of editing before calling it a night. We didn’t make it all the way to the Mormon Lake area, but we’re only about 6 miles short of it. We texted Gorf to coordinate a meeting with her tomorrow morning. She’s going to drive out around 8AM to meet us at the trailhead past Mormon Lake. Which is pretty funny now that we’re planning to go into town tomorrow evening from an earlier road crossing! We get to load up heavily on food and then carry it for another nearly 35 mile day. I actually did a great job this time around though and have the perfect amount of food leftover. So I won’t be loaded up too terribly once we see Gorf tomorrow.
