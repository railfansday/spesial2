I don't agree with your "loan from government" perspective w.r.t. money put in tax-deferred retirement accounts (you've argued that in other threads, and I don't see it that way (and others have said the same)).

It's not a loan if no taxes were due on the money, and there may NEVER be taxes due on the money. All sorts of decisions can mean that no taxes (or an extremely low tax rate, lower than the tax rate you paid for the tax year when the money was originally put into the tax-deferred retirement account) will ever be due on the money put in a tax-deferred retirement account. This has been discussed in other threads. They can include (among many other potential options) QCDs, high medical expenses that lower taxes, pre-tax retirement balances that aren't high enough to generate much income, even when RMDs kick in. (One of many threads discussing similar issues: viewtopic.php?t=476254 )

It's important to keep in mind it's not necessary to convert all pretax funds to Roth. For some people, there's no need to convert any. For others, just a portion will be sufficient. The specifics depend on the exact circumstances of each person's situation, now and what is projected for their future, and their own goals for their retirement, and whatever estate they have left after death (plus the tax and estate laws of their state, and the federal government, plus their own opinions about any possible future changes to any laws).
My apologies for the insulting comment, your apples to oranges comparisons led me to believe you were making false assumptions.

Regarding "10% later is always the better option", I and others have pointed out that they are mathematically equivalent due to the commutative property. We're merely asking in what way is it "always better"? You are not "in control" of your entire tIRA, just try spending a sizable amount from your tIRA in one year.

Which brings up another oft overlooked advantage of conversions, you can accumulate a sizable balance by converting moderate amounts at reasonable tax rates over several years. In some ways "10% now" and over several years could actually be preferred by providing spendable cash that's 100% "in your control".
