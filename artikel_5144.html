Asian stock markets suffered heavy losses on Wednesday, with roughly $750 billion in value erased across the region’s major exchanges. South Korea was hit hardest. The KOSPI fell as much as 6%, prompting the exchange to pause program selling for five minutes, the 62nd such halt this year. Taiwan’s market dropped about 4%, Japan’s fell around 3% and mainland China slipped about 1.1%. Chip and AI stocks bore the brunt of the selling in Tokyo, where Kioxia sank 13.7%, Advantest lost 11% and SoftBank fell 9.6%. The losses follow fresh weakness in US AI stocks and reports of new US tariffs targeting China, the European Union and 14 other countries, though the tariff details have not been officially confirmed. The pressure is set to carry into Wall Street, with S&P 500 futures down 0.7% and Nasdaq futures off 1.2%. The rout comes just two days after markets rallied on Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia.
