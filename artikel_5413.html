Egan-Jones Notes Higher Borrowing Costs Alongside Steady Credit Fundamentals

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

NEW YORK, Nov. 25, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Egan-Jones released its November 2026 review of US credit conditions, finding that borrowing costs sit well above year-ago levels while corporate and consumer credit quality has held up. The review attributes most of the increase to higher Treasury yields, with wider spreads adding to it.

The 5-year Treasury yielded 4.59% on 5 November 2026, against 3.76% a year earlier. Spreads, the extra yield investors require over Treasuries, widened as well: BB-rated spreads reached 213 basis points on 5 November, their widest since March, and BBB spreads stood at 116.

Credit fundamentals have not followed. Median interest coverage, which measures earnings against interest expense, stands at 11.4x across 404 issuers on their latest filings, and the June quarter-end median of 10.3x was the highest since 2023. Leverage trends divide by sector, with 48% of covered real estate issuers improving while 53% of energy issuers worsened, even as oil and gas ranks strongest on the firm's measure of credit momentum.

Consumer credit has also improved. Credit-card delinquencies fell for an eighth consecutive quarter, to 2.85%, and unemployment, at 4.1% in October, is below its 4.5% peak of November 2025. Collateral prices are mixed, with commercial property up 8.8% and homes 3.0% over the year while used cars and apartments are down. After an allowance for delinquencies, Egan-Jones calculates, a new-car loan now yields less than a 30-year mortgage.

The review finds how Egan-Jones ratings have performed over the longer record. Across issuer groups formed each year from 2000 to 2025, recorded default rates on the firm's unsolicited ratings, made from public information with no issuer fee, sit below the published rates of two legacy agencies at investment and speculative grade and at one, five and ten years, though Egan-Jones cautions that the three may not define default the same way.

In every year from 2015 to 2025, other agencies' rating actions moved toward the Egan-Jones rating on the same issuer at least three times in four.
