Elisity to Support Texas Water Utilities Through Project Watershed 250

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

No-cost assessments and hands-on microsegmentation support help utilities advance Zero Trust using existing infrastructure

DALLAS, Nov. 17, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Elisity, a Zero Trust security company, today announced its participation as an eligible Assessor in Project Watershed 250. Led by Texas Cyber Command, Project Watershed 250 is a pilot connecting Texas water and wastewater utilities with no-cost cybersecurity assistance. Elisity will offer assessments of utility networks and hands-on help implementing segmentation on compatible existing infrastructure, with plant operators controlling when policies take effect.

The offer includes engineering time and project management, along with the software needed for the pilot. Participating utilities will receive an inventory of in-scope assets, a map of network communications and prioritized remediation recommendations. Elisity IdentityGraph brings together identity, asset and risk context to inform access policies. Where infrastructure supports enforcement, engineers will work with operators to limit devices to the communications they need, testing policies in simulation before activation.

"Water utilities need to keep essential operations running while they strengthen security," said James Winebrenner, CEO of Elisity. "Zero Trust starts with understanding what is connected and limiting access to what each device needs to do its job. We're committing engineers and software to help utilities put those controls to work on the infrastructure they already own, at no cost during the pilot."

Project Watershed 250 brings together the White House Office of the National Cyber Director, the Office of the Governor of Texas, Texas Cyber Command and private-sector participants. The pilot addresses resource gaps facing water providers, including small and rural utilities with limited cybersecurity staffing. Texas Cyber Command facilitates introductions; each utility independently chooses its Assessor and authorizes the scope of work.

Elisity's offer draws on its existing work with the City of Waco, Texas. After deploying the pilot in less than a day, Waco deployed Elisity across three water and two wastewater treatment plants in 41 days, using existing network infrastructure without adding hardware or re-addressing devices.

"The plant operator has to know what a security change will do before it goes live," said Mike Searight, senior adviser to Elisity and former chief information officer of the City of Waco, Texas. "Start with the devices and the communications the plant depends on. Test the policies against that traffic, review the results with the people running the systems, and let them decide when to enforce. That's how I want us to approach this work with water utilities."

Utilities will receive a no-cost assessment and segmentation design, with the gaps documented for future planning. No purchase is required. Participants retain their assessment deliverables and data exports, and pilot services do not automatically convert to a paid subscription. Software expiration and closeout arrangements will be documented with each utility before work begins.

Information about the pilot is available at https://www.txcc.texas.gov/project-watershed-250. Participation does not constitute a contract with or endorsement by Texas Cyber Command.
