Shipwreck Off Senegal Kills Dozens as Canary Islands Declare Migrant Emergency

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Migrant arrivals to Europe are down overall but still claiming scores of victims, overwhelming local officials and dominating political debate.

By Amelia NierenbergSaikou Jammeh and Carlos Barragán

Saikou Jammeh reported from Dakar, Senegal, and Carlos Barragán from Madrid.

On one side of the water, a nation is in mourning. On the other, alarms are sounding.

The dueling reactions in Senegal and the Canary Islands came after dozens of Senegalese migrants trying to reach Europe died when their boat capsized last week. Their deaths along one of the world’s riskiest migration routes occurred at a moment when arrivals to Europe are down overall but still overwhelming local officials and dominating the political debate.

The Senegalese authorities said on Monday that at least 39 bodies had been recovered off the shore of the West African nation, where stubbornly high unemployment has driven many young men to attempt dangerous sea crossings to Europe in recent years.

More than 100 people were on board the boat that capsized on Friday, according to Senegal’s interior ministry. A local official, Ndeye Nguénar Mbodj, said the migrants had been hoping to reach Spain, and she warned on Monday that the death toll could rise. One person was rescued and others swam to shore, but it was unclear how many others had made it or were missing, she explained.

Many migrants try to reach Europe by sea in the fall, when conditions in the Mediterranean and the Atlantic can be more favorable. The Greek Coast Guard said it recovered 20 bodies from the Mediterranean on Monday alone. Last month, at least 139 people died in a capsizing near Djibouti, one of the deadliest shipwrecks ever recorded on that route. Another popular crossing runs from West Africa to Spain, often via the Canary Islands, an autonomous community of Spain about 600 miles to the north.

That’s where the authorities were scrambling to address a spike in the number of migrants reaching one island, El Hierro, in the Spanish archipelago. More than 5,000 migrants have arrived on El Hierro over the past nine days, the local authorities said on Monday. They declared a state of emergency to unlock funding for a response on the island.
