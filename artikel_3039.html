What's Going On With Netflix's Stock?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Netflix shares have fallen sharply. Is the market overlooking a long-term opportunity, or is slower growth a warning investors shouldn't ignore?

By Lawrence Nga – Nov 22, 2026 at 12:00PM EST

Netflix (NFLX -2.96%) has had a difficult year. Its shares have fallen roughly 45% over the past 12 months (as of this writing), despite the streaming giant continuing to generate billions of dollars in revenue and profits.

The sell-off reflects a growing concern: Netflix may be entering a slower-growth phase just as investors expect its advertising business to become a major source of future revenue.

I don't think Netflix's business is broken. It remains highly profitable and has ample opportunities to increase revenue from its global audience. Still, Netflix needs to prove it can keep growing, build a successful advertising business, and continue producing shows and films that people want to watch.

Three developments explain why investors have become more cautious.

Netflix's latest results show a business that is still growing, but at a slower pace.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue increased 11.8% year over year to $12.87 billion. That's respectable growth for a company of Netflix's size. However, it was also its slowest revenue growth in recent quarters.

The slowdown has been gradual. Revenue growth declined from 17.6% in the fourth quarter of 2025 to 16.2% in the first quarter of 2026, 13.4% in the second quarter, and 11.8% in the third quarter. Management expects 11.5% growth in the fourth quarter.

Why does this matter? Investors have long valued Netflix for its ability to grow faster than traditional media companies. But as its business gets bigger, maintaining that growth becomes harder. Netflix also competes for something every entertainment company wants: people's attention. YouTube, TikTok, video games, and other streaming services give consumers more choices than ever.

To win in the long run, it must keep giving viewers a good reason to open its app. If it cannot, attracting new customers and raising subscription prices will become harder.

Advertising was supposed to become Netflix's next major growth engine.

The opportunity is easy to understand. Netflix has a huge global audience and a library of popular shows and films. Offering cheaper ad-supported subscriptions can attract more price-sensitive customers while generating additional revenue from advertisers.

However, having millions of viewers doesn't automatically make a successful advertising business. Netflix recently lowered its 2026 advertising revenue expectation from approximately $3 billion to $2.7 billion. Management said delays in rolling out certain advertising features had slowed progress.

This isn't a disaster. Advertising revenue is still growing rapidly. But the lower forecast shows that building this business is harder than simply putting advertisements between shows.

Netflix has the audience. Now it must prove it can turn that audience into a meaningful advertising business.

Ultimately, Netflix sells entertainment. Its ability to produce popular shows and films is what attracts viewers, supports increases in subscription prices, and gives advertisers a reason to spend money.

Management has acknowledged that the company can do better. Netflix has also changed its senior content leadership, with Peter Rice replacing Bela Bajaria as chief creative officer. It has also announced approximately 1,000 job cuts, representing around 6% of its workforce.

These changes suggest management wants to improve its content offering and focus on making Netflix a service people cannot do without.

Don't get me wrong: There's nothing to suggest that Netflix's content creation is deteriorating. Besides, there are reasons to remain optimistic. Netflix's operating margin reached 34.6% in the third quarter, up from 28.2% a year earlier. Management expects full-year revenue of $51.1 billion and an operating margin of 31.5%.

In other words, Netflix is making more money from each dollar of revenue. Its financial position remains strong. But higher margins alone won't guarantee future growth. Netflix still needs to produce content that keeps viewers coming back.

Netflix isn't facing a financial crisis. But there is evidence that it's facing a growth challenge.

The company remains profitable, generates substantial cash flow, and has opportunities to earn more from its existing audience. But slower revenue growth, advertising delays, and questions about content quality have made investors less confident about its future.

I remain interested in Netflix's long-term potential, but I'd like to see advertising execution improve and its content strategy deliver stronger results before becoming more bullish.

In other words, I'm not rushing into buying the stock today.
