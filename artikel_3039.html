Nvidia's Troubles Are a Stark Reminder of This Investing Lesson

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Even the strongest investment cases are not foolproof.

In recent days, perhaps no mega-cap stock has surprised investors more than Nvidia (NVDA -2.01%). The stock attained the world's largest market value under the vision of co-founder Jensen Huang, ultimately leading to dominance in the AI accelerator industry.

Now, Jensen Huang's tenure at Nvidia has ended amid his personal indiscretions. The incident serves as a reminder that no investment case is guaranteed to succeed, and investors need to always be prepared to adapt as new information comes to light.

Huang stepped down after admitting to being "unfaithful" to his wife. The infidelity allegedly involved an affair with a woman rumored to be tied to Chinese intelligence, a situation that has tremendous implications for both Nvidia and the U.S.'s national security amid AI competition. He also faced allegations of an affair with a subordinate.

Due to this shocking news, Nvidia and its investors are in a difficult position. CFO Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while the company's board searches for a permanent replacement, but investors may understandably be confused as to how to move forward with the stock.

The incident serves as a stark reminder that no investment case is 100% solid. Thus, for Nvidia investors who are not particularly diversified, it may be a reminder for Nvidia shareholders to rotate some holdings into other investments.

The good news for shareholders is that Nvidia was never the only successful investment, and the market continues to offer investors opportunities. While not every individual holding will succeed, investors who buy numerous stocks in multiple sectors protect themselves from such uncertainty while giving themselves the opportunity to prosper from company successes.

Moving forward with Nvidia itself is more uncertain. Its revenue of $178 billion in the first half of fiscal 2027 (ended July 26) rose by 96% year over year. Nonetheless, rapidly rising capital expenditures (capex) by its customers call into question how long this growth can continue.

The capex-related concerns may not be as costly as losing Huang as CEO. Some founder-led companies (such as Apple under Tim Cook) prospered after their founders departed. Others, such as BlackBerry, were forced out of their original business without the guidance of the founding leaders.

Ultimately, investors need to avoid viewing one single stock as a guaranteed success and prepare for the unknown by staying diversified.

Nobody could have predicted that a personal indiscretion by Jensen Huang could have threatened the investment case of Nvidia. However, investors always need to prepare for the unexpected, and they can mitigate that by spreading their holdings across multiple investments.

What to do about one's Nvidia holdings probably comes down to risk tolerance. For now, Nvidia benefits from the massive demand for AI accelerators, giving current shareholders a tremendous incentive to stay in Nvidia.

However, Nvidia's path forward brings with it tremendous uncertainty. Those who cannot handle the possibility may want to reconsider owning this chip stock, at least for now.
