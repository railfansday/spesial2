RTX Unit Pratt & Whitney Wins $2.5 Billion Navy Contract

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The company's aircraft engine subsidiary will perform work on the F-35 Lightning II fighter.

By Eric Volkman – Nov 27, 2026 at 2:01PM EST

Storied defense company RTX (RTX +1.17%) notched a win just before Thanksgiving with a new and sizable contract from the U.S. Navy. This is hardly the first contract it's secured with our nation's military, and it surely won't be the last.

Regardless, it's another feather in its cap and, hopefully, a sign of good things to come for both the company and Pratt & Whitney, its aircraft engine subsidiary. Here's some detail about the deal, and how it'll affect RTX.

On Wednesday evening, after the stock market closed for the Thanksgiving holiday, the Department of Defense (DoD) announced the award of the contract. Specifically, this is a nearly $2.53 billion award to Pratt & Whitney to deliver equipment and services for the F135 jet engine that powers the advanced F-35 Lightning II combat aircraft.

Per the DoD's press release on the award, it's an "undefinitized contract action with cost-plus-fixed-fee, cost-plus-incentive-fee, cost, and fixed-price-incentive-fee contract line items" for the plane.

Put in less bureaucratic language, the arrangement is for the RTX subsidiary to begin work on the project now, with the understanding that certain terms (such as final payment) are to be finalized later. This is a fairly standard type of defense company contract with the U.S. military, used when there is some urgency to a project, or simply a need to keep one running consistently and smoothly.

Many experts consider the F-35 Lightning II to be the most advanced multi-role fighter aircraft in our arsenal today. Given that, the Navy needs to ensure there are minimal gaps in the fleet's repairs and upkeep.

The DoD added that the bulk of this work will be performed in corporate facilities or on military bases in this country. 14% is expected to be performed abroad, specifically in Italy, the U.K., Norway, the Netherlands, Japan, and Australia. The project is expected to be completed in November 2027. Defense was to obligate over $436 million upon contract award.

This wasn't a particularly surprising development, which is likely why the investor reaction to the news was muted (RTX's stock was up only 0.5% in pre-market trading on Friday). Much of this is due to the long history both Pratt & Whitney and RTX's legacy Raytheon business have with the F-35 project, which stretches back to its beginnings in the 1990s.

Additionally, RTX is a sprawling defense conglomerate that pulled in nearly $89 billion in revenue last year. Of course, $2.5 billion-plus is more than just a drop in the bucket, but it's not going to suddenly transform the company in a meaningful way.

That said, the new F-35 contract is sizable. The DoD's announcement was part of a broader press release detailing six recently-awarded contracts by branches of the U.S. military. RTX/Pratt & Whitney's was the largest and more than double No. 2, a $1.13 billion award from the Navy to Northrop Grumman (NOC +1.02%) for work on strategic communications aircraft. Outside of the Northrop Grumman contract, the others are in the tens of millions of dollars.

The DoD is also significantly increasing its reliance on RTX, as the new contract's ceiling is around 58% higher than the preceding sustainment award RTX and its subsidiary received in late 2025. So, at the very least, I'd take this as a significant act of confidence and trust in the company and its veteran engine specialist to perform mission-critical work during a time of increased global conflict. That's enough to keep me bullish on RTX's future.
