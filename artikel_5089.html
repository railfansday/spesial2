Bitcoin slipped back below $62,000 on Tuesday after a sharp rally stalled near $64,000. The chart still leaves room for a recovery that could carry the price above $100,000, but getting there will take a series of technical steps that haven’t happened yet.

The largest cryptocurrency has rebounded 16% to 17% from its weekend low near $55,000, its biggest bounce since the decline began. The move also broke above a resistance level, which Bitcoin hadn’t done once during the sell-off.

The rally hit a wall at about $64,000, the price level with the heaviest trading volume on the chart. Bitcoin is now on track to close the day below $62,088. That level acted as support in July and August and has since turned into resistance.

Despite the swings, the bigger picture has barely changed all year. Bitcoin dipped below its July low only briefly and is back above it. That keeps the price inside a wide range that is starting to look like a megaphone pattern, with swings getting larger in both directions.

The market is oversold, and selling momentum on the MACD is fading. Both point to better odds of a larger bounce. Strong support begins at $56,350, the same area where buyers stepped in back in July.

There is no firm timeline for the move. The first test comes over the next few sessions, when Bitcoin needs to hold short-term support between $57,010 and $60,755. A deeper pullback into that zone is likely before any fresh push higher.

In crypto, rallies driven by headlines often fade quickly, and this bounce has been choppy rather than forceful. A rejection inside the first resistance zone would put lower prices back in focus. Even a breakout might only lift Bitcoin to the upper edge of its 2026 range, well short of $100,000.
