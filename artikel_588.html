Jesse Eisenberg Says Fans Aren’t Crazy With Him Because ‘There’s No Sexuality to My Work’: Life Is ‘Different’ When People ‘Want to Sleep With’ You

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Jesse Eisenberg recently told the New Yorker that fans of his are “mostly fine” when he’s in public, a fact he attributes to his generally sexless resume.

“People are mostly fine,” he said to reporter Rachel Syme when asked if strangers ever interrupt him when they spot him working on scripts. (The “A Real Pain” actor told Syme that his typical schedule revolves around sitting at a branch of the New York Public Library, where he writes from nine to five). “It’s because there’s no sexuality to my work,” he continued. “I think once the public is asked to want to sleep with a person onscreen, that person’s life is fundamentally different.” Related Stories 'Avedon' Review: Ron Howard's Thrilling Documentary About Richard Avedon, the Great Photographer Who Turned Head-On Portraits Into an Art Form 'Hershey' Review: In a Sugary Sweet Biopic, Finn Wittrock Plays the Candy Man Who Turned Chocolate Into an American Empire

Though, despite Eisenberg’s assertion, his “Fleishman Is in Trouble” co-star Claire Danes told Syme that people began to “rubberneck” when the two had a coincidental run-in on the street and stopped to chat. “People are very aware of him as a figure, even though he is so determined to be invisible,” she said.

Elsewhere in the lengthy profile, Aaron Sorkin, who directed Eisenberg as a young Mark Zuckerberg in “The Social Network,” told the outlet that “I hardly know him at all,” noting that the actor reminds “me of Holden Caulfield, if that makes any sense. He has absolutely no patience for phonies.”

Eisenberg earned an Oscar nomination for the part, but has since been outspoken about his decision not to reprise the role in the director’s follow-up, “The Social Reckoning,” released earlier this year. “I’d be out at a bar, and a drunk guy would say, ‘Hey, I love your website,’” he told the New Yorker. “And as I’m pretending to laugh, I’d just wish all of the oxygen would leave my lungs, and that I would collapse on the floor in front of this guy and show him what he’s doing to me.”

The actor-turned-director, who is currently promoting “The Debut,” his latest project, also spoke about creating “nonhierarchical” environments on his sets. “How could you be making a movie about a woman who’s an underdog, who no one notices, and then have a set where the people walking around feel like that?” he said. “You’d have to be so stupid and blind and compartmentalizing, in a psychopathic way, to write a movie about that and then perpetuate it.”

“The Debut,” Eisenberg’s third feature as a director, stars Julianne Moore as Mona Friedman, a New Jersey housewife who auditions for a part in her local theater’s production of a fictional musical. After she’s cast as Miss Danielle, Friedman’s life begins to unravel as she becomes more consumed with the role and falls under the spell of director Jerry Blankenbuehler (Paul Giamatti).

“The Debut” opens from A24 in theaters on Dec. 11
