The MAC runs through…Michigan?!

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Mid-American Conference is a rarity in college basketball's current landscape. The group of 12 – temporarily 13 in 2026-27 – teams last season has remained relatively local, despite television deals and the increasing stretch of NIL funds.

The MAC's three staples in its northernmost representative, Michigan, have been a defining part of the end-of-season MAC Tournament chase year after year down in the league rankings. Last season, the trio were 10th, 11th and 12th in the conference – all tied by overall record at 10-21.

They rarely fight for titles. Rarely do they produce Players of the Year, but they can always be relied upon for end-of-season dramatics.

Two weeks into the 2026-27 season, Eastern, Central and Western Michigan sit at a combined 10-2, with the Chippewas – who were last in the MAC last season – still undefeated through four games.

The MAC looks like it might run through Michigan this year, for many reasons. There are new coaches, Player of the Year hopefuls, and early promise of sustainable success.

Last year, Central Michigan brought in Andy Bronkema from Ferris State. Bronkema had done little less than establish the program as a Division II dynasty before getting his first Division I shot in Mount Pleasant.

He had to bring in 15 new players, cobbling together Ferris State call-ups, high-major rejects and whoever else he could find.

The other Michigan schools followed suit this past offseason. Kahil Fennell – formerly of UT-Rio Grande Valley – was announced as Western Michigan's 16th head coach in March. Six days prior, Eastern Michigan brought in Billy Donlon, who spent the last four years assisting Brad Brownell at Clemson.

Coaching changes were a theme across the MAC this offseason. Some of the new hires were asked to sustain success, like Dustin Ford at Akron, but others were forced to turn around a program in the dumps, like the three at the helm in Mount Pleasant, Ypsilanti, and Kalamazoo.

Of the MAC's leaders in box-plus/minus, five of the top 10 are from the three Michigan schools, including the top two.

The Eagles' Mohammed Habhab and Jaiden Martin have been the MAC's two most impactful players by the metrics' estimation. In two games, Habhab is averaging 20.7 points, 8 rebounds, and 2.7 assists per game. Martin, an NJCAA transfer who played a key role on Parkland's championship team, is averaging 12.7 points and 2.7 boards per game, shooting a blistering 9-of-17 from the 3-point range.

Over in Mount Pleasant, the state of Michigan's former No. 1 high school prospect, Phat Phat Brooks, is taking over in year two with the Chippewas. The 6-foot-2 guard is averaging 21 points per game, shooting 65.9% from the field. Efficiency has been the difference, especially from 3, where Brooks is just one more 3-pointer away from tying his total made last year. His partner in crime has been 7-foot-2 center Conrad Luczynski, a transfer from D2CCA Lenoir-Rhyne. The duo is fourth and seventh in the MAC in box-plus/minus.

Then, it's Lamar transfer Rob Lee Jr. taking the reins for the Broncos of Western Michigan. Lee's style is that of an undersized, combo-guard scorer. He's been prolific from all over the floor, shooting 60% from both 2 and 3, as well as 75% at the free-throw line. His exploits position him as the MAC's fifth-most impactful player early on.

Western Michigan isn't just 4-1; it's the third-ranked team in the MAC by adjusted overall efficiency, behind powerhouses Miami (OH) and Akron. The Broncos are bombing from deep, shooting 46% of their shots from beyond the arc, and 42.7% on those shots – the 13th-best mark in the country.

Their 3-point defense is the biggest discrepancy of any number they've posted thus far. Opponents are shooting 33.9% of their shots from deep – the 39th-lowest mark in the country – and 26.7% on their 3s. The shooting makes up for a lack of shots at the free-throw line, lifting the Broncos to a top-35 eFG% mark in the nation.

Despite a smaller backcourt, they're also dominant on the defensive glass and blocking shots.

It's not the same level of dominance for the other two Michigans. Eastern Michigan is dotted with red across its statistical profile, and narrowly escaped being 1-2 in a 71-70 opening win over Coastal Carolina, in which the Eagles posted a poor 45.3% eFG.

Central Michigan may also be due for a short-lived time at the top. The Chippewas have played Louisiana-Monroe, Alma, Youngstown State and Northern Kentucky in their 4-0 start, with games against Dayton, Pitt, and Notre Dame before starting their conference slate against Miami – all on the road. Central Michigan has played well in its start, but tough times may be ahead.

Only time will tell if the MAC runs through Michigan, but fans of each program should enjoy their time at the top for as long as it lasts.
