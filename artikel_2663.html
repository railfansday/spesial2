'You'd think it would be because of Rohit or Virat' - Kyle Jamieson on how Vaibhav Sooryavanshi incident forced him to turn off social media comments

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

New Zealand pacer Kyle Jamieson opens up on the online backlash that made him shut off comments.

A Vaibhav Sooryavanshi wicket in Jaipur drew a reaction from fans nobody expected.

The Delhi Capitals pacer was also sanctioned by the match referee after that incident.

New Zealand fast bowler Kyle Jamieson has revealed that it was the wicket of a 15-year-old, Vaibbhav Sooryavanshi, and not a modern great, that finally pushed him to switch off the comments on his social media accounts.

Jamieson, who turns out for the Delhi Capitals in the Indian Premier League, removed Sooryavanshi early in the Rajasthan Royals innings, and the celebration that followed is what set off the storm.

The incident took place in Match No. 43 of the tournament, at the Sawai Mansingh Stadium in Jaipur. Jamieson sent down an accurate yorker in the second over of the Rajasthan innings and cleaned up Sooryavanshi for just 4.

What caught the eye, though, was his reaction after the stumps were hit. The tall bowler let out an aggressive clap and walked up close to the batter, staring at him in a way that many viewers read as confrontational. Because the batter was only 15, the send-off was widely seen as unnecessary and capable of provoking a response.

Soon after the game, the pacer was found guilty of a Level 1 offence under Article 2.5 of the IPL Code of Conduct. That rule covers language, actions or gestures that disparage another player or could provoke an aggressive reaction from them.

Jamieson admitted the charge and accepted a one-demerit-point penalty handed down by match referee Rajeev Seth.

According to Jamieson, abuse from fans is not new to him, and he has learned to live with a bit of stick whenever he takes a big wicket. Getting rid of Kohli or Rohit, he said, would normally bring a few unkind comments his way, and he usually brushed them off without much thought.

This time, however, the reaction was on another level, and it was enough to make him turn off his comments.

"It was the final straw that made me turn my comments off on social media. Usually it was like a wicket of Virat or Rohit or something that would get you a little bit of stick, but, yeah, apparently getting a 15-year-old out is not a good thing," Jamieson said on the Sport Nation NZpodcast.

The host teased the bowler once more by saying "bullying now, mate", and Jamieson responded in the same spirit, but there was a clear note of sarcasm in his answer.

"You can't get out someone who strikes at 400. I don't know who you can get out," he said.

Jamieson's comments are funny on the surface, but they point to something quite serious about the way fans and players interact today. A bowler dismissing a batter is the most normal event in cricket, and yet a single wicket was enough to make an international player close the door on his own followers.

It is easy to understand why supporters feel protective of a teenager who has captured the imagination of the country. Even so, a fast bowler who produces a perfect yorker is only doing his job, and no one should have to face a flood of abuse for taking a wicket. The line between passion and harassment has become very thin online, and cricketers are often left to manage it on their own.

That said, Jamieson himself should accept that the send-off was the spark. The league's code of conduct exists for a reason, and a clap and a stare at a 15-year-old, however spontaneous, was always going to be noticed. The referee's decision to give only a warning and a demerit point looks fair, because it recognises the lapse without treating it as something far worse.

The positive from all this is the humour Jamieson has shown in talking about it. His sarcastic line about a batter who strikes at 400 captures the absurdity of the situation. If supporters can cheer for their young stars without attacking the opposition, the sport will be better for it.
