Nokia vs. AT&T: Comparing Cyclical Sales Patterns vs. Steady Revenue

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

AT&T's revenue base dwarfs Nokia's by roughly five times, but Nokia's sharp Q4 spikes reveal a business model that operates on a different rhythm entirely.

Nokia Oyj (NOK +2.17%) primarily generates revenue by engineering and delivering advanced mobile radio access technologies, fixed fiber access solutions, and specialized cloud network infrastructure solutions to global communications service providers, major internet hyperscalers, and digital industries.

It recently completed the sale of its business in fixed wireless access customer premises equipment to Inseego Corp. for an ownership stake, and it signed a letter of intent with the Ministry of Defence of Ukraine to develop customized satellite communications systems.

AT&T (T -0.23%) primarily generates revenue by delivering domestic wireless voice communication services, mobile data solutions, high-speed fiber optic internet, and integrated cybersecurity technology offerings to an extensive base of residential consumers, small enterprises, and multinational corporate clients.

It recently announced an agreement to sell its Mexican telecommunications operations, while securing a multi-year infrastructure agreement with Corning Incorporated for ongoing supplies of connectivity solutions.

The revenue trajectories of AT&T and Nokia tell very different stories to investors. Both are experiencing year-over-year sales increases, but while the former shows steady top-line growth, the latter sees spikes in the fourth quarter that reflect how IT customers seek to spend their budgets before the end of each calendar year.

Nokia is trying to galvanize greater sales by shifting its focus toward high-growth artificial intelligence and cloud networking markets. In its third quarter earnings report, the company's revenue represented a 9% year-over-year jump on a constant currency basis, but its sales to AI and cloud customers grew 123%. Over time, its current revenue trajectory could look very different from the last eight quarters if its AI business continues to post triple-digit growth.
