Cowboys vs. Eagles: Brian Schottenheimer tops our losers list

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Dallas Cowboys looked like they were going to run the Philadelphia Eagles out of AT&T Stadium on Thanksgiving.Then everything changed.

Dallas built a commanding 31-10 advantage before allowing the Eagles to storm back, turning what should have been a comfortable victory into a frantic finish. There were some outstanding individual performances, but also several familiar problems that ruined the afternoon and saw the Eagles secure a comeback victory.

It's hard not to lay a lot of the blame for this loss on the shoulders of head coach Brian Schottenheimer. His repeated inability to finish out games has crushed the Cowboys hopes this season.

Late in this game, Schottenheimer had a chance to be aggressive and go for the killing touchdown with Dallas up 38-36. Instead, once he got in field goal position he got conservative and played for the three points. Brandon Aubrey missed the kick.

Later in the fourth quarter, he had a 4th-and-1 decision to make. He chose about the worst avenue. Instead of punting, or kicking a long field goal, he went for it even though not converting would give the Eagles the ball close to field goal range. To make matters worse, while going for it, he didn't use Javonte Williams, or got Prescott out on the edge with a run/pass option. Instead, he called a drop back pass which resulted in a sack.

It was gross mismanagement of the end of the game.

Dallas stopped Philadelphia from running the football. Then Joe Milton and DeVonta Smith ripped the secondary apart. Milton threw for 331 yards and three touchdowns, averaging over 12 yards per attempt.

Smith was even more damaging, producing 11 catches for 200 yards and two touchdowns, including scores of 50 and 45 yards, those numbers are insanely brutal.

Dallas repeatedly allowed receivers behind the secondary and surrendered exactly the kind of explosive plays that have plagued this defense throughout the season. The Cowboys had Philadelphia buried at 31-10. The pass defense gave them a route straight back into the game.

This might have been the single worst play of the afternoon. Dallas led 31-17 early in the fourth quarter and simply needed to get the punt away. Instead, Philadelphia blocked Bryan Anger's punt and recovered it for a touchdown. Suddenly, a comfortable two-touchdown advantage became a seven-point game.

A blocked punt returned for a touchdown while trying to protect a fourth-quarter lead is about as bad as it gets. Then, of course, there's the missed Brandon Aubrey field goal that would have kept the game out of reach at the end of the fourth quarter. That field goal was crucial and he failed to connect the kick for three points. It's a brutal miss but it was just another addition to the miscues on special teams this week.

Dallas led 31-10, Philadelphia then scored 29 points while Dallas managed only seven. The Cowboys allowed a backup quarterback to turn what looked like a Thanksgiving demolition into a game that came down to the final minute.

The blocked punt played a major part, but this wasn't one isolated mistake. Explosive passes, penalties and defensive breakdowns all contributed. Dallas has shown repeatedly that it can build leads, but they haven't learned how to finish opponents. We've failed to see this repeatedly so don't expect it to change.

Dak Prescott gave Dallas everything it needed offensively. Prescott completed 19 of 28 passes for 291 yards and two touchdowns, while also adding a 14-yard rushing touchdown in the fourth quarter. He consistently attacked Philadelphia downfield, protected the football and kept answering whenever the Eagles threatened to take control of the game.

CeeDee Lamb continued his outstanding run of form. Lamb finished with seven receptions for 134 yards, averaging more than 19 yards per catch despite not finding the end zone.

His ability to create explosive plays consistently stretched Philadelphia's defense and opened opportunities elsewhere for George Pickens and KaVontae Turpin. Lamb has reached the stage where 100-yard performances almost feel routine.

Javonte Williams carried a massive workload and rewarded Dallas for sticking with him. Williams rushed 29 times for 101 yards and two touchdowns, while also catching a 12-yard touchdown pass from Prescott.

His one-yard touchdown opened the scoring, his 12-yard run extended Dallas' first-half advantage, and he later added the receiving score just before halftime. Three touchdowns on Thanksgiving is a pretty convincing way to land in the winner column.
