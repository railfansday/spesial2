Rams PFF grades: Aaron Donald and Myles Garrett turn back clock

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The first NFL pre-Thanksgiving game brought a delicious victory for the Los Angeles Rams on Wednesday night. LA defeated the Green Bay Packers 26-13 to move to 7-4 on the season. After starting 2-4, winning five-straight didn't seem to be in the cards. Their fortune seems to be shifting even in the face of injury.

Who stood out on an individual level from this game? Let's review the Pro Football Focus (PFF) grades and data in order to lean into some analytical takeaways.

*Final grades are not currently available as of the writing of this post. Due to the Thanksgiving holiday I am going to run with the data still under review. Close enough.

Mumpfield was the Rams' passing offense on Wednesday night. He earned 69 yards on four catches and provided the only threat vertically. The next closest pass catchers in terms of yardage were Blake Corum at 21 and Kyren Williams at 19. Xavier Smith was the only other receiver to catch a pass and his solo reception went for four yards.

Is that a reliable way to play offense and win games? Not really. The Rams must find better answers from their receiver group. They cannot rely on Davante Adams to completely reverse their fortunes on his own, especially coming back from a significant injury this late into his career.

Dedich filled in for the injured Bill Murray who had replaced Steve Avila in the starting lineup. Dedich always seems to fare well in terms of PFF grading, and it's encouraging to see him pickup where he left off after suffering an injury this summer that has limited his availability. Dedich is undersized. That may prevent him from ever becoming a full-time starter. He still has value in a reserve role.

Veteran tight end Tyler Higbee's most important contributions came as a run blocker where he earned a mark from PFF of 70.2. He added one catch for 14 yards and came close to another big play. Higbee played roughly half the game before suffering an injury and being ruled out. Davis Allen was also ruled out. The statuses of both are worth monitoring moving forward.

What more is there to say about Blake Corum's performance not just Wednesday night but over the last seven weeks? Against the Packers he grinded out 184 yards and a score for an average of 5.80. PFF credited the back with 103 yards after contact (3.22 average). The offensive line was road grading as well, as they generated 2.58 yards before contact on Corum's carries. Not a bad day at the office.

It would be a welcome development—given the amount of players across the Rams' roster that need new contracts—if Limmer could takeover the starting center job on a going forward basis. Injured starter Coleman Shelton is approaching the end of his 2025 free agent deal. Limmer has one year left on his rookie contract. He continues to excel in pass protection in the middle of the offensive line.

Matthew Stafford finished 17th in grades out of the 19 players who appeared on offense. PFF graded him at a mark of 54.2. The outlet did not credit the veteran quarterback with a single big-time throw. He had one turnover-worthy play that resulted into an interception that could have proved costly; however, Jordan Love made a mistake of his own just two plays later.

Stafford has now thrown an interception in all but two of 11 games this season. He's at 11 on the year, so averaging one per game in the aggregate. If the Rams are going to be limited offensively and lean into ball control into order to constrict the opposition into submission, one requirement of that is taking care of the football. Can the Rams trust Stafford in this area? His track record so far this year suggests not.

Rams fans are waiting for Stafford to turn his season around. It hasn't happened yet. He's still the 21st-graded quarterback by both offensive grade and passing grade. It's clear he has taken a major step back this year. The end could be nearer than expected.

It's difficult to say anything other than 2027 seems to be Ty Simpson's year to takeover.

Donald and Garrett each had five total pressures on Jordan Love even though neither finished with a sack. The Rams only brought down Love once (Bryon Young), although the pressure did ultimately force three turnovers by the Pakcers' offense. Love easily could have finished with at least five interceptions—including dropped picks by Quentin Lake and Trent McDuffie and a takeaway by Kam Curl that was reversed because of penalty.

Kinchens forced a major collision along the sideline with receiver Christian Watson that dramatically altered the rest of the game. Kinchens was ejected, which resulted in controversy. Watson entered concussion protocol and was ruled out for the second half. The Rams seemingly had no answers for the dynamic receiver up to that point, and the Packers had little going through the air in the second half without Watson.

Make it another hallmark punch out for Nate Landman. He stole the ball away from tight end Tucker Kraft for a key takeaway.

The veteran linebacker played just 25 snaps. Omar Speights had 10. Shaun Dolac played seven. The roles of these players are changing as Lake took over the green dot duties. That isn't necessarily a bad thing. The defensive line is limiting opposing rushing attacks on their own. The Rams are able to pivot more towards additional safeties to focus on tightening up through the air. It's working. Stick with it.

Lake finished with the second-highest coverage grade on the defense (87.0) behind only Landman (90.5). He dropped an interception that likely would have scored and put the Packers away even earlier. In total he finished with two pass breakups.

The Packers went after reserve corners Josh Wallace and Emmanuel Forbes often with mixed results. Each had four targets into their coverage. Wallace allowed three receptions for 15 yards and the touchdown to Watson. Forbes gave up three catches for 79 yards (36 after the catch) and he caught the interception that effectively sealed the game.

While their PFF marks were low relative to the other members of the defense (18th and 19th out of 20), these coverage numbers aren't something that will get you beat. The standard is not perfection for these players as they look to replace injured starter Jaylen Watson. There will be negative moments. The Rams must hope that the positives—like Forbes' interception—outweigh the bad.
