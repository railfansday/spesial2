'We belonged'

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Nov. 27—MOSES LAKE — The Moses Lake Mavericks 2026 football season has come to an end after suffering a 56-28 loss to the Puyallup Vikings in the quarterfinals of the 4A State Football tournament. No matter the result, Head Coach Kyler Haneberg said he is proud of how his team fought to the final whistle.

"That's the number one takeaway, is they showed up," said the coach. "Our number one thing is don't be fearful. They got the jump on us early, but we were able to claw back a little bit, make it interesting, and we kind of stuck around with them for quite a long time and I think the score doesn't show how close it really felt on both sides."

The Vikings found the endzone first, taking a 7-0 lead in the first quarter. From there, they added three more in the second while the Mavericks found some momentum to put seven of their own on the board to make it 28-7 at halftime. The Mavs battled throughout the second half, putting up three scores. However, the Puyallup offense was too tough to stop as they added 14 points in both the third and fourth quarters, sealing the win to advance to the semifinals.

Though they fell short, the Mavs offense was productive, especially in the run game. Big 9 League MVP Elijah Burns continued his efficiency on the ground with 106 yards and two touchdowns on 22 carries. Teammate John Rainey also put up 100 yards on six carries and scored two touchdowns.

"Our guys showed they could fight and they belonged there," Haneberg said. "Everyone kind of picked them to get blown out by five touchdowns, but I think we showed that we belonged the whole time."

Another positive the Mavericks are taking away from this game is Burns' performance being good enough to set the single season rushing record with 1,498 yards, surpassing Moses Lake legend B.J. Garbe. The Mavericks running back room also made their own history after putting up a team total of 2,658 yards.

"It was kind of a cool thing to be a part of," said the coach. "And that game set us over the team total rush record for the school, so I was really happy to see that with our guys, and that was kind of something in the back of my mind I wanted to accomplish coming into the game."

With his first season as head coach now under his belt, Haneberg feels the Mavericks were able to accomplish a lot in terms of setting a foundation and garnering community respect.

As he looked back on the season, he felt their games against the Richland Bombers and Wenatchee Panthers were statement wins where Moses Lake really began to figure out what they're capable of.

"The Richland game for sure, I think, let the state know that we're still hanging around," he said. "I think that Wenatchee game, the way they were getting all the hype, they were going to be back to what Wenatchee used to be. I thought they were a great team, and the way we were able to pound the rock and put it on them and pull away the way that we did, kind of let me and the rest of my staff know that this team is legit, and we can we can go as far as these boys want to take us."

Moses Lake was a motivated group this season, too. After such a successful season the year prior, they felt they were ready to prove they weren't going anywhere.

"The outside chatter is hard to ignore when everywhere you go, you hear about it," Haneberg said. "You hear the naysayers, and so we used what people were saying as our fuel this year. I think the boys really bought into that, and they wanted to show we are more than what we were, and that no matter what, we're going to continue moving forward. Just because some guys leave and we graduate quite a bit of guys, that we're still Grind City football."

The Mavs are graduating seven of their eight captains, back-to-back Big 9 Defensive MVP John Rainey being the lone returner out of that group. As they turn their focus to the offseason and eventually the 2027 season, Haneberg is eager to learn who the next group of leaders will be.

He cited a few players like quarterback Nolan Decubber and receiver Case Anderson that give him the confidence they're going to be in a similar situation as last year, to reload, not rebuild.

"That's going to be the biggest need, is (which) guys are going to step up into those leadership roles. Towards the end of the season, we started seeing a couple of those guys doing it," he said. "I'm really proud to see those guys doing it as well, and I think that's just the biggest change right now. We know we got dudes at the skill levels that will help us succeed, because the way that our youth program has been going in the middle schools and the CBYF and Grid Kids, and then what we've seen on our sub varsity teams, we know that Moses Lake is going to be around for a long time."

Haneberg noted the potential he sees in players like Grady Green, Romeo Moreno-Jackson, River Hutchinson, Jackson Miles, Royce Reffett and Gabe Walker to elevate their impact on the team next season, as well.

"It's our skill position is what's huge right now, we're going to have to go out with five receiver sets because we got five, six receivers that we can't afford to not have on the field," said the coach. "Then defensively, Royce Reffett at our outside linebacker stepped up immensely. Gabe Walker coming off a freshman year; he had three sacks at linebacker, and then we moved him to D-line, and he finishes with five or six. I could name off 20 guys that potentially could see major roles for us."

With the 2026 season now in the books, Haneberg looks ahead to the future with the same hunger and fire as he came into this season with.

"Looking forward to the off-season grind and then getting back after it, next year. Can't come soon enough. I tell you that," he said. "I'm standing right here across from Lions Field, waiting for a birth certificate for my daughter, and kind of wants to bring me to tears that I'm not going to be there in a couple minutes."
