Dwight Howard regrets shutting down the Magic's attempt to trade for Steph Curry

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Dwight Howard regrets shutting down the Magic's attempt to trade for Steph Curry originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

In an alternate timeline, Steph Curry is representing the Orlando Magic.

Curry has established himself as an NBA legend over the past 18 years, but his tenure in the Bay Area could have been over soon after it began.

On "The AM Hoops Show," former Magic superstar Dwight Howard told host Casey Keirnan that he shot down an opportunity to team up with the future two-time MVP in Orlando.

"At the time, I wanted to play with Monta Ellis," Howard said. "The Magic wanted to get Steph, and I said 'No.' I said no because I didn't know. I didn't know Wardell Steph Curry was gonna be one of the greatest players in the world."

When asked to expand, Howard clarified that Curry was dealing with injuries during the 2011-12 NBA season.

"(Steph) was hurt … At the time, Monta was the one who was playing all the minutes," Howard said. "Stupid. No, I regret it."

Howard proceeded to go silent for several seconds while appearing to think about his decision to shut down the trade.

At the time, Howard was a three-time Defensive Player of the Year in the midst of a stretch of five consecutive First-Team All-NBA selections.

Howard averaged 20.6 points and 13.9 rebounds per game during that span, leading the NBA in rebounds four times and blocks twice, while taking the Magic to the 2009 NBA Finals.

Curry, meanwhile, dealt with recurring ankle issues that threatened to derail his young NBA career. He played just 26 games in the lockout-shortened 2011-2012 season, as Golden State went 23-43.

Orlando traded Howard to the Los Angeles Lakers the following offseason, starting Howard's decline from superstardom.

Howard later tweeted that he had the chance to play with Curry during the 2022-23 season but wasn't signed by Warriors general manager Mike Dunleavy Jr.

Curry has become one of the greatest players in league history, and it is interesting to see how one trade rejection changed the course of the NBA.
