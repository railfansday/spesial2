Bridgeland star Kash Schwab plays through a shoulder injury as the Bears chase a long playoff run

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

As the afternoon sun beamed down on the football field at Bridgeland High School on Tuesday, the smooth and soulful melody of "It Was a Good Day" by Ice Cube drifted through the air. For senior running back Kash Schwab, any day he gets to strap on the shoulder pads is a good day. It's not an opportunity he ever takes for granted.

"I started playing when I was young - really young," Schwab said. "I was maybe 3 or 4, and I just kind of fell in love with it. I played all sports, though: basketball, baseball, soccer, everything. Then I kind of fell in love with football going into middle school. That was the sport that I felt like I could play at the highest level."

Playing at a high level hasn't been a problem for Schwab, the Bears' dynamic game-breaking star who is hoping to keep his final high school campaign rolling for as long as possible. Bridgeland (10-1) will play Jordan (8-3) at 1 p.m. Saturday at Legacy Stadium in Katy with a spot in the Class 6A Division I regional semifinals up for grabs.

For Schwab, the second-round playoff game represents another chance to go to battle with his teammates, and they're hoping to make an extended stay in the postseason after stumbling to an early exit a year ago.

"It's tough knowing that at some point you won't be playing with these guys ever again," Schwab said. "But at the same time, you're taking in every moment, every practice. … Being out here with the guys is fun, and I'm excited to keep going. I don't think we're done yet. I don't think anybody out here wants to be done yet."

Bridgeland High School running back Kash Schwab, center, takes a handoff during practice at the Bears' practice field in Houston, Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026. (Brett Coomer/Houston Chronicle)

Schwab has been one of the top running backs in the Houston area since his breakout sophomore season, which ended with a trip to a regional final and various individual accolades, including Newcomer of the Year honors from the Chronicle. With more than 5,000 career rushing yards, the UNLV commit hasn't slowed down since.

Schwab has rushed for 1,920 yards and 30 touchdowns in 10 games this season. He is averaging 9.8 yards per carry and has piled up 2,196 all-purpose yards and 33 total touchdowns when factoring in his work as a receiver. During the Bears' dramatic 30-28 victory over Austin Vandegrift in the opening round of the playoffs last week, Schwab rushed for 263 yards and three touchdowns on 33 carries and added two catches for 47 yards.

Senior kicker Austin Anderson connected on a 27-yard field goal with 1:20 remaining to give Bridgeland the lead, and Vandegrift missed a 47-yarder on the other end as time expired.

"It was crazy," Schwab. "I think we knew that game was going to be close. It's the playoffs. You can't underestimate a team. That's a tough (Vandegrift) team, a physical team. I think we just trusted our kicker to go make a kick when we needed it, and he did it. And then we needed a defensive stop, and they did what they needed to do."

When Schwab first arrived on campus, Bridgeland head coach Lonnie Madison remembers him being a "tall, thin little running back type of kid." Madison didn't know much about him at the time, but once Schwab started breaking off long touchdown runs for the freshman team, the premonition that he could be a special player started to develop.

Three years later, Schwab is now a versatile 6-foot, 195-pound back capable of scoring any time he touches the ball.

"I think one of the biggest things is just his physicality," said Madison, who has coached Bridgeland to three district championships in four years. "He's never been afraid of contact. He's never shied away. He's always been great in pass protection, lead blocking for other people. He's always been good at those things. But his physical style of running this year has really improved dramatically. He's always been able to make guys miss, but now he's running through contact, running through people. He's just a bigger, stronger version of himself this year as a senior."

Schwab has a very distinct running style. According to Madison, he has a masterful understanding of what the Bears are trying to accomplish on each snap and knows how to use his offensive linemen. Every play is different, of course, and Schwab relies on his vision and instincts to guide him. But most of his best highlights have some common denominators. Once he finds a crease, he sticks his foot in the ground and explodes through the hole. When Schwab reaches the second and third levels of the defense, he becomes even more dangerous.

Madison said Schwab's high football IQ and approach to each carry will serve him well at the college level because he's not just relying on his pure athleticism. He's executing his assignment.

"He's got some of the best vision of any running back that I've seen in 24 years of coaching," Madison said. "Some running backs are better at your gap schemes where you kind of tell them where to go. Some are really good at zone because you can kind of let them go wherever they want, and they can reverse field. He's good at all of it. He knows how to set up his pullers, his blockers. He knows where the vision is on zone schemes, inside and outside. So he is a one-cut guy and rarely gets caught once he breaks into the open field. Not a lot of wasted movement."

Bridgeland High School running back Kash Schwab (24) runs with the ball during practice at the Bears' practice field in Houston, Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026. (Brett Coomer/Houston Chronicle)

Schwab has been dealing with a left shoulder injury for weeks now. He's been wearing a brace for stabilization and already has plans for surgery once his season is over. But despite that ailment, he's continued to thrive. After sitting out of a district game against George Ranch in early October, Schwab knew he couldn't take anymore.

After getting scans and having the necessary discussions with medical professionals and his future coaches at UNLV, Schwab decided to keep playing. That selflessness has paid huge dividends for the Bears, who have won eight consecutive games since suffering their only loss in September against an elite Ridge Point team.

"I want to play through those things because this means so much to me," Schwab said. "I missed the George Ranch game, and right then I could tell that I couldn't deal with that. So I had to get back out there."

Throughout his football career, Schwab has leaned on his faith. He has a script tattoo on his right forearm of the Bible verse Proverbs 3:5, which reads, "Trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding. In all your ways submit to Him, and He will make your paths straight." On his left arm, Schwab has a tattoo of a tree with a cross and crown of thorns, further illustrating the beliefs that have molded him as a person.

"Knowing that everything is God's plan, I think that's a big thing that I fall back on," Schwab said. "Sometimes things do get tough, but he's always there. He's always there for me, and I can always talk to him."

Faith has played a big role for Schwab during his recruiting journey. Rivals has him rated as a three-star recruit, and while the major power conference offers never came, he was able to find a home at UNLV. Schwab committed to the Rebels and head coach Dan Mullen in May and remains locked in as the early December signing day approaches.

"It's been stressful and crazy," Schwab said of the process. "I don't think people understand how stressful it is. You get the offers, but then you've got to go find out which school is best for you, and that's tough. You've got all these coaches that are trying to come get you, but you never know which ones are really for you. So I think the relationships were the biggest thing for me to help me develop as a person and, of course, as a player."

Schwab is excited for his future out in Las Vegas, but for now, he's focused on the task at hand - beating Jordan. Bridgeland won 12 games and reached the fourth round of the playoffs in 2024 before falling to Dallas-area power Duncanville. But the Bears were ousted by Klein Collins in their postseason opener a year ago, and Schwab and the rest of the returning players are determined to atone for that shortcoming. Everything else is just outside noise.

"I think there were a bunch of people who had us going 6-4 this year," Schwab said. "And I was like, who cares? It doesn't matter at the end of the day. People are going to say stuff. That's just what it is. You can't worry about that kind of thing. You've got to go out there and just show up. We have to go out there and show who we are."
