DC Announces The Justice League’s Biggest Crossover In Years After The Death Of A Beloved Anti-Hero

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Justice League unite with Darkseid and some of their greatest villains for an unexpected crossover that leaves all previous conflicts aside. The Justice League have headlined legendary crossovers pioneered by 1963's Crisis on Earth-One!, which brought together the Silver Age Justice League and the Golden Age Justice Society of America, and launched decades of annual DC multiverse team-ups. Later on, Crisis on Infinite Earths' multiverse-resetting spectacle set the bar for continuity-wide crossovers, and other events like Infinite Crisis, Multiversity, and Dark Nights: Death Metal put their own unique spin on the concept.

When the conflict becomes especially difficult or its scale grows sufficiently large, the Justice League find themselves forced to join forces with their own enemies. In Forever Evil, Batman teams up with Lex Luthor, Captain Cold, Black Adam, Sinestro, and Black Manta to overthrow Earth-3’s tyrannical Crime Syndicate after the rest of the League is trapped inside Firestorm. Similarly, Dark Nights: Metal forces Batman into a desperate alliance with the Joker to defeat the Batman Who Laughs, while Justice League: No Justice had heroes like Martian Manhunter and Wonder Woman divided into teams alongside villains like Brainiac, Starro, and Lobo to stop the cosmic Omega Titans.

Currently in primary DC Comics continuity, Justice League Unlimited's villain amnesty program has made villains as famous as Lex Luthor, King Shark, and Giganta members of the Earth's top superhero team. Yet, not even this surprising change in DC's hero-villain dynamics surpasses the latest Justice League crossover.

In Skottie Young and Jorge Corona’s Lobo #9, "Burial for a Bastich", the DC Universe's biggest icons call a truce to mourn the Main Man. Following his heroic self-sacrifice to stop the lethal Blood Garnet Lantern in "The Last Death" arc, Lobo finds himself rejected by both Heaven and Hell and stuck haunting the cosmos as a ghost. Looking down on his own memorial service, Lobo watches bitter enemies put their differences aside to pay tribute. Villains as evil and apathetic as Darkseid, the Joker, Sinestro, Cheetah, and Captain Cold are seen weeping. Notably, Darkseid's tears are actually Omega Beams that fall in their traditionally angular and asymmetric shape.

Of course, seeing Darkseid and the Justice League openly weeping at Lobo’s funeral is likely an exaggerated Czarnian hallucination. Throughout Skottie Young and Jorge Corona’s run, every battle, ally, and foe warps into an over-the-top caricature whenever filtered through Lobo’s massive ego and twisted worldview. In reality, half of the people attending Lobo's funeral would barely shred a tear, and the other half would almost certainly shrug off the Main Man's demise or throw a celebration. Even heroes like Aquaman, Mera, Nightwing, and Plastic Man would hardly be moved by Lobo's death.

Lobo's funeral is a clear parody of one of DC Comics’ oldest and most reliable crossover traditions. Justice League's most famous members gathering around a fellow hero's casket is a recurring image in DC Comics history. Landmark examples include Superman’s emotional burial in Funeral for a Friend, Barry Allen’s memorial after Crisis on Infinite Earths, Oliver Queen’s Star City service, and Sue Dibny’s tragic gathering in Identity Crisis, where heroes pack pews and stand silently in the rain. Lobo’s self-aggrandizing fantasy pokes fun at the heroes' melodramatic grief, which is almost always negated by their fallen friend's inevitable resurrection.

The superhero funeral trope is part of a long list of classic visual tropes that writers and artists love to recreate and subvert. Perhaps even more famous is the image of the Justice League lying defeated at the feet of their newest enemy, a staple cover used countless times to establish a villain or rebellious hero’s power. Similar recurring rituals include the solemn tribunal where Justice League heroes pose for a team photo, as well as the latest squad's first race towards the battlefield, with every member running or flying towards the reader. While repetitive, these tropes are so pervasive precisely because of how satisfying to see they usually are.
