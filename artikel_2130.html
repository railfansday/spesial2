Middlesbrough should not be 'concerned' with Spurs' Will Lankshear clauses

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Middlesbrough have made a strong start to the new Championship season after a summer of heavy spending.

Boro missed out on promotion last year, losing in the play-off final to Hull City, but responded with big money moves for the likes of Radek Vitek, Ashley Phillips, Max Arfsten and, most notably, Will Lankshear.

The forward arrived in a deal worth a total £20 million, and he has duly delivered with a standout first portion of the campaign.

The former Tottenham Hotspur player has already scored 13 goals from 13 appearances in the Championship this season.

He is the early leader in the golden boot race, and his performances have earned him a lot of attention after just a few months at the Riverside.

However, there are other clauses involved in the £20 million deal, including matching rights for Spurs, as well as a sell-on clause for any future sale.

When asked if these clauses involved in the Lankshear deal are any cause for concern for the club, FLW’s Middlesbrough fan pundit, Liam Day, claimed that they are a non-issue.

He believes that the matching rights won’t have any impact on the forward’s possible sale in the future, and that a sell-on clause has become common practice within the game, and that Boro are likely to utilise one of their own whenever he is sold.

“I’m not really concerned about any of the clauses in all honesty, because sell-on clauses are quite a common thing in football,” Day told Football League World.

“And when we inevitably do sell him for however much we do, we’re probably going to insert quite a nice sell-on clause in there, it’s just how football works.

“I think we’ll make up the transfer fee with that, so that’s not too much of a concern.

“Obviously the matching rights is something that’s been talked about quite a lot as well, and I don’t really get that.

“That means Boro have to accept a transfer offer from another team, and Spurs have the opportunity to match that.

“It doesn’t really mean anything, and if that one does come into play, it means we’ve got an offer that we’ve deemed acceptable.

“So, I’m not really concerned about that.

“Then, obviously, the transfer fee is what it is.

“It’s £20 million, and it seems like a bargain to me, to be honest.”

Lankshear has scored three goals more than the next best player in the Championship so far this season, with Jack Moylan and Andri Gudjohnsen both tied for second with a tally of 10.

The 21-year-old signed a five-year deal when he agreed to make the switch from Spurs last summer, keeping him at the Riverside until June 2031.

Middlesbrough missed out on promotion last season after spending much of the campaign inside the top two.

Kim Hellberg’s side dropped to fifth in the final months of the term, and eventually lost to Southampton in the play-off final, but were later given their place in the final anyway, where they lost 1-0 to Hull.

But the Teesside outfit have started the new season well, and are second in the table, level on points with Birmingham City in top spot.

Next up for Boro is a home clash against Bristol City on 21 November in a 3pm kick-off.

A sell-on clause will mean that Boro only see the majority of any future transfer fee for Lankshear, but they can always insert performance-related clauses in their own deal whenever he is sold.

The forward is sure to attract attention with his standard of play so far, and it could be as soon as January that a Premier League club comes knocking.

The key for Boro will be to hold onto him until at least the summer because someone capable of scoring this consistently has the potential to power a team to promotion, which is worth more than any fee they could reasonably receive in the winter.

Spurs will be more frustrated with the clauses, as he is playing like someone that perhaps Roberto de Zerbi could be using now, instead of potentially in the future through matching rights.
