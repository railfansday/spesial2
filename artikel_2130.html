Stoke City can find Sorba Thomas 2.0 with incredible Huddersfield Town swoop

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Stoke City will be hoping to continue their excellent form into the next block of fixtures post the international break, with the Potters emerging as serious promotion contenders in the early stages of this campaign.

Stoke occupy 3rd place in the Championship as things stand, behind only Birmingham City and Middlesbrough on goal difference, with no side in the division winning more than their nine games at this point.

It's been a remarkable turnaround for the Potters, who lost all three of their opening league games to Swansea City, Southampton, and Wolverhampton Wanderers, as head coach Mark Robins will be hoping his side don't repeat their exploits from last year.

At this stage last campaign, Stoke found themselves chasing Coventry City and Middlesbrough at the top of the second tier table before suffering a calamitous drop-off around this time last year, though this time, they won't be relying on one player to carry them forward.

In Robins' first summer at the helm of Stoke, the former Coventry City boss sought to stamp his authority on the Potters squad, and brought in the likes of Robert Bozenik, Lamine Cisse, Steven Nzonzi, Maksym Talovierov, and Sorba Thomas.

Thomas, 27, made the move to the bet365 Stadium in the summer of 2025 from Huddersfield Town for a mere £1.5 million, having made his intentions of leaving the Terriers permanently clear after loan spells with Blackburn Rovers and Nantes.

Upon his arrival, a great deal wasn't exactly expected of the Welsh international after a quiet year on loan in France, but it very quickly became apparent that the Potters had found a bargain.

Thomas, by all accounts, was Stoke's star player last season, with ten goals and 15 assists in 52 appearances across all competitions in North Staffordshire, directly contributing to over 40 per cent of the club's league goals across the Championship campaign.

Played predominantly off the left flank where he could cut in on his preferred right foot, Thomas' whipped delivery proved to be a nightmare for opposition defenders and goalkeepers to deal with, as crosses against Charlton Athletic, Portsmouth, and Wrexham all found their way through an array of bodies and into the back of the net via his delivery.

Unsurprisingly, his form didn't go unnoticed, and as it appeared as though the Potters would win the battle to keep him until at least the January window, Hull City upped their offer to £10.5 million for the Welshman on deadline day, which Stoke reluctantly accepted.

Since then, incredibly, Stoke have lost just one of their 12 league games since his departure, winning nine, whilst his new club, Hull, are yet to taste victory since his arrival; with the Potters in a serious promotion battle, they should look to replicate this deal in January.

Since Thomas' departure, Stoke have switched from a 4-2-3-1 shape to a 3-4-2-1 instead, as this change has not only likely saved Robins' job, but turned the club from relegation contenders into promotion chasers.

Jack McGlynn's introduction into one of the two attacking midfield roles has proven to be a masterstroke, with the USMNT international nailing a place down in the starting XI, though alongside him, each of Justin Devenny, Million Manhoef, and Bae Jun-ho have been used to varying degrees of success.

Jun-ho is out of contract in the summer, and is unlikely to sign fresh terms, whilst Manhoef could well leave in January, as Stoke would need to find a replacement.

The perfect partner for McGlynn could be right in front of their noses, as Huddersfield's Derensili Sanches Fernandes ought to be on their radar.

The Dutchman has been playing as one of the two attacking midfielders in the Terriers' 3-4-2-1 shape in League One this season to great effect following his summer move from Excelsior, with seven goals and six assists in 14 league games thus far.

Under contract until the summer of 2030, the 25-year-old won't come cheap, but if he can help to guide the Potters back to the Premier League, he will be worth every penny.

Stoke certainly have the money to tempt Huddersfield into a deal, though whether terms could possibly be agreed this winter is a matter of debate; what isn't a matter of debate is that Sanches Fernandes would certainly boost the Potters' prospects of promotion.

A couple of key cogs are missing from Robins' side right now to make them definite shoe-ins for a top eight spot in the Championship, with attacking midfield being one of them, and in Sanches Fernandes, that could be fixed and then some.
