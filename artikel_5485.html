Krach Institute for Tech Diplomacy at Purdue Launches the World's First Global Trusted Tech Standard (xGTT) with the Backing of Dozens of Visionary Leaders

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

A free, open standard gives governments, businesses, universities and other decision-makers a common framework to assess whether companies operate in alignment with democratic values

WASHINGTON, Nov. 13, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The Krach Institute for Tech Diplomacy at Purdue today announced the launch of the Global Trusted Tech Standard (xGTT), the world's first open standard for trusted technology. The principle behind xGTT is straightforward: trust must be earned through conduct. In democratic markets, companies earn trust by respecting the laws, rights, and principles that underpin free societies. The xGTT Standard provides governments, businesses, universities, and other institutions with a common, evidence-based framework for assessing whether a company has earned that trust.

Unlike standards that narrowly focus on technical performance, xGTT goes one step further, covering companies' compatibility with democratic values, such as respect for the rule of law, human rights, intellectual property, transparency, accountability, and others. xGTT applies to 15 critical and emerging technology (CET) sectors that are central to economic competitiveness, national security, and democratic resilience. Its criteria draw on the laws, regulations, standards, and established practices of 18 technologically advanced democracies, providing a shared reference point grounded in multiple jurisdictions rather than the position of any single government.

The Global Trusted Tech Standard (xGTT) was announced at the Krach Institute for Tech Diplomacy at Purdue's 2026 Trusted Tech Summit, the premier forum for Tech Diplomancy. The annual Summit brings together U.S. and allied government officials, technology executives, investors, and strategic partners to address the alliances, standards, and strategies shaping trusted technology. The launch of xGTT advances that mission by providing a shared, evidence-based framework for assessing corporate trustworthiness across critical and emerging technology sectors.

By grounding economic collaboration in democratic principles, the Global Trusted Tech Standard makes it easier to operate efficiently in the free world, giving companies that earn trust a real competitive advantage," said Keith Krach, Chairman of the Krach Institute for Tech Diplomacy at Purdue. "Together, we can ensure tomorrow's technology advances freedom for generations to come."

"Trust is the free world's superpower, and it is how we set the pace," said Michelle Giuda, CEO of the Krach Institute. "The Global Trusted Tech Standard delivers a real-world definition of trusted technology. It's simple, open, and free. The xGTT Standard is guided by a Board of Governors, comprising 20 distinguished international experts spanning government, industry, academia, and civil society across 9 countries:

The Board reviews the criteria on a standing basis, so the xGTT Standard adapts as the underlying technologies, and the risks they carry, continue to evolve. xGTT is guided by people who understand how real-world technology decisions are made. Their experience helps keep it relevant to the people and institutions that will use it.

The xGTT Standard contains 31 criteria for assessing a company's trustworthiness. These include 10 Red-Line Disqualifiers and 21 additional criteria covering Four Dimensions of Trust:

A confirmed Red-Line failure disqualifies a company and ends the evaluation. For all other criteria, each applicable criterion receives a verdict of PASS, FAIL, UNCLEAR, or N/A, depending on the available evidence and whether the criterion applies. xGTT does not produce a single overall score or rank companies against one another.

xGTT helps a broad range of leaders make faster, evidence-based decisions about the trustworthiness of companies they may buy from, invest in, or partner with. Its criteria cover both technological excellence and democratic values, and each user applies them under its own authority and for its own purposes:

The xGTT Standard is for informational use only. It is not investment, legal, or financial advice, and xGTT evaluations do not constitute recommendations for or against any company, are not a regulatory finding or legal determination, and are not a substitute for independent due diligence. All decisions remain the responsibility of the organizations that use xGTT.
