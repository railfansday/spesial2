IBM Stock Is Down About 23% in 2026. Here's Where I Think It Will Be in 2030.

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Can a software comeback and an ordinary valuation bring the stock back near its old peak?

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 19, 2026 at 9:04PM EST

10 stocks we like better than International Business Machines ›

My best estimate for International Business Machines (IBM -2.04%) is a share price of about $300 when 2030 ends. From roughly $227 as I write, that comes to a rise of about 32%, or about 7% a year before dividends.

I get there by multiplying the non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings per share I think IBM can earn in 2030, about $16.75, by 18 -- a price-to-earnings ratio in line with the stock's own history.

Even $300 is below the June 2 record close of $329.23. Shares today trade around 31% under that high, and they're down about 23% in 2026.

Most of this estimate depends on IBM's software business. For the third quarter, the segment made up around 46% of IBM's $17.1 billion in revenue but about 65% of its segment profit (the profit IBM reports for each business line).

Showing how much the business has bounced back, software revenue grew 9% year over year in constant currency (a measure that removes exchange-rate moves) in the third quarter. This was a big acceleration from 5% in the second quarter. And the second quarter had itself marked a slowdown from 8% in the first quarter and 11% in the fourth quarter of 2025. Better yet, software's segment profit margin improved to 35% from 33% a year before, so the segment is keeping more profit per dollar of sales.

In January, management expected software to grow around 10% this year. On IBM's Oct. 21 earnings call, CFO Jim Kavanaugh said the company now sees around 7% to 8%, with 7% as the base case.

But IBM's annual recurring revenue, a yearly run rate for its recurring software business, rose 8% to $24.9 billion in the third quarter. And Kavanaugh said he expects the growth rate to approach double digits in the fourth quarter.

IBM's adjusted earnings per share reached $11.59 in 2025, up 12% from 2024. So far in 2026, through September, they've risen around 10% to $7.79. Quarterly growth moved from 19% to 5% to 11% -- the same drop and rebound software went through.

If fourth-quarter earnings per share just equal last year's $4.52, IBM would end 2026 at around $12.31.

From there, I assume earnings per share climb around 8% a year, hitting about $16.75 in 2030. That's slower than the last two years, mostly because the rest of IBM grows more slowly than software. Consulting, its second-biggest segment, grew just 2% at constant currency in the third quarter. Also, IBM's diluted share count ticked up in the last year, to around 954 million, so buybacks aren't doing much for earnings per share.

When 2022 ended, IBM stock traded at around 15 times earnings, using that year's adjusted earnings per share. The ratio climbed to about 17 times earnings by the close of 2023, then to 21 times earnings a year later and 26 times earnings by the end of 2025.

At the June record, shares cost around 28 times adjusted earnings for the previous four quarters.

Now IBM stock goes for about 18 times the adjusted earnings it posted in the last year, and about 17 times analysts' average earnings forecast for 2027. So shares arguably sit toward the low end of their recent range already.

I pick 18 times earnings for 2030 -- close to today's price-to-earnings ratio and under the average of the four year-ends, about 20 times earnings. Applied to about $16.75 in earnings per share, this gives a stock price near $300.

Of course, software might move the result either way. If it gets back to the 10% growth IBM expected in January and earnings per share gain about 10% a year, the stock could hit about $325 at the same valuation.

But if software settles near the second quarter's 5% rate and investors pay 15 times earnings, like they did at the end of 2022, shares might finish 2030 about where they are now.

IBM, which has paid dividends every year since 1916, also pays $1.69 a share each quarter, a yield of around 3% at today's price. Add it to about 7% a year from price gains, and my estimate suggests a total return of about 10% a year through 2030.

That's not a stellar return, but I think it's a good one for a company whose most profitable segment is growing faster again. I'd be comfortable buying IBM shares near today's price.
