1 Growth ETF I'm Buying Even When the Market Is Expensive

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

There'll be short-term volatility, but the long-term appeal is still strong.

By Stefon Walters – Nov 21, 2026 at 11:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Invesco NASDAQ 100 ETF ›

Many tech or tech-adjacent companies have seen their valuations surge during the current AI boom. Of course, this has been good for investors who were along for the ride, but one "downside" is how expensive the market has become.

Depending on the metric you're using, the last time the market was this expensive was during the dot-com bubble, which didn't necessarily end with investors throwing confetti. That said, even with the market this expensive, it's not a bad time to pick up a quality growth ETF, especially with notable ones falling over the past month.

One worth considering is the Invesco Nasdaq 100 ETF (QQQM -5.42%), which has thrived over the years but is down over 8% in the past month.

QQQM mirrors the Nasdaq-100, an index that tracks the 100 largest non-financial stocks trading on the Nasdaq stock exchange. Although it doesn't have "growth" in its name like other popular growth-focused ETFs, it's just as qualified to be considered one.

This ETF has only been trading since October 2020, but the Nasdaq-100 has been around for 41 years and has routinely outperformed the S&P 500.

Source: YCharts. Averages based on closing levels on Nov. 17.

Over the past 20 years, the Nasdaq-100 has outperformed the S&P 500 15 times. Its bad slumps have been worse than the S&P 500's, but that's what you expect from a growth stock or ETF. Past results don't guarantee future performance, but I see this trend continuing, as the tech sector has a bit more runway ahead.

Right now, QQQM has a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio -- which tells you how much you're paying per $1 of a company's earnings -- of 27.02, which is historically expensive. That could make the fund more volatile in the short term or limit immediate upside, but it doesn't take away from its long-term appeal.

The current AI arms race will strongly influence QQQM's performance, but even beyond that, it holds companies across other sectors that act as a crutch and present different non-tech growth opportunities.

Growth ETFs, whether QQQM or another, can be great portfolio pieces, but they can be redundant if you're investing in the same companies across multiple funds. Over 80% of QQQM's holdings are also in the S&P 500. That doesn't mean you can't invest in both; just be intentional about how much exposure you want to a handful of stocks.

I wouldn't make QQQM the bulk of my portfolio (despite its historical outperformance) because of its heavy tech concentration, but it could be a great supplemental piece.
