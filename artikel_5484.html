Porsche SE swung to loss on Volkswagen write-down, urged faster cuts

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Adjusted group result after tax fell to €0.9 billion from €1.6 billion in the first nine months of 2026

Porsche SE reported a group loss after tax of €2.8 billion ($3.1 billion) for the first nine months of 2026, a result that stemmed from a non-cash writedown of the book value of its stake in Volkswagen. The company posted a profit of €1.2 billion in the same period a year earlier.

The holding company, which owns a majority of Volkswagen's ordinary shares, said its adjusted group result after tax fell to €0.9 billion from €1.6 billion. Income from equity accounting of the Volkswagen investment was €0.8 billion, down from €1.7 billion. The equivalent result from Porsche AG was €0.2 billion, up from €0.1 billion.

“The Volkswagen Group’s quarterly results clearly show that the agreed measures to strengthen competitiveness, reduce costs and sustainably improve profitability must be implemented swiftly,” Hans Dieter Pötsch, chairman of the board of management of Porsche SE, said in a statement, according to The Wall Street Journal. “It is now the management’s task and responsibility to deliver.”

Pötsch also chairs Volkswagen’s supervisory board, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Group net debt stood at €5.1 billion as of Sept. 30, the same as at the end of 2025. For fiscal 2026, Porsche SE expects an adjusted group result after tax of between minus €0.5 billion and plus €1.5 billion. It expects net debt of between €4.7 billion and €5.2 billion.

The result after tax in the portfolio investments segment rose to €135 million from €13 million a year earlier, the company said. It attributed the gain to fair value measurements, particularly the remeasurement of its Quantum Systems stake. Porsche SE also acquired an interest in Stoke Space Technologies, a U.S. space firm, during the past quarter, paying a sum in the low tens of millions of dollars.

Porsche SE also reported a ruling in its favor in a legal case tied to its increase of the Volkswagen stake. Germany's Federal Court of Justice dismissed the plaintiffs' appeals against an earlier ruling by the Higher Regional Court of Celle. The case stemmed from lawsuits by 40 plaintiffs, mostly U.S. hedge funds, seeking €5.4 billion in damages plus interest. The company said the ruling is final and binding on the courts handling the six suspended initial proceedings.

Volkswagen reported an after-tax loss of €5.8 billion for the July-September quarter on Oct. 29. Revenue fell 6.3% from a year earlier, and the results included a €6.4 billion charge on its Porsche holding.

The automaker cut its full-year profit outlook in September to an operating return on sales of up to 1%, from a prior range of 4% to 5.5%. Its supervisory board approved a restructuring plan that month that doubles planned job cuts to 100,000 and halves the model lineup.
