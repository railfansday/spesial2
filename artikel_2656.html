Remembering Mike Doyle on his 80th birthday

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Today we remember Mike Doyle on what would have been his 80th birthday.

Doyle played 570 times for City – putting him third on the all-time appearances list behind only Joe Corrigan with 603 and Alan Oakes with 680.

He also scored 41 goals during 14 trophy-laden seasons with the Blues.

In total he won eight major honours at Maine Road – the Division Two title in 1965/66, the First Division title in 1967/68, the 1968 Charity Shield, the FA Cup in 1968/69, the European Cup Winners Cup and League Cup in 1969/70, the Charity Shield in 1972 and the League Cup in 1975/76.

His City story started when joined the Blues in 1962 as an apprentice when chief scout Harry Godwin visited his home in Reddish after hearing several clubs were on his trail. The story goes that Godwin said he knew he had a good chance of signing the Stockport Boys half-back when he saw his father's police helmet on the dining room table with a City programme next to it!

Godwin didn't have to convince the teenager to join the Blues – he jumped at the chance and soon made an impression at Maine Road, once asking Bert Trautmann if his back was OK after conceding eight goals.

Trautmann wasn't impressed and told the cheeky youngster what he'd do if he showed a lack of respect again and Doyle ended up washing Trautmann's Volkswagen Beetle every week for the next few months.

An important lesson learned, Doyle buckled down and made his first-team debut at Cardiff City in March 1964 coming up against Welsh legend John Charles in a 2-2 draw.

Joe Mercer was quick to spot his potential when he took over as manager the following year and Doyle played 19 League games, scoring seven goals after playing several matches as an emergency striker and bagging an impressive six goals in four games.

It wasn't until the 1967/68 campaign that Doyle was finally handed the No.4 jersey for keeps and he formed part of the backbone of the side that won the title that season, playing 40 of the 42 League games.

As the years went on, he liked nothing more than to wind Manchester United fans up, once claiming he loved playing the Reds because it was "four easy points" – during a time when it was just two points for a win.

Doyle seemed to dig even deeper when City played United and was on the losing side just once in the 16 derbies he played in between March 1968 and September 1975 – quite a feat.

He added winner's medals for the 1968 Charity Shield after a comprehensive 6-1 win over West Brom, the 1969 FA Cup after a 1-0 success over Leicester, the 1970 European Cup Winners' Cup by seeing off Gornik Zabrze 2–1 and the 1970 League Cup when Doyle bagged a goal in a 2-1 win over West Brom.

His form at club level was such that he was included in the provisional squad for the 1970 World Cup in Mexico, but when his wife Cheryl fell ill, he stayed by her bedside and informed England he wasn't available for selection.

He finally won the first of his five England caps in 1975 and the arrival of Dave Watson saw Doyle forge a superb central defensive partnership that became the scourge of strikers throughout the land.

Doyle was also handed the captain's armband in 1975 for the first time and he celebrated his first year as skipper by lifting the League Cup in February 1976 with a 2-1 win over Newcastle at Wembley.

Doyle almost captained City to the title the following year, too, missing out by a single point to Liverpool.

He managed just 13 starts during the 1977/78 season before calling time on his 16-year association with the Blues by joining Stoke in June 1978 for a fee of £50,000.

He later had spells with Stoke, Bolton and Rochdale before retiring in 1984.

Sadly, Mike died in June 2011 at the age of 64, leaving four children and six grandchildren, including Tommy who spent three seasons with the Blues under Pep Guardiola.

Today, on his birthday, we remember the late, great Mike Doyle - never forgotten!
