Golf commentator suggests one golf rule which came into play in 2019 is actually ‘slowing down play’

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

It first became legal to tap down spike marks back in January 2019.

Before then, players were only allowed to repair blemishes in their line to the hole if they could prove it was a pitch mark.

However, since the rule changed seven years ago, players have obviously taken full advantage of the fact that they are allowed to perfect their line.

Most players were in full agreement when the spike mark rule changed.

It seemed completely unfair that a spike mark could be in a player's line to the hole and there was nothing they could do about it.

However, the rule change in 2019 has brought about another issue, according to Sky Sports commentator David Howell.

During the DP World Tour Championship on Friday, Sky Sports commentator David Howell was asked whether he likes the rule that allows players to tap down spike marks on the greens.

"It has been discussed behind the scenes about how often some people seem to be taping down spike marks. We call it trenching, which is one of the reasons why it was stopped in the first place.

"I don't actually think it speeds the play up. I think it slows it down because people are finding, somehow, many more spike marks on the greens."

That is a great point from Howell. The more spike marks that are tapped down, the longer rounds are taking.

And that is the last thing the professional game needs right now, with rounds at major championships taking longer than six hours at times.

Trenching has become a real issue in the professional game over the past few years, especially when tournaments are played at courses with soft and spongey greens, like Pebble Beach for example.

Now that it is perfectly legal to tap down spike marks, players seem to be finding more spike marks than they ever did before.

That's because some players tap their putter head down all the way from their ball to the hole.

This has become known as trenching because they are essentially creating a trench between their ball and the hole.

There are certain players who do this far more than others, and it is definitely something that needs to be eradicated from the game.

However, that may well mean going back to not allowing spike marks to be tapped down, and that in itself is unfair. It's a really tricky one that probably doesn't have a satisfactory solution either way.
