Crypto host who once promoted a $50 billion memecoin vision now tells traders to get out

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Ansem’s wallet hack exposed security risks at Bullpen and prompted renewed debate over crypto's reputation.

Jonah, co-host of the 1000x podcast, wrote on Nov. 24 to “get out while you still can” after crypto trader Ansem said his wallet had been hacked.

Jonah predicted that staying in crypto would carry a stigma. At 03:07 UTC on Nov. 25, trading app Bullpen said the known impact of its breach was limited to 19 early beta accounts.

At 03:53 UTC, Ansem advised users to withdraw their funds until he could confidently say they were safe.

Ansem’s initial post said he suspected that someone had accessed the Bullpen profile where he had imported the private key for a Solana wallet he used on Pump.fun, and Jonah’s quote-post came in response to that claim.

Bullpen’s first public notice came later Tuesday and identified Ansem as one of the users affected.

In its initial notice, Bullpen gave a preliminary range of 18 to 19 affected accounts, all opened between April and June 2024. The company said it took the affected test service offline.

Its Wednesday update gave a figure of 19 accounts and said it had seen no further movement of the funds at that point.

Bullpen said the attacker took over the older accounts, exposing wallets users had imported into them. For users who had imported a wallet, the access Bullpen described extended beyond the trading app to funds in that wallet.

The private key Ansem said he imported could authorize transfers from the wallet he used on Pump.fun. The company said 99.8% of the funds taken came from one account, which it did not name.

Bullpen added that it was working with the FBI and other authorities, along with the services involved, to freeze and recover the funds. Ansem said he tried to freeze the money while it was still in USDC, but the attempt required a court order.

The updates describe efforts to recover the money and leave the recovered amount undisclosed.

In his follow-up, Ansem said two of his Solana wallets were compromised, identifying them as the wallet he imported from Pump.fun and a wallet already on Bullpen.

He attributed the breach to a weakness in older accounts that he said let someone bypass Bullpen’s two-factor login. Ansem also said a separate BitGo wallet requiring multiple signatures, which he said held 49% of the ANSEM token supply, was safe and unmoved.

An ANSEM sell-off unfolded on Nov. 24, driven by a large ANSEM sell-off from a publicly labeled wallet. Bitquery reported about $2.57 million in USDC at one Arbitrum address at 17:00 UTC that day.

Jonah’s comments drew a quote-post from user soby and a “This you?” reply from @CupOJoseph. In another reply, Jonah said he liked Ansem and had helped launch a token a couple of years earlier.

Jonah had published a BODEN roadmap in April 2024. The post imagined the memecoin listing on the New York Stock Exchange at a $50 billion valuation and becoming a reserve currency.

In a separate post early Wednesday, he distinguished between launching a token he considered worthless and selling it, which he said made someone a scammer.

In his own post Tuesday, 1000x co-host Avi Felman wrote that “crypto is fine,” arguing the industry grew stronger through previous downturns.
