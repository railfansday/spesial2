Tottenham Hotspur were ultimately comfortable victors on Saturday as they beat Ipswich Town 2-0.

Luca Williams-Barnett and Dominic Solanke got the goals in North London, with the latter's performance stealing the show.

The 18-year-old was making his full Premier League debut, and marked it with a superb goal and a dazzling assist.

Tottenham's defeat at Leeds United before the international break was a blip, but Roberto De Zerbi's men have now won four of their last five Premier League games.

Sunderland will be up next for the Lilywhites, with clashes against Arsenal and Liverpool also appearing on the horizon.

Here, Football Insider has outlined five moments you may have missed from Saturday's game.

There has been plenty of clamour for Williams-Barnett this season, and he was clearly worth the wait.

The winger certainly enjoyed netting his first goal after getting on the end of Mohammed Kudus' cross, and took his time to celebrate with the help of Solanke.

The striker noticed Williams-Barnett celebrating towards the camera and instead pushed him over to where he would be able to soak up the home fans' adoration.

Julio Enciso is undoubtedly Ipswich's most dangerous player, and Tottenham rightly treated him as such.

Conor Gallagher, in particular, stuck to him like a rash, constantly breathing down his neck and putting in tackles.

One sliding lunge just before the break got the better of Enciso and left him furious, throwing his arms up in the air in frustration.

It is not often that a player can get away with putting their hands on a referee without picking up, at least, a yellow card.

But James Maddison did exactly that without punishment on Saturday, reacting after referee Rob Jones had awarded a goal kick instead of the corner Tottenham wanted.

The midfielder shared a moment with the official, putting both his hands over Jones' shoulders as the pair laughed.

Williams-Barnett already enjoyed a dream full Premier League debut on Saturday, but it could have got even better.

Maddison broke through and drove towards the box in the second half, and could have fed the teenager, who was in acres of free space inside the box.

The midfielder opted to stay on the ball and eventually lost it, with Williams-Barnett's only response to bring his hands to his head in disbelief.

Despite his team's comfortable performance and key three points, De Zerbi seemingly failed to muster up a single smile.

The usually passionate Tottenham manager was pictured in the stands on several occasions throughout the game as he serves his touchline ban.

Late in the game, cameras pictured the Italian looking glum, presumably unhappy with something he had seen.
