Cleveland Guardians Should Target One Of These 5 Free Agent Pitchers

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Cleveland Guardians have a credible starting pitching staff.

But the Guardians would like to add a starting pitcher before teams are potentially "locked out" of the game by Major League Baseball team owners December 1st. That is the day the Collective Bargaining Agreement expires between players and owners.

Major issues remain before a new Basic Agreement will be signed.

Heading to 2027, the Guardians control a starting rotation of right-handers Gavin Williams, Tanner Bibbe, and Slade Cecconi. They round out the rotation with lefties Parker Messick, and Joey Cantillo.

Lefty Foster Griffin, 31, who was obtained from the Washington Nationals for prospects on August 3, 2026, is eligible for free agency.

Griffin helped lengthen the rotation, but his performance with Cleveland was less than anticipated.

espn.com projects Griffin will earn a two-year, $30 Million contract as a free agent.

He may be priced out of Cleveland's range.

With Griffin potentially gone, a hole will appear in the Guardians starting rotation.

Williams and Messick front the staff. Both are capable of throwing a no-hitter on any given day. Both are capable of being the Cy Young Award winner in the American League.

Bibee was inconsistent, and was the forgotten man in the MLB playoffs. However, the Cleveland front office still believes in Bibee's ability to offer quality in the rotation. Can Bibee bounce back and pitch to his five-year, $48 Million contract he signed in 2025?

While Bibee's contract may seem like a bargain, it depends upon what he returns in the way of quality on the mound.

Cantillo showed signs of brilliance, but inconsistent command and control were often the cause of short outings.

Cecconi may be considered a fifth starter, or as a reliever, depending upon how the rotation shakes out. He might take the place of Griffin if the Cleveland front office comes up empty in their search for an additional starter.

The length and financial terms of any free agent contract will be of critical importance to the fiscally prudent Cleveland Guardians front office.

I believe it would be difficult for Cleveland to offer more than a one-year, $13 Million "make good" contract to a free agent pitcher.

Mize was traded to the Padres from the Tigers last year. As espn.com noted, "Similar to Foster Griffin, Mize…was notably worse at his next stop."

Mize is projected to sign a one-year, $15 Million contract, which may be too steep for Cleveland's comfort level.

May would be leaving the World Champion Milwaukee Brewers if he signs elsewhere.

Often plagued by injuries with the Los Angeles Dodgers, May still has upside, if he can stay healthy.

May should command a one-year, $13 million contract.

Buehler is an interesting name, as he made 32 starts last year for the Padres.

Buehler is a perfect candidate to benefit from the stellar pitching coaches of the Guardians.

The Guardians might be able to sign Buehler in the $12 Million range.

Peterson would be leaving the Chicago Cubs, where he was a proven "ground ball" pitcher, but certainly not an "ace."

Durable enough to be counted upon, Peterson could command $11 Million a year in a new contract.

Boyd pitched for the Guardians in 2024. He would be leaving the Chicago Cubs.

Boyd could fetch a one-year, $10 Million contract, which could be attractive to Cleveland.
