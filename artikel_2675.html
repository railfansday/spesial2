Ducks bounce back with suffocating win over Flames

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

ANAHEIM, Calif. – With its winning form starting to slip from their grasp, the Anaheim Ducks responded with an all-out effort to stop the bleeding.

Anaheim's offensive fire lit back up with two goals from Beckett Sennecke and Cutter Gauthier's 16th goal in a nine-game goal streak, as the Ducks completely controlled the Calgary Flames from puck drop in an impressive 6-1 win on Sunday at Honda Center.

Anaheim (11-9-1, 23 points) broke out of back-to-back losses following a five-game winning streak with another standout offensive game. The Ducks have scored five or more goals in nine of their last 11 games with an appropriate 9-2-0 record in that stretch. Anaheim is 11-1-1 when scoring three or more goals and 0-8-0 when scoring two or fewer.

Following last night's 6-2 debacle against the New York Islanders, Anaheim bounced back to dominate the shot clock in the first (11-5) and second period (17-8).

"The better you defend, you get the puck back and you get to keep it," Joel Quenneville said. "We showed today that we're better managing the puck, whether it's cleaner exits, through the middle of the ice, some offensive zone plays. We had the puck on our stick, which was something we didn't have the last two games."

Jeff Malott screened a Sennecke shot and spun around to put home the rebound just 76 seconds into the game, and Gauthier sniped his 16th goal in a nine-game goal streak coming down the right wing later in the first period.

Gauthier is now the solo holder of the second-longest goal streak in Ducks history and trails only Teemu Selanne's 11-game streak from 1997-98. Gauthier is just the fifth player age 22 or younger with a nine-game goal streak joining Bob Dye (1920-21), Mike Bossy (1978-79), Wayne Gretzky (1981-82) and Selanne (1992-93).

"He's truly the best shooter I've ever been on the ice with," said Ian Moore, who made his season debut off of injured reserve tonight. "Feels like he could score from anywhere. Obviously, he's had a good run here. I think he'll keep it going, the way he shoots the puck."

Into the third period, Tim Washe dug away for Anaheim's third goal of the game, and just 25 seconds after Mikael Backlund broke the shutout on a Calgary breakaway, Moore fed Sennecke for the one-timer, 4-1. Moore played the third-most minutes of any Ducks defenseman on Sunday (16:59) on a top pairing with Jackson LaCombe.

Sennecke and Mikael Granlund hit the empty net for the soaring 6-1 scoreline.

"Communication was a big focal point for us tonight. Playing fast and connected," Malott said. "Guys were coming back (defensively). We were keeping it tight from the center out, and I think we're just challenging with good sticks."

Ville Husso stopped 20 of 21 shots in his first start in 17 days and first appearance since a comeback win over the Flames in two periods of relief on Nov. 6. While not an overwhelming workload, Husso shined in the second period with a handful of post-to-post saves.

"He had some huge saves," Moore said, "and we're trying to help him out as best we can.  And overall, as a group of five, I think we played, whoever the 5 are out there, I think we played together defensively. We're all kind of blocking shots, trying to have good sticks, communicating. So we're kind of focusing on those details and I think we did well tonight."

Devin Cooley made 28 saves for the Flames

Anaheim next heads out for a four-game trip, which begins in St. Louis on Tuesday. Ducks return to Orange County for its first game of the season against Macklin Celebrini and the San Jose Sharks on Nov. 25.
