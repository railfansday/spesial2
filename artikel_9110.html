Wyoming Art Dealer Sentenced After Falsely Claiming Native American Heritage

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Executive Editor, ARTnews & Art in America

Jose Martinez, who adopted the name Joseph Skywolf while he ran Skywolf’s Gallery in Jackson, Wyoming, has been sentenced after pleading guilty to a felony charge of falsely representing himself as Native American and claiming certain offerings were created by Native American artists or tribes.

According to the US Department of the Interior, Martinez violated the Indian Arts and Crafts Act, PL 101-644, while working at the namesake of Skywolf’s in 2023 and 2024. “Martinez offered or displayed for sale various kinds of artwork, such as headdresses, jewelry, paintings, and beaded tanned hides, that he falsely represented as made by an Indian or by a specific Indian Tribe,” reads a statement from the Department of the Interior. He has been sentenced to 24 months of supervised probation and ordered to pay $14,100 in restitution. “Authentic Indian art and craftwork is an important tool for passing down cultural traditions and artistic skills from one generation to the next,” said Meridith Stanton, director of the Department of the Interior’s Indian Arts and Crafts Board (IACB). “While falsely claiming to be Chiricahua Apache, Apache, Navajo, and Native American when selling his counterfeit Indian art, Martinez undercut authentic Indian artists and Indian economies, culture, and traditions.”

The director added a warning to others who might follow Martinez’s path: “For those selling counterfeit Indian art and craftwork it is important to know that wherever you are we will diligently work to find you and prosecute you under the Indian Arts and Crafts Act.”
