Southwest Airlines Lied To Major News Publication About Compensating Man’s Broken Stroller

The saga continues between the world's largest operator of the Boeing 737, Southwest Airlines, and the Piquette Family. Earlier this month, Jason Piquette and his family, including twins, had traveled with Southwest to Portland (PDX) in Oregon, and when they arrived, they noticed that their double stroller was badly damaged.

Southwest initially denied that the airline had damaged the stroller, which turned a routine baggage policy complaint into an accusationwhere the carrier told USA Today that they had offered the Piquette family compensation, a claim that has been disputed by the family on TikTok.

On November 7, the Piquette family had flown to Portland to visit family, and on arrival from their Southwest flight, identified that the stroller they had checked into the hold had been delivered with damage sustained, including bent and wobbly wheels, and a damage to the stroller frame.

Piquette challenged the airline, but the airport personnel for Southwest initially denied liability and claimed the airline's limited release of liability policy, which suggests that strollers and car seats travel at the passenger's own risk. This left the Piquette family without a stroller for their visit to Oregon, leading Piquette to take to social media and repeatedly ask the airline for answers.

Simple Flying reached out to Southwest for comment on the matter; however, an airline spokesperson could not immediately be reached.

After Piquette's initial videos on social media were seen by millions, a Southwest executive contacted Piquette, who, according to the traveler, noted that the airline refused to reconsider the limited liability policy. The executive is claimed to have said, 'I'm the highest up person you're gonna get'. On the call, a $200 flight voucher was offered, with an expiry date as offered. Piquette outlined that this voucher would be 'useless', as he and his family had no intention of flying the airline again.

Despite being several weeks after the initial incident, a recent report by USA Today has surfaced, following the Dallas-based airline confirming to the major news outlet that a $500 reimbursement for the stroller, in addition to the $200 flight voucher, was offered. This upset Piquette, which resulted in him posting another video asking for Southwest to explain why they "lied".

Piquette has repeatedly called out to ask why Southwest misled USA Today, suggesting that the airline was trying to 'save face', after confirming that during his call with the airline executive, no $500 reimbursement was offered.

Parent shares shaky footage of damaged stroller and a cold customer service reply that left repairs unaffordable.

According to the US Department of Transportation (DOT) ruling, all major carriers are liable for up to $4,700 for lost, damaged, or delayed domestic checked baggage. However, airlines often require passengers to sign or accept limited release waivers, which apply automatically under the Contract of Carriage that is agreed to when purchasing a ticket. This move exempts the airline from damage claims unless the item is lost completely.

Piquette's use of social media has highlighted his ongoing frustration with Southwest, as the airline has continuously refused to replace or acknowledge the damage to the essential stroller. Through Piqeutte's videos, a number of people have offered donations, and even one viewer lent the family a stroller to borrow during their time in Oregon.

Simple Flying asked Southwest Airlines whether they had any immediate plans to review its limited release of liability policy following the event and following the widespread media attention this incident has created. Families traveling with young children on Southwest are entitled to an additional child item on top of the existing checked baggage allowance; this includes one stroller and one car seat/child restraint per child at no extra cost.
