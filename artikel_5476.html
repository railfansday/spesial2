Chicago Fed chief said inflation is still moving in the wrong direction

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Austan Goolsbee said central bank has work to do as markets fully price in a quarter-point rate hike in December

Chicago Federal Reserve President Austan Goolsbee told CNBC on Friday that inflation is moving in the wrong direction and the Fed has "work to do to continue to reestablish its credibility."

Goolsbee said on "Squawk Box" that inflation has been above the Fed's 2% target for almost six years. "Lately we're not making any progress, and if anything, it's going the wrong way," he said, according to CNBC.

He also pointed to two drivers of price pressure. "Inflation is rising. It's proved far more persistent than we predicted at the beginning from the supply shocks, and now we're adding this old-fashioned demand overheating coming from the AI data center buildout, that looks like it's starting to spread out of its lane," Goolsbee said.

Markets now treat a quarter-point hike as a certainty for the Federal Open Market Committee's Dec. 8-9 meeting, according to CME $CME Group's FedWatch gauge. The gauge also puts the odds of a half-point increase at more than 10%. Goolsbee does not vote on the FOMC this year but will in 2027.

Goolsbee stopped short of saying a more forceful response could be needed from the central bank. "It's all going to be about this question: What is happening to inflation, and are we on path back to 2%?" he said. "My feeling is, right now we're not."

His remarks followed a Bureau of Labor Statistics report on Friday showing the producer price index rose 0.3% in October and 6.5% from a year earlier. That was slightly below expectations for the monthly gain, according to CNBC. CNBC also said the annual rise was the largest since November 2022.

Goolsbee called the October consumer price report "borderline disturbing" in an interview on "PBS NewsHour" on Tuesday. The consumer price index rose 0.4% in October, and core CPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.3%. Asked about a bigger hike in December, Goolsbee said "everything is always on the table."

Fed Governor Christopher Waller said in a London speech on Tuesday that the Fed should consider a half-point increase in December if October core CPI came in around 0.3% or higher. The Fed held its benchmark rate at 3.75% to 4% at its October meeting.
