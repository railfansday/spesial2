United Airlines’ Most Frequent Boeing 777-200 Flights In 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Boeing 777-200 entered service in 1994 and was primarily aimed at US domestic airlines; the 777-200ER followed in 1997 with more of an international focus. The 777-200ER extends the range from around 5,240 nautical miles to 7,065 nautical miles (depending on mission profile). These are workhorses for United's transatlantic routes, as well as some inter-hub routes, routes to Hawaii, and select destinations in East Asia. The type is aging, with older examples struggling with fuel efficiency compared with modern aircraft, although as paid-off aircraft, they also offer much lower capital expenses for United.

Here is what to know about where United Airlines is flying its Boeing 777-200/ER fleet in 2026, according to data provided by Cirium, a data analytics company. The flight data below is for the full 2026 calendar year and includes both the 777-200 and the 777-200ER, as Cirium lacks distinct aircraft codes for the sub-variants. The data is also for the full year, even if the 777s were replaced with other aircraft partway through the year on the routes.

United Airlines operates a fleet of 74 Boeing 777-200/ER airliners, of which Planespotters.net lists 61 as active and 13 as parked. The overall age of the fleet is rapidly aging and is now 27.5 years old. These include 19 Boeing 777-200s, with the remainder being 777-200ERs. United is the launch customer and the largest operator of the 777-200, with the fleet being configured for domestic routes. The 777-200ER includes four examples with domestic configurations. The remainder of the 777-200ER is configured for international operations.

According to Planespotters.net, United has not divested any of its 777-200/ERs since 2005, after eight examples had been transferred to Indian and Brazilian operators. The oldest example in United's service is registered N777UA and was delivered in 1995, making it 31.5 years old. The youngest member of the family is registered as N27015 and was delivered in 2010. It is now around 26.7 years old.

Cirium's data shows United is operating the 777-200/ER from its six main hubs: San Francisco (12,591 route-pair flights), Newark (6,635), Houston (5,549), Washington Dulles (4,900), Denver (3,059), and Chicago O'Hare (1,165). Adding these directly would result in double counting of 1,759 flights due to flights between the hubs. For the full year, United is expected to operate a total of 32,140 flights with the aircraft carrying a total of 9.6 million seats.

Chicago O'Hare sees the fewest flights with United's Boeing 777-200/ERs. Its most frequent pairings (outbound and inbound) are to its other hubs, including 373 flights on the San Francisco pairing (1,604 nautical miles), 309 flights on the Los Angeles pairing (1,517 nautical miles), and 236 flights on the Denver pairing (772 nautical miles). The year is also seeing 174 flights from Chicago to Mexico's Cancun (1,255 nautical miles).

Denver is to see 3,059 United 777-200 family flights carrying 1.1 million seats. Its leading routes by distance and frequency are to Hawaii, including Honolulu (1,226 flights at 2,929 nautical miles), Maui (744 flights at 2,872 nautical miles), and Kona (238 flights at 2,894 nautical miles). That is a total of 2,248 flights on Denver's Hawaii pairings and 73% of the airports' total United 777-200/ER operations.

Other routes include Chicago O'Hare, already mentioned above (236 flights), Houston (225 flights), Cancun (174), as well as a limited number of flights to San Francisco, Orlando, and Washington Dulles. In other words, most of United's non-Hawaii paired 777-200/ER flights from Denver are to its other major hubs in the continental United States.

Examining the busy schedule of one of the most distinguished jets in the world's biggest fleet of airliners.

Washington Dulles has 4,900 United 777-200/ER operations, with the bulk of these being transatlantic flights to Western Europe. Dulles' most frequent pairing is with London Heathrow at 996 flights, followed by Germany's Frankfurt (744 flights). The next most common pairing is transpacific to Tokyo Haneda, with 728 flights. After that Japanese route, it's back to Europe with the pairings to Paris (656 flights), Lisbon (557 flights), and Rome Fiumicino (492 flights).

Other European pairings include Athens (168), Brussels (126), Barcelona (32), and Zurich (18). Domestic routes are to San Francisco (193 flights) and Los Angeles (136 flights). Most European routes from Dulles average between 3,000 and 4,000 nautical miles, although the route to Athens is considerably longer, reaching 4,492 nautical miles. United Airlines dominates Washington Dulles International Airport (IAD), accounting for roughly 66% to 70% of passenger traffic.

From Dulles, United connects the US capital with various intercontinental destinations in Europe, Asia, and even Africa. Somewhat surprisingly, given the US's strong demographic and cultural connections with South America, the only route to that continent operated by United is to São Paulo with Boeing 767-400ERs. Avianca also operates flights between Bogotá and Washington Dulles. The lack of flights is because airlines concentrate US–South America hubs in southern and coastal gateways (Miami, Houston) with stronger passenger demand.

In Houston, George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) is dominated by United Airlines, while William P. Hobby Airport (HOU) is dominated by Southwest Airlines. At IAH, United boasts around 74.1% of the passenger market share, perhaps more now that Spirit has collapsed. Spirit had around 5% of the market share. From Houston, the most frequent 777-200/ER route is to London Heathrow, whose pairing is seeing 1,397 flights in 2026.

Houston is also a significant gateway to South America, with the next busiest pairing being with Buenos Aires (942 flights). The only other South American destination is Chile's Santiago, with 209 flights. These are United's main routes to South America, meaning they aren't used very heavily in that direction. There are no frequent United 777-200/ER routes to Mexico, the Caribbean, or Central America. This is partly due to many of these destinations being within range of narrowbody aircraft. For example, United operates seven daily A320-family flights between Houston and Mexico City.

The third-busiest pairing is with United's West Coast hub of San Francisco (681 flights), with another 225 flights on the Houston-Denver route and 52 flights on the Houston-Newark route. The other regular routes are transatlantic routes to Germany, with the Frankfurt pairing getting 730 flights in 2026 and Munich getting 580. Munich is the longest route at an average distance of 4,708 nautical miles, followed by Frankfurt, Buenos Aires, London Heathrow, and Santiago — all of which are above 4,000 nautical miles.

Two major US airlines currently operate the Boeing 777.

The airport that hosts the second-most United 777-200/ER flights is Newark Liberty, with 6,635 operations. United Airlines holds roughly 60% to 68% of passenger market share at Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR), making it by far the airport's dominant carrier and largest hub. It is from Newark that United connects the New York region with much of the world, including many transatlantic destinations in Europe.

Even so, the single busiest city pairing is with San Francisco, with 1,314 flights, while the second-busiest transcontinental pairing is with Los Angeles, with 710 flights. Otherwise, transatlantic routes dominate the list and include Paris (863 flights), Dublin (728 flights), Milan (582 flights), London Heathrow (413 flights), Amsterdam (284 flights), Munich (205 flights), Barcelona (184 flights), and Rome (139 flights).

Other notable city pairings include Tokyo Haneda (593 flights) and São Paulo, Brazil (526 flights). The Tokyo route is notable for being one of the longest routes United is assigning its 777-200ERs, at an average distance of 5,877 nautical miles. Currently, United is operating a daily flight to Tokyo Haneda using its 787-9s, suggesting the 777-200ERs have been pulled off the route. United is also operating 787-9s on the twin Newark-Tokyo Narita route.

San Francisco is by far the busiest hub for United's fleet of 777-200/ERs in 2026, with a total of 12,591 operations. Being located on the West Coast, San Francisco also sees the largest range of destinations, with flights to Asia, Australia, New Zealand, Europe, and domestic destinations within the United States. The busiest pairing is the one mentioned above with Newark Liberty (1,314 flights).

European routes include Frankfurt (1,077 flights), London Heathrow (744 flights), Paris (656 flights), Munich (716 flights), Rome (410 flights), Barcelona (266 flights), Zurich (262 flights), and Amsterdam (238 flights). Routes to East Asia are Tokyo Haneda (730), Beijing (728), Osaka (714), Taipei (690), Seoul (273), and Tokyo Narita (239). Already mentioned above are other hub pairings with Houston, Chicago, and Washington.

San Francisco, along with Denver, is a major hub for flights to Hawaii's Honolulu (794). San Francisco is also the only hub where United's 777-200/ERs are flying to Australia (Brisbane with 524 flights and Sydney with 296 flights) and New Zealand (Auckland with 490 flights). The Sydney route is the longest one of all for United's 777-200ERs with an average distance of 6,447 nautical miles followed by Brisbane at 6,140 nautical miles. New Zealand is a bit closer at 5,665 nautical miles.
