Cowboys Week 12 opponent: Another look at the 2026 Eagles

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Dallas Cowboys are coming off one of their best offensive performances of the season, but they won't have much time to celebrate. Dallas returns to the field on Thanksgiving to face a familiar division rival, the Philadelphia Eagles, in a rematch that comes with a few new wrinkles.

The Eagles are coming off a 19-16 upset victory over the Pittsburgh Steelers, snapping a seven-game losing streak and improving to 3-7. More importantly, they have a new quarterback under center: former Cowboys quarterback Joe Milton III.

Philadelphia signed Milton off Dallas' practice squad in October, and he is now getting an opportunity to lead the Eagles' offense. It's an unusual twist in an already important NFC East matchup, especially considering the Cowboys handed Philadelphia a 38-21 loss the last time these teams met. The Eagles are still trying to find consistency, but Sunday's win showed that they shouldn't be taken lightly.

The biggest storyline for the Eagles heading into Thursday is undoubtedly Milton. The former Cowboys practice-squad quarterback is now preparing to make his third NFL start, and his first against his former team. Philadelphia turned to Milton after Jalen Hurts was shut down and they struggled to find consistency at quarterback. The move has given the Eagles a different look under center.

Milton's performance against Pittsburgh was far from perfect. He completed 10 of 20 passes before his final completion, and he missed several opportunities downfield, including an overthrown pass to DeVonta Smith that could have resulted in a 68-yard touchdown. He did, however, make the most important throw of the afternoon.

With just over a minute remaining and Philadelphia facing third-and-six, Milton connected with Smith for a seven-yard gain to move the chains and effectively seal the victory. It was a meaningful moment for a young quarterback who has struggled with consistency, and it showed that head coach Nick Sirianni is willing to put the game in his hands.

Milton has a powerful arm and the athleticism to make plays outside the pocket. Through his first two appearances this season, he has completed 29 of 55 passes for 310 yards, with no touchdowns or interceptions. He has also rushed 14 times for 71 yards.

The Cowboys should know better than anyone that Milton has the arm strength to push the ball downfield. The challenge will be making him prove he can consistently deliver accurate throws and make good decisions. Dallas needs to pressure him, keep him contained in the pocket and avoid giving him easy opportunities to extend plays.

Regardless of who's playing quarterback, Philadelphia's offense still revolves around Saquon Barkley. The running back has dealt with injuries and inconsistent production throughout the season, but he remains one of the most dangerous players on the Eagles' roster. Through 10 games, Barkley has rushed for 371 yards and four touchdowns on 86 carries, averaging 4.3 yards per attempt.

He showed signs of life against Pittsburgh, finishing with 75 yards on 17 carries and a 38-yard touchdown run. Unfortunately for Philadelphia, Barkley left the game after getting shaken up late, and his status heading into Thanksgiving will be worth monitoring.

The Eagles have other backfield options, including Dameon Pierce, Tank Bigsby and Will Shipley. However, none brings the same combination of explosiveness and big-play potential as Barkley. The first meeting between these teams reminded everyone what Philadelphia can do on the ground. A hobbling Barkley rushed for 62 yards on seven carries, averaging nearly nine yards per attempt.

Dallas ultimately controlled the game by establishing its own running attack and finished with 204 rushing yards. That will be an important matchup again, particularly with the Cowboys' defense still trying to find consistency against the run. If Barkley is healthy, Dallas cannot afford to let him get comfortable. The Eagles need their running game to take pressure off Milton, and the Cowboys need to make Philadelphia one-dimensional.

Milton's biggest challenge will be getting the ball to Philadelphia's playmakers consistently. DeVonta Smith is back in the lineup after missing significant time with a hamstring injury. He returned against Pittsburgh and finished with five receptions for 41 yards on 12 targets. The numbers weren't spectacular, but his presence gives Milton a reliable receiver who can create separation and make plays downfield. Smith has 31 receptions for 351 yards and two touchdowns this season.

Tight end Dallas Goedert has also been a productive target, recording 32 receptions for 346 yards and two touchdowns. He remains an important part of the passing game, particularly when Philadelphia needs a dependable option underneath.

Rookie Makai Lemon has emerged as another contributor, leading the team with 38 receptions for 315 yards. Dontayvion Wicks has added 284 receiving yards, while Darius Cooper has 259 yards and four touchdowns. Dallas' secondary has struggled this season, especially in preventing explosive plays. The Cowboys cannot afford to give Smith and the rest of Philadelphia's receivers free access downfield. Making Milton work through his progressions and forcing him to be accurate will be essential.

The Eagles' defense has had its share of problems this season, including a difficult performance against Dallas in October. That performance should give Dallas confidence heading into the rematch, but the Eagles looked much better against Pittsburgh. Philadelphia recorded five sacks, held the Steelers to 255 total yards and limited them to just 73 rushing yards on 20 carries. The defense also held Pittsburgh to five conversions on 17 third-down attempts and kept the Steelers out of the end zone on two red-zone opportunities.

Nolan Smith has been one of the team's most productive pass rushers, leading Philadelphia with six sacks. Defensive tackle Jalen Carter has added five sacks, while Byron Young recorded two against Pittsburgh.

The Eagles still have a talented secondary led by cornerbacks Quinyon Mitchell and Cooper DeJean. Mitchell left Sunday's game with a hamstring injury, so his availability is uncertain. Still, significant personnel questions remain. Linebacker Zack Baun is on injured reserve, and Philadelphia traded defensive tackle Jordan Davis. Those are meaningful losses for a defense that will need to find a way to slow down a Dallas offense that has scored 91 points over its last two games.

The biggest question is whether the Eagles can replicate Sunday's defensive performance. If they can generate pressure and limit Dallas' rushing attack, they have a chance to make this game interesting. If they allow the Cowboys to control the line of scrimmage again, it could be a long afternoon.

The first meeting between these teams offered a pretty clear blueprint for how Dallas can beat Philadelphia. The Cowboys rushed for 204 yards on 38 carries, averaging 5.4 yards per attempt. Williams led the way with a career-high 155 rushing yards and a touchdown. Prescott added three passing touchdowns, and Dallas scored on seven of its nine offensive possessions.

Philadelphia simply couldn't stop the run.

That needs to be the starting point again. Dallas has found more balance offensively over the last several weeks, and the running game has become an important part of its success. Against an Eagles defense that just held Pittsburgh to 73 rushing yards, the Cowboys will need to establish that physical identity early.

Defensively, the game plan should be centered on making Milton uncomfortable. He has the arm strength to make big plays, but his accuracy and decision-making remain question marks. Dallas should pressure him without losing its discipline, particularly when he tries to escape the pocket. Easier said than done for this bunch.

The Cowboys also need to keep Barkley from taking over the game if he plays. If Philadelphia can establish its running attack, it can protect Milton and control the tempo. Dallas has struggled to consistently stop opposing offenses this season, and it cannot afford to let the Eagles dictate the terms of the matchup.

There is also a little extra motivation on the other sideline. Milton knows the Cowboys' organization, and he will undoubtedly want to make a statement against his former team. But Dallas has the more established offense and already showed it can take advantage of Philadelphia's defensive weaknesses.

The Eagles are coming off a win, and the rivalry always brings a different level of intensity. Still, the Cowboys have an opportunity to make another statement in the NFC East. If they can run the football like they did in October and force Milton to win through the air, they should have a good chance of leaving Thanksgiving with another division win.
