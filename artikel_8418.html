TurboTax vs. H&R Block – H&R Block Costs Less, Even With Expert Help

Choose H&R Block over TurboTax for most returns, since it charges less for the same forms, its free tier covers more returns, and its paid tiers include live help from tax pros. Choose TurboTax only if you prefer its software and experts enough to pay more.

Counting one state return, H&R Block charged less than TurboTax at every paid tier last season, for 2025 returns, the latest prices either company had posted by mid-November. A return that itemizes deductions such as mortgage interest and charitable gifts needed Deluxe at both, $114 at H&R Block against $143 at TurboTax. Stock sales or a rental needed Premium, $154 at H&R Block against $203 at TurboTax. Deducting business expenses needed H&R Block Self-Employed, at $179, or TurboTax Premium, at $203.

The widest gap falls on returns with unemployment income or credits for college costs. H&R Block Free Online covers both, with a free state return, while TurboTax Free Edition leaves both out, so those returns cost $143 in TurboTax Deluxe with a state.

TurboTax sells help as Expert Assist, which adds unlimited advice by phone, chat, or video and an expert’s final review. H&R Block’s paid tiers include unlimited live help from tax pros, and its Tax Pro Review adds a pro’s check, signature, and e-filing. With a pro’s review and one state, Deluxe came to $179 at H&R Block against $198 at TurboTax.

For help in person, H&R Block has offices in every state, where a pro prepares your return from $99 plus a state fee. TurboTax’s Full Service experts prepare the return online or at 500-plus offices by appointment, from $129 for one W-2 and one Form 1040, plus $69 a state.

Both import last year’s return from each other, and both can charge about $40 to take their fee from your refund, so pay by card.

TaxAct, a third choice, charged less than TurboTax at every paid tier last season, but its $64.99 state fee kept it within $11 of H&R Block’s price with one state. Filers who put price first can weigh TaxAct against TurboTax and against H&R Block too.

H&R Block fits most filers, since it costs less for the same forms with or without a pro. TurboTax fits a filer who prefers its software and experts enough to pay $24 to $49 more for a do-it-yourself return with one state.

Choose TurboTax if you like its software and want Expert Assist, which adds unlimited advice by phone, chat, or video and a final review, at a higher price.
