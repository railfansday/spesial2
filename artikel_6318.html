NASA’s Curiosity rover, expected to last 23 months, has now climbed 1 kilometer up a Martian mountain 15 years after launch

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Curiosity rover has now spent more than 5,000 sols on Mars, gained 0.62 miles of elevation on Mount Sharp and drilled its 49th hole into Martian rock.

On November 26, 2011, NASA launched the Curiosity rover toward Mars. When it left Earth, it was the largest and most capable rover ever sent to the planet, according to NASA.

The car-size robot landed in Gale Crater on August 6, 2012. Its job was to find out whether Mars was ever able to support microbial life.

NASA described that first phase as a “23-month primary mission.” Fifteen years after launch, Curiosity is still working, and it is now one kilometer (.62 miles) up the side of a Martian mountain.

Curiosity did not need all 23 months. NASA said in 2013 that evidence of a past environment well suited to support microbial life came “within the first eight months” of the primary mission, from the first rock sample ever drilled on Mars.

“We now know Mars offered favorable conditions for microbial life billions of years ago,” the mission’s project scientist, John Grotzinger, said at the time.

On June 24, 2014, the rover completed its first Martian year, which lasts 687 Earth days. By then it had driven almost 8 kilometers (about 5 miles), according to a NASA video from the team, and was heading for the base of a mountain.

That mountain is Mount Sharp, a peak about 3 miles (5 kilometers) tall in the middle of Gale Crater. Curiosity has been climbing it since 2014.

On August 26, 2026, the rover “marked an elevation gain of 1 kilometer in its journey from Gale Crater on Mars up the slope of Mount Sharp,” NASA said.

That is one kilometer of height above the crater floor, not distance driven. NASA called the milestone the high point of the mission so far.

William Farrand, a senior research scientist at the Space Science Institute who was on the science team for the earlier Spirit and Opportunity rovers, put the climb in perspective on NASA’s Curiosity blog.

When the Spirit rover reached the top of Husband Hill in August 2005, it had climbed 106 meters (about 348 feet) above its landing site. Curiosity has passed 1,000 meters (about 0.62 miles).

Three days later, on August 29, Curiosity marked 5,000 Martian days, or sols, on Mars. Because a day on Mars lasts 24.6 hours, that is more than 5,137 Earth days, the blog noted.

The rover keeps photographing the mountain as it goes. Earth.com has reported on Curiosity’s photos of Martian cliffs with a layer in the wrong color.

The rover is still collecting samples. After drilling in late October, the team wrote on the mission blog: “We successfully drilled our 49th drill hole, called ‘Nazca.'”

The team chose that rock because it was full of small disc-shaped forms that they interpret as possible crystals. If they are crystals, the team wrote, they could be “very interesting indicators of the environmental conditions.”

In mid-November, the team said it was time to “hit the Martian road again and resume our climb up Mount Sharp.”

The rocks in its path are covered in small polygons with raised ridges, a texture that can form when water is lost from minerals, among other causes, according to the blog.

“After over 5,000 sols of exploring with Curiosity I’ve learned Mars is full of surprises,” said Abigail Fraeman, the mission’s deputy project scientist, in the post.

Curiosity has company on Mars. NASA’s younger rover, Perseverance, has now driven farther than any vehicle on another world.

The information was obtained from NASA’s Curiosity mission page, the news releases NASA Curiosity Rover Approaches First Anniversary on Mars (2013) and Curiosity Reaches 1-Kilometer Elevation Gain (2026), and the Curiosity team’s mission blog.
