Simon Jordan drops 'crisis' claim as West Ham move to replace Nuno with Scott Parker

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Former Crystal Palace chairman and talkSPORT pundit, Simon Jordan, has weighed in on the debate which has unsurprisingly surrounded West Ham United's decision to part ways with Nuno Espírito Santo.

The Hammers' former boss found himself under increasing pressure following Saturday's 3-1 loss against West Bromwich Albion at the London Stadium - a result which also made it successive home losses in the Championship for the pre-season title favourites.

Results elsewhere meant that the East London outfit had slipped down to eighth after 13 matches played, four points behind Swansea City, who occupy the final automatic promotion place ahead of the midweek round of fixtures.

Nuno opted to stay in E20 in the summer despite the intense speculation which surrounded his managerial position after West Ham were relegated from the Premier League for the first time in 14 years on the final day of last season.

The hierarchy at the London Stadium have swiftly turned their attention to sourcing a replacement, though, with it being widely reported that talks have reached an advanced stage for Scott Parker to take charge.

West Ham United have parted company with Portuguese head coach Nuno Espirito Santo and set their sights on Scott Parker

Nuno lasted just under 14 months in charge of West Ham, having taken over from Graham Potter last September following a disastrous start to last season.

Despite losing key names such as Mateus Fernandes and Crysencio Summerville this summer, it was largely expected that the 52-year-old would add to his previous Championship-winning term with Wolves, such is the quality that had remained at his disposal.

Nuno won 20 of his 53 matches in charge, but when asked for his thoughts on the club's major call by Jim White, Jordan said: "They're eighth in the league. Six points off top spot, which is not a catastrophe.

"It's not ideal for many sides. How did Ipswich get on last year when they got relegated? Were they setting the world alight when they first got down? I don't think they were. I think they were mid-table for significant parts of the season."

Jordan then continued his debate on whether moving for Parker, who was named the Football Writers' Association's Player of the Year in 2010/11 despite West Ham's relegation, is the right decision given his track record in the EFL and the Premier League.

"They've made a decision. Scott Parker has proven to be someone that's capable of getting teams out of the Championship. He's also proven to be someone that can't keep teams in the Premier League," stated Jordan.

"The aim, clearly, for West Ham, is to make decisions that get them out of the Championship as quickly as they possibly can because of the economics of it.

"Whether other people were involved in the decision. I should imagine David Sullivan is still a shareholder in West Ham, and whilst we're not seeing him front and centre anymore, I imagine he's got something to say about who the manager might be.

"With those things in mind, Scott Parker obviously played for West Ham. He will have a regard for the club, the club will have a regard for him," the pundit added. "He will do a job.

Although West Ham have underperformed in recent weeks, the timing of the sacking has caused widespread debate, and so has the decision to move for Parker, although an appointment is still yet to be confirmed.
