Steelers locker room 'prideful' but muted after win amid Aaron Rodgers firestorm

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

PITTSBURGH – Pat Freiermuth found himself in the center of the mess involving the Steelers and now-former quarterback Aaron Rodgers this week.

Rodgers told ESPN's "Pat McAfee Show" that the veteran tight end was the player who informed him coach Mike McCarthy was benching him in favor of Mason Rudolph late Tuesday night, Nov. 24.

Rodgers went on to say a lot of other explosive things about McCarthy, general manager Omar Khan and owner Art Rooney II over the course of his rambling 34-minute interview that captivated the NFL world.

You know the themes by now. Rodgers said McCarthy was sleeping in meetings and slacking on his film study. He called McCarthy unhealthy and unfit to be calling the offense for a team in the heat of a race for a playoff spot in the AFC. Et cetera, et cetera.

It was noteworthy, then, when Freiermuth was one of the players to address the situation most directly in the locker room after his team cruised to a 27-5 victory against the Denver Broncos on Black Friday afternoon at Acrisure Stadium.

"Pretty tough. I'm not going to lie. It was pretty tough," he said when asked how he's felt the past couple of days. "A lot of emotions. Just really, just sad. It was just a sad situation. We've just got to move on. Aaron's still one of my closest friends, but we just have to continue to move on and keep winning."

Most of his teammates tried to stay away from it, obliquely referencing the difficulty of the preparation amid a national firestorm while trying to keep the focus on what happened on the field.

That emotion was palpable, though. The room cleared out pretty quickly. Post-game victory music lasted maybe 10 minutes between the end of the game and media arriving for post-game interviews. Right tackle Brock Hoffman wasn't even sure who'd received the game ball, if one was awarded. There were few hoots and fewer hollers.

Not quite the scene you'd expect after what should have been a rousing home victory against one of the top teams in the conference. One the Steelers could see again in the postseason as they seek their first playoff win since 2016.

"Prideful" was the word Freiermuth used to describe how he was feeling about the result.

Earlier in the week, he'd delivered a fiery speech to the offense. He's one of the unit's most tenured players, with most of six seasons in Pittsburgh under his belt. He was glad to see his group respond with some explosive plays that complemented two defensive touchdowns in a game that was never really close.

"I was feeling passionate about what was going on," he said. "It was more from the heart of what the Steelers mean to me, and to my family and the Rooney family. And, just in general, nobody cares what we're going through at all, so we're either going to act soft about it or go out there and do something about it."

Hoffman found the comments "motivating" and felt they added energy to what was left of the short week of practice after the bombshell of the Rodgers news had landed, then settled.

The team rallied around quarterback Mason Rudolph, who completed nine of his 15 passes for 142 yards and a touchdown with no interceptions in Rodgers' place. The running game added 117 yards to control the clock.

"Obviously, the whole world was laughing at us," Hoffman said. "I'm not going to comment on that situation, but it felt like everybody was kind of counting us out. And for us to come in here, still stay undefeated at home and beat a really good Broncos team? You can't write that any better."

A turning point this week was what coach Mike McCarthy called a "players council" meeting after Rodgers was benched, where many parties apparently spoke freely about what was and wasn't working for the team that had been mired in a two-game losing streak against Cincinnati and Philadelphia.

McCarthy had touted it as productive in responding to Rodgers' comments, but few players really had their chance to tell their side of the story.

Freiermuth said he appreciated it while implying, as Rodgers explicitly said in his interview, that some of it was overdue.

"We were able to have grown conversations," he said. "We were able to hold each other accountable and talk to each other about what we want to fix. Or what we can do better as a team, as players, as coaches. Really impressive about the conversations we've had this week. The asks, the wants that coaches and players see. And it was just great to put it together in a win and have those tough conversations. Because at the end of the day, when stuff happens and you talk it through, you're able to get closer."

It's therefore possible that these Steelers are fixable. They now sit at 7-4, a half-game behind the Bengals in the AFC North standings.

Their schedule down the stretch is manageable. Jacksonville is the only remaining opponent with a winning record, so there's a runway for this team to position itself well if it can continue to come together.

Freiermuth pointedly stopped short of praising McCarthy, however, when asked how it felt to deliver a win for him personally. That felt noteworthy, given his proximity to Rodgers, and indicated that there's probably quite a bit of healing still to come in a locker room that seems to lack buoyancy, even if the most recent result feels satisfying.

"Obviously that's tough for him and his family," Freiermuth said of the criticism McCarthy has faced in the wake of Rodgers' comments. "We just wanted to win in general."
