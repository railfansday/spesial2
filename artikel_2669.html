Fantasy Football Rankings: Hayden Winks' QB/TE/DEF/K Blueprint for Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Blueprint is my weekly column and basically everything that I use to build out the most accurate rankings I can. There will be an RB column, a WR column and this combination column on the onesie positions. You will find film notes and endless charts. Maybe enough charts to stress test the Yahoo website. I kid.

It's impossible to get every ranking thoroughly early in the week, so be sure to keep checking in on these columns through Sunday morning. As always, my full rankings at every position are available.

Not all players are listed in the markets.

1. Joe Burrow (at WAS) - 24.3 proj. points

Burrow is playing like the MVP. He's scored 24.6+ fantasy points in seven straight games, depending on the scoring system. His +0.26 EPA per dropback is fourth, and he's doing it on the highest neutral pass rate (67%) by a wide margin. He's at his mental and physical peak.

The Commanders defense has allowed the sixth-most half PPR points per game to QBs (20.8), and opposing offenses pass 59% of the time in neutral situations against Washington (third). Sign me up. The Bengals roll in as 6.5-point road favorites, carrying a 30.5-point implied team total (third).

2. Josh Allen (vs. MIA) - 23.9 proj. points

Another year of Allen averaging 26.3 fantasy points per game, which somehow is QB2 behind Joe Burrow. The only real change this year is the presence of a real downfield threat in DJ Moore, who scored a post TD and hit on a couple of crossing routes last game now that he's actually healthy.

The Dolphins just placed three key DTs on injured reserve and are quite inexperienced in the back. Allen's history against the Dolphins is exquisite. He's been a top-10 fantasy QB in 9 of 10 outings against them since 2022, with five top-5 games. Buffalo enters as 16.5-point home favorites with a 31.0-point implied team total (second).

3. Dak Prescott (vs. TEN) - 22.2 proj. points

Prescott is coming off his best performance of the year, hanging 343 passing yards and five touchdowns on the 49ers in Week 10. That's what will happen whenever he's pushed because the defense is failing, as usual. Prescott is currently the QB5 in fantasy, while sitting eighth in EPA per dropback. He's having a great season again.

The Titans aren't. They've allowed the most passing yards per game (282) and are on the worst three-game stretch of their team's history based on total yards allowed. It's hovering around 1,500 yards. Yikes. They won't have superstar DT Jeffery Simmons, nor S Amani Hooker moving forward. Dallas enters as 8.5-point home favorites with the top implied team total of the week at 31.5 points (first).

4. Caleb Williams (vs. NO) - 20.9 proj. points

Fresh off the bye, Williams is hoping to hit his stride after an injury and difficult schedule slowed him down to open the year. He's the QB12 on a per-game basis (19.8), including his rain/hamstring clunker against the Vikings. He's been the QB4 in passing yards since then but hasn't run as often (9.5 YPG). That hopefully will change after the break.

Week 11 brings a home date against a Saints defense that allows the 22nd-most half PPR points to opposing QBs (17.5 per game). New Orleans can rush the passer fairly well, but it's been terrible at tackling and can be run on at will. The Bears could lean on that here, but there's nothing about the LB and DB rooms to be fearful of. New Orleans is 11th in pass YPG and sixth in rush YPG. The Bears head into Sunday as 11.5-point home favorites with a stout 30-point implied team total (fourth).

5. Jayden Daniels (vs. CIN) - 21.3 proj. points

Daniels is not rushing nearly as often the last month (19.3 YPG) as he typically does, which has kept him in check in fantasy as the QB11. They aren't calling designed QB runs, so we're at the mercy of unstable scrambling production. That can return at any point, however, and this is a matchup that should incentivize just that. The Bengals are weakest at LB by far and there are more scrambles while chasing points. Washington is listed as 6.5-point home underdogs carrying a 24-point implied team total (11th). This is a spot to chase the ceiling. Cincy has allowed the second-most rushing yards per game to QBs versus their opponents' seasonal average.

6. Patrick Mahomes (vs. ARI) - 20.4 proj. points

Mahomes exploded for 348 yards and five passing touchdowns against Atlanta in Week 10, bringing his season-long baseline up to QB6 (20.8 PPG). He has delivered +0.15 EPA per dropback (QB9) across his nine starts while chipping in 11.1 rushing yards per game (28th). I can make the case that the return of Rashee Rice and the addition of Josh Downs have the pass game in the best spot of the year right now.

Plus, the Cardinals defense can't stop anyone, allowing the third-most fantasy points to QBs this season. Matthew Stafford turned in three scores despite missing his top receivers last week, and Dak Prescott (QB11), Bo Nix (QB3) and Jared Goff (QB4) have all lit it up since September. It's not a surprise to see the Chiefs projected for 28.5 points (sixth). They may not have Kenneth Walker III (groin) either, which could mean additional throwing production on the margins.

7. C.J. Stroud (vs. IND) - 21 proj. points

Stroud is the QB4 this season, averaging 274 passing YPG (QB3) with 2.1 TDs (QB6). The Texans have much better WR depth now than before, while still struggling to run the ball with any consistency. Houston is suddenly fifth in neutral pass rate (57%), and the Colts are missing all of their best defenders in CB1 Sauce Gardner, CB2 Charvarius Ward and DT1 DeForest Buckner. Recent fantasy QB1s Dak Prescott (336 yards) and Trevor Lawrence (three scores) have come through against them. Houston enters Thursday night as 12.5-point home favorites sporting a strong 28.5-point implied team total (fifth).

8. Trevor Lawrence (at NYG) - 18.8 proj. points

Lawrence has been in a groove as fantasy's QB8 (20.7 PPG), generating +0.29 EPA per dropback (QB3) with 21 passing touchdowns across nine starts. The only issue at times is not having a ton of volume due to game script, as the Jaguars easily lead the NFL in point differential. They're actually 31st all time in that metric through nine games.

This matchup is expected to be a bit closer, partially because the Giants defense has outkicked expectations. They've still allowed the seventh-most fantasy points to QBs, despite getting breakouts from rookie LB Arvell Reese and CB1 Deonte Banks. They're well coached with solid safety play. Where they've been lit up is against the scramble, as both Tyler Shough and Jayden Daniels have run like crazy. That's unlikely for Lawrence, so a more mediocre output wouldn't be too surprising. Jacksonville travels as 6-point road favorites with an implied team total of 26.25 points (seventh).

Bo Nix has more games below 10.0 fantasy points (three) than he does above 20.0 (two) so far. It's largely the same issues as in previous seasons, but to his credit, things historically have looked way better in the second half of seasons. I haven't noticed that he's a worse athlete coming off the dislocated ankle. It's more of just decision-making inside the pocket, but hopefully Sean Payton got into his head during this bye week. Hopefully Jaylen Waddle was listening, too!

To be fair, Nix has had the third-hardest schedule to date and the easiest schedule moving forward. This week kicks that off. The Raiders have been horrific on defense in both phases. They've allowed the second-most fantasy points to QBs and the second-most rushing yards to all positions. That's difficult to do! Nix has been the QB17, QB23, QB8 and QB8 against the Raiders historically, but this defense's current six-game streak of allowing a top-13 fantasy QB is more important. Denver is set up well as 10-point home favorites with a 25.25-point team total (eighth).

10. Jared Goff (vs. TB) - 17.9 proj. points

Goff's weird schedule is behind him after facing the Vikings, traveling outside to Miami and then ending up in Germany. Back in Detroit, Goff should normalize. He was the QB4 from Weeks 1-7 before all that and still is the QB6 in EPA per dropback and QB5 in passing yards per game.

This is still not an easy matchup, though. The Buccaneers bring the heat, something that has historically bothered Goff. Tampa Bay has an awesome CB duo and more depth along the defensive line, while still being coached by a Super Bowl winner. The Bucs are the fourth-best fantasy QB defense, despite everyone knowing this is a relative pass funnel (32nd in rush YPG). Caleb Williams squeaked by as the QB9 in Week 9, but they haven't allowed a top-10 since Week 2 otherwise. The Lions return home as 7-point home favorites with a solid 25.25-point implied team total (ninth).

11. Brock Purdy (vs. MIN) - 18 proj. points

Purdy is balling. He's the QB2 in EPA per dropback, as usual, but because he's making more plays out of structure and running around, Purdy is suddenly the QB3 in fantasy this year. That's impressive considering the WRs he's had at times, and Keenan Allen being around is a great way to calm down that room.

They'll need to be calm against the blitz-crazy Vikings. Minnesota hasn't just been the best fantasy QB defense — it's been an all-time unit. My site has a schedule-adjusted metric that essentially shows how many fantasy points each defense allows versus their opponents' seasonal average. The Vikings allowed 8.5 fewer points than QBs' averages. That's insane. Next closest is 4.3 points below average. Whew.

For context, if Purdy scores 8.5 fewer points than his 23.2 average, then he'd be the QB25. I don't think it'll be that bad because he has Kyle Shanahan and a better OL than others who have struggled, but Jordan Love (QB23), Josh Allen (QB17) and Jared Goff (QB21) are all fantasy QB1s, yet didn't finish like one in the most recent games against Minnesota. San Francisco enters Sunday night as 3-point home favorites with a 24.25-point implied team total (10th).

12. Cam Ward (at DAL) - 17.4 proj. points

This is the best Ward projection of his entire career. He's playing a bit better of late with the coaching staff meeting him halfway with their scheme. They've upped their no-huddle rate to the top of the league recently, and he's been running a bit more this month (23 rushing YPG) than he typically does. Ward is still too loose with things and has cleared 20.0 fantasy points in only three games this year, but if there's a time to view him as an upside QB2, it's now.

The Cowboys are just that bad. They've taken away play-calling duties from their DC, to summarize their season. They've been fifth-worst against fantasy QBs on the eighth-most passing yards per game (252). They've also allowed the most rushing yards per game (154). Offenses tend to play balanced ball against Dallas, but if Carnell Tate (shoulder) returns, then the Titans should take their chances downfield, too. Tennessee is listed as an 8.5-point road underdog with an implied total of 23 points (13th).

1. Colston Loveland (vs. NO) - 50.5 proj. yards

Loveland is the TE5 on TE3 usage since Week 6, when Caleb Williams returned. In his last 10 games together dating back to late in his rookie season, Loveland is averaging 11.4 half PPR points on 13.2 expected points, making him a fringe elite TE1. The true elites have disappeared with Brock Bowers healthy and the Cardinals projected for 12.5 points, so Loveland has a real case to take over as the short-term TE1 overall.

The Saints defense ranks sixth in half PPR points allowed to TEs at 13.1 per game, and they don't have the LB/S play to worry about. Harold Fannin Jr. had one of the best reps of the season against them in Week 9, and Isaiah Likely hung 99-1 on them over their last month of games. Chicago enters as 11.5-point home favorites with an implied team total of 30 points (fourth).

2. George Kittle (vs. MIN) - 51.5 proj. yards

Kittle enters as the TE6 on TE1 usage over his last four games, averaging 8.3 targets and 13.1 expected half PPR points per contest. He earned a 30% target share across that window and drew at least eight targets in three of those four appearances. There's more target competition now that Keenan Allen is in and Deebo Samuel Sr. is back, but he's been balling by any measure.

Of course, the Vikings are just as awesome. They've allowed the fewest fantasy points to TEs (6.7 per game) and the second-fewest passing yards (191 per game). Offenses tend to keep their TEs in to block more often against this blitz-heavy unit, and they've passed the ball in neutral situations at the second-lowest rate. Nothing about this works in Kittle's favor. This is simply great versus great. This is must-watch TV. San Francisco is listed as 3-point home favorites with an implied total of 24.25 points (10th).

3. Isaiah Likely (vs. JAX) - 54.5 proj. yards

Likely leads all tight ends as the TE1 on TE5 usage across his last four games, averaging 15.0 half PPR points on a 24% target share. He has scored in three of those four contests, peaking with a 9-127-1 line on 11 targets last week with J.J. McCarthy. He's way too important as the clear-cut No. 2 target to not get home whenever the Giants are pushed.

They are 6-point home dogs against the pass-funnel Jaguars this week. They've been stout against fantasy TEs (8.7 half PPR per game), but Likely is more of a slot WR than anything. Offenses facing them have passed at the second-highest rate in neutral situations (59%), and the Giants may be forced into more dropbacks than they want to if their defense can't stop this Jags offense.

4. Trey McBride (at KC) - 48.5 proj. yards

McBride's fantasy case has taken a hit due to the wild injury "luck" the Cardinals have run into. Last week, the offense couldn't run the ball, nor stop the run on the other side. The Cardinals ended up with 34 plays on offense, one of the lowest of the entire century. McBride still had five receptions and a 30% target share, while competing with Yahoo fantasy analysts for targets. My eyes tell me Carson Beck isn't quite as bad as what last week's box score showed. The INTs weren't his fault, and he didn't seem panicked. His accuracy is definitely worse than Jacoby Brissett's, but McBride is so QB-friendly that I tend to believe he'll survive despite the shocking result last week.

Kansas City sits eighth in half PPR points allowed to TEs (12.8 per game), while opposing offenses face them with a 45% neutral pass rate that ranks 32nd (lowest). There won't be much "neutral" game script here, however. The Cardinals will quickly get the ball out to McBride all game. The Chiefs have awesome CBs to take away all WRs, and they're a bit worse at stopping quick throws over the middle. A 12.5-point team total stares us down deep into our souls.

5. Travis Kelce (vs. ARI) - 40.5 proj. yards

6. T.J. Hockenson (at SF) - 42.5 proj. yards

We'll make sure he plays later in the week, but adding Kirk Cousins, who is a TE merchant, and subtracting Jordan Addison is an ideal run out for Hockenson. He's already the TE9 on TE9 usage this season, too. I'm open to him finishing as an every-week TE1 down the stretch.

A matchup with Fred Warner and the 49ers isn't ideal, but the Vikings haven't trailed too often this season, and they just might here.

11. Mark Andrews (at CAR) - 33.5 proj. yards

Andrews' 8-82-0 game last week came out of nowhere. He looked smooth on tape after being pretty clunky throughout the year. There was a report that he's been playing with a broken hand since Week 3. Equally as helpful as not having a broken body part that's very commonly used to catch an object is having dropback-heavy Jacoby Brissett at QB. He's targeted this position throughout his career as a risk-averse PPR scam artist. Keep in mind the Ravens have averaged 29.7 dropbacks per game since 2022, which is easily the fewest.

The matchup is just fine. The Panthers are solid at LB and are healthy along the DL, but they've been just average against the position. The Ravens' 20.5-point team total is also just average.

12. Tyler Warren (at HOU) - 37.5 proj. yards

I watched all of Warren's 2026 targets this week to get a feel for what's going on, and I came away without too much of an opinion either way. There are some moments of straight-line athleticism and plenty of physicality-based wins, but his route-running chops and fluidity are closer to average. The Colts haven't figured out how to get him unlocked beyond 5ish yards from the line of scrimmage, and they are run-heavy in the red zone, so it's hard for Warren to find a ceiling.

In theory, Warren should be a PPR scam while the offense tanks without their top-3 WRs. In practice, it hasn't happened. Warren is the TE13 on TE7 usage this month on a 22% target share. Better days are coming out of desperation for targets, but the offense seems content handing it off to both of their good-looking backs right now.

The Texans won't have LB Azeez Al-Shaair, but the Colts won't have All-Pro LG Quenton Nelson, nor their starting OTs. Daniel Jones is about to get crushed, and Warren might be tasked with chip and releases on Thursday Night Football. It's possible the score gets so lopsided that the target volume is overwhelming, but it's a tough needle to thread with the Colts projected for 16.0 points. Warren has been the TE13, TE23 and TE11 against the Texans to start his career.

We'll make sure Brock Bowers (ankle) doesn't play later on.

14. Mike Gesicki (at WAS) - 24.5 proj. yards

The Commanders have allowed the second-most fantasy points to TEs (16.9) despite having LB Sonny Styles, and the Bengals project for over 30 points while on fire. "Who is scoring the red-zone touchdown?" is a fun game to play while we have six teams on bye. Isaiah Likely just posted 127 yards with a score against them last week after finishing as the TE1 overall in Week 5, too. They aren't totally similar, but there is some overlap.
