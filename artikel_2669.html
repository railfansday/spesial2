4 instant takeaways from the Chiefs’ last-second loss in Buffalo

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Kansas City Chiefs fought to the end of a messy, low-scoring game against the Buffalo Bills, but ultimately fell 13-10. Here are four takeaways from Thanksgiving night:

From the jump, Kansas City's defense was ready for a long evening of containing James Cook and taking on the battering ram that is Josh Allen. On a rainy evening, Buffalo used a high-volume run game to control the contest, but the Chiefs never allowed Cook to gain more than 10 yards on any of his 32 carries.

Buffalo's running backs were held to 3.4 yards per carry, but quarterback Josh Allen gained 43 yards on the ground and found room for a 17-yard gash at one point. However, the Chiefs' coverage was tight and held Allen to just 81 yards and two interceptions on 10 of 21 passing; he finished with an 18.3 passer rating.

The Bills were able to march in the red zone a couple of times that Kansas City eventually forced to a screeching halt with clutch interceptions by cornerback Nohl Williams and safety Alohi Gilman.

As a low-scoring game came to a close, defensive coordinator Steve Spagnuolo dialed up the blitz to contain Allen in the pocket and bring him to the ground multiple times. From start to finish, the Chiefs' defense played winning football on Thanksgiving night…

… and the special teams unit did not play winning football. If you thought the conditions of the game were sloppy, the performance of the special teams topped it.

Whether it was a dropped kickoff, a dropped punt, a misfielded punt backed up deep, netting eight yards on a punt or the various penalties, it was a typical first half for the Chiefs' special teams unit. Unfortunately, the miscues kept coming, including a 46-yard field goal missed by the over-trusted Harrison Butker.

At the very end, punter Matt Araiza was backed up and had his punt tipped, setting up enough of a return for the Bills' game-winning field goal. It was a gross night for long-time special teams coordinator Dave Toub's unit.

From the opening drive, an apparent miscommunication between rookie left tackle Kahlil Benson and left guard Kingsley Suamataia left edge defender Bradley Chubb unblocked to make the third-and-one stop, it was a rough showing for the Chiefs' offensive line.

Backups Hunter Nourzad and Fred Johnson were inserted into the game and had costly penalties in the second half, but those were not the only two flags on the Chiefs' offensive line. One of the most crucial was called on left guard Kingsley Suamataia after the snap, which took Kansas City from a manageable third down to a third and long they were unable to convert.

Quarterback Patrick Mahomes and running back Emmett Johnson found space to make plays enough times to give the Chiefs a fighting chance, but the offense was overcoming backups up front as much as the rain in this one.

After the football world had its mind blown with Jacksonville Jaguars wide receiver Parker Washington's non-catch a few weeks ago, wide receiver Rashee Rice's catch on the field being overturned was the latest in the storied history of "what is a catch?"

No one will seemingly ever have a confident answer to that question. Alas, it wasn't the reason Kansas City lost this game, just an insane call.
