Personal Finance (Not Investing) • Re: Anyone having experience with ARVA [Annually Recalculated Virtual Annuity]?

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

ARVA is amortization by another name.
You amortize the portfolio on top of the floor over the remaining N years with a fixed discount rate, and you re-amortize every year. You'd get the exact same results in ABW.

This is not a diss on thebestthird and Shardansky's paper. That method is a special case of TPAW (off portfolio income, specific constant future stocks average return, fixed AA at 100% stocks) and I'm a huge fan and user of TPAW.

What I find interesting (and still looking at) is how the floor is handled when it has a horizon of over 30 years, since there are no TIPS past that point.
I don't see best third as a special case of TPAW. I see ARVA, the bestthird approach, TPAW, ABW and countless others as merely particular implementations of an amortization approach to withdrawing from a portfolio. The differences usually have to do with the choices of discount rates/expected returns, whether or not there is an attempt to keep total portfolio risk constant throughout retirement, whether or not future cash flows (like a pension or social security) are considered by discounting them back to the present as well as other features such as spending tilts, income floors, etc. Seemingly endless possibilities and choices that different folks might be interested in and depending on whether they want to roll their own spreadsheet or whether they're OK with whatever is available off-the-shelf, whether free or not.

The oldest mention I've found so far of using amortization for this was in a paper back in 2012 from "Bud" Hebeler, "Withdrawal Strategies in Difficult Times", available here: (see page 2, method 2 in the middle of the page) http://www.analyzenow.com/Articles/Plan ... -22-12.pdf

He has no footnotes, but it wouldn't surprise me if the idea had been around even longer, maybe even before spreadsheets were available.

There are ways to handle a floor over 30 years. First, by definition, the SS portion of the floor is essentially a lifetime annuity. But Kevin M has shown a way with his toolset to extend a TIPS past 30 years since some of the longer TIPS which have durations past 30 years. It would require some maintenance (buying/selling TIPS) for a while until you got to the point where all you needed was the 30 year set and forget ladder. Alternatively, the non ladder portion of the portfolio could be used to extend the ladder one year at a time by selling risky assets and buying another rung. There is interest rate risk in doing that of course.

Cheers.
