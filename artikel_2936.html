Planet Labs Teams Up With Stoke Space -- Just In Case SpaceX Really Retires the Falcon 9

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

If SpaceX retires the Falcon 9, Planet Labs will be ready with a replacement ride to space.

By Rich Smith – Nov 19, 2026 at 1:30AM EST

Planet Labs owns the world's biggest constellation of Earth observation satellites -- approximately 200 satellites in orbit, comprising the Dove and SkySat constellations that capture over 25 terabytes of imagery each day. These satellites only last about three to five years in orbit, however, before they fall out of the sky and must be replaced. This means that, even without growing its constellation size, Planet Labs needs to continually launch rockets with new satellites to refresh its fleet.

And here's the problem: Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.27%), the company that launches most of these satellites on its Falcon 9 rockets, is planning to retire those vehicles.

I argued recently that once SpaceX realizes how big of a problem it's creating for other space companies by retiring its Falcon -- and how much money it could make if it keeps Falcon operating -- SpaceX might reconsider and postpone Falcon's retirement.

But Planet Labs is making contingency plans. The company announced it has signed a multi-launch agreement with private space start-up Stoke Space Technologies.

As Planet Labs explains, its contract calls for Stoke to carry Planet Labs' satellites atop 15-ton-payload Stoke Nova reusable rockets, which will begin launching from Cape Canaveral Space Launch Complex 14 (SLC-14) in 2027.

No price was specified in the contract announcement, so we don't know exactly how much Planet Labs will pay for these launches. It's also not 100% clear when Stoke will launch the first Planet satellites.

Stoke has announced an early 2027 target date for the 1.0 version of Nova, which it is calling Nova Pathfinder. The first launch will carry payloads from space-mining company AstroForge and space-cremation company Celestis, which may leave little room for Planet Labs’ satellites.

Additional Nova Pathfinder missions are planned throughout 2027 and 2028, however, before Stoke transitions to a larger model: Nova Block 2. And even before Block 2 arrives, the original recipe Nova Pathfinder rocket is expected to have a 3-ton payload to low earth orbit -- more than enough to replace Planet Labs' entire SuperDove constellation in one go.

Long story short, Payload appears to have found itself a workable solution to the problem of vanishing Falcon 9 rockets. If Falcon missions do end in 2028 as expected, and Stoke succeeds in getting Nova operational this year -- or even next year or the year after that -- it should arrive in time for Planet Labs to avoid any SpaceX-related complications to its business plan.
