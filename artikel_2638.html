Cover 32: Why Jets have every reason to trade Breece Hall, plus Week 8 games balls, gaffes

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The New York Jets set the NFL ablaze at last year's trade deadline. The club made a couple of seismic moves, shipping away corner Sauce Gardner and defensive tackle Quinnen Williams for a collection of draft picks. Fast-forward about 12 months, and they're being eyed again as a seller. For the most part, folks have pondered the possibility of star wideout Garrett Wilson being available, but the organization has slammed that door shut.

The door that should be kept wide open, however, is trading running back Breece Hall.

Following the Week 8 loss to the Raiders, New York is 2-6 on the season and will travel to Kansas City to face the Chiefs in Week 9 before the Nov. 10 deadline rolls in. In all likelihood, they'll have accumulated seven losses over their first nine games once they reach the deadline. That's a recipe for a team to look itself in the mirror, realize the 2026 campaign won't bear any fruit, and move pieces to build for tomorrow.

Hall fits the bill as a player who is more valuable to them as a trade asset rather than a contributor on the field. That's thanks, in part, to the emergence of fellow running back Braelon Allen.

As Hall has been sidelined with a quad injury, Allen has flourished in the starting role. Over the last five games with Hall on the shelf, Allen has started in each contest and has rushed for 504 yards on a 5.4 yards-per-carry average and has two rushing touchdowns. Allen is also averaging 3.92 yards per carry after contact over this stretch, which is elite. For reference, that's better than Derrick Henry (3.11) and Jahmyr Gibbs (3.39) this season.

In the loss, Allen remained one of the bright spots for the Jets, rushing for a career-high 147 yards on a 7.0 yards-per-carry clip.

With Allen, a 2024 fourth-round pick, under team control through 2027 and on his rookie contract, Hall becomes an expendable piece that the Jets should exploit before the deadline.

When healthy, Hall is an electrifying piece out of the backfield, notching his first-ever 1,000-yard rushing season last year for the Jets. In three games this year, before missing time with this quad injury, he's posted modest numbers, totaling 265 yards from scrimmage. Still, he would garner plenty of interest on the trade market if New York puts him on the block, especially as he nears a return to the field. Despite not playing in Week 8, Hall was limited in practice all last week, signaling he's close.

There are a slew of teams interested in adding a back at the deadline, but the supply may be running thin. Jonathan Taylor and Saquon Barkley have been two big names circulating in the rumor mill, but neither may end up being moved. The Eagles effectively shut down any talk of them moving Barkley, while the Colts are not actively shopping Taylor and would likely need to be blown away with an offer to deal him.

This creates quite the opportunity for the Jets to cash in with Hall. They could deal him to an RB-needy team, further strengthen their treasure trove of draft capital, and turn the keys of the backfield over to Allen permanently.  They'd also get out from under Hall's contract, which runs through 2028 and sees his base salary jump from $5.16 million in 2026 to $13.1 million in 2027 and $14.1 million in 2028.

It makes too much sense to explore a trade and ship Hall to the highest bidder.

The Kansas City Chiefs have dropped two straight games, and kicking issues have been at the center of both defeats. In the 28-24 loss to Denver on Sunday, K.C. had an opportunity to ice the game despite a late rally from the Broncos. Facing a fourth-and-8 situation from the Denver 36-yard line, Any Reid decided to send Harrison Butker out for a 54-yard attempt to extend the Chiefs' lead to six points. However, Butker's attempt sailed wide right.

Harrison Butker goes wide right!@Broncos take over with good field position on CBS & Paramount+ pic.twitter.com/rzelhOMh3n

Denver took advantage of the missed kick, moving 52 yards down the field in just two plays to take the 28-24 lead, which proved to be the final score.

Given Butker's struggles kicking from distance this season (and even dating back to last year), it was a curious decision by Reid to take the ball out of Patrick Mahomes' hands in that spot. Had they kept the offense on the field for that fourth-down attempt and converted, they would've been able to chew even more time off the clock and better position themselves to win. Again, considering how shaky Butker has been, that was probably the higher percentage play.
