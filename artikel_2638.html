Celtics lose Group C to Hawks 87-86, will be Play-In wildcard

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Jalen Johnson and the Hawks' defense proved too much for the Celtics in a 87-86 loss in the final game of NBA Cup group play. Boston had a chance to lock up East Group C and home-court advantage through the semifinals of the NBA Cup's elimination rounds, but the loss allowed the Hawks to win the group via tiebreaker.

However, their record and point differential still leave the team with the wild card spot, so the team is set to face the Detroit Pistons in the quarterfinals.

Boston started Derrick White, Hugo Gonzalez, Paul George, Jayson Tatum, and Neemias Queta against Atlanta. Mitchell Robinson remained out as he recovers from COVID for the fourth straight game.

The Hawks started Kingston Flemings, Nickeil Alexander-Walker, Dyson Daniels, Jalen Johnson, and Onyeka Okongwu. The team's loaded injury report included CJ McCollum, Jock Landale, Mouhamed Gueye, Asa Newell, and Henri Veesaar.

The game got going with a couple ugly offensive possessions for the C's, but Gonzalez put Boston on the scoreboard with a putback layup off a Queta miss. Luckily, the Hawks also had a rough start and missed their first 5 shot attempts of the game. Daniels was the first to break the ice for Atlanta by hitting a three-pointer from the corner, but the early game was defined by defense.

While the Celtics struggled to break free from their defenders along the perimeter, Gonzalez hit the team's first three-pointer of the game off an assist from Tatum to put Boston up 8-3. That score held for a couple minutes, as neither team could score consistently. However, the Hawks had the hardest time breaking through, as they missed 11 of their first 12 shots from the field.

With about 6 minutes left in the quarter, the bench was subbed in to play alongside Tatum and made an immediate impact as Baylor Scheierman hit a three-pointer that was only possible due to an offensive rebound by Luka Garza, who got a layup of his own shortly after. At that point, both teams got into a better offensive rhythm. JT took a more active role on the floor and hit a couple shots, while Johnson and Luguentz Dort sunk a couple of their own. By the time Payton Pritchard finished a layup with 2:23 to go in the quarter, the Celtics were ahead 21-12.

The Hawks' perimeter defense was doing a great job at running the C's off of the line, so Boston attacked the paint early and often. That attack resulted in a solid contested layup by Tatum, who followed it up with a slick step-back three-pointer on the next trip down the court. He had 13 points in the first quarter alone.

The Celtics finished the quarter in dominant fashion, as Pritchard hit a three-pointer and the defense locked down the Hawks to end the frame.

Boston opened the second quarter with a 29-14 lead, but immediately surrendered a three-pointer and turned the ball over on their side of the court. Queta got the scoring going with an easy layup over Daniels. A couple plays later, Queta got an offensive rebound and passed it out to Sam Hauser for an open three-pointer.

That started off an impressive little stretch by Hauser, who then got an offensive rebound of his own off a missed Pritchard three-pointer and put it back for an easy layup, then stole the ball on a bad pass from Corey Kispert. However, he was subbed out soon after in favor of Ron Harper Jr.

The Hawks scored a quick 5 points to narrow Boston's lead to 37-25, and had a chance to cut it to 10, but White got a nice block on Okongwu and got it ahead to George for a transition layup and his first points of the game. Unfortunately for George, he racked up 4 turnovers over his first 12 minutes of play and was subbed out with a little more than 6 minutes to go in the frame.

Tatum came back into the game around the same time and hit another contested layup to put the C's up 43-30 with less than 5 minutes to go in the half, but Boston looked a little sloppy and called a timeout about a minute later.

Following the break, Tatum missed a tough shot but got immediate redemption with a steal that led to a Gonzalez transition layup. Gonzalez also hit a beautiful floater while driving down the lane a couple plays later. While White masterfully broke up a fast break by the Hawks, Atlanta's offense clearly started clicking before halftime. They scored 9 points over the final 3 minutes of the half, which finished with the C's up 51-39.

At halftime, Tatum had 16 points — which led the team — on 54.5% shooting from the field, along with 4 rebounds, 3 assists, and 3 steals.

No other Celtic finished the first half with double-digit points, but Gonzalez wasn't far behind with 9 points on 4-6 shooting. Queta had only 3 points, but was incredibly active on the boards, as he racked up 12 rebounds.

On the Hawks' side of things, Johnson led the way with 12 points (on 4-12 shooting), 7 rebounds, 2 assists, and 3 stocks. Like Tatum, he was the only player on his team to finish with double-digit points.

Overall, the half closed with the Celtics shooting 50% from the field and 40% from three-point range, while the Hawks shot 28.9% from the field and 19.2% on three-pointers. However, Atlanta put up 7 more field goal attempts than Boston and 11 more three-point attempts.

Coming out of the half, White got a nice steal, but George's off night continued as he missed an open three-pointer. The Hawks capitalized and started the second half with a much stronger offensive showing than the C's. They went on a 9-4 run to cut the lead to 7 points less than 2:30 into the third quarter, with Johnson scoring 7 of Atlanta's 9 points during the stretch.

That's when George finally woke up. He hit two three-pointers over the course of three offensive possessions to bring Boston's lead back up to 13 points but, unfortunately for the Celtics, everyone else went cold. Tatum missed an easy, wide-open corner three-pointer, and White missed another open 3 the next time down, leading Head Coach Joe Mazzulla to call a timeout the next time the C's got the ball.

It didn't help much, though. While Hauser was subbed in for White, the Celtics could not buy a shot from deep. On the other hand, the Hawks scored 10 of the first 12 points scored out of the break and cut the lead down to 5, 65-60, with less than 4 minutes to go in the third quarter — leading Boston to call another timeout.

When play resumed, Garza cut into the paint for a nice layup, but the C's surrendered a three-pointer to Johnson and another to Aaron Wiggins a few plays later. By the time Kispert cut in for an easy layup with 1:30 to go in the frame, the lead had been chipped down to 69-68.

Daniels gave the Hawks their first lead of the night with a floater with 12 seconds to go, and Tatum missed a layup to end the quarter. Atlanta finished the frame with a 70-69 lead.

The Celtics still looked disjointed as the fourth quarter began, but White got a steal and knocked down a layup to put Boston on top again — at least until Dort hit a couple free throws to retake the lead. Pritchard hit a good-looking floater, but it was negated by a shot clock violation. Thankfully, he hit a step-back three-pointer from the top of the arc soon after to tie the game up at 74 with about 10 minutes left in the game.

Nevertheless, the momentum remained in the Hawks' favor, as Dort hit a three-pointer and drew a charge on Garza, which was upheld after a ridiculously long official review. The failed challenge left the C's with 2 timeouts with 9 minutes left to go.

With the help of a Queta free throw and a Hauser three-pointer, the C's took a 78-77 lead with 7:48 remaining. Tatum was subbed back in soon after, but he struggled to get the offense going again. On one play, Boston got multiple offensive boards, but missed all three shot attempts of the possession. On top of that, the refs were stingy with the whistle and called offensive fouls on both Queta and George, ending 2 possessions prematurely.

Fortunately for the Celtics, the Hawks offense also came back down to earth, and a few minutes passed without any scoring on either side. The next points weren't scored until the 4:38 mark of the fourth quarter, when Okongwu hit a floater to put the Hawks up 79-78. The Hawks got the next bucket on a Johnson dunk and took a 3-point lead with 4:06 to go.

Turnovers were killer. Queta, who was dominant on the boards and set a new personal record of 21 rebounds with 3 minutes left in the game, turned the ball over twice in quick succession. Tatum then lost his handle and lost a ball out of bounds.

The Celtics didn't score again until Dort fouled White, who knocked down a pair of free throws to narrow the Hawks' lead to 83-81 with 1:42 left in the game. White then hit a floater on the next offensive possession to tie the game at 83 with 1:10 to go.

Alexander-Walker followed up White's buckets with a driving dunk to retake the lead for Atlanta, and Johnson blocked Tatum on a drive into paint to prevent the tying bucket. With 11.9 seconds remaining and the Hawks with the ball, Tatum got a steal but turned the ball over again right after. Alexander-Walker sealed the game with a pair of free throws that put Atlanta up 87-83 with 3 seconds left to go. White hit a buzzer-beating three-pointer in the remaining time, but it wasn't enough to get the win

Tatum finished the game with 20 points on 36.4% shooting from the field, 9 rebounds, 5 assists, and 4 steals.

Queta racked up 11 points, 22 rebounds, 3 assists, and 3 stocks.

On the Hawks' side, Johnson finished with 24 points and 12 rebounds, while Alexander-Walker finished with 20 points and a couple crucial last-minute plays.

The Celtics will play Victor Wembanyama and the San Antonio Spurs next at 4 p.m. Sunday, Nov. 29, at the Frost Bank Center in San Antonio.
