All 24 EFL Championship clubs' best defender ranked from worst to best

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The EFL Championship is home to so many good players, with a collection probably capable of being comfortable of being in the Premier League - and even picking all 24 clubs' best defender is a challenge.

But who has the best and worst 'best' defender in the entire second tier of English football?

It's going to be a contentious topic, but FLW has tried to rank them from 24th to 1st...let's take a look.

After a positive start to the 2026-27 season, Lincoln City have somewhat been on the slide in recent weeks, leading to the sacking of co-managers Chris Cohen and Tom Shaw at Sincil Bank.

Their summer recruitment was a mix of domestic loans and some overseas punts for players that will have been heard very little of in Lincolnshire - such as Universitatea Cluj defender Andrei Coubis.

Once on the books of AC Milan, Coubis is a towering 6ft 5in defender who at the age of 23 has his best years ahead of him, and is capable of playing on the right-hand side of a back three, or as a right-back/wing-back.

It hasn't exactly been a comfortable start to life in the Championship for the Romanian, but he's showing positive signs in a struggling side - however, he's yet to do it for a consistent period that can put him any higher than bottom in the list.

The creatively labelled 'Mount Forino' by Bolton Wanderers fans, Chris Forino is a Saint Lucia international who had to battle in non-league before getting a chance in professional football in 2021 with Wycombe Wanderers.

Three years of experience with the Chairboys though led to a free transfer move to Bolton in 2024, and despite having a large spell on the bench last season in the third tier under Steven Schumacher, Forino forced his way back into the starting 11 in January, and is now one of the first names on the Trotters team-sheet when he's fully fit.

Whilst not the most technically-gifted when it comes to the modern-day centre-back, Forino does the traditional things you want your solid, no-nonsense defender to do, and he does it generally well, but he's still very low down on this list because of his lack of experience at the level.

As it stands, Watford are on a downward spiral and perhaps have been for the last few years. Many players have come and gone in that time, as well as managers, but one of the constants has been Mattie Pollock.

Signed in 2021 from Grimsby Town, Pollock is now the Hornets captain and was named the club's Player of the Year for 2025-26, and he regularly puts a shift in despite his team-mates faltering around him.

Pollock is another no-nonsense defender who is physically imposing and he may get better because he's only 25 years of age, yet he's still one of the lower quality best centre-halves in the division.

Blackpool fans weren't happy when bitter rivals Preston North End snapped up their former loanee Odel Offiah from Brighton in January 2025 - and for good reason.

Offiah has proven to be a strong, quick and athletic defender, best used on the right-hand side of a back three, but he's also capable as a wing-back - even though his end product in the final third perhaps leaves a bit to be desired most of the time.

There won't be many stronger or quicker defenders than the 24-year-old Lilywhites man on this list, but he's still got a bit to prove at Championship level if he wants to make it higher in the standings.

Michael Skubala may not know where Rio Cardines' best position is at Bristol City, but it surely has to be as a full-back or wing-back.

The 20-year-old Trinidad & Tobago international joined the Robins on loan from Crystal Palace in the summer, and immediately started making waves with a flurry of assists from both right and left-back, notching six in his first eight outings.

Recently, Skubala has been using Cardines in a more advanced role out wide, and the assists have dried up - it's surely time for the youngster to return to the City backline where he belongs, as he's arguably their best defensive player, even if his biggest strengths are going forward.

Lloyd Jones had obvious potential as a youngster after being plucked from Plymouth Argyle by Liverpool as a teenager, but it took the centre-back a number of years to find a permanent home where he could flourish.

A two-year stint at Cambridge United though - where he won the Player of the Year award prior to his 2023 exit - unlocked the door for a move to Charlton Athletic, and he's come on leaps and bounds since.

Jones has won back-to-back Player of the Year awards for the Addicks, both as a League One and Championship player, and he's the rock at the back that Charlton will desperately need if they're going to remain in the second tier.

He has his limitations though, and he is 31 years of age now, but as far as Championship centre-halves go, he's an incredibly solid and reliable one.

Despite having very little Championship game-time so far due to injuries, Dylan Lawlor is a player that has an incredibly bright future for Cardiff City - or perhaps for a top flight club given all the past interest.

Lawlor was a key player in the Bluebirds' 2025-26 League One promotion-winning campaign, and he has even broken into Craig Bellamy's starting 11 for the Wales national team, such is the talent he's showed.

Bayern Munich were reportedly tracking the 20-year-old during last season's exploits, but if there's one drawback to his game so far, it's fitness - Lawlor suffered from a number of niggling injuries last season, and he's already suffered two setbacks in 2026-27 with his knee and then hamstring.

If Lawlor can fight fit again and get an extended run of games, then there's little doubt he'll show what a talent he is at Championship level.

It's been a desperately poor first half of the Championship season for Burnley, and fan apathy is at an all-time high - their defending at times has left a lot to be desired.

Kyle Walker is perhaps showing his age, and to be blunt, none of the defensive options are pulling up any trees.

For whatever reason though, despite his past form for Sheffield United at Championship level, Anel Ahmedhodzic is not getting much game-time under new Clarets boss Fabio Celestini after his summer move from Feyenoord, which set them back a reported €7 million fee.

The Bosnian is proven at the level but cannot get in the starting 11 - perhaps it's time for the 27-year-old to get more of a chance, as the likes of Max Alleyne and Igor are not providing what is needed right now at Turf Moor.

There's a number of bargain buys in Portsmouth's current team that are flourishing in the Championship - and none more-so than Terry Devlin.

Brought in from Northern Irish side Crusaders back in 2023 as an energetic midfielder, Devlin has been converted into a permanent right-back by John Mousinho - and that trick has worked wonders.

Devlin isn't shy of finding the back of the net from that position, and he's now also starting to force his way into the Northern Ireland starting 11, even though he's got stiff competition with Trai Hume and Conor Bradley in the same area of the pitch.

Still only 23 years of age, Devlin's got room for improvement, but he's already a very well-rounded Championship full-back, and will have the Premier League in his sights.

There's plenty of talent to be had in Northern Ireland for English clubs, as evidenced with Devlin at Portsmouth, as well as Ruairi McConville's rapid rise in Norfolk.

Snagged from Linfield in 2021 by Brighton when he was just 16, McConville's only senior Seagulls game had come against Norwich City in the FA Cup in January 2025 - he impressed enough though in his cameo for the Canaries to sign him permanently for £1.5 million initially, which could rise to £2.75 million.

Arguably, McConville is worth a lot more than that figure now thanks to his lengthy run of starts under Philippe Clement last season, and the 21-year-old has kicked on further in 2026-27, becoming one of the first names on the team-sheet.

Already notching 18 Northern Ireland caps to his belt, McConville is developing at a rapid rate of knots, and it's impossible to know how good he'll actually get - Brighton may have let a good one go.
