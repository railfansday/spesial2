Tony Gonzalez, Ryan Fitzpatrick tee off on ‘selfish’ Aaron Rodgers after Steelers mess

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Prime Video analysts didn't mince words when dissecting Aaron Rodgers' rant — and subsequent divorce — from the Steelers this week.

Ahead of the Steelers' game against the Broncos on Black Friday, the pregame show opened with a segment about Rodgers' benching, his now-infamous interview on "The Pat McAfee Show," his scathing accusations of Pittsburgh head coach Mike McCarthy and his release Wednesday afternoon.

Hall of Famer Tony Gonzalez called Rodgers "selfish" and former quarterback Ryan Fitzpatrick added that Rodgers will be remembered as "one of the most complicated athletes of all time."

"It's sad," Gonzalez said. "It's really sad for everybody. … It makes no sense. You're talking out of two sides of your mouth. What you're saying, it's insane the way Mike McCarthy is acting, but you're the one who vouched for him to come over here, to get him into the building. And then you nuke everybody. You nuke your teammates, a playoff football team. I just think it makes no sense. It's one of those things that's gonna go down in history."

Aaron Rodgers of the Pittsburgh Steelers reacts against the Philadelphia Eagles in Philadelphia on Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. The Eagles won 19-16. Getty Images

Fitzpatrick said that the Steelers' locker room is like a chess board for Rodgers, where all the pieces are "expendable" when they "can't protect the king."

"He is loved by his teammates wherever he's been," Fitzpatrick said. "And this Pittsburgh Steelers locker room, they loved Aaron Rodgers and the teammate that he is. But the complicated part is what did he do? He blew everything up. He sees the locker room like a chess board, is what I feel like.

Amazon Prime Video host Ryan Fitzpatrick appears on the pregame show before a game between the Pittsburgh Steelers and Denver Broncos, Friday, Nov. 27, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

"All the pieces that he can manipulate and move, they're very precious to him as long as they're protecting the king, but as soon as they don't have value and they can't protect the king, then all of a sudden, they become expendable — and he throws them out. And this is what happened with Mike McCarthy."

Tension between Rodgers — who was in his second year with the Steelers — and McCarthy dated back to their years with the Packers, but it boiled over again this week.

Despite two consecutive losses, McCarthy stood by Rodgers as his quarterback Monday. Then, one day later, the Steelers dropped a press release at 11:10 p.m. that named Mason Rudolph the starter for Friday's home tilt.

And one day later, Rodgers returned to McAfee's show and proceeded to shred McCarthy, claiming that the longtime NFL head coach fell asleep in meetings and broke down a halftime huddle Sunday with "1-2-3 Packers."

Amazon Prime Video host Tony Gonzalez speaks on the pregame show before a game in Pittsburgh, Friday, Nov. 27, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

The Steelers, via owner Art Rooney II, have reiterated their full support for McCarthy, who admitted to the Packers blunder, didn't deny the sleeping allegations during his session with reporters Wednesday and said Rodgers' "ultimatum" led to Pittsburgh cutting ties with the signal-caller.

Even with their recent pair of losses, the Steelers only sit a game behind the Bengals in the AFC North, and they'll now turn to Rudolph as their starter after releasing Rodgers.

And even though Rodgers cleared waivers, Prime Video insider James Palmer reported on Friday's pregame show that there's "little to no interest" from teams in signing the 42-year-old and four-time NFL MVP — whose storied career on the field just took its latest turn away from it.

"I've never seen anything like this in my life," Gonzalez said, "and we've played this game for a long time. And just not in football. Basketball, hockey, it is unprecedented what went on, and it's one of those things — it's like a little hint of narcissism … to come out on air on 'The Pat McAfee Show' and do what he did."
