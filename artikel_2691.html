6 takeaways from Detroit Lions coordinator prior to Bucs game

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Detroit Lions coordinators met with the media on Thursday in preparation for their big game against the Tampa Bay Buccaneers this week. Here are six takeaways I had from Drew Petzing, Kelvin Sheppard, and Dave Fipp.

Kyle Juszczyk inspired how the Lions use Sam LaPorta

Recently, the Lions have utilized a lot of return motions with Sam LaPorta—where he'll be in motion in one direction during the snap, and suddenly change directions after the snap. Here's a good example from last week:

Petzing revealed that he was inspired by how the 49ers use fullback Kyle Juszczyk—someone Petzing worked with at Harvard—when putting LaPorta in that role.

"You have got to have somebody who's athletic, who can play on the move, who can live in the gray a little bit because it's not like when he's going in full motion, he knows exactly what the look is or exactly where everybody's lined up," Petzing said. "And I think Sam does such a good job of navigating that. And then there's pass game stuff that comes off of it, obviously, which Sam is elite at. So, I think that's kind of where it came from and why we feel like it really fits us and him as a player."

Here's Juszcyzk doing something similar (albeit with no pre-snap motion)—against the Lions—but building some of the pass game stuff Petzing was referring to.

Our own Al Karsten was one of the first to notice some incredible splits between the first and second half pass protection. In short, the Lions have been one of the worst pass protecting units in the first half and one of the worst in the second:

The Lions have allowed 21 sacks this season, and 18 of them have come in the first half. Petzing had several explanations for the phenomenon.

"A lot of it is we've really ended the last five or six games running the ball probably 70 to 75 percent of the game, which probably has a little bit of hand in that," Petzing said. "I think settling in is a part of that. I like to think that we wear on people and bring fatigue in the way that we play and the way that we go through a game. So, some of those things could certainly play into it. And then part of it's probably just chance, a little bit of both."

Tyleik Williams already one of the best nose tackles in football

If you were just box-score scouting, you may overlook the impact of Lions 2025 first-round pick Tyleik Williams. But the defensive tackle has taken his game to the next level, and Sheppard believes he's alreayd one of hte best nose tackles in the league who is still scratching the surface with his pass rushing abilities.

"I'm seeing one of the best nose tackles in the NFL right now. And things may not show up on the stat sheet but just turn on the tape and show me where an offensive lineman blocks him one-on-one and has success. Rarely to never," Sheppard said. "And credit to him because he does have a lot more in his toolbox, a lot more in his arsenal where he could be saying, 'Oh, I need more pass rush.' He's never done that. He's taken advantage of his ops. He knows where we need him for us to play collectively at a high level and he's doing it."

Despite his high level of play, Williams has only played in 301 defensive snaps, just under 50%. For reference, Day 3 rookie defensive tackle Skyler Gill-Howard is barely behind him at 288 snaps. That likely has to do with game situation, and the Lions facing more passing situations than run on defense. But even so, Sheppard expects Williams to see his playing time rise soon.

"Are we going to try to find more plays for him? Absolutely. He's one of our better players right now."

While D.J. Reed remains sidelined, the Lions have leaned heavily on Roger McCreary. Sheppard told an interesting story on why he's drawn to a player like McCreary—who has his share of doubters, as the Lions are now his third team in the last two seasons. McCreary asked Sheppard not to pigeonhole him as an outside cornerback or nickel—just given him an opportunity to win anywhere.

Now, after a strong couple of games in Detroit, the roles have reversed. Whereas McCreary was leaning on Sheppard to give him an opportunity, now Sheppard is leaning on McCreary to produce on Sundays.

"He's worked his tail off schematically, physically to do the things that we want done here the way that we do them. And he's doing it at a high level," Sheppard said. "This past month, he's been one of our best secondary players. He's been one of our best corners, honestly. And I lean on him now. The same way he leaned on me with that trust factor of, 'Where should I sign?' I'm leaning on him now, now that things are hitting the road and that's a player that I count on come Sunday and he's earned that trust."

Early in the year, Lions second-round rookie Derrick Moore was not getting many opportunities. After missing significant time in training camp, Detroit wasn't prepared to give him more than a situational role. Sheppard noted that Moore never complained, just put his head down and put in the work. His role subsequently increased not too long after.

Now, with Aidan Hutchinson out for the rest of the year, Moore will be leaned on more than ever, and Sheppard believes he's already rising to the occasion—starting with his two-sack performance last week after Hutchinson went down.

"When his opportunity comes, when you operate in that way, when you operate like that, you're never going to squander that opportunity when it comes because this is what he's been doing all along," Sheppard said. "It's just at a bigger sample size now. But I'm seeing a player that's playing faster and faster each week. And that was the biggest message to him. We know what he's capable of. It's just doing it at a high rate over and over at a consistent basis."

Given Hutchinson's injury and some noticeable slipping on the turn in Munich, Germany, some have criticized the groundskeeping at Allianz Arena for the Lions vs. Patriots game last Sunday.

Don't count Dave Fipp among them. He believes the conditions were quite similar to that of Lambeau Field.

"I feel like that field reminds me a little bit of Green Bay, like afternoon, evening game when the sun goes down in Green Bay gets a little bit slick, sort of similar," Fipp said. "If you wear the right footing, I don't think it's an issue at all. We had some guys change cleats early in the game because I think they felt like they were slipping around a little bit. You could see it on a takeoff on field block or rush or a takeoff on a punt rush or return. Some of those guys are in three point stances digging in. But, other than that, yeah, no factor. I mean, both sides are playing on it. I felt like the conditions were great. Yeah, I had no issue."
