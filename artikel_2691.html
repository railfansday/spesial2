John Stones faces trial over alleged motoring offence

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

England defender John Stones faces trial in 2028 over an allegation he failed to provide driver information to police.

The 32-year-old footballer, who left Manchester City for Inter Milan this summer, is alleged to have driven his £150,000 Range Rover at 58mph (93km/h) in a 50mph zone on the M60 in March 2026.

Greater Manchester Police (GMP) said Stones did not respond to two letters and on 27 April was charged with failing to furnish driver information. He has since pleaded not guilty.

Stones is represented by Freeman & Co Solicitors, a law firm whose owner Nick Freeman has advised several celebrity clients in motoring cases and has trademarked his nickname of Mr Loophole.

Stones now faces trial on 29 September 2028 at Warrington Magistrates' Court.

He has denied receiving the request for information from the police and asked prosecutors to prove it was served.

In a letter to the court, Stones's lawyers added: "It was not reasonably practicable for the defendant to give the information within the requisite period."

They also claimed GMP included "hearsay" in its single justice procedure notice dated 20 October 2026.

Stones's team requested that the trial should last at least two hours and be heard in the morning.

They also asked for permission for him to be excused from a pre-trial case management hearing were one to be required.

The England defender joined Inter Milan in the summer after 10 years at Manchester City [Press Association]

Earlier this year Stones was convicted of speeding and landed with a £1,052 court bill after he missed a police letter while at the World Cup in the United States and Mexico with England.

He had been caught driving his Range Rover at 43mph along a country lane near his Cheshire mansion in May.

Stones admitted being the driver when first contacted by Cheshire Police, but did not respond to a second letter offering him the chance to pay a fixed penalty to keep the speeding offence out of court.

He was then prosecuted and convicted in the single justice procedure, with a magistrate last week issuing him four penalty points and ordering him to pay a £666 fine, £120 in costs and a £266 victim surcharge.

Stones wrote to Warrington Magistrates' Court to say: "Nothing to say about the speeding.

"The reason I am only responding to the offence now is because I represent our country for football and so I have not been home and seen any post.

"I sent off my letter on the 28th May to give my details [and then] I went off to join England.

"I went on holiday and then moved to Milan, so this has been found by chance, as no-one has been home for almost four months.

"This is the reason I also would not be able to be present as I no longer am residing in the country."

Central defender Stones, who played for Barnsley and Everton before a 10-year spell with Manchester City, has made four appearances so far for Inter Milan since his summer switch to the San Siro.
