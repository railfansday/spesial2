How the Bears’ offense grades after their bye week

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Bears had their bye week this week, using the weekend to recover and regroup heading into their final eight games of the 2026 season. All told, their 7-2 record is one to be proud of, and a good chunk of that success comes down to how their offense has performed this year.

Ben Johnson has done a great job in his second year as the Bears' head coach and their offensive play-caller, and Caleb Williams has led the passing attack to become one of the better units in the league. As the eye test can tell you if you've watched enough of this team, the analytics are quite favorable to this offense as it heads out of the Week 10 bye.

All year, I've been recapping how the Bears have done analytically based on PFN Impact grading. Coming out of the bye week, I wanted to recap how each of their offensive players have ranked at their respective positions throughout the course of the season.

As a team, the Bears rank No. 8 in the NFL with an 80.3 PFN Offense Impact grade. Among the categories they rank within the top 10 among offenses include explosive rate, success rate, EPA per play, percentages of drives resulting in a score, and points scored per drive.

It's been a great year for Caleb Williams, who remains highly ranked in spite of his down game against the Buccaneers in Week 10. He ranks fifth in the NFL with a 68.7% completion percentage, and he ranks seventh with 0.19 EPA gained per dropback. His three-game absence due to a hamstring injury hurts his volume statistics, but from an efficiency perspective, he's been one of the top passers in the league.

Williams has been helped by a strong group of weapons and a talented offensive line. Including Williams, seven of the Bears' 11 offensive starters have graded as above-average starters so far this season. This includes Darnell Wright, who has the highest PFN OL Impact grade of any offensive tackle in the NFL, and Jonah Jackson, who continues to perform at an incredibly high level at right guard.

D'Andre Swift's nose for the end zone has made him a valuable piece of the Bears' offense, even though his efficiency numbers have dipped slightly without Kyle Monangai healthy. In the passing game, both Luther Burden III and Rome Odunze rank in the top 25, generally indicating they would both be the bonafide WR1s on a handful of teams around the league. Burden has graded higher than the likes of Justin Jefferson, Malik Nabers, and Davante Adams so far this year, cementing himself as one of the top young receivers in the NFL today.

Additionally, Kalif Raymond serves as the Bears' WR3 but has graded as a respectable WR2 by the NFL's standards, which is always encouraging to see from a contributor. Colston Loveland started off the year slowly but has improved his PFN TE Impact grade over three of his last four games.

The Bears' interior offensive line has seen slight regression this year, as Joe Thuney has fallen from his 91.1 PFN OL Impact grade in 2025, which led all guards that year, to an average starting grade of 75.8. Likewise, Garrett Bradbury has been a considerable drop-off from Drew Dalman, their Pro Bowl center from last year, who retired abruptly this offseason. However, the strong play of Jackson and Wright has kept Chicago's offensive line afloat, and Theo Benedet has been a pleasant surprise as an above-average starting left tackle this year.

In the grand scheme of things, the Bears have one of the most complete offenses in the NFL this season. They face a difficult schedule for the rest of the regular season, but their offense has shown that it can compete with the best of them.
