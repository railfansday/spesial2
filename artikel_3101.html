Schwab's Dividend ETF Keeps Hitting Records. I'd Still Buy It Now.

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Its yield has slipped under 3%. How did buyers do the last time a strong year left it there?

The Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD -0.97%) ended Friday, Oct. 30, at $35.78. That was the fund's 28th record close in 2026, and it traded near $36 as of this writing.

On price alone, the fund is up about 31% this year, while the S&P 500 has climbed about 11%. With dividends, the fund has returned about 34%, leaving it on track for its best calendar year since it started in 2011. Its strongest full year yet was 2013, at around 33%.

But for a fund of dividend-paying value stocks that most people buy for income, a higher price means a lower yield for anybody buying now. After all, the payout doesn't grow just because the share price does. And since mid-October alone, the price has climbed around 5%.

I still think the fund is worth buying. I'd just expect less than this year has brought.

The fund doesn't set its own dividend. It passes on what its 102 holdings pay quarterly, minus an expense ratio of 0.06%. Its last four payments, including the one made on Sept. 28, come to around $1.05 a share, according to Schwab.

At today's price, that's a yield of around 2.9%. It was around 3.1% in mid-October and about 3.8% at the end of 2025.

In dollars, $1,000 invested now buys around $29 a year of income at the current payout rate. The same $1,000 at the end of last year bought about $38.

But the fund has had a yield this low before.

The closest recent match is the end of 2021. The fund gained around 30% that year including dividends, finished the year just under its record close, and yielded about 2.8% at year-end -- a little less than today.

The next nine months were tough. By Sept. 30, 2022, the fund's price had fallen around 18% from where 2021 ended. And after a brief climb in early January 2022, it didn't close above that year-end mark again until July 2024. Counting dividends, the fund still lost around 3% in 2022.

The payout, though, kept growing. The fund's 2022 payments rose about 14% over 2021's. Still, a buyer who reinvested every dividend didn't get back to even for good until January 2024.

In all, that buyer is up around 58% with dividends reinvested, or about 10% a year. And with the yearly payout up about 41% since 2021, the income on that original purchase now works out to a yield of about 3.9%.

Of course, the 2021 buyer got help from fast payout growth. The fund's yearly payout climbed around 11% in 2021, then another 14% in 2022.

Now, the payout's growth is cooling. Yearly growth slowed from around 12% in 2024 to about 5% in 2025. And the last four payments are only about 2% higher than the four before them.

Put another way, a buyer today is counting on the payout picking up again, and it might not do so quickly.

The holdings' valuation doesn't look rich, at least. Schwab showed the fund's price-to-earnings ratio at around 18 at the end of September. For the Schwab U.S. Large-Cap ETF, which tracks the broader large-cap market, the number was around 25.

That same day, the dividend fund's trailing distribution yield was about 3.2%, versus around 1% for the large-cap fund. The dividend fund has gained about 10% since then while the S&P 500 has been nearly flat, so both gaps have probably shrunk.

Yes, a fund up around 31% over 10 months might lose a big chunk of that, like it did in 2022. But the 2021 buyer kept collecting a rising payout through that drop, and a buyer now should still collect one, even if it grows slower.

Is the fund still worth buying at a record? I think so. A yield just under 3% is less income than buyers got in January, but it's near what 2021's buyers paid, and the holdings seem to trade at a lower price-to-earnings ratio than the broad market. I'd buy shares at this price, maybe adding a bit at a time. I just wouldn't count on another year like 2026.
