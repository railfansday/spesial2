It's Official! Social Security's Trump Bump-Driven 2027 COLA Will Provide a Unique "Bonus" for Up to 35 Million Retirees.

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

This’ll be the first silver lining for tens of millions of retirees since 2023.

By Sean Williams – Nov 22, 2026 at 9:06AM EST

For many of Social Security's more than 55 million retired-worker beneficiaries, their monthly payout isn't optional. According to a quarter-century of annual Gallup surveys, Social Security income is needed by 80% to 90% of retirees to cover their expenses, in some capacity.

Given how foundational Social Security income is to the financial well-being of our nation's aging workforce, it should come as little surprise that the cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) reveal is one of the most anticipated announcements each year.

Social Security's COLA is the "raise" passed on to beneficiaries most years that offsets inflation (rising prices) and combats a loss of buying power. In 2027, traditional beneficiaries will receive one of the largest raises since 1993, thanks in large part to two of President Donald Trump's policies.

President Trump's policies have led to one of the largest Social Security raises in 35 years. Image source: Official White House Photo by Shealah Craighead, courtesy of the National Archives.

However, this Trump bump-driven 2027 COLA will be especially welcome for up to 35 million retirees, who are officially in line for a unique "bonus" that hasn't occurred since 2023.

The need to increase Social Security payouts to offset inflation has been a near-annual occurrence since the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W) became the program's inflation-measuring tool in 1975. Deflation (falling prices) has occurred only three times (2010, 2011, 2016), meaning beneficiaries have seen their monthly Social Security checks climb every other year.

Modest inflation is expected when the U.S. economy is expanding. In other words, businesses should have some degree of pricing power over their goods and services when the economy is firing on all cylinders.

But 2027's announced cost-of-living adjustment of 3.6% is well above the norm. It ties for the fifth-largest percentage increase since 1993, surpassed only by COLAs of 4.1% (2006), 5.8% (2009), 5.9% (2022), and 8.7% (2023), the latter of which was the largest jump since 1982.

Beneficiaries are receiving an outsize raise because the prevailing inflation rate is well above average -- and inflation is elevated due to two of President Trump's policies.

In each of the last two years, the president's tariff and trade policy has influenced consumer prices and, therefore, Social Security's annual COLA.

According to a new report by three New York Federal Reserve economists writing for Liberty Street Economics ("How Fast Do Tariffs Pass Through into Consumer Prices?), Trump's 2025 tariffs increased goods price inflation by an estimated 2.9 percentage points by February 2026. The authors predict that prices would have otherwise fallen without the tariffs.

Adding duties to imported goods directly raises their prices and can increase domestic manufacturing costs when tariffs are assigned to unfinished goods (e.g., steel).

The other Trump policy responsible for boosting the inflation rate and resulting in one of the largest Social Security raises in 35 years is the Iran war. The closure of the Strait of Hormuz stymied the daily movement of approximately 20 million barrels of petroleum liquids, sending gas and diesel prices soaring.

These inflationary pressures have led to quite the Trump bump for 2027.

While the program's nearly 72 million traditional beneficiaries -- retired workers, workers with disabilities, and survivors of deceased workers -- will all receive a 3.6% bump in their monthly payout come January 2027, a group of up to 35 million retirees currently enrolled in original Medicare are now officially set for a separate bonus.

Approximately 99% of workers won't pay a penny for Part A, but Parts B and D have standard monthly premiums that can exceed the standard amount for higher-income individuals. This year, the standard monthly Part B premium is $202.90, and it's usually deducted from a retiree's monthly Social Security payout.

While traditional Medicare is designed to protect our nation's aging workforce, Part B premiums have a terrible habit of offsetting Social Security's near-annual COLA.

For instance, Social Security payouts jumped by 3.2%, 2.5%, and 2.8% from 2024 to 2026. These increases were met by Part B premium increases of 5.9% in 2024, 5.9% in 2025, and 9.7% in 2026. Throughout much of the 21st century, Part B premiums have outpaced Social Security's annual COLA -- but things are about to change.

Last week, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services announced that the Part B premium would climb by $6.50 per month, or 3.2%, to $209.40 in 2027. This marks the first time since 2023 that Social Security's COLA will rise at a faster pace than Medicare's Part B premium.

More importantly, this dynamic signals that retirees enrolled in traditional Medicare and Social Security will retain more of next year's raise, rather than giving it right back due to a rapidly rising Part B premium. At long last, tens of millions of retired-worker beneficiaries have their silver lining.
