How we generate ID-style photos with AI (and where AI must not be used)

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

An ID photo looks like the simplest image task there is: one face, plain background, fixed size. In practice it is one of the least forgiving. A portrait for social media can be stylised freely. An ID photo has to be geometrically correct, visually clean and, above all, still the same person.

Our ID-photo tool at CoolMe AI turns a phone selfie into a 3×4, 4×6, 2×3 or 1×1 photo with a clean background and an outfit template (suit, office shirt, áo dài and so on). Keeping the two separate is the main design decision. Generative models should never decide where the face goes or how big it is.

Most bad ID photos are caused by bad input, not bad models. Rejecting early saves the user money and avoids "fixing" what should simply be retaken.

The thresholds are placeholders; you tune them on your own data. The important part is that the messages tell the user what to do, not just that something failed.

Once you have facial landmarks (any decent landmark model works), the crop is arithmetic: level the eyes, then place the face so that the head occupies a fixed share of the frame height.

Notice what this function refuses to do: it does not pad the image with generated content when the head is cut off. If the shoulders or the top of the head are missing, the honest answer is "retake the photo", not "let a model invent them".

Background clean-up, light balancing and outfit templates are where generative models earn their place. A selfie taken in a bedroom becomes a flat white or light-blue background; harsh shadows are softened; a T-shirt becomes the suit template the user picked.
