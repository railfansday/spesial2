3 Financial Stocks to Buy and Hold That Turned $1,000 Into More Than $5,000 Over the Past Decade, Counting Dividends

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

American Express, Visa, and JPMorgan Chase all outperformed the S&P 500.

Financial stocks can be shaky short-term investments, since they're sensitive to interest rates. But over the long term, blue chip financial stocks can generate market-beating gains as the global economy expands and they compound their reinvested dividends.

Over the past decade, three of those well-known stocks -- American Express (AXP -0.48%), Visa (V +0.16%), and JPMorgan Chase (JPM -2.31%) -- turned $1,000 into more than $5,000 with reinvested dividends. That same $1,000 investment in Vanguard's S&P 500 ETF (VOO -1.89%) would be worth just over $4,000 today. Let's see why those three financial stocks beat the market -- and if they're still worth buying in this turbulent market.

A $1,000 investment in American Express ten years ago, with reinvested dividends, would be worth $5,040 today. From 2015 to 2025, its EPS grew at an 11.8% CAGR, even as the global economy endured the pandemic, interest rate shocks, inflation, and other macro headwinds.

American Express thrived because it operates a more balanced business model than other credit card companies and banks. Unlike Visa and Mastercard (MA +0.08%), which don't issue any cards, American Express mainly issues its own cards and backs them with its own bank.

American Express' closed-loop system forces it to expand more slowly than Visa and Mastercard's open-loop network of banking partners. However, it also forces the company to vet its customers more carefully and avoid subprime borrowers. Those tighter standards reinforce its reputation as a more selective card for higher-income consumers.

Rising interest rates can throttle its card transaction fees, but they boost its banking segment's net interest income. When interest rates fall, its rising card fees can offset its declining net interest income. That balance makes it more resilient than many other financial stocks, and it still looks like a bargain at 16 times forward earnings.

A $1,000 investment in Visa would have grown to $5,880 over the past decade. From fiscal 2016 to fiscal 2026 (which ended this September), its EPS grew at a 17.4% CAGR.

Visa's strength is its scale. By partnering only with banks and other financial institutions that issue their own cards, Visa can expand rapidly without shouldering any risk. It merely takes a cut of each transaction that flows through its payment network.

Merchants often protest those 2%-3% "swipe fees". But most of them will continue to accept Visa's market-dominating cards rather than risk losing a large share of their potential customers. Visa also continues to upgrade its financial rails with stablecoin settlements and AI agents, and it's already tightly tethered to digital wallets and other mobile payment apps.

Visa will remain a popular target for merchant groups and antitrust regulators, but it will continue to grow as its cards penetrate more cash-based markets. It's still reasonably valued at 28 times forward earnings, and it should continue to rise as the macro environment improves.

Lastly, a $1,000 investment in JPMorgan ten years ago would be worth $5,590 today. From 2015 to 2025, its EPS grew at a 12.8% CAGR.

As the largest U.S. bank by total assets and market capitalization, JPMorgan has the scale, liquidity, and moat to survive deep recessions. Over the past decade, it expanded its banking and payments businesses, maintained strong returns on its equity investments, streamlined its business to improve its operating margins, and bought back a quarter of its outstanding shares.

In the near term, inflation will likely drive up JPMorgan's labor and technology costs. It would also struggle if higher interest rates trigger another credit crunch. But over the long term, its investment banking and wealth management arms should continue to expand. It also authorized a new $50 billion buyback plan (5% of its market cap) earlier this year. JPMorgan isn't immune to a recession, but it still looks undervalued at 14 times forward earnings.
