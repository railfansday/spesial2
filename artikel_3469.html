5 of the Most Popular TV Shows Right Now, According to Rotten Tomatoes (Nov. 13-15)

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Ben Brewster is a filmmaker, critic, screenwriter, and actor whose work sits at the intersection of film culture, craft analysis, and storytelling.

He is a movie list contributing writer at Collider and a contributing writer for High on Films, where he covers contemporary cinema, independent film, and festival releases with an eye toward narrative structure, performance, and visual language.

Ben is an active film festival participant, having worked with Newport Film, the Nantucket Film Festival and as a screener for the Austin, Boston & Woodstock film festivals.  He brings to that work a programming instinct shaped by years of close engagement with independent and documentary cinema. This comes with a deep investment in the kinds of films that rarely find their way into the mainstream conversation without someone willing to advocate for them.

His background spans documentary and narrative filmmaking, film marketing, and production, including work with award-winning directors. This as well as developing his own indie production company, Lilac Creative Studio, through which he develops and produces original work across fiction and nonfiction forms.  His debut documentary, Diamond in the Rust, is an examination of economic redevelopment and displacement in the Queens area of New York City. The film was recently accepted to the Unisphere Film Festival, the Defense of Democracy Film Festival, and the Urbanite Arts & Film Festival. This would mark his first festival recognition as a director.  In addition to his writing and programming work, Ben hosts Beneath the Bloom, a film podcast exploring the work behind the scenes in cinema, and the struggles one can face to make a movie become a reality. The show reflects the same sensibility that runs through all of his work. A conviction that film is not merely entertainment but one of the most powerful tools human beings have for understanding each other.

Across mediums, his work is driven by a deep belief in film as a communal, transformative art form.

The mix covers spy comedy, serial killers, android angst, and a town nobody can leave. If you want something you can finish in a weekend, Slow Horses Season 6 runs six episodes, and VisionQuest runs eight, and both are complete. Thanksgiving is two weeks away, and a lot of viewers are already lining up something to watch around the holiday. Nocturne wraps on Christmas Day and Dexter: Resurrection closes on January 1. Both keep weekly episodes coming through the holidays. Popularity lists tend to reward whatever people are talking about this week. It's how a new Apple thriller and a horror series in a tight Netflix window can beat a perfect-scoring and critically renowned spy drama.

Critics are kind to almost everything here, with scores running from about 85% up to 100 for Slow Horses season six. Audience scores tell a different story, particularly for Nocturne at 58% after two episodes and From Season 4 at 66%. From has seasons one through three on Netflix, with Season 4 available on MGM+. The final season is filming and set for a 2027 release. Marvel fans also have the impending Avengers: Doomsday on December 18, which is plenty of reason to fly through the last chapter of the WandaVision saga. We keep spoilers light, though a few plot points come up below.

Slow Horses is the show about spies nobody wants, and season six deepens storylines and pushes the crew further into real danger. Gary Oldman returns as Jackson Lamb, the slovenly, brilliant boss of Slough House, where disgraced MI5 agents are sent to be forgotten. The first episode is titled "Circle of Life," and it picks up with River Cartwright stuck exactly where season five left him and Olivia Cooke's Sid Baker back in the mix. Mick Herron's novels Joe Country and Slough House are adapted together this time, the first season of which is to do so. Kristin Scott's Diana Taverner has her own scheme that sets off a fatally high-stakes game of retaliation and revenge and forces the team out on the run.

Critics have been almost unanimous. Season 6 opened at 100% on Rotten Tomatoes, the third season of the series to reach that mark after seasons two and four. Forbes pointed out that it now has the best score of any Apple series with five or more seasons, edging past Trying. ScreenRant said Season 6 delivers some of the most compelling drama and exciting subversions the series has produced. Jack Lowden is also reportedly among the actors linked to the final James Bond screen tests, which has thrust the show into the cultural zeitgeist even further. Apple has already announced a seventh season.

Dexter Morgan is older, and this season seems the most self-aware of that fact. Michael C. Hall's antihero is dealing with the psychological challenges of middle age while two very different killers prowl New York City. One is Brian Cox's Don Framt, better known as the New York Ripper, an infamous killer introduced towards the end of season one. Dan Stevens plays Owen Stark, the Five Borough Killer, while Gabriel Luna guest stars as the Sleepy-Eyed Stranger, and Krysten Ritter reappears as Mia LaPierre, better known as Lady Vengeance. Uma Thurman is back as Charley, and Jack Alcott returns as Harrison.

Season 2 premiered on October 30 on the Paramount+ Premium plan, with a third episode releasing this Friday. New episodes also air on Showtime on Sundays for anyone who prefers cable. The franchise turns 20 this year, leaning into the idea of an older and grizzled Dexter. Rotten Tomatoes had the season at 86% on seven reviews as of November 3. That's a slight step down from season one, but it runs a full ten episodes by the new year, so it has time to bring that back up.

Before there was Madden, Rudy runs through the tunnel, Boobie Miles blows out a knee, and a blimp circles the Super Bowl.

Showrunner Terry Matalas, who ran the third season of Star Trek: Picard, brings a very different tone to Marvel's android. VisionQuest picks up with a rebooted Vision on the run from the people who tried to weaponize him and a large bounty on his head. The show follows the first two entries, WandaVision and Agatha All Along. Paul Bettany returns for the eight-episode limited series, and James Spader's Ultron, who appears in human form, becomes his closest and most dangerous companion. Spader has described their dynamic as a strange mix of father, son, therapist, and psychopath.

The series arrived in double drops, the last of which landed on Disney+ on November 4. Rotten Tomatoes opened it at 87%, and it has since settled in the mid-80s, ahead of Agatha All Along at 84% and behind WandaVision's 92%. Critics singled out Bettany and Spader, with one reviewer calling their chemistry the highlight of the series, which is hard to argue with. Comic Book Movie called it one of the best stories the MCU has told, while Rolling Stone said it lacks a clear sense of vision. Even some positive reviews flagged an uneven second half and difficulty balancing storylines. But with the impending Avengers: Doomsday release, the finale has gotten extra attention from Marvel fans.

Nocturne starts with an unsettling idea. A victim of the notorious serial killer Jurek Walter turns up alive, and a retired homicide detective named Jonah Lynn is pulled back into the case. Liev Schreiber plays Lynn, who works and lives in a small western Pennsylvania town and is accompanied by FBI agent Saga Bauer (Zazie Beetz). Stephen Graham is the antagonist, Walter, a cunning and manipulative killer who is held at a psychiatric hospital when the story begins. Episode titles like "Lazarus, Come Forth!" can tip off audiences to its biblical and philosophical tonal weight.

Graham has been receiving the most praise from critics, and the Tomatometer has landed in the mid-80s for Nocturne's inaugural season so far. Several reviewers also note that Schrieber and Beetz bring a tenderness that balances out the darker thematic narrative. Others remarked that the series relied on well-worn serial killer stereotypes, and audience scores have been a step-down at 58% so far through two episodes. Even so, Nocturne reached No. 1 on Apple TV's global chart, passing Ted Lasso, Slow Horses, and Silo handily. Tom Hardy was originally attached before Graham took over. New episodes hit every Friday until Christmas Day.

From has been around for a while now, but it is finally getting the mass audience it had desired thanks to Netflix. Seasons one through three hit the streamer on October 19 on a limited-time license, and within 24 hours the show had reached second place on Netflix's U.S. chart. It also reportedly led Nielsen's streaming chart, with the largest weekly total of any acquired series this year. Harold Perrineau, who played Michael Dawson on Lost, leads as Boyd Stevens, the self-appointed sheriff of a town that traps everyone who drives in. Each night, creatures come out of the surrounding forest, and the residents spend their days arguing over the best way to survive. Season 4 brings a new arrival named Sophia and gives the Man in Yellow a more active and prominent role.

Creator John Griffin calls the show a character drama dressed up as a horror show, and the description fits well. Season 4 is the one that played on MGM+ from late April through June, and the service calls From the most-watched series in their history. It gives longtime fans answers they have been waiting for while Boyd and the rest of the town keep pushing for a way out. RT lists season four at 96%, the highest of the four seasons. However, the audience score opened at 92% and has since slid to 66%, which suggests some fans wanted more terror and fewer conversations. The final season is filming in Halifax and is headed for 2027, with no official premiere date.
