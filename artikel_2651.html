Steelers players express support for Mike McCarthy after winning first game since Aaron Rodgers divorce

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

It can't be denied the Pittsburgh Steelers are a different team after one of the most eventful weeks of the season, but that apparently isn't all bad.

A number of Steelers players expressed their support for head coach Mike McCarthy in the aftermath of a 27-5 win over the Denver Broncos on Black Friday, the team's first game since a very public divorce with starting quarterback Aaron Rodgers.

The saga saw Rodgers make several claims while appearing on "The Pat McAfee Show" that painted McCarthy as disconnected at best and dangerously incompetent at worst after the Steelers benched him, and shortly before they released him. It's a situation with a little precedent in the NFL, and it led to all eyes on McCarthy and the Steelers in Week 12.

Behind the scenes, there was plenty of talking. James Palmer of NFL Network reported before the game that the team's leadership council — a group of veteran players who regularly meet with McCarthy — met on Wednesday and held an "airing of grievances" to address some of the stuff Rodgers was talking about. The meeting reportedly left McCarthy "humbled," but also allowed him to explain to the players why he handled everything the way he did.

The meeting apparently left the team on firmer footing, with Steelers cornerback Jalen Ramsey saying the team expressed its support to McCarthy:

"For the past couple days, we as players, we were like, 'Coach, you good.' Let us turn this up, let us get in our zone."

Quarterback Mason Rudolph, Rodgers' replacement under center, also said the players were behind McCarthy:

"I think the leadership council rallied around coach McCarthy. I think in a weird way, the adversity probably brought us closer together. We've just got to keep carrying that in week to week."

McCarthy, meanwhile, seemed happy with how the team responded:

"Everything that was discussed was healthy. Lot of things in there, but what came out of it was urgency. I thought our leaders did a tremendous job of bringing the urgency to their rooms and it definitely showed up."

One particular point of criticism from Rodgers was that McCarthy had allegedly spent little time engaging with the Pittsburgh defense, despite the quarterback advising that he should. McCarthy's background is almost purely on the offense and he's well known to prioritize that side of the ball while leaving the rest to his defensive coordinator.

McCarthy actually admitted Rodgers was right about his lack of focus on the defense:

"I have not spent enough time with the defense. That's something as far as job responsibility, job description, these are things that will be looked at in the offseason, but it's a point where in our year, we're making adjustments. Definitely conscientious of it."

Steelers defensive end T.J. Watt indicated the players spoke to McCarthy about that, and the results were encouraging:

"Mike just admitted that he has got to spend some more time with the defense. We've had a lot of really good conversations, especially recently, and look forward to continuing to build off of what you saw tonight."

Mike McCarthy had a tough week, but got a win.

The Steelers veterans also had plenty of praise for Rudolph, whose experience with the team goes back to 2018. Watt didn't seem surprised he was ready for the game:

"Mason Rudolph is a true professional. He's been a true professional since I've known him. A guy that's trained like the starter no matter how long he's been here, whether he's the backup or the starter. We have extreme belief in him and he's a great locker room glue guy."

Rudolph didn't exactly light up the scoreboard against Denver, but he was at least efficient, going 9-for-15 for 142 yards, 1 touchdown and 0 interceptions. It was a run-heavy game script with the Steelers leading 14-0 after the first quarter and by multiple scores for the rest of the game.

Still, Steelers defensive tackle Cameron Heyward noticed Rudolph doing what was needed:

"He's ready for all those moments. He understand that with everything that went on, he couldn't have a drop-off. I'm just appreciative to have a guy who when s*** hits the fan, he's able to say, 'Alright, I've gotta step up.'"

It was a strong performance at least compared to, well, the standard Rodgers set during the season. NFL Network's Michael Florio noted his 0.19 EPA per dropback, an advanced stat measuring offensive efficiency, was the second-best mark by a Steelers quarterback all season. Rodgers exceeded that level only once all season, in Week 3 against the Cincinnati Bengals.

McCarthy did at one point go out of his way to note "I'm the offensive play-caller," a notable statement given that Rodgers reportedly gave the Steelers an ultimatum that the team give him offensive play-calling duties or release him.

However, he seemed far more interested in moving on with the team as constructed, and said he was grateful for the response to a turbulent week:

"There's a lot going on. Anytime you're in a situation where time is taken away from winning and your team responds, it is extremely important and gratifying. The reality of this game — and it will always be as far as my approach — this is a players' game. We have a very important role in this sport, but you have to make sure you keep it about the players. We still have work to do from my position and we'll continue to do that. We've just got to keep building off things that we've done well.

"We got the win today. It was definitely a hard travel week for us as a football team. I'm proud of our players. I'm proud of our coaching staff. I'm proud of our support staff and I really appreciate all the other support."

Meanwhile, Rodgers, passed along his own thoughts via Al Michaels during the game.
