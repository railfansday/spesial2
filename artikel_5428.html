IMBs lose $30 per loan in Q3 2026 as higher rates reverse historic trend

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

IMBs and bank mortgage units reported a $30 per-loan pretax production loss in Q3 2026, reversing a $973 profit in Q2, MBA said. Servicing income rose to $195 per loan serviced, but fewer firms were profitable overall.  AI Summary

Independent mortgage banks (IMBs) and mortgage subsidiaries of chartered banks reported an average pretax net production loss of $30 per loan in the third quarter of 2026, down from a profit of $973 per loan in the second quarter, according to the Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA).

The results mark the first quarterly production loss since the fourth quarter of 2023 and the lowest third-quarter performance since 2023, the MBA said Tuesday in its latest Quarterly Mortgage Bankers Performance Report.

“Average pre-tax net production income reached its lowest level for a third quarter since 2023. The results are particularly disappointing given that the third quarter has historically been the strongest quarter of the year,” said Marina Walsh, MBA’s vice president and head of industry analysis.

Rising mortgage rates over the summer contributed to lower production volumes and higher costs. Average first-mortgage loan balances also declined for a second consecutive quarter, contributing to an approximately $325-per-loan drop in production revenue, Walsh said.

“Although production volume dropped, the number of production employees per firm was relatively flat compared to the previous quarter and was higher compared to last year’s third quarter. Staff reductions and other cost-cutting measures may accelerate the rest of this year and in the first quarter of 2027,” she said.

Average production volume fell to $632 million per company in the third quarter, down from $689 million in the second quarter and $634 million a year earlier. Companies originated an average of 1,848 loans, compared with 1,958 in the previous quarter and 1,866 in the third quarter of 2025.

Production revenue declined to $11,585 per loan, down from $11,909 in the second quarter. Meanwhile, production expenses increased to $11,615 per loan, up from $10,936.

On a basis-point measurement, lenders recorded an average pretax production loss of 4 basis points in the third quarter, compared with a profit of 25 bps in the second quarter. The long-term quarterly average production profit, dating to the third quarter of 2008, is 38 basis points.

Mortgage servicing provided some relief. Net servicing financial income more than doubled to $195 per loan serviced in the third quarter, up from $80 in the previous quarter. Slower prepayments and higher mortgage servicing rights valuations contributed to the increase.

Including both production and servicing operations, 59% of mortgage companies reported a pretax net financial profit in the third quarter, down from 85% in the second quarter. Without servicing operations, only 44% of companies would have been profitable.

The purchase share of first-mortgage originations by dollar volume was 83% among the companies surveyed, compared with an estimated 73% for the broader mortgage industry.

The average first-mortgage loan balance declined slightly to $386,133, from $386,359 in the second quarter. The average balance across all mortgage products, including second mortgages and home equity lines of credit, fell to $364,840 from $368,357.

The MBA‘s report included production data from 335 companies. Independent mortgage companies accounted for 82% of the firms reporting data, while the remaining 18% were subsidiaries and other nondepository institutions.
