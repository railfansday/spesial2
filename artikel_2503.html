How Glenn Schumann must juggle Georgia, FSU football demands

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Florida State football has its next head coach. Now comes the difficult part for newly hired Glenn Schumann: building FSU's future while helping Georgia pursue another national championship.

Schumann is expected to remain in his role as Georgia's defensive coordinator through the Bulldogs' postseason run, creating a unique challenge as he juggles recruiting, roster management and staff-building in Tallahassee while preparing for some of college football's biggest games.

While FSU officials appear comfortable with that arrangement, the timing of the hire presents several hurdles as Schumann transitions into his new role.

Conference championship weekend: Dec. 4-5

College Football Playoff begins: Dec. 18-19

Perhaps Schumann's most immediate challenge will be recruiting.

With the early signing period approaching, he must begin establishing relationships with FSU commitments and targets while maintaining responsibilities at Georgia. Recruiting has become a year-round endeavor, and Schumann will be tasked with laying the foundation for the Seminoles' 2027 class while also getting a head start on future cycles.

One advantage is his familiarity with North Florida and South Georgia areas he has recruited extensively during his time at Georgia. One of his most notable recruiting victories was Tallahassee native Raylen Wilson, a former five-star linebacker from Lincoln High School who developed into a key contributor for the Bulldogs.

The bigger question is whether Schumann can persuade any prospects with Georgia ties to consider FSU. Flipping commitments from one of the nation's premier programs is never easy, especially for recruits who have spent months or years envisioning themselves in Athens.

Complicating matters further is the calendar. The early signing period and conference championship weekend arrive almost simultaneously, forcing Schumann to divide attention between Georgia's championship aspirations and FSU's recruiting needs.

Recruiting is only part of the equation. The transfer portal may ultimately be the key to accelerating FSU's rebuild.

The winter transfer portal window opens shortly after the College Football Playoff begins, creating another scheduling challenge for Schumann. As postseason games intensify, roster construction for the Seminoles will also be entering a critical phase.

That places added importance on communication between Schumann and FSU general manager John Garrett. Together, they will need to evaluate portal options, manage scholarship numbers and identify impact players who can address immediate needs.

Quarterback is likely to be among the program's top priorities. Given the late timing of Schumann's arrival and the realities of modern recruiting, the portal could provide the fastest path to improving the roster for 2027.

The biggest test for FSU may simply be patience.

If Georgia advances deep into the College Football Playoff, Schumann's attention will remain tied to the Bulldogs through the end of the postseason. Meanwhile, FSU's offseason program will already be underway with early enrollees and new arrivals getting acclimated to campus.

A lengthy playoff run would delay Schumann's full-time presence in Tallahassee, though it would also reinforce one of the qualities that made him an attractive hire: experience operating at the sport's highest level.

The challenges awaiting Schumann are significant, but they are largely a byproduct of success. FSU hired a coach who is still competing for championships, not one watching from home. The balancing act between Georgia's postseason run and FSU's rebuilding efforts will require careful coordination, particularly in recruiting and the transfer portal.

How effectively Schumann manages those responsibilities over the next two months could shape the Seminoles' trajectory long before he coaches his first game in garnet and gold.
