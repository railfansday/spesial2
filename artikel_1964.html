Etihad's chances of success in legal action revealed amid Man City update - Ex-CEO

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Manchester City's main sponsors Etihad only have a "remote chance" of success if they decide to take legal action against the Premier League.

That is according to former Everton chief executive and ex-Premier League board member Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the league have no reason for concern over the issue.

Etihad and Etisalat, two of City's biggest sponsors, have threatened to sue the Premier League over their guilty verdict on the club's financial charges.

However, Wyness feels that it is likely just a "tactic" from the pair to try and heap the pressure on the Premier League to issue a more lenient punishment to City.

Premier League clubs have voted to cover the cost of legal defence for the Premier League's five directors, should Etihad end up taking them to court.

City and Nottingham Forest were the only two clubs to vote against that notion, with the other 18 all in favour of trying to protect the league's directors.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that Etihad's threat is an attempt to make it as difficult as possible for the Premier League to punish City.

The threat of legal action would potentially "increase the cost" of the league issuing a sanction, as they could potentially then face a compensation payment to the club's sponsors.

However, speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained how Etihad's chances of success are hugely limited.

"I think there's a remote chance of success. I don't think it's a great chance at all, but this is all about increasing the cost and making the battle personal," he said.

"That's what we're seeing right now. And as costs go up across the whole legal issues, in fact, I'm speaking on a panel next Thursday about the growing cost of legal defence right now.

"That's just a genuine tactic that we're seeing from Etihad, and the ownership group at City, to try and make the pain even harder.

"That's what this is all about. So I don't think there's going to be a great chance of success, but nevertheless, it's going to be very, very expensive to defend."

Football Insider revealed last week that Man City are "confident" of sealing an agreement over a new contract for Matheus Nunes sooner rather than later.

Sources say that there is a "willingness" from all parties to get the deal done, with talks already underway for Nunes to stay at the Etihad Stadium beyond 2028.

The Portuguese remains happy in Manchester, and the club continue to value him and his versatility within the squad.

Nunes has become adept in the right-back role in recent years, and is a valuable part of City's project under Enzo Maresca.
