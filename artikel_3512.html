Unbelievable Is Netflix's 8-Part True Crime Thriller With 98% On Rotten Tomatoes

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Netflix's acclaimed 8-part true crime thriller follows two detectives pursuing a serial attacker while a young survivor struggles to be believed. Released back in 2019, this limited series remains one of the most critically acclaimed Netflix shows ever produced, delivering top-tier performances with masterful storytelling that brings a shocking true crime story to life with great execution and impact. Starring Kaitlyn Dever, Toni Collette, and Merritt Wever in some of their career-best roles, this is one of the best weekend binges on Netflix.

Unbelievable is one of the best limited series on any streaming platform released over the last seven years, as evidenced by its near-perfect 98% Rotten Tomatoes score and 4 Emmy nominations, including Outstanding Limited Series, Writing, and Casting. The 8-episode crime thriller is an ideal weekend binge with a total runtime of 6 hours and 25 minutes that can be viewed from start to finish in just a few sittings. Unbelievable delivers a masterclass in police detective work through an assault case investigation, making it a rewarding watch.

Similar to Netflix's celebrated miniseries Adolescence, Unbelievable can be a tough series to watch but is worth every single minute. Dever stars as Marie Adler, a young woman who reported being sexually assaulted by an intruder in her home in Lynnwood, Washington. Despite her claims to law enforcement, police discounted her allegations by discrediting her account as inconsistent and convinced her to withdraw her report, slapping her with a false police report charge. Marie faced public backlash from these events, which resulted in her losing trust in others even though her report was entirely true. Marie not only experienced trauma from the assault, but psychological trauma as well.

The most appalling aspect of Marie Adler's true crime case is that the justice system penalized her for reporting a legitimate crime, which represents systemic institutional corruption inflicted by those meant to protect the public. Following further investigation in 2015, seven years after the initial incident, Adler was eventually awarded a $150,000 settlement by the city of Lynnwood, Washington.

A third-party investigation determined that Marie was coerced by law enforcement personnel into saying that she lied about the assault in her report. Two Colorado-based detectives discovered a connection between Marie's report and Marc Patrick O'Leary, a serial rapist, who was later convicted on multiple assault charges. Netflix's Unbelievable powerfully chronicles this chain of events through Marie's psychological lens.

Collette and Wever portray two of the key Colorado detectives who investigated O'Leary and eventually provided justice for Marie. Collette plays Detective Grace Rasmussen, inspired by Edna Hendershot of Colorado's Westminster Police Department, while Wever plays Detective Karen Duvall, based on Stacy Galbraith of Colorado's Golden Police Department.

Their outstanding performances exemplify the determination of both real-life detectives, and the narrative is rooted in the actual police work, such as reviewing forensic and surveillance evidence used to put O'Leary behind bars. O'Leary pleaded guilty to 30 counts, including sexual assault and rape, and received a 327.5-year sentence in Colorado and a 68.5-year sentence in Washington. He will spend the rest of his life in prison at the Sterling Correctional Facility in Sterling, Colorado​​​​​​.

Julia Garner leads Netflix's new 8-part true-crime drama about an $8 billion crypto scandal, an explosive romance, and the rise and fall of an empire.

While other exceptional detective series on Netflix, such as Mindhunter, focus intently on the psychology of the criminals, Unbelievable is far more interested in examining the psychological toll on the survivors, specifically Marie Adler. Collette and Wever deliver contrasting investigative styles through their performances, which make them one of streaming's best detective duos, in the same league as Woody Harrelson's Marty Hart and Matthew McConaughey's Rust Cohle in True Detective season 1.

Unbelievable is also comparable to Hulu's hit detective series, Furious, and HBO's crime masterpiece, The Night Of, both of which earned critical acclaim for their portrayal of moral ambiguity and institutional failures within law enforcement and the court system rather than delivering justice to its fullest potential. To witness a masterclass in detective work on Netflix, press play on Unbelievable this weekend.
