Massachusetts Water Treatment Plant Durability Issues with Penetron Technology

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Completed in November 2026, the $34 million water treatment plant in Sharon, Massachusetts will help the town meet state and federal water quality guidelines and future water demands. PENETRON ADMIX SB, an NSF-61-certified crystalline waterproofing admixture, was specified to waterproof all concrete structures exposed to high groundwater levels and hydrostatic pressure of treated drinking water.

ORLANDO, Fla., Nov. 24, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- A typical New England town of around 20,000, Sharon is located about 17 miles (27-km) south of Boston. The new municipal water treatment plant will help the town reliably meet state and federal water quality guidelines and future water demand from existing sources. The new facility also addresses treatment of polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS),* as well as manganese and iron levels.

"The design and construction of concrete water tanks for the new water treatment plant in Sharon, MA focuses on waterproofing and leak prevention," explains Richard Farmer, Eastern Region Sales Manager for Penetron USA. "There's not only concrete's normal porosity and micro-cracks that can cause trouble, but construction joints also become vulnerable if not sealed properly, and the freezeâthaw cycles common in New England can open cracks and create leaks."

The plant's concrete treatment and storage tanks are steel reinforced for strength, but steel embedded in concrete can become vulnerable if moisture and oxygen penetrate into the matrix. Chloride ions from groundwater can break down the passive layer around steel and accelerate corrosion. The resulting rust of the rebar expands, causing cracking, spalling, and loss of structural capacity.

The town's new water treatment facility was built on the site of well station #4, a primary water source for the plant. The new facility also treats water from wells #2, 3 and 4. Wells #2 and 3 require treatment for manganese, iron and PFAS while well #4 requires treatment for PFAS levels.

Dauphinais Concrete, the ready-mix concrete supplier, added PENETRON ADMIX SB, a crystalline waterproofing admixture, to the concrete mix for the walls and foundation slabs of all treatment and holding tanks of the Sharon water treatment plant. The admixture is completely non-toxic, and NSF 61-certified for potable water applications.

PENETRON ADMIX SB-treated concrete is impermeable, stopping water and chemicals from entering the concrete, even under the constant hydrostatic pressure encountered in a water treatment plant. Its ability to permanently self-heal any future microcracks mitigates deterioration from occurring over the service life of the concrete.

"Penetron has an excellent working relationship with Dauphinais Concrete," adds Richard Farmer. "They also appreciate our soluble bag packaging for easy dosage and the green tracer to confirm PENETRON ADMIX SB is in the mix."

* Chemical compounds commonly known as PFAs (Polyfluoroalkyl substances) are widely used in stain- and water-resistant fabrics and carpeting, cleaning products, and non-stick cookware. Recent research found there are potential health risks with any level of PFAS exposure, including liver damage, thyroid disease, decreased fertility, high cholesterol, obesity, hormone suppression and cancer. Currently, the amount of PFAS allowed in drinking water is regulated not to exceed 20PPT (parts per trillion).
