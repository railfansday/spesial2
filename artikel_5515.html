Chapter Renovation Reveals How Delays Can Turn Lower Contractor Bids into Higher Total Costs

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The company's 38-week average completion time highlights how shorter schedules can cut the cost of waiting

NEW YORK, Nov. 3, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Chapter Renovation, a full-service, technology-driven design-build firm, announced today how faster renovation schedules can help homeowners save money by reducing the time they spend paying for ongoing property expenses while the home remains unfinished. The analysis highlights the importance of weighing proposed completion dates alongside contractor bids since a project with a lower initial quote can ultimately be more expensive if it takes longer to finish.

To help homeowners account for those added expenses when comparing bids, Chapter Renovation's Cost of Delay Calculator estimates the financial impact of a longer schedule. Users can adjust monthly carrying costs and the number of additional months beyond a 38-week plan to see how delays could offset an initially lower price. The financial analysis builds on Chapter Renovation's 38-week average for completed full-scope luxury renovations, compared with an approximate 77-week industry average identified through its analysis of publicly available research and market data. Results may vary. Timelines depend on scope, approvals, material selections, and client changes.

"The cheapest quote from a contractor can often become the most expensive choice if the job takes months longer than anticipated," said Eran Polack, CEO and Founder of Chapter Renovation. "Homeowners understandably focus on what the contractor will charge, but ongoing housing and carrying costs like temporary accommodations, storage, and overlapping financing continue even when work has stalled. A lower bid only saves you money if the savings hold up once the renovation is finished."

The Cost of Delay Calculator illustrates the financial impact through a Manhattan scenario with estimated monthly costs of $47,000. At that amount, the calculator estimates that a five-month delay would total $235,000 in estimated costs. For owners planning to rent out a renovated property, earlier completion can also allow rental income to begin sooner.

"An idle week not in your home still has a price tag, even if nobody sends an invoice for it," said Eli Moyal, Founder and COO of Chapter Renovation. "The question to ask a contractor is what will keep the next phase ready to start. If materials are ordered only after approvals arrive, or trades are scheduled only after the previous crew leaves, the homeowner can end up paying for those gaps."
