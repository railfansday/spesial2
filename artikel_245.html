Jensen Huang Apologizes to Nvidia Employees in First All-Hands Since His Return

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologized to employees on Tuesday for violating the company’s code of conduct. During his first all-hands since returning to his role as CEO, Huang said he made some “very serious personal mistakes” and that’s why he submitted his resignation.

“This is going to be the hardest company meeting I’ve ever done, and the second hardest meeting I have ever done,” Huang said, according to remarks reviewed by WIRED. “The hardest meeting I’ve ever done was with my wife.”

Huang told staffers that he has been going to therapy “every day for an hour and a half” and that it’s “been helping me a lot.” He added that he “made some very serious personal mistakes. I hurt my family, I hurt my wife.”

The CEO emphasized that Nvidia’s code of conduct, much of which he said he wrote himself, still governs how employees are expected to treat one another and is intended to “create an environment that’s safe for people.”

Huang told employees that violations of the code would not be tolerated. But that “does not mean, the moment you violate a code of conduct, we walk you out the door,” he said. “Facts, circumstances, a lot of assessments” would factor in.

Huang also pushed back against the notion that he had received preferential treatment because of his status in Silicon Valley. “There are rumors on the internet that apparently I’m a tech bro, and therefore I’m back,” Huang said. He rejected the suggestion that he was above the law or Nvidia’s code of conduct.

Later in the meeting, Huang addressed criticism of his notoriously intense management style, acknowledging that his behavior had at times negatively affected employees. While Huang maintained that the intensity he brings to his job elevates those around him, he conceded that he sometimes takes things too far.

“It disempowers them, and that’s not right,” Huang said. The Nvidia CEO said he needed to figure out how to maintain his level of dedication to the company without hurting the people who work there.

“I need to hold myself to a higher standard at this time,” Huang said. He said that he would undergo a 360-degree performance review, soliciting feedback as often as monthly from people who work with him. The CEO added that he would be the first and only person at Nvidia to undergo such a review and that he did not want to institute the practice companywide.

Nvidia board members accepted Huang’s resignation earlier this month, citing concerns about his personal behavior and lack of candor following news reports about extramarital affairs, including one with a woman accused of having ties to Chinese intelligence services. The very same board reinstated him on Monday, just 11 days later, after receiving fresh assurances from Huang and learning from the Trump administration that he was not under federal scrutiny.

While some Nvidia employees celebrated Huang’s return, others remain concerned about his conduct and the board’s decision to reinstate him. Huang and Nvidia also continue to face legal and regulatory scrutiny. The board is continuing an investigation into whether he was the target of foreign intelligence. US lawmakers are expecting him to testify before Congress next week. Lawsuits and other probes are ongoing into Nvidia’s disclosures about Huang’s behavior and its compliance with US export controls on chips.

During the all-hands meeting, Huang also sought to reassure women at Nvidia, describing himself as “one of our greatest advocates for creating an environment for women” and touting the company’s flexible work policies, which he said allow employees to simultaneously build their families and careers.

“If I did anything that put that in jeopardy, I cannot tell you how sad I am, and I’ll do everything I can to fix it,” Huang said.

The CEO also argued that his brief absence had demonstrated Nvidia’s ability to operate without him. “I promise you, whether I’m here or not, this company is wired up to execute, execute, and execute,” Huang said.
