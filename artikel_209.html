Nvidia’s Jensen Huang resigns, chipmaker names CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Jensen Huang has resigned from his position as president and chief executive of Nvidia, the company said on Thursday, just a few days after he admitted to a romantic affair with an alleged Chinese spy, sparking a U.S. national security crisis.

In a regulatory filing, Nvidia said its board accepted Huang’s resignation on Wednesday.

Nvidia said its current chief financial officer and executive vice president, Collete Kress, will take over as interim CEO until the company finds a permanent successor. Kress will continue to serve as the company’s CFO.

Nvidia’s shares were down nearly 12% in early morning trading on Thursday. Since Friday, the company’s stock has fallen by as much as 19%, resulting in a market cap loss of more than $855 billion.

Huang founded Nvidia in 1993 and has led it as CEO ever since. Under his leadership, Nvidia played a crucial role in developing the the graphics processing unit (GPU), a specialized chip that does graphics processing for PCs, as we know it today. The chips were not an immediate hit, and the early days of Nvidia were characterized by struggle and layoffs, but eventually, it struck on a GPU design that performed so well, it became the standard for graphics processing.

These chips also turned out to be extremely good at doing large numbers of calculations simultaneously, which has made them popular for computation-intensive tasks like crypto mining, and more recently, training and running neural networks, which are what AI models are at their core.

Nvidia was initially focused on making GPUs aimed at gaming, but it had already moved into general-purpose computing by 2006 with the launch of the CUDA architecture, which lets developers use the chips for other tasks, such as doing calculations and research. After AI researchers began using the company’s GPUs to train neural networks over a decade ago, Huang leaned in towards developing chips for AI and then data centers, and grew it from there into its dominant position.

The potential implications of Huang’s departure for the AI and data center industries are vast, given how active Huang, and by extension Nvidia, has been in furthering the AI boom. In particular, it raises questions about the future of Nvidia’s sprawling AI investments, which have helped bankroll everything from startups developing new technologies to the enormous data centers needed to run them.

Under Huang, Nvidia deftly used its financial muscle to support companies buying its chips, effectively helping to finance the market for its own products, as well as lobbying Washington to relax potential rules around AI and data center regulation.

As of the end of its second quarter, Nvidia reported $99 billion worth of investments and another $25 billion of commitments. It also has $36 billion in cloud service commitments, which are arrangements that help AI cloud operators finance the GPUs and data center equipment they buy from Nvidia, with the chipmaker saying it will buy compute from them and potentially earn a share of their operators’ revenue.

Nvidia has also been investing directly in neoclouds, the companies serving a lot of the AI processing needs. In January, the company invested $2 billion in CoreWeave, and another $2 billion in Nebius in March. The chipmaker was said to be considering being a guarantor for Nscale’s planned West Virginia data center project. It’s also signed up to provide up to $105 billion worth of credit guarantees for an OpenAI data center campus in Ohio.

Nvidia’s also a major investor in the labs building the AI models that run on its GPUs, putting $30 billion into OpenAI, and $10 billion into Anthropic. It’s also backed Mira Murati’s Thinking Machines Lab as part of a deal involving at least 1 GW of its Vera Rubin GPU systems. The chipmaker has also invested $3.5 billion in MediaTek to help the Taiwanese company develop custom AI chips that work with Nvidia’s systems.

Many investors have grown uneasy with the enormous amounts of debt companies in the space were raising to sustain the AI infrastructure buildout, and Huang’s exit throws another uncertainty-shaped wrench into the works. CoreWeave’s shares were down about 9% on Thursday, reversing part of a 25% rally on Wednesday, while Nebius is down about 16%. IREN fell 6.6%.

Nvidia’s board has also appointed Harvey C. Jones, a managing partner of Square Wave Ventures and former CEO of Synopsys, as the company’s first-ever chair of the board of directors. Jones has been on Nvidia’s board since 1993. As chair, Jones is likely to play a central role in Nvidia’s search for a permanent CEO to replace Huang.

Huang is the single largest personal shareholder at Nvidia, but he owns only about 3.5% of the company’s shares. Unlike many of Silicon Valley’s tech giants, he does not hold supervoting rights, and the board had the power to vote him out at any time.

Huang famously ran a very flat organization, with around 60 direct reports. There was no heir apparent or succession plan either (although two of his children also worked in executive roles at the company). Huang famously maintained tight control over the company he founded, making it challenging for Nvidia to find a path forward without him.

The industry will get its first glimpse of that next week during the quarterly conference call. Nvidia reports earnings on Tuesday.

“Jensen’s contributions to NVIDIA are profound. NVIDIA is one of the most consequential technology companies in the world. The Board and the company thank Jensen for his immeasurable contributions to the company and the industry,” the company said in a statement.
