Information‑gain design trims training compute

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Amortizing sequential Bayesian experimental design turns the expensive, inference‑time search for information gain into a cheap forward pass, letting LLMs ask smarter questions with far less compute. The shift from on‑the‑fly optimization to a learned policy reshapes how we think about interactive dialogue systems.

Previously, adaptive query selection relied on explicit expected information gain (EIG) calculations at inference, a bottleneck that made real‑time reasoning impractical. Deep Adaptive Design showed that “Deep Adaptive Design … amortizes sequential experimental design into a design policy , which maps the history to the next design … This shifts the computational cost to training, allowing the learned policy to directly select the next design at inference time without EIG optimization” [1].

When applied to Twenty Questions, the AR‑BED policies “improve information acquisition and task performance, outperform inference- and training-time BED baselines at substantially lower computational cost, and generalize to unseen categories” [1]. The same reasoning transfers to Medi‑Q, a clinical dialogue benchmark, demonstrating that learned information‑gain skills survive domain shifts.

The paper notes that “the bound becomes tight as , making this design choice a trade‑off between estimator bias and computational cost,” highlighting an inherent tension: pushing compute savings too far can introduce bias that degrades decision quality [1]. Moreover, the experiments focus on relatively small reasoning tasks; it remains unclear how the approach scales to multi‑billion‑parameter models or more complex conversational settings.

If amortized EIG truly cuts inference overhead while raising success rates, future dialogue pipelines should replace runtime BED modules with pretrained design policies and re‑evaluate interactive benchmarks under this regime.
