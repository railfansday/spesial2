'Her injury did not stop her; she just found a different role'

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Nov. 27—"But I knew I had two options: I could either sit and think about it and feel sorry for myself or I could just try and find the best ways that I could still help my team because I'm still captain and I still have a job to do."

MOSES LAKE — Maddy Witwer recently completed her high school career with the Moses Lake Mavericks volleyball team. Though her senior season didn't exactly end the way she had planned due to injury, it opened a new opportunity to grow for the young athlete.

"I wasn't able to finish the season how I hoped, but I think in a way I've tried to look at it as a positive and see the game from a different perspective," said Witwer. "Maybe more from a coach's perspective and I still tried to give my input to the team where I could, not in a physical skill set way, but more of a mental way."

Witwer's athletic career didn't start out with volleyball as the senior said she was first a softball player throughout her childhood. Then middle school rolled around and Witwer decided to give volleyball a shot.

"Just through my first season, I really fell in love with the sport, and I've been playing ever since," she said.

What stuck out to her about volleyball was not only the indoor aspect of the game, but also how much the players rely on each other for success.

"I think it's one of the most team-oriented sports, so you really need to have trust in the people next to you," said Witwer. "That's what made me fall in love with the game."

The senior made a huge jump going from having no experience in the sport to going all in during middle school. Witwer had grown so much in the sport that by the time she reached high school, she was already getting noticed as a top player.

She said this quick growth comes from her desire to give her all in anything she sets her mind to. To reach this level, she always made sure to learn from those around her.

"I never want to be the most experienced in a room, I always want to surround myself with people who are better than me because that's the number one thing that helps me grow," said Witwer. "I remember choosing to do that from a young age starting in middle school ... and I think that really helped my mindset and helped me turn into the player that I wanted to be from a young age."

This mindset was perfect as she entered her freshman season where she would be surrounded by older and more experienced players, she said. Witwer's skills caught the attention of the Mavs coaches early on and she began high school starting on junior varsity and as a varsity substitute.

"I remember her very first practice when she was an eighth grader coming into ninth grade," said Mavs Head Coach Krystal Trammell-Walt. "We were doing hitting lines and she gets up there and she's just pounding away. She needed some fine tuning, but you could see then how delightful she was, a smile on her face. She understood the game, she had the basic fundamentals, you just knew there was something about her."

Getting to play varsity games during her freshman season was a very special opportunity for her, said Witwer.

"That was a privilege with the trust that the coaches placed in me from a young age," said the senior.

Going into her sophomore season, Witwer took on a bigger role on the varsity team as she took on the libero position, which she said helped hone her defensive skills.

For Trammell-Walt, this showed the versatility that Witwer was capable of at a young age.

"You could see her progression to different positions and her coachability and the overall love for the game," said the coach. "She's not negative, she always finds a way to win or make things better."

This growth proved to be important in her junior year as she had to develop further into her role on the varsity roster. During this time Witwer was put into the outside hitter position, which was what she had been accustomed to in previous seasons.

"Junior year was a really transitional and I think foundational year for me," she said. "Just really getting back, honing into my offensive skills."

Witwer had the opportunity that season to work alongside a strong group of seniors with whom she became good friends. Being on a team with this group helped her mature and develop into the leader the team would need as she entered her senior season.

"That group of seniors, them and I were very close; I spent a lot of my personal time with them," said Witwer. "That made me trust them even more and we all went into the season last year with a killer, driven mindset. That's especially something that I've tried to take and bring into this year as well."

Being with those seniors also brought up her own level of maturity, she said. With her being an upperclassman last season, she knew there was a new energy she needed as she became a leader on the team.

"She was on varsity a long time with a lot of those seniors and you would have absolutely felt that she was a senior," said Trammell-Walt.

Beginning her senior year was exciting for Witwer as she became team captain and got to fully step into a leadership role and make the team her own, said the senior.

"I really enjoyed that and especially the girls around me helped me lead," said Witwer. "It wasn't all me and I appreciate them."

Witwer said she had a lot of goals set for herself throughout her senior season and that determination showed on the court. With her leadership, the Mavs had a strong start to their season with multiple nonleague victories and a league win over Sunnyside.

The senior's energy helped fire up her teammates to rally and have strong performances and come close in each game. She said despite her teammates being young, they all had the heart to become the best version of themselves.

"From a leadership perspective, I really appreciated that and that helped me every day be like, 'I want to be here on this court so I can help them or impact their game in any way I can,'" said the senior.

Unfortunately, Witwer faced adversity in the middle of October when a medical issue popped up taking her off the court. After a trip to the doctor, she learned the solution would require surgery and from there it was determined that she would be on injury reserve for the rest of her senior season.

"Once I knew that I wasn't going to be able to finish out my season, that was definitely a hard day for me because I felt like I couldn't accomplish the things that I had set in my head," she said. "But I knew I had two options: I could either sit and think about it and feel sorry for myself or I could try and find the best ways that I could still help my team because I'm still captain and I still have a job to do."

The latter was the clear option as Witwer said she set out to find 100 other ways to help her team. She continued to show up to every practice and did anything she could to help the girls with their technique and mental game.

Trammell-Walt said she saw Witwer start to gain a deeper understanding of the coaching side of the game during this time.

"When she was taken off the court as a player, she actually had a different set of eyes, a different perspective," she said. "That made her stronger and she was able to bring that to the group... Her injury did not stop her; she just found a different role."

Her dedication to the Mavericks even after the injury was on full display each game day as she was in the pregame huddles and on the sidelines every game being vocal and supporting her teammates. This was all culminated for senior night where she gave her team a pregame peptalk and even was given the honors of making the opening serve for her last time on home court.

"It wasn't the senior night I expected or planned in my head, but I was just so grateful for the love that I had around me, my teammates, my coaches, my family," said Witwer. "That was just so cool to see how many people still supported me and still wanted to show out and watch a team that I was a part of."

Trammell-Walt said she was very happy to see how far Witwer developed as both a player and an athlete during her time with MLHS.

"Where she started out as a freshman and then compared to where she ended her senior year, a lot of growth and maturity, not only in her skill set, but just the overall development into a wonderful young lady," said the coach.

Witwer's senior volleyball season has since concluded and while she doesn't have plans to play at the collegiate level, the senior said she plans to find ways to stay on the court through the next chapter in her life.

"I wish that she would play on, she definitely would get some offers, but I know she's going to be valuable in a coaching or recruiting role," said Trammell-Walt. "I don't think the game will ever leave her."

Being with the Mavs volleyball program has been the most important part of becoming who she is today. She said volleyball taught her teamwork and dedication and will always appreciate her coaches for bringing the team together as friends.

"The main things that this program has given to me are amazing friends and amazing relationships that I'll never forget but (it) also has taught me so many life skills and taught me to keep my head up when I fail," said Witwer. "I'm just very grateful to Krystal, Ashlyn Haneberg and all of them. They have done a really good job with the program, and I can't wait to come back and watch in later seasons."
