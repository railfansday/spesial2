Fantasy Football Rankings: Hayden Winks' WR Blueprint for Week 12

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The Blueprint is my weekly column and basically everything that I use to build out the most accurate rankings I can. There will be an RB column, this WR column and a combination column on the onesie positions. You will find film notes and endless charts. Maybe enough charts to stress test the Yahoo website. I kid.

It's impossible to get every ranking thoroughly early in the week, so be sure to keep checking in on these columns through Sunday morning. As always, my full rankings at every position will be updated here soon.

1. Ja'Marr Chase (vs. NO) - 93.5 proj. receiving yards

2. Jaxon Smith-Njigba (at SF) - 97.5 proj. receiving yards

Smith-Njigba is the WR2 in per-game half PPR scoring on WR6 expected-points usage over his past four games with Drew Lock and Sam Darnold splitting time. He has topped 100 receiving yards in three of those four contests while drawing a 44% target share in that span. What's new?

San Francisco ranks 11th in half PPR points allowed per game to opposing wideouts, having surrendered 71.2 fantasy points to the Cowboys receiver room in Week 10 when chasing points. JSN has tons of experience against this defense, most recently posting 118, 19, 84, 124 and 118 yards. Seattle travels as 3-point road underdogs with a 21.75-point team total (17th). Throwing the ball to the perimeter is the best way to move the ball against the Niners, in my opinion.

3. CeeDee Lamb (vs. PHI) - 89.5 proj. receiving yards

Lamb is the WR3 on WR2 usage this year, ranking second in targets per game (11.8) and third in yards per game (100.7). Through 11 weeks of an NFL season, Lamb's 208.2 half PPR points are 13th all-time. All-time.

The Eagles have been bad against Lamb-type receivers all year and now won't have All Pro LB Zack Baun. They've allowed the third-most points to fantasy WRs, and Lamb finished as the WR5 on WR1 overall usage against them in Week 7. Dallas carries a 28-point team total (fourth) as 8.5-point home favorites.

4. Amon-Ra St. Brown (vs. CHI) - 84.5 proj. receiving yards

St. Brown is having his usual season. He's the WR5 on WR7 usage, after going on a touchdown-less rut throughout October.

This matchup has the potential for a blowup game. The biggest factor will be if slot CB Kyler Gordon can play after he missed last week. The Bears have been pretty woeful in the secondary beyond him, while shutting down the run since Jeffery Simmons appeared. The Sun God was the WR5 and WR1 in matchups against them last year, both wins for Detroit. Jaxon Smith-Njigba hung 118-1 against them in Week 8 in a similar role, too. Detroit enters as 2.5-point home underdogs carrying a 26.5-point implied team total (sixth).

5. Tee Higgins (vs. NO) - 70.5 proj. receiving yards

6. Parker Washington (vs. TEN) - 78.5 proj. receiving yards

That really should've been a touchdown. Come on, refs! Washington still was a top-20 WR last week and is now the WR13 on WR12 usage across the season. He can be up-and-down based on the Jaguars' lopsided history, but when games are tighter, Washington catches fire, as he did last week when he racked up 16 targets while trailing.

This week, the Jaguars are expected to go crazy on offense. They have a league-high 31.0-point team total against the Titans, who have allowed 362 passing yards per game over the past month. Read that again. And again. It's one of the wildest stats of the year. It doesn't take a genius to feel good about Washington's ceiling odds here. He had 152-1 against them just two weeks ago.

7. George Pickens (vs. PHI) - 70.5 proj. receiving yards

We're so back. Pickens has 38.0 and 20.3 half PPR points, respectively, in his two games since the bye. He had one of the best grabs of the year last week along the sideline, while Dak Prescott had all day to pass behind a great Cowboys OL. It's an environment for Pickens to have monster games, even if his role and style come with some volatility. He's at his best when teams go to single high coverage, something the Eagles typically don't do.

That said, CB1 Quinyon Mitchell is dealing with a new hamstring injury, and the other star players won't be available with Jordan Davis on the Jaguars and LB Zack Baun on IR. Mitchell's status is a big deal for Pickens' matchup. Across the season, the Eagles have had far too many blown coverages, leading to the third-most fantasy points allowed to WRs (31.3). Pickens had 57-1 against them in Week 7 and 146-1 against them late last year. Dallas carries a 28-point team total (fourth) as 8.5-point home favorites.

8. Malik Nabers (at IND) - 64.5 proj. receiving yards

Nabers had a handful of underneath receptions against elite CB1 Travis Hunter last week, then teleported into the back of the end zone against Jacksonville's nickel defender to bring Nabers to a top-15 WR finish. Since Week 4, Nabers is the WR9 on WR4 usage with Puka Nacua as one of the receivers ahead. Things will always be up-and-down with J.J. McCarthy, who missed him way out of bounds for a potential back-shoulder TD, but Nabers is way too good to not be treated as a fantasy WR1 now.

The Giants have a healthy 24.0-point team total this week, and that'll easily be one of their best all year. The Colts are tanking and have allowed the 10th-most passing yards per game over their last four games. We'll see if CB1 Sauce Gardner or CB2 Charvarius Ward will return this week. Nico Collins had an awesome deep touchdown against their backups last week, and Nabers had a 171-2 game against the Colts during his rookie season, for what it's worth. Long story short, Nabers looks great this week.

9. Justin Jefferson (vs. ATL) - 84.5 proj. receiving yards

Jefferson is the WR18 on WR8 usage over the past two weeks with Kirk Cousins. The fumble on that in-cut explosion was a real bummer, but that completion had a lot of timing and anticipation from the QB, something Jefferson hasn't had since 2023. He had plenty of breaking routes against the 49ers, and Kevin O'Connell drew up two different downfield shots to him, too. A big game is long overdue. The signs of it happening soon are there.

This is a sweet matchup for just that. The Falcons don't have Pro Bowl FS Jessie Bates, and CB1 A.J. Terrell is having a down season in isolation. The Vikings getting their OL healthy again would be nice, but Jefferson should have room to operate either way. The Falcons have allowed the fifth-most passing yards per game this year and the seventh-most fantasy points to WRs. Minnesota has a strong 24.75-point team total, and Jordan Addison (finger) isn't expected to play.

10. Nico Collins (vs. BAL) - 63.5 proj. receiving yards

Collins ranks as the WR18 in half PPR points per game on WR31 expected-points usage across his last four appearances. His weekly target volume has fluctuated wildly, bottoming out at four targets in two separate contests while spiking to 15 targets in Week 10 against Cleveland. The good news is the Texans have recently ramped his snap rates up out of desperation and with Marvin Harrison Jr. and Jaylen Noel sidelined. Collins is the WR11 on WR20 usage this year. That's a fair range for him. A long dunk-on-you TD made him the WR18 last week.

Baltimore's defense has underwhelmed. We'll see if podcaster Marlon Humphrey is back this week, but he's been bad this year anyway. Collins will likely see a bunch of CB1 Nate Wiggins, who has the speed to match with Collins downfield. Opponents facing the Ravens have posted a 46% neutral pass rate, the third-lowest rate in the NFL and the Texans are 6.5-point home favorites. He likely won't have that many targets here, but a 26.5-point team total (seventh) suggests he'll be a big reason why that is.

11. Matthew Golden (at LAR) - 74.5 proj. receiving yards

Golden has produced as the WR8 in half PPR points per game on WR5 expected-points usage over his last four games. The breakout sophomore has commanded a 33% target share alongside 10.8 targets and 133 air yards per game, topping nine targets in all four contests. These are the type of metrics that make the drops livable. Golden is one of the best separators in the NFL. Full stop.

The Rams present a stingy draw on paper, ranking 30th in half PPR points allowed to WRs (21.5 per game). Opposing offenses have largely avoided testing them through the air, but have CB2 Jaylen Watson on IR right now, at least. Golden may see a handful of Trent McDuffie in this one, so he may not be as open as he usually is. The Packers simply don't have other avenues to move the ball, so Golden should still see plenty of looks. Green Bay is listed as 3-point road underdogs with a 20.75-point team total (19th).

12. Christian Watson (at LAR) - 75.5 proj. receiving yards

Watson is the WR7 on WR16 usage this season, while the past month has cooled off (WR15 on WR28 usage). He's already faced the Vikings twice and the Bucs this year, and he's been masterful in the other contests. Green Bay won't have Josh Jacobs and will need its signature splash plays to win on Thanksgiving Eve on the road.

The Rams have been tough on WRs, ranking 30th in half PPR points allowed to the position at 21.5 per game. They also sit 27th against deep passes over 15 air yards, allowing only 6.3 points per game on downfield attempts. Their structure is to stop this aerial offense, but easier said than done. CB2 Jaylen Watson being on IR certainly helps Watson's odds. Green Bay travels as 3-point road underdogs with an implied team total of 20.75 points (19th).

13. Mike Evans (vs. SEA) - 59.5 proj. receiving yards

Removing screens, Evans is the WR1 overall in yards per route (4.1) this season as a 33-year-old grown man. He's been a total monster at the catch point as usual, and the 49ers are often in the red zone to take advantage of his size and ball tracking. He's the WR12 on WR19 usage throughout the year, including his injured games. He put up 109 yards against the dang Vikings in Mexico City last week and his best game of the year was against the Seahawks. He doesn't care about matchups.

Speaking of, it's rematch SZN with Evans taking on Seattle at home here. He dunked all over CB2 Josh Jobe in Week 5, when he was the WR6 on WR5 usage. That's largely been the only great game at receiver against the Seahawks all year, with nobody posting more than 60 yards at WR since. The 49ers have a 24.75-point team total, which is a sign of respect to the Hawks defense. San Francisco is averaging 31.6 points per game.

14. Garrett Wilson (at MIA) - 59.5 proj. receiving yards

We'll see if Omar Cooper Jr. (ankle) can return later on. Wilson is used a little less often when the entire skill room is healthy. He's been the WR25 on WR17 usage this year, through no fault of his own. He's the WR3 overall in ESPN's on-field tracking metrics, and I've seen him wide open way too often on inaccurate passes. He's turned 12.8 expected points per game into 8.1 points this month. That does make him a positive regression candidate, though we've said that for his entire career.

The Dolphins have quietly been great against fantasy WRs, only allowing 21.6 half PPR points per game to WR rooms (29th). Part of that is that first-round rookie CB Chris Johnson is turning into a real player already. DJ Moore was able to do some damage in matchups away from Johnson last week, however, finishing with 91 yards and a score.

15. Jameson Williams (vs. CHI) - 56.5 proj. receiving yards

Jamo is one of a kind. Only he can front flip into the end zone one week after Aidan Hutchinson tore his ACL celebrating with a jump. The good news is not that he landed it (he didn't); it's that he keeps finding the end zone. He's the WR20 on WR34 usage this season now. Jared Goff is dynamite right now and was able to hang in the pocket to deliver a strike to Jamo on his post TD. He's only averaging 3.6 receptions per game this year, but he certainly makes them count as usual.

This matchup is a dreamy one. The Bears have been nuked on double moves and go balls by outside speed receivers this year. They've allowed the seventh-most fantasy points to WRs on 15+ air-yard throws, while limiting underneath receivers with slot CB Kyler Gordon (questionable) as their top corner. Williams has been the WR20, WR10 and WR4 in his most recent games against them. The Lions are a rare underdog here, yet still have a 26.5-point team total (fifth) in what is obviously a potential shoutout.

16. Luther Burden III (at DET) - 60.5 proj. receiving yards

Burden is going to be very volatile in this role. He only played 12 of 27 snaps with multiple TEs on the field, playing behind Rome Odunze and Kalif Raymond. The Bears love his YAC ability but don't trust him in all ways at the moment. That's part of the reason why he's an All-Pro in first halves and goes away in second halves. Burden is the WR21 on WR42 usage in games with Caleb Williams this season on a team-high 22% target share.

This is a matchup to be very excited about, especially if CB1 D.J. Reed can't suit up in the secondary. The Lions won't have DE1 and LB1, and they have already been averaging 254 passing YPG (fourth most) as a pass funnel. A team total of 29.0 points (third) with the division on the line are the cherries on top.

18. DJ Moore (vs. KC) - 50.5 proj. receiving yards

Moore is going overlooked because of all the partial games earlier in the season. He's the WR13 in yards per route, removing screens. He's also the WR12 on WR16 usage this past month after returning from injury. He's winning on run-away routes, and the Bills will move him around, too. It's been a great marriage into the offense. Moore had the best breaking-route tape of his season just last week, ultimately scoring a red-zone touchdown on the backside of a Josh Allen progression. A 23% target share on a great offense is a great place to be with his YAC ability. He had a season-high 17.0 expected half PPR points last week.

The Chiefs are a very real test for Moore in every way. Albeit against an easy schedule, Kansas City is second-best against fantasy WRs this season, allowing the second-fewest points on 15+ air yard targets where Moore is the most lethal. Offenses facing the Chiefs have run the ball at the highest rate in neutral situations (46%), likely to stay away from this awesome cornerback duo of Nohl Williams and first-round rookie Mansoor Delane. Both are very physical when fully healthy. Allen is far more experienced against a menacing Steve Spagnuolo defense, but few have got it done at receiver against them so far. JSN was the outlier when he hung 163 yards on them. Buffalo enters the week as 3-point home favorites with a strong 26.75-point team total (fifth).

19. Zay Flowers (at HOU) - 67.5 proj. receiving yards

20. Drake London (at MIN) - 64.5 proj. receiving yards

21. DeVonta Smith (at DAL) - 64.5 proj. receiving yards

Smith is going to be chasing air yards and expected fantasy points the rest of the season with Joe Milton at QB. There's an accuracy problem to all levels of the field. Smith was missed on a couple of out routes, on an out-and-up along the sideline and on a potential 69-yard go ball. Smith finished as the WR40 on WR11 usage. The good news is he looked healthy, and the defense is in a bad spot, so there will be game script on his side often.

The matchup with the Cowboys is a bit more challenging with CB1 Joey Porter Jr. healthy again, but they have been hilariously bad all year for a million reasons. Calvin Ridley and Carnell Tate had wild scores against this unit last week, and it was 109-2 for Deebo Samuel the week prior. They find new ways to be embarrassed. And still, the Eagles have a 19.5-point team total (23rd).

22. Stefon Diggs (at ARI) - 46.5 proj. receiving yards

23. Tetairoa McMillan (at TB) - 60.5 proj. receiving yards

24. Denzel Boston (vs. LV) - 47.5 proj. receiving yards

25. Rome Odunze (at DET) - 47.5 proj. receiving yards

Odunze is the WR33 on WR42 usage since Week 6 when Caleb Williams returned. He's not getting the volume of a must-start WR3, but Odunze is a downfield and red-zone threat in an awesome offense. If he can secure the ball cleanly, there will be a massive game from him down the stretch here.

The matchup is a great one, with the Lions allowing the fourth-most passing yards per game. It'll get even better if CB1 D.J. Reed (hip) can't play again. This unit has allowed the 11th-most fantasy points to WRs on 15+ air yard targets, and that will be a lot easier with a backup corner on Odunze. Back in Week 2 of last year, Odunze had 128 yards and two scores against this defense. That was his best game in terms of targets (11), receptions (seven) and fantasy points (28.3 half PPR). The Bears enter as 2.5-point road favorites with a 29-point team total (third).

27. Rashee Rice (at BUF) - 49.5 proj. receiving yards

Rice isn't going to reach his top-10 fantasy WR status in this new role, but he's held the line as a fantasy WR2/3. Over the past month since his injury, Rice is the WR28 on WR36 usage with Josh Downs now involved on similar intermediate routes. He's had a 20% target share this month with a 5.6 aDOT. The Chiefs opted for a WR-by-committee last week to make sure Xavier Worthy, Cyrus Allen and Andrew Armstrong got snaps, too. Rice played 22-of-39 receiving snaps (56%) through three quarters. We'll see if he keeps getting the occasional deep target as he's had recently. That'd be his best way to add the occasional spike week.

The Bills are weakest through the air, though new S1 Amani Hooker is going to shore things up moving forward. They've allowed the 14th-most fantasy points to WRs this year and CB1 Christian Benford hasn't played recently. If he can't go, there is a clear opportunity for all WRs. Rice had 86-1 against Buffalo last year for what it's worth. The Chiefs enter Thanksgiving as 3-point road underdogs with an implied total of 23.75 points (11th).

32. Khalil Shakir (vs. KC) - 42.5 proj. receiving yards

Shakir delivered his most productive outing of the year in Week 11 against Miami, posting season highs with 14.3 expected points, 101 receiving yards and 118 air yards on eight targets, complemented by occasional backfield carries. Is that a coincidence with slot TE Dalton Kincaid sidelined? Possibly, but there are schematic reasons to believe those are correlated. Across the season, Shakir is the WR43 on WR46 usage in half PPR scoring, but he's at least had 6.5 fantasy points in seven straight games for those in deeper leagues.

Kansas City presents a tough assignment, ranking 31st in half PPR points allowed per game to wide receivers at just 19.5 per contest. Offenses have passed at a league-low 46% clip against the Chiefs in neutral situations. They can also tackle really well. If we squint at the matchup, the Chiefs' outside CBs are better than their slot options where Shakir roams. Buffalo enters as 3-point home favorites with an implied team total of 26.75 points (fifth).

34. Jakobi Meyers (vs. TEN) - 51.5 proj. receiving yards

35. Ryan Flournoy (vs. PHI) - 29.5 proj. receiving yards

The Eagles are tanking on defense with injuries piling up, so Dallas has a healthy 28.0-point team total (fourth) at home. He had 70 yards against them in Week 7.

The Steelers can't guard Ja'Marr Chase, but they've been very good against just about everyone else this year. The most half PPR fantasy points at WR they've allowed aside from the Cincy duo is just 14.3 (Emeka Egbuka on 14 targets). This being the week that Waddle goes off would be twisting the knife.
