BepiColombo Captures Mercury Orbit After 8-Year Trip

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The planet Mercury might be one of the least explored planetary bodies in the solar system, but its is arguably the most crucial in helping scientists better understand the formation and evolution of the inner solar system. Its position as the closest planet to the Sun makes it a prime target for how the rocky planets formed and evolved and what this could mean for finding life beyond Earth. However, just traveling to Mercury is a lengthy and time-consuming effort, often taking a spacecraft several years just to achieve orbit. More on that later.

Now, the lauded joint mission between the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) called BepiColombo might have just become the second mission to orbit Mercury. This is because mission leaders recently announced that BepiColombo successfully achieved Mercury orbit insertion (MOI) on November 21 at 18:27 UTC (1:27 pm EST/10:27 am PST). This historic achievement comes 8 years, 1 month, and 1 day since BepiColombo launched on October 20, 2018, and included one Earth flyby, two Venus flybys, and six mercury flybys to achieve orbit just a few days ago.

While MOI is essential for achieving Mercurian orbit, it is not the final orbit BepiColombo will achieve to conduct science operations, which are expected to commence in April 2027.
