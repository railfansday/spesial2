USPS is proposing to make its fuel surcharge permanent and raise shipping rates in January

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The Postal Service's proposal would set the surcharge at 11% and lift domestic shipping prices 5.9% on average from Jan. 17

The U.S. Postal Service filed a proposal on Thursday to convert an 8% price increase into a permanent fuel surcharge of 11% and raise domestic shipping prices 5.9% on average. The changes are scheduled to take effect Jan. 17, 2027.

The USPS Board of Governors approved the adjustments this week, and the Postal Regulatory Commission will review them before they take effect, the agency said. The 5.9% increase applies to Priority Mail, Priority Mail Express, USPS Ground Advantage and Parcel Select.

The 11% surcharge takes the 8% transportation-related rate bump the agency rolled out in April and adds 3 more points to reflect continued rises in fuel costs, the agency said. The surcharge will not apply to international products under the proposal. The agency plans to revisit the surcharge in July 2027, using fuel costs from the first six months of that year as its basis.

The surcharge rate will not fluctuate when it launches in January, according to Supply Chain Dive. The agency intends to shift to weekly adjustments pegged to fuel-price averages that the U.S. Energy Information Administration publishes, according to Supply Chain Dive. The publication also said the 5.9% average matches the increases FedEx $FDX and UPS have announced.

Prices for Priority Mail Express International, Priority Mail International, First-Class Package International Service and International Priority Airmail would rise 7.9% on average. The Postal Service also plans to adjust prices on some competitive special services and introduce new fees.

"The USPS governors believe these new rates will keep the Postal Service competitive while providing the agency with much needed revenue as it continues to struggle with financial issues," the agency said in a statement.

The USPS first added a fuel-related charge in April, according to Bloomberg, as diesel costs spiked following the outbreak of the war in Iran.

The agency raised the Forever stamp price to 82 cents from 78 cents in July as it dealt with deepening financial losses.

The USPS ended fiscal 2026 $6.5 billion in the red, versus a $9 billion shortfall a year earlier. Postmaster General David Steiner has warned that the agency's cash could be exhausted as soon as February, according to Bloomberg.
