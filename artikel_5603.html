How brokers can support new agents without overspending

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Brokers can reduce agent churn by treating education and mentorship as an investment. A 3-part support system, technical training, personal development, and mentorship, can be built gradually and scaled over time.  AI Summary

I believe a lot of brokers really miss the mark when it comes to supporting their agents, and in the long run, that hurts them just as much as it hurts agents.  They treat things like education and mentorship like a cost, rather than an investment, so they miss out on a lot of agents who had the potential to become top producers.

Think about it like this — you meet an agent who has the right mindset, but they don’t yet have the experience or confidence to come out of the gates running. You hire them anyway.

Broker A adopts a sink or swim approach, forcing them to learn the job on the fly. After all, they’ve already learned the technical side in their licensing classes.

Most agents fail in that environment and start bouncing from broker to broker, picking up a few things along the way. The brokers who take this approach tend to suffer a lot of agent churn, earn little to no repeat business and face wild swings in revenue. It creates an unnecessary additional workload and cost, and becomes disastrous during slow market conditions when the volume of new agents declines.

Broker B, on the other hand, adopts a mentorship approach. They figure if they’re going to take the time to hire and onboard a new agent, they might as well give them everything they need to be as successful as possible.

Agents are more likely to thrive in this environment, and they get traction a lot faster too. As a result, they also tend to stick around longer, which means lower HR costs and more repeat business. It’s a win for you, your agents, and clients.

The challenge is how to implement a system to effectively support your agents without breaking the bank. And the good news is it’s not as expensive or time consuming as you might think.

Rome wasn’t built in a day, and your support system won’t be either, so you’ll need to break it into three elements:

Start small and simple. A well-rounded library of real estate books that align with your approach is the foundation of your training. You or your top agents can hold weekly classes teaching the material, followed by Q&A.

Then, begin developing your own training materials that are tailored to your business and market. Start by creating an outline of the topics you want agents to master, and then prioritize them by order of importance. Now start teaching classes on these topics, and after a few sessions, refine the material based on the questions your agents ask. Once it’s dialed in, you can record a perfect version, and only do the Q&A portion live. Over the course of a year, you should have a robust library of training videos.

Just be sure to keep it up to date as laws and regulations, industry and market statistics, and your internal processes change.

This is an area you probably shouldn’t create your own training material because it’s a unique expertise.

You can start with the same foundational approach as with the technical training by teaching your agents based on trusted books from authoritative experts. Tony Robbins, Jocko Willink, Robert Greene, Gary John Bishop, and if you’re feeling particularly edgy, maybe even David Goggins, are all great options. And there are many interactive programs, both in-person and virtual, that you can send your agents to.

In full disclosure, I published a book on this topic titled “Unlock Your MVP”, but it was based on my own journey of building a brokerage producing over $2 billion in transactions, surviving three heart attacks, battling three NAR class action lawsuits, and then going on to build a new brokerage from scratch to over 1,100 agents in just nine months as its CEO and qualifying broker. So I have what most would consider a fairly unique perspective.

This is where it gets tougher because mentorship requires a sizable investment of time and energy.

The approach I’ve taken is to be the primary mentor. As those agents become more successful, other agents become more receptive to participating. And it’s easier to get your top producers to take an active role in mentoring agents if you incentivise them and frame it from the perspective of mentorship helping them to become more proficient themselves and opening opportunities to partner with their mentees for a commission split.

Growing your brokerage requires you to create an environment that enables your agents to become more successful more quickly. Most brokers never make this investment and they suffer as a result, so if you want to thrive, build an effective support system.

Derek Carlson is the president and managing broker of Realty ONE Group MVP, a Florida based real estate brokerage firm with over 1,100 Realtors.
