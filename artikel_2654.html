Rams lean on Blake Corum's 184 yards and a strong defense to defeat Packers

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The stadium rocked. The crowd roared. The players delivered big hits.

And that's just what happened during legendary rock band U2's halftime performance when the Rams faced the Green Bay Packers on Wednesday night at SoFi Stadium.

Before and after, the Rams put together perhaps their most important effort of the season in a 26-13 victory that extended their winning streak to five games and improved their record to 7-4.

Blake Corum rushed for a touchdown and a career-best 184 yards, Kyren Williams rushed for a touchdown and the Rams' defense forced three turnovers in a win that kept the Rams in playoff contention.

The game, and what it might mean for the Rams moving forward, played out like U2's songbook.

Rams linebacker Byron Young sacks Green Bay Packers quarterback Jordan Love in the fourth quarter Wednesday. (Allen J. Schaben / Los Angeles Times)

For coach Sean McVay, the team that opened the season as the Super Bowl favorite actually started to look the part.

McVay was again at his best, orchestrating an effective game plan despite unexpected turns and injuries

It's been a season of ups and downs, and McVay said his college coach "used to say, 'Have a plan. Work the plan and plan for the unexpected.'

"We've had a lot of that ... come to life to plan for the unexpected this year."

Star wide receiver Puka Nacua and tight end Terrance Ferguson are out for the season. Star receiver Davante Adams remains on injured reserve.

Center Coleman Shelton and guard Steve Avila did not play because of hamstring injuries, and offensive lineman Bill Murray and tight end Tyler Higbee did not finish the game because of injuries.

Receiver Konata Mumpfield caught four passes for 69 yards, several in key situations.

Center Beaux Limmer stayed the course for a line that gave up only one sack by star edge rusher Micah Parsons.

The Rams took a major step toward achieving that with a victory that gave them the head-to-head tiebreaker if they are tied with the Packers at the end of the season and jockeying for a wild-card spot in the NFC playoffs.

The Rams have games remaining against the Kansas City Chiefs (8-2), the NFC West-leading San Francisco 49ers (9-1), the Dallas Cowboys (5-6) and two games against the defending Super Bowl-champion Seattle Seahawks (6-4) sandwiched around a trip to play the Tampa Bay Buccaneers (3-7).

Rams wide receiver Konata Mumpfield, left, celebrates with running backs Blake Corum, center, and Kyren Williams in the third quarter against the Packers on Wednesday night. (Allen J. Schaben / Los Angeles Times)

Corum celebrated his 26th birthday by carrying the ball 32 times and eclipsing 100 yards rushing for the second consecutive game.

"It meant a lot," he said, "but it's just another game."

It was the most carries by an NFL back this season.

"I probably was tired, but I was telling myself I wasn't," he said. "It just felt good."

Williams rushed for 46 yards and a touchdown in 12 carries and caught four passes despite playing with a toe injury that he said he suffered three weeks ago.

The rushing attack helped the Rams control the ball for more than 40 minutes. A relentless rushing attack causes opponents to start "chirping" at each other, guard Kevin Dotson said.

"That's when you know you're in their mind at the very least, and that's golden for us," he said. "You're like, 'OK, they're fighting. They're tired.' We just got to keep going, do the same play, tell them the play, we doing it again.'"

McVay preaches complementary football, a game in which the offense, defense and special teams play well.

The rushing attack was dominant, the defense stout, kicker Brandon McManus kicked four field goals and the return and coverage units did not give up a big play or commit a costly penalty.

Now if the passing game can just get back into high gear...

Rams quarterback Matthew Stafford looks to pass in the first quarter against the Packers on Wednesday. (Allen J. Schaben / Los Angeles Times)

Quarterback Matthew Stafford is 38 and he is a weekly presence on the injury report because of a right elbow issue.

He is not playing at the level that earned him his first NFL most valuable player award last season, but the 18th-year pro has led the Rams to five straight wins.

Against the Packers, he completed 16 of 23 passes for 140 yards, with an interception.

Nate Landman has an uncanny knack for forcing fumbles.

In the first quarter, Packers tight end Tucker Kraft caught a pass over the middle, but Landman punched the ball out of his grasp and it was recovered by Quentin Lake.

It was the fourth fumble forced by Landman this season, the 14th of his career.

That's what the Packers had to be feeling when the Rams' defensive front repeatedly penetrated into the backfield.

On a night the Rams broke out their "Fearsome White" uniforms to honor the team's famed Fearsome Foursome defensive line of the 1960s, Myles Garrett, Aaron Donald and Co. did not disappoint.

Garrett, Donald and Byron Young tackled running backs for losses. Donald batted down a pass. And Young sacked quarterback Jordan Love.

"I think we honored them in a reverse way of stopping that run," defensive lineman Braden Fiske said. "We didn't get the sacks we wanted but ... being able to stop the run was the biggest thing. ... All in all, I think we showed out."

"We feel like we have a taste to be the gold standard as far as defenses in the NFL," he said, "and getting everyone back healthy, it's a hell of an opportunity to go out and prove that."

That's the number of victories McVay now has over Packers coach Matt LaFleur.

Going into Wednesday night's game, McVay was 0-5 against his friend, who was the Rams' offensive coordinator in McVay's first season in 2017.

U2 performs during halftime between the Rams and Green Bay Packers at SoFi Stadium on Wednesday. (Allen J. Schaben / Los Angeles Times)

McVay said he was proud of the "flexibility and adjustability" his players have displayed throughout a trying season.

"Love this team, love this staff," he said. "I'm excited about what they've done and I'm excited about continuing to push the envelope and see where we can grow from here."

"I believe the things we went through early on in the season were going to set us up for where we are now," he said, "and where we're going in the future."
