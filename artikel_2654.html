Rapid Recap: Pistons 113, Bucks 98

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The Milwaukee Bucks started extremely well, but the Detroit Pistons' defence—which sits atop the league without Cade Cunningham—proved too much to overcome, and the Bucks lost 113-98. Brayden Burries led the Bucks with 23 points and seven rebounds, while Isaiah Joe starred for the Pistons with 22 points amid a spread of contributors.

The Bucks got off to a ripping start on both ends, taking advantage of the weapons the Pistons had out, running on misses to build a 10-2 lead and forcing an early timeout. Clearly, the timeout didn't do much, as Milwaukee stuck to their game plan, forcing Detroit into tougher shots and playing even more in transition as Kyle Kuzma, Jaime Jaquez Jr., and Ryan Rollins starred early for a combined 17 points—Bucks up 19-3 six minutes in! The Pistons finally got into gear after that as their bench filtered in, going on an 11-3 run as Tyler Herro and Myles Turner lost their men on the perimeter. Milwaukee would right the ship, though, as Brayden Burries checked in and scored a quick seven points to put the Bucks up 27-14 with two minutes to play. Unfortunately, some late mistakes by Milwaukee's youth allowed the Pistons to close on a 10-2 run, with the Bucks up 29-24 after one.

The Bucks started the second quarter with the same brand of silly basketball they finished the first with, repeatedly turning it over and taking bad shots as they went scoreless for nearly five minutes and let the Pistons take the lead. If not for Detroit missing a bunch of open shots, Milwaukee would have been in some strife. Luckily for the visitors, the game became a slog, and they stayed close, with neither team surpassing the 40-point mark until there was about three minutes to go. Yeah, it was ugly. Tyler Herro finally made his first field goal shortly thereafter, giving the Bucks a two-point edge, which they took into the locker room, up 50-48 at the half.

The Pistons came out ready and raring to go in the third, starting with an 11-4 run as Ausar Thompson began to make his mark. Both teams finally began to flow on offence as the Pistons nursed a 5-8-point advantage over the next few minutes, but a Bucks push cut the deficit to two, 71-69, with four to play. Milwaukee played decent half-court defence, but the minute they turned it over in live play—which they did a lot—Detroit almost always converted. That was, of course, until the final few minutes of the third, when the Pistons went bonkers no matter how they got their shots up, dominating on both ends as they closed on a 19-7 run—which included a buzzer-beating transition heave from Daniss Jenkins—to lead 90-76 after three.

Detroit kept turning up the defensive intensity early in the fourth, and with Tyler Herro resting, Milwaukee had nobody to calm them down and generate half-decent shots. Then we got some fireworks: Ausar Thompson beat Myles Turner down low—which he had done all night—shoved him in the chest, and the two came to blows, earning double technical fouls after Turner threw the ball at Thompson. From there, Detroit skied downhill as the Bucks struggled even to get shots up, and the deficit expanded to 20 points by the four-minute mark. It was all academic from that point forward as the wounded Pistons rolled over the Bucks.

I mean, to me it's just the Bucks' shooting percentage of 43.2%. What Detroit can do defensively, no matter who is sitting, is truly special.

The Bucks may never shoot well from three again.
