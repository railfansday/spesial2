Our 4 takeaways after Kansas State football's snoozer at TCU

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

FORT WORTH, Texas — Kansas State football couldn't snap its losing streak, dropping its fourth straight game amid a hectic travel day.

The Wildcats lost 20-10 to TCU at Amon G. Carter Stadium on Saturday, Nov. 14, in Fort Worth, Texas.

The loss dropped K-State to 4-6 with two games remaining. Kansas State had to fly into Fort Worth on Saturday morning due to Friday's FAA ground stoppage.

Here are our takeaways from the Wildcats' latest loss.

There was no bigger surprise throughout the game than when the Wildcats gained enough yards for a first down. Moving the chains looked extremely difficult for the Wildcats.

Dillon Duff got his second start of the season at quarterback. Though he was better than he was at Colorado (which wasn't hard to do), there was a lack of pocket awareness that led to four first-half sacks. Some of that can be blamed on the offensive line.

K-State's biggest play came late in the third quarter when busted coverage left Jaron Tibbs alone deep downfield. Duff connected with him for a 53-yard score.

The Wildcats moved the ball better in the second half, but it still felt like pulling teeth.

Down by 10 in TCU territory with 6:15 left, Wildcats coach Collin Klein opted to punt. It was fourth-and-15 from the TCU 40, but given the time remaining and the Wildcats' limited opportunities in opposing territory all game, it was head-scratching.

The Wildcats' defense ultimately got a quick stop after TCU burned a few minutes, giving them the ball back with 3:41 remaining. It didn't matter after Duff fumbled, turning the ball over to TCU.

There was little doubt that the Wildcats would show up on Saturday and play better than they did last week after allowing 66 points to Oklahoma State.

K-State's defensive line had its moments, sacking TCU quarterback Jaden Craig five times. Kamari Burns led the team with 1.5 sacks.

It wasn't perfect. With their backs against the wall after an early Rodney Fields fumble deep in their own territory, three K-State defenders missed a tackle as TCU's Kari Ashley cruised to a 19-yard score.

When points were tough to come by for the offense, K-State needed its defense to pitch a perfect fourth quarter after the Wildcats made it a one-score game. An 11-play, 57-yard drive turned into a TCU field goal, taking a two-score lead and putting the game out of reach.

There's a reason why Sonny Dykes' days as the Horned Frogs' head coach may be numbered. TCU is bad, which makes the K-State loss and performance worse.

Craig, a Harvard transfer at quarterback, was touted as a potential All-Big 12 performer and NFL prospect all preseason. While there's arm talent, his accuracy is barely existent.

Maybe TCU is as impressive defensively as its numbers suggest. It was hard to tell when Kansas State's offense looked as poor as it did, and it didn't feel like the Horned Frogs were doing that to them.
