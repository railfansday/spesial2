Alonso: ‘Fragile’ Chelsea not ‘at the level required’

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Another aggravating weekend in the Land of the Blues, with another historic home defeat suffered against one of our historic rivals. First, it were Spurs winning at the Bridge for just the second time since 1990. Now, it's Leeds United walking away with all three points for the first time since 1999. And they probably were partying like it, too.

And it's not like either of those two results were undeserved. There were no miracles here. We weren't undone by an unlucky bounce or an egregious call, even if the penalty called on Morgan Rogers was a bit harsh (if the shoulder or shirt sleeve count for offside, it shouldn't be a handball). Spurs and Leeds, both, were by far the superior team — in motivation, in intensity, in cohesion, in teamwork, in execution … in all aspects. This weekend's 4-1 (four!) embarrassment against the latter was likened in commentary to a training exercise against a youth team for the visitors. While Clearlake may wish the team to be that young for future profit considerations, in theory, we are a senior professional outfit with actual aspirations and ambitions of winning and trophies.

The only way those objectives could be any further away from us at the moment is if we had actually been dissolved by the UK government back in 2022.

At least that's what it feels like, even 36 hours removed from the result. And Xabi Alonso wasn't exactly trying to put a positive spin on things either with his post-match comments.

"After the equaliser […] we lost completely the momentum. Until then we were doing okay, I think that we were creating, playing a lot on the pitch, we were coming out for the pressure, but from that moment they started believing and we started not believing and after that we never got the good feeling mentally in any moment.

"[We] are in a fragile moment mentally, confidence when we receive a punch that we didn't recover that strong, that tough enough and today that was my feeling. [We] still have many minutes to play and it's not that the game is over [but] we didn't recover that good momentum."

As Mike Tyson once famously said, everyone has a plan 'til they get punched in the mouth.

Except when we get punched in the mouth, we forget not only the plan (however good or bad that might have been) but we seem to completely forget how to even play football.

Unfortunately, this isn't a new issue either. We've struggled with these sorts of situations ever since the galaxy brains in charge dismantled the team and put up a kindergarten in its stead. And while some of those kids are now a bit older, they "grew up" in a team that had practically no veteran leadership or winning ethos. So how can we expect them to live up to any sort of legacy?

Our various attempts to establish a new culture, to grow it organically, inorganically, or surgically haven't worked (or didn't take after working for a brief while). Alonso himself seems to be at a loss as well, and is simply hoping that things will turn around with the ebbs and flows of the tides of time.

"We need to support each other, together we need to find solutions to do better […] For sure it's not the easiest moment, it's a fragile moment but we need to work together and stick together because there is still so long way to go."

"I understand [the fans'] disappointment for sure and we share that with them and whatever we can do is work, try to give our best to turn it back and to do much better. [We] could do that better in a football way, not giving the ball away, trying to progress a little bit more, choosing better options, so that's the solution but as well, the physicality is a part and the mentality part, we can do better."

Well, it certainly would be hard to do any worse.
