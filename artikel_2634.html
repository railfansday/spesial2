Tommy McMillen calls overdose 'wakeup call,' but party likely not over

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Tommy McMillen admits what happened to him late on the night of Nov. 7 was not a good thing. But don't expect him to change his lifestyle too much as a result of it.

Speaking on the incident for the first time at-length, McMillen joined UFC commentator Joe Rogan on an episode of "JRE MMA Show" released Tuesday. The episode was recorded just eight days McMillen was found unresponsive in a Scottsdale, Ariz. nightclub. Narcan was administered and he transported to a local hospital.

"F*ck, man. For one, the only thing I'll say about that is I wasn't in the bathroom," McMillen said. "I was in the middle of a club. I had a few different substances on me, and then maybe mixed the wrong kind of substances, or maybe had a little bit of a bad batch of something. Yeah, I guess my body gave out and I started to almost choke. Stuff was coming out of my mouth. I was almost choking on it. Then I was unresponsive.

"Then, I don't remember any of this. I don't even remember getting to the club that night. I guess they had to give me like two Narcans or something like that, take me the hospital and gave me an IV. Then when I finally came to, I think that happened at 11 p.m. at night (or) 12 a.m. I didn't wake up and come to until like 5 a.m. the next day. I was out for six, seven hours. Something like that. I just got a bad batch of something pretty much."

McMillen did not elaborate on what substances exactly he took, though Narcan is typically used to combat the effects of opioids.

"I tried to be smarter than that most of the time," McMillen said. "If I'm really drunk and I'm already faded, my decisions (are off). My judgment starts to go down a little bit. It's like, bro, at that point I probably could've taken more than I already took of something and I wouldn't have even known. I probably would've swallowed the whole bag of something because I'm that faded. I wouldn't even remember doing it. That's kind of the point I do get to sometimes with partying. I get so faded and keep functioning for the longest time. Then when I'm functioning in those moments, I'm not there at all. The best decision I will say for that would be to get a guy that could just be like, 'Hey.'"

Rogan tried to push back on McMillen, telling him to stay off the pills and not risk dying. While McMillen indicated he might try to figure out a way to tone his drug use back a little, it doesn't sound like the party is over.

"It's a tough situation," McMillen said. "Because in my head, it's a wakeup call for the kid. It's a wakeup call in certain ways. But I think the one thing people have a misconception is on is that people thinking I'm partying more than I ever have or something. That's not even close to true at all. It's a wakeup call, but I don't know if I'm going to stop partying cold turkey and f*cking become a f*cking ... what do you guys want me to do?"

McMillen, 28, is one of the promotion's most popular rising stars. A protege of former UFC bantamweight champion Sean O'Malley, McMillen has embraced from its start. After a dazzling UFC contract-earning win on Dana White's Contender Series in 2025, McMillen has gone 3-0 in the UFC with three Fight of the Night bonuses.

He most recently competed in September when he defeated Marwan Rahiki by unanimous decision. McMillen was back in the gym Wednesday, less than four days after the overdose.
