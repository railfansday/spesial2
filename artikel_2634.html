Mike Tomlin responds to Steelers ‘dumpster fire’ after Aaron Rodgers debacle

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

After coaching Aaron Rodgers for one season, Mike Tomlin is watching his downfall from afar.

Rodgers rocked the football world on Wednesday when he went on a scathing rant on "The Pat McAfee Show" about current Steelers coach Mike McCarthy before the quarterback was swiftly cut from the team. Most in Pittsburgh's organization — including owner Art Rooney — are taking McCarthy's side.

Tomlin was not one of those people, opting for neutrality while talking about the situation with Mike Tirico on NBC on Thursday.

"I had a range of emotions watching those course of events yesterday," Tomlin said. "I think the prevailing one is just general sadness. Sad that we might be looking at the end of a legendary career of a quarterback … to see laundry aired out that way in a public forum, those are things that kind of run counterculture to professional football."

Head coach Mike Tomlin (left) walks off the field with Aaron Rodgers after the Steelers' 29-24 win over the Lions on Dec. 21, 2025. Getty Images

Tomlin, now a football analyst for NBC, was Rodgers' head coach in Pittsburgh last season until he resigned in January after 19 years at the helm for the Steelers. McCarthy, who coached Rodgers for 13 years in Green Bay, was hired as his replacement.

Despite Rodgers' allegations of McCarthy falling asleep in team meetings, not watching film and saying "1, 2, 3, Packers" to break down the team after a huddle — the latter of which McCarthy confirmed — Rooney said he has "100 percent confidence" in his head coach and intends to bring him back next season and beyond.

Does Tomlin think his successor will be able to lead the locker room for the final seven games of the season?

"It certainly created some challenges, and challenges is probably an understatement if all of those things are true," Tomlin said. "But his ability to overcome that or inability to overcome that, in my opinion, is based on relationships and the depth of those relationships and how those guys feel about him or how those guys buy in to what he's selling."

Rodgers was popular with the players and coaching staff last season, Tomlin said, making it even more shocking to hear the 42-year-old quarterback sever those relationships on live television.

"He was beloved, and I'm not overexaggerating that," Tomlin said. "He worked at it, and people appreciated his openness getting to know and understand the Steelers culture, to be engaged with teammates.

Aaron Rodgers and head coach Mike Tomlin leave the field after the Steelers' preseason win over the Panthers on Aug. 21, 2025, in Charlotte, N.C. Getty Images

"And particularly from my perspective, I've worked with a lot of veteran players over the years and at times, they lose a little patience for the process, or they lose patience for relationships with younger guys, et cetera. Man, he seemed to embrace all of that and really embrace it in a real, natural way."

The Steelers next turn to Mason Rudolph for Friday's matchup with the Broncos, with the organization squarely in the playoff hunt at 6-4 on the season.

Regardless of how Pittsburgh's season ends up as they move on from their starting QB, the whole debacle is sure to leave a dark scar on the Steelers organization.

"I'm upset about it. It's a dumpster fire," Tomlin said. "And I don't say that in a judgmental way — I've presided over a few dumpster fires in my 19 years. But it certainly is upsetting, and I'm sure Steeler Nation is upset."
