Callahan Admits He Was Wrong About Drake Maye’s 2026 Leap

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Andrew Callahan has to eat his own words after predicting a massive jump for Drake Maye in 2026. The Boston Herald writer admitted his bold claim was flat out wrong just days after the Patriots lost 35-10 to the Detroit Lions in Munich. Maye threw three interceptions in that game and then suffered a fractured ankle that could end his season. Callahan wrote his third annual column to own his error, stating that accountability must go both ways for the team and its observers.

Callahan had promised that Maye would make a "Year 3 leap" starting on a Wednesday night in Seattle back in September. The 24-year-old quarterback did not come close to that mark before his injury. His stats from the first three games showed a turnover rate more than double the previous year. He had just one touchdown against six interceptions during that rough stretch.

The Patriots are now sitting at 5-5 entering their bye week with a broken roster. The loss in Germany exposed deep cracks in a squad that looked like a Super Bowl contender just months ago. Fans are left wondering if this "Super Bowl hangover" is permanent or just a bad patch.

Callahan also got the defense completely wrong in his summer training camp preview. He claimed the edge defender spot was the team's biggest weakness and that Elijah Ponder needed to break out or the Patriots would be in serious trouble. Ponder has 7.5 sacks and is tied for seventh-most in the NFL. Among all edge defenders at Pro Football Focus, rookie Gabe Jacas is 15th in pass-rush grades, ahead of stars like Maxx Crosby. Dre'Mont Jones and Ponder both hold pass-rush grades that place them within the top 40. The position Callahan called a liability is now a strength.

The offensive line remains shaky, and the wide receiver room is a disaster after the A.J. Brown trade. Brown is dealing with injury issues and struggling to catch passes while on the field. The team has gotten better production from almost every other wide receiver than Brown this season. Reporter, Jeremy Fowler notes that the Patriots face uncomfortable questions about who will be the WR1 next year.

Fowler writes, "In the long term, where the offense turns next is equally fascinating." He adds that the question of a Maye contract extension should be an easy "yes," but the team lacks momentum entering the offseason. The real worry is who Maye will target as his top receiver if Brown cannot recover his form. The Patriots have seven games left on the schedule to figure out their identity, but the path to a Wild Card spot looks narrow with their current injuries.

The Patriots are up to 15th in the league in some defensive metrics, but the offense is in a free fall. As Brown said this summer about the up-tempo style, "It just throws the defense off a lot, you know, all phases." When asked if the team would pay Maye or find a new WR1, the answer remains unclear. "I don't want to get into that," Brown said.
