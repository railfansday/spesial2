Zach LaVine, Kings hope Darius Acuff Jr. won't be negatively impacted by losing

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Zach LaVine, Kings hope Darius Acuff Jr. won't be negatively impacted by losing originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SACRAMENTO – Zach LaVine has no illusions about what this season is about for the Kings.

The veteran guard, who became Sacramento's oldest player during the offseason, likely will be playing elsewhere after his massive $215 million contract expires this summer.

But despite that, the 31-year-old has maintained perspective that his team's season is all about rookie point guard Darius Acuff Jr. and the team's other impressionable young players that are going through a historically bad stretch to begin the season.

"We just don't want to let the younger dudes hang their head and get into these rhythms of, OK, this is OK and this is how we're going (to do this). It's bad," LaVine said Wednesday. "And that's not a way that you want to start your career or have that type of intensity. I think some of those dudes are keeping everybody positive in the gym, working for the next thing."

LaVine was the first member of the Kings to the postgame podium after Sacramento lost to the Phoenix Suns, 128-107, falling to 1-13 on the 2026-27 NBA season and tying the 1990-91 team for the worst start in franchise history.

Worse yet, the Kings dropped to 0-9 at home. Losing to Phoenix meant Sacramento lost every game of its seven-game home stand, the longest of the season.

And things won't get any easier. They travel to Los Angeles to take on the 13-3 Lakers on Friday before hosting the 11-4 Boston Celtics at Golden 1 Center on Saturday. The next five games are all against teams with winning records before the Kings welcome the struggling New Orleans Pelicans on Nov. 30 for the second night of a back-to-back.

"There's not a lot of positive things to say. I got to call a spade a spade. We're 1-13," LaVine said. "But besides us looking at film and looking at the good things we do, the general public isn't going to see that. They're not going to see the hard work or the things that we put into trying to win."

The Kings didn't come into the season thinking they would compete with the elite teams in the Western Conference and make a deep run into the NBA playoffs. They hoped they could be healthier than last season when lengthy absences from LaVine, Domantas Sabonis and Keegan Murray played a role in finishing just 22-60.

They hoped Doug Christie in his second full season would improve as head coach. They hoped Acuff would reinvigorate the franchise after the messy departure of former coach Mike Brown led to the eventual trade of centerpiece De'Aaron Fox in the deal that brought back LaVine.

But none of that has come to fruition, and Acuff's offensive play has been the only bright spot in an otherwise historically bad start to Sacramento's season.

Christie was asked about LaVine's comments and the organization's emphasis on setting the right foundation for Acuff and other young players to build on.

"To be honest, it's everything because that's what we're trying to build here. We're trying to build a sustainable winner," Christie said. "There are parts in what we're doing that are headed the right way. But then all those little things that I'm talking about, those are the things that you got to push aside and you got to stay consistent with it, and you got to make sure you're doing it on a day-to-day basis."

As repetitive as losing has become for the Kings, so has Christie's messaging. He has maintained an upbeat attitude despite all the losing while also admitting he believes he's always on the hot seat.

But it's hard to envision a scenario where Christie remains head coach much longer. The Kings current winning percentage of 0.71 percent would pace out to a 6-76 record, which would be the worst over an 82-game schedule in NBA history.

For Acuff's part, he's saying the right things and trying to maintain a positive attitude.

"You just got to keep high spirits," he said Wednesday after scoring a game-high 36 points, just two shy of his career best. "The key thing is just staying together, making sure we stay together through a tough time. That's the most important part."
