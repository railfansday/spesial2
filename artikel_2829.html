TCU Coaching Candidates 2026: Identifying Potential Targets for Horned Frogs Coaching Search

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

It’s time for another TCU Horned Frogs coaching search, with the program looking for someone to turn things around after a disappointing 2026 season. After head-coaching stability for more than two decades under Gary Patterson, the Horned Frogs will be looking for their third head football coach since 2022.

Let’s dive into our TCU coaching candidates to replace Sonny Dykes.

There’s a very real possibility that the TCU coaching search will focus on in-state options with head-coaching experience. Those are two things that should put Texas State Bobcats head coach G.J. Kinne near the top of the program’s list. Kinne bounced around as quarterbacks coach at Hawai’i (2020) and UCF (2021) before landing his first head-coaching job at Incarnate Word, immediately delivering a 12-2 record with a trip to the FCS Semifinals. That success landed him at Texas State, where he had a lot of work to do given the football program went a combined 13-35 in four seasons with Jake Spavital. Kinne immediately doubled the Bobcats’ win total year-to-year, and he’s won at least seven games in each of his four seasons at the helm with the offense ranking seventh in scoring (38.7 PPG) this season. Kinne’s roots are in Texas, and the combination of his track record of winning with two different programs paired with his offensive mind make him an ideal option for TCU.

If there’s a favorite to be the next TCU football coach, it might be Jeff Traylor. The 58-year-old started at the high school level, first as an assistant and then taking over as the head coach at Gilmer High School. The success there got him a job on the Texas Longhorns coaching staff (2015-16) coaching special teams, followed by stops at SMU (2017) and Arkansas (2018-19) on the offensive side of the ball. UTSA, having gone 7-17 in the final two years under Frank Wilson, took a chance on Traylor. The Roadrunners immediately took off with a seven-win season in Traylor’s first year as a head coach, followed by consecutive campaigns with double-digit wins. At UTSA, Traylor is on the verge of another 10-win campaign with a potential American Conference Championship title to go with it. He won state titles as a high school coach and has taken UTSA’s football program to new heights. At TCU, Traylor could eventually get the Horned Frogs into the College Football Playoff.

The primary focus with our TCU coaching candidates centers on those who have experience being a head coach. That’s partly due to the fact that there aren’t a ton of appealing coordinators right now who are worth taking a chance on. Miami Hurricanes play-caller Shannon Dawson is an exception. He’s helped elevate quarterback Darian Mensah into being a likely Heisman Trophy finalist in 2026, with the Hurricanes offense being one of the biggest reasons why they’ll make the playoff in consecutive seasons. Dawson also has some familiarity with coaching in Texas, having served on the Houston Cougars coaching staff from 2019-2022. Dawson would be a bit of a riskier hire, but there’s also the opportunity that the Horned Frogs strike gold with a brilliant mind who can lead a perennial Big 12 Conference contender.

Relationships matter in a coaching search. TCU athletic director Mike Buddie decided to make a head-coaching change just months after signing Dykes to a contract extension. Buddie was Army’s athletic director when Monken led the school to a 12-2 record. It’s fair to wonder in a situation like that if the person overseeing the Horned Frogs coaching search already has someone in mind. It’s been a disappointing 2026 season for the Army Black Knights, who could potentially fail to win six games for the first time since 2019. Monken, age 59, may now realize he’s taken this program as far as it can go given the constraints that come with being the head coach of an academy school. He’d have far more resources available to him at TCU than he ever did as head coach at either Army or Georgia Southern. Given he’ll be 60 years old in April, this hire would also fall in line with the Horned Frogs’ recent history with head coaches.

Zach Kittley had his work cut out for him when he agreed to become the head coach at Florida Atlantic University. The FAU Owls gave Willie Taggart a shot and bailed on that after three seasons (15-18 record) only for things to get even worse under Tom Herman (6-16). Kittey had to overhaul everything in 2025, leading to just a one-win improvement. This season has offered a far better glimpse of what he’s capable of when he brings in the players he wants and there starts to be cohesion in the system. FAU (7-4) has delivered its best record since the Lane Kiffin years, with the Owls’ passing game (326.7 pass ypg) leading to one of the highest-scoring offenses in the nation. If Kittley can turn Caden Veltkamp from a quarterback who threw 17 interceptions from 2024-25 into a highly-efficient passer (33-6 TD-INT and 70% completion rate), imagine what he could do with an even better quarterback and supporting cast at TCU.
