Mercedes-Benz is tossing its hat into the partial autonomy ring with the launch of two new features next year: hands-free highway driving and a point-to-point city navigation system. The new features will put the German automaker in competition with Tesla, which is currently leading the auto industry in the development of hands-free systems.

Mercedes’ point-to-point driver assist feature, called MB.Drive Assist Pro, will handle most of the driving on local roads and in dense, urban traffic. Once a destination is plugged into the vehicle’s navigation, the system assists across the entire route, handling highway exits, lane changes, intersections, and traffic lights. But unlike Tesla’s Full Self-Driving, MB.Drive Assist Pro requires that drivers keep their hands on the wheel while navigating through cities.

The system is powered by 27 sensors (10 cameras, 5 radars, 12 ultrasonic sensors) and a high-performance computer capable of 254 TOPS running on the new MB.OS architecture. That computer runs on Nvidia’s Drive AV stack, blending pure AI with a deterministic “Halos” safety guardrail layer. (We got to experience the point-to-point system as a passenger during a demonstration in San Francisco last year.)

The Hands-Free Highway Assist, meanwhile, works similarly to GM’s Super Cruise or Ford’s BlueCruise, in that drivers are able to take their hands off the steering wheel when driving on approved, controlled-access highways. The highway assist can also handle speeds of up to 85 mph, which is a big jump from Mercedes’ previous generation Drive Pilot’s 40 mph limit. It also includes Proactive Lane Change Assist, which automatically initiates lane changes to pass slower cars or follow navigation toward an exit.

Earlier this month, a number of journalists got to test out the new features in San Francisco. InsideEVs called it a “real challenger” to Tesla FSD, praising how confidently and predictably it handled double-parked trucks, construction zones, and pedestrians during a 25-minute downtown loop. The Drive noted that the system’s “confidence and fluidity” felt more assured than rival systems. And Verge contributor Tim Stevens (writing for Endgadget) said it handled jaywalkers, cyclists, and sudden U-turns “without issue” and took the stress out of city driving.

Mercedes will begin rolling out both features via an invite-only access program in California next month, ahead of a broader nationwide launch in 2027 — with highway assist arriving in the spring. Eligible vehicles include the CLA Electric, GLC Electric, GLE, GLS, and S-Class, with more vehicles to follow in 2028. While Mercedes hasn’t announced exact pricing yet, eligible owners will be able to unlock the software over the air through the automaker’s online store using one-month, one-year, or three-year subscription plans.

The launch of the new features comes after Mercedes paused development of a Level 3 system, in which drivers can take their eyes off the road, in addition to their hands off the wheel, during certain approved stretches of highway. By pivoting to hands-free highway driving and point-to-point city navigation, Mercedes is betting that its Nvidia-powered, Level 2 features will have more wide appeal — and less regulatory resistance.
