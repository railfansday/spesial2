Oracle CEO admits tech sector made a ‘classic mistake’ not engaging local communities on data centers but sees new strategy to win ‘hearts and minds’

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Oracle co-CEO Mike Sicilia says the tech industry made a “classic mistake” in how it sold data centers to the towns asked to host them: “flying people in to say, ‘I’m from HQ, and I’m here to help.’ That’s not how we’re gonna win hearts and minds.”

His comments, at the Fortune 500 Innovation Forum in Detroit on Monday, came two weeks after voters turned the midterms into a referendum on the AI buildout. For companies racing to build AI capacity, the bottleneck is now local permission as much as power or chips.

Tech and data-center companies aren’t used to having to make these arguments, he said. “That’s actually not surprising, given many tech company have traditionally been suppliers not operators in local communities,” the AI hyperscaler chief told Fortune‘s Jeremy Kahn.

Trane Technologies CEO Dave Regnery pointed out that rhetoric coming out of candidates during campaigns can be different after they get elected. In fact, Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has signaled she will not block data center projects that have already secured permits.

Regnery said educating the public is also key as there are some myths about data centers need to be debunked. The one that especially bothers him is the notion that data centers use too much water. But in reality, most use closed-loop systems, meaning water within a data center is recirculated.

As for the complaint that data centers suck up so much electricity that utilities are hiking rates for customers, Regnery noted that buildings waste about 30% of the energy they pay for, but Trane’s agentic AI controls could get them to run more efficiently.

“These are solutions that save our customers a whole bunch of money and by the way they’re very, very sustainable and lower the carbon footprint for the buildings,” he said.

Another factor in the messaging around data centers is showing the benefits of AI, and Sicilia said healthcare is probably the best place to demonstrate positive return on investment by showing off medical breakthroughs. Regnery echoed that point, saying AI will change how we live.

“If you talk to any of the drug companies, they will tell you we are so close to solving problems that we never thought would be solved in our lifetimes—never be solved—and we’re so close, in fact, in some cases they may already be solved,” he added.

But Sicilia acknowledged the tech sector has to adjust who is delivering its message, not just the message itself.

He cited a “classic mistake” that the industry made: “flying people in to say, ‘I’m from HQ, and I’m here to help.’ That’s not how we’re gonna win hearts and minds.”

A model the tech sector could look to is from finance and its practice of community banking, where regional managers in charge of lending are from that region, Sicilia argued.

“That’s the kind of thing I think we need to learn lessons from other industries is that we’re investing in heavily hiring folks who live, work, and operate in the community, and I think you’re going to see that shift,” he said.

Jason Ma is the weekend editor at Fortune, where he covers markets, the economy, finance, and housing.
