A JPMorgan Chase software bug slowed transactions, with delays spilling to other banks

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Chase said there is no evidence the issue was cyber-related and that customers kept access to their accounts and funds

Jamie Dimon, chief executive officer of JPMorgan Chase. (Eva Marie Uzcategui/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

JPMorgan $JPM Chase said a software bug in on-premise hardware systems slowed service for some customers on Monday. The bank said it was working to resolve the problem by the end of the day.

Chase said there is no evidence the issue was cyber-related. It said customers could still get into their accounts and reach their money, though service for some was slower than normal.

By Monday evening, a bank spokesperson said transactions were back to moving at their usual pace, according to Bloomberg.

Chase said the problem was not systemic. It said other banks may have been affected to the extent they were party to a transaction with Chase.

Thousands of customers said they could not easily get into their online accounts at a number of large American banks, as well as on the Zelle money transfer service, according to The Associated Press. Users were already flagging problems by around 11:30 a.m. ET, according to CNBC. Downdetector logged spikes in reports for Bank of America $BAC and Wells Fargo $WFC, according to Bloomberg.

The other banks described a narrower impact. A Bank of America spokesperson said its systems were operating as usual, according to Bloomberg. The Associated Press said the banking services at Bank of America and Citigroup $C were unaffected.

A U.S. Bank spokesperson said the bank was "not directly impacted" but that clients sending or receiving Zelle payments with another institution "may experience transaction delays," according to The Associated Press. A spokesperson for Early Warning Services, the owner of Zelle, said the network was up and running and that problems at banks could be holding up some payments.

Some Chase customers saw a message that read, "Some services aren't available. We're working on them. Please try back later," according to The Associated Press.

JPMorgan stock fell 2.3% to $338.21 on Monday. Bank of America stock fell 1.8%, and Wells Fargo stock fell 1.4%.
