Top 10 EFL clubs with the most fan arrests revealed

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The passion shown in English football is often seen as one of many aspects which make the country's pyramid so great.

Even if some elements of the matchday experience in the modern game are far less enjoyable than they once were due to costs or other factors, it is hard to argue against the fact that many fanbases up and down the country are amongst some of the most vociferous in Europe or, potentially, the world.

Many would also feel that the further down the pyramid you go to get your football fix on a Saturday afternoon or Tuesday night, the more passion you are likely to find, with results meaning more across the season due to the fact these divisions are not heavily reliant on cash windfalls in the first place.

That said, a recent study from the Home Office has detailed how many arrests were made among each of the fanbases relating to the sides currently in the Premier League, Championship, League One and League Two, as well as those who follow England.

Given the sheer number of local rivalries in the EFL right now, it may come as no surprise to some that specific clubs in the EFL rank high in these numbers, albeit, once, for the wrong reasons.

That said, FLW have looked at which clubs in the '72' have seen their fans arrested the most during the 2025/26 season, with football-related arrests and police actions increasing from 2,037 to 2,403.

Getting this list underway is the Bolton Wanderers fanbase.

It was a joyous 2025/26 campaign for Whites fans on the whole, as Steven Schumacher guided the club back to the Championship after a seven-year exile, emphatically seeing off Stockport County in May's League One play-off final at Wembley Stadium.

However, in terms of football-related arrests, there were 27 of the club's supporters who were arrested for football-related incidents last term.

It may come as a surprise to some that Wrexham's fanbase sit ninth on this particular ranking, with 28 of those associated with the North Wales club arrested last season for football-related disorder.

2025/26 was, of course, the Red Dragons' first season in the Championship since 1982 having achieved three successive promotions under Phil Parkinson beforehand.

As such, the spotlight has firmly increased on events at the Racecourse Ground, with the number of fans the club are able to attract also growing and resulting in major changes to the famous stadium's outlook.

20 of those aforementioned arrests were also in relation to public disorder crimes.

In eighth are the followers of Queens Park Rangers.

Although the 2025/26 season meant there were no West London derbies for the Loftus Road faithful to enjoy, there were, unfortunately, 30 arrests made by the authorities relating to football matches.

Six of those were also related to alcohol consumption, the joint-third highest number within said category alongside Chelsea, Manchester United and Manchester City.

The fourth Championship club in succession is up next in the form of Middlesbrough.

It is known that football is followed religiously by those in the North East, with Boro's fanbase certainly one of the most passionate in the second tier in recent times.

Kim Hellberg's side were agonisingly close to a Premier League return on the pitch, and whilst their fanbase continued to follow them in great numbers, 31 arrests were made by the police last season - 15 of which were for violent disorder.

The first League One fanbase to feature on this ranking is that of Bradford City.

Great strides of progress have certainly been made on the pitch at Valley Parade in recent times, and this has coincided with strong ticketing incentives provided by the Bantams hierarchy.

Those residing in this part of West Yorkshire are notorious for following the club irrespective of its fortunes, but that passion can sometimes boil over, as was the case in 2025/26, where 34 supporters were arrested.

In fifth on this list are Southampton, whose 2025/26 campaign was well-documented for several reasons.

Being a one-club city, those in this part of Hampshire haven't stopped their loyalties despite a recently unstable period in terms of relegations and promotions at St Mary's.

However, that didn't always see the fanbase represented in the greatest of lights, as 38 arrests were made involving Saints supporters last term.

West Ham United's fanbase is, unarguably, one of the most passionate in English football, and that has remained the case during an indifferent start to the club's first Championship season in 14 years.

Whilst Premier League away allocations are capped, this season has already seen the Hammers fans turn up to certain matches in their droves, although that will certainly come with a police presence after 44 supporters were arrested last season.

20 of those related to public disorder offences, whilst a further 10 were for the possession of Class-A drugs.

Birmingham City's fanbase is also known for being one of the most hostile you can find in English football, with the crowd at St. Andrew's @ Knighthead Park famed for getting under the skin of some of the greatest players of the Premier League's recent past.

Renovation work in this part of the West Midlands means that the B9 stadium is often full to the brim with home and away supporters, which only enhances the atmosphere throughout the 90 minutes.

Blues fans are also one of the loudest travelling contingents in the Championship, and therefore, it may come as little surprise to learn that 51 of the club's fans were arrested last season, with only 10 fanbases receiving higher numbers.

The majority of those arrests related to violent disorder, with a tally of 15 being the same as Middlesbrough and just one behind those who follow Manchester City.

The EFL club with the second-highest number of arrests last season were Portsmouth, with 56 in total.

John Mousinho's side are often backed in strong numbers away from home despite the long travelling distances required to do so, whilst Fratton Park has continuously retained its character in the modern era.

Last season was also the first time since 2012 in which Pompey had faced off against Southampton in the South Coast derby in a league capacity, which is always bound to have an effect on such figures.

However, those in this part of the country also saw the most arrests - 22 - for the possession of Class-A drugs.

However, topping the charts are Millwall's fanbase, with 64 Lions supporters arrested during what was the club's best season for 36 years.

Although the South London club have done their utmost to crack down on misdemeanours in the stands, there is always a strong tag which follows the fanbase around, whether that is unfair or fair in this day and age.

That said, Millwall's fans didn't top any of the categories analysed within the study, although the majority of said arrests - 28 - were in relation to public disorder.

Only Manchester United, Arsenal, Chelsea and Manchester City fans saw a greater number of arrests than Millwall last season, meaning that, had Alex Neil's side gone on to achieve promotion in 2025/26, it would have been intriguing to see the policing operations that would have likely been commissioned.
