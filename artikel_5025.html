14 Best Airbnbs in the South of France for Bucolic Romance (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The South of France has long been shorthand for bucolic romance. The region has drawn painters, film stars, and sun-starved travelers for generations, seducing them with endless vineyards and lavender fields, sleepy medieval villages, and its glittering Riviera. From Provence to the Côte d’Azur, the south of France offers a storybook mélange of rustic farmhouses, centuries-old castles and estates, along with honey-hued stone houses tucked into timeless hilltop towns.

Whether you fancy swimming in a pool besides your own château, late evening pétanque games at a centuries-old castle, gathering a group of friends at a hillside estate, or cozying up in a rustically romantic village apartment, Airbnb has options for turning every south of France fantasy into a reality. Here are some of the most extraordinary homes for rent in the region—spots so tempting, you might just want to cancel your return ticket and settle in for a year in Provence.

Perks: Mountain views, lake access, sauna, balcony, hot tub, indoor and outdoor fireplaces, pool table

While the South of France may be synonymous with summer, this contemporary chalet makes a compelling case for a winter escape with floor-to-ceiling windows that frame Sancy Valley’s mountain views. There’s plenty of room for up to 10 guests to spread out in this five bedroom home with a generous living space that includes three bathrooms and a pool table. Tucked in the hilly countryside, proximity to both Alpine and Nordic skiing is the property’s main draw for those looking to hit the slopes. After a day spent outside, both the indoor fireplace or sauna provide a perfect spot to warm up, though you could also enjoy the fresh air on the expansive balcony while keeping toasty by the outdoor fireplace or hot tub. From $1,048 per night.

Located at the foot of Signal du Mailhebiau in the Aubrac region, this two-bedroom getaway is immersed in its rural landscape, surrounded by rolling fields, grazing cattle, and the French Countryside’s natural beauty. Originally built as a buron, or shepherd’s hut, the home has been thoughtfully transformed into a modern retreat that celebrates its architectural history. Hand-laid stone walls, vaulted ceilings, and a loft preserve the structure’s rustic character, while a dramatic wall of windows introduces a contemporary counterpoint, framing picturesque views of the expansive countryside. From $484 per night.

Once the orangery of a stately chateau, this converted studio apartment retains its romantic design with soaring ceilings, a wall of French doors, and cheerful green-and-white checkerboard floor. Today, the space is an elegant retreat for two (or a couple with a baby thanks to the available crib), whether you’re spending leisurely afternoons indoors, sipping wine on the terrace, or exploring the surrounding vineyards and forests. For those looking to wander or partake in the region’s rich wine history, you’re about a 30-minute drive to the center of Bordeaux. From $165 per night.

Perks: Vineyard views, pool, outdoor shower, private backyard, fully-equipped kitchen, free parking, cleaning available for a fee

This isn’t so much a home to stay in as a castle to occupy. Situated on nearly ten acres of private parkland, this spacious 16th century château sleeps eight, while an additional two cottages can accommodate another eight guests. A large swimming pool, sun-drenched terrace, and lawn dotted with cypress trees practically beg you to throw a summer soirée. Inside, Château du Hameau’s historic bones remain prominent with original stone flooring, sweeping stairway, and ornate coiffured ceilings. With multiple fireplaces, soaking tubs, and seemingly endless views of fields and vineyards, this is a place to dream about long after you leave. From $1,825 per night.

Perks: Private tennis court, private pool, concierge service

Rustic architecture meets contemporary comfort at the sprawling Mas de l’Aiguebrun Estate, a super-sized stay with enough space to house up to 15 lucky guests. Old world design features like exposed brick and wood beam ceilings that date back to the 11th century contrast modern amenities like a La Cornue oven, waterfall showers, and even a mini rock climbing wall to entertain younger guests. The property has a prime location in Bonnieux, a village with an abundance of restaurants and cafes, as well as a Friday morning market that visitors swear by for local produce and baked goods. Back at the Airbnb, you’re encouraged to spend sunny days en plein air taking advantage of the picturesque outdoor amenities, which include an impressive stone-carved outdoor dining setup, 20-meter swimming pool, and a red clay tennis court to channel your inner Nadal. From $2,150 per night.

Perks: Private terrace, updated air conditioning, complimentary tea and coffee

Natural light floods this 16th century townhouse located in Saint-Paul-de-Vence, a village whose most famous residents include the likes of Pablo Picasso and Henri Matisse. Beautiful interiors are expected when your host is an interior designer, and this property delivers spatial excellence with considered details like a restrained color palette, layered textures, and contemporary furnishings. The combined kitchen and dining area is a perfect place to enjoy slow mornings (in fact, we think the envy-inducing pottery shelf is an idea you’ll want to recreate in your own space). A copper bath tub faces a king-sized bed in one of the main bedrooms, complete with a window view overlooking the surrounding foliage. For more private guests, the property also has three en suite bathrooms in a glassy green color palette, each housing their own tubs and showers. If you’re hoping to explore your charming provincial surroundings, lots of art and shopping attractions await; plus, the 40-minute drive to Nice makes it convenient to plan a day trip or two. From $649 per night.
