Why Compass is pulling its leaders from Realtor associations — and what it means for the industry

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Compass adopted a policy blocking salaried employees and brokerage managers from holding Realtor association leadership roles. Analysts say the move reduces conflicts and legal exposure as Compass sues 15 MLSs, many owned by Realtor associations.  AI Summary

Compass International Holdings’ adoption of a new policy preventing salaried employees and brokerage managers from holding leadership roles at Realtor associations, makes a lot of sense in light of the firm’s recent announcement that it has filed lawsuits against 15 MLSs, many of which are owned by Realtor associations.

“They are suing MLSs, they are being sued by MLSs, so from that standpoint it makes sense,” Amit Kulkarni, a co-founder of Alloy Advisors, said. “They probably don’t want any of their employees on the board of an entity that is suing them or that they are suing.”

Craig McClelland, a partner at McClelland and Hahn Consulting, said he would also be wary of conflict of interest issues.  “If I was in a bunch of lawsuits and my staff were part of a lobbying organization that I was involved in a lawsuit with and they could potentially be in confidential meetings with confidential information they could share, that could be an issue,” McClelland said. “You are suing them, yet you sit on their board — that is probably problematic, so they need to create some level of separation.”

Kulkarni’s Alloy Advisors co-founder Russ Cofano, who is an attorney, shares a similar view, but with a broader legal lens.

“Compass restricting employees from participating in Realtor association leadership roles, from a purely prophylactic standpoint, in terms of risk management, makes sense,” Cofano said. “The argument that has been used is that if you are from one of these big companies in Realtor association leadership, you are part of a federal antitrust conspiracy because you are part of the decision-marking apparatus that created these allegedly illegal rules and procedures, so your company then gets roped into antitrust complaints.”

In its lawsuits against MLSs, Compass International Holdings and its CEO Robert Reffkin have brought up the fact that they believe MLS and Realtor association boards are anticompetitive as they are made of boards of Compass’s competitors. In public statements, Reffkin has claimed that MLSs “abuse their power by creating mandatory rules,” and that the MLS has been “weaponized” against agents, as it fines them and punishes them if they “compete too hard.”

While McClelland, doesn’t necessarily share Compass’s view point on this, like Cofano, he sees the possibility of legal risk for companies who have employees in Realtor association leadership roles.

“I think it’s actually good business etiquette,” McClelland said. “Employees probably shouldn’t be sitting on Realtor association boards.

Still, Kulkarni finds it surprising that a company that, he says, is so clearly looking to reshape how organized real estate transacts business, would prevent its employees from being involved in an organization that is making the decisions regarding how the industry operates.

McClelland, however, sees things differently.

“I don’t think it is good to have your people sitting on boards voting on policies and rules when you are actively in a dispute about that organization’s policies and rules. That is a big problem,” McClelland said.

But beyond the confines of Realtor association or MLS policies, Kulkarni also views this as Compass stepping away from the National Association of Realtors’ (NAR) regulatory and legislative advocacy efforts.

“Whether you like it or not, NAR is still a big voice in how policy decisions are made and how this industry moves forward, and they [Compass] are basically saying we don’t want to participate in that.”

Overall, however, Kulkarni says this is just another example of Compass working on restricting access to inventory, as he believes control over inventory is how the company is looking to generate more revenue, grow its margins and keep shareholders happy. However, Reffkin says it’s also about seller choice.

“The entire point of existence for Compass’s board is to increase shareholder value,” Kulkarni said. “Nohting in there is about increasing satisfaction for a buyer or seller or making sure they get the best deal. To me, that is one of the biggest challenges that this industry is facing right now. Do we want to be an industry that increases shareholder value at the expense of actually doing what is right by the American public?” After all, he says, these “are the people that pay the freight for all of these people that are trying to increase shareholder value. Or, do we do the right thing by the consumers?”

In addition to this tension, Compass is also seeking to reshape how homes are bought and sold by publicly marketing listings without inputting them into the MLS. In order to do this, Cofano said, they need to cut NAR out of the equation.

“They need to push NAR out of this functional tension, which, in previous years, NAR has been a huge player and they need to say, you’re no longer at the table,” Cofano said.

Kulkarni agrees, but so far, he doesn’t feel Compass has been very effective in its efforts thus far.  “To me, it feels like they are starting to throw a bit of a tantrum because they are not getting their way and saying, ‘we aren’t going to participate because we aren’t getting our way,’” he said.

Despite this, Kulkarni does understand why Compass and other brokerages would push back against Realtor associations and MLSs.

“NAR and the MLSs control the apparatus of how things get done, yet the brokers have to foot the bill for how they get done,” Kulkarni said. “So, when you have people who don’t actually have any skin in the game telling you how you can and can’t do business, then as a business owner it makes sense [that you would] pushback and Compass has just been the more forceful and vocal one.”

As to whether we may see other companies follow suit and remove employees from Realtor association leadership roles, industry analysts are unsure.

“I think they should decide if this is the best use of time for their staff to be on these boards and committees,” McClelland said. “Is it aligned with their business model? You have to make a good business decision on if that is the right thing for your company to do or not.”
