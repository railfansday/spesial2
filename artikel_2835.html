Alisson Screams at Jacquet After the Wall Gap That Let Greenwood Score

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Alisson, 34, was furious with Liverpool teammate Jeremy Jacquet after Mason Greenwood's free kick deflected off Jacquet's midriff and wrong-footed him, the Mirror reported.

Ally McCoist and Karen Carney said the goalkeeper was right to be angry, and Steve McManaman called it an awful goal.

Fenerbahce came from behind twice to beat Liverpool 3-2 in Istanbul.

Alisson rarely loses his composure. On Wednesday night in Istanbul he did, and Jeremy Jacquet was on the receiving end. Fenerbahce had just equalised through a Mason Greenwood free kick that took a heavy deflection off Jacquet, and the goalkeeper let his teammate know at once. The 34-year-old screamed at his centre-back and waved his arms in his direction. Jacquet, 21, appeared to realise his mistake and lifted a hand to cover his eyes.

Dominik Szoboszlai fouled a Fenerbahce player to give Greenwood the set piece. Jacquet stood at the end of the Liverpool wall and turned his body away as Greenwood struck it, which left a gap. Greenwood's shot looked to be heading wide until it hit Jacquet's midriff and went through the space. Alisson had been moving to his left, so the change of direction wrong-footed him and the ball went the other way on the wet surface. He threw his arms out at Jacquet.

The goal cancelled out Trey Nyoni's opener. Alexander Isak then came off the bench to put Liverpool 2-1 up, part of Liverpool's new attack this season. Fenerbahce hit back with goals from Archie Brown and Vedat Muriqi within six minutes and held on in the pouring rain.

Ally McCoist was on commentary for TNT Sports and had his verdict ready as the goal went in. "He gets a break, doesn't he? I think the goalkeeper will not be too happy with his wall," McCoist said on TNT Sports. "Does somebody turn their back on it?"

McCoist went on. "It's the perfect answer. It's Jacquet and he's livid, does he turn his back? He certainly does, you've got to face it."

"The goalkeeper is livid, absolutely livid and with justification as well. He's (Jacquet) is side on for someone who has had a good start to the season."

Karen Carney took the same view when she analysed the incident at half-time. "Alisson was fuming. Rightly so. You can't turn Jacquet. I don't even know if it was going in."

Ex-Liverpool player Steve McManaman was just as critical. "That's self-inflicted; that's an awful goal," he said.

"A big gap in the wall. It bounces off the big, tough centre-half turning his back. It should never ever happen. That's a poor goal."

Jacquet has made a positive start to his Liverpool career. He joined from Rennes in the summer and has replaced Ibrahima Konate, who left on a free transfer and signed for Real Madrid. Jacquet has partnered Virgil van Dijk in almost every game this season for manager Andoni Iraola, whom Van Dijk and Wirtz have backed as Liverpool adjust.

Jamie Carragher had already praised him. "I am getting a bit carried away with him? I don't know and I apologise to viewers at home. He's really impressed," Carragher said in Liverpool's win over Bournemouth.

"He's young, he will have bad games and he will make mistakes, of course that is the nature of young centre-backs.

"But he has played very well and he also looks the part. You know when you just look at a player and think he looks like a centre-back, he looks like a good player.

"It doesn't always work and I certainly wouldn't class myself as looking the part when I played."

The wall was not McCoist's only complaint on the night. About 20 minutes in, Greenwood was played through on goal and Kostas Tsimikas shoved him, knocking him off balance before he could shoot. Penalty claims from Greenwood and the home support were waved away and VAR did not review it. McCoist watched the replays and was adamant. "It's a penalty, it's two hands, not even one hand. That prevents him for me getting a shot on goal. Both hands. I think it's a penalty, I really do. It's a shame because it's a great ball and an exquisite touch."

He was just as sure that Tsimikas had got away with one. "I think so (got away with one), I really think so. He pushes him both hands when he's in the process of having an attempt on goal just because it doesn't look like it, doesn't make it not a penalty."

Liverpool went straight up the other end and Nyoni scored with a deflected effort that further angered Fenerbahce.

Liverpool left Istanbul beaten after leading twice, while Dirk Kuyt, the Liverpool great now in interim charge of Fenerbahce, celebrated a famous win. Jacquet is still only 21, and Carragher's point stands: young centre-backs make mistakes.
