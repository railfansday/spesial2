How ATS Parses Location, Remote & Relocation

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

A recruiter filters candidates to "Remote — US only" or "Austin, TX / open to relocate." Your header says "Karachi | Open to opportunities." Depending on how Greenhouse, Workday, Lever, iCIMS or Taleo tokenizes that line, you either land in the shortlist or get screened out before a human reads a single bullet. Location is not free text to most ATS systems. It is a set of fields: city, state/region, country, and often separate flags for remote and relocation.

I build Smart Resume Analyzer, an ATS resume checker, and I have spent nine years as a Principal Software Engineer in Karachi writing search and matching code in PHP, Laravel and Node. I wrote this with help from AI tools and checked every command and claim myself. This piece covers how location strings get parsed, what "Remote" and "open to relocate" actually do in common systems, and how to rewrite your contact header so both the parser and the recruiter see the same intent.

Direct answer: Most ATS parsers pull a city, state/region and country from your contact header into structured fields, then store "Remote", hybrid and "open to relocate" as separate preference flags or free-text notes. Write a plain city–state–country line, put remote or relocation preferences in clear words next to it, and avoid graphics, tables or multi-city laundry lists that confuse field extraction.

The structured fields power filters ("show me candidates in Texas"). The raw text still powers keyword search ("open to relocate"). You need both to be correct.

Location follows the same rule as how ATS parses your resume contact header: plain text near the top, one idea per line, no icons.

Vendors differ, and each employer's configuration differs more. Treat this as a safe operating assumption, not a claim about one customer's setup.

These products extract tokens and fill fields; they do not "understand" clever one-liners. Graphic banners and two-column headers with location on the right drop extraction quality—the same failure mode as hidden characters that break ATS keyword matching.

Recruiters filter three axes separately: where you live now, work arrangement (remote / hybrid / onsite), and relocation willingness. Collapsing them into "Remote / Open" hurts you. A US employer filtering "Austin metro + open to relocate" will miss a header that only says "Remote." Remote companies may still need your city for tax reasons. Put the city first, then preferences.
