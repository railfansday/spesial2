SEC is proposing to cut required executive pay disclosures to just CEO and CFO in most cases

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The proposal would also raise the minimum reporting threshold for executive perks to $150,000 from $10,000

The Securities and Exchange Commission on Friday proposed rule changes that would limit executive pay disclosures to a company's principal executive officer and principal financial officer in most circumstances.

The change would cut the number of named executive officers to two, from five or more under the current rule, the SEC said. The agency said those two positions carry the compensation most material to investors. The current rule covers the chief executive officer, the chief financial officer and the three other most highly paid executives, according to Bloomberg.

The proposal would raise the aggregate reporting threshold for perquisites to $150,000 from $10,000 and add a de minimis tracking exclusion. Perks include protective services and trips taken for personal purposes, according to Bloomberg. Companies would also gain flexibility to decide whether a benefit counts as a perquisite, as long as the decision is reasonable and made in good faith.

Three new tables, with accompanying narratives, would replace the four primary compensation tables. They are the Earned Compensation Table, the Granted Compensation Table and the Status of Equity Awards Table. The Compensation Discussion and Analysis section would cover three topics, and the list of 15 example topics in the current rule would be eliminated.

The SEC would simplify pay-versus-performance disclosure by reducing the required performance measures. It would replace the "Compensation Actually Paid" calculation with the total compensation earned amount from the proposed Earned Compensation Table. The agency said the change would still satisfy the Dodd-Frank Act mandate.

Under the pay ratio rule, the "median employee" determination would cover only U.S. full-time employees and would be required every five years. The proposal would also eliminate required check boxes on annual report cover pages and some disclosures about the application of clawback policies. The mechanics of a company's clawback process would not change.

"These amendments reflect a comprehensive reform of Item 402 that, together with other previously proposed actions, are central tenets of my agenda and focus on transforming the SEC's public company disclosure regime to one rooted in materiality," SEC Chairman Paul S. Atkins said in a statement. He said the proposal draws on more than 80 comment letters tied to the SEC's July 2025 roundtable on executive compensation.

The rules were last comprehensively reformed in 2006, the SEC said. The public comment period will stay open for 60 days after the proposing release is published in the Federal Register.

A final rule would need approval in a commission vote before taking effect, according to Bloomberg. The rulemaking process has historically taken 12 to 18 months, Bloomberg said.
