2027 COLA: 10 facts on SSI, Social Security and notices from the SSA release

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

The 2027 Social Security COLA is 3.6%, adding $75 a month to the average retirement check, and these 10 details explain who gets it, when and how notices arrive

A 3.6 percent raise is coming to Social Security and Supplemental Security Income (SSI) checks. The Social Security Administration (SSA) puts the average retirement benefit increase at $75 per month. The agency announced the 2027 cost-of-living adjustment on October 14, 2026. The change reaches two groups on two different schedules.

About 7.3 million people who receive SSI will see higher payments starting December 31, 2026. Nearly 72 million Social Security beneficiaries will get the increase starting in January 2027. Some people collect both types of payments. Those two groups overlap rather than adding up to a single total.

The 2027 Social Security COLA follows a formula written into the Social Security Act. The yearly adjustment tracks the rise in the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers, known as the CPI-W. The Bureau of Labor Statistics, part of the Department of Labor, determines that index. The COLA applies to Old-Age, Survivors, and Disability Insurance as well as to SSI.

The announcement also explains how beneficiaries learn their new amounts. The COLA notice is a simplified one-page document written in plain, personalized language. It lists exact dates and dollar amounts for the new benefit and any deductions. People with a personal my Social Security account can read that notice online starting in late November. That is up to three weeks before a paper copy would arrive. Anyone without an account has until November 18 to create one and opt into paperless notices. People who have not signed up for paperless notices will receive the notice by mail in December.

Medicare enrollees will find their 2027 premium through the same channels. The release does not state the premium amount. Medicare's 2027 changes will be posted on Medicare's official website.

The 10 slides below walk through specific numbers and dates from the SSA's October 14 release. They are a selection drawn from one announcement, not a complete list of every 2027 Social Security change. The release does not address the paycheck side of the program. It gives no 2027 payroll tax rate, taxable earnings cap or withholding figures. Those numbers do not appear here. Each slide explains one item from the release and what it means for people who receive benefits.

Social Security and Supplemental Security Income payments will rise 3.6 percent in 2027. The Social Security Administration set one cost-of-living adjustment rate for both of its main payment programs.

The first program is Old-Age, Survivors, and Disability Insurance, known as OASDI. It is the formal name for what most people call Social Security. It pays monthly benefits to retired workers and to the surviving family members of workers who have died. It also pays workers who can no longer work because of a disability, along with certain of their dependents.

The second program is Supplemental Security Income, or SSI. It is a separate, needs-based program that the same agency runs. SSI pays monthly amounts to people with limited income and resources who are 65 or older, blind or disabled. Eligibility does not depend on a person's work record.

The two programs are also funded differently. OASDI benefits are paid for through Social Security payroll taxes. SSI is paid from general U.S. Treasury revenues. Despite those differences in purpose and funding, the 2027 adjustment treats them the same way.

The COLA is a percentage, not a flat dollar amount. The size of each person's raise depends on the size of the payment it is applied to. A larger monthly benefit gains more in dollars than a smaller one at the same rate. In proportional terms, though, every recipient moves up by the same share. A retired worker, a surviving spouse, a disability beneficiary and an SSI recipient all see their payment grow by an identical 3.6 percent.

The raised amount also becomes the new base. Any later cost-of-living adjustment is applied to the higher 2027 payment rather than to the old one. That is how annual adjustments build on one another over time.

Beneficiaries do not need to apply for the adjustment. It is added to existing payments automatically.

The average Social Security retirement benefit will rise by $75 a month in 2027, according to the Social Security Administration. That raise is the dollar result of the 3.6 percent cost-of-living adjustment the agency announced for the year.

The $75 figure is an average across retirement benefits. It is not a flat amount added to every check.

The COLA works as a percentage. The agency applies it to each retiree's own monthly benefit. A retiree with a larger benefit gets a larger dollar raise. A retiree with a smaller benefit gets a smaller one. The $75 average falls within that spread. An individual retiree's raise may land above or below it.

Retirees who collect Social Security will see the higher payments starting in January 2027.

The $75 figure covers retirement benefits only. The 3.6 percent adjustment also applies to survivor and disability benefits under Old-Age, Survivors, and Disability Insurance, and to Supplemental Security Income. The agency's average-increase figure in this announcement is for retirees. The release gives no comparable dollar average for disability, survivor or SSI payments.

The $75 average describes the change in the benefit before any deductions. The amount deposited in a bank account can differ. Some retirees have deductions taken from their monthly benefit, such as Medicare premiums. The release does not state the 2027 premium amount. The net change for those retirees cannot be read from the average.

Each beneficiary's 2027 COLA notice shows the new benefit and any deductions in exact dollars. That lets a retiree see a personal figure rather than the national average.

The agency's October 14, 2026, announcement gives the size of the average monthly increase. It does not give the dollar value of the new average retirement benefit.

Nearly 72 million people who collect Social Security benefits will receive the 3.6 percent cost-of-living adjustment starting in January 2027, according to the Social Security Administration. That count refers to Social Security beneficiaries only. The agency reports it separately from the Supplemental Security Income caseload.

The January 2027 start date applies to this group. SSI recipients follow a different calendar. Their higher payments begin before the new year.

Social Security Commissioner Frank J. Bisignano cited a different number when he described the adjustment. "Social Security will implement this cost-of-living adjustment for 76 million Americans, giving them greater financial stability," he said.

The agency's announcement does not explain how the commissioner's 76 million figure relates to its count of nearly 72 million Social Security beneficiaries. The nearly 72 million figure is the agency's own tally of beneficiaries getting the raise in January. The 76 million figure appears only in Bisignano's statement. The release offers no reconciliation of the two numbers.

Bisignano also commented on the agency's service. "Under President Donald J. Trump, Social Security is delivering better, faster, higher-quality service for the American people," he said.

The release gives the head count and the start month for this group. It does not break the nearly 72 million down by benefit type, age or state. It also does not say how many of these beneficiaries receive SSI as well.

About 7.3 million people who receive Supplemental Security Income will get higher payments starting December 31, 2026, according to the Social Security Administration. That date falls in 2026. SSI recipients therefore see the 2027 adjustment before the year it is named for begins.

The higher SSI payments come from the 3.6 percent cost-of-living adjustment the agency announced for 2027. SSI recipients receive the same percentage increase as Social Security beneficiaries.

Social Security beneficiaries follow a later schedule. Their increase begins in January 2027.

The 7.3 million figure counts SSI recipients alone. Some of those people also collect Social Security benefits. The agency's SSI count and its Social Security count overlap and cannot be combined into one head count.

The announcement does not give a new dollar amount for SSI payments. It also does not explain why the SSI increase starts on December 31 rather than in January.

Some Americans collect both Social Security benefits and Supplemental Security Income (SSI). For them, the 3.6 percent cost-of-living adjustment for 2027 arrives on two dates, one for each program.

The SSI portion comes first. Higher SSI payments begin on December 31, 2026, according to the Social Security Administration.

The Social Security portion follows in January 2027. That is when the adjustment takes effect for Old-Age, Survivors, and Disability Insurance benefits.

The percentage is the same for both programs. Only the start date differs. A person enrolled in both programs gets the raise in two steps rather than one.

The overlap also affects how to read the agency's head counts. The SSA puts the number of Social Security beneficiaries receiving the COLA at nearly 72 million. It puts the number of SSI recipients at about 7.3 million.

Some people appear in both groups. Adding the two figures together would count those people twice. The sum would overstate the number of individuals who get a raise.

The announcement does not say how many people receive both benefits. It also does not describe the combined dollar increase for a typical person enrolled in both programs.

Social Security's 3.6 percent cost-of-living adjustment for 2027 is the product of a rule in federal law. The Social Security Act ties each year's COLA to the rise in the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers, or CPI-W.

Two federal agencies handle different parts of that process. The Bureau of Labor Statistics, which sits within the Department of Labor, determines the CPI-W. The Social Security Administration does not produce the index. It applies the statutory formula to the increase the bureau measures.

That design links the size of each year's raise to a published government price measure. The resulting increase applies to Old-Age, Survivors, and Disability Insurance benefits as well as to Supplemental Security Income payments.

The CPI-W tracks prices for a defined segment of the workforce. The Bureau of Labor Statistics lists the qualifying occupations as clerical work, sales, protective and other service work, labor, and construction work. Those categories determine whose spending the index is built around.

The bureau publishes more than one consumer price index. Most other federal cost-of-living adjustments rely on a different measure, the CPI-U. That includes the indexing of federal income tax brackets. Social Security's cost-of-living adjustments are the ones figured with the CPI-W.

The Social Security Administration's announcement does not list the CPI-W readings behind the 3.6 percent figure. The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes the CPI-W data itself.

A personal my Social Security account lets beneficiaries read their 2027 cost-of-living adjustment notice online starting in late November. That is up to three weeks before a paper copy would arrive in the mail, according to the Social Security Administration.

The online version is the same notice that explains how the 3.6 percent COLA for 2027 changes each recipient's payment. Reading it in late November shows the new amount before the higher payments begin.

Each personal my Social Security account includes a secure Message Center that works as an inbox for agency notices. Users can choose to receive two notices there electronically. One is the yearly cost-of-living adjustment notice. The other is the income-related monthly adjustment amount notice.

Account holders do not have to keep logging in to see whether the notice has been posted. They can sign up for text or email alerts instead. An alert goes out each time a new message appears in the Message Center. The COLA notice counts as one of those messages.

Switching to online notices does not end paper mail on its own. Account holders who do not decline the mailed version of a notice that is also available online get both a paper copy and an electronic one. Stopping the paper copy means declining the mailed version. For beneficiaries who still rely on paper, the agency advises keeping the mailing address it has on file up to date.

The account handles more than notices. Holders can use it to apply for retirement, spouse or disability benefits. They can also apply for Medicare, check the status of an application and request a new Social Security number card.

Commissioner Frank J. Bisignano described the agency's service more broadly when announcing the 2027 COLA. "Under President Donald J. Trump, Social Security is delivering better, faster, higher-quality service for the American people," he said.

People who want to read their 2027 cost-of-living adjustment notice online must create a my Social Security account and opt into paperless notices by November 18. The Social Security Administration set that cutoff in its announcement of the 2027 adjustment.

Sign-up takes place at www.ssa.gov/myaccount.

Meeting the deadline involves two separate steps. The first is opening a personal my Social Security account. The second is choosing to receive notices electronically rather than through the mail. The agency lists both steps as conditions for seeing the notice online.

The cutoff decides how a beneficiary first sees the new benefit figures. People enrolled in time can open the notice in the Message Center of their account. Those who are not signed up for paperless notices get the COLA notice by mail in December.

Having an account does not by itself end paper copies. Account holders who do not decline mailed versions of notices that can also be viewed online receive paper notices along with the electronic ones.

The Message Center carries more than the yearly COLA notice. Account holders can also elect to get the income-related monthly adjustment amount notice there electronically.

The account opened to meet the deadline has other uses as well. Holders can apply for retirement, spouse or disability benefits, and they can apply for Medicare. They can also look up the status of an application and request a new Social Security number card.

The 2027 cost-of-living adjustment notice is a simplified, one-page document written in plain, personalized language. It gives exact dates and dollar amounts for each recipient's new benefit, along with any deductions taken from it.

Everything a beneficiary needs to check sits on a single sheet. The notice is personalized, so the amounts printed on it belong to that recipient alone. They are not an average or a sample case. The notice, not an agency-wide estimate, shows what one person will receive.

The notice covers deductions as well as the benefit itself. A reader sees the new payment and anything subtracted from it in the same place. Seeing both together shows the net amount that will be paid. The dates on the page mark when each new amount takes effect.

Paper copies go out in December. Beneficiaries who have not signed up for paperless notices receive the COLA notice in the mail that month, according to the Social Security Administration.

Mailed statements depend on an accurate address. The agency advises people who will get the paper version to keep the address it has on file up to date, according to CNBC.

The online version lives in a secure Message Center inside each personal my Social Security account. There, a user can elect to receive the yearly COLA notice electronically. The same option covers the income-related monthly adjustment amount notice.

Opening an online account does not by itself stop paper delivery. Account holders who do not decline mailed versions of notices that can also be viewed online receive paper notices as well as online ones. Those people get the same one-page notice in two places.

Medicare enrollees can check their 2027 premium in the Message Center of their my Social Security account starting in late November, according to the Social Security Administration. Enrollees who have not opted into online messages will find the figure in the cost-of-living adjustment notice mailed in December.

The agency's announcement does not state the premium amount. It names the two places where each enrollee's own figure will appear.

The two channels run on different calendars. An enrollee who reads messages online can see the premium figure in late November. An enrollee who relies on mail waits for the December notice.

Details on Medicare's broader 2027 changes will be posted at www.medicare.gov. The Social Security channels carry each enrollee's individual premium. The Medicare website is where the program's wider changes for the year will appear.
