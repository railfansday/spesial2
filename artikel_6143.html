A mid-air brawl broke out on a recent Qatar Airways flight from Doha (DOH) to Chicago (ORD) on Monday, which prompted the airline to request that the police and FBI agents respond to the aircraft upon its arrival. The flight, operating as QR725, takes 14 hours from the Qatari capital to Chicago.

According to AirLive, the passenger who was seated in row 32 allegedly attacked another passenger in row 31. Multiple passengers witnessed the altercation. This forced the crew and other passengers to step in and intervene, and the pilot contacted air traffic control in Chicago prior to landing, requesting police presence at the gate.

According to Simple Flying's Flight Tracker, the Boeing 777-300ER had departed from Doha on Monday, November 16 at 8:08 AM, some 28 minutes behind the scheduled departure time of 7:40 AM. Flying towards the US, the service was expected to arrive in Chicago at 2:30 PM that afternoon. It remains unclear at what exact point the two passengers had an altercation onboard; however, the situation was not treated as life-threatening, which would have forced an emergency diversion at the discretion of the captain, or it occurred in proximity to its final destination.

Touching down in Chicago at 1:46 PM after the 14-hour and 38-minute trip, the police and FBI met the aircraft at the gate, and the suspect was taken into custody for further questioning. Witness statements were also taken from key witnesses with information on the incident.

Simple Flying reached out to Qatar Airways on Monday evening for comment; however, an airline spokesperson was not immediately available.

The widebody jet belonging to the oneworld alliance carrier landed into Chicago on runway 28C. Following communication with the air traffic controller, the aircraft made its way promptly to Terminal 5 and the gate for authorities to board.

Qatar's 777 jet carried registration A7-BAC, which is approximately 18.4 years of age and has operated for the airline since August 2008. It is up to the captain to decide whether unruly or fighting passengers would pose an immediate threat to others onboard, including crew or passengers, along with the safety of the plane itself. Depending on the severity, the captain may decide that the plane must divert to the nearest available airport, allowing local law enforcement to board and remove the disruptive individuals.

Should a flight be forced to divert, the passengers can face bills due to the diversion or extra landing fees, additional fuel used, and rebooking costs for all passengers. Airlines also have the power to permanently ban passengers who engage in physical fights. It remains unclear if the passenger in this incident will be held responsible; Simple Flying has requested clarification on this matter directly from Qatar Airways.

Fights onboard aircraft are more common than you may think. Travel Noire reported that a fight broke out on a flight from Bogota (BOG), where a scuffle erupted between two passengers after one attempted to move the other's hand luggage in the overhead bin. This resulted in the crew needing to separate the two passengers for the remainder of the flight.

Also this month, a domestic Qantas Airways flight from Perth (PER) to Melbourne (MEL) experienced a passenger turn aggressive when they were asked to move from the exit row. As reported by 7News, the individual shoved the flight attendant with both hands before being eventually restrained and arrested by federal police and taken in for questioning.
