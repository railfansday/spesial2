Runway stretches to 2027 as oncology vaccine focus sharpens.

Evaxion (EVAX -9.45%) management announced a strategic refocus to prioritize the AI-Immunology platform toward oncology programs, specifically advancing its off-the-shelf and personalized cancer vaccines. The company reported the extension of its cash runway into the end of 2027, supported by disciplined resource allocation and at-the-market equity sales. Clinical validation for the platform was highlighted through three-year phase II data for the EVX-01 vaccine, showing high target precision and durable immune responses in metastatic melanoma. Management indicated that future development of infectious disease and autoimmune assets will be conducted exclusively through external partnerships to maximize the value of internal resources.

Birgitte Rønø: Thank you for that, Helen. Our priorities are, as mentioned, to advance our off-the-shelf cancer vaccines, EVX-04 and EVX-05, toward clinical development, and further to expand EVX-01's potential across indications through partnerships, and to continue mining the dark genome for cancer vaccine antigens. The scientific update that follows show the evidence behind this focus and how we are translating our AI platform's capability into oncology product opportunities. Let me start with the rationale behind personalized cancer vaccines.

Personalized cancer vaccines targets neoantigens. These are sequences that are arising from cancer-specific mutations, and they're found exclusively in the tumor, but absent in normal tissue, making them highly attractive therapeutic targets. The challenge lies in identifying which of the patient's neoantigens that will generate a tumor-targeting immune response. AI-Immunology selects the most promising neoantigens by modeling the steps from cancer-specific mutations through expression, translation, presentation on the MHC molecules, and T-cell recognition. The AI-Immunology platform sits within a broader patient-to-vaccine process. For each patient, tumor and blood samples are collected, and they undergo DNA and RNA sequencing. AI-Immunology then, based on this information, predicts the patient-specific neoantigens in 24 hours, informing the design of the personalized EVX-01 vaccine.

Following manufacture, the vaccine is then administered in combination with checkpoint inhibitors. The total turnaround time from biopsy to vaccine administration is approximately seven weeks. In our EVX-01 phase II study, we reported an 89% precision in predicting immunogenic neoantigens. I'll now turn to the clinical results and the immune response data behind this record-high precision. In our EVX-01 phase II study in advanced melanoma, the two-year results showed an objective response rate of 75% and a complete response of 25%. We also saw improvement in the response from a week 12 assessment following initiation of EVX-01 immunization.

Seven out of 13 patients who had stable disease or a partial response after 12 weeks of anti-PD-1 therapy improved their response, corresponding to 54% following EVX-01 administration. Also of importance is that EVX-01-related adverse events were limited to grade 1 and grade 2. The one-year extension provided longer follow-up in a smaller group of patients, and the clinical responses in all six patients who participated in this extension with EVX-01 monotherapy lasted until the end of the trial. Overall, these are very encouraging clinical results that demonstrated clinical benefit of EVX-01 in combination with anti-PD-1 therapy in advanced melanoma. Turning to immunogenicity, EVX-01 induces potent vaccine-specific T-cell responses against the vaccine neoantigens.

The key point is that the platform can identify antigens recognized by the immune system. EVX-01-induced vaccine-specific T-cell responses that are sustained throughout the three-year period, including in the absence of anti-PD-1 therapy, which is provided only or was provided only during the first two years. As mentioned, we were able to demonstrate a record-high vaccine target precision of 89%. That is a clear testament to the predictive power of our AI-Immunology platform. Additionally, 86% of the assessed neoantigen responses were de novo, in other words, newly induced responses rather than preexisting responses. This is important as therapy-induced new T-cells have been correlated to clinical benefit.

Let's look more closely at the phenotype of the T-cell responses and how the strength of the EVX-01-induced T-cell responses relates to AI predictions. First, EVX-01 induces both CD4+ and CD8+ antigen-specific T-cell responses. In all evaluated patients, we were able to demonstrate a CD4+ response, and in nine out of the 15 patients, we were also able to detect CD8+ responses. We are still evaluating samples from the study, so stay tuned. More immune data and biomarker data will be shared in the next year. Secondly, we were able to demonstrate a positive correlation between the AI prediction score assigned to these single neoantigens, and the magnitude of the observed immune response.

Higher prediction score also were associated with a stronger immune response. This is an important link between the platform's predictions and the biological responses measured in the patients. With that clinical and translational foundation, let's look at how AI-Immunology can be applied more broadly. The broader opportunity is to use AI-Immunology across antigen sources, cancer indications, and vaccine approaches. The platform processes complex patient data, including genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic data, to identify and prioritize cancer antigens. As shown here, our work spans neoantigens and endogenous retrovirus, or ERVs, from the dark genome, and we are now looking at additional dark genome antigens.

That creates two complementary vaccine approaches: personalized vaccines that are tailored to an individual patient and targets private antigen, as exemplified by EVX-01 in melanoma, and off-the-shelf vaccines that are tailored to a patient population and target conserved ERV antigens, as in EVX-04 for acute myeloid leukemia and EVX-05 for GBM. The other indications shown on this slide, including head and neck, lung, ovarian, bladder, colon, esophageal, testicular, and stomach cancers, illustrate the potential breadth of the application based on deep data mining and antigen profiling of patients. The strategic point is that the platform is not confined to one cancer type or to one vaccine approach. It gives us basis for pursuing opportunities across personalized and off-the-shelf cancer vaccines.

Let's now look at AI-Immunology's key differentiators. Broad discovery across multiple antigens, sources, and high target precision. That translate into strategic product opportunities for both personalized and off-the-shelf cancer vaccines across more patients and cancer types. AI-Immunology, as mentioned, explores neoantigens from cancer-specific somatic mutations, ERV antigens from genome-integrated viral sequences, and additional dark genome antigens which are currently in development. This provides a broader antigen repertoire. With our record-high target precision demonstrated in clinical trials, exemplified by the 89% vaccine target precision rate in EVX-01 phase II trial, we are well-positioned in finding the right targets.

The core component of AI-Immunology is instrumental in identifying the most relevant T-cell epitopes, and this one is a key driver of the high target precision rate. The core code is antigen type agnostic and provides a common foundation for designing high-precision cancer vaccines irrespective of the antigen of choice. These two AI-Immunology differentiators, broad antigen repertoire and high target precision, translate into strategic product opportunities. Greater antigen breadth significantly increases the chance of finding effective targets for more patients and cancer types. The precision at the target level increases the likelihood of clinical success, as it is the foundation for stronger clinical outcome.

Taking together, this positions AI-Immunology not just as a platform for a single vaccine, but as a foundation for developing a broader portfolio of cancer vaccines, combining the opportunity to reach more patients with the potential for stronger clinical outcome. EVX-04 is a concrete example of how we are translating this broader discovery capability into an off-the-shelf cancer vaccine program. Starting with sequencing data from AML patients, we identified ERV tumor antigens. From 5 million ERV antigen fragments and corresponding MHC ligands, AI-Immunology selected the 16 optimal antigen fragment sets. Currently, multiple vaccine candidates were designed and tested, and the lead candidate is now moving forward in GMP manufacturing.

We are now progressing through final preclinical testing and validation of the selected ERV antigens alongside activities to enable clinical trial application, or CTA, with the submission expected in the second half of this year. In summary, AI-Immunology combines broad antigen discovery with high target precision. We can identify cancer antigens from multiple sources, neoantigens, ERVs, and additional dark genome antigen approaches in development using the same core predictive technology across antigen sources.
