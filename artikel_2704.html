‘The sky is the limit’ – 49ers don’t see an offensive ceiling after record-setting explosion

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

For the rest of the NFL, the scary thing about the 49ers' dominant offensive performance against the Dallas Cowboys will not be the numbers they put up, but that they did so while missing one of the most integral pieces of their passing game.

San Francisco's attack was remarkable in the Niners' 46-40 shootout win over the Cowboys on Sunday, racking up 584 yards, the most Dallas has ever given up at home.

Quarterback Brock Purdy threw for 389 yards and three touchdowns in a game that marked another stunning display of his playmaking ability, while Christian McCaffrey had 223 scrimmage yards (161 receiving, 62 rushing). He scored on receptions of 74 and 66 yards and also found the endzone on a 16-yard fourth-quarter rush.

McCaffrey became the first running back in NFL history with multiple receiving touchdowns of at least 65 yards in one game, and the first player in the league history to have 160 receiving yards, two touchdown receptions, 60 rushing yards and a rushing touchdown in the same game.

Those numbers reflect a performance that was McCaffrey the receiver at his most devastating — though he was aided by one particularly favorable assignment on his third and final score — and yet, even after a game in which the San Francisco's main focal point exploded for the most scrimmage yards he has recorded as a 49er — there was consensus in the Niners' locker room that they have room to hit even greater heights.

This was the 49ers' third successive game without top receiver Mike Evans, with the future Hall of Famer still battling an Achilles issue. Though the Niners' effort against the Cowboys showed there is no reason to rush Evans back, his prospective return is something tight end George Kittle is relishing.

"When you add Mike Evans to it, when Mike Evans is on the field and the threat that he poses, I don't get to get covered by corners anymore and that's all Mike Evans," Kittle told his postgame press conference. "So it makes my life easier, it makes Christian's life easier.

"Everybody on the field, their live is easier because of how good he is and how much of a threat he is. So I've actually never been more confident in an offense when I look out and I see Mike Evans and he just has a go ball. It's like sick. So I'm excited to get Mike back, hopefully."

McCaffrey, though, is less focused on what the 49ers could achieve offensively, and more on the frame of mind that has allowed them to continue delivering outstanding production regardless of who is in the lineup, with Keenan Allen putting up 61 yards on five catches on his debut.

"I don't know I don't focus on any of that," McCaffrey said when asked about the offense's ceiling.

"All I know is we got the right mindset. We got the right group of guys. We have a lot of guys. It's cool because we're a very veteran run offense. We've had a lot of guys who have had a lot of success, but we really love each other. I think that shows in practice, that shows in the game.

"I think everybody just wants to win no matter how it is. And I think that's what makes it really cool. Guys like Mike, guys like Keenan, we come here and just do your job, do it really well, help the team win however you can and let's see where we can go. But the standard's really high. We got the right guys in the locker room and it's a fun team to be a part of."

Of course, the enjoyment factor is heightened when the offense can score as easily as the Niners did in moving to 8-1.

"The ceiling is high as we set it," Deebo Samuel said after a two-touchdown display. "We got a lot of playmakers on both sides of the ball and we go out there and put up 40 points. It just shows you what we're capable of on offense."

That sentiment was echoed by future Hall of Fame left tackle Trent Williams, who believes the 49er attack does not have a ceiling when the group delivers the kind of execution he displayed with his downfield blocking on several key plays.

"I think we can be as good as we allow ourselves to be," Williams said. "As long as we execute, we play together.

"We got to cut down on the penalties and shooting ourselves in the foot. But when we execute, I think the sky's the limit for us."
