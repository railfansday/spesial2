Bartow football ends season in first round; 3 observations

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

OVIEDO — Bartow High School football entered Friday's FHSAA Class 5A first-round playoff game against Oviedo High School on the road as underdogs. But the local school truly played until the last whistle to show that the program is headed in the right direction under head football Grady Morrell.

Still, the Yellow Jackets fell 57-30 to a more talented team with Division I players to end the season at 6-5.

"Hats off to a great program. Oviedo scores a lot of points early and often. A lot of ability on that side of the ball. We had to try to match those guys and it got out of hand when we couldn't figure out how to play inside the box and stop the run. We came into the game stressing to stop the pass, and they got some good receivers, but they came out with running backs that toted the pill," Morrell said after the game.

Yes, Oviedo's running back got Oviedo's lead to 22-3. But thanks to Bartow senior quarterback Amire Hall's 5-yard swing pass and touchdown to senior running back Brayden Scott, the Yellow Jackets were right back in the game thanks to a 22-10 score.

On Oviedo's next offensive possession, a tipped pass ended up in the hands of 6-foot-4 junior defensive end Dajon Tollie, who returned it 31 yards to put Bartow back in business.

This got the ball back in the hands of Hall, who on the run to the outside, threw on a rope to senior receiver Erik Koshinski for a 14-yard reception and first down. A couple of plays later, however, an unblocked defender on the edge ran a straight line and blasted Hall, who fumbled the ball.

Oviedo quarterback Sebastian Galeano threw a short screen lob, and it ended up being a 76-yard touchdown to eventually open the game up to 29-10 with 3:07 left in the second, and Oviedo never looked back.

The early runs opened up the passing game and Galeano found his main target in 6-foot-6 receiver Jordan Donahoo, who is committed to the Ohio State University. The Galeano-to-Danahoo connection was there all night as the Divsion I recruit was constantly getting open on the sidelines and making clutch catches before opening the game up in the end zone. Oviedo also kept running screen passes that were huge chunks.

But those chunks didn't dissaude the team, as coaches and players kept hyping up the team as soon as the second half was about to start. And Hall kept on going back to that muscle memory thanks to those countless hours training with his mother and his coaches. Hall, who did a nice job of throwing on the run, making the touch pass when needed or driving the ball with force in traffic, would go on to throw a 7-yard touchdown pass to Tollie, a 23-yard touchdown pass to junior wide receiver Moscow Shaw and a 9-yard touchdown pass to Jakobie Terry in Cover 3 and Cover 4. Folks a part of the chain crew were emphasizing how tough and good Hall was on the field, comparing his skill set to a Kyler Murray — the good version.

"I was never giving up. Even when we were down in the game, I told my coach let's keep firing at these guys, man. We never want to lay down for a team no matter if they have their first string or their second string. We still want to fire, and we know (what we bring) to the table. I'm glad my team kept battling with me and they didn't give up on me. ... We fight for every inch. I'm glad I'm a part of this senior team," Hall said.

This team went ahead and produced a winning season for the first time in two years. But their senior leader and gunslinger has his sights set on Peru State College in Nebraska to play football, though there is more interest from other colleges. Players returning include junior running back and defensive end Alijiah Galva, junior defensive tackle and offensive guard Carmello Harden, junior linebacker and leading tackler Jacob Richardson, Tollie, junior defensive end Brysyn Walters, among other key players.
