Palmetto Publishing Releases Fr. Michael J Rieder's New Memoir of Parish Life and Ministry

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Now available online, You Give Them Something to Eat collects more than a hundred short chapters drawn from a Catholic priest's work in parishes, jails, schools, and mission travel.

CHARLESTON, S.C., Nov. 18, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Palmetto Publishing has released You Give Them Something to Eat, a memoir in short chapters by Fr. Michael J Rieder. The book takes its title and its organizing question from the feeding of the crowd in the Gospels, when Jesus answered the disciples' report of an empty pantry with an assignment rather than a plan. Rieder, ordained in 1994, has spent decades turning that sentence over.

The book gathers more than a hundred very short chapters, most of them a single remembered moment. A seminary assignment places the author in a rough Brooklyn neighborhood, where a sister looks at the burned out cars and says, "Ah. All this and heaven too." Spanish classes in Guatemala begin with a friend's greeting, "I hope you are changed by this experience." There is Mass in Spanish and English behind bars, a Scout ranch in New Mexico, fifth graders who name their circle on the rug "our special time," and a parish school that closes.

The chapters do not run in chronological order. Rieder writes about wrestling with God before entering the seminary at twenty-four, about learning ministry by doing it during formation, and about work as a high school chaplain, mission journeys in Latin America, and an interfaith trip to Israel. He also writes about the setbacks. A parishioner walks into the sacristy after the school closing and asks, "Can I give you a hug?" The recurring claim of the book is that the people doing the serving were fed as well.

The book is written from inside Catholic parish life, and Rieder notes that Catholic readers will recognize certain details more quickly than others will. The questions he returns to are broader: where grace hides, why obligation makes a poor substitute for opportunity, and how forgiveness loosens its grip. The format places it alongside short inspirational collections such as Chicken Soup for the Soul and Love Does, aimed at adult Christian readers who keep such a book near a favorite chair and read one entry at a time.

The eighth of nine children and the younger of fraternal twins, Rieder earned a BA in philosophy with a minor sequence in sociology from Cathedral College of the Immaculate Conception and a master of divinity from Immaculate Conception Seminary. He has served mostly as a parish priest.

You Give Them Something to Eat is available for purchase online at Amazon.com and BarnesandNoble.com.

About the Author: Fr. Michael J Rieder took the scenic route to the priesthood, and he says so himself. The eighth of nine children and the younger of a pair of fraternal twins, he was educated partly in Catholic school and partly in public school, then spent years changing majors, taking time off, and wrestling with God before entering the seminary at twenty-four. He earned a BA in philosophy with a minor sequence in sociology from Cathedral College of the Immaculate Conception and a master of divinity from Immaculate Conception Seminary, and was ordained in 1994.

He has been mostly a parish priest. He served as chaplain at McGann-Mercy Diocesan High School in Riverhead, New York, spending his weekends celebrating masses at parishes across the East End of Long Island; as pastor of St. Therese of Lisieux Parish in Montauk for five years; and, since 2014, as pastor of St. Joseph Parish in Ronkonkoma. He has also been a catechist, a youth minister, a leader of youth choirs, a diocesan Scout chaplain, and an associate vocation director, and he worked summers as a chaplain at Philmont Scout Ranch in Cimarron, New Mexico, where he hiked the inaugural St. George Trek in 1992 and another in 2002.

His Spanish, begun in the seminary and continued over several summers of one-on-one study in Guatemala, eventually brought him to the Suffolk County Jail in Yaphank, where he has celebrated mass since 2018. He has sponsored children through Unbound since shortly after ordination, has traveled with the organization to Honduras, Colombia, Guatemala, and El Salvador, preached on its behalf around the country since 2007, and served on its Board of Governors from 2016 to 2018. He is a crier, a recovering klutz, and a fairly accomplished air guitarist who eventually learned the real thing. One hundred percent of the proceeds from You Give Them Something to Eat go to St. Joseph Parish.
