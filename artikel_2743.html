Three things to watch as Trump, Xi and Putin meet in China

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

SHENZHEN, China — Leaders of the U.S., China, Russia and other major Asia-Pacific economies face a series of critical issues from wars to tech security as they gather for an annual summit this week.

Since the last APEC leaders' meeting, China's tensions with Japan have escalated. Barely stabilized U.S.-China relations face new tests in the wake of Washington's scrutiny of Nvidia's China chip sales. And the Iran war, now in its ninth month, is still pressuring global energy prices, while the Russia-Ukraine war has dragged on.

But expectations are low for any breakthroughs.

Meanwhile for Beijing, host to this year's series of Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation meetings, the multilateral gathering is yet another venue to build its global influence. Chinese President Xi Jinping met Tuesday with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney and the President of Vietnam To Lam. Vietnam is hosting next year's APEC meetings.

Three things to watch as Trump, Xi and Putin meet in China

Russian President Vladimir Putin arrived in Shenzhen early Wednesday morning, where he was greeted by Chinese Politburo Standing Committee Member Cai Qi, according to Chinese state media. U.S. President Donald Trump is in the Philippines for the ASEAN Summit before heading to Shenzhen.

Trump and Xi are expected to meet alongside the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting Wednesday and Thursday. Their summit around the gathering last year in South Korea ushered in a one-year trade truce of lower tariffs and some rare earths flows, which was extended by two months, to January 2027.

In this week's summit, Trump does not plan to raise the issue of Nvidia and allegations that its former CEO Jensen Huang had an affair with a Chinese spy, U.S. officials said. They said the talks would focus on implementing the trade agreement.

The U.S. and China in September said they would lower tariffs on $30 billion worth of goods from the other country, without specifying an effective date. China said this month that it would only increase purchases of U.S. agricultural products if those tariffs were lowered.

However, Washington is already moving to tighten restrictions on loopholes that had given Chinese companies access to American artificial intelligence technology. So far, China's response has been to threaten countermeasures.

"If there is room for genuine U.S.-China cooperation this week, it's on managing risk rather than sharing advantage," said Reema Bhattacharya, head of Asia research at risk intelligence company Verisk Maplecroft. That could include cooperation on an artificial intelligence security hotline.

"Across Asia, governments under mounting pressure to pick a side will be watching Shenzhen for signs of how much room they still have to maneuver," Bhattacharya said. "Most have little appetite for a region split into two technology camps."

On Thursday, Xi is set to meet with Trump and Putin, according to Beijing's official confirmation of the event, billed as the "China-Russia-U.S. Summit."

It's the first-ever gathering of the three leaders, albeit with low expectations that there will be sufficient time for thorough dialogue. But the event creates the opportunity for a globally influential photo-op similar to the one that Xi, Putin and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi had on the sidelines of a China-hosted Shanghai Cooperation Organization summit in September 2025.

Also closely watched will be any interaction with Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi. Last November, her comments drew Beijing's ire after indicating Tokyo would support Taiwan if the island were seriously threatened by Beijing's military.

Takaichi is expected to join the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting in her first trip to China since becoming prime minister. She had met Xi on the sidelines of the APEC event last year in South Korea.

Attendees from other APEC economies are hoping for more signs of cooperation.

Anindya Bakrie, CEO of Indonesian conglomerate Bakrie & Brothers, told CNBC that when Trump and Xi meet, the narrative is hopefully one of peace and stability.

Tech issues are likely to be in heavy focus. Tesla and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk is set to join Trump on the Shenzhen trip. Other American executives, such as Visa CEO Ryan McInerney, joined a two-day APEC CEO Summit that kicked off Tuesday.

The APEC host city, Shenzhen, has been dubbed one of China's "Silicon Valleys" and is home to Huawei and BYD. The summit organizers are also using the event to showcase local robotics startups.

A slew of tech company earnings is coming out at the same time as the APEC meeting, keeping the corporate side in the spotlight as well.

Nvidia released quarterly results Wednesday morning Beijing time, following a tumultuous two weeks of scrutiny that forced the chipmaker's founder Jensen Huang to resign. The company boosted its outlook and shares rose in extended trading.

Shenzhen-based gaming and social media giant Tencent is set to report its quarterly earnings Wednesday evening. The company has previously noted constraints from its limited computing power.

U.S.-based AI giant Anthropic is pricing its highly anticipated initial public offering this week, and expected to list on the Nasdaq Thursday Nov. 19. Its CEO Dario Amodei has urged a slowdown in AI development and warned of the risk if China does not follow suit.

—CNBC's Penny Chen and Anniek Bao contributed to this report.
