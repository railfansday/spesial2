Eagles vs. Steelers: 5 bold predictions for the Week 11 matchup

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Philadelphia Eagles are running out of time to find something resembling an offensive identity. There's hope that the defense catches fire before it's too late. Seven consecutive losses have turned what once looked like a promising season into a weekly search for answers.

What's fortunate (or unfortunate depending on your paradigm) is that they're still right in the thick of things in the NFC East. They're four games behind the division-leading New York Giants with seven games remaining. As bad as all four teams have played at times, any team can go on a run or a losing streak and change the complexion of this race for superiority.

The upcoming matchup with the Pittsburgh Steelers offers another opportunity to see whether anything has changed for the better. The bye week gave Joe Milton extra time to get comfortable with his surroundings and the coaching staff time to address some glaring problems. A turnaround remains difficult to envision, but there are reasons to expect a more competitive performance than Philadelphia has delivered recently. Here are a handful of predictions for the coming contest that reflect both that possibility and the larger issues that continue to hold the Eagles back.

DeVonta Smith was very blunt this week. He didn't work so hard during the offseason to 'shut it down' with so many games remaining.

"I didn't get up at 4:30 in the morning all summer just to not play."

After limited participation at practice on Wednesday, he got his legs under him a day later and was a full participant on Thursday. He says he's 100 percent healthy. Something tells us an offensively starved Eagles attack will get him the ball early and often against the Steelers.

He'll lead the Eagles in targets, receptions, and yards gained. Figure 100 of the latter, but that doesn't mean Makai Lemon will be forgotten. He and Milton have worked together for a while, and that level of comfort should guarantee the former looks for his young rookie to pay dividends.

Lemon already showed what his role can look like with Milton. He led the Eagles with six catches for 61 yards on nine targets against the Giants. If Smith returns, Philadelphia can use him outside while keeping Lemon involved on quick throws from the slot. That gives both receivers a path to the yardage totals without asking Milton to spend the afternoon waiting on deep routes.

There is still plenty to like about Joe Milton's physical ability, but his first start was an especially difficult introduction to life in the NFL. He was thrown into the lineup less than a month after joining the team, behind an offensive line that offered little protection. Even a quarterback with Joe Burrow's experience and ability would have faced a difficult assignment under those circumstances.

Milton now has a start behind him and a bye week to prepare for Pittsburgh. He said he would spend much of that time in Philadelphia to build familiarity with the offense and his teammates. A spectacular performance might be too much to expect, but protecting the football and avoiding costly mistakes would represent meaningful progress.

Milton went without an interception against New York, but he lost a fumble on a strip-sack by Arvell Reese. Against Pittsburgh, protecting the ball starts with having an answer when the first read is covered and the rush closes in. A checkdown or a throwaway can keep a drive alive without giving the Steelers a short field.

Aaron Rodgers has thrown at least one interception in five of Pittsburgh's nine games this season, and he's been bailed out a few times in games where he hasn't. That's an opportunity Vic Fangio and the Eagles defense should be prepared to exploit, particularly if the pass rush can make the veteran quarterback uncomfortable.

Rodgers is experienced enough to recognize pressure and punish defensive mistakes, so forcing him into difficult decisions will be important. Still, Philadelphia has shown it can create disruptive moments defensively. The bold prediction here is that Fangio's unit comes away with two interceptions, even if the Eagles fail to turn both takeaways into points.

Two interceptions would be a significant jump for a defense that enters Week 11 with three all season. The best chance starts with pressure from four rushers. If Philadelphia can hurry Rodgers and keep seven defenders in coverage, it can leave help over DK Metcalf while keeping a defender available to break on a shorter throw.

Philadelphia's offense has struggled to establish a reliable ground game, and there isn't much reason to expect that the problem has disappeared. Sure, everyone is hopeful, but many have also taken the 'We'll believe it when we see it' approach. Here's the good news. The Steelers have allowed 242.2 passing yards per game. If protection holds up, Milton and the passing attack an opportunity to find some rhythm.

The prediction is that Philadelphia hangs a number slightly above Pittsburgh's average. Figure approximately 250 yards through the air thanks to Lemon and some returning reinforcements. The Eagles haven't scored 24 points (a season high) since Week 2. They haven't topped 20 for two weeks, but what the heck? These are bold predictions, right?

Call it stubborn. Call it insanity, since a quiet day from the running game is also part of this prediction. Philadelphia hasn't offered a consistent, sustainable formula, but a surprisingly successful air raid and fewer three-and-outs could make this game more high-scoring than expected.

We hate to disappoint you. After all of that, you may have had your fingers crossed for an upset, but the facts are the facts. Right now, Pittsburgh is the better team. The Eagles have opportunities to finally put some points on the board, but Philadelphia has lost seven straight for a reason.

As always, the home crowd will be supportive enough to try to provide a spark for a team that desperately needs one. Still, a better offensive showing doesn't automatically solve the team's larger problems. The defense can keep the Eagles within striking distance for much of the afternoon, but eventually the offense must make enough plays to win. Sadly, none of us have seen them do that enough to believe they'll get it done in this scenario.

Milton provides a few encouraging moments, but Pittsburgh does enough to offer Philadelphia an eighth consecutive loss. A more watchable performance and a winning result are two very different things, and Philadelphia still has a long way to go before those outcomes become interchangeable. Prediction: Steelers 27, Eagles 20
