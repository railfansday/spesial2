‘I hope they win Ashes 5-0’ – Stokes backs Root and Fleming's new England

Former England captain Ben Stokes says he hopes they win next year's Ashes 5-0, though he would not be drawn on Sandpapergate or the possibility of him making a comeback for the series.

Speaking to Sky Sports upon the launch of a new podcast he's presenting with former England team-mate Mark Wood called 'That's Not Cricket', Stokes hinted that he might have more to say on the saga dominating Australian cricket on that platform.

"It would be pretty bizarre for it not to be mentioned [on the podcast]", Stokes said. "It's all kicking off at the moment out there, isn't it?

"What Australia have going on there is far beyond anything we had to deal with [as a leadership group].

"I imagine Joe [Root] and Flem [Stephen Fleming] would be thinking, 'imagine if this happened three weeks before the Ashes next year, as opposed to now'."

England's Test team enter into a new era under Root - who returned to the captaincy with a 3-0 series sweep of Pakistan following Stokes' international retirement - and Fleming, who begins as head coach with a three-Test tour of South Africa next month.

The series, against the reigning World Test Champions, serves as an ideal challenge for the new regime as they steel themselves for the visit of Australia next summer where they'll look to regain the Ashes for the first time since winning on home soil in 2015.

"I'm doing a Glenn McGrath here; I hope England win the Ashes 5-0," Stokes said. "I'm not saying they will, but I wish nothing but the best for them.

"I'm still an England cricket fan. I want the players, Joe and Flem to do well.

"There is no animosity whatsoever between any of us, there's no jealousy."

Stokes did add, however, that he was "surprised" to see Root take on the captaincy again, knowing first-hand how much the job takes out of you - as well as the toll it had taken on Root by the time he handed over the reins in 2022 after five years in the role.

"I was a little bit surprised, if I'm being honest, just because he knows what it's like and he's done so well as a player but also, knowing Joe, he feels like he can take the team from where it was and build on it again," Stokes said.

"It's an exciting time for someone to think like that and when Stephen Fleming got appointed, I thought 'these two are going to work really well together'.

"I did a lot of work with Flem in the IPL. He's a great man manager; everyone has already seen how he speaks, which is very impressive. I'm excited to watch them go about it."

Stokes' time as Test captain and as an England cricketer came to an end with a 2-1 series defeat to New Zealand, which was on the heels of a disastrous 4-1 Ashes drubbing in Australia during the winter.

"I was in the right headspace for the Ashes," Stokes reflected. "It was all after that. Before the Ashes, I was so confident.

"Everyone was fit, firing and ready to go. Fans and pundits were all confident, and Australia weren't confident, so we couldn't have been in a better place going out there.

"But emotion and the feeling of what you think is going to happen is completely different to getting out there in the heat of the battle. Everything after that was tough."

Stokes added: "Unfortunately, to do that role, you have to immerse yourself in it, have to throw yourself into it.

"For all the positive stuff that comes with it, there is a negative effect that can flow over into your home life and your family.

"I think every England captain will have that. I don't think there is anything you can do.

"You can try your hardest, but when you do that role you just have to do it. I don't think you are doing the role justice if you take it lightly.

"I always felt on as England captain. Now I can just be Ben, not Ben Stokes captain of the England cricket team. Looking back, it's not really a healthy place to be at."

As well as still playing for his county side Durham - helping them clinch promotion back to Division One of the County Championship - and an upcoming stint with the Adelaide Strikers in the Big Bash League, Stokes is using his newfound freedom to venture into the world of podcasting.

"I have been in the public eye for so long but people who don't know me probably only know 10, 15, 20 per cent of what I am actually like," he said. "That is the exciting thing: I now get a chance to say what I want to be going out.

"I don't feel like you're always necessarily able to give that full account of yourself as a person, because you're wary of upsetting people. The only person I can upset now is myself.

"It's really exciting and I can't wait to get going. We've got a lot to talk about and a lot to say."
