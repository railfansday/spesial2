Netflix's 10/10 True Crime Show Is So Good, It Got 30 Episodes And A Spinoff

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

All 30 episodes of this Netflix true crime series are excellent, and the same goes for its spinoff too. Netflix has changed a lot in the years since it began. I'm not just talking about its switch from a mail-order DVD service to a streaming platform either. The streaming years alone have seen big changes.

The first years of Netflix were filled with some of the best original programming the streamer has ever produced. Look at any list of the top Netflix shows, and many, if not most, of them will be from the first three to four years of the show's existence. Not to say it only produces bad shows now, but there does seem to be a quantity-over-quality approach these days.

Case in point, one of Netflix's best shows came out two years after the company began its streaming services. Narcos premiered in 2015 and tells the story of Pablo Escobar (Wagner Moura) and the Medellín Cartel. The series takes two points of view, one through Escobar's eyes, and the other through the vantage point of the DEA working in Colombia.

Boyd Holbrook and Pedro Pascal co-star as Steve Murphy and Javier Peña, respectively, two real-life members of the American task force. Through voice-over, dramatic scenes, and archival footage, Narcos explains how Escobar came to be the most powerful drug dealer in the Americas, and how he fell from the top.

After Narcos ended, Netflix kept the series alive with Narcos: Mexico, a spinoff that's more like seasons 4 through 6 of the show. After the end of the Medellín Cartel, new powers rose in Mexico and Central America, most notably the Cali and Guadalajara Cartels, which eventually gave rise to the same Cartels operating in the Americas today.

Narcos is one of the best shows Netflix has ever made. It's extremely unique in how it tells its story. The blend of real footage and the dramatic scenes speeds things along, and while voice-overs can be a crutch in other productions, Holbrook's delivery and the unbelievable crimes and actions taken by the Medellín Cartel make it very enjoyable.

The three main characters are each worthy of a show dedicated to their exploits. While Murphy and Peña are not as interesting as Escobar, because, who is, they are given a lot of screen time to explore their personal lives and inner thoughts. It makes their hunt for Escobar feel more important and understandable.

The series weaves together Escobar's crimes, the expansion of the Cartel, and the DEA's search expertly. As soon as one piece of the story gets a little tiresome, Narcos knows exactly when to jump to the next section. Even after Escobar dies, the events he's set in motion become just as compelling as the ones he was a part of.

Narcos: Mexico takes on a new setting and target. This three-season series was originally intended to be seasons four through 6 of Narcos, but was made into a companion piece. The development of the Mexican Cartels is at the center of the story, and there is no longer just one head honcho that the DEA must stop.

Considering how relevant the Mexican Cartels still are, Narcos: Mexico can hit a little too close to home sometimes. Narcos felt more dramatic with the "Robin Hood"-like story of Escobar. Narcos: Mexico is still an excellent series, but it's the cat-and-mouse game between Escobar and the DEA that really makes Narcos stand out.
