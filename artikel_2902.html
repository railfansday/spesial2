Knicks turn it up in fourth for convincing win to continue domination of Nets

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

For three quarters, the Knicks were playing down to their opponent. They were, in a word, terrible. Knicks-Nets was so ugly it should've been blurred out for indecency.

But then the defending champs woke up. OG Anunoby caught fire, Josh Hart provided a spark, Jordan Clarkson took advantage, and the Knicks ultimately cakewalked to a 103-90 victory Monday at MSG.

"We finally played fast, got stops," said Hart, who had eight points and seven assists in 27 minutes. "We've been playing very slow, very stagnant. We finally got out, we ran and we got good looks."

New York Knicks guard Jalen Brunson (11) reacts after hitting a 3-point shot during the first half against the Brooklyn Nets, Monday, Nov. 2, 2026, in New York. Jason Szenes for NY Post

With Jalen Brunson on the bench at the start of the final period, the Knicks (3-4) went from a four-point deficit to a 10-point advantage in about four minutes. It spurred the Knicks' 15th straight victory over the Nets (1-6), a streak that began in 2023 — right after the Durant-Kyrie era in Brooklyn.

But Monday was in doubt for the majority of the night.

"The issues are still there," Hart said. "I think defensively, we were solid. I think this year, for the most part, our defense has been pretty good. Obviously there have been some lapses. … But our offense has to get in a better flow, and that's kind of what's holding us back right now. So a win doesn't erase the problem or erase the issues. We got to figure those out and get better."

Brooklyn Nets forward Julius Randle (30) drives to the basket against New York Knicks guard Mikal Bridges (25) on Monday, Nov. 2, 2026, in New York. Jason Szenes for NY Post

The first half was a slopfest from both sides, an absolute eyesore. The Knicks shot 36 percent but still held a seven-point advantage at the break because of their abundance of free throws.

Brunson and Landry Shamet were the lone bright spots. The Nets were a misfiring mess but found a rhythm after the break — particularly Michael Porter Jr. — and won the third quarter 35-24.

Mike Brown responded with a new lineup — with Tyler Kolek and Clarkson in the backcourt — and the Nets folded with just 11 total points in the fourth quarter.

Anunoby scored eight of his 18 points in the first six minutes of the fourth quarter. Karl-Anthony Towns finished with 16 rebounds and 11 points. Brunson had 19 points but was again inefficient on 6-for-16 shooting. Shamet scored all 20 of his points in the first three quarters. Clarkson took over in the fourth. Mikal Bridges, meanwhile, pulled another Houdini and was benched for all but three minutes of the second half. He had six points on 1-for-7 shooting from the field.

New York Knicks center Karl-Anthony Towns (32) pulls down a rebound against Brooklyn Nets forward Chaney Johnson (31) on Monday, Nov. 2, 2026, in New York. Jason Szenes for NY Post

For the Nets, Julius Randle, the former Knicks All-Star, forced the issue and often missed — shooting 5-for-16 with a team-high 21 points.

The interborough matchup, once touted as a powerful rivalry when the Nets moved from New Jersey to Brooklyn, now carries the juice of a steamrolled grape. The Knicks have always dominated the attention of New York City, and it's never been more lopsided than in 2026 — the year of James Dolan's first title.

The Nets have become an afterthought under the stewardship of GM Sean Marks, who is second to only Brian Cashman as the longest-tenured top sports executive in town. They haven't won a playoff game since 2021, and there's little hope Marks' latest incarnation will break the streak.

They have two win-now veterans in Randle and Porter, who are surrounded by the young and unproven, operating a great distance from contending basketball.

Still, the Knicks were in trouble and playing ugly ball until the fourth quarter began. They're still not where they want to be.

"We definitely played until June last season and I think everyone still feels it," Anunoby said.
