4 Takeaways From Grizzlies' 126-98 Win Over Warriors

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Memphis Grizzlies finished a back-to-back Thursday night at FedExForum by beating the Golden State Warriors 126-98. Memphis led from the first possession to the last, won its fifth straight game, and moved to 12-4. Golden State is 8-8.

Golden State was charged with 20 player turnovers and 2 team turnovers, and Memphis scored 35 points off them. The Grizzlies had 9 steals. Taylor Hendricks had 4 of them, 2 in the first 2:31 of the game.

The night started with a shot-clock violation. On the Warriors' first trip, Gui Santos drove for a floater, Cedric Coward blocked it, and Golden State ran out the 24 seconds.

Memphis had only a jog-through between Wednesday's 124-121 win at Cleveland and Thursday's tip-off. Grizzlies coach Tuomas Iisalo said the staff used it to pick out a couple of details that would create more disruption.

"And then taking that right away into the game, that is very encouraging," Iisalo said. "Usually it takes a lot longer. But what we've also spoken about, that eventually, at some point, this group hopefully will play in the playoffs, and there you have one day in between the games, time to make those adjustments. And then you've got to get your body to execute it also on the court."

"And you only get better at it if you're put into situations where you actually get to practice that," Iisalo continued. "And that's why it's such a luxury for young teams to get reps in the tight games that we have had, that we've spoken about also this season, different time and score scenarios."

Nobody wants to be "the weakling defensively," Coward said in a locker-room clip posted by Grind City Media, and he went back through that opening possession.

"I think like in that 24 seconds, everybody was doing their job to perfection," Coward said. "Honestly, like I know it ended with me, I think, getting a block or something or whatever may happen, but ... It's not about me getting the block. It's about before the possession, Jay Wells got through the screen. We were tagging the rollers."

"Whatever it was, all the chips fell in the right directions for them to not be able to really get a shot towards the rim," Coward added. "You know what I mean?"

Cameron Boozer made all 6 of his shots from the field and all 4 of his free throws for 16 points in 27:29. He added 6 rebounds and 5 assists and was plus-16. Nine of the points came in the first quarter, on 3-of-3 shooting and 3-of-3 at the line.

Boozer is part of a lot of functional lineups now, Iisalo said, with five players on the floor playing with confidence, and that has made it easier for the rookie to feed teammates.

"He's starting to figure it out," Iisalo explained. "His big strength really is, I think we talked about this, we've talked about it after several games, but just taking what the game gives him. And I think he's been better at it. Also the guys around him are playing better."

"And you saw in Washington, for example, they were sagging off of him," Iisalo continued. "Everybody still keeps feeding him. He keeps taking those shots. And I think he went, what, 7-for-8 after missing the first five. And that speaks a lot about his character."

"We are thinking about the long run and also about not just that, but the development of these players, and understand that there's always going to be some fluctuation," Iisalo added. "... So the main thing is he generally makes great decisions, and he will get better at it, too."

Boozer had 29 points on 10-of-21 shooting in the 136-120 win at Washington on Monday. He is 19 years old, and Iisalo said early-season shooting numbers for a teenager should be compared with those of the other teenagers who have come into the league.

Coward's 3-pointer with 4:40 left in the first half put Memphis up 52-34. The Warriors answered with 10 straight points, the last 3 on a Will Richard 3-pointer at 2:09, and the margin was down to eight. Memphis led 55-47 at halftime.

Coward hit a 3-pointer 37 seconds into the third quarter. Jaylen Wells added 3-pointers at 7:38 and 6:41, and the lead was 78-59. Memphis won the quarter 37-26 and led by as many as 34 later in the night.

In the locker room, Coward said this year's group takes defense more personally and sets a higher standard that starts at that end of the floor.

"Defense was the main thing, and rebounding," Coward explained. "I think we kept giving them extra possessions. They had like 10 more shots than us at halftime. We were making shots, but we can't rely on our shot making. We gotta lock in on the defensive end."

Coward finished with 19 points and 4 assists. Wells finished with 22 points on 8-of-10 shooting.

Golden State grabbed 18 offensive rebounds and scored 25 second-chance points. Memphis had 16 offensive rebounds and 17 second-chance points. Charles Bassey had 13 rebounds for the Warriors, 3 on the offensive end.

The Grizzlies did a much better job after halftime of not fouling and keeping Golden State's drivers off their strong hand, according to Iisalo.

"And the rebounding stayed as an issue," Iisalo said. "But we, on the other hand, were able to force some turnovers and negate that rebounding differential."

Memphis finished with 41 rebounds to Golden State's 40. The Grizzlies made all 17 of their free throws, and the Warriors went 27-of-32.

Memphis is 8-0 on the road. It plays at Minnesota on Saturday at 7 p.m. CT and at Charlotte on Monday at 6 p.m. CT.
