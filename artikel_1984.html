Sources: Chelsea learn the 'hefty fee' needed to complete Jules Kounde deal

Chelsea will need to set aside a "big fee" if they want to sign Barcelona right-back Jules Kounde, sources have told Football Insider.

The Spanish giants are prepared to cash in on the France international in the coming months, but only if the deal is done on their terms.

Chelsea are understood to have plenty of "hurdles" that must be overcome if they are to land the 28-year-old in January.

Sources say that it would take a "hefty fee" to convince Barcelona to sell the defender mid-season, despite his limited football of late.

Kounde has only started three games in La Liga so far this season, and is unlikely to see his game time boosted any time soon.

Chelsea are long-term admirers of the former Sevilla ace, but may have to break the bank in order to sign him.

Kounde is growing increasingly frustrated with his lack of football at the Camp Nou, and is plotting a move away this winter.

Football Insider revealed on Tuesday that the defender is growing keen to leave Barcelona in search of more regular opportunities elsewhere.

The veteran is understood to be desperate for games at this stage of his career, and is prepared to push for a move to make that happen.

However, Chelsea do not view their pursuit of him as a top priority heading into the window.

Xabi Alonso already has Reece James, Josh Acheampong, Malo Gusto and Marco Palestra who can play at right-back.

As a result, it may take an exit for the Blues to truly pursue the move that Kounde wants.

Football Insider also recently revealed that the "only way" that Chelsea will sell Romeo Lavia in January is if they bring several new faces into their midfield.

The Belgian starlet has emerged as a target for Aston Villa, who are keen to do business with the Blues once again.

However, Chelsea failed to replace Enzo Fernandez in the summer, and have no desire to cash in on Lavia unless they secure at least two new midfielders.

They struck several transfer deals with Villa in the summer, but a deal for Lavia appears unlikely to get off the ground at this stage.
