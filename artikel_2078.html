'Crazy' - Stefan Borson reacts to transfer expert's '£500m' Man United January claim

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Mauricio Pochettino has "no chance" of splashing £500million on transfers in Janary with the current Manchester United owners.

That is according to former Manchester City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that the hierarchy are unlikely to push more funds into the club.

The Glazers and INEOS are under tremendous pressure, even with Michael Carrick sacked to make way for Pochettino, who has only just arrived at Carrington.

Man United's owners are often under the spotlight for their lack of investment on the pitch, with the manager recently escaping a lot of criticism.

Speaking to Football Insider, Bryan King has attempted to provide a sliver of hope, suggesting that the Red Devils could splash upwards of £500m on transfers in January.

Cole Palmer and Lewis Hall are both targets, and have been for quite some time, but any chance of securing their signatures looks very unlikely at this moment in time.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson has ruled out the idea of Man United's owners giving Pochettino a war chest in January.

"This Manchester United? There's no chance they're going to do that, is there? Man United have capacity to spend money," he said.

"But it's going to require the owners to put money in, in terms of the cash itself. And in any event, there's literally no chance that United are going to buy Cole Palmer.

"It's pretty unlikely that Newcastle are going to be interested in a deal for one of their best players in January, who's just signed a new contract, for anything less than a crazy price.

"I don't think Man United are going to do it. So I don't really agree with much of that analysis, to be honest."

Man United's owners appear to have settled for mediocrity, and supporters are making their frustrations known from top to bottom.

Instead of investing heavily, they've been focused on reducing the wage bill, and even more funds could soon open up due to exits.

Borson has already told Football Insider that the Red Devils' wage bill simply has to rise again if they are to have any chance of competing at the top level.

The chances of that, however, are very minimal, based on what the owners have done over the last few transfer windows.
