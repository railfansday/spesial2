‘Deeply concerning’: NHS in England reports more than 3,000 incidents of staff snooping on patients’ records

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Campaign groups and regulators say data breaches are distressing for patients and families and erode trust in the health service

The NHS in England has recorded more than 3,000 incidents of staff inappropriately snooping on patients’ medical records in the last five years.

Patient campaign groups and regulators said the figures were “deeply concerning” and distressing for patients and families, and eroded trust in the health service. A lack of transparency from NHS organisations after data breaches were uncovered further compounded the heartache, they added.

The figures likely underestimate the scale of the abuse. Only those cases where the NHS had confirmed that staff had wrongly accessed patients’ notes or medical histories were included, and nearly half of trusts failed to provide precise data.

The disclosure follows a recent series of high-profile cases. Last month it emerged that 15 NHS staff had been sacked after accessing records belonging to victims of the 2023 Nottingham attack, in which Valdo Calocane killed two 19-year-old students, Barnaby Webber and Grace O’Malley-Kumar, and Ian Coates, a 65-year-old caretaker, and attempted to kill three other people.

Nottingham university hospitals NHS trust (NUH) said in October that it had so far dismissed 15 staff, issued 15 final written warnings and two first written warnings.

In May, it emerged that dozens of staff at University Hospitals of Liverpool Group had looked at the records of victims of the 2024 Southport attack, which left three girls dead and 10 others injured.

The records of a three-year-old boy hurt after falling into a crocodile pit in Cambridgeshire in June were also viewed by about 40 hospital staff.

Figures obtained under freedom of information laws by the Press Association show there were 3,029 incidents of staff inappropriately accessing patient records between 2021-22 and 2025-26.

But the true extent of the problem is likely to be much higher. Only 79 of 143 acute hospital and ambulance trusts in England provided precise figures.

Trusts must report breaches posing a risk to patients’ rights to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) within 72 hours. The regulator’s chief executive, Paul Arnold, has written to health leaders demanding a crackdown on the snoopers.

“When we seek medical care, we trust the NHS with some of our most sensitive information and we need to be confident that only people with a legitimate reason will ever access our records.

“Looking at medical records out of curiosity is unacceptable and jeopardises the vital trust people place in the health service. These figures are deeply concerning and I know how much distress this type of behaviour causes patients and families.”

Rachel Power, chief executive at the Patients Association, said the incidents eroded trust in the wider NHS, and a further lack of transparency “deepens that damage”.

“Patients need confidence that strong safeguards are in place, that inappropriate access will be detected and acted on without delay, and that they will be the first to know, not the last, when their confidentiality has been breached.”

In September, the head of NHS England, Jim Mackey, said staff suspected of snooping on patients’ medical records should be suspended immediately and barred from using health service computers.

Earlier that month, the Health Service Journal reported that 214 NHS staff had been sacked, three suspended and 540 given final written warnings for snooping on patients’ records since 2020.

One employee was dismissed after viewing up to 179 people’s records, another had accessed their spouse’s details in a “potential domestic violence situation”, and others had shared information they had gathered in WhatsApp groups.
