OBM and OCEAN bring DATUM to Foreman

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

CHEYENNE, Wyo., Nov. 18, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- OBM and OCEAN today announced DATUM on Foreman, helping miners reduce stale shares and protect their margins through the industry-leading fleet-management platform they already use. The integration distributes work locally and gives miners building their own templates access to OCEAN's 1% pool fee, half its standard rate.

DATUM on Foreman, helping miners reduce stale shares and protect their margins through the fleet-management platform.

Foreman gives operators control over the systems that determine their fleet's performance. From monitoring hashrate and power to coordinating curtailment and responding to issues automatically, it helps miners keep equipment productive and sites running efficiently. Foreman's Trusted Pools and Pool Change Approval tools also catch unapproved pool changes, so one compromised login can't redirect a fleet's hashrate.

The integration starts installation of a Bitcoin Core node and the DATUM Gateway on a supported machine running Foreman Pickaxe. The same hardware that supports fleet management can also support local template creation and work distribution, without requiring a separate dedicated DATUM device.

"DATUM gives miners real advantages, and now setting it up in Foreman is easier than ever," said Dan Lawrence, CEO and co-founder of OBM. "With just a few clicks, Foreman customers can reduce wasted work and take control of their block templates without adding new hardware or another system to manage."

Developed by OCEAN and launched in 2024, DATUM lets miners build their own block templates locally while participating in pooled mining. Operators choose which transactions their equipment works on, rather than leaving that decision to the pool.

For miners at remote sites, including those using Starlink, connectivity can be a constraint on performance and a costly part of the operation. DATUM reduces that constraint by aggregating thousands of miner connections into one connection to the upstream pool. This reduces bandwidth usage by approximately 96% and allows miners in remote areas using Starlink or 5G to have essentially the same performance and efficiency as if they were using fiber optics.

"We already use DATUM and Foreman at our sites and are big believers in both," said Todd Perlmeter of Abundant Mines. "We've tested this integration, and bringing them together on the same machine just makes sense. It simplifies our infrastructure without giving up the capabilities we value. This is the kind of practical modernization that helps prepare mining operations for the future."

Adding DATUM to a mining operation should have noticeable results for miners. The local DATUM Gateway distributes work to the fleet over the local network, helping reduce stale shares caused by delays in receiving replacement jobs from a remote pool. It also aggregates machine connections, reducing upstream connection overhead.

"Miners invest heavily in turning electricity into useful work. Delays in getting new jobs to their machines can waste a portion of that work," said Jason Hughes, OCEAN's CTO and DATUM's creator. "DATUM brings template creation and work distribution on site. Integrating it with Foreman makes that infrastructure easier to deploy through a platform operators already trust."

Miners building their own templates with DATUM pay a 1% OCEAN pool fee, compared with OCEAN's standard 2%. OCEAN's transparent reward accounting lets miners verify how actual block rewards are distributed, down to the shares used in the calculation.

"Miners deserve to keep more of what their work earns," said Mark Artymko, President of OCEAN. "This partnership makes that easier, while strengthening the Bitcoin network their businesses depend on."
