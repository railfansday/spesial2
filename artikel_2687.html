Xabi Alonso reflects on the predictably tragic consequences of his own decisions

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

After the week that he had had, there was surely no way Wesley Fofana was going to play, let alone start Chelsea's match against Nottingham Forest last night, right?

Even if we (somehow) ignored his generally poor performances on the pitch, after all the drama stemming from his unsanctioned interview with a podcast, including the visceral negative reaction from most of the fanbase regarding his comments about his penalty miss and financial motivations, there was surely just one, and only one obvious choice to make regarding his selection.

Just leave him out, for his sake and all of ours. Protect him from us, and save us from him. Win-win.

Alas, Xabi Alonso decided to do something inexplicable.

Fofana started at center back. And just to make matters even more interesting, he was partnered by Moisés Caicedo. (In fairness, Caicedo did well enough, though he's very much needed in midfield so this was also a net-negative.)

The travelling "support" made their feelings known loud and clear even before Fofana gave away a penalty early in the second half. Mercifully, he was soon after finally spared any further blushes. He surely cannot play for us again — or at least not for a good long while, until things settle down and become "normal"-ish for him. Though I suppose the manager might surprise us again, and not in a good way.

Alonso did try to explain his thinking after the 3-2 defeat at the City Ground. His attempts to do so were in line with Chelsea's performance on the night.

"We have to stick together. Everyone that is in the changing room, everyone in the team is needed. We need to help and we need to go through this together. We don't leave apart anyone. [We] need to support everyone because we need everyone. We need the fans, we need all the players, we need to stick together because if not things will get more complicated.

"[…] for the next games we will see [but] it's always [what] I think [are] the best tactical decisions approaching each game and today that was my decision."

To be clear, the loss is not wholly on Fofana. It's on all the other players, too (except maybe Emmanuel Emegha, who, contrary to Fofana, should have definitely started). It's on all the defenders, all the midfielders, and all the forwards, and the goalkeeper, too. We've now dropped six points against two of the worst teams in the league, while suffering home defeats against teams who simply cannot be allowed to win at the Bridge — all in the last few weeks.

We can blame the squad, the players in it, and those who were responsible for assembling it.

But this loss in particular is on the manager. The team may be unbalanced, fragile, and lacking confidence, but you still have to put them in a situation that's actually conducive, at least in theory, to winning, that maximizes your chances of not failing. Alonso keeps pushing the wrong buttons, like an untrained AI agent. Maybe Claude can be our next manager when Alonso gets the inevitable boot.

I don't want to see that happen on principle (both in terms of managerial stability and in terms of unchecked AI proliferation), but it's unfortunately hard to see how we recover from this run of results and performances and all the other sideshows. There's always next season, I suppose.

In the meantime, the beatings will continue until morale improves.
