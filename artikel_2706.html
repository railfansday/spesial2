Stone makes history as Knights fall to Stars in shootout

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

LAS VEGAS — It was bound to happen eventually for Mark Stone.Truth is, it should have happened sooner. A lot sooner.The Vegas Golden Knights captain became the franchise's scoring leader when he set up Victor Olofsson's second-period power play goal against Dallas Thursday at T-Mobile Arena. With the helper, Stone passed Jonathan Marchessault for the record with what was his 418th point.But as was the case Tuesday, the Knights couldn't hold a third-period lead. They lost again, 4-3, this time in a shootout as Jason Robertson and Tyler Seguin were successful against Adin Hill and only Trevor Connelly could score for the Knights, who picked up just five out of a possible 10 points on the five-game homestand.Stone, the lone captain in the franchise's 10-year history, did it in 424 games (147 goals, 271 assists). But he has missed 166 games in his time with Vegas since being acquired from Ottawa in 2019. So it's anyone's guess what his points total would be had he not missed so much time. His career NHL numbers, which includes his six-plus years with the Senators: 270 goals, 459 assists, 729 points."Mark epitomizes what it is to be a Golden Knight," said Knights coach Ryan Craig. "His character. How he carries himself. The kind of teammate he is. His work ethic. He cares about winning."With that, he's allowed himself to lead our team, to become the all-time points leader. He's put himself in a position to do that by being who he is. I've seen him continue to grow as the captain and leader of our hockey club and he's the right man for the job."Jack Eichel, who has been Stone's teammate and linemate for the last five years, said Stone's milestone accomplishment was well earned."He's a competitor and he's super smart with the puck, making plays," Eichel said. "He thinks the game at an elite level."It's not surprising. Stoney's been here a while and leading the charge so it's a great milestone and (I'm) happy for him." The Knights trailed Dallas 2-0 going into the second period. And given what transpired Tuesday when Vegas squandered a two-goal lead late and wound up losing in overtime, 4-3 to the Stars, this was shaping up to be a true uphill climb.But when Stone set up Olofsson for the power play goal, it energized the Knights. Later in the period William Karlsson was nicely set up by Braeden Bowman and Connelly to tie it 2-2 and get the home team back on even footing.The Knights took a 3-2 lead 5:34 into the third period from an unlikely source with Jeremy Lauzon beating Jake Oettinger from the point for his first goal of the year. But once again, they were unable to close it out and Thomas Harley's goal with 3:02 left in regulation gave Dallas new life.Connelly was sensational in overtime, getting three really good chances to win it for Vegas. But he was unable to deliver and ultimately, the Stars got the additional point with the shootout success."We need to learn from these situations and grow from it," Craig said. We're down 2-0 in the first. We come back to tie. Then we take the lead. We have to find a way to finish the game. We do. We do."We had some chances to win in overtime. One of those go in, we're not talking about just getting one point. We'll continue to hammer away at it."Eichel said: "We did some good things. But at the end of the day we lost. We've got to do a better job of closing things out."
