discarded traffic signs fold into urban tables for farmers’ markets in new york city

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

architect juan fernández gonzález repurposes decommissioned aluminum road signs into folded standing tables that bolt directly onto standard U-channel signposts.

JFG edited by christina vergopoulou November 13, 2026 3 min

Conceived by architect Juan Fernández González, Traffic Sign Urban Tables is an urban furniture project that repurposes decommissioned aluminum traffic signs into standing tables mounted onto existing street signposts in New York City. With over one million traffic signs across the five boroughs regularly removed due to graffiti, wear, or legibility loss, the project intercepts municipal waste streams to provide compact dining surfaces for farmers’ markets and mobile food vendors, which typically operate without seating or surface amenities.

Fabricated from retired road signs sourced from the Mercer County Department of Transportation and Infrastructure in New Jersey, each aluminum sheet is bent using a press brake at Serett Corp. in Jersey City into a narrow cantilevered shelf. The designer hand-paints the tables in a pink-and-white checkered motif referencing traditional disposable street-food tablecloths. Installation relies entirely on pre-existing U-channel signposts—a Federal Highway Administration standard featuring pre-punched mounting holes spaced one inch center-to-center. Secured with standard bolts, nuts, and washers, the lightweight units can be height-adjusted, mounted without obstructing pedestrian circulation, and deployed temporarily during street closures. Following initial installations at the Carnaval de la Cultura Latina in Queens and GrowNYC’s Inwood and Union Square Greenmarkets in Manhattan, the modular attachment system is designed for scalability across North America wherever standardized U-channel posts are present.
