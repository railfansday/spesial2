Cowboys point/counterpoint: Setting goals for the rest of the season

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

While the Dallas Cowboys are far from eliminated as the potential winner of the NFC East, it really doesn't feel like they have much of a chance. They are 4-6 with seven games to go. To eventually win the East, they would have to really change the direction they have been heading.

So what do they face now? And what do they need to accomplish so we no longer feel like this season is just circling the drain? Our Tom Ryle and Sean Martin offer their ideas.

Tom – Well, the obvious thing is that they need to win games, but that is the obvious goal each and every time the team takes the field. Clearly, a loss to the 1-8 Titans would be a horrible sign. It is hardly beyond the realm of possibility, sadly. To me, this is about more than simply getting a W against a hapless team. They have to look in control and capable of playing a strong game from start to finish.

But then comes a much more important goal: They need to string wins together. And their next game against the badly depleted Philadelphia Eagles is arguably the last really favorable chance for that this year.

This is important. Under Brian Schottenheimer this year, the Cowboys have yet to accomplish back to back wins. There just seems to be a true lack of confidence on the team. Putting two together could go a long way to creating some. And if we are going to keep talking about getting to the playoffs as the NFCE representative, it would also get them to .500 and keep them in striking range of the current division leading New York Giants. Then we can see what they can do against some stiffer competition down the stretch.

Sean – The Titans have certainly been in a lot of games this season, Tony Pollard has had some monstrous games out of the backfield, and will have to love what he saw from his former team's defense against Christian McCaffrey, and they have yet another defensive minded head coach to scheme against Schottenheimer and try to have the upper hand. Plus, the Cowboys have played two other games against the AFC South, narrowly escaping with a win at the Houston Texans, and beating the Colts in a fashion that set us up for exactly what we saw in the latest loss.

So yes Tom, getting out of this game with a win is a solid enough goal, and one might even go as far as saying controlling it for four quarters like we'd want to see is a sign of progress to play the first complete game of the season. But, it's not a win against a conference opponent not in the NFC East, something Schotty is still searching for. How do you have any playoff viability if you can't beat anyone good in your own conference? It's not in any way a win against a quality opponent, and it has absolutely just as much of a chance to be forgotten about as every other win this season against those Texans, Colts, Commanders, and Eagles.

The Cowboys are playing so poorly they have a chance to sweep the Eagles for the first time since 2021, winning their second game of the week next week in order to do so, and still have it all be a footnote.

I guess if you want to circle back on one of these wins with the context we now have through ten weeks, beating a Commanders team that went on to beat the Giants team the Cowboys are still chasing at home is a decent result. But nobody will forget that Jayden Daniels was knocked out of the game, and nobody is getting away with moving the goalposts a country mile for this season along with that. From potential playoff contenders to happy to be in the mix with the… 5-3-1 Giants? I'm not buying it, and oh by the way the most glaring and obvious thing the Giants do better than the Cowboys to start having instant success is coach better under their new leadership.

Tom – Maybe that's another way to look at things. The Cowboys need to prove they can show they are a viable NFL franchise, one that can play a full sixty minutes and close out games. Those elements have been lacking all season, even in some of their wins. The team is so up and down, even from play to play. They will have a strong defensive stop on one play, maybe a sack or a run stuff, then give up a big gainer – usually, it seems, on third and long. And the offense is not exempt. They will have several good plays, then a couple of dropped passes or a breakdown in the blocking scheme, and Bryan Anger or Brandon Aurbrey will have to come out. And if things weren't bad enough, Aubrey is showing signs of having the yips, missing kicks we used to take for granted.

And that's one other way to consider it. This team just does not have one thing it can consistently hang its hat on. Even Dak Prescott will have several bad reps in a row and things will stall.

There isn't one fatal flaw on this team. There are a collection of things that are making it so hard to keep up with their competition. I don't know how they can start to heal these wounds, but it would certainly be nice to see at least one thing get cleared up. That just might be the real goal for them.

Oddly, the news of Christian Parker being forced to surrender play-calling duties to Mike Pettine is, for me, encouraging. At least it shows the owner sees the problem and is willing to do something. (Yeah, I don't think this was Schotty's call.)

Sean – For fans with one eye toward the offseason already, the only good news about what you said is that the team is putting themselves in a position where getting help for any position group in the offseason would be seen as a good move. The bad news is we know how this always goes. The Cowboys will have to prioritize one thing or the other, meaning they sell us on already being stable at some other position to do this, and then that very thing holds them back on gamedays.

Dak Prescott starting a streak with this Titans game of not only playing well but winning would be great, but by season's end if they still aren't a playoff team, we'll have to do it all over again in year 12 for Dak and hope for a third straight year of good health from him. The defense needs an answer for how not to waste Quinnen Williams and Joey Porter Jr. right now, but nobody has those answers.

I do also like the idea of Pettine having more overall control now, to run this defense through the front seven where the denser nucleus of talent has shown itself. Not just Williams playing like a game wrecker, but Shemar James and even Caleb Downs as a box player too. This is almost surely more stable ground to build something around than Christian Parker's preferred position groups, cornerback and safety, where the injuries have been rampant for years.

Still, I can't recall a time where both the present and the future feels so shaky for the Cowboys, or trust in the leadership that can get an organization through shaky times is so low. After all, Pettine has only been here since October, and now he's calling plays? I don't know if we're even grasping how much of a structural failure it takes to get to that point in any season.

Maybe they'll still win the NFC East and find the faintest something to hang their hat on? Who knows.

One thing I do know is that it's Brawl of the Wild week as Montana State hosts Montana in one of the best rivalries in college football, and I can't wait for that! Here's hoping it's not just a fun Saturday but a successful Sunday for our Cowboys as well, Tom. Thanks for chatting.
