Arsenal’s late goalkeeper gamble says everything about their season and WSL race for Europe

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Arsenal goalkeeper Misa Rodriguez is going up for the corner. It's November, but don't think about that.

This is crunch time and your top three hopes are currently snagged in a vice called Michele Kang's London City Lionesses and Issy Goodwin's right foot. Thirty seconds stand between you and a 1-1 draw, which ideally you'd avoid. You've already drawn four times this season in the Women's Super League (in a row, a league record). And while you drew six times last season and still finished second, that was after 22 games. It's November. This is game number 10. And now that you mention it, you've only scored 11 goals so far, only two from set pieces.

So yeah, Rodriguez is up for the corner. Head coach Renee Slegers has her hands clasped in an almost prayer on the touchline. Frida Maanum is staring incredulously at the empty Arsenal goal 90 yards behind her. Because this is where Arsenal are right now. And as the corner goes out of play well before getting anywhere near Rodriguez, referee Melissa Burgin signals for a London City goal kick and, seconds later, the match's end; it is also where Arsenal are right now.

"We wanted and needed three points," Slegers said in her post-match press conference. "At the moment, with our position, how we need to stay connected and compete for the top three, this one was very important for us."

There's no point sugarcoating it: This was an important match. Not a top-three decider but certainly a top-three probability indicator, what punters might call an educated bet. Another draw puts Arsenal sixth in the table at the almost half-way point of the season, 14 points in the bag. Turn your head one way, they're just six points off London City Lionesses in third. Turn your head the other way, they're just five points off Crystal Palace in 12th.

Generally it's at this point that the WSL table begins to take shape, the various stratified leagues coalescing and congealing, the trio — and sometimes quartet — of Chelsea, Manchester City, Arsenal and Manchester United beginning to pull away. That's not the case this season. While the title race looks to be moulding into a two-pronged sprint between two familiar faces – Chelsea edging ahead after defeating reigning champions City 1-0 on Saturday – the shape of the third-place battle is messier, juicier, more anarchic.

London City sit third but only two points separate them from Manchester United in fourth. Tottenham Hotspur are close behind in fifth, just one point off United, with Arsenal three points behind them, and Aston Villa a point off in seventh.

Dubbing this some kind of tectonic shift is premature, but that we're here, calculating only in half-jest whether Arsenal have a better chance of reaching Europe next season by triumphing in it this season than via the league table, speaks to something.

What precisely that is remains ineffable for now. London City, for all their obvious individual effervescence, remain largely a team of just that, a compilation of greatest hits and glitter, who know all the words but are still getting to know the music.

Arsenal multiple times slipped through the pockets of their exposed midfield and found joy on the counter in the first half. Yet, that head coach Eder Maestre seemed content to allow the gaps in midfield to remain until the second half, which felt not like a sign of merely his faith in his defence and the aggression of Spanish international Mapi Leon, nor the excellence of goalkeeper Mary Earps, but Arsenal's own impotence in the final third.

Seventeen minutes into the match, Sky Sports flashed a graphic on screen stating that Arsenal's conversion rate of 5.49 per cent was the lowest in the WSL since the 2021-22. Only three of Arsenal's 17 shots on Sunday registered an xG of higher than 0.10, with Maanum's opener after 10 minutes the highest at 0.18.

Despite boasting the second-most touches in the final third in the league (443), Arsenal sit fifth in the table for xG (19.2), sixth for big chances (25), fifth for big chances missed (20), joint-11th for goals scored (11). They sit second-from-bottom for big chance conversion (18.2 per cent). They've kept two clean sheets this season (sixth best in the league), the last back in September in (another) draw with newly-promoted Crystal Palace.

They sit joint-third in the league for goals conceded (11), yet with an xGA of 8.2 (second-best in the league), which means they're actually underperforming in defence by nearly three goals (joint-12th worst in the league alongside Spurs). And as Leon effortlessly threaded a pass through Arsenal's midfield to striker Goodwin just 80 seconds after Maanum's opener, Goodwin finished the move with an exquisite show of strength, power and finesse despite the three Arsenal defenders around her; this stat seemed to scream in the sudden silence of the Emirates.

The inquest into Arsenal's start to the season has been extensive. Fingers have been pointed at muddled recruitment strategies, tactics, confidence in the front line, confidence in the back line, squad bloat, the merits of playing your best goalscorer as a No 10, whether a right-back is still a right-back if they're played at left-back, injuries, inconsistent team selection, whether Arsenal have a deep-rooted problem with the entire month of October, timings of substitutions, quality of chances, bad luck. Nikita Parris told BBC Sport before Sunday's match that Arsenal have lost their "fear factor" at home, having yet to claim a win this season.

All of which, when rolled and smushed together, can feel like reasons Arsenal are chasing in one of the most unpredictable races for a European spot in recent memory.

Yet, if there's a lesson to be gleaned from the weekend, it's the value in taking risks this season, in being a team that still can stir scares. Maestre spoke after Sunday's draw of his team's exigency to play brave football as they have done with increasing competence. United, for all the early season risk and struggles of bringing in Eva Olid just three weeks before the new season, look all the better for it, now unbeaten in eight WSL matches. Villa, after parting ways with Natalia Arroyo, sit one point off Arsenal under interim head coach Brian Sorensen.

"You have to be brave to make that decision in that moment," said Bompastor, a wry smile spreading upon explaining her decision to substitute 31-year-old centre-back Kadeisha Buchanan for 20-year-old Veerle Buurman just 11 minutes into Saturday's match against City, ultimately shifting the game's dynamic in Chelsea's favour. Because it is those types of gambles that will define not only the title winners but also who will join them in Europe.
