Cadillac Cancels Plan To Build Its Own V6 F1 Engine

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

General Motors is pumping the brakes on its Formula 1 engine timeline.

Cadillac will push back the debut of its in-house F1 power unit by one full year, shifting from 2029 to 2030 so its factory engine program launches alongside Formula 1's newly proposed V8 regulations. Rather than dumping millions of dollars into building a complex V6 hybrid for a single lame-duck season, the American team will extend its customer engine partnership with Ferrari through the end of 2029.

Originally, GM planned to transition Cadillac from a Ferrari customer outfit into a full-fledged works team in 2029, operating under the assumption that the current 1.6-liter V6 hybrid cycle would run through 2030 before new rules arrived in 2031.

That timeline accelerated ahead of this week's Las Vegas Grand Prix. Formula 1, the FIA, and existing engine manufacturers reached an agreement in principle to cut the current engine cycle short and introduce the 2.1-liter turbocharged V8s with scaled-back hybrid assistance starting in 2030. The paperwork still awaits final sign-off from the FIA World Motor Sport Council next month, McLaren CEO Zak Brown confirmed it was happening during Thursday's team principals' press conference.

"Yes, we would remain a customer of Ferrari through 2029 and bring the Cadillac power unit online in 2030 with the V8," Cadillac F1 CEO Dan Towriss told Motorsport.com in Las Vegas.

"I think we're glad to see the series really coming together under the leadership of the FIA to set that direction for a V8 in 2030," Towriss said. "We're super happy with that." Why not? General Motors has a rich history of building race-winning V8 engines, from muscle cars to sports cars to IndyCars.

Work back in the States is ramping up fast. General Motors will officially open its dedicated F1 power unit headquarters in Charlotte, North Carolina, on December 18. Once the doors open, engineers will temporarily run parallel development programs-using their existing V6 groundwork as a baseline while pivoting primary design efforts toward the 2030 V8.

"Well, for a period of time, we're working on both," Towriss explained. "So they've already got a V6 base to build off of and then we're working towards the V8. There are a number of designs that are in motion now, so there's a lot of good foundation for what we're trying to build and the direction we're headed in."

Of course, there are questions about the team's future after news broke that Mark Walter is reportedly exploring a sale of TWG's stake in Cadillac Formula 1 amid the ongoing DOJ and SEC investigation into the holding company. With an asking price of no less than $3 billion, there are rumored to be at least seven interested parties.
