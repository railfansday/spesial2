Troy Aikman's latest comments invite speculation that he's hoping for an early exit

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

In isolation, the latest comments from ESPN’s Troy Aikman about Monday night partner Joe Buck would have barely been noticed. In context, they sent another shockwave through the folks who monitor the comings-and-goings of broadcasters.

Aikman’s crack that Buck “always wanted a one-man booth” and the snark-oozing observation that Buck is “ubiquitous” happened two days after their mutual boss, ESPN president Jimmy Pitaro, called the media coverage of the Aikman-Buck feud “disappointing.”

That was a clear message to the folks in position to add fuel to the fire: Knock it off. Aikman, in his first opportunity to speak publicly during a weekly Thursday radio segment on 96.7 The Ticket in Dallas, sprayed lighter fluid on it.

The development has sparked an uptick in speculation that Aikman will be gone not after the upcoming Super Bowl, but before it. The real question, as we see it, is whether that’s what Aikman wants.

If he knows it’s done after the Super Bowl, why delay? And if he can say just enough to get ESPN to choose to take the cringe out of an awkward kitchen — but not so much to be fired for cause — he gets the rest of the year off, with full pay.

Given that Aikman has well-documented aspirations to work for a team, why be tied to ESPN through Valentine’s Day? If ESPN tells him to take the rest of the year off, he can immediately start looking for a landing spot in a front office.

Sure, Aikman would be giving up the chance to call a Super Bowl. But he has already called six of them. And this one will play out with increasing chatter about his future, making the perfunctory ESPN press conference during Super Bowl week a standing-room event with endless questions about the Aikman-Buck relationship and Aikman’s next step.

For now, everyone will be watching and waiting. On Monday night, Aikman and Buck will be back in the booth together. Will Buck make reference to the “one-man booth” remark? If so, will Aikman take the bait?

With seven Monday night games and a wild-card broadcast to go, there will be plenty of chances for the simmer to become a boil during a broadcast. The closer it comes to happening, the more inclined ESPN may be to give Aikman what he possibly wants.
