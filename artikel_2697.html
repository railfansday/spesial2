Steelers owner makes clear statement on Mike McCarthy's future amid Aaron Rodgers feud

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Steelers fans heard from now-former QB Aaron Rodgers and coach Mike McCarthy on Wednesday, but owner Art Rooney II stayed out of the limelight as the franchise fell into upheaval.

Rooney broke his silence on Thanksgiving, releasing a statement backing McCarthy and making some sharp criticism of Rodgers a day after the franchise unceremoniously released the four-time MVP.

Here's a look at what Rooney said and what it means for McCarthy's future with the Steelers.

Rooney spoke to the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette and ESPN on Thursday to offer his support for McCarthy and help explain what led to Rodgers' benching and release.

"I just want to say that I have 100 percent confidence in Mike McCarthy and I expect he'll be our coach this season, next season and beyond," Rooney said, indicating the first-year coach's job isn't at risk despite Rodgers' allegations against him.

Rooney also downplayed concerns Rodgers seemed to raise about McCarthy's health.

"Mike's as healthy as the rest of us," Rooney said. "Health was not an issue. I'm not sure where that part comes."

Rodgers was the central story in the NFL and in sports on Wednesday, but as reports corroborated many of his allegations against McCarthy — including that the veteran coach fell asleep during a meeting, missed key meetings and didn't get to know defensive players — questions started to be raised about his coaching future in Pittsburgh.

A coaching change would put the Steelers in an unprecedented position, as they have long has more stability in that role than any other franchise. While Rooney's statement wasn't definitive, it does give McCarthy an opening to stick around beyond 2026.

As for Rodgers, Rooney referred to his demand that he be released if McCarthy didn't give up play-calling as "insubordination" that forced a decision to be made.

"Insubordination can't be tolerated in any organization," Rooney said. "When you start getting ultimatums, you can't live with that."

Rooney said Rodgers was not being a "good teammate" with the way his words affected the rest of the team just days before a game.

"Decisions were made on what we believe is in the best interest of the Pittsburgh Steelers," Rooney said. "Our main focus right now is preparing for tomorrow's game."

Rodgers undoubtedly has more to say, but the Steelers' brain trust is united in moving forward without him as the team tries to keep itself in the AFC playoff picture.
