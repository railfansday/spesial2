Wings drill down on offense, as losses mount and goals vanish

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

DETROIT — The Red Wings aren't scoring enough goals these days, not nearly enough.

So, the entirety of practice Thursday, Nov. 19 was spent on offense, attempting to rectify and improve an area of the Wings' game that everyone suspected would be an issue — and has turned out to be one.

The 4-1 loss to Boston Wednesday, Nov. 18, was the Wings' sixth defeat in seven games. They've scored 12 goals during that span, with two goals or fewer in six of those games.

"It goes back to working hard and sticking with the process," said Alex DeBrincat, who has one goal in his last 13 games. "The tides will turn eventually. It's frustrating, but we're working hard to be good at both sides.

"It's just not working for us right now and we have to figure out a way, and it's on us, to figure out and break through."

Thursday's practice was a touch more physical than normal, and there were some battle drills. But coach Todd McLellan insisted it wasn't intended to be any sort of punishment for the way the Wings played against Boston.

"It can be construed as a little bit of punishment, but that's not the case," he said. "That doesn't work anymore for coaches. But we have to be a little heavier and harder and hold on to the puck a little bit more to create more offense, so some drills were created that way. We have to sustain attack time a little bit more. A lot of those drills were built for that.

"The whole day was offensive oriented. On the other side of the coin, you have to have somebody to defend it so we're working on both sides of our game. But the principles were based on offense."

"Not just offense passing and shooting into the net. It's about creating space and holding on to pucks and being physical in certain areas. Your forecheck is part of offense. Understanding what a shot might do and finding garbage around the net. Those concepts, we worked on today and they were approached with directness and seriousness."

With Dylan Larkin not a member of Red Wings anymore, much of the pressure to produce offensively falls on the trio of Lucas Raymond, DeBrincat and Viktor Arvidsson. They are the lone three forwards who have a history of scoring goals and creating offense in the NHL.

Raymond, after a slow start this season, has come around and has 18 points in his last 14 games. But DeBrincat and Arvidsson (one point in nine games) have struggled.

"I put a lot of pressure on myself regardless," DeBrincat said. "I want to be a guy who helps the team win in any way. One of those ways is scoring goals and creating plays for my linemates, and they're just not going in right now.

"So, you keep working hard and it'll turn eventually. Just waiting for that moment right now."

DeBrincat has had similar streaks in his career before, and can go back to those situations to learn from.

"It's just a matter of getting back to the basics," DeBrincat said. "Getting to the net. It just takes one to get going, and we have to get that one and put it in the net and get going as a team."

Arvidson, too, gets back to previous experiences to battle his way out of a scoring drought.

"You tend to get between situations and kind of hesitate," Arvidsson said. "You just have to go back on your instincts and trusting your decisions and not second guess yourself, play like you've always been playing and go on from there."

McLellan agrees that a single goal, from any source, could ignite the Wings.

"Stick with it and when one comes, the rest will," McLellan said. "Maybe that's what our team needs, too, is just to get one first from an unusual character and see where it (goes)."

Despite the losses piling up and the Wings being stagnant in the standings, DeBrincat feels the Wings aren't losing confidence.

"There's a lot of confidence in this room," DeBrincat said. "Other than (Boston loss), and I guess it's tough to say about a five-game losing streak, but I didn't think we played that bad. We gave a couple of third period leads and it's definitely something we have to learn from. But we haven't been playing that bad, and good defensively. (The Boston game) was an anomaly, but hopefully we can come back and nip it in the bud quickly."
