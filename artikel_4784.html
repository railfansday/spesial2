Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, reports of cryptocurrency theft involving tampered Ledger hardware wallets have emerged in Dubai. Eastern Europe recorded the steepest decline in crypto activity of any region tracked by Chainalysis, and Google revealed it had identified hackers using AI agents to fully automate the discovery and exploitation of software vulnerabilities for the first time.

Two Ledger owners have reported cryptocurrency thefts involving hardware wallets they say were purchased from an authorized retailer in Dubai, raising concerns that a hardware wallet tampering campaign had also spread to the UAE.

An X user posting as Rewinsky on Monday said they found their Ledger Flex had been tampered with after reporting the loss of about 1,300 Solana (SOL. Mark Karpelès’ Ledger Implant OSINT registry subsequently confirmed the device as containing a hardware implant.

A second owner, posting as New Babylon Order Worldwide, said Tuesday that 507,000 USDt (USDT) was stolen from their Ledger Stax on Saturday. They said the device was bought new at Virgin Megastore Dubai on Oct. 11, two days after Ledger publicly disclosed an investigation into crypto losses involving devices sold by Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis.

The reports suggest the Ledger device tampering has extended past retailers in just Asia and Europe. Ledger lists Virgin Megastores among its authorized retailers in the United Arab Emirates.

Eastern Europe recorded the steepest decline in cryptocurrency activity among the regions tracked by Chainalysis in the year ended June 2026, falling 7.9% even as retail-sized transfers increased in value, according to a report published Tuesday.

The region’s crypto economy shrank to $642.1 billion from $697.1 billion a year earlier, compared with a 1.6% decline globally, the report states. Russia, which accounted for roughly 44% of the regional total, saw activity fall 14.7% to $284.1 billion over the period. By contrast, Belarus recorded 27.6% growth, while Bulgaria, Moldova and Ukraine saw smaller increases.

The value of transfers of $10,000 or more to crypto services fell 17.4%, from $591.5 billion to $488.6 billion, according to the report.

The value of smaller transfers to crypto services, meanwhile, rose 8.3%. These retail-sized transfers accounted for 13.1% of the value sent to crypto services in Eastern Europe, the highest share of any region studied and above the global average of 10.1%.

Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) said it had, for the first time, identified attackers using AI large language model (LLM) frameworks to automate software vulnerability discovery and exploitation without step-by-step human direction.

In a report published Monday, the researchers described a tool found on an exposed staging server that compared software versions, identified potential flaws, and generated and executed exploit code.

“In the past quarter, GTIG identified threat actors using LLM frameworks to fully automate vulnerability discovery and exploitation without a human in the loop for the first time,” the researchers said.

GTIG identified an exposed staging server hosting a multi-agent vulnerability research and weaponization framework, set up to conduct continuous differential analysis comparing code before and after patch announcements on popular web applications. The model would autonomously discover and exploit software vulnerabilities.

Google warned that these automated workflows give organizations less time to apply security patches before attackers develop ways to exploit vulnerable systems.
