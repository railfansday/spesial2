Part 26: Five builds, five differences

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Five builds are done: MuleSoft, Boomi, SAP, Azure and AWS. Each one hosted the same nine tools from Part 7 and kept the same approval rule from Part 6. This part puts them on one screen: what actually differed, in five rows.

It's a summary of Parts 11 to 25, all documentation-only. On 15 November 2026 I spot-checked two of the facts most likely to move, the MuleSoft MCP Connector version and Boomi's runtime regions, and neither had changed. There's no winner here. That needs hands-on builds.

The difference: how many MCP servers the agent sees, and who owns the door in front of them. On SAP, it's the ERP vendor. On the others, it's your integration or cloud team.

The difference: low-code versus code, and whose runtime it runs on. The build experience itself is the one criterion I couldn't score anywhere from documentation.

The difference: in the agent, in a gateway, or in the vendor's rules of access. The strongest designs put the hard rules outside the agent's code.

The difference: SAP is the only platform where the approval lives in the system that posts the invoice. Everywhere else, the approval is assembled from parts.

The difference: whether traces leave the platform. That decides whether one operations team can watch agents from several vendors.

Some things stayed the same on all five, and they matter more than the differences:

The provisional averages are MuleSoft 3.3, Boomi 2.8, SAP 3.0, Azure 3.6 and AWS 3.6. But they're documentation scores, capped at 4, with a different number of criteria scored for SAP. Part of the gap is simply how much each vendor documents.

The differences above are more useful than the averages. They tell you which platform fits your constraints: where your approvers work, who owns your gateway, and whether your operations team needs one place to see every agent.
