‘The Rings of Power’s Most Shocking Proposal Just Set Up Númenor’s Ultimate Fate

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Editor's note: The below contains spoilers for The Rings of Power Season 3 Episode 5.

The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power continues to throw new curveballs at its audience while pivoting toward J.R.R. Tolkien's lore in its exciting third season — especially with the recent updates to the Númenor plot. At the tail end of Episode 5, a harrowing proposal is made by Ar-Pharazôn (Trystan Gravelle) with the intention of uniting the split factions of Númenór under a single crown.

For casual fans, The Rings of Power made a wild move by revealing that Míriel and Pharazôn would be married. The whole thing comes on the heels of the bloodshed raging between the faction called the King's Men and the underground sect known only as "the Faithful," who remain servants of Eru Ilúvatar and the Valar. Hoping to stop the war before it truly begins, Míriel accepts Pharazôn’s proposal, with the pair to be wed to save Númenor from complete and total civil war. For many, this probably comes as much of a shock as Gil-galad’s (Benjamin Walker) restoring of her sight, but Tolkien lovers knew this had to be coming.

In the author's greater legendarium, Pharazôn is traditionally said to have forced marriage on Míriel, though some accounts note that it was voluntary due to her infatuation with him. In either event, her cousin usurps her throne, eventually sailing to Middle-earth to wage war against Sauron (Charlie Vickers) — just as the Elves intend for Pharazôn to do in The Rings of Power. Although the Númenóreans take the Dark Lord prisoner and bring him back to Númenor, the whole thing is a trick to seduce Pharazôn into Sauron’s service, as the hearts of Men are soon turned to Melkor/Morgoth worship. This leads Númenor into the further depths of evil. Ultimately, this leads to the island realm’s destruction, and it appears that’s where the Prime Video series is headed.

'The Rings of Power' Season 3 is currently streaming on Prime Video.

Within the context of The Rings of Power, it’s easy to see why Míriel makes this choice. She believes that this union will unite their peoples, stop further bloodshed, and allow the Faithful to practice their religion in peace without the threat of death. After having her literal sight restored, it's likely Míriel sees this as nothing more than a sign from the Valar themselves that she is on the right path. Perhaps Míriel believes this is how to prevent her vision of the Great Wave — back in Season 1 — from coming true. Maybe she sees this marriage as a way to keep the King's Men from having complete control of the island kingdom's culture and voice. Either way, her goals are undoubtedly quite different from Pharazôn's.

However you slice it, this marriage appears to be the most viable option for the Númenoreans, though it is clearly a solution that Elendil (Lloyd Owen), who rides after her, believes to be ill-fated. Of course, he's ultimately right. Just as Season 3 kicked off with a gut-punch in the Númenor plot after Eärien's (Ema Horvath) death, so too will this marriage end in disaster. In Tolkien's work, Sauron's eventual seduction of Pharazôn leads him to sail to Valinor to ensure that Númenor would never fall. The result is actually the exact opposite, as Eru Ilúvatar sends that Great Wave to annihilate not only Pharazôn and his forces, but the entire island kingdom itself. This is what Míriel saw coming years ago, and, in fear of what is to come, is possibly why she is so willing to do all she can to prevent it.

Since much of Season 3 has dealt in themes of divine providence, holy missions, and the tension between darkness and light — seen in how The Rings of Power has introduced the Secret Fire as well — it doesn't come as a surprise that Míriel accepts the proposal after the Elves arrive and her vision is returned to her. This plot pivot will no doubt set up greater tensions between the Faithful and the King's Men, despite the supposed peace the union should produce, and the bigger question now is how Elendil and his sons will respond. Perhaps this is what finally gets them to leave Númenor to establish a truly united kingdom that would oppose Sauron and all of Mordor.
