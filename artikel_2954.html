Clemson: 3 Things Learned From Win Over Duke

The Clemson Tigers didn't have time to feel sorry for themselves after losing 20–6 to Georgia Tech last week. Clemson had a short week before getting set to face the Duke Blue Devils on the road.

The Tigers came into Friday night's game with a 6–4 record, needing a win over either Duke or South Carolina to clinch a winning record on the season. (For Clemson, it's the little things that matter in 2026.)

Clemson's defense did the bulk of the work on Friday night, limiting Duke to just 247 yards of total offense. The Tigers gained 384 total yards of offense. Clemson and the Blue Devils both have strong defenses and lackluster offense, and the Tigers got the best of the battle on Friday night with a 20–10 win.

Clemson will next play South Carolina next week with a chance to finish the regular season 8–4. That's not impressive by Clemson's standards, but the program can gain some momentum heading into 2027.

Here are three things we learned from Clemson's win on Friday night.

220 of Clemson's 384 yards came on the ground. 51 of those yards came from quarterback Trent Pearman, who got the start with Christopher Vizzina out for a concussion and Tait Reynolds out for the season with injury. Pearman scored a touchdown on the ground.

Jarvis Green led Clemson in rushing with 84 yards on 16 carries. Chris Denson rushed for 71 yards and a score on 16 carries.

Clemson got it done via the defense and running the football, which is a staple of head coach Dabo Swinney's teams.

Coming into the game, the fear for Clemson was how to stop Nate Sheppard, who is breaking Duke records almost by the game this year. Sheppard scored on a 55-yard touchdown run and finished the night with 116 yards on 19 carries.

But he was basically Duke's only offense. The Blue Devils only had 98 yards on the ground total as Walker Eget accounted for -17 yards. Eget was limited to 149 yards and one interception through the air.

The Tigers aren't throwing in the towel and played a solid game against a good ACC opponent one week after Clemson suffered one of the worst losses of the Swinney era. The Tigers should be plenty motivated to beat South Carolina.
