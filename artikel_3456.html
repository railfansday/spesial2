SPOILERS AHEAD for The Body season 1.After eight captivating episodes, The Body's story is far from over. Creator Quinn Shephard's dark coming-of-age drama has taken the world by storm in a matter of days, cementing itself as a smash hit Netflix original before its opening week has even concluded. From horrifying mass hysteria to homoerotic friendships, The Body seamlessly mixes an early 2000s aesthetic with a spiraling mystery that forces the viewer to question everything they see, hear, and think about the overarching story.

Unlike the legions of straight-to-streaming miniseries that aim to tell a comprehensive story in a single season, Netflix's newest psychological horror series left the door open for plenty of expansion. Amazingly, nearly every storyline was left unresolved, ramping up The Body's season 2 potential in one of the riskiest moves the streaming platform has seen in years. As promising as the terrifying teen drama is, however, it's impossible to tell whether the gamble will pay off in the long run.

As of mid-November 2026, no decision has been made regarding The Body's future— but it seems likely that season 2 will be confirmed sooner or later. While the campy, anachronistic period piece isn't everybody's cup of tea, The Body has had an amazing showing thus far, which should bode for an impending renewal. For the most part, critics agree the style overtakes the substance, but the irreverent tone and stunning visuals of the drama make for an enthralling binge-watch nonetheless.

Of course, what truly makes the new release so impressive is its reception from the viewers. The Body's audience response on Rotten Tomatoes sits at a staggering 97% approval rate, while the critical opinion rests at a respectable 87%. Similarly, The Body is currently the most-watched series on Netflix according to FlixPatrol, a website that tracks viewership metrics on major streaming sites.

Between widespread fandom activity online and global critical and commercial acclaim, The Body should, in theory, be a safe bet for renewal. Unfortunately, it's impossible to be certain. Historically, sapphic supernatural teen dramas have been confined to one-season wonders, like First Kill, I Am Not Okay with This, and Teenage Bounty Hunters. Nevertheless, the aforementioned series didn't have the prominence of a worldwide trending premiere, meaning The Body may break one of Netflix's most frustrating patterns.

Luckily, if Netflix renews the series for a second season, the St. Mark's Lions will be back in action before long. If the show-stopping debut is anything to go by, The Body season 2 could release as early as fall 2027. Season 1's principal photography began soon after the cast announcement in October 2025, meaning everything from initial filming to post-production editing to marketing and press junkets happened in the span of a single year.

Netflix’s new 8-part psychological thriller follows a school dance initiation gone wrong, chilling visions, and a town gripped by hysteria.

Within seven days, The Body has become one of Netflix's best shows of 2026, and if season 2 follows suit, it could dominate the charts again this time next year. Plus, considering showrunners Gina Gammell and Quinn Shephard already have concepts for a second season, scripting and other pre-production work could come together incredibly quickly. Likewise, since the ensemble cast has had a year to live in their roles, the characters would be fully formed and raring to go for The Body season 2. Barring any major delays, the follow-up would drop in late 2027 or very early 2028.

Despite each main character retaining some degree of unfinished business, the biggest unanswered question from The Body's first eight episodes revolves around what really happened to Grace (Sofia Wylie). The devout dancer spent nearly the entire series in an inexplicable coma, only to announce herself as "God" as soon as she woke up. Given the vast majority of the community began viewing her as a Saint in her absence, The Body could either go full-blown supernatural or pivot and establish Grace is feeding into the hysteria.

Meanwhile, the ending of The Body season 1 saw Anya (Kristina Bogic) confessing her sins to Father Franklin (Nnamdi Asomugha), not least of which being that she drove Grace home on the night she seemingly died. With Grace now awake, Anya won't be arrested for manslaughter, but her honesty may not be enough to win back the trust or affection of Elise (Sara Boustany). The fates of the Anderson siblings, the Lions, and the literal Devil are also left ambiguous, offering The Body season 2 ample room for creative elaboration and what would surely be a record-breaking sophomore showing.
