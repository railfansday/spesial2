‘The X-Files’ Writer Reimagines Adam and Eve in Brutal New Fantasy Release

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

Adam and Eve have taken the blame for what happened in the Garden of Eden for thousands of years, and Matthew Medney would like a word with the gods who built it. In his version of paradise, violence is already a fact of life by the time anyone has the vocabulary to call it a sin. The people who put humanity there still haven’t settled how much of a say humans should get in what happens to them. That’s quite a mess for two people to inherit before either of them gets anywhere near the fruit.

Medney, the former publisher of Heavy Metal, was more interested in what Adam and Eve took with them when they left. Once you know you’re going to die, there’s a reason to finish something while you’re here, and maybe even leave something behind for the person who comes next. It’s a strange upside to getting thrown out of paradise, and it sent Medney to a writer who’d already spent some time in the garden himself.

Collider can exclusively reveal The Serpent’s Vine, an adult dark fantasy graphic novel from GUNGNIR, created by Medney and co-written with The X-Files comics writer Stefan Petrucha. Illustrated by Bobby Raneshigue and Matteo Gennai, the book puts Adam and Eve in the middle of a dispute involving two other figures, Atticus and Luce. They disagree over humanity’s place in creation, and each has ambitions that reach well beyond the garden. GUNGNIR published the hardcover on November 17.

Four worlds and four completely different ideas of what a good life looks like. This is where you belong, what it would ask of you, and what you would get back in return.

You belong somewhere old enough that the mountains have names and somebody can still tell you who gave them. Middle-earth is for people who think the right thing is worth doing even when it will not be finished in their lifetime.

You want the sea, a crew, and absolutely nobody telling you where you are allowed to go. The Grand Line is the world for people whose main ambition in life is finding out what is over there.

You would rather be feared and useful than liked and useless. The Continent is a world where the monsters are the straightforward part of the job and the people are where it all gets complicated.

You want the myths to be real and loose in the actual world, and you want to find out at about thirteen that you were never anywhere near as ordinary as everybody kept telling you.

Atticus and Luce give Medney and Petrucha room to ask a question that interested them about the original story. If human beings could make a disastrous choice in Eden, what was stopping the powers that created them from making one first? “What if paradise wasn’t perfect? What if the gods were just as flawed as the people they created?” Medney said in a statement shared with Collider.

“The idea that really drove this story was that death created scarcity, scarcity created inspiration, and inspiration gave birth to creativity.”

For Petrucha, that invitation came thirty years after he explored a Gnostic version of Genesis in 1996’s The Bandy Man. This time, he scripted Medney’s story for Raneshigue and Gennai, whose artwork takes Eden from lush landscapes to scenes of graphic violence. Gennai also illustrated the cover, with Marcio Freire on colors, Dave Sharpe on letters, and Scarlet Witch writer Steve Orlando editing. Mohamed Samah contributed additional artwork and book design.

The Serpent’s Vine is now available in hardcover from GUNGNIR, distributed by Macmillan, for $30 (USD)/$40 (CAD). Stay tuned at Collider for more.
