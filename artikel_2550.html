SEC to vote on potentially terminating LSU’s membership amid legal battle, RJ Luis eligibility saga

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The fight between the SEC and LSU is far from over.

The SEC announced on Monday night that it filed an amended complaint and a motion for a temporary restraining order in its federal lawsuit against LSU. The conference also said that presidents and chancellors of universities in the conference will meet on Dec. 3 to "deliberate LSU's ongoing membership in the conference."

Expulsion from the conference has been something that the SEC has kept on the table throughout its legal battle. The SEC said in its lawsuit that it intends to hold a vote to consider terminating LSU's membership, via Yahoo Sports' Ross Dellenger.

"The lawsuit seeks to protect the conference's ability to govern itself and to enforce the rules and policies its member universities have voluntarily adopted," the league said in a statement. "The conference maintains its right to associate only with institutions that meet its established standards and expectations of membership and that demonstrate appropriate institutional control of their athletics programs.

"Monday's filing advances the conference's obligations to defend its governance framework and the shared interests of its member universities."

LSU and the SEC have been feuding for months over the eligibility of athletes who had previously signed professional contracts. LSU football coach Lane Kiffin moved to potentially add players who had signed contracts with NFL teams to his roster earlier this fall, but the three players at the center of that battle never made the Tigers' roster.

The issue started after the NCAA's five-for-five rule, which stated that players will have five seasons of eligibility moving forward. That's led to players filing lawsuits in order to gain another season of eligibility, as the five-for-five rule only starts in the high school class of 2023 and beyond. The SEC also passed a rule this past summer banning athletes who had previously signed pro contracts from playing for teams in the conference.

The fight between LSU and the SEC was renewed in recent weeks over RJ Luis Jr., a former St. John's standout who signed a two-way contract with the Utah Jazz after he went undrafted in 2025. Luis played in three preseason games with the Celtics, but received a preliminary injunction earlier this month to play for the Tigers. A judge in Louisiana ruled that the SEC and NCAA could not penalize LSU for playing Luis.

Luis then appeared in a game for the Tigers a few days later. Basketball coach Will Wade said after that game simply that everyone who had taken the court was eligible to do so.

But the next day, LSU president Wade Rousse said he instructed Wade to not play Luis moving forward. In the SEC's lawsuit, the league cited evidence that Wade supported Luis in his legal efforts to play. Luis' attorney said in a statement that LSU was threatened with expulsion from the conference again, too.

LSU is a founding member of the SEC, and joined alongside nine other universities back in 1933. While kicking LSU out of the league completely feels like it may be a drastic step, the legal battle between the institution and the conference has only escalated since it started. Neither side appears to be backing down.
