Jasprit Bumrah goes wicketless on Day 1 at Christchurch: Star India pacer's third-longest drought by overs has fans worried

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Jasprit Bumrah goes wicketless on Day 1 at Christchurch: Star India pacer's third-longest drought by overs has fans worried originally appeared on Cricket News. Add Cricket News as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

India pacer Jasprit Bumrah went wicketless on Day 1 against New Zealand in Christchurch, bowling 19 overs for 37.

His drought has reached 38.4 overs, the third-longest stretch of his Test career.

He also tried a new one-handed run-up, and fans are split over his form.

Jasprit Bumrah's long wait for a wicket carried on into the second Test in Christchurch, where the star India pacer ended Day 1 empty-handed and left plenty of supporters anxious about his form. It wasn't a day of poor bowling by any measure, yet the scoreboard once again told a different story.

India won the toss on Friday, November 27, 2026, and chose to bowl first on a green-tinged surface at Hagley Oval. New Zealand reached 246 for 5 in 85 overs by stumps, with Tom Blundell unbeaten on 49 and Glenn Phillips on 32. Bumrah's own figures read 19-4-37-0, an economy rate of 1.95, which made him the most economical bowler in the side.

Bumrah hasn't taken a wicket since he removed Tom Latham in the first over of the second innings in Wellington, and he has now gone 38.4 overs without a breakthrough, the third-longest wicketless stretch across innings in his 53-Test career.

His day was full of near-misses. He beat the outside edge again and again, and he drew a thick edge from Devon Conway that KL Rahul put down at second slip. In the 56th over, Rishabh Pant couldn't hold a difficult, high chance offered by Daryl Mitchell off his bowling.

This is also the longest he has gone without a wicket in a single series, as he passed his previous worst of 36 overs, set in the 2021 World Test Championship final against New Zealand.

Only twice in his career has he bowled more wicketless overs across innings.

The longest of those runs covered the end of the 2021 home series against England and the WTC final in Southampton, where he went 0 for 62 across two innings. The 2018 stretch ran from the last Test of the England tour into the opening Test of the Border-Gavaskar series in Australia.

If he goes six more overs without a wicket in this match, he'd move past the 42.1 overs and into second place on that list.

The opposition is a factor too, because New Zealand have always been difficult for him. Going into Christchurch, he had 9 wickets in 5 Tests, or 10 innings, against them, which gives him 1.80 wickets per match, his lowest rate against any Test nation.

The change in action was first spotted in the 48th over of New Zealand's innings. Shubman Gill brought Bumrah on for a short spell, after Anshul Kamboj and Akash Deep had picked up two important wickets, and he was bowling to Tom Blundell when viewers noticed something unusual.

Normally, Bumrah holds the ball in both hands while he runs in. This time, he seemed to carry it in one hand from the point where he loads up, which meant his hands weren't in the position fans have seen for over ten years.

It's still unclear why he did it, but there are a few possible explanations. His pace was one, because he was clocked at 128.1 kph in that same over, and he stayed in the 130 to 135 kph range through the day. It could be that he wanted extra speed, or that he hoped to cut down on the effort of his approach.

His wicketless run is another possible reason, since players often experiment when luck isn't going their way. There was visible frustration as well, as he kicked at the surface several times when the ball went past the bat.

However, Akash Deep, who finished with 2 for 52, said the change wasn't a surprise inside the camp.

In the post-match press conference after Day 1, he said: "He was practising that run-up in practice also. Jasprit Bumrah hai wo, kuch soch ke hi kar rahe hoge (He's Jasprit Bumrah, after all. He must be doing it after giving it some thought)."

Supporters were quick to share their views online, and the reactions covered a wide range, from worry to humour to firm defence.

@cricketnmore wrote: "Jasprit Bumrah has gone 38.3 overs without a wicket, with his pace also dropping from his usual levels. 😳"

A parody account took the other side, arguing that people who only look at the scorecard are missing what happened on the field. It pointed to the dropped catch and a couple of close lbw calls.

@93Yorker wrote: "People will look at the scorecard and troll Jasprit Bumrah, but those who watched the match live know how well he bowled. A couple of LBW decisions going against him and a dropped catch have made his overall figures look ordinary"

Another user went for humour, comparing himself with the great bowler on the number of five-wicket hauls in New Zealand.

@Lommy0Tatham wrote: "Me and Jasprit Bumrah have the same number of five-wicket hauls in New Zealand."

A final post summed up what many fans feel about luck and bowling.

@rohitjuglan wrote: "Bowling isnt always rewarding...You beat the bat, catche go down, and umpires call saves the batter... Jasprit Bumrah is the ultimate reminder that greatness isn't measured just by the wickets column always.. it's about how relentlessly well you bowl."

It's easy to panic when a great bowler goes more than 38 overs without a wicket, and the figures are certainly unusual for Bumrah. A pace of around 130 kph is lower than we're used to, and the new run-up suggests he's searching for something. India's management will have noticed, and they should keep a close eye on it.

Even so, the fans should remain patient. He beat the bat repeatedly; he induced a thick edge that was dropped at second slip, and he saw another chance spilled by Pant. His economy of 1.95 shows he's still controlling the game, and wickets often follow that kind of pressure.

History helps too. He has gone through two longer droughts and ended both with wickets, so he's built for these spells. New Zealand have also been his hardest opponent, and the green pitch hasn't offered him the help it gave others.

The one real concern is the pace. If the speed doesn't return, India may have to rethink his workload. However, the sensible approach for now is to wait for Day 2, because Bumrah is more likely to end this drought than extend it.
