ESPN taps The Athletic’s Sergio Gonzalez to oversee digital coverage

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

For the second time in a week, ESPN has gone outside Bristol to fill a senior newsroom job, this time raiding The Athletic's editorial leadership.

According to Ryan Glasspiegel of Front Office Sports, ESPN has hired Sergio Gonzalez from The Athletic as its vice president of digital editorial. An ESPN spokesperson confirmed to FOS that Gonzalez will oversee editorial operations across ESPN.com and the company's social platforms, including the newsbreakers and reporters who power its digital coverage. He will report directly to Mike Foss and will begin later this month.

According to his LinkedIn, Gonzalez's career started at CBS SportsLine in 2002, where his first job was keying live statistics into the site's GameTrackers. He worked his way into writing and eventually became CBS's lead fantasy basketball analyst, producing weekly NBA columns, statistical projections for every player in the league, and on-air segments for its videos and podcasts. In 2011, he took command of the site's NBA content and launched CBS's first NBA Draft coverage, then closed out his CBS tenure as an assistant managing editor steering MLB and soccer, building CBSSports.com's soccer coverage from scratch.

The Athletic hired him in July 2018 to run its NBA vertical as senior managing editor, a post he held for more than five years before the outlet elevated him to editorial director in September 2023 and gave him a hand in shaping its overall editorial strategy.

Gonzalez is now the second editor ESPN has lured from The Athletic in under a year. The network hired Roxanna Scott last December, mere months after she took over The Athletic's daily desk, and installed her as SVP and editor-in-chief. The newly minted role consolidated control of newsgathering across television and digital under a single executive, put investigative and enterprise reporting under her command, and revived the structure ESPN had relied on under Vince Doria, the former Boston Globe sports editor who ran the network's news desk until his 2015 retirement. Scott arrived with nearly two decades at USA Today behind her, having climbed to executive editor and vice president of sports.

Her first boss in Bristol was David Roberts, the executive vice president and executive editor of sports news and entertainment, whose purview stretched across ESPN's entire news operation, along with its studio shows, digital programming, radio, and podcasts. Roberts was an architect of much of ESPN's NBA and WNBA coverage and one of Stephen A. Smith's most powerful allies inside the company, and in April, The Athletic's Andrew Marchand reported that he was preparing to retire, with Foss poised to inherit a broader portfolio.

ESPN handed Foss that title in May, placing the news division under an executive whose profile at the network had been defined largely by studio programming and his role as its chief liaison to Pat McAfee. Foss had been SVP of studio and event production since 2023 after rising through ESPN's digital side, where one of his earliest major projects was launching the network's YouTube channel. Before Bristol, he founded USA Today's For The Win and played a central role in expanding Fox Sports' digital content.

The restructuring reached Scott in late August, when ESPN eliminated her position roughly eight months into her tenure. Glasspiegel reported at the time that her exit stemmed from a broader reorganization of the news division, not another wave of layoffs.

Gonzalez follows Adi Joseph, who announced last week that he was leaving CBS Sports after nine years to become ESPN's vice president of news. Joseph had overseen CBSSports.com, 247Sports and SportsLine as CBS Sports' vice president of editorial since 2023. His tenure there began with the earliest rehearsals for CBS Sports HQ, included a stretch with CBS Sports ranked as Comscore's No. 1 sports brand, and ended amid a procession of ownership changes, with Skydance completing its takeover during his final week.

Joseph inherits the post David Kraft held until ESPN announced his retirement in January. Kraft joined the network in 1996 as an editor on its Olympic coverage, was promoted to vice president in 2021 after six years as a senior director and executive editor, and helped build the Universal News Group, which sets editorial direction across ESPN's television, audio, digital and social platforms and governs its app push alerts.

ESPN hasn't indicated whether it plans to restore a single editor-in-chief or keep the newsroom divided between Joseph and Gonzalez beneath Foss. Joseph started on Monday, and Gonzalez will join him later this month.
