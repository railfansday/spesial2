Patrick Ewing, John Wall give massive advice for Wizards’ AJ Dybantsa amid rookie spotlight

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Patrick Ewing, John Wall give massive advice for Wizards' AJ Dybantsa amid rookie spotlight originally appeared on The Sporting News. Add The Sporting News as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

Many woes arise during the first few games of an NBA rookie's career. Pressure also builds up when someone gets picked first overall in the draft. Patrick Ewing and John Wall know this too well. Both had to deal with the rookie wall while also still living up to the mantle of being the saviors of their franchises. AJ Dybantsa now has to deal with the same woe. Thankfully, the Washington Wizards rookie has an amazing support system to help him get over these problems. He even had the other two legends give him advice.

AJ Dybantsa has played seven games during his rookie campaign with the Wizards. In that span, a lot of woes started to show up in his game. His status as the first overall pick in the 2026 NBA Draft amplified that.

Dybantsa is still averaging 16.7 points on 43.3% from the field, but his 27.3% from three is holding him back. In addition, the Wizards rookie is getting 6.1 rebounds, 2.1 assists, and 1.1 steals. There's obviously a lot to fix in his game, but two former NBA first-overall picks offered the rookie some valuable advice.

Patrick Ewing and John Wall knew the struggle well, which prompted them to give Dybantsa something to carry forward, via Josh Robbins of The Athletic.

"You have to stay humble, put in the work, and continue to believe in yourself. I don't care what pick you are. You're going to have highs, and you're going to have lows. You can't get too high when things are going well, but you also cannot get too low. The guy that we have is a special talent. To be the first pick, you are a special talent. But you have to continue to do all the things that got you to be the No. 1 pick. Things are going to be rough at points. Things are going to be great at points. But stay even-keeled and just keep on working and keep on believing in yourself," Ewing declared.

The Knicks legend had a similar woe during his first year in the NBA. Despite averaging 20 points, he couldn't get his field goal percentage above 47.4%. Ewing fixed that problem and kept his field goal percentage at 50% or higher for the next nine seasons.

John Wall was another legend who offered Dybantsa some useful advice. He knows these woes well too, especially since he grew up in the Wizards system.

"Everybody's got something to say. Just let your game do the talking. That's kind of how I let it go. Everybody knows it's a team in a rebuilding process, though they're trying to win now. So, they're going to stick with you as long as they see you're giving 110 percent effort. The spotlight's a dramatic difference now. You've got social media. People have camera crews. Somebody's following you around every day. You have NIL money. So, it's a lot different than what it was when I was there. There's still pressure. Still, somebody wants you to be great. It's still an organization that's taking you and looking at you to be the next face of their franchise. So, it's a little bit different, but it's still the same. It's still basketball at the end of the day, no matter what's going on," Wall said.

Wall worked a lot for the Wizards to succeed when he was the star. It took him four seasons before he got to lead them to an NBA Playoffs berth. Even after that, Wall was still under a microscope for the rest of his career. It got worse when he reached the postseason only four times, without ever hoisting the Larry O'Brien trophy.

Dybantsa has a lot to learn as he traverses his career with the Wizards; there's a lot of truth to that. However, he will not have to face these woes alone. He has giants to stand on, like Ewing and Wall. The efficiency and media problems will dissipate soon, which will enable him to be a star.
