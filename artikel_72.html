US court blocks regulator's early review of ABC's broadcast licenses

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The FCC chairman had ordered an early review of ABC's licenses a day after President Donald Trump called for the broadcaster to fire Jimmy Kimmel earlier this year. The licenses were not set to expire until 2028.

A US judge on Wednesday barred the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) ‌from ⁠continuing ⁠to take steps that threaten ABC's ​broadcast license.

In April, FCC Chairman Brendan Carr ordered an early license review for eight ABC stations, even though their licenses were not set to expire until 2028.

ABC sued, arguing that the move was retaliation for broadcasting content that angered President Donald Trump.

On Wednesday, US District Judge Loren AliKhan said the FCC ​chair appeared to have taken "marching orders" from Trump's rhetoric.

"This is not a close case," she wrote in her ruling. "No one doubts that the commission may enforce the law in the manner it sees fit. But in doing so, defendants may not retaliate against or punish plaintiffs and other broadcast license holders for disfavored speech."

Carr's order to review ABC's licenses had come a day after Trump called for ABC to fire late-night host Jimmy Kimmel over a joke he made. His show was taken off the air for six days amid pressure from the White House.

The broadcaster, which is owned by the Walt Disney Company, welcomed the ruling on Wednesday.

"Today's decision is an important victory for the First Amendment," an ABC News spokesperson said in a statement.

Meanwhile, a spokesperson for the FCC said the ruling was "wrong on the facts and wrong on the law — as controlling Supreme Court precedent makes clear."

"We will ​continue to use our lawful authority to hold broadcasters accountable to their public interest obligations," the spokesperson added.

But one dissenting opinion within the FCC came from its sole Democratic commissioner, Anna Gomez.

She said the ruling brought "badly needed accountability to a commission and an administration that have politicized the machinery of government in a sustained campaign of censorship and control."
