Lindsay Clancy Returns to Court as Defense Seeks To Block Retrial—Inside the Home Where Her 3 Children Died and Its Puzzling Sale

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Lindsay Clancy is returning to court today as her legal team is arguing to block a retrial in the murder case against her—two months after her first case ended in a mistrial and more than three years after she was accused of murdering her three children inside the family's Massachusetts home.

Clancy, 36, has admitted to taking the lives of her daughter, Cora, 5, and sons Dawson, 3, and 8-month-old Callan, but has pleaded not guilty to the three first-degree murder charges against her, with her attorneys arguing that she was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis at the time of their deaths.

Over the course of the five-week trial, her defense team has sought to convince jurors that Clancy should not be held legally accountable for the killings because of a "lack of criminal responsibility," according to the Boston Globe.

Following 19 hours of deliberation, the 12-person jury was declared deadlocked after telling state Judge William Sullivan it was unable to reach a unanimous verdict. Sullivan instructed the jurors to return to the deliberation room and continue their efforts to reach a verdict.

Now, the judge has revealed that the jury remains deadlocked, meaning that there will be a mistrial in the case.

“I’m going to declare a mistrial at this point,” Sullivan told the court.

Now, Clancy is set to return to court today, where her legal team will attempt to argue that she can't be retried due to double jeopardy in hopes to have the charges dropped.

Her lawyers will also attempt to seek reduced charges as an alternative, meaning there could be a bench trial instead of jury trial.

Meanwhile, the prosecution has asked the judge to decline the motion to dismiss the charges, claiming they have the right to have a retrial.

Clancy's attorney, Kevin Reddington, previously claimed that he was ready to begin the retrial as soon as possible. He also requested President Donald Trump pardon Clancy; however, presidents can only pardon federal offenses.

Though Clancy was present in the courtroom in Plymouth, MA, she did not testify during the trial, which featured sworn testimony from dozens of witnesses, including her former husband, Patrick Clancy, medical professionals who treated her for mental health issues, and a former nanny who looked after her children.

The former nurse has accused several medical providers of malpractice and negligence, claiming that she was repeatedly misdiagnosed, even as her symptoms continued to worsen. On the night of Jan. 24, 2023, she reportedly experienced suicidal hallucinations and heard a voice telling her that it was her "last chance" to kill herself and her children.

The prosecution, however, is arguing that she wasn't diagnosed with postpartum depression and that the murders seem premeditated, calling attention to the fact that Clancy had reportedly looked up ways to murder on her cellphone prior to the deaths of her children.

While her husband, Patrick, was picking up a takeout meal, Clancy strangled her children and tried to take her own life by cutting her neck and wrists, then throwing herself out of her bedroom window at the family's Duxbury, MA, home.

Cora and Dawson were pronounced dead that same night, while Callan died in the hospital a few days later. Lindsay survived her injuries, but was left paralyzed from the waist down.

Clancy's husband took to the stand to testify on behalf of his wife—as did the couple's former nanny, Elaine Rossi, who described the former nurse as a "wonderful mother."

"I observed a wonderful mom who loved her kids," the nanny said, according to NBC10 Boston. "She cared for their safety, she was very concerned with Callan's eating and sleeping. She was a wonderful mother."

Rossi also told the court that Clancy had revealed to her she was "suffering from postpartum."

Patrick has previously spoken out about his wife's horrifying crimes, sitting down with The New Yorker in October 2024 to defend her against critics, insisting that she was not "a monster."

At the time of the interview, the father of three explained that he had since relocated from Massachusetts to an apartment in New York City, quietly selling off the family's Duxbury home in an off-market deal.

In the interview, Patrick insisted that he "wasn't married to a monster," explaining how his wife had been suffering from anxiety and insomnia, which were brought on by her pregnancy with Callan.

Weeks before the terrible tragedy, he said, she had been hospitalized before being sent home with antidepressants. Despite all this, he said that he believed that her only diagnosis was a generalized anxiety disorder.

“I wasn’t married to a monster—I was married to someone who got sick,” he told the publication, adding that he had chosen to speak out to prevent further "lies and misinformation" from being spread.

Shortly after the deaths of his children, Patrick had issued a personal statement in which he asked the public to forgive his wife.

"A lot of people have said they can’t imagine and they’re right, there’s absolutely nothing that can prepare you," he wrote in the statement, which was shared on a GoFundMe page set up by his loved ones.

"The shock and pain is excruciating and relentless. I’m constantly reminded of them and with the little sleep I get, I dream about them on repeat. Any parent knows, it’s impossible to understand how much you will love your kids until you have them.

"The same goes for understanding the devastation of losing them. Cora, Dawson, and Callan were the essence of my life and I’m completely lost without them.

"My family was the best thing that ever happened to me. I took so much pride in being Lindsay’s husband and a dad to Cora, Dawson, and Callan. I always reminded myself that each day with them was a new gift."

He then shared touching tributes to each of his children, calling attention to his favorite things about them, including son Dawson's "bold, brown eyes" and daughter Cora's "infection laugh."

However, despite his grief, Patrick insisted that he had forgiven his wife, while urging the public to do the same.

"I want to ask all of you that you find it deep within yourselves to forgive Lindsay, as I have," he continued.

"The real Lindsay was generously loving and caring towards everyone—me, our kids, family, friends, and her patients. The very fibers of her soul are loving. All I wish for her now is that she can somehow find peace."

While the Clancy family home does not appear to have been listed for public sale since the tragic events unfolded inside it, Patrick revealed to The New Yorker that he had, in fact, sold it to an anonymous buyer, adding during the interview that he'd just been informed the sale has officially closed.

“There’s no house anymore,” he said. “There are no kids. All that’s left is me and Lindsay.”

He explained that he cleared out the house shortly after taking a solo trip to Central America and Europe in February 2024. He drove around his neighborhood gifting his children's stuffed animals and unused arcade cards to their friends.

Built in 1972, the home has been updated with refinished hardwood floors, a newer roof, and a redone kitchen with granite counters and new appliances and flooring. The three-bedroom, 1.5-bath interior spans 1,732 square feet with a remodeled extra room between the garage and kitchen.

The home sits on a tree-lined lot on almost an acre, with a deck, stone patio, and grassy lawn. The coastal town offers an "acclaimed" school system and a beautiful beach.

According to Realtor.com® records, the home last changed hands in 2018 for $500,000.
