Darryn Peterson posts first career double-double, but the Jazz fall to the Suns to snap win streak

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Utah Jazz spent most of Friday night flirting with a comeback. Unfortunately for them, they never quite sealed the deal.

Utah's two-game winning streak came to an end with a 111-101 loss to the Phoenix Suns at the Delta Center, a game in which the Jazz struggled to find their shooting touch and spent much of the night trying to climb out of a double-digit hole.

Utah made things interesting late, cutting Phoenix's lead to four points at 105-101 with under a minute remaining, but the rally came too late and the Jazz couldn't complete the comeback.

Utah had a chance to build on its back-to-back wins in Los Angeles, but the same issues that have haunted the team throughout the season showed up again at home.

The Jazz turned the ball over 15 times, struggled to get stops in transition and left points at the free-throw line. Phoenix took advantage, building a lead as large as 16 points and forcing Utah to play catch-up for much of the night.

The Jazz's shooting certainly didn't help. Utah finished a measly 10 of 39 from deep and left 13 points at the charity stripe.

But head coach Will Hardy wasn't interested in using the poor shooting as the only explanation for the loss.

"Some nights when the shooting for us is this poor, it's easy to point at that and say, 'Oh, that's why we lost,'" Hardy said. "We missed a bunch of free throws and open 3s."

Hardy noted the team has to understand the difference between what it can and can't control. Players are trying to make shots, he said, but the Jazz still have to be better in areas that don't depend on the ball going through the basket.

"In particular tonight, I thought it was our transition defense and our rebounding," Hardy said.

The Jazz had opportunities to make a run, but too often the offense struggled to create anything easy.

Utah finished with 35 made field goals on 84 attempts, shooting 42% from the field. Ace Bailey struggled mightily from downtown, finishing 0 for 7 from beyond the arc and 3 for 11 overall. The Jazz also struggled to generate enough movement and pressure around the basket to make Phoenix's defense uncomfortable.

The problem became more noticeable as the game went on. Utah spent long stretches working deep into the shot clock without finding a clean look, and the offense lacked the kind of quick, easy scoring that can help a team build momentum.

Hardy said the Suns' physicality bothered the Jazz more in the second half, which made it even harder for Utah to find a rhythm and turn its late push into a real comeback.

Even on a tough shooting night, rookie Darryn Peterson found ways to make an impact.

He finished with 20 points alongside career highs of 10 rebounds and eight assists, giving the Jazz a little bit of everything as they tried to stay within striking distance. That effort earned him his first double-double in his young career.

He also kept attacking despite struggling from the field. He finished 7 of 20 from the floor, including 0 of 7 from 3-point range.

Peterson's ability to contribute beyond scoring was especially important as Utah searched for offense. He helped create opportunities for teammates, battled on the glass and continued to make plays even when his shot wasn't falling.

Jaxson Hayes also had a productive night with 18 points and 14 rebounds, while Brice Sensabaugh added 18 points.

Still, the Jazz couldn't generate enough offense elsewhere to erase Phoenix's lead. When the starting lineup shoots a combined 2 for 24 from 3-point range, climbing out of a hole becomes nearly impossible.

Utah will have to clean up the controllable mistakes quickly, and the late rally showed the Jazz could make things competitive. As

Sensabaugh put it earlier Friday, this roster is "good enough to compete with a lot of teams" — provided it stops digging early holes.
