10 Takeaways: Landeskog Remains Unafraid to Make His Presence Felt

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The Avalanche have won three straight games for the first time this season, putting an end to a three-game road losing streak in the process.

I wasn't planning on writing one of these until after Tuesday night's game in Columbus, but I had too many mental notes piling up as Monday's game unfolded. At the top of the list was Gabe Landeskog and his impact on the night, which had nothing to do with the scoresheet.

Also Read:Postgame Reaction: Avalanche Responded in More Ways Than One Against Chicago

1. Gabe Landeskog wasn't willing to let the hit from Artyom Levshunov on Cale Makar slide. He was in the young defenseman's kitchen all night afterward and made his feelings known postgame.

2. I agree with Landeskog that the hit should've been reviewed for more than just a two-minute minor for elbowing. Levshunov's feet left the ice when he made contact with Makar. I'd imagine that if the Avs' superstar had stayed down, Levshunov would've been ejected. I just hate that it takes an injury to send a message that those types of plays are unacceptable.

3. Nazem Kadri seems to have put that scoring slump entirely behind him. He's now got four goals and six points in his last five games. He's also got 13 points in 15 games overall, which puts him on pace for more than 70 in a full season. I'm curious to see how his season plays out because, between him and Brock Nelson, you have to imagine one of them takes a slight dip in production if they both remain at center.

4. Speaking of which, Jared Bednar alternated Kadri and Nelson's lines, even after a victory over Calgary the other night. He said after that game that he didn't think the Kadri line with T.J. Hughes and Jaden Schwartz had a good night. So, Nelson played with those wingers instead while Kadri lined up with Nic Roy and Landeskog, two guys he's already got great chemistry with. The result? Two goals from Kadri, one of which came on the power play. It was nice to see that type of response after a so-so game.

5. T.J. Hughes seemed to benefit from the change as well, recording two assists to capture his first career multi-point game.

6. Actually, all three guys on that Kadri line from Saturday night had a great bounceback. Schwartz recorded two goals to also finish with multiple points. Through the first 15 games of the season, Schwartz has recorded two points on five separate occasions. I've been very pleased with his fit. It feels like he gives the Avalanche the level of production they wanted from Ross Colton in a middle-six role over the last couple of years.

7. I wasn't sure if Bednar would go with Mackenzie Blackwood in Chicago or Columbus, but I'm very happy he chose to play his starter in the first game. I don't care that Blackwood has now started 11 of 15 games and the last three overall. Bednar is basically challenging him to be the guy, giving him the same level of respect he gave Alexandar Georgiev when he manned the net before him.

Overall, Blackwood has responded well. He's now 6-1 in his last seven starts and 8-3-0 overall. He's had more good games than bad at this rate and is tied for third in the league in wins. Expect Blackwood to be on the bench against Columbus and Scott Wedgewood to get the nod. I wouldn't be surprised if Bednar starts Blackwood each of the next two games after that. We shall see how it plays out.

8. The Avalanche were a perfect 4-for-4 on the penalty kill, and Bednar said postgame that Blackwood was his best PKer. I agree.

9. I'm not sure why I'm so surprised, but I've been marveling all season at the production the Avalanche are getting from Devon Toews. After recording another goal and an assist, the 33-year-old blueliner has 15 points in as many games.

Toews consistently produced in his first five seasons with the club before his production fell off last season. It's just crazy to see that he's already surpassed his goal total from a season ago and is nine points away from matching his point total.

10. The standard for goaltender interference continues to change and I absolutely do not like where the league is headed with this stuff. Landeskog did what Ryan Smyth, Tomas Holmstrom, Joe Pavelski, and so many other greats did for years. He parked himself at the top of the crease to take the goalie's eyes away. The minor contact he made with Spencer Knight was irrelevant and didn't play a role in whether the goalie was going to make a save. That puck was going in whether Landeskog's arm grazed Knight or not. They can't possibly watch that replay and truly think it had any effect on the play.

The league is getting a lot of things right these days, but this is not one of them. Just a brutal call.
