Norris confident F1 not putting people at risk with Middle East races

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Lando Norris is confident Formula One bosses are not putting the sport's 2,000-strong travelling circus at risk by ending the season in the Middle East.

Following Saturday's race on the Las Vegas Strip, the grid's 11 teams - 10 of which have bases in the UK - will head to Qatar for the penultimate round on November 29 before a concluding fixture in Abu Dhabi the following weekend.

Imola had been placed on standby to host the final race amid the ongoing United States-Iran conflict.

However, F1 chiefs confirmed last week that they will push ahead with completing the 23-round season, as planned, in the Middle East.

UK's current Foreign Office advice says the situation in the region "remains deeply unpredictable and the risk of escalation is high."

It adds that "British nationals in, or planning to travel through, the Middle East should be prepared for possible flight cancellations, airspace closures, and travel disruptions," as well as "a risk of further attacks across the region at short notice."

But speaking in Las Vegas, 27-year-old Norris, who won the previous race in Sao Paulo, said: "I am looking forward to going. From my side, I am fine. It is a big team that we travel with, and it is more, 'what do they think?'

"But I have my friend's wedding in Bahrain next week, so I was planning on going there anyway, and then going on to Qatar and Abu Dhabi.

"So, from my side, it is not a concern. I just don't believe that this sport would put us at risk."

The rounds in Bahrain and Saudi Arabia were cancelled earlier this year, although a 'Bahrain Grand Prix' was relocated to Malaysia's Sepang venue last month. Next year's calendar is due to begin in Bahrain and Saudi Arabia.

"I don't have any hesitation to go or know of anyone that is concerned," said Mercedes driver George Russell ahead of the concluding two rounds.

"I am sure some people have their reservations. But I know people who live in the region, and they have said it quite bluntly, that if they didn't feel comfortable, they wouldn't remain there with their families.

"There are a lot of terrible things going on in every region of the world, and you have got to trust in the people that say it is safe. F1 are not stupid, so there is zero concern from my side. Fingers crossed it'll all be fine."

Kimi Antonelli was crowned world champion last month in Mexico, while his Mercedes team wrapped up the constructors' title the following weekend in Sao Paulo. Red Bull's Max Verstappen leads the battle to finish as runner-up in the drivers' standings. The four-time world champion is 17 points clear of Russell and 18 ahead of Lewis Hamilton ahead of Saturday's night race.
