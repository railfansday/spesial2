Aaron Rodgers-less Steelers end chaotic week with resounding win over Broncos

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Mike McCarthy has smiled before on the football field, in the aftermath of a win, in the walk from the sideline to the locker room as the handshakes unfold. But this one, this grin that turned into a beam by the time he left the grass at Acrisure Stadium after the Steelers' 27-5 win over the Broncos on Friday, felt different.

This one, after a week when Pittsburgh staunchly supported McCarthy when Aaron Rodgers — who led him to the Super Bowl in Green Bay, vouched for him to land the Steelers job and then shredded him in an interview that thrust the NFL's model of stability into a state of turmoil — contained a hint of validation. Less than 24 hours after ex-Steelers head coach Mike Tomlin called their present a "dumpster fire," McCarthy and Mason Rudolph, the two members in the spotlight the most after Rodgers' scathing rant Wednesday, proved they might survive the chaos after all.

With Rodgers now released, with signs and pictures of No. 8 removed from around the stadium, with Pittsburgh just over 48 hours removed from what Prime Video broadcaster Al Michaels described as the "shot heard 'round the football world," the Steelers delivered a resounding statement to move to 7-4. Rudolph completed 9-of-15 passes for 142 yards and a touchdown, converting when needed and facilitating a fast start for Pittsburgh. T.J. Watt and Jalen Ramsey returned a fumble and an interception for a touchdown, respectively, and the Steelers defense silenced Bo Nix (22 of 40, 169 yards, two interceptions).

"There's a lot going on," McCarthy told reporters. "Any time you're going through a situation where time is taken away from winning and your team responds, it is extremely important and gratifying. You have to make sure you keep it about the players."

Maybe it was the initial roar from the crowd, the one that followed Rudolph's introduction as the starting quarterback, that suggested the Steelers — and, by extension, McCarthy — might survive. Maybe it was the first touchdown pass from Rudolph to DK Metcalf, who'd been ejected from the game last weekend in what ultimately served as one of the final turning points of the Rodgers era in Pittsburgh.

On Monday, after the Steelers dropped a second consecutive game, McCarthy reiterated that Rodgers was his quarterback. One day later, Rudolph was named the Steelers starter and Rodgers was listed as inactive in an 11:10 p.m. X post. Then, on Wednesday, Rodgers went on "The Pat McAfee Show" and claimed that McCarthy fell asleep during meetings, broke down a halftime huddle with "1-2-3 Packers" and missed walk-throughs. Rodgers was released by the Steelers later that day, and the organization has since reiterated its belief in McCarthy.

Rudolph made his 20th career start Friday, but it marked his first since Week 12 of last season. The circumstances surrounding it were shocking too. With their win Friday, the Steelers demonstrated that the trust — in Rudolph by turning to him over young quarterbacks, in McCarthy by keeping him on staff even after the accusations — was warranted. They jumped out to a quick 14-0 lead behind the touchdowns from Watt and Metcalf, and even after the Broncos got on the board with a safety and crept closer with a field goal, Pittsburgh managed to create some breathing room before halftime. Chris Boswell connected on a field goal, and Ramsey returned an errant pass from Nix 36 yards for a touchdown to make it 24-5 with 1:33 remaining in the second quarter.

"In a weird way," Rudolph said on the Prime Video broadcast postgame, "the adversity probably brought us closer together."

Neither offense managed much in the second half. Nix struggled the whole game. And Rudolph didn't need to play hero. By that point, the Steelers had already made their statement. By that point, they'd already produced a sample that showed that perhaps the organization isn't scrambling after all, that maybe they can rebound from the chaos Rodgers left them with.

They're now 7-4. They'll be tied with the Bengals atop the AFC North if Cincinnati falls Sunday. And they ended a week of questions with a resounding answer.

"That dude has had to step into some critical situations over his career," defensive tackle Cam Heyward told reporters of Rudolph, "and he's ready for those moments. … He understood that, with everything that went on, he couldn't have a drop-off. And I'm just appreciative to have a guy that s–t hits the fan, he's able to just say, 'All right, I've gotta step up.' "
