Southwest Premier Card Review: Is The $149 Mid-Range Card Worth It?

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Chase and Southwest have a great portfolio of personal credit cards at three different price points. This is my in-depth review of the Southwest Rapid Rewards® Premier Credit Card, the Southwest personal card with a $149 annual fee (which is neither the highest nor lowest annual fee product).

Now is an especially good time to consider this card, both because it’s offering an elevated welcome bonus, and because it’s your last chance to use credit card welcome bonuses to qualify for Southwest’s Companion Pass benefit under the old system.

When applying for credit, cards, the welcome bonus, eligibility rules, and fees, are obviously major factors that potential cardmembers consider. So let’s cover those details for the Southwest Premier Card, as the card has a great bonus, nuanced eligibility requirements, and a relatively manageable annual fee.

The Southwest Premier Card is offering a limited time welcome bonus where you can earn 85,000 bonus points after you spend $2,000 on purchases in the first three months from account opening. This is a limited time, elevated offer, as the most recent base offer was for 55,000 points.

Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program. Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider Rapid Rewards points to be worth 1.2 cents each. So to me, the points from this welcome bonus are worth $1,020, making this a solid welcome offer.

When it comes to Southwest credit card eligibility rules, the Southwest Premier Card isn’t available to anyone who currently has a Southwest Rapid Rewards personal card, or anyone who has received a welcome bonus on a Southwest Rapid Rewards personal card in the past 24 months. However, you are eligible if you have a Southwest Rapid Rewards business credit card.

Beyond the restrictions related to which Southwest cards you have, there are general application rules for Chase cards, including the Chase 5/24 rule, whereby you usually won’t get approved for a Chase credit card if you’ve opened five or more credit card accounts in the past 24 months.

The Southwest Premier Card has a $149 annual fee, and you can add authorized users to the card at no extra cost. Southwest’s personal credit cards have annual fees of $99, $149, and $229, so this is the “middle child” of the Southwest and Chase personal card portfolio.

The Southwest Premier Card has no foreign transaction fees, so you can use it abroad without worrying about extra charges.

In addition to the benefits, one of the main reasons you’ll want to consider picking up a card is because of its return on spending, so you’re sure it aligns with what you’re looking for. In the case of the Southwest Premier Card, it has a limited number of bonus categories, and spending can also help you toward earning elite status with Rapid Rewards. So let’s cover those details.

Based on my valuation of 1.2 cents per point, that’s like a 3.6% return on Southwest purchases, a 2.4% return on grocery stores and restaurants (up to the cap), and a 1.2% return on everything else.

The Southwest Premier Card also lets you earn unlimited Tier Qualifying Points (TQPs) toward Southwest status. Through the end of 2026, you can earn 1,500 TQPs toward A-List status for every $5,000 spent on the card. Starting January 1, 2027, every Southwest credit card will earn 1 TQP per dollar spent, so $35,000 in spending on any of them would get you to Silver status.

A-List is Southwest Airlines’ version of elite status, and offers perks like priority boarding, a 25% points bonus, same-day standby, priority check-in and security lane access, and a dedicated phone number. A-List status ordinarily requires earning 35,000 TQPs in a calendar year, so spending on a Southwest Premier Card could help you earn that. Whether or not that’s worth it depends on how much you’d otherwise fly with Southwest.

Currently, Southwest Rapid Rewards elite status has two levels: A-List and A-List Preferred. Beginning January 1, 2027, though, the program will grow to three tiers, with new names: Silver, Gold, and Platinum. Qualifying for Silver status will require the same 35,000 TQPs needed for A-List, while Gold and Platinum will require 65,000 TQPs and 140,000 TQPs, respectively. I covered these Southwest Rapid Rewards program changes in detail here.

This brings me to another big upcoming change to the Southwest program and how cards like the Southwest Premier Card fit into your strategy — and this is a pretty unwelcome one.

One of the best values in travel is the Southwest Airlines Companion Pass. With a Companion Pass, you can have someone travel with you for the entire year (whether on a cash ticket or award ticket) and just pay taxes and fees for them. This is such an incredible deal and basically doubles the value you can get from flying Southwest.

For a long time now, the points earned as part of Southwest credit card welcome bonuses have counted towards the 135,000 qualifying points needed to earn the Companion Pass. But starting January 1, 2027, credit card welcome bonuses will no longer count toward qualification.

This eliminates the easiest way to earn this valuable benefit, and it’s not the only change: referral bonuses, anniversary points, bonus points from spending, and partner spending, won’t count toward the Companion Pass either. In fact, Southwest’s rolling the Companion Pass into its highest elite tier, Platinum, and requiring either 140,000 TQPs or 100 qualifying flights to earn it.

This change isn’t specific to the Southwest Premier Card, as it applies to welcome bonuses across all Southwest credit cards. But the good news is that welcome bonuses earned before the end of 2026 do count toward Companion Pass qualification, so now is your last chance to take advantage before this opportunity ends.

I have a dedicated post that covers how to redeem Southwest Rapid Rewards points for maximum value, but I’ll briefly cover how redemptions work.

Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program, which means that the value you’ll get from your points is tied to the cash cost of Southwest tickets. That said, there are a few different levels, ranging from Basic tickets to Choice Extra fares.

Basic award fares tend to offer the highest redemption value, and in my experience the best-value Southwest point redemptions are usually for international routes to destinations like Costa Rica and Aruba. For instance, I’ve seen Basic awards from Tampa (TPA) to Liberia, Costa Rica (LIR) for 12,000 points and $24.21. The same fare cost $200 in cash, giving you a very strong value of 1.46 cents per point.

One great thing about the Southwest Rapid Rewards program is that there are no change or cancellation fees, and rebooking a fare when the price drops gets you a refund equal to any difference in points. Just note that Basic fares can only be changed by upgrading to a higher fare bundle, so for a cheaper Basic award you’d cancel and rebook rather than change.

The Southwest Airlines Premier Card offers some benefits that could make the card worth keeping long term. How do the perks compare to Southwest’s lower and higher annual fee cards?

The Southwest Premier Card gets the primary cardholder and up to eight additional passengers on the same reservation a first checked bag at no additional cost. Checked bags on Southwest cost $45 each way, so this can save you up to $90 roundtrip on checked bag fees per person. With even a couple of roundtrip flights per year, this benefit can offset the annual fee.

The Southwest Airlines Premier Card lets you pick a Preferred seat on Southwest, if any are available, starting 48 hours before departure. The cost for a Preferred seat can vary from $28 to nearly $50 depending on the flight, so this perk is another potential money-saver.

Another Southwest Premier Card benefit is early boarding. You’ll be able to board with Group 5, or earlier if you have elite status or a fare or seat type that qualifies you for an earlier boarding group. Southwest has eight total boarding groups, so while there are several ahead of Group 5, it’s better than boarding last.

On your cardmember anniversary every year, you’ll receive 6,000 bonus Rapid Rewards points. Southwest Rapid Rewards is a revenue-based frequent flyer program, and you can generally expect that you’ll need around 83 Rapid Rewards points for every dollar of airfare you book.

In other words, 6,000 bonus points get you around $72 in Southwest airfare annually. That covers nearly half of the annual fee right there.

Each year on your Southwest Premier Card cardmember anniversary, you’ll get a 15% promo code that applies to Southwest flights. Just note that this excludes Basic fares.

The Southwest Premier Card offers 25% savings on inflight purchases of Wi-Fi and drinks. Simply pay with your card, and you’ll automatically receive a statement credit for that amount.

The Southwest Airlines Premier Card is Southwest’s mid-range card, and the $149 annual fee is in line with many other co-branded airline credit cards. The card is offering a fantastic bonus, which is one good reason to consider this product.

On an ongoing basis, the 6,000-point anniversary bonus can get you around $72 worth of airfare. Whether the Southwest Premier Card is worth it in the long run depends on how much you value the other perks like a free checked bag, and whether you plan to use spending on the credit card to qualify for Southwest elite status.
