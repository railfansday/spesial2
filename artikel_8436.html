I'm not a CPA or tax expert, but do my own planning, so, for what it's worth:

If you are single, and you have no other income (pension, dividends, interest, neither tax free or taxable).

I'd work backwards. Based on your age, you're first RMD likely begins the year you turn 73.
your current income: 55,000 possibly less if doing the worksheet to determine how much of your SS is taxable
RMD on 1.75 tIRA: 66,038 (divisor is 26.5) and likely might see some growth before end of your 72nd year)
Total income>>>>>>>>>>>121,038 slightly less if some of SS is taxable. (we feel it's been worth it to convert even if it means 85% of SS is taxable), but aware many would disagree.

according to the finance buff website re: IRMAA, the 2026 IRMAA starts at 115,000, so at 121k, you'll be over (ie paying the penalty)
and you would also want to look at what your tax rates will be when you start your RMD

Without doing the computations, I'd guess you'll be in the 24% tax bracket, At a bare minimum, I would think conversions at least up to just under the IRMAA limit might be helpful in some way. And it's water under the bridge fretting about the lost years of conversions that might have been more beneficial.

we started off with a similar tIRA size but started converting much earlier with a complete reversal ratio.
