Bruno Guimaraes could be enemy No.1 amongst Newcastle United fans as Matthias Jaissle faces the sack at St James' Park.

Supporters were already angry enough when he swapped life on Tyneside for the Emirates Stadium in the summer, moving in a £75million deal.

On Saturday, 21 November, Guimaraes will return to St James' Park for the first time since the move, where everything has since imploded.

As well as several key players leaving, Eddie Howe stepped down, and Jaissle's appointment has been nothing but frustrating so far.

Football Insider understands that the 38-year-old has the owners' full backing, for the moment anyway, but that could quickly change.

And one player who definitely deserves to be starting for the Gunners is Guimaraes. If he has a starring role, he might be the cause of Jaissle's impending doom.

The Brazilian already impressed in the 3-2 defeat to Japan earlier in the international break, bagging two assists during the 90 minutes.

He then started Brazil's 7-0 win over Singapore on Tuesday, 17 November, being given a well-earned rest on the hour mark.

Still, 60 minutes was enough time to make an imposing impact, and his stats are simply terrifying, especially for his former Newcastle teammates.

In 60 minutes, he created six chances, three of which were big, completed 100 per cent of his dribbles, 75 per cent of his long balls and took 57 touches [FotMob].

Defensively, the midfielder made three tackles, six recoveries, won 67 per cent of his ground duels and even made a clearance.

There is every chance that Mikel Arteta makes a surprise decision to drop Guimaraes, despite how he's been playing over the last few weeks.

He has quickly become part of Arsenal's midfield trio, though, alongside Declan Rice and Martin Odegaard, and appears to be undroppable.

The emotions around returning to St James' Park could play an impact in Arteta's thinking, but Guimaraes more than professional enough to handle it.

Martin Zubimendi and Mikel Merino have both made positive impressions with Spain during the window, but Guimaraes simply has to start.
