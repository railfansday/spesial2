Record Store Day Black Friday’s Most Wanted, 2026: Beatles, Noah Kahan, Bruce Springsteen, Prince, Sombr, Billy Joel, Katseye and Other Vinyl Must-Finds

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Senior Music Writer and Chief Music Critic

Record Store Day is back in black. As you likely already know, RSD is a semiannual holiday for vinyl hounds, with the Black Friday edition being a less jam-packed adjunct to the primary event in April — meaning that, this year, for instance, there are “only” 188 titles being released, almost exactly half of the 355 exclusives that were put on sale in the spring event. Even with that lesser amount of limited-edition discs going out onto the shelves, there is truly something for everybody in this list. Or, to put it another way: If you can’t find anything that grabs you in this lineup, then, to quote Leonard Cohen, you don’t really care for music, do ya?

Our best guess is that there will not be utter pandemonium in the indie shops or in the lines outside this Black Friday morning, if only because of the lack of a Taylor, Olivia or Billie title this time. Which does not mean that there will not still be a healthy contingent of young people joining the veteran vinyl geeks in the checkout lines. In an interview published earlier in the week in Variety, RSD co-founder Michael Kurtz said that the majority of customers for Record Store Day events are under 35. That may continue to be the case as this one has titles from hot and/or younger-skewing artists or franchises including Noah Kahan, Katseye, Sombr and the “Wicked” and “Kpop Demon Hunters” films.

That said, boomers are still gonna boom, and they, Gen-X-ers, millennials and others who have a taste for classic rock, soul and jazz — meaning every generation, really — will find something to salivate over in a list of vintage material that includes Bruce Springsteen, Prince, Joni Mitchell, Miles Davis, Billy Joel, Bill Withers, Art Blakey and even a collectible from the Beatles that was added to the list after it was first announced in October.

If you want to see the entire list of 188 vinyl releases (a few of which are also having CD editions made available for the occasion) scroll all the way down. But first, if you will, look at our annotated list of 27 of the titles that we consider most covetable, ranging from Carly Rae Jepsen and Cher EPs to a 10,000 Maniacs boxed set to an expanded version of NRBQ’s classic Christmas album.

14,500 copies on 4x LP; 3,700 copies on 2x CD; black vinyl

Springsteen buffs will not always agree on favorite individual shows from his and the band’s 50-year-plus history, but it’s hard to imagine a single fan not considering the “Born to Run” era to be peak form. or not taking a serious interest in this rendering of a long and loose New Year’s Eve concert at the close of 1975. The only question might be which physical format you want it in, since Sony is offering it for Record Store Day Black Friday in both LP and CD form. The much greater amount of vinyl pressed indicates an obvious consumer preference for vinyl nowadays, but with a concert this length, some may reconsider whether they want to flip sides seven times to get it all and opt for compact disc. Whether you pick up a vinyl slipcase or CD jewel box, there are succinct but sweet liner notes from Erik Flannigan, who points out that, yes, that is the band (except for Bruce) decked out in tuxedos in the photographs in the packaging. Good thing any formality about this wonderful show ends there.

10,000 copies of vinyl 12-inch EP; Alpenglow-colored vinyl

Kahan’s exclusive releases have been among the biggest draws at many recent Record Store Days, which is no surprise, given the ongoing presence of two of his studio albums in the top 10 and stadium tours that sell out instantaneously. Fans will already be familiar with the four songs on this EP — “Lighthouse,” “Staying Still,” A Few of Your Own” and “Orbiter” — since they first appeared as bonus tracks on a digital deluxe edition of his smash “The Great Divide” album (and “Orbiter” is a proper single). But having the four tracks partitioned off into their own separate physical release will be irresistible to diehard stickheads.

It’s not like you’re going to get any songs here that you didn’t already get on the recent “Rubber Soul” 60th anniversary super-deluxe edition… or, for that matter, that you haven’t had access to since 1966. But then, that’s the point: This single LP is a recreation of the exact album that U.S. Beatles fans got in ’66, down to the original stereo mix. But that’s been out of print for a long time… and has never been uin print as a gold-colored record, which is the main novelty here. The good news for Beatles collectors is that it’s been pressed in numbers that fall at the very top end of pressings for Record Store Day; the bad news is that 15,000 still won’t last long.

9,400 copies on 3x vinyl; 2,700 copies on 2x CD; vinyl color is crystal blue curaçao

The Prince estate is really making the most out of the fact that it had a pro-shot film of Prince’s six-month 2006-7 residency in Las Vegas in the can. A film of “3121 Las Vegas Live” will briefly hit the big screen in early December, followed by a Blu-Ray edition for home consumption. In the meantime, if you want a physical manifestation of just the music, Record Store Day participant shops are the place to go. The performance captured here starts at a slow simmer but quickly heats up. Suffice it to say that, after hearing this purely audio version, I’ll be lined up for the movie.

Every time there is a new CD boxed set in the ongoing series of Joni Mitchell archival anthologies, a single live performance from that collection gets broken out to have its own separate LP edition for Record Store Day. No complaints at all about that. This ’72 concert is all Joni, accompanying herself on guitar, piano or dulcimer, all the way to a closing “Circle Game” that does have her suddenly surrounded by a chorus of famous and unfamous friends. Some will prefer Mitchell in her purest early-ish form here, and some may prefer the big band era that followed. But hearing the purity of that voice with an emphasis on the then-recent “Blue” and then-forthcoming “For the Roses” is a knockout experience. The only thing more you could wish for is a gatefold jacket instead of putting both discs in a single sleeve (the product description promises gatefold, but it’s not), or liner notes, but it’s still a handsome-looking as well as -sounding package.

The last Billy Joel live album, released for Record Store Day Black Friday 2025, was “Live From Long Island,” pressed in a quantity of 1,500 copies — and fans are still pissed about that. (Resale copy prices are not completely exorbitant, but they do go for more than $100.) Fortunately, the record company got the message and is putting three times that many of this followup on sale, 4,600, although that still may not completely sate the market for live Joel. It’s a spirited set, from a hyperactive “Angry Young Man” opening forward, recorded Dec. 5, 1976 at Connecticut College for a TV special. It includes 15 never-before-released tracks from either the original live shows or a followup that was done to get better takes for the special. The blurb on the back cover promises that four more tracks will be included in a “Turnstiles” 50th anniversary boxed set coming out next year. But few Joel fans will complain about that wait given the wealth of heretofore unreleased music here.

Boone: 5,000 copies on black and white galaxy vinyl

The Sombr and Benson Boone releases are very similar, in that each is a first-time vinyl issue of the EP that started their major-label careers before they broke a lot bigger with a full album that followed. Bot EPs are also mellower than some of the hit music that followed for these artists — Sombr lives up to his name more here. His EP, at four tracks, is less expansive than Boones, which runs to eight songs, and comes with a 12-by-24 insert that has a poster image on one side and lyrics on the other. Apart from their musical merits, both discs just look fab, pulled out of their sleeves and placed into the wild.

Withers: 2,600 copies on LP; 1,300 copies on CD; LP is black vinyl

Various artists/Sweet Relief: 1,200 copies

Now here’s an unofficial two-fer anyone should think about picking up and turning into a double feature, even if their simultaneous release, coming from different labels, may be coincidental. First, there’s Withers’ own 1974 performance, captured for British TV. You can tell the audio is unedited: Withers is heard telling the studio audience that some of his band members are out sick, and in one case restarting a speech. The relaxed feel carries over to the superlative songs. Meanwhile, I have to give an even more enthusiastic endorsement to a double-LP tribute album put together as a benefit for the Sweet Relief Foundation that includes all-new tracks. I was hoping the great Chris Pierce — one of the closest things we have to a modern-day Withers right now — would be one of the artists chosen, and to my sweet relief, he was, contributing “Better Off Dead.” There are also choice cuts from Rickie Lee Jones, Lady Blackbird, Marilyn McCoo and Billy Davis Jr., Ben Harper and others who know how to mix their own spin on things with takes that feel faithful to Withers’ vibe.

2,600.copies of six-LP boxed set; multiple vinyl colors

Rhino has gotten in the habit of issuing one complete boxed set per RSD event for a renowned artist from the Warner-Elektra catalog of riches, from John Prine to Devo. This one for 10,000 Maniacs, composed of all the albums the group put out before Natalie Merchant split to go solo in 1993, is being seen by fans as a very necessary corrective to the fact that a lot of that stuff has been out of print on vinyl forever. Four studio albums are joined by the pre-fame compendium “Hope Chest” and a newly assembled rarities disc full of delectable covers. Merchant is credited with helping oversee the package, including the design of the physical elements, although the liner notes (which are very good) do not include interviews with her or any of the bandmates who continue to carry on without her. It’s worth noting that all of the albums included except the rarities LP are due to be issued individually early next year, if it’s just one or two you need to pick up. But as a fan from their earliest major-label days, I couldn’t imagine not getting the whole thing right now. A matter of curiosity: All of the box and LP packaging here changes the band’s name to Ten Thousand Maniacs from 10,000 Maniacs (although the numerical usage pops up in the liner notes). Some elements of the album cover art have been changed, along with the band-name logos — it has to be a Merchant thing. But if you pick up the individual issues in a few months, apparently the name and artwork will be as we remember them. (The fact that the albums in this box have different covers is further cemented by the lack of barcodes.) Those peculiarities aside, t’s a great trip down maniacal memory lane.

Jepsen has been nothing but prolific in this era, first issuing “Day and Night” as a hefty double-album, then adding eight additional tracks onto the original 24 for a deluxe edition, “Day and Night (Continued).” that runs to an hour and 55 minutes of material. Some things may be more easily digested in smaller chunks, of course, and this EP — also titled “Day and Night (Continued)” — breaks out those eight fresher tracks and gives them their first appearance in a physical format. Plus, you get still more from her photo shoot at Eagle Rock’s Casa Bianca, the best pizza joint in L.A., and if that’s not reason enough to pick it up, you’ve got eight more.

The previous RSD exclusive editions of the “Wicked” and “Wicked: For Good” sold out so quickly, I never did actually catch a glimpse of either of them in my many store visits. That will probably prove true for this combined set that packages both soundtracks together in a 4-LP set in a slipcase with a poster. (The track list, if accurate as rendered online, leaves out the bonus track that was added onto the original RSD “Wicked” issue.) Good luck hunting this down, unless it turns out every franchise fan was sated by the previous two releases after all, which isn’t likely. It’s gonna be popular.

The first of several releases we’ll mention here produced by “jazz detective” Zefv Feldman is a welcome incursion into the wonderful world of Blakey. It comtbines two previously unreleased performances in Paris, the first from 1963 and sporting famous players like Freddie Hubbard and Wayne Shorter, the second with a different ensemble in ’68. “Moons” were on the band leader’s mind, as the collection includes the two big musical moon shots of the era, “Blue Moon” and “Moon River.” The latter opens the collection and might give Henry Mancini a heart attack if he were still around to have one — it’s like “Moon River” on amphetamines (not speaking literally, friends, just figuratively), and it’s quite a fantastic flood. The liner notes booklet is copious as with almost any Feldman-related release, and even though a CD edition will follow for the general market in a week, it’s always worth picking up the limited-edition vinyl versions of these releases just for the larger type and photographs. And, sure, the wide-open grooves, too.

5,000 copies of 2x LP; on “Third Pyramid Pink” vinyl

I would have taken an interest in a B-52s live set anyway, but this one turns out to be particularly a wowser because of the addition of a three-man horn section that really helps some of the songs rock in an augmented way. Fans may already know this Roseland Ballroom NYC ’82 performance from when it was included in the “Ancient Culture: Mesopotamia Revisited” set, but for those of us who didn’t have that, or anyone who did but just likes a good breakout, it’s a delightful revelation of the band at its early ’80s peak.

3,500 copies of 12-inch vinyl EP; red vinyl

Cher had not released any original material in many years until she came back with a Christmas project that included some songs newly written to add to the holiday tuneage canon. With this EP spinoff, the emphasis is on the dance-ier chestnuts, including three different mixes of “DJ Play a Christmas Song” and an added duet with Kelly Clarkson. The lady-in-red cover shot is different from any previous artwork in her Christmas-music campaign, too.

We were not able to preview this particular release, so we cannot vouch for what the “dance break” versions of three of Katseye’s most popular songs sound like — “Debut,” “Gnarly” and “Gabriela.” But we can tell you the way it will fly off the shelves will be gnarly indeed. All three mixes are congregated on one side of the vinyl, with a mystery etching on the other. Each copy is said to includes two out of six “randomly selected unique photocards,” so fans will be figuring out how to get around the one-copy-per-customer rule in most stores, as if this needed an incentive to sell out faster.

Another archival production from Zev Feldman, who is stretching out from being seen as “just” a jazz guy with his Bobby Womack and Marianne Faithful releases this Record Store Day. Womack can also stretch out, as it turns out, with two numbers in this live set that go past the 12-minute mark, “You’re Welcome, Stop on By” and “Communication” are both over 12 minutes long. Recorded in Paris in 1976, where the liner notes say he was having a higher profile at the time than in the U.S., this collection feels essential as a revelation of just what an expansive showman he was as well as crafter of classic soul singles.

10,000 copies; “Derpy Sussie Splatter” vinyl

The hunt for “Kpop Demon Hunters” collectibles never stops. Record Store Day just had dual editions of the soundtrack that were quick and easy sellouts, but that could hardly stand in the way of yet another version — and surely not the last — this one featuring fresh art created by official “Demon Hunters” production designer Mingjue Helen Chen and visual development artist Marion Bordeyne. We’ll let the product description do the rest of the talking: “This Derpy Sussie Splatter vinyl includes exclusive holographic art on both the front cover and triple gatefold, 3 of 6 mini inserts, 1 of 3 holographic mini inserts, a sticker sheet, and an oversized poster.” You might have to take their word for it since it won’t be surprising if most or all of the copies are gone by the time you get your lazy post-Thanksgiving butt to the shop.

Sax man Getz is the only one with his name above the title on this release, but for the first three of its six sides, legendary guitarist João Gilberto is his equal costar. Together, they recreate the magic they’d made together in a historic studio teaming in one of the few live shows they did together to follow that. All the music here was previously released by Resonance more than a decade ago, but this three-LP set for the first time combines two albums — one of Getz with Gilberto in SF, one without — into an exemplary single package (and the Getz-only half of the equation never made it onto vinyl before). The multiple interviews or essays in the copious liner notes even include a sizable explanation of the bossa nova craze that finds a kind of quiet culmination here.

Fountains of Wayne’s final album before their breakup sometimes gets undervalued because it’s their least comical, most sober outing, perhaps reflecting the more dominant influence of Chris Collingwood, versus the wryer touch favored by Adam Schlesinger, who’d run the show more on the preceding record. Regardless, there’s wit enough here, along with some of the band’s starker songs. It’s been challenging for FoW fans to complete their vinyl collections, since this fast went out of print after a single pressing in 2001. Even now, 2,000 is not a lot, but it’ll be at least enough to satisfy some of us who never stopped believing that this brilliant group had it going on.

This is the “Bitches Brew”-era Miles who took free jazz into the land of psychedelia, if you hadn’t already guessed that from the cover art by his favorite cover painter in the ’60s, Mati Klarwein. It might as well have been recorded at the Fillmore, even though Davis actually had his all-star quintet (Wayne Shorter, Chick Corea, Dave Holland and Jack DeJohnette) in Copenhagen for a TV taping. The music has previously been heard on a DVD and on a one-time vinyl pressing of just a few hundred copies, but this mastering is said to be a revelation even for the relatively small number of cultists who’ve already heard this dynamic performance in some form.

3,500 copies; on “Pawn Shop Patina”-colored vinyl

As the title and cover hint, this is a collection of cover songs these Dolls have done over the years, and what great taste they’ve had in other bands, as evidenced in a collection that runs from the Plimsouls’ “A Million Miles Away” to the Kinks’ “Catch Me Now, I’m Falling,” with stops for Tom Petty, INXS, Prince and… Supertramp! (A CD and digital release will follow a week after this vinyl exclusive.)

5,000 copies; 3D zoetrope picture-disc vinyl

We did not get a review copy of this, but we’re going to guess from the product description alone that it might be fun, with or without actually listening to what’s in the grooves, just from looking at them. “Zoetrope” discs have been a thing for a while, turning vinyl into animation, but this is the first one we can recall seeing that is billed as a 3D zoetrope disc, with cardboard glasses to put on to enjoy the effect. We look forward to hearing whether the effect of watching that is as mind-bending as the Gizzard boys’ music typically is, and what substances it is best combined with.

1,800 copies on three-sided 2x LP set; black vinyl

We’re still morning Faithful’s passing, so it’s moving to hear her in her prime, or one of her primes, in a performance captured in 1983, after a comeback run of three acclaimed albums on Island, including of course “Broken English.” The tape of the D.C. set ends with the title track from that latter album and is missing a few final songs from the actual performance, so it’s spread across three sides and the fourth is devoted to an etching of a William Blake quote in concentric-circle script.

2,215 copies of six-45-rpm-singles boxed set; different marbled color variations

Jazz Dispensary dispenses something worth picking up for every single RSD edition, and this time they’ve eschewed the usual single-LP compilation format to put out a box of singles. It comes in a candy-style box, with six different vintage singles in different but equally delicious-looking colors, plus some glow-in-the-dark 45 adapters as a bonus. As usual, even though the description calls these “jazz,” the music therein leans more toward the funk side of the fence, from Eddie Jefferson’s honestly groovy “Psychedelic Sally” to Patrice Rushen’s kinda-weird “Kickin’ Back.” Obscure but charming singles from Rusty Bryant, Idris Muhammad, Pleasure and Johnny Hammond are also represented. Will you play these a lot, or easily find shelf space for an odd-shaped package? Probably no in both cases, yet it’s kind of irresistible as a hep novelty package nonetheless.

What could be better for celebrating the season than NRBQ’s 1978 “Merry Christmas From NRBQ” EP? Very possibly, a modern expansion of that project that extends it to 19 tracks, 10 of them making their vinyl debut, one (the closing “Auld Lang Syne”) getting its premiere altogether. We weren’t able to preview this package to tell you whether they do or don’t rock out the newly added “Jesus Loves Me” and “The Christmas Song,” but we hope to score a copy and fill in that part of the NRB-FAQ.
