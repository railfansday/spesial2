Watching the Market Nov. 9-15: Cavs bench unit on the rise

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

It is just a few weeks into the season, and the Cleveland Cavaliers have been up and down, though the players heading in those directions are not what one would have expected.

The Cavs sit at .500 after 14 games, but it could be a lot worse if not for the play of Tyrese Proctor. The second-year guard out of Duke has been exceptional, shooting the basketball with excellent efficiency from all over the floor while limiting turnovers. Proctor's 6'4" frame and 6'7" wingspan makes him more difficult to exploit on defense as well, a boon to an otherwise small backcourt. It is still early, but the Cavs are 10.2 points per 100 possessions better offensively with Proctor on the floor versus off, per Cleaning the Glass. Craig Porter Jr. was seen as the favorite to command the backcourt off the bench, but Proctor has leaped him in the depth chart.

In what will be seen as one of the more special recent wins, under dubious circumstances, Proctor was the catalyst to a shocking 127-117 victory over the Dallas Mavericks. On a night where every starter was ruled out due to injury and/or flight issues, Proctor put up 24 points, 11 rebounds, and 10 assists for his first career triple-double. He has earned more playing time, and will likely be getting it going forward.

That win over the Mavericks was fun, and easily the most fun win of the season.

The fan punching bag got off to a slow start this year, but the Cavs do not win that game at Madison Square Garden without the hot shooting of James Harden. His 32-point effort pushed Cleveland to a much-needed victory, especially after the horrific display the team put on in Charlotte two nights earlier.

Harden has a documented history of starting seasons slow, opting against heavy offseason conditioning and instead "playing into shape". The 37-year-old, now in his 18th season, admitted as much just a few days ago. The Cavs are still statistically better with Harden off the floor, but hopefully he is starting to come out of his early-season fog.

Late in the team's win over the New York Knicks, Mobley suffered an injury to his big toe while going up for a block and has now missed two straight games as a result. Even before the injury, Mobley looked passive — even by his standards — which begs the question as to whether this toe injury had been bothering him prior. Regardless, the Cavs will be without their star big man for an uncertain amount of time.

While the Cavs' offense is exceptionally worse with Mobley on the floor (-12.8 points per 100 possessions, per Cleaning the Glass), the same cannot be said on the defensive end. However, the Cavs' defense has not been as big of an issue so far this year. It's the offense that has looked clunky, and Mobley is certainly part of that problem.

It cannot be excused just how bleak things looked after getting absolutely walloped in Charlotte, who at the time had the worst offense in the league. The Cavs' starters allowed the Hornets to run all over them, and part of that blame has to be put on the head coach. Then there is the decision not to close games with Jarrett Allen (or Mario Hezonja, for that matter), despite the team very clearly needing the size out there.

When asked after the team's second loss in a row to the Oklahoma City Thunder, Atkinson said not closing with Allen is down to how things looked in the moment. "It's a feel, it's a feel," Atkinson said. "Who do they have on the court? How are we playing? Do we need more shooting? Do we need defense? There's a myriad of reasons that we could change or stay the same."

The problem with this is that the Cavs have been absolutely killed on the glass all season. Mobley, despite being as tall as a center, is not one, nor does he rebound the ball like one. That makes it difficult for the Cavs in crunch time to get their own misses or get clean rebounds off their opponents' misses. Allen's ability to run the floor and his relationship with Harden and Mitchell as a screener and roller is an easy offense to run when you need a basket. Why Atkinson would opt not to play is best, and maybe only, center is a puzzling one.
