Packers Winners and Losers: Week 12 vs. the Rams

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

On Thanksgiving Eve, the Green Bay Packers and Los Angeles Rams faced off in a game which had heavy impact on the playoff chances for both teams. RG Kevin Zeitler made his first start for the Packers. EDGE Micah Parsons made his long awaited return after sustaining a torn ACL during the 2025 season. It was also another chapter in the Matt LaFleur vs. Sean McVay saga, and, for the first time since the two both became NFL head coaches, McVay and the Rams came out victorious, 26-13. Let's take a look at what worked, and what didn't.

It feels so good typing these words right now. After wondering what the heck was taking so long with the return of the Packers star edge rusher after a December 2025 ACL tear, it was revealed that the damage was much more severe than many of us realized. Last night, finally healthy, Parsons made his first start of the 2026 season, playing about 37% of total defensive snaps. And he looked…great!

Parsons nearly got to QB Matthew Stafford a few times, and then just before halftime, we all got to see the sack we'd been waiting 346 days for. With how things have been called all year for the Packers, I was certain this would be flagged for roughing the passer. Parsons finished the night with 1 sack and 2 QB hits, and was credited with 4 pressures on 26 snaps. Good stuff, and it'll only get better.

WR Christian Watson was the only reliable pass catcher for the Packers last night, and he was knocked out of the game in the first half after taking a nasty blow to the helmet, which saw Rams S Kam Kinchens ejected. Prior to the hit, Watson was the only one able to hold onto the ball or…do anything right, really. The routes were nice. The hands were strong. He finished the night catching all 4 targets for 65 yards and 1 TD. His concussion status will be one to monitor as the Packers head into their next game next Sunday.

The Packers have had a problem with TE Tucker Kraft all season, and now, it's got me begging them to stop giving him the ball. Kraft has had some pretty egregious drops this year, has played like he's scared of getting hit, and has just been generally BAD. Last night, he logged his second fumble of the year, and the Rams were able to turn it into 3 more points. Kraft finished the night catching 3 of 5 targets for 24 yards. They don't really have any other options at TE, so I guess we just hope for the best? And cry a little?

Last night was the first time I've seen QB Jordan Love visibly angry, and it came on the heels of an interception that seemed to have been caused by WR Matthew Golden running the wrong route. Golden has been great this year. He even showed flashes of that last night with his big 39 yard gain, but this error killed any sort of momentum the Packers were developing. Golden finished the night with 1 catch on 5 targets for 39 yards.

It may not be a good idea to stop running and then just watch as the defender intercepts your quarterback.

We can blame the quarterback (not really), we can blame the offensive line, we can blame the receivers, we can blame the run defense, we can blame the head coach! At the end of the day, this goes back to GM Brian Gutekunst, who put the team together. Now—there are things he can't control, like the drops, and the injury bug flying through the locker room. But the poor depth at offensive line has been the catalyst for almost all of the bad things to happen to the Packers offense this season.

Jordan Love has been running for his life. He's being hit while throwing. The RB room doesn't stand a chance. The receivers have had almost no time to get open. An even mediocre offensive line fixes a lot of this. By failing to put together an o-line group that can sustain any sort of run game, any sort of passing game other than "sling it and hope for the best", Brian Gutekunst has bungled the 2026 roster.

The 6-5 Packers make their next appearance next Sunday, December 6, against the 3-7 New Orleans Saints. The game is at 1 pm ET/12 pm CT, and yes, that's a threat.
