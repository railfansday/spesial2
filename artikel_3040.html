Should You Invest in Anthropic Stock Now or Wait? Here's What History Says.

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The hype was fast, and the pullback was faster. Here's what past big tech IPOs suggest about buying now versus waiting.

By Patrick Sanders – Nov 27, 2026 at 1:19PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

If you wanted to purchase Anthropic (ANT +10.98%) stock last week but couldn't get shares at the initial public offering (IPO) price of $83, you didn't have to wait very long for another opportunity.

Anthropic stock closed at $97.17 on its first day of trading on the Nasdaq but fell below $83 by Nov. 24 -- just its fourth day as a publicly traded company.

I think that Anthropic is a great company, and I'm fired up about the IPO. But I also have no interest in investing anytime soon -- because I've seen way too many big tech stocks fall before they can find their footing.

History says some of the best opportunities to invest in technology companies have come several months after their much-anticipated IPOs.

According to Equity Comp Academy, citing research from Nasdaq (NDAQ +1.93%), more than 70% of technology stocks underperform after going public. And some of the biggest tech IPOs of 2026 have been particularly disappointing for investors who bought shares on the first day of trading.

Cerebras Systems (CBRS +5.08%) went public in May at $185 per share and closed its first day of trading at $311. Anyone who bought Cerebras stock at that price was sitting on a nearly 65% loss by the end of October, when shares traded at roughly $110.

And Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +4.83%) stock went from an IPO price of $135 in June to nearly $161 at the end of its first day of trading. But SpaceX shares are now about $127.

You can find similar examples going back several years. What's particularly interesting is how little time investors often had to wait to get those stocks at a discount to their IPO prices.

Data source: Author research, public Nasdaq data.

I also wouldn't get too hung up on the IPO price when deciding when to buy a stock. Investors who bought Cerebras stock at $185 after watching it soar to $311 may have thought they were getting a bargain. But they still could have saved themselves a lot of money by waiting.

Perhaps my favorite example is Meta Platforms (META +3.13%), which went public as Facebook in 2012 at $38 per share. The stock fell below its IPO price on its second day of trading and didn't get back to that level for 14 months.

Meta has become one of the most valuable companies on the planet. Investors certainly didn't need to buy Facebook stock in May 2012 to become wealthy from owning it.

Established technology companies have years of quarterly earnings reports for investors to review, assessing how well management has met its projections and how Wall Street has valued the stock relative to its earnings or revenue.

But that's not the case with Anthropic -- or any big tech IPO. So it's no surprise that those companies see significant volatility in the first few days. Before I invest in Anthropic, I want more detail about how much it spends on computing infrastructure. Anthropic can have outstanding revenue growth and still be years away from generating the kind of profits that justify its valuation.

Am I risking paying more for Anthropic stock six months from now? Sure. Arm Holdings (ARM +6.22%) and Airbnb (ABNB +4.07%) both more than doubled in value in their first year as publicly traded companies, according to data from S&P Global Market Intelligence.

But I'd rather pay a little more for Anthropic stock after seeing a couple of quarterly earnings reports if those numbers give me greater conviction in the company's ability to make money.

Plenty of people have made fortunes investing in technology stocks without ever participating in an IPO. In the long run, buying based on business fundamentals rather than debut-day hype is the far more reliable path to wealth -- and I don't see why investing in Anthropic should be any different.
