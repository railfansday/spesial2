5 quick hitters with Silver and Black Pride: Browns’ offense can take advantage of Raiders’ interior

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

This Sunday, the Cleveland Browns return from their bye at home as they take on the Las Vegas Raiders in Week 12. To help preview a few topics from the Raiders' perspective, we reached out to Matt Holder from Silver and Black Pride and exchanged five questions with him.

Chris: "The Raiders are a big draw to watch again (at least to me) with Fernando Mendoza now starting. How have things looked for the first overall pick through two games?"

Matt: "Mendoza has certainly had his ups and downs over the last two weeks, as facing off against the Seahawks' and Broncos' defenses are brutal opponents to get your feet wet as a starting quarterback in the NFL. He had some issues deciphering Mike Macdonald's complex pressure looks and has taken way too many sacks (11) over the last couple of weeks. However, I thought he did a better job of feeling pressure and navigating the pocket in Denver. The Broncos were just able to get to him later in the game when the Raiders were trailing, and Denver's pass-rushers could pin their ears back.

One thing that Mendoza has brought to the offense is the ability to create off-script plays, which is ironic because that was viewed as one of his weaknesses coming out of Indiana. Don't get me wrong, Kirk Cousins did a great job operating the offense, but the 38-year-old's limited mobility and arm strength made it difficult for the offense to get out of bad situations. Meanwhile, the rookie has used his legs as a weapon, either by taking off and running or scrambling and making some impressive throws on the run. The clip below (link) highlights a few plays/throws that simply weren't happening with Cousins at quarterback.

So, there have definitely been some rookie struggles over the last two weeks. But the traits that made the No. 1 pick the No. 1 pick have shown up, and everyone in Raider Nation should be really excited about the future despite Mendoza being 0-2 as a starter so far."

Chris: "What do you view as the Raiders' biggest weakness on defense right now?"

Matt: "The defensive tackle position, and it's not particularly close.

Everyone knew Las Vegas' interior defensive line needed to be addressed in the offseason, but general manager John Spytek didn't add a defensive tackle until the seventh round of the draft. Now, according to Pro Football Focus, the Raiders' defensive tackle who has the highest pass-rush win rate (Thomas Booker IV at 8.6 percent) ranks 54th at the position among players with at least 74 pass-rush snaps.

Additionally, the two best run defenders at the position — Foley Fatukasi and Tim Keenan III — weren't even on the roster to begin training camp. Fatukasi had been available as a free agent for about six months before getting signed after the Raiders' first preseason game, while Keenan was a seventh-round pick by the Rams before failing to make Los Angeles' roster and getting added to Las Vegas' practice squad. To clarify, Keenan has played in the last five games and was added to the active roster a couple of weeks ago. As a result, the Raiders are allowing the second-most rushing yards per game (149.5) and are third-to-last in yards per carry allowed (4.8).

Defensive coordinator Rob Leonard is trying to run a variation of Macdonald's defense in Seattle, but Leonard simply doesn't have the talent on the interior of the defensive line that the Seahawks have to properly execute it. In my opinion, that's the biggest root cause of the defensive issues in Vegas."

Chris: "Cleveland went into Las Vegas last year around this same time and terrorized Geno Smith with ten sacks, and pretty much dominated that game. Besides QB, what are the biggest differences you see between last year's team and this year's that could make for a different outcome?"

Matt: "Klint Kubiak is the biggest difference. Last year's Browns game ended up being Chip Kelly's last as offensive coordinator, with Pete Carroll firing Kelly shortly after the contest. The rumor is that Carroll was forcing Kelly to run a specific type of offense instead of letting the play caller put together his own scheme, and I can say that Kelly's offense looked nothing like I expected it to be with very generic play calling and little to no creativity.

Meanwhile, Kubiak is in full control as the head coach and offensive play-caller. Whether it's Cousins or Mendoza at quarterback, the Raiders' offensive scheme has been much better than it was a year ago. That's led to a dramatic difference in points per game, going from ranking dead last at 14.2 ppg to 11th at 25.0 ppg. Fun fact: It only took 10 games for the 2026 team to surpass the 2025 team's point total (241) for the entire season.

Also, while the Raiders' current offensive line is far from perfect, the unit is better than last year's at this point in the season. Of the six linemen who played against the Browns last year, only one remains on the current active roster: right tackle DJ Glaze, who is arguably the team's most improved player. So, while I mentioned previously that sacks were an issue for Mendoza, he's already shown growth in that area, and the line is better than it was at this point a year ago."

Chris: "Tell us about one player who Browns fans may not have heard of, but who could have an impact on this Sunday's game."

Matt: "I'm going to cheat and go with two, one on each side of the ball: wide receiver Jack Bech and safety Treydan Stukes.

Bech, the 2025 second-round pick, is coming off his best game as a pro with eight catches for 88 yards and a touchdown. Week 10 was ugly, where he had a couple of drops and was unproductive with just two grabs and 10 yards. However, Bech and Mendoza seem to have good chemistry together, as the second-year wideout leads the team with 15 targets over the last two weeks, per PFF. He isn't a great athlete who is going to generate a ton of separation on his routes, but the TCU product is good on contested targets and at finding holes in zone coverage.

Stukes, the 2026 second-round pick, has primarily been a free safety and has done well in that role. He's constantly around the ball, with a pair of interceptions, five passes defended and several quality tackles to limit gains in both the passing and running games. Recently, the coaching staff has been tapping into the Arizona product's versatility, playing him at some nickelback (his college position) and having him line up against tight ends. It wouldn't surprise me if we see Stukes and Harold Fannin Jr. face off against each other frequently on Sunday."

Chris: "The Browns are underdogs against the Raiders on Sunday (Browns -4.5 as of this question, per FanDuel). Who would you pick to win the game?"

Matt: "Call me a homer, but I kind of like the Raiders to pull off the upset. Cleveland has become known for having slow starts and making fourth-quarter comebacks so far this season, ranking 31st in first-quarter points per game (1.3) but tied for 10th in fourth-quarter ppg (7.4). Meanwhile, Las Vegas' defense has generated the most fourth-quarter turnovers (10, h/t Ari Meirov on X/Twitter), three more than any other team, and ranks 10th in ppg allowed (6.1) during the final frame.

So, I think that tilts in the Silver and Black's favor, and the numbers above remind me of the Saints game from earlier in the season, where the Raiders won as underdogs in New Orleans. I'll go 24-21 Vegas."

Thanks again to Matt for taking the time to answer my questions.
