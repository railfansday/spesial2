PHOENIX, Nov. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- University of Phoenix College of Doctoral Studies has published a new white paper, "Learning to Use AI: Challenges and Opportunities," by Louise Underdahl, Ph.D., and Alma Gojkovic, DBA, examining how organizations can better support employees learning to use artificial intelligence (AI) as workplace adoption expands. Drawing on the 2026 Career Optimism IndexÂ®, Gojkovic's 2025 qualitative doctoral research on AI implementation in accounting and additional scholarly literature, the authors examine shortcomings in workplace AI training, risks associated with unstructured adoption and approaches to improving organizational implementation.

The authors examine these findings alongside research on AI integration, including Gojkovic's 2025 qualitative exploratory case study of accounting managers, accounting staff and AI technology project leaders in selected organizations in the southwestern United States.

Participants in that study described AI training as frequently too general, too brief or insufficiently connected to specific accounting responsibilities. They also identified limited opportunities for ongoing feedback, practical application and post-training support.

"The accounting professionals in my research described a need for training that went beyond introducing AI tools," said Gojkovic. "They wanted practical examples relevant to their responsibilities, opportunities to apply what they learned and ongoing support as new technologies became part of their daily work. Their experiences highlight why role-specific training deserves greater attention."

Why does unstructured AI adoption create organizational challenges?

The authors also examine a broader organizational challenge: Traditional approaches to technology implementation may not adequately account for AI systems that adapt and evolve through use. Drawing on research involving complex adaptive systems, the paper explores how staged learning, continuous feedback and coordinated changes across people, processes and technology can inform more effective AI integration.

"Learning to use AI is not solely a question of acquiring technical skills," said Underdahl. "Organizations also need to consider how employees learn, how new technologies change established practices and what support structures are needed for responsible use. Effective integration requires attention to people, processes and organizational culture alongside the technology itself."

Underdahl is a doctoral instructor at University of Phoenix College of Doctoral Studies and a research fellow with the College's Center for Educational and Instructional Technology Research (CEITR).
