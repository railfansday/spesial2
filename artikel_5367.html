Poundland collapsed into administration, putting 12,000 U.K. jobs at risk

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Administrators from AlixPartners are seeking buyers for all or part of the discount chain after owner Gordon Brothers' sale process failed

Poundland went into administration on Wednesday, putting about 12,000 jobs at risk, according to Reuters. Administration is a form of creditor protection.

Restructuring firm AlixPartners was appointed administrator after a sales process run by Poundland's U.S. owner, Gordon Brothers, "did not identify a viable solution for the business as a whole," according to Bloomberg. Clare Kennedy, Daniel Imison and Kevin Wall of AlixPartners are the joint administrators, according to Property Week.

Poundland will keep operating while the administrators look for buyers for the company or its assets, either entirely or piecemeal, according to Reuters. Every shop and distribution center is still open for business. The chain operates about 600 locations spanning the U.K. and Ireland, according to Bloomberg.

"Our priority now is to work with all stakeholders, including representatives from government and [trade union] Usdaw, as we identify the best possible route forward," a spokesperson for the administrators said, according to Property Week.

Last year, Gordon Brothers acquired Poundland from Pepco Group for £1 ($1.31), then restructured the chain, closing 149 stores and cutting about 2,200 jobs, according to Bloomberg. Gordon Brothers said it injected £85 million ($111 million) of fresh funding after every other potential backer declined to step in, according to Property Week. It also said the business could still be hurt by inflation or a slide in sales.

U.K. lawmakers allege that Gordon Brothers pulled millions of pounds out of the retailer and left it short of the money to pay suppliers ahead of Christmas, according to Bloomberg. Gordon Brothers rejected the accusation, saying it has made funding available to the business.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham had called on Gordon Brothers to prevent the collapse, according to Bloomberg. "The news that it has gone into administration just weeks before Christmas will be deeply concerning for workers and their families," Burnham said in a statement, according to Bloomberg.

Parliament's business select committee has asked Mark Newton-Jones, who leads Gordon Brothers' European business, to give evidence in open session about the collapse, according to Bloomberg. The committee wants assurances that Poundland will not have Gordon Brothers, or anyone connected to it, serving as its administrator, liquidator or in any other insolvency capacity.

Retail trade union Usdaw has asked the administrators for an urgent meeting, according to Bloomberg.
