ICE: Borrowers reach record tappable equity as cash-out refis drive runoff

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Mortgage borrowers held $17.5 trillion in home equity in Q3, with tappable equity averaging $204,000 per borrower, according to ICE’s November Mortgage Monitor. With 30-year rates above 7%, refi activity is dominated by cash-out loans, which now account for about one in seven rate locks and 75% of refinance-driven servicing runoff.   AI Summary

Mortgage holders collectively had a record $17.5 trillion in home equity in the third quarter, up 1.4% from a year earlier, according to Intercontinental Exchange’s (ICE) November Mortgage Monitor report.

That equity is being tapped through cash-out refinances at a time when traditional rate-and-term refis have largely disappeared in a 7%-plus rate environment. Meanwhile, servicers are holding on to a shrinking share of the borrowers who do refinance.

ICE reported that tappable equity — the amount owners can borrow against while still keeping at least 20% equity — rose 1.2% to an average of $204,000 per borrower, $16,600 more than a year ago. Rising home values also created 2.4 million “equity millionaires,” homeowners with at least $1 million in tappable equity, up 6% year over year.

With 30-year fixed mortgage rates above 7% in early October, rate-and-term refinances remain scarce and most refi activity is being driven by borrowers seeking cash. Cash-out refinances accounted for about one in seven rate locks in recent weeks, while purchase loans made up roughly 80% of locks.

Cash-out refis are also driving portfolio runoff. They represented 75% of all refinance-related servicing attrition in the third quarter, signaling that most borrowers who tap equity are doing so with a different lender or investor.

“In a higher rate environment, cash-outs are making up a greater share of refinances, with those homeowners giving up historically low mortgage rates and taking on higher monthly payments to tap into their equity,” Andy Walden, head of mortgage and housing market research at ICE, said in a statement.

Overall servicer retention for refinancing borrowers fell to 27% in the third quarter, down four percentage points from the prior quarter, according to ICE. Retention among rate-and-term refinancers dropped nine points to 30%, while cash-out retention held at 26%.

Servicers performed worst with borrowers who originated their previous loans in 2019 or earlier. Only 15% of those cash-out borrowers were retained by their prior servicer.

“Traditional rate-based retention strategies aren’t effective for borrowers who are motivated by access to equity rather than interest rate savings,” Bob Hart, president of mortgage technology at ICE, said in a statement. He said servicers need better visibility into borrower equity and tools to engage them before they seek financing elsewhere.
