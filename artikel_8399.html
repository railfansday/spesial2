Because 14.7% is a long, long way from 6.5%. Saying there's a 50-50 chance the ten-year is above, or below, 6.5% doesn't mean that all possible outcomes outside of 6.5% are equally plausible.

Predicting 6.5% but seeing a realized 7.2% (or 5.9%) over ten years would probably be viewed as not missing by all that much. 14.7% is more than double 6.5% -- that qualifies as missing by a lot by most any definition.
Given S&P500 volatility, that's about 1.73 standard deviations from the baseline.

That's an error rate to be expected about 8% of the time.

So it's within the expected error bars, not an extreme outlier.
