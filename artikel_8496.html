Eat the Same Produce for Less – Skip the Precut Packs and Buy Frozen Off-Season

To spend less on fruits and vegetables without eating fewer of them, buy them whole instead of precut and switch to frozen or canned when fresh is out of season. Plan meals around your store’s weekly produce sale, and buy only the fresh produce your family will eat before it spoils.

In the USDA’s fruit and vegetable price data for 2023, cauliflower cut from a whole head averaged 66 cents per cup you’d eat, against $1.13 for precut florets. Celery from a bunch you trim yourself averaged 48 cents a cup, against 70 cents for sticks.

Fresh prices also change with the season. Strawberries averaged $2.22 for a 12-ounce pack in July 2025 and $3.61 in December, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. In the same USDA data, frozen blueberries averaged $1.39 a cup, against $1.73 fresh, and canned green beans 54 cents, against 87 cents fresh.

Frozen keeps its vitamins, too. A University of Georgia study found no real difference in vitamin content between frozen and fresh produce in most of its comparisons.

So buy each fruit or vegetable in whatever form costs least when you shop.

At the USDA’s 2023 prices, a family eating 4 cups a week each of cauliflower, celery, and blueberries saves about $4 a week by buying the first two whole and the berries frozen instead of precut and fresh. That comes to more than $200 a year.
