Serena Williams is Launching Another VC Firm to Invest in AI Startups

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Tennis star Serena Williams is (almost) folding her first VC firm, only to open another one. She's founding her new firm, Starfire Ventures, with her longtime business partner Beth Ferreira, and is targeting $200 million for its debut fund. The fund seeks to invest in early-stage startups, particularly those using (no points for guessing) AI in healthcare, financial services and consumer services.

Serena Williams and Beth Ferreira first met in 2015 when they both invested in MasterClass. The duo then continued co-investing in startups like Daily Harvest and Ollie Pets.

In 2022, when Serena Ventures, Williams' first VC firm, raised its first institutional fund of $111 million, Ferreira became an adviser and eventually its general partner in 2024. While Serena Ventures has stopped making new investments, Starfire will take the steering wheel with $200 million for its debut round.

Unlike Serena Ventures, Starfire will operate independently of Williams' personal brand and establish a longer-lasting investment platform. The plan is to invest in 20 to 25 startups, writing considerably larger checks than Serena Ventures typically did — with individual checks ranging from $1 million to $10 million.

Money doesn't have time to wait or waste and so Starfire has already made an investment in an AI assistant startup called Town last month. It participated in a $108 million funding round that valued the company at $1 billion.

The distinction between Serena Ventures and Starfire is the gap between Serena Williams the tennis star and Serena Williams as a general partner at a VC firm.

Serena Ventures was initially built around Williams' personal wealth, reputation and investment interests. It was her platform to support entrepreneurs, particularly women and founders from underrepresented communities. But that's what it was: a celebrity VC fund cutting relatively small investment checks to early-stage companies.

Whereas Starfire is designed to operate more like a traditional institutional venture capital firm. Its $200 million target would provide a dedicated pool of capital to invest across a defined portfolio, with Williams and Ferreira acting as general partners. The goal here is to build a firm that can survive beyond Williams' celebrity and eventually establish its own investment track record.

Williams and Ferreira have not disclosed how much of the $200 million has already been committed or how much they are personally contributing, but apparently the two have already met with more than 30 companies.

Starfire is entering the picture when enormous amounts of VC money are flowing into a concentrated pool of startups. PitchBook-NVCA's second-quarter 2026 Venture Monitor shows U.S. venture investment hit $413 billion in the first half of the year. But nearly 88% of that capital went into deals of $100 million or more. And the VC firms attracting and deploying the capital are also a small concentrated number.

Andreessen Horowitz, Thrive Capital and Founders Fund attracted 48% of all capital raised by U.S. venture funds in the first half of 2026. That's the gap Starfire wants to exploit. By identifying promising early-stage AI startups, Starfire can identify opportunities that larger investors might overlook or later on invest in.

While Williams doesn't want to lend her name to this firm, her clout and celebrity aren't going anywhere.

The next and immediate step is to raise the desired $200 million. Sure, Starfire has the advantage of Williams' profile and Ferreira's experience, but it is still a new VC firm without a real history in a sea of sharks. Williams and Ferreira need to prove that their access and influence can translate to measurable financial returns.

The firm's strategy to back underserved founders does not automatically guarantee commercial success. Investing in AI means entering a very competitive market. There are thousands of startups attempting to commercialize AI and the signal-to-noise ratio is skewed.
