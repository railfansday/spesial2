Jensen Huang's affair aided at least $7.5 billion in AI chip shipments to China, report says

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Nvidia's board has launched an internal investigation, and the FBI is examining whether the CEO was targeted by foreign intelligence

Nvidia $NVDA CEO Jensen Huang’s relationship with a Chinese businesswoman suspected of spying for Beijing helped enable at least $7.5 billion in Nvidia chip shipments to Chinese companies, according to The Information, which cited two people who know her. Reuters said it could not independently verify the claims.

The businesswoman, Alice Huang, assembled a web of firms spanning China and Singapore, partnering with her sister and husband to obtain the chips, according to The Information. She is not related to Jensen Huang and is also known as Huang Le. When orders for chips caught up in U.S. export curbs stalled, she leaned on her connections at Nvidia and its distributors to lock in supply, according to The Information. That effort got roughly 240,000 chips, spanning H100, H200 and some A100 models, into China.

Shanghai Century Litong, a company based in China and founded in June 2023, sat at the hub of the network, according to The Information. By October 2023, it had been designated a preferred cloud partner by Nvidia, which put it first in line for the chips. Century Litong leased out computing capacity and ran data centers built on Nvidia chips, some of which are prohibited from being sold to China. A portion of the hardware ended up supporting Alibaba, Tencent and several smaller AI labs in China, according to The Information.

Alice Huang held a senior role at Megaspeed, a Singapore-registered company that U.S. authorities have investigated over the flow of Nvidia chips from Southeast Asia into China, according to The Information. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing and says it abides by all relevant regulations, according to Bloomberg. Nvidia has said before that its own review of Megaspeed turned up no sign of diversion.

Jensen Huang confirmed the affair on Sunday, and Nvidia's board has opened an independent investigation, according to Bloomberg. Nvidia said the probe followed reports that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The FBI is looking into whether Huang, 63, became the subject of a foreign intelligence operation, according to Reuters.

The New York Times reported that American officials received a briefing saying Alice Huang worked for China's Ministry of State Security, according to CNBC. China has rejected the allegation, calling it a smear. President Donald Trump said on Monday that Jensen Huang told him he "had no idea that she was a spy."

Jensen Huang has been asked to appear at a closed-door session before several House committees meeting jointly, to discuss Nvidia's security protocols. The session is scheduled for Nov. 18, according to Bloomberg.

Sen. Elizabeth Warren asked Huang in June to testify before the Senate Banking Committee about Nvidia's compliance with U.S. export control laws and its business in China.
