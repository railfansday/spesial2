How Hibernian made Hampden history against Hearts and booked final date with Rangers

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Hibernian made a little piece of Hampden history this afternoon when they came from behind to beat Hearts in the second Premier Sports Cup semi-final.

The Easter Road club had lost all four of their previous meetings with their age-old Edinburgh adversaries at the Mount Florida venue before today.

When Claudio Braga put the Tynecastle men ahead in just the fourth minute it looked like they might be heading for another defeat.

But first-half goals from Martin Boyle and Jamie McGrath gave Hibs a deserved lead and they held on to book a place in the final against Rangers next month.

Marink Reedijk, who had failed to win any of his first four games as manager, savoured the victory as much as any supporter.

Here are five talking points from the last four meeting.

Stragglers were still taking their seats inside the packed stadium when Hearts broke the deadlock.

Braga got in between Jason Kerr and Marko Ivezic and on the end of a Calvin Miller cross in the Hibs six yard box and headed beyond Raphael Sallinger from a few yards out.

The Portuguese forward was mobbed by his team-mates on the touchline as the maroon half of the stadium celebrated wildly.

But there was not much more for them to shout about after they had edged ahead.

Hibs recovered superbly from the early setback. Felix Passlack, their captain, was unfortunate not to restore parity just a few minutes later after being sent through on goal by Joe Newell. Beau Reus just managed to keep his deflected attempt out.

Owen Elding also should have done better after being supplied by Adam Mayor. The striker shelled over when he only had the keeper to beat. But he atoned for his miss soon afterwards by teeing up the leveller.

He controlled a pass from Newell and then scooped a chip over the heads of the opposition defence to Boyle who turned a shot in off of Reus and the inside of the left post.

The Hearts players and fans appealed for offside but linesman Douglas Ross kept his flag down.

VAR officials Andrew Dallas and Nick Walsh had a long look back at the replay over at Clydesdale House and found that there colleague had made the correct call. Jordi Altena had played the winger on.

Jamie McGrath put his side in front with an opportunistic strike at a Boyle corner three minutes before half-time.

The ball broke to Newell, who had just skimmed the top of the crossbar from distance, and he burst forward and then unleashed a powerful shot which was blocked by Malachi Fagan-Walcott. McGrath was perfectly positioned to net the rebound.

Hibs fans had high hopes of seeing their struggling heroes produce some Total Football when Dutchman Reedijk replaced David Gray as manager at Easter Road last month.

There has, though, been little evidence of that in the former Ajax youth coach’s opening games.

His charges produced some nice passages of play after falling behind at Hampden and deserved to go in front. They dominated long spells of the encounter with their capital rivals. But they did so by winning their battles and bullying their opponents.

Josh Mulligan and Kerr were booked by referee David Dickinson for fouls on Stephen Kinglsey and Dhanda respectively. The players in green and white shirts were hungrier and far more physical.

Miller going off injured – he was replaced by Sabri Guendouz shortly after the half hour mark and was later seen hobbling about on crutches – did nothing to help Hearts’ cause. He had set up the opener and posed a threat whenever he got on the ball.

Still, they could have no complaints about the scoreline when they returned to their dressing room. Braga had headed a Josh McPake cross well wide after his side had fallen behind. But if he had found the target and levelled it would have flattered them.

Hearts, doubtless after a few choice words from their manager Vrancken, played with far greater purpose when the action resumed in the second half. Sallinger had to produce fine saves from both Braga and Guendouz to keep the Leith men in front.

McPake, who should be named in the Scotland squad for the Nations League games against North Macedonia and Switzerland on Tuesday, was a real handful throughout.

However, Hibs looked dangerous on the counter too. Reus had to deny Boyle and Ivezic and Kingsley slid in a denied Passlack on two occasions as well. It was, to use an old football cliché, real end-to-end stuff.

The meeting between Aberdeen and Rangers on Saturday evening had been a tense affair that was devoid of chances as well as a difficult watch for the neutral observer. This was the perfect antidote. Everyone in the 41,673-strong crowd was royally entertained.

This has been another wretched season for Hibs fans and they travelled along the M8 in hope rather than expectation.

The Leith men last won a William Hill Premiership game back in August and are in bottom spot in the top flight table.

But Passlack and his team-mates buried the memory of the Scottish Cup defeats that Hibs they had suffered against Hearts at Hampden in 2006, 2012 and 2022 and the League Cup loss they were on the receiving end of on the South Side of Glasgow in 1955.

There were tears in the eyes of their longsuffering followers during the rendition of Sunshine on Leith which they belted out following the final whistle.

If Hibernian perform as well in the final against Rangers next month then their fans may have more reason to party.
