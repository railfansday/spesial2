These 5 AI Prompts Can Take Over the Marketing Tasks That Eat Up Your Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

You're viewing the US website. Looking for the Site edition?

Copy these ready-made prompts into your favorite AI tool and get hours of your week back for the work that actually grows your business.

Opinions expressed by Entrepreneur contributors are their own.

Running a business means wearing a lot of hats, and marketing is a common one that slips. You know you need a content calendar, a steady blog, ad creative, and emails people actually open. Finding the hours to build all of it from scratch is the hard part.

AI prompts help with that. A good prompt hands you a first draft of the thinking work: a month of post topics, a blog outline, a stack of headlines to test. While you make the calls, the AI gets you past that blank page so you can get moving.

The prompts below cover tasks that eat up a small team’s week: planning content, outlining blog posts, writing ad copy, testing email subject lines, and researching competitors. Each one comes with the prompt itself, what it does, who it’s for, which AI tools it works with, and steps for using it.

Copy the prompt, swap your details into the brackets, and run it. The follow-up steps are where the real shaping happens, so don’t skip them. Check every result against what you know about your business and your customers before you publish anything. AI works from patterns, so it can miss details only you have.

The Prompt: “Act as a content strategist for a [industry] business. Build a 30-day social media content calendar for [platform]. Mix educational posts, behind-the-scenes posts, and promotional posts at roughly a 3:1:1 ratio. For each day, give me a topic and a one-line angle, not a full caption. Flag any days where a seasonal event, holiday, or industry moment could shape the topic.”

What It Does: This prompt turns a blank content plan into 30 ready-to-write topics in one pass. It keeps the mix balanced, so promotional posts don’t crowd out the content readers actually want, and it surfaces timely hooks you might otherwise miss while planning weeks in advance.

Who It’s For: Founders juggling their own social media without a marketing team behind them, plus small teams who split content duties across two or three people and need one shared plan to work from.

The Prompt: “Act as an SEO content strategist. I’m writing a blog post targeting the keyword ‘[target keyword]’ for an audience of [audience]. Give me a title, a meta description under 155 characters, and an outline with H2 and H3 headers that covers what a searcher expects to find. Flag any subtopics competitors likely already cover.”

What It Does: This prompt saves the research phase of blog writing by mapping out structure and gaps before a single sentence gets drafted.

Who It’s For: Marketing teams and solo bloggers who write regularly but don’t have time for a full competitive audit on every post.

The Prompt: “Act as a direct-response copywriter. Write 8 short ad headlines and 4 primary text variations for [product/service], targeting [audience]. Each headline should lead with a different angle: a pain point, a benefit, a number, and a question. Keep headlines under 40 characters.”

What It Does: This prompt gives you a stack of testable ad variations in minutes instead of staring at a blank headline field.

Who It’s For: Anyone running their own paid social or search campaigns without an in-house copywriter.

The Prompt: “Act as an email marketer. Here’s my current subject line: ‘[insert subject line]’. Give me 6 alternatives that increase curiosity or urgency without sounding like clickbait. For each one, explain in one sentence why it might outperform the original.”

What It Does: This prompt takes a flat subject line and turns it into a set of sharper, testable options, along with the reasoning behind each one.

Who It’s For: Newsletter writers and small marketing teams who send regularly but rarely A/B test subject lines.

The Prompt: “Act as a market research analyst. I compete with [competitor name(s)] in the [industry] space. Based on what you know about their positioning, list 5 topics or content angles they likely haven’t covered well, and explain the opportunity for each.”

What It Does: This prompt surfaces content and positioning gaps a competitor has left open, giving you a shortlist of angles worth owning first.

Who It’s For: Founders and marketers trying to differentiate in a crowded niche.
