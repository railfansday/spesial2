Path Robotics raised $300 million, nearly quadrupling its value to $2.45 billion

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Georgian led the Series E round for the maker of AI software for welding robots; the company plans to expand into defense and energy

Path Robotics, which builds programs that guide self-operating factory robots, raised $300 million in a Series E round that puts its valuation at $2.45 billion, according to Bloomberg. The valuation is close to four times its previous level.

Georgian, a growth-stage investor that concentrates on artificial intelligence, led the round, according to Bloomberg. Existing investors Addition, Drive Capital, IronArc Ventures, Tiger Global and Yamaha Motor Ventures took part, as did new backers D. E. Shaw Ventures and Naver Ventures. Path Robotics has taken in roughly $610 million in total funding.

Founded in 2018, the company has centered its work on its Obsidian physical AI model, which gives robotic arms and quadrupeds the ability to weld upwards of 12 different parts, according to Bloomberg. The model lets robots perceive their surroundings, think through problems and adjust on the fly, rather than executing a fixed set of instructions.

Co-founder and Chief Executive Officer Andy Lonsberry told Bloomberg the company started with welding because it was the biggest pain point manufacturers described. "But as you de-bottleneck a task like welding, and you can weld 10 times as much, now we have a problem with assembly or inspection or painting or grinding," Lonsberry said. "We're seeing customers want us to try to help them deliver more tasks."

Lonsberry said the company plans to put the new capital toward entering additional sectors, including defense and energy infrastructure, according to Bloomberg. The company is also aiming to broaden the range of manufacturing jobs it can handle and to work with a wider variety of robot platforms.

Annual recurring revenue is now 17 times what it was a year and a half ago, according to Bloomberg. Staff numbers are up 97% since October 2025, and the company is targeting a headcount of 400 before the year closes.

Path Robotics has 50 advance orders to deliver for Rove, a welding robot that pairs Boston Dynamics’ Spot quadruped with Path Robotics’ technology, according to Bloomberg. According to the startup, the robot is set to join human welders at shipyards, construction sites and large industrial campuses beginning next year.

Customers include U.S. shipbuilder Huntington Ingalls $HII Industries and Saronic Technologies, a maker of maritime drones and unmanned military ships, according to Bloomberg.

"There is more demand than capacity today in the U.S.," Lonsberry said. "There's an opportunity to grow capacity utilizing robots to keep jobs here but then grow jobs that support the robots or the factory to do more. None of our customers have ever fired a welder because of a task robot."
