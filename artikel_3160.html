Bono Bowl Review: U2 Delivers Halftime Goods & Hits On Netflix & NFL’s First Thanksgiving Eve Game, With A Little Help From Dolly Parton

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Executive Editor, Legal, Labor & Politics

No matter how many hits and tricks U2 pulled out of the hat at L.A.’s SoFi Stadium, today’s Bono Bowl on Netflix was always going to live in the shadow of the streamer’s square dancing filled Beyoncé Bowl from Christmas Day 2024.

Accepting the inevitable, the elder statesmen of anthem rock took their halftime spin on stage at the NFL’s first ever Thanksgiving Eve game to go full bread and circuses. Giving the people and the streamer what they wanted, the nine-minute show wasn’t as dramatic as the currently 13-13 match-up between the hometown L.A. Rams and the Green Bay Packers, but it didn’t have to be.

All U2 had to do tonight was show everyone they still have gas in the tank.

Under those criteria, in their first time headlining on Netflix, the Irish rockers succeeded.

Veering towards the tried and true, Bono, guitarist The Edge, bassiest Adam Clayton and drummer Larry Mullen Jr thundered through their Boomer classics of “Sunday Bloody Sunday,” The Joshua Tree’s “Where The Streets Have No Name” and MLK tribute “Pride (In The Name of Love).” Add to that a sprinkle of The Supremes “Stop! In the Name of Love” via some glorious vocals thrown in from teh Grammy nominated Womack Sisters and the beats of an on-field marching band and you have an All-American party — which is precisely what a NFL halftime show is supposed to be.

In a nod to their latest release and the times we live in, the second song in the set poignantly was the “Torn” duet with Dolly Parton

Dolly Forever <3 U2 pays tribute to Dolly Parton with “Torn” during their halftime performance pic.twitter.com/c4CfeoQiXi

With the obvious exception of the inclusion of the late great Dolly, who was displayed on flowing curtains that came down from the stadium roof, it was a set that could have come almost anytime the last 40 years. In fact, besides “Torn” (Parton’s last recording), every song in U2’s Thanksgiving Eve set came from when Ronald Reagan was President.

Bono’s vow not to use backing tracks and the pre-halftime shot that Netflix showed of U2 heading through Sofi’s catacombs on gold carts sorta told you all you needed to know.

To their credit, unlike most Super Bowl halftime shows of the past dozen years, (and expected for Jay-Z’s Super Bowl LXI set on February 14 at Sofi), a relatively stripped down U2 didn’t bring on any super star guests for the L.A. crowd. Walking out on the field to a chorus, the usually-chatty band took the in-the-round stage with a “thank you for having us” and said little more.

Oddly ending a bit too quickly, tonight’s shindig came halfway through the NFL’s first Thanksgiving Eve match with the Rams battling it out with Packers at the L.A. team’s camera-ready stadium. A clear belt notch in the league’s desire to dramatically expand the incredibly lucrative football franchise, tonight’s much-hyped game between the Packers and the Rams was given all the gloss of a playoff clash or the Super Bowl itself by the Ted Sarandos and Greg Peter-run streamer.

Having already played a few warm-up gigs — like opening the November 14 Rock and Roll Hall of Fame ceremony — during the extensive promotional tour this month for their recently released Carnaval De Luz album, tonight gave U2 a further chance to reach fans old and new. Aided by a stage that was rolled out and set up in less than 8 minutes, a 52-foot telescopic camera crane and a specialty camera coming down from the Sofi roof, the Jesse Collins Entertainment-produced set had a very professional shine.

For a soaring-gesture band never shy of taking big stages in a big way, the global reach can only help U2 hawk a few more tickets for their upcoming Greatest Show on Earth tour. The tour begins in Mexico City in February. After shows in South America and Europe, the band and their unofficial 50th anniversary tour ends up in North America for a 15-stadium run starting in Miami on August 13.

At the same time, as the Rolling Stones, Bruce Springsteen and many other stadium-playing legacy acts will tell you, the role of old bands is to play the old hits — no matter how good or bad the latest album is. Bono, the Edge, Mullen and Clayton knew that back in 2002 when they played Super Bowl XXXVI in the aftermath of the 9/11 terror attacks. Using their Elevation Tour set at NOLA’s Superdome, U2 in less than 20-minutes whipped off “Beautiful Day,” “MLK” and “Where The Streets Have No Name” and paid tribute via curtain with the names of the fallen on it to those who lost their lives that horrible day in NYC.

One of the biggest bands in the world for 40 years, U2 haven’t toured since 2019, and they had a bit to prove tonight, as they made clear.

Yet, the GameDay halftime show wasn’t their first time on stage this decade.

A 40-show stint at Vegas’ Sphere starting in September 2023 saw the band pulling it off Zoo TV style, even with an injured Mullen sitting it out. Still, a big live draw since the mid-1980s no matter how their records charted, the Irving and Jeffrey Azoff-managed U2 are leaving no opportunity unexplored this year in a hope to return to relevance.

Even Sofi itself got in on the act on Wednesday.

We are excited to welcome @U2 for their premiere appearance at SoFi Stadium during today's halftime show 🏈 And even more excited for their return on October 1 & 2! Tickets on sale on December 4 on Ticketmaster 🎟️ pic.twitter.com/KnB9CkgIQp

With ads galore and L.A. beauty shots tonight, Netflix has finally worked out most of the kinks that marred some of their past live sports events. Fueled by play-by-play from Noah Eagle and analysis from Hall-of-Famer Luke Kuechly, the primetime result so far delivered a slick and seamless product that moved at the pace of the action on the field. Simply put (and hear me Jeff Bezos), Netflix equaled what ESPN and Monday Night Football have been doing for ages — which is no small feat when you consider that the streamer’s initial live sporting event was just over three years ago with the mash-up golf tournament The Netflix Cup.

From the big picture perspective, tonight’s game and that halftime show can’t but help Netflix’s ongoing bid to FIFA for the 2030 World Cup. For U2, the ride may be vintage, but turns out there’s still quite a bit of gas in the old tank.
