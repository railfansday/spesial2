Exporting a Weebly blog: why the archive ZIP skips it, and how to list what's missing

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Weebly's site archive (Settings → General → Archive) doesn't contain your blog. Weebly says so on its Back Up Your Site page (last updated 2020-09-10, read 2026-10-26):

Blog and store pages aren’t included in the export as they rely directly on our database. Also, some components of your site may not work correctly outside of the Weebly editor, like the Contact Form and Slideshow elements.

The pages that link to the blog are still in the archive, though. So the menu in your exported index.html still has a Blog link, and the page it points to isn't in the ZIP. This post shows how our converter finds those links, and how to do the same with grep.

Weebly's global operations notice (last updated 2026-08-10, read 2026-10-26) says sites there "will be unpublished on 2026-09-27" and that "You will still have access to your Weebly account until 2026-12-26". Before that date it says to:

Download your site content and data from Account Settings > My Data > Download My Data to migrate your content to another website provider or to retain it for your records. [...] If you wish to just download data from a specific site in your account, you may also do so from Website > Edit Site > Settings > Archive

The notice doesn't say what the Download My Data package contains. If you need the blog posts, request it and check. It's also a different layout from the site archive, and our converter doesn't accept it. Its error text says: "An account-level “Download My Data” package is a different layout, and this tool cannot turn it into a website."

resolveLocalTarget() handles the absolute form. It parses the URL and treats it as local only if the hostname is one of the site's own hosts. Otherwise it returns null, and the link counts as external. Root-relative and relative hrefs are resolved from the current file's folder.

The hrefs land in the generated RESCUE-REPORT.html under "Missing blog posts" and "Missing store / products". Above that, the report shows the fixed note: "The site-archive export does not include blog posts or store pages. This tool cannot recover them."

Unlike the TypeScript, this doesn't strip ?query or #hash first, so /blog?page=2 won't match. Check each result against the files in site/. Anything that isn't there is a page you have to get some other way. On our own real archive, which has no blog, the pipeline prints nothing.

It lists missing pages. It doesn't recover them. The posts aren't in the archive, so no converter working from that ZIP can bring them back. If the blog matters, request Download My Data before your access ends, and check it for your posts.
