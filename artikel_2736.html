Columbus Crew's Laurent Courtois chides Lionel Messi on 'sad' behavior

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Laurent Courtois holds great respect for Lionel Messi's immense soccer talent, but the Crew's interim coach isn't a fan of his antics, such as whipping a soccer ball into an opposing player's face or intentionally stomping on an opponent's foot.

Messi, 39, was caught doing both of those things to Crew players in games this season, each time as a hot-blooded reaction to being defended hard or defied.

The stomping incident occurred during the Crew's 2-1 victory over Inter Miami CF on Sept. 27 at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field, where Messi intentionally stepped on midfielder Santiago Rodriguez's foot, and the ball-to-face incident happened Nov. 21 in Miami, sending forward Gonzalo Tapia to the turf late in the first half of a 3-0 victory for Columbus in Game 1 of a best-of-3 first-round playoff series.

The latter occurred near the Crew's bench, so Courtois got a front-row view. Messi and Tapia were shown yellow cards for their roles in what happened, but many took to social media right after it occurred to complain about superstar treatment preventing a red.

"Everybody knows it was more than a yellow card," Courtois said after the game. "It's just sad. It's just really sad."

Asked again about the incident Nov. 23, Courtois didn't back down. He just built upon his argument.

"There's multiple ways to see that [situation]," Courtois said. "If you talk about purely the soccer part, I think when he dove inside of our box [earlier in the game], it should've been a yellow [card] to start with. And this incident is not only the [throw-in]. It's the whole sequence that leads to it, and you can tell that there's weird intentions at the least, so it is what it is."

Prior to taking the ball off his face, Tapia had successfully challenged Messi on the Crew's half of the field. He sent the ball rolling out of bounds and chased after it, which prompted the Miami forward to give him a shove from behind. Taking his time getting back to the field, Tapia put himself in Messi's personal space and didn't appear in any kind of hurry to move.

So Messi grabbed the ball, bopped Tapia on the nose with it and quickly whipped it into his face at high velocity from close range, attempting to make it look like an ordinary throw-in. As Tapia fell to the turf grabbing his face, Messi turned to locate the first official he could find and began complaining that his fallen opponent wasn't called for a hand ball.

It was a situation that could have easily thrown the Crew for a loop while seeking retribution for Tapia. Instead, they turned a 1-0 lead at halftime into a 3-0 lead early in the second half on goals by Taha Habroune and Tapia — who had scored on a penalty shot in the first half.

"I'm just happy my guys stayed [level] and the referee did, I would say, except on that call, an incredible job to keep his judgment the right way," Courtois said. "And that's all we ask, to be fair and just."

The MLS Disciplinary Committee could review the incident for additional punishment, such as a fine or suspension, but the latter would be a shocking decision prior to Game 2 of the series Nov. 27 at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field.

"The other part is we have kids, nephews, little girls [watching] and you want to be, I believe, somebody you want to look up to," Courtois said. "So, there's stuff that you should be doing. That's all."

Messi has also done that against the Crew this season, tying the Sept. 27 game 1-1 with a jaw-dropping goal scored by bending a shot off free kick over both a defensive wall and the outstretched hands of goalkeeper Patrick Schulte.

Prior to taking the shot, Messi appeared to gesture to fans seated nearby, telling them to watch closely. Upon scoring, Messi pumped his fist at the crowd nearby. That was gamesmanship, similar to Tapia crowding his space.

Messi's overreaction to that crowding was childish and fitting for a Miami team that's filled with stars who act full of themselves, arguing every call that goes against them, often in groups, and parading around the field like they own it. Expect more of the same in Game 2, which the Crew already knows.

"Obviously, there's the dark arts of the game, in a way, and you're going to have the fouls, the crying to the referee, things of that nature," Crew captain Sean Zawadzki said. "[If] you want to go far in the playoffs, you have to be smart in those moments, and you can't let those little charades ... impact you. It's 'How can you keep your head?' and I think we have a team that's done really well with that."
