Enzo Maresca is still searching for answers after Man City's draw with Napoli: There were glimpses of improvement but individual mistakes again proved their undoing, writes JACK GAUGHAN

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Published: 17:07 EST, 24 November 2026 | Updated: 17:14 EST, 24 November 2026

The message from Enzo Maresca ought to be clear when Manchester City convene for their meeting at the training ground on Saturday before heading down to London. Clear because there is no other appropriate message to impart, really.

He has to say that Arsenal away must be considered the perfect game for his players at this point. The opportunity to reignite their season, to stand up, to show the Premier League they are not going anywhere just quite yet. Squads who went before them would certainly approach a fixture of this magnitude with that attitude.

City were still searching for answers against Napoli, despite a point rescued late on by Rayan Cherki, with the strange thing about this that they seemed to have more of a clue despite another disappointing outcome.

An inverted full back, Nico O’Reilly, gave them more structural security and should have allowed the attacking talent freedom to do their thing. City were undone by individual mistakes rather than the overarching plan, although for large spells this meandered rather than crackled, not the sort of occasion you might pair Kevin De Bruyne and this stadium with.

The gentle hum, the half-hearted chants for a returning king, resembled an afternoon session in front of a crowd drowsy from their liquid lunches.

The run rate almost ground to a complete halt at one point as City chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak intriguingly entertained UEFA president Aleksander Ceferin in the directors’ box.

City, sporting a fourth kit that had an unfortunate Guantanamo quality to it, certainly had more of a clue than they did here on Saturday. There were clearer patterns – noticeably Marc Guehi piercing midfield lines with one incisive pass for inverted full back Nico O’Reilly – but this is a team whose confidence has been shaken in recent weeks and that will not return overnight.

Interchanges between Elliot Anderson and Enzo Fernandez offered glimpses of added ingenuity, something not seen against Fulham, while Napoli’s dogged shape – a deep back line with the threat of rapid counters – made life tough for the hosts. There have been plenty of nights like this over the years and City have invariably found a way.

Massimiliano Allegri had seen the Rodrigo Muniz’s goal on Saturday, originating from a throw in deep inside Fulham’s third. And he had seen one of Liam Delap’s recently as well, coming direct from a City corner. He left three Napoli players up from City set pieces, knowing they are susceptible to those quick breaks, in a vaguely engaging game of cat and mouse.

Eighth in Serie A and with only one victory so far in the Champions League group phase, the Italians arrived looking as though they ought to be ripe for picking. Yet Gianluigi Donnarumma’s stunning save, hurling himself left to thwart Sam Beukema’s free header, left them wanting more before the break. Matteo Politano earlier darted beyond O’Reilly but squared to nobody instead of letting fly.

City’s supporters definitely demanded more caution thrown to the wind. Anderson’s dangerous crosses from that right-hand channel – the area presided over by De Bruyne for a decade – occasionally proved unsettling for Napoli, while Allan should have scored from a ricocheting corner as captain Erling Haaland claimed a foul for being dragged down.

In a sign that Maresca was placing more players in threatening areas, O’Reilly had headed over a Matheus Nunes cross – yet chances had been at a premium, those who should be protagonists taking too many safe options rather than pushing chests out and relishing responsibility.

And when there was risk, Napoli pounced. Cherki the culprit, flicking a ball around the corner aimlessly into the middle of his own half. Billy Gilmour gave thanks, punched towards Rasmus Hojlund and he spun Guehi to flash under Donnarumma ten minutes into the second half.

City had been warned too, when Noa Lang went clean through before checking back upon realising he didn’t possess the acceleration to complete the job.

Maresca’s assistant, Danny Walker, shook his head on the bench as they pored over the goal’s replay. Fernandez was ramming his boot into one of Vanja Milinkovic-Savic’s posts when the goalkeeper nonchalantly plucked a floated cross from the night sky.

City had stirred somewhat, helped by the inclusion of Jeremy Doku – ironically introduced seconds before Hojlund’s opener – and it was the Belgian who made their equaliser six minutes after the hour.

He waited and waited inside Napoli’s box for defenders to commit, finding Ayyoub Bouaddi alone and he wrapped his left foot around the pass and was quickly racing back to halfway.

There had appeared a head of steam, only for O’Reilly to be caught well out of position as Napoli countered – City carved open – and Lang thundered a volley past Donnarumma with 14 minutes left. Cherki made amends for his earlier error by hammering into the far corner.
