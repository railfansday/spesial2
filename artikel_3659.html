Susan Smith's Ex-Husband David Will Oppose Her Parole: She's Not 'Remorseful'

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

More than 30 years after Susan Smith shocked the nation by drowning her two young sons, her ex-husband, David Smith, is preparing to face the parole board to keep her behind bars.

Ahead of Susan’s upcoming parole hearing on Thursday, November 19, David tells Us that he and his wife, Tiffany Smith, plan to attend and urge the board to deny her release.

Susan’s first parole hearing occurred in 2024, and the board unanimously denied her release following emotional pleas from David, Tiffany and their daughter, Savannah.

“I’m not looking forward to speaking again and going through that, but I do plan on being there every time,” David tells Us.

David says he plans to “speak from the heart” when addressing the board, wanting to keep the focus on his late sons, Michael and Alex, who were just 3 years old and 14 months old when they were killed in 1994.

“I’m going to tell them, don’t keep her incarcerated because of what she’s done to me. Keep her incarcerated because of what she did to Michael and Alex,” David says. “Her behavior through the years [in prison] has shown it’s still all about Susan. In my heart of hearts, I don’t think she’s remorseful, and I don’t think she really cares what she did.”

Susan, now 55, was sentenced to life in prison in 1995 after rolling her car into South Carolina’s John D. Long Lake with her boys strapped inside. The case drew nationwide attention, with Susan initially claiming a man carjacked her and took the children. She famously appeared on TV with David by her side, making a heartfelt plea to have the children returned safely before confessing to the crime nine days later.

David tells Us that the last time he spoke directly to his ex-wife was just one month after her confession. “They let me talk to her for about an hour, and even then, she very nonchalantly said, ‘I’m sorry. I don’t know why I did it,’” he shares. “And that was it. I’m not exaggerating or downplaying. That’s the way she put it.”

While incarcerated, Susan has been disciplined for numerous violations ranging from drug offences to sexual relations with prison staff. David’s wife, Tiffany, questions whether Susan should be eligible to appeal for parole at all.

“Sometimes it feels like she’s not being punished enough,” Tiffany tells Us. “When she was sentenced, she was given life. We think the life sentence should mean her whole life. You took two precious little lives, yet you’re going to get another chance at life after 30 years — or 32 now. They didn’t get that chance. It’s very unfair.”

Tiffany also says she is “appalled” at Susan’s lack of accountability over the past three decades.

“When Susan spoke at the last parole hearing, it was all about her, and she had those same crocodile tears that she did in the nine days when she claimed that they were missing,” Tiffany shares. “She’s had 32 years– if she wanted to write a letter to (David) to let him know how sorry she was or anything, [she could have], but no, she’s not even done that.”

David admits that “not a day goes by” without thinking of his sons, and while relieving the crime at these hearings is especially painful, he “made peace” with the tragedy years ago.

“I accepted early on when this happened that I wasn’t gonna be mad at the world or bitter,” he tells Us. “I didn’t blame God for what happened. The world didn’t do this to me. Susan did it.”
