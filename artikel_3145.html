‘Clashing Through The Snow’ Stars Christopher Briney & Michelle Randolph Talk Comedy’s “Important” Presence In Script And How They Built Their Characters

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

In Prime Video’s latest original Christmas romantic comedy, Clashing Through the Snow, Christopher Briney (The Summer I Turned Pretty) and Michell Randolph (Landman) portray rival editors whose relationship takes an enemies to lovers arc during a road trip to New York .

Ryan Mitchell (Briney) and Hannah Hendricks both hear from their boss Jeremy (P.J. Byrne) separately that layoffs loom at Latitude Publishing, where they will compete for a merged version of their roles in the new year. The ambitious employees then both set up secret meetings with prospective authors that could respectively land them the job, but require travel to New York city over the Christmas holiday. The pair exhibit fundamentally different taste in books and what they aim to sell, but their shared love of reading provides them common ground on which to bond when a winter storm forces them together during their grounded travels to the east coast.

“Ryan’s sort of an interesting case because he’s obviously like, ‘How do we market this and how do we make this sellable and profitable?’ But he loves books. There’s a big part of his heart — and they get into it in the movie — that appreciates literature, and really he just wants people to read books,” Briney said of his character, who leans more into numbers and trends. “That’s what it’s about. It’s not really about money. It’s about exposure. At least that was sort of my approach to it. It’s about, ‘How can we get more people to read books?’ which I admire and appreciate in him.” Watch on Deadline

A good visual representation of Ryan lies in a snapshot of his home, where he has framed photos with Hunger Games author Suzanne Collins and Divergent author Veronica Roth. He also mentions his discovering Fourth Wing author Rebecca Yarros two years after it happened. Michelle’s editor Hannah tends to go for the more niche, Awards-bait books that are high brow.

“She’s a literary traditionalist. I remember when I first booked [the role], I was like, ‘I’ve got to read Virginia Woolf,’ because she talks about how that’s who raised her. I love that Hannah’s whole thing is, ‘I just want whatever people are reading to mean something,’ for there to be a real purpose behind it,” Randolph told Deadline. “And I’ve I’ve definitely thought about what books would Hannah love and what authors, like Jane Austen. I think that she would read a lot of the classics. Joan Didion. And it definitely made me want to read more classics.”

In the below interview, the stars also break down their reactions to the script, Briney’s Jim Carrey-like approach to physical comedy, Hannah’s career focus, working with Lukas Gage, Buc-ees knockoff Luc-ees and more:

DEADLINE: Had you both read this script before signing on to the project? What drew you to doing an original Christmas rom com, and especially this one, when rom coms are starting to get rebooted nowadays?

MICHELLE RANDOLPH: I read the script multiple times before I signed on to the project. I actually I have this app called Speechify, and I remember I got the script and I was driving and I was like, ‘I just really want to read it right now. ‘So I listened to it for the first time with Gwyneth Paltrow reading it/speaking it to me. And then once once I got home, I sat down and I actually went through it, a very different experience, but it’s such a joyful, fun script. It’s silly. It’s absurd. I love [that] both the characters have a real emotional core and very specific personalities. I love this script.

CHRISTOPHER BRINEY: It made me laugh, and I think it’s really sweet, but I loved that that comedy was an important character in in this script, and it felt like a fun, challenging project, and it was certainly challenging and certainly fun.

DEADLINE: Chris, I feel like you really leaned into the physical comedy of this one.

BRINEY: I hope it’s funny, dude. I might like look like an idiot.

DEADLINE: No, that’s not what I’m saying! Could you talk more about the challenge of that and how it was fun to explore that as an actor?

BRINEY: Yeah, I mean, it was really fun. I think that the first couple weeks on a project, you’re always finding your footing, and there was some like physicality to Ryan that that I think somewhere in there I had found, and there were some things that Carlson said about…

BRINEY: Right, there were some Jim Carrey moments, and there was like this scene that we shot at Webster Hall when I’m handing the paper through the [glass barricade], and there was just something about how cool he thinks he is, and how like flimsy he really is that really unlocked a lot of that character for me. It was fun. It was really fun. I loved it.

DEADLINE: Michelle, I wanted to ask you too how you know Hannah’s very career focused versus l love life, and I feel like that’s just really relatable for a lot of women right now. I’m curious your thoughts and what you wanted to bring out in her to make her more more than just that?

RANDOLPH: Yeah, I love how ambitious she is and passionate. Books are her life. Her authors — she’s so invested in her career. She’s a literary purist, and I think that passion in someone is so admirable. I also think that’s what makes it really sweet when you can see the moments in the movie when she starts to fall for Ryan because it’s the first time she’s letting herself not take her work so seriously. It’s a protection, throwing yourself into work, it’s like, “Oh well, I’m distracted and I don’t have time for it, so that’s why I’m not with somebody.” I think slowly she realizes, “I can have both.” There’s a balance.

DEADLINE: What was it like filming at Luc-ee’s, which I don’t know if you’ve been to a Buc-ee’s, but how did it compare if you have?

BRINEY: Is it good? I mean, I’m familiar with the idea and how awesome it appears to be.

RANDOLPH: It’s magical. There are so many Bucky’s because I film in Texas most of the year, and there’s a bunch. I always pass them on the way to set, and I went for the first time with my cast, and we all got the pajamas, the onesie. We all got Bucky’s onesies and all the snacks and it’s huge. It’s like Costco where they have gas, they have car washes…

BRINEY: I’m more familiar with like Cumberland Farms and Wawa on roadtrips. I feel like Wawa’s maybe junior Buc-ee’s. Wawa’s great. I support Wawa.

DEADLINE: What was it like working with Lukas Gage in that smaller role?

BRINEY: I could not keep it together. I really couldn’t. I am sure that there’s evidence of that.

RANDOLPH: It’s in the movie, which it’s like Hannah and Ryan are laughing at the absurdity of what Lukas Gage’s character is. But I also know that it was actually Chris and Michelle in that coverage, we broke.

BRINEY: He’s so funny, man. He is so good in this movie.
