Andy Reid’s Tenured Professor Era Sparks Chiefs Fans Debate

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Andy Reid has entered a phase where he acts like a tenured professor who is tired of student questions. The Kansas City Chiefs head coach has made it clear that he does not enjoy answering the same inquiries from the media week after week. This attitude was on full display after the team defeated the New York Jets 35-12 on Sunday. While the win ended a two-game losing streak, the postgame interaction highlighted a growing divide between the coach and the people asking about his decisions.

The coach has plenty of passion for his players and still shows creativity on the field, but his public demeanor has changed. He now tells reporters exactly what he will and will not discuss. This shift has led to frustration among fans who feel he is avoiding accountability for recent struggles. The situation has become a weekly occurrence where Reid cuts off questions before they are fully formed, creating an air of dismissal toward the press corps.

One of the main topics Reid refuses to discuss is the job security of kicker Harrison Butker. The veteran kicker has struggled with long-distance field goals this season. Since 2024, Butker has made only 45 percent of his field goals from 50 yards or more. At a 69.5 percent rate, every other NFL kicker is converting these attempts. Despite these poor numbers, Reid told reporters he would not make any decisions on the spot during the press conference.

Reid opened his remarks by stating that he knew he would be asked about Butker. He said he would address the situation as the week goes on and that there is no need for reporters to ask the question again. He made it clear that he would look at everything before making a move. This response was accepted by the media, as not a single reporter pushed him on the issue during the session.

Following Butker's failure to convert two additional field goals before halftime in the matchup versus the Jets, reluctance to tackle the kicker's performance has emerged. One of those misses was rendered moot because an offside penalty was called on the defense. Reid knew the questions would be coming, yet he chose to shut down the conversation early. This approach suggests he is protecting the player or perhaps avoiding the scrutiny that comes with making a high-profile change.

Another source of irritation for Reid is the constant questioning about left tackle Josh Simmons. A back injury sustained in mid-August caused the Chiefs player to miss the final two preseason games. Reid initially insisted the injury was minor, which became a point of discussion when Simmons missed the first regular-season game. By the time the team entered their bye week in Week 5, Simmons had not played a single snap despite being on the active roster.

Progress stalled further when a bulging disc in his back worsened the situation. He was eventually placed on injury reserve, ensuring he remains sidelined through Week 11 at the minimum. The will-he-or-won't-he nature of the update kept reporters confused and fans frustrated. Reid played coy most of the time, giving indirect and vague answers that only brought about more scrutiny.

Reid clarified his stance on confronting the reality of the injury during one update. He told reporters, according to Arrowhead Addict, "I hope this isn't asked to me every week. I mean, let's just put this to rest. He'll take care of that. This isn't that big of a story." His desire to end the line of questioning shows how much he dislikes the repetitive nature of injury updates. The communication from the team always felt indirect, which only fueled more speculation about the true state of the player's health.

Reid also made a controversial decision to bench Kingsley Suamataia at left guard in Week 8 against the Denver Broncos. He decided to play Mike Caliendo instead of the rookie lineman. Suamataia was benched for the second time, after being substituted in Week 4 against the Las Vegas Raiders. The move signaled a lack of confidence in the young player's ability to protect the quarterback in key situations.

Fans and analysts have pointed to these benching decisions as evidence that the offensive line is not gelling. The inconsistency at the guard position has led to more pressure on the quarterback and fewer running lanes for the ball carriers. Reid's choice to go with Caliendo suggests he believes the veteran can handle the job better than the rookie. However, the move has not stopped the criticism regarding the team's offensive execution.

The benching of Suamataia adds to the list of personnel decisions that have drawn scrutiny. It is part of a pattern where Reid makes changes that confuse the fanbase. The lack of a clear explanation for why the rookie was benched has left many wondering about the criteria for playing time. This uncertainty contributes to the feeling that the team is not operating with a clear plan.

The most notable moment of the press conference came after a failed quarterback sneak during the game. On a third-and-1 in the first quarter, Reid inserted backup quarterback Justin Fields to execute a sneak. The play failed to convert, and Fields fumbled the ball. The Jets recovered the fumble and capitalized with a field goal to take an early lead. This play marked the first quarterback sneak with an actual quarterback for Kansas City since 2019.

Reid blamed the media for the failure of the play. He joked that he felt pressured by reporters to call the sneak after they criticized him for not calling one in the previous game against Denver. This remark was made in a joking tone, but it highlighted his annoyance with the constant media scrutiny.

Reid explained that the play is difficult to perfect because it is hard to practice live. He stated, "Yeah, so listen, we've got to make sure—those are hard to practice live." He added that the quarterback sneak is hard to practice unless you do it in a game. He emphasized the need to get the two players on the same page and ensure the ball gets up first in the quarterback's hands. Despite the explanation, the failed play remains a coaching error in the eyes of many.

Fans have expressed strong frustration with Reid's handling of the situation. A fan commented that a scapegoat always exists to sidestep the actual issue, which they identify as Andy Reid. The fan noted that last year it was Matt Nagy, last week it was Mike Caliendo, and this week it will be Harrison Butker. This pattern of shifting blame has angered supporters who feel the coach is not taking responsibility for the team's struggles.

Reid's reaction to the botched play drew criticism from another fan who labeled it entirely unserious behavior. They wrote that Reid gave the team the most ill-prepared quarterback sneak ever and then told them to shut up about it. The fan argued that a head coach needs to take short-yardage futility much more seriously. This sentiment reflects a growing belief that the coach is not engaged in the details of the game.

One commenter stated bluntly that the average fan fails to grasp just how terrible Andy Reid is at his job. They called the situation pathetic. A different user contended that the failed sneak indicated a coaching issue rather than a reason to abandon the play entirely. They believe the team managed to turn a simple quarterback sneak into a turnover, which is a coaching failure. These comments show a deep divide between the coach and the fanbase.

Observers have likened Reid's present condition to Bill Belichick's concluding season in New England. OutKick's Bobby Burack wrote that Reid looks more confused than ever. He characterized Reid as an all-time great coach appearing utterly baffled by the current iteration of the NFL. This comparison draws parallels to Belichick's difficult 2023 season, where the Patriots finished 4-13.

Belichick's final season was a disaster, with the team ranking bottom of the NFL offensively and averaging just 13.9 points per game. Elimination from playoff contention happened with four weeks left. signifying their earliest removal since Belichick's first season. A 24-year streak featuring six Super Bowl titles concluded due to these struggles. Critics worry that Reid is heading down a similar path of decline.

The comparison is not just about wins and losses but about the decision-making process. Reid's reluctance to call quarterback sneaks and his vague injury updates mirror the confusion seen in Belichick's final year. The team has shown flashes of dominance, but the overall execution has been inconsistent. This uncertainty has led to questions about whether Reid can adapt to the modern game.

The team also left Sunday's win with significant injury concerns. A hamstring injury struck wide receiver Xavier Worthy during the game. Patrick Mahomes absorbed a hit to his right throwing elbow during a second-quarter strip-sack. Mahomes returned before halftime and finished the game, but he admitted he wasn't throwing the ball the way he wanted to early in the second half.

Worthy has struggled to become the reliable explosive option the team envisioned. Rashee Rice lost time to a hamstring injury, while Tyquan Thornton endured a significant ankle injury. Reid and Mahomes find it harder to maintain offensive momentum without a consistent downfield threat. When the running game fails to generate ground production, the receiving corps' lack of dependable output becomes a critical problem.

General manager Brett Veach admitted prior to the trade deadline that the Chiefs were investigating the receiver market.
