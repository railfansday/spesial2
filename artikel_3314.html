'The Gilded Age' Season 4 Takes Its Darkest Turn With This Fan-Favorite Character

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Editor's note: The below contains spoilers for The Gilded Age Season 4 Episode 4.

The Gilded Age has covered some dark storylines, from George Russell's (Morgan Spector) shooting and subsequent revenge mission to unexpected character deaths like John Adams (Claybourne Elder) and Luke Forte (Robert Sean Leonard). Even though the series usually deals in relatively petty social drama, these moments add a more serious tone, and the show's latest reveal pushes the story into even darker territory. Fans have been noticing George's erratic behavior in Season 4, and this week's episode finally explains that he is struggling with an addiction to laudanum. Though The Gilded Age has foreshadowed this, the storyline is a concerning shift for a beloved character.

Following the bullet wound George sustained in Season 3, it's not shocking that he would need strong pain medication, but his continued reliance on it is concerning — especially since Season 4 picks up months later, after his injury has healed. This not only explains George's strange behavior, but also sets up a dangerous storyline for the rest of the season. His addiction to laudanum has affected the state of his business — George can no longer get through a meeting without storming out — and puts his health at serious risk.

George Russell has been a fixture of the story since The Gilded Age's very first episode, known for his powerful position in society and clever plotting, but Season 4 has shown a very different side of him. He has been quick to lash out — most often at his son, Larry (Harry Richardson), who is trying to help — and his profuse sweating has raised suspicion. Bertha (Carrie Coon) even calls in a doctor, who warns that the medication prescribed for his bullet wound could be addictive. Despite George's denials, Season 4's latest episode reveals that the problem goes beyond that; he has been sneaking sips from the bottle and even tries to cut himself off by throwing it into the fire. Yet the attempt ultimately fails, and, paired with the doctor's warning, fans should be concerned.

The series stars Carrie Coon and Morgan Spector.

Addiction isn't a subject fans would necessarily expect The Gilded Age to tackle, but it is historically grounded. George's issue is specifically with laudanum, which contains both opium and alcohol and was often used to treat pain during this era but was also highly addictive. The U.S. didn't regulate such substances until the early 20th century, several years after The Gilded Age takes place, meaning that George could easily get his hands on the substance. George's addiction isn't unusual for the time, so it makes sense for his story to take this direction.

It's easy to classify The Gilded Age as a feel-good show, with its romance storylines and focus on societal conflicts, but the series has never hesitated to explore darker elements. When it comes to George, in particular, his life has been at risk before, and his laudanum addiction presents another such danger. Though he's successfully hidden it from his family for now, more people are growing suspicious. Beyond Bertha and Larry, Oscar (Blake Ritson) has shared that rumors about George being a drunk have begun circulating, and his confrontation with John Rockefeller (Neal Huff) will only fuel those rumors. Luckily, Larry, who finds George passed out at his desk with the bottle nearby, may have just uncovered the truth if his father accepts his help.

George's addiction is already affecting his business; he has lashed out at colleagues in meetings and failed to negotiate an agreement that will save his company. Additionally, his state could now impact the Presidential dinner Bertha is hosting, putting the Russells in the public eye when George is struggling — not to mention the risk of overdosing. George's addiction gives The Gilded Age plenty to explore in future episodes, but something needs to change before he is too far gone.
