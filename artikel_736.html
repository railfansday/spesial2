The War on Drugs Wake Up a 97-Year-Old New Jersey Theater With Spellbinding, Two-Hour Set Featuring New Songs

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

How do you wake up a 97-year-old theater? A spellbinding two-hour set from the War on Drugs.

The Philadelphia rock band headlined the Loew’s Jersey Theatre on Saturday night, welcoming music fans back into the historic venue after a five-year, $130 million renovation. The grand reopening featured two sold-out concerts; the Trey Anastasio Band played on Friday.

Fans swarmed into the theater on a chilly night, marveling at its ornate ceilings and the fun, archaic features that survived the remodel — telephone booths sans telephones, a large room with a fireplace that leads into a restroom. In the auditorium, orange light bathed the Baroque vault, and red curtains hung overhead. One of the best aspects of the venue: The floor is tiered and slightly sloped, making it easier to find a vantage in the general admission area. After the lights dimmed, the crowd broke into eager woos and applause. And the band simmered into “Harmonia’s Dream,” a slow build that crests into a final refrain — “You’re on your own!” — before erupting into a guitar solo, as all the best War on Drugs songs do.

Decisive piano chords ushered in “Who’s That,” the band’s first single in five years. The audience had clearly been savoring the new song, singing along with frontman Adam Granduciel, “It’s only my depression, baby!,” a hook that’s also printed on fresh merchandise.

“Red Eyes” was, of course, a highlight of the setlist, its euphoric rush of guitar bliss sending the crowd into a tizzy. And the climactic, 10-minute “Under the Pressure” received an applause break as saxophonist Jon Natchez fluttered around cresting synths and guitars.

Granduciel isn’t a big talker during his sets, but he did take a moment to wish his mother a happy birthday. He explained that she was meant to fly down for the show but couldn’t because of the FAA shutdown. As the crowd emitted a sympathetic “aww,” Granduciel said, cheerfully, “So she drove down instead!”

While the new single is surely an indication, a new War on Drugs album has yet to be officially announced. But that didn’t stop the band from giving fans a taste of music on the way. With zero introduction or explanation, Granduciel led the group in two unreleased songs: the power ballad “Walk Away” and the dreamy, piano-heavy “Now We’re Talking.”

No song was more exciting than “An Ocean in Between the Waves,” its machine-like drumbeat a canvas for Granduciel to paint — his guitar a roaring, distorted beast. The song title is fitting; so much of a War on Drugs concert feels like the sea. You’re drifting, lulled by currents that swell so slowly you barely notice, the band moving in tidal unison. Every so often, you spot it on the horizon: a joyous, intoxicating surge. Before you know it, you’ve been swept up and carried to shore.
