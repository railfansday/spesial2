Connected Nation podcast spotlights two urgent issues shaping America's digital future

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Bonus episodes recorded at the Broadband Nation Expo examine the threat to E-Rate and how Google is helping set the first quality standard for data centers

NEW ORLEANS, Nov. 20, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- A federal program that connects more than 100,000 schools and libraries is in jeopardy. The data centers that power the internet are changing faster than the rules can keep up.

Two new bonus episodes of the Connected Nation podcast take on both issues. Connected Nation (CN), a national nonprofit, interviewed leadership at SHLB and Google during the Broadband Nation Expo taking place in New Orleans Nov. 18-20.

Ep. 57: The program that keeps schools and libraries online is in danger

The federal E-Rate helps connect more than 100,000 K-12 schools and libraries across the U.S. Now its future is uncertain.

Joseph Wender, Executive Director of the Schools, Health & Libraries Broadband (SHLB) Coalition, explained the Fifth Circuit ruling that puts the $8 billion-a-year program at risk. The court found that USAC, which runs the Universal Service Fund, was improperly set up.

Wender also covered the FCC's proposal asking whether E-Rate should end, and what schools and libraries are telling him.

"They're saying E-Rate cannot go away. It just cannot," Wender said of the schools and libraries SHLB represents. "That if it goes away, it will blow a hole in the budgets of schools and libraries."

Listeners who want to help protect the program can visit saveourerate.com.

Ep. 58: We asked Google about data centers, and a Google exec answered

Data centers are evolving almost faster than the rulebook can be written. Google is helping write it.

Gino Tozzi, who leads data center quality and commissioning at Google, explained DCE 9000, the first quality standard for data center infrastructure. Google and the Telecommunications Industry Association (TIA) are building it with more than 90 companies, and it's expected in early 2027 (Q1).

Tozzi also explained why some suppliers face as many as 50 audits a year, and what really goes into building a data center.

"We need to go at hyper speed because we need it fast," Tozzi said of developing the standard. "We couldn't afford to wait three or five years."

"These conversations get at what it takes to get and keep America connected," said Jessica Denson, Communications Director, CN, and host of the podcast. "One episode is about protecting the program that keeps students online. The other is focused on the infrastructure behind almost everything we do on the internet.
