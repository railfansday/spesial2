Nick Sirianni denies flipping the bird; looking for answers in bye week

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Nick Sirianni denies flipping the bird; looking for answers in bye week originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

Nick Sirianni on Monday afternoon was adamant that he was "absolutely not" directing a middle finger toward fans in a video that surfaced after Sunday's loss to the Giants at the Linc.

Sirianni said he learned about the clip, which has made its rounds on social media, from Eagles senior VP of communications Bob Lange before his virtual news conference on Monday. The gesture in the video is ambiguous.

"That was a surprise to me," Sirianni said. "But absolutely not."

If Sirianni did give the middle finger, it would have been just about the only offensive thing from the Eagles' 19-11 loss to the Giants.

Sirianni says he wasn't flipping the bird, but he is flipping the page after a horrid first half of the season. The Eagles are 2-7 at their Week 10 bye week after losing seven straight. And even though this season looks lost, it's up to Sirianni to try to get it back on track.

That process has already started inside the Jefferson Health Training Complex with the Eagles' self-scouting process. The Eagles go through this process at every bye week, but there are clearly more things to fix this season. The Eagles rank 30th in points and 32nd in yards in the NFL.

"This week gives us a great opportunity to spend extra time," Sirianni said. "You're always working self-scout, you're always trying to figure out what you do well, what you don't do well. This week just gives us more opportunities for that. We'll use it very similar to how we've used it in the past.

"Obviously, we have new pieces here that have not been here so we'll take the good things that we've used in the past here and the good things that our other coaches have used in the past and we'll come up with our best path. I think that's important that we get going there. And that's what we're working on now after we reviewed the game."

Sirianni explained that the workload in the self-scouting process is divvied up among the coaching staff. Different coaches are responsible for reviewing different areas and putting together summaries of their findings. Those findings lead to more areas to study over the rest of the week.

The big question hovering over the team is whether or not Sirianni will make any staffing changes before the Eagles get back on the field in Week 11 against the Steelers in a game that was already flexed from the 4:25 p.m. slot to 1 p.m.

Sirianni was asked on Monday if he's making any staff changes.

"No, right now we're just going through … we went through the game this morning," Sirianni said. "Right now, like I said earlier, we're going through our self-scout process. Guys are working on studies right now and we're determined to figure things out and get on the right track and that's what we're worried right at this particular time."
