Which Bank ETF Is the Better Buy: State Street’s Regional Banking KRE or First Trust’s FTXO?

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

The cost gap is wide, but the bigger difference lies in which banks each fund holds and how far each has fallen during market stress.

The State Street SPDR S&P Regional Banking ETF (KRE +0.30%) offers a cheaper entry point and broader regional focus compared to the more concentrated, mega-cap-heavy First Trust Nasdaq Bank ETF (FTXO +0.48%).

Investors looking for exposure to the U.S. financial sector often pivot between two distinct strategies: owning the giants or owning the locals. While both funds provide direct access to the banking industry, they differ significantly in their cost structures and the size of the institutions they hold. This comparison breaks down the trade-offs between these two banking benchmarks.

The SPDR fund is more affordable with a 0.35% expense ratio compared to 0.6% for the First Trust fund. Additionally, income-focused investors may prefer the higher payout from the regional banking portfolio.

State Street SPDR S&P Regional Banking ETF focuses on the financial services sector, specifically targeting the regional banking industry. It uses a representative sampling technique to track a diversified basket of 167 holdings that includes both growth and value stocks across various market capitalizations. Its largest positions include United Community Banks (UCB +0.13%) at 1.19%, Glacier Bancorp (GBCI -0.06%) at 1.19%, and Eastern Bankshares (EBC +0.53%) at 1.18%. The fund was launched in 2006. State Street SPDR S&P Regional Banking ETF has paid $1.61 per share over the trailing 12 months, which on its recent ~$73.09 share price works out to a 2.2% yield.

First Trust Nasdaq Bank ETF also weights the financial services sector but selects its components based on liquidity and fundamental factors. It maintains a much more concentrated portfolio of 50 holdings, aiming for high correlation with the Nasdaq US Smart Banks Index. Its largest positions include Wells Fargo & Company (WFC +0.34%) at 8.07%, U.S. Bancorp (USB +0.86%) at 8.02%, and JPMorgan Chase & Co. (JPM +0.98%) at 7.81%. The fund was launched in 2016. First Trust Nasdaq Bank ETF has paid $0.77 per share over the trailing 12 months, which on its recent ~$40.20 share price works out to a 1.9% yield.

I'd go with the State Street SPDR S&P Regional Banking ETF (KRE), because it gives you a wide mix of regional banks at a lower price than First Trust Nasdaq Bank ETF (FTXO). Regional banks earn much of their income the old-fashioned way, by lending out local deposits to businesses and families, so KRE's results track how local communities are doing. That has been working lately, with several big regional banks reporting stronger results in October, a sign the sector is on firmer footing.

FTXO holds just 50 stocks and leans on giants like Wells Fargo, so it's a bet on the biggest names on Wall Street rather than the regional lenders. That concentration means a stumble at one or two of its mega banks could weigh heavily on the fund.

Regional banks can be bumpier, since many have loans tied to commercial real estate, but owning well over 100 of them means no single bank can sink the fund. For most long-term investors, KRE is the cheaper and broader way to own banks. FTXO is better for investors who believe the largest banks will keep outperforming smaller ones and are willing to put a concentrated bet on that view.
