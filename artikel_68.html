Australia sanctions individuals and company involved in illegal West Bank settlements

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Foreign affairs minister Penny Wong says rapid expansion of settlements and settler violence are ‘extinguishing the possibility of a two-state solution’

Australia has announced another tranche of sanctions on those involved in illegal settlement expansion and human rights abuses against Palestinians in the West Bank.

The sanctions prohibit Australians and Australian businesses from trading with or providing financial or other support to three individuals and four organisations.

In a statement, the foreign affairs minister, Penny Wong, said it was the first time Australian sanctions had targeted a construction company involved in the building of settlements. The statement did not name the company.

“We are taking these actions because the rapid expansion of settlements and settler violence are extinguishing the possibility of a two-state solution,” Wong’s statement said.

“This includes the E1 settlement where demolitions and the displacement of Palestinians have occurred in recent weeks. Israeli settlements in the West Bank are illegal under international law.”

Wong said the sanctioned individuals and organisations had “actively participated in the planning, coordination and establishment of new settlements, including raising funds, recruitment of volunteers and providing support to violent settlers”.

Speaking on ABC radio on Wednesday morning, Wong said Australia had been coordinating with the UK on the sanctions.

“We thought it was very important to work with the United Kingdom to have a coordinated set of sanctions ahead of those decisions.

“We urge Israel to desist from proceeding with this settlement, which renders likely unviable the prospect of a Palestinian state.”

Wong said she had spoken to her Israeli counterpart, Gideon Sa’ar, on Tuesday night when she “outlined” Australia’s position.

“We’ve made clear we will continue to take action in respect of those involved in the settlement enterprise. The settlement enterprise is violent.”

When asked whether Wong was concerned Israel would respond to Australia sanctions with countermeasures, the minister said it had in the past.

“We’ve seen the Israeli government take action against Australia, including asking our diplomats to leave Ramallah. We’ve also seen the caretaker prime minister, Netanyahu, be very public in his criticism of Australia.

“So obviously, there is a reaction to Australia acting, but we think this is the right thing to do.”

The statement warned Australian businesses they must not bid for construction tenders for illegal settlement developments.

Individuals breaching sanctions can attract a penalty of up to 10 years’ imprisonment and a fine of up to $910,000 or three times the value of the relevant transaction.

Businesses can be fined up to $3.64m or three times the value of the relevant transaction.
