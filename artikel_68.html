Germany news: Volkswagen begins wage talks with IG Metall union

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Germany's largest labor union IG Metall and automaker Volkswagen are holding wage negotiations, as the company looks to embark on cost-cutting measures. Meanwhile, Germany's president is in Ghana. DW has more.

Read below for a roundup of headlines from and about Germany on Tuesday, November 24:

A new survey has revealed a growing sense of pessimism among Americans and Germans when it comes to the state of relations between the two countries.

The survey conducted by Pew Research Center and the Körber Stiftung found a cooling of attitudes, with one-third of Americans saying the US-Germany relationship "is bad."

Some 62% of Americans said the relationship was good, down from 71% in 2025 and 84% in 2024.

Meanwhile, Germans "remain deeply skeptical of the countries' current relations: 21% say they are good, and 77% say they are bad," the Pew Research Center said, adding that sentiments were similar to what they were early in the first Trump administration.

The Germany survey found a general trend of Germans being "down on the West"

Among the findings, 61% of Germans felt they could not rely on the US "nuclear umbrella" to provide protection in the event war. Nearly 70% said they wanted a French or British nuclear protection arrangement.

About two-thirds of those surveyed opposed Germany taking on a military leadership role, while 35% were in favor.

Around 60% of Germans said Western unity — or "the concept of political and cultural linkage between the US and Europe — no longer exists."

The US findings came from a survey involving 1,014 adults conducted between September 18-24, 2026. The German findings were from a survey involving 1,501 eligible German voters between September 15-28, 2026.

German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier is in Ghana's capital, Accra, on Tuesday, on the second stop of his three-nation tour of African states.

Steinmeier is due to get the sixth German-African Business Summit started in Ghana along with President John Mahama.

Representatives of companies and business associations from Germany and Africa will be attending.

The previous summit took place in Kenya two years ago.

Steinmeier is hoping to encourage German companies to become more involved in African markets that could help patch up declining business in traditional markets such as the United States and China.

According to figures from Germany's Federal Statistical Office, German foreign trade with Africa grew by 10.5% in 2025, reaching a record value of €63.4 billion ($68.3 billion).

Steinmeier was in Ivory Coast on Monday, and after Ghana he is scheduled to travel to Kenya for the last leg of his trip.

Germany's largest labor union IG Metall and the country's largest automaker Volkswagen (VW) are embarking on a first round of talks in the northern city of Emden amid protests and threats to strike.

Volkswagen has said it wants to place German workers' ‌pay in ​2027 and 2028 on ice, while also cutting a range of benefits. It is also looking to tinker with the union's 35-hour week, asking staff to work longer on the same pay when needed.

VW is facing increasing competition from China and has told its German workforce that domestic production costs have become too expensive.

"The VW board is stabbing its employees in the back. Volkswagen is facing a critical test, the extent of which the employers apparently still don't grasp," said chief negotiator Thorsten Gröger, IG Metall district manager for Lower Saxony, last week.

IG Metall has warned that strikes could begin from January 1, with the union saying that VW's restructuring push puts it in violation of a 2024 deal which included 35,000 layoffs in return for employment and investment guarantees.
