Did OKC Thunder make a mistake trading away Aaron Wiggins?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

In what has been their most frustrating 2026-27 regular-season loss to date, the Oklahoma City Thunder mustered only 10 measly bench points against the LA Clippers this past Friday. Juxtapose that to what Aaron Wiggins has done on your average night, he'd eclipse that number pretty easily by himself.

One month into the 2026-27 NBA regular season, is it fair to question if the Thunder made a mistake trading away Wiggins this past summer?

The yes-or-no pontification requires nuance. It'd be unfair to judge the Thunder on the end result, but there's no doubt the decision-making process looks a little more wobbly now than it did back in June when the trade first happened.

Facing financial constraints, the Thunder had to move on from some of their players this past summer. It was infeasible to run it back with the same championship-winning roster for another year. As Wiggins played his way out of the rotation with a career-worst year, he made the decision pretty easy to make once the summer approached.

Let's rewind to his final season in OKC — Wiggins averaged career lows across the board. He rim-finishing dipped. His outside jumper was icy-cold. The Thunder gave him chance after chance to turn things around in the regular season, but to no avail. By the end of the 2026 NBA playoffs, he was outside of their rotation.

With the Thunder doing everything in their power to avoid the NBA's pseudo-hard cap of the second apron and other tax penalties, they opted to go for younger pieces waiting in the wings. Wiggins was traded to the Atlanta Hawks for two future second-round picks. But considering how a couple of those developmental projects have panned out, was it a sound decision? Even back then before OKC embarked on its current apocalypse?

Maybe not. Sure, the Thunder had to move on from a couple of players. But it didn't necessarily have to be Wiggins. In fact, I'll give credit to NBA expert Nate Duncan for brainstorming this unorthodox idea. They could've actually shed more money if they had moved on from Nikola Topic and Thomas Sorber instead.

For context, Wiggins has a $9 million 2026-27 salary. In comparison, Topic and Sorber have a combined $10.3 million 2026-27 salary. In fact, you can argue that the former is one of the best value deals among role players. The 27-year-old has three-years, $25.4 million remaining on his current contract. The cherry on top? It's a descending salary.

"I question OKC essentially prioritizing Kenrich Williams at $5m plus 2 of the 4 of Topic, Sorber, Stirtz, and Mara over Dort," Duncan posted on social media on July 19. "He's a little overrated and keeping him after this year would have been hard, but don't make your team worse when you're a top 2-3 contender."

At the time, Thunder fans absolutely grilled Duncan. How can you expect the Thunder to move on from two recent first-round picks so soon? Fast forward to five months later, his radical suggestion doesn't seem half bad. Both Topic and Sorber have had all-time bad luck in the health department. Time is running out for them to prove their worth as NBA talent.

Heck, the Thunder have already cut ties with Topic. They declined his 2027-28 team option. Even if he hits his best-case scenario and balls out in this current stretch, he'll likely sign a deal elsewhere this upcoming summer. And for Sorber, he hit another big-time hurdle. He underwent his third knee surgery since he initially sustained a torn ACL in Sept. 2025. His first two years are basically a wash. And even though OKC has picked up his 2027-28 team option, I just don't know if you can reasonably project him to be a sure-fire role player with this group. He hasn't played a game at any level in almost two years — two years! That's an eternity in a basketball lifespan.

Obviously, the Thunder don't have a time machine. They couldn't have foreseen both Topic and Sorber going through more setbacks. That said, considering their checkered health past, could you have honestly entered the year expecting big things out of them? I just don't think so.

Which makes you wonder — why didn't the Thunder go with the safe bet that Wiggins will be better this season than Topic and Sorber? He's in his prime. You're in a championship right now. Even with his down season last year, we've seen proof of concept of him helping an NBA champion as a bench scorer.

Heck, even Alex Caruso isn't surprised that Wiggins is thriving on the Hawks. He believes he was one of the biggest victims in OKC's previously-rich depth. You're seeing that theory gain some accuracy — albeit in the weaker conference — as he's proudly taken on the Hawks' Sixth Man role.

Wiggins is averaging a career-best 13.9 points on 45.7% shooting, 3.6 rebounds and 2.5 assists. He's shot 39.2% from 3 on 6.9 attempts. That would be a godsend for the Post-D.C.C. (Dub, Chet, Caso) Thunder right now — who sport a 17th-ranked 113.4 offensive rating right now.

"I think Wiggs obviously gets a lot more opportunity there than he did with the teams that we had here. Just based off how our roster was constructed. With how many players we've had," Caruso said. "So, it's nice to see him get out there and maybe get to show people how good of a player he is consistently. When maybe that got overshadowed a little by how good of a team we were here."

Obviously, the Thunder's rash of season-altering injuries has made Wiggins' ascension a little more painful to see. Jalen Williams, Chet Holmgren and Cason Wallace are out for the foreseeable future — with the latter two possibly done for the whole year. Considering those circumstances, Wiggins would've been boosted to be one of OKC's top scorers in this what-if hypothetical.

But even if everything was fine in OKC, Wiggins' play makes you question why the Thunder didn't go alternative routes to save money. Even if it meant making unprecedented like moving on from two recent top-15 picks after barely logging NBA action.

The result has been all-time bad for the Thunder, frankly. Can you hold that against them? No, not really. But can you hold the process against them? Maybe. Topic and Sorber have been swing-and-misses. And barring a basketball miracle, they will likely not work out in OKC. With Wiggins, you could've safely banked on him being a real rotation piece. He did so in his first five seasons with you.

As the Thunder try to salvage their 2026-27 season, Wiggins' highly-anticipated return and career revival have put OKC's decision to move on from him in the limelight. A bird in the hand is worth two in the bush.
