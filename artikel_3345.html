‘Yellowstone’ Meets ‘Desperate Housewives’ in Netflix’s Ambitious 2-Season Thriller

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

Of all the things Sophie O’Neil (Brittany Snow) could have packed for her first night out in East Texas, the one that might have spared her a great deal of trouble was a sanitary pad. When a stranger asks her for one in the bathroom at an NRA fundraiser, Sophie offers a tampon, the stranger declines, and the two discover something they can agree on in Sophie’s purse. By the time they’ve each taken a Xanax, Sophie has made her first friend in Maple Brook. She’s also about to discover that the woman who just got half undressed in front of her is the wife of her husband’s boss.

There are any number of places The Hunting Wives could have introduced Margo Banks (Malin Åkerman), including the stage where she joins her oil tycoon husband, Jed (Dermot Mulroney), as he floats the idea of running for governor. Having Sophie meet her in the bathroom first is considerably more fun. Everybody else at the party sees a prospective first lady of Texas. Sophie gets to spend the rest of the evening looking at that woman and thinking about what the two of them just did. Anybody who spent eight seasons of Desperate Housewives waiting for company to arrive at Bree Van de Kamp’s house at the worst possible moment should feel right at home. Adapted by Rebecca Cutter from May Cobb’s novel, The Hunting Wives has an eight-episode first season on Netflix and a second arriving on Thanksgiving Day, November 26. That gives you a perfectly respectable excuse to spend the long weekend with the Banks family, provided you’re prepared to explain what’s happening on the television when a relative wanders in.

John Dutton would understand Jed Banks just fine. One man made his name in Montana cattle country and the other made his in Texas oil, but both have spent enough time being the most important man in the room to figure they might as well be governor. What gives The Hunting Wives its own angle on familiar Yellowstone territory is where it sends Sophie once Jed finishes talking. Margo wants to know what she thought of his speech. Imagine being new in town, having exactly one professional connection through your husband, and discovering that his boss’s wife would like a little constructive criticism.

A look around Margo’s friend group explains how much is riding on her liking the answer. Callie (Jaime Ray Newman) is married to the sheriff. Jill (Katie Lowes) is the pastor’s wife, and her son Brad (George Ferrier) is the local basketball star. Between them, these women have Sunday morning, Friday night, and any trouble you might get into in between pretty well covered. Most jealous friends have to settle for scrolling back to the beginning of your Instagram. When Callie catches on to Margo’s interest in Sophie, she has the resources of the sheriff’s office at her disposal.

Jill, meanwhile, can be perfectly sweet to Brad’s girlfriend, Abby (Madison Wolfe), while making it clear she thinks her son could do better. Lowes gets at something wonderfully aggravating about a mother who has promoted herself to the third person in a teenage relationship. You can imagine how Jill would describe the encounter to Brad afterward, and how ridiculous Abby would sound trying to explain what his mother did wrong. That’s where the Wisteria Lane resemblance runs deepest. Half the pleasure of watching these women misbehave comes from knowing whose side the neighbors would take if anybody complained.

Snow is funny in the early going because Sophie seems to share the audience’s judgment about everything happening around her, right up until Margo asks if she’d like to come along. There’s a lovely bit of cowardice in how long Sophie can pass off her interest as good manners. Going to the fundraiser was Graham’s idea, after all. Dinner with the Bankses could conceivably fall under the heading of supporting her husband’s career. Buying a pistol and leaving it at Margo’s place because Graham wouldn’t approve takes a little more explaining.

Åkerman makes Margo’s attention feel as good as Sophie thinks it does, even when we have an excellent view of the woman who was getting that attention yesterday. Early on, Margo offers Sophie a ride home from a bar, leaving a visibly disappointed Callie behind. You could spend the whole ride anticipating what might happen between the two women in the car, or you could spend it dying to get back to the bar to hear what Callie has to say. Even the woman who didn’t get invited along has a scene you want to see.

There is, of course, a murder to solve. The Hunting Wives opens with a girl running for her life before taking us back three weeks, and what makes those eight episodes go down so easily is how often you can forget about the shooting altogether. Jed and Margo have Sophie and Graham over for dinner, the hosts have rather different ideas about what’s on offer than their guests do, and suddenly the most pressing question in East Texas is how on earth these people are going to face each other at work on Monday.
