The 13 Best Christmas Movies of the Last 5 Years, Ranked

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Christmas and the holiday season have proven to be a time-honored tradition and a great setting for several beloved and cheerful films that perfectly use the festivities to create timeless classics. It's become a staple of many households to put on some of the most iconic and beloved holiday films for the season, with a great number of highly memorable Christmas films to choose from. However, while it always works to put on a classic like Home Alone, National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation, and Elf, there are still a great number of holiday films being created during the modern era.

While it may seem like a Herculean task to create a modern Christmas film that can live up to the undeniable stature of the many classics, a few successfully create similar cozy feelings perfect for the season. While no Christmas film can attain the legacy and strengths of its predecessors, the best and most iconic Christmas films of the modern era act as a great addition to the zeitgeist of the holiday season on film.

Animated family Christmas films have been a long-lasting trend in holiday filmmaking for decades, with a wide array of different filmmakers utilizing the medium to bring to life feel-good stories of holiday cheer and whimsy. While That Christmas doesn't break the mold or stray too far from the traditional values of animated Christmas films past, its inherent earnestness, combined with an array of lovable vocal performances, makes it one of the most underrated animated holiday films of recent memory.

The film follows an array of different storylines that intertwine with one another, almost acting like an animated equivalent to Love Actually, another film written by acclaimed screenwriter Richard Curtis. Animation proves to work with this style of storytelling tremendously, especially when the connecting link that brings all of these stories together is a disastrous snowstorm that vastly alters everyone's individual holiday plans. While it may not be as iconic as Curtis's other holiday films, it certainly has the potential to be a hidden holiday gem of the modern era.

A goofy, musical comedy adaptation of the holiday classic A Christmas Carol, Spirited provides just the right amount of comic absurdism and charm to make for a compelling, modern rendition of the Dickens classic. The inherent chemistry and dynamic between Ryan Reynolds as a Scrooge-styled media consultant and Will Ferrell as the Ghost of Christmas Present is enough to make for a joyously fun comedic time. The musical aspects of the film also work surprisingly well, with the entire supporting cast able to bring the holiday spirit required to make its musical numbers so cheery and infectious.

While the film has been largely overlooked and overshadowed due to being an Apple TV original, it has slowly amassed a reputation as one of the more entertaining and approachable Christmas Carol adaptations of the modern era. Its inherent concept of the Scrooge-archetype character flipping the script of A Christmas Carol back onto the ghosts immediately distinguishes the film from other adaptations, giving it a niche that will help it considerably as time goes on.

One of many prominent comedies from director Michael Showalter, Oh. What. Fun. sets itself up as a Christmas movie playing to the appeals and struggles of mothers, specifically mothers who are not thanked or appreciated for the work they put into the family. Michelle Pfeiffer in the leading role plays this perfectly, as she plays a family matriarch who is fed up with her family after being left home alone, deciding to set off on her own adventure and leaving her family scrambling to find her.

While Pfeiffer herself is the major draw and strength of the film, it's the comedic chemistry and dynamic of the entire supporting cast that makes Oh. What. Fun. such an engaging modern Christmas film. The likes of Denis Leary, Felicity Jones, Dominic Sessa, Jason Schwarzman, and Chloë Grace Moretz each add their own signature charms and strengths to make this one of the most memorable Christmas movies of the streaming era.

One of several successful parody slashers from writer Michael Kennedy, best known for his work writing Freaky and Heart Eyes, It's a Wonderful Knife brings the energy and scares of a horror slasher to the base premise of the all-time Christmas classic It's a Wonderful Life. The film sees a young teen (Jane Widdop) still reeling from the pain and trauma of having saved her town from a psychotic killer on Christmas Eve. However, when she wishes that she were never born, she ends up transported to a parallel world where the killings never stopped and have gone on non-stop for a full year.

While a lot of the film hinges on the ingenuity and intrigue of its wild parody premise, there are a lot of inherent strengths that help give the film its own identity beyond simply being a cheesy parody. Most notably is the hilariously over-the-top villain performance from Justin Long, who, complete with a goofy high-pitched Southern accent, steals the show. The film also doesn't shy away from brazen and openly LGBTQ+ themes and characters, being much more accepting than the average slasher or Christmas film.

Even just describing the plot of the film simply doesn't do justice to Hot Frosty's chaotic and absurdist nature, as it's the type of wild, nonsensical experience that simply needs to be seen to be believed. The film follows a young widow living painfully alone during the holiday season before unexpectedly bringing an attractive, male model snowman to life with a magic scarf. She finds herself doing her best not just to make sure the snowman doesn't get into trouble, but also keeping her feelings in check as he slowly but surely begins to warm her heart.

Taking the mythos and fantasy concepts of the holiday season and creating a massive blockbuster experience with a $250 million budget out of it, Red One is arguably the first film that comes to mind for general audiences when considering memorable holiday films of the past few years. This Dwayne Johnson and Chris Evans-led blockbuster made waves by initially releasing in theaters in early November before having a massive streaming release right in time for the holiday season.

It certainly does a good job of replicating the feel and energy of a modern blockbuster, seamlessly implementing classic Christmas elements and transforming them for modern, digital-technology times. The action comedy strives for mass appeal above all else, playing to the strengths of its leading stars and certainly having fun in its execution as they deal with the goofiness of Christmas magic on a massive blockbuster scale.

Not for the faint of heart, even for die-hard horror fans, Terrifier 3 brings the same level of shockingly gruesome gore and violence that the previous films provided, yet with the added texture and energy of the holiday season. The film fully commits to the iconography and energy of the holiday season, whether it be through Art dressing up as Santa for a large chunk of his kills or using holiday-themed weaponry to create maximum carnage. It builds upon the chaos and energy of previous entries to make for a gruesomely good time, although it's definitely the type of Christmas film not meant to be watched by the whole family.

Terrifier 3 is easily at its best when it allows Art to play into the madness and absurdity of holiday slashers as a whole, even when many of his antics are absolutely sickening and abhorrent to watch unfold. While many simply won't be able to handle the overwhelming gore and sadistic pain that the film presents, it's a shockingly good time for those who are accustomed to this style of gruesome slasher. As far as modern holiday horror films are concerned, Terrifier 3 easily stands above the rest as the most entertaining and sickening of them all.

Four Netflix characters, four completely different ways of showing up for somebody. This is the one who fits how you actually do friendship, and what having them in your life would be like.

You do not do friendship at half volume, and neither does he. What you want is somebody who says yes to the plan before you have finished describing the plan.

You are the friend who makes things happen, and so is she. Together you would be mildly exhausting to everyone around you and completely delighted with yourselves.

You have no patience for pleasant lies, and she has no ability to tell them. What looks like coldness from the outside is the most reliable friendship on this list.

You look after whoever is having the worst day. So does he. It is the least strategic way to move through the world and the best possible quality in a friend.

A short yet sweet side story in the MCU, The Guardians of the Galaxy Holiday Special sees Drax and Mantis setting out on a mission to make this Christmas unforgettable for Quill. The team heads off to Earth in search of the perfect present, but Earth proves to be a challenge to the hapless duo, leading to a great deal of chaotic Christmas mischief.

Despite its short runtime of less than an hour, The Guardians of the Galaxy Holiday Special makes use of every available moment, filled with all the classic comedic greatness that the trilogy is so beloved for. It's a testament to the chemistry and dynamic between the Guardians as a whole and just how well they can adapt comedically to any situation, as they make a surprisingly perfect fit for holiday festivities. Its short runtime also makes it incredibly rewatchable, an easy and cozy film to have to put on and let loose for a while.

Violent Night sees a team of mercenaries break into a wealthy family's compound on Christmas Eve and take everyone inside hostage. However, the unthinkable happens, as Santa Claus finds himself on the premises in the middle of delivering presents. After the young child of the family calls out for Santa's help, he makes it his duty to go from nice to naughty and show the mercenaries a taste of their own medicine.

Action movies and Christmas go surprisingly hand in hand more often than not, especially for those who consider Die Hard a Christmas film. Violent Night fully commits to the comedic premise of "John Wick but with Santa Claus," with the perfect balance of silly hijinks and exhilarating action to make the film a cheerful blast to watch. Especially for those who are tired of the safer and wholesome energy that overwhelms the season, Violent Night provides enough high-octane brutality and action to bring joy to any Grinch's heart.

There really isn't an experience quite like a holiday get-together with extended family, as this charming gathering of beloved family members over sweets, games, and presents is all sorts of comforting, but has a hint of isolation in the most unlikely of places. It's this unique feeling and energy that Christmas Eve in Miller's Point seamlessly recaptures in its execution, showing both the beauty and the difficulties of the holiday with family in its own melancholy way.

The indie dramedy film certainly isn't as approachable as a traditional Christmas comedy, as it doesn't strive to have a major overarching plot, instead showing a scatterplot of moments and characters interacting with one another during the holiday season. However, in a way, this adds to the beauty of the film, because family get-togethers are never about linear stories, but are instead embraced and remembered for the feelings that they conjure up in us, and in that way, this film succeeds with flying colors.
