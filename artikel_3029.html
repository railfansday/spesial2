BYD vs. General Motors: Which Carmaker Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

BYD and GM are both leaning on very different engines, and the valuation gap between them may matter more than either company's EV pitch.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 25, 2026 at 1:23PM EST

The global automotive market is shifting as electric vehicles challenge traditional engines. Investors must decide if high-growth BYD (BYDDF -2.40%) or the established legacy of General Motors (GM +2.03%) offers a better path forward.

BYD focuses on new-energy vehicles, batteries, and rail transit, establishing itself as a leader in the transition away from fossil fuels. General Motors relies on its massive scale and profit-heavy internal combustion engine sales to fund its own electric transformation. Both are competing for dominance in a rapidly changing transportation landscape.

BYD is a prominent player in the global automotive industry, which is a major part of consumer discretionary stocks. The company designs and sells new-energy vehicles while also operating in electronics, batteries, and rail transit systems. Its footprint covers more than 400 cities worldwide, supported by a massive workforce of nearly 869,622 employees.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $120.4 billion. The company reported a net income of approximately $4.7 billion during this time frame. Revenue growth was roughly negative 4.5% compared to the prior twelve-month period, reflecting recent shifts in the global market.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was nearly 0.7x. This ratio compares total debt to shareholder equity to show how a company funds its operations. Its current ratio, which measures the ability to cover short-term debts with short-term assets, was approximately 0.8x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly negative $14.6 billion, representing the cash remaining after the business covers its day-to-day operations and capital equipment.

General Motors designs and builds a wide variety of trucks, crossovers, and cars, supported by more than 10,000 authorized dealerships globally. The company maintains strategic joint ventures for battery production and vehicle sales, particularly in the Chinese market. It also operates a captive finance arm to provide financing options for customers and dealers.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue was about $187 billion. Net income for the period was approximately $4.0 billion. This represented a revenue growth rate of nearly negative 0.3% as the company navigates the transition to new technology.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio reached roughly 2.1x. This metric indicates the relationship between total debt and shareholders' equity. The current ratio was close to 1.2x, representing the company's short-term liquidity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached approximately $11.1 billion.

BYD faces significant challenges due to geopolitical trade tensions, as many regions are considering or imposing tariffs on imported vehicles. The company also operates in a crowded Chinese market where price wars can weigh on financial results. Furthermore, its global expansion requires significant capital investment in diverse regulatory environments.

General Motors appears to be the more conservatively priced option, as it carries a lower multiple of both earnings estimates and sales.

General Motors has had a decent year, widening its market share in the U.S. to more than 17%, to stay the biggest car seller in the country. Also, hybrids like the Chevy Equinox and Blazer and the EV Cadillac Lyriq have been better sellers than expected, calming some fears that GM was falling behind in the trend toward hybrids and EVs.

The company has also been aggressively buying back shares, which supports the share price in the market and makes profitability appear to be improving on the per-share level. GM also shifted its shares to the Nasdaq Stock Market recently to try and get itself listed on the Nasdaq 100 index, which would also be supportive for shares, given that about $1.4 trillion in assets are indexed to the Nasdaq 100. The company is also lauded on Wall Street for handling the various Trump tariff issues that had generated fears the business would be adversely impacted.

That said, top-line growth is slow, with 2027 revenue seen rising $3 billion to $189 billion and net income exceeding $12 billion, up from about $9 billion expected for full year 2026.

Turning to BYD, while the company doesn't have a U.S. presence, it is one of the top automakers in China. The automaker's September vehicle sales increased about 17% compared to the same month in 2025, headlined by a remarkable 154% jump in overseas shipments of passenger vehicles and pickups. It's part of the company's goal of catching Toyota Motor Co. (TM +2.77%) as the world's largest automaker. Overseas sales were about 39% of BYD's sales in the third quarter. Yet it has its domestic challenges, with Q3 China sales declining more than 18% for BYD, due to intense pricing competition and a hurting Chinese consumer.

Still, foreign sales should help BYD push 2027 sales up 16% in its reporting currency of Chinese yuan, to about $145 billion at today's exchange rate.

So which stock to go with? BYD's push into Europe appears to be going very well, but that is counterbalanced by domestic problems. GM is a bargain price at a forward P/E ratio of 5.8x. The Great Recession bailout by the U.S. federal government showed how important GM is to the U.S. economy. The bet here is that GM's leading position in the world's second-largest car market, the U.S., will continue to hold, and management will continue to find ways to improve returns to shareholders.
