Google staff push back on cutting off Search for schools that reject AI

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Schools that switch off Google's AI tools will lose Search too, and staff warn that could drive them away.

More than 540 Google employees and contractors have signed a union petition asking the company to let K-12 schools use Search without its AI features, Bloomberg reported. Google plans to turn AI off by default for students, but schools that want Search must accept the AI that comes with it.

More than 540 Google employees and contractors have signed a petition asking the company to let schools use Google Search without its AI features, Bloomberg reported on Thursday. The Alphabet Workers Union, which rallied staff over job security in July, organised the letter.

“Cutting off access to Search for schools that don’t wish to use AI will not help persuade our users,” the workers wrote. They warned that schools could end up “shutting out Google products altogether.”

The letter is addressed to CEO Sundar Pichai, head of Search Elizabeth Reid and education chief Chris Phillips. Google did not immediately comment to Bloomberg.

Earlier this month, Google said it would switch off Gemini and Search for K-12 student accounts unless schools turn them on. But AI is built into Search, so schools that switch Search back on get its AI features too. Schools that want no AI at all must go without Google Search.

The plan drew criticism from parents and schools. Senator Ed Markey wrote to Google on 9 November, asking whether schools could keep Search with the AI switched off. In July, Common Sense Media called Google’s AI Search an “unacceptable risk” for students.

The stakes are high for Google. Its Chrome operating system, which will stop getting updates in 2034, ran on 58% of laptops and tablets shipped to US schools last year, per Futuresource Consulting.

Earlier this month, a family sued Google over its daughter’s conversations with its AI tools on a school device, and Google said it was reviewing the claims. AI companies already face a growing wave of lawsuits over alleged harms to users, including children.

Leaving Google is not easy for schools, said Dan Carroll, co-founder of edtech startup Clever. Chromebooks are built around Google’s software, and switching search engines takes time and training.

“It’s a real strategic pickle,” Carroll said.
