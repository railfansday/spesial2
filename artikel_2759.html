NZ pull off an escape in 'flat' Wellington: Was Gautam Gambhir's team selection justified in a Test dominated by India?

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

India dominated most of the Wellington Test, yet New Zealand escaped with a draw.

Gautam Gambhir's selection calls came under scrutiny, especially the omissions of Mohamed Siraj & Ravindra Jadeja.

A flat pitch and a tiring attack left India searching for answers late on.

New Zealand walked away from the Basin Reserve with a draw that very few would have predicted on the opening morning when they were reduced to 70/5, and the question hanging over the result is whether India's team management left something on the table.

The Basin Reserve has a reputation for helping the seam bowlers, and the surface did behave that way early in the match. As the days went by, however, it lost its life and turned into a placid strip on which the ball neither seamed nor bounced enough to trouble the batters.

India set the tempo for roughly four and a half days and pushed hard for a victory on the final day, but a gritty partnership from Daryl Mitchell & Glenn Phillips saved the Test match for the hosts.

The starting lineup chosen by Gambhir and Gill put India in a commanding position and showed that the team had enough quality to outplay New Zealand on their own soil.

Leaving out Mohammed Siraj in favour of Prasidh Krishna and Anshul Kamboj was always going to attract attention, given the senior bowler's standing in the side. Critics were quick to question the move, particularly once the match ended without a result.

Yet the evidence for that criticism is thin. Krishna and Kamboj both hit the deck hard and gave India control through the earlier innings, and on a dead final-day surface with no seam movement or bounce, there is nothing to suggest Siraj would have done better than either of them. A bowler who relies on movement off the pitch would have found little help on that last afternoon.

The support staff too backed the combination. Assistant coach Sitanshu Kotak spoke of the extra height and bounce on a damp, grassy first day, while bowling coach Morne Morkel also backed the line-up, and Gill wanted additional batting depth from Kamboj and Akash Deep.

"There is nothing wrong [with Siraj]. I mean, I can't see anything wrong," Kotak told reporters after the opening day's play in Wellington. "That's the best possible XI the team management thought and that is the one which played [this game]. Sri Lanka, obviously, it was different conditions, and Siraj played both the Tests there."

"Obviously pace is one thing because when you are playing on a wicket which is a little damp, soft to start with, with a lot of grass, obviously one with height, which gives [extra] bounce [is an advantage]."

Siraj's red-ball numbers in 2026 were modest, with five wickets at 28.60 over three Tests. Moreover, he has admitted that a two-month break upset the rhythm of his run-up. And Gill said the decision was about current form, not reputation.

"We played the best attack for this match. Siraj's a really good friend and dropping him was a really tough call," he said.

"I had a chat with him before the Test explaining him the call, and he understood that. See, every player will see a phase where they are not at their peak, and we picked on current form. A player can make a comeback by performing well."

The more difficult decision to defend was the choice to bench Ravindra Jadeja in favour of Washington Sundar.

It was the boldest call of the match, and it came under fire on the final day, when India badly needed an experienced spinner who could win a game in the fourth innings.

Sundar bowled a marathon 55 overs over the course of the match and ended with only one wicket. The flat track gave him no meaningful turn or rough to work with, and he could not offer the kind of relentless control that Jadeja has provided for India over the years. That absence was felt most keenly when the match was there to be won.

With the frontline bowlers exhausted and unable to separate the batters in the last session, India tried every option available. The captain turned to part-timers Yashasvi Jaiswal, Dhruv Jurel and Devdutt Padikkal, hoping that a change of pace or angle might produce a breakthrough. But Mitchell & Phillips remained unshakeable at the crease.

A specialist like Jadeja, with his accuracy and control, might have tightened the screws in a way that the part-timers could not.

However, Gill stood by the selection, and when he was asked whether including Siraj or Jadeja would have changed the outcome, he said, "I don't think so."

Both sides of the argument have merit. Jadeja's omission remains the most debatable decision, and Sundar's 55 overs for one wicket will keep that discussion going, particularly among those who value an experienced spinner on the last day.

On balance, however, the calls made by Gambhir and Gill look sound. India made 720/9 declared, controlled most of the Test and gave up the win only to a dead pitch and a weary attack.

The part-timers came on because the main bowlers had been worn out, and the captain says he would have kept going had there been anything in the surface.

It seems that the pitch, more than the team sheet, is what stopped India. With the series level at 0-0 and the second Test at Christchurch starting on November 27, that is also the strongest argument for staying the course, rather than making wholesale changes to the line-up.
