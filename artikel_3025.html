Value Stocks Are Doing Something They Haven’t Done Since 2000. Here’s How They're Setting Up for 2027.

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

By David Dierking – Nov 22, 2026 at 1:00PM EST

I'm guessing few investors expected this heading into 2026: Large-cap growth stocks beat large-cap value by wide margins in 2023 and 2024. They led by a narrower margin in 2025, but the assumption was that the artificial intelligence (AI) trade would continue to lead as long as earnings growth remained strong.

Earnings have thus far delivered, but market leadership has sharply reversed. Year to date, the Russell 1000 Value Index now leads the Russell 1000 Growth Index by nearly 28% in 2026, which would be its widest margin for a calendar year since 2000.

It's been a major rotation away from what's worked in 2023-2025. But can value continue to lead in 2027?

What we see in almost every boom is an eventual normalization. Investors drive prices up beyond what fundamentals can support. Then when growth rates start to slow, stock prices experience a sharp pullback.

We saw it with internet stocks during the early 2000s and cybersecurity stocks around 2015. And we may be in the early stages of seeing it with AI stocks.

The key to the growth-to-value rotation centers around AI spending and data centers. Concerns that tech companies will see a requisite return on their investments, coupled with a wave of negative sentiment around building data centers and the possibility of a coordinated effort to slow AI development, are finally pushing investors out of tech and growth.

But they're not abandoning equities altogether. S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) earnings growth is helping to ensure that. Instead, they're rotating into value and defensive equities. Whereas the biggest earnings growth before was concentrated in tech, it's now beginning to spread to other sectors that may be second-order beneficiaries of the AI build-out.

The Fed Funds futures market is currently pricing in about three rate hikes by the June meeting. Environments where rates are steadily rising generally don't coincide with growth stock outperformance. Investors tend to be a little more discerning about how much they pay for stocks as rates rise. Currently, that's looking like what could happen in 2027.

Goldman Sachs also sees S&P 500 earnings growth slowing rapidly in 2027. It's expecting 11% growth next year compared to an estimated 36% in 2026. That's still more than enough to allow stock prices to keep rising, but slowing growth tends to work better for value overall.

One catalyst that may not work for value stocks, however, would be an end to the Iran war. That would likely begin bringing inflationary pressures down as well as long-term interest rates. In that scenario, the Federal Reserve might be less compelled to raise rates, which could lead to another stretch of growth-stock leadership.

Taken together, I think conditions are leaning more toward value than growth in 2027. I wouldn't expect such a large gap between the two styles, but I believe a significant slowdown in the theme that has driven the S&P 500 over the past few years would be the biggest catalyst for value stocks next year.
