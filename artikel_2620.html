The Hot Read: The Jaguars have quietly become the NFL’s most complete team

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Each NFL season, there is always a handful of teams that stand above the rest in terms of their dominance. The San Francisco 49ers sport the league's best record at 8-1 and are coming off a 46-point performance against the Dallas Cowboys. Tied with them for the NFL's top record is the Buffalo Bills, who have won each of their last three games by 17+ points. Across the way in the AFC, Joe Burrow and the Bengals just defeated their division rivals, the Pittsburgh Steelers, by 20 points in prime time while Burrow tied an NFL record by throwing four touchdowns in five straight games. And need we not forget about the ever-present Kansas City Chiefs, whose quarterback is coming off his own high-end performance, throwing five touchdowns in a blowout win over the Falcons.

But as the blue bloods of the NFL convene atop their respective standings, standing alone in the corner sipping a Mai Tai with their loose ties hanging from a shirt with the top button loose, are the Jacksonville Jaguars. They don't want to be at this get together. They, frankly, don't care if anyone is talking about them as real Super Bowl threats. They just wanna take the field, kick ass, and go home – which has been their calling card over the last month of the season.

The Jaguars have scored 40+ points in two of their last three games, the third of which featured a 33-point outing. In their last three games, they have outscored their opponents 115-40, and Trevor Lawrence has submitted himself as a MVP candidate for the second straight season.

After finishing fifth in MVP voting a season ago, the former No. 1 overall pick has thrown seven touchdowns to just one interception over Jacksonville's three-game win streak, and he currently sits near the top of the league in nearly every major statistic. Lawrence is fourth in EPA per play among all quarterbacks with at least 100 dropbacks, per Sumer Sports. He is also fourth in success rate, and his 21 touchdown passes are third in the NFL.

It's not just Lawrence that has been fantastic, though. Far from it. Wide receiver Parker Washington has built off a strong 2025 season and blossomed into a true star receiver in 2026. The Penn State product has six touchdowns and is averaging nearly 18 yards per reception as the leader in a stacked Jags' receiving corps. Bhayshul Tuten and Chris Rodriguez have proven to be an electric running back duo, as well, with the two combining for 30 impact plays, 842 rushing yards, and nine rushing scores.

Jacksonville's defense has been terrific, too. They are the league's best run defense in terms of EPA per rush and rush success rate, and they are one of just three teams who have more interceptions than touchdown passes allowed.

Overall, the Jaguars are the only team in the NFL that is top five in both offensive and defensive EPA. They are the most complete team in the NFL. And, oh by the way, they added Jessie Bates and Jordan Davis at the trade deadline, both of whom are expected to make their Jaguars debuts in Week 11 after sitting out while the Jags dropped 40 on the Titans. And among all this well-constructed talent sits a head coach in Liam Coen who has come in and immediately established himself as one of the game's best. In his first two seasons as head coach, Coen is 20-6. He was the fastest head coach to reach 15 wins, and he is now among the likes of George Seifert and Matt LaFleur as the fastest to reach 20 wins. His presence immediately changed the culture and the ceiling of a franchise that had long found itself among the laughing stocks of the league.

That said, many remain hesitant to mention the Jaguars in the same breath of the current elites in the league until they make a deep playoff run. Every empire needs its first conquest, after all. By the looks of it, Coen's group is well-equipped for a takeover.

That was the cold open – this is The Hot Read.

Now of course things can change between now and January, but as things currently stand, Bengals quarterback Joe Burrow is the 2026 MVP. I'll go ahead and give myself a small pat on the back for predicting as much back in July. I also said he'd throw for 50 touchdowns – he is currently on pace for 51. He has thrown five more touchdowns than anyone else in the league, and if the season ended today, he would be the new NFL record holder for highest completion percentage in a season. His total EPA leads all quarterbacks, and his pass EPA is 15 points higher than second-place Brock Purdy.

It's not even much of a conversation as to who is the top dog in the race. The aforementioned Lawrence is certainly in the conversation, as is Brock Purdy, but after that? Josh Allen, sure, but he only has 13 touchdown passes. And I'm not one who diminishes the rushing touchdowns being from a yard or two away, but at the same time, he's not rushing for a lot of yards with those touchdowns that would justify him being in that top spot. Patrick Mahomes has played second fiddle to Kenneth Walker as the big star of the Chiefs' offense this season, so I wouldn't put him in the picture as of right now. Jordan Love and the Packers have been wildly inconsistent despite his individual brilliance. The clear leader in the race is Burrow. And should he remain on this pace, he'll be a potential unanimous MVP.

A year and a half into DK Metcalf's extension that sees him make $33 million per year, the deal has been an abject disaster for the Steelers. It doesn't get a major spotlight outside of the 412 area code because the Steelers won the AFC North in 2025 and currently sit with a 6-3 record, but their lack of passing game is in large part due to the fact that Metcalf has been a massive disappointment.

The Ole Miss product currently sits with 482 receiving yards, 115 of which came in one game agains the Browns in Week 4. If you take that outlier away, he would have just 317 yards on the season. Five times this season, he's put up 40 or fewer receiving yards, and twice, he's put up 12 or fewer. He has 48 fewer receiving yards than Davante Adams despite the Rams' star playing in three fewer games. He ranks 70th in yards per route run among receivers who have run at least 100 routes, and his target success rate ranks 99th.

Throw in the fact that he has the most accepted penalties of any receiver in the NFL this season, and you get a player who is playing nowhere near the level of someone who is the seventh-highest paid player at his position. Pittsburgh has weathered the storm, largely leaning on Jaylen Warren to be the engine of their offense, while tight ends Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington have been relied on as go-to targets in the passing game. However, the lack of vertical passing game is hindering Pittsburgh. Aaron Rodgers is just 9-of-44 on passes 20+ yards downfield, which allows teams to be a bit more aggressive against the short and intermediate passes, as well as the run game – see Cincinnati in Week 10. It's not that Metcalf hasn't played to the level of his contract, it's that he has been a net negative when on the field. And unless something changes drastically between now and the playoffs, the Steelers won't have nearly enough firepower to take on the AFC's top teams.

Like Jake Gyllenhaal and Heath Ledger in Brokeback Mountain, I truly wish I could quit the Houston Texans. After blowing a 17-0 lead against the Cleveland Browns, you would think it would be easy to do that. But then I look at their schedule and see a ton of winnable games still on the horizon. They play the Colts at home on Thursday night, followed by another home game against a bad Ravens team. Two wins get them to 5-6 going into Week 13, where they will travel to Pittsburgh for a Sunday night game that could ultimately decide their season. If they win that game, though, they then face the Washington Commanders, another winnable game. And at 7-6 (presumably) they would then host the Jaguars before trips to Green Bay and Philadelphia and a Week 18 home game against the Titans.

Of their final eight opponents, six rank 18th or worse in offensive EPA, two of which are in the bottom three. And while Houston's defense has underdelivered after being overhyped, their own offense has been quite good, ranking ninth in total EPA and points per game.

The problem for Houston is that their magic number appears to be one. If they get to eight losses, it becomes very hard to see a path to the postseason, especially because the current No. 7 seed Browns hold the tiebreaker. Should Houston want to make another improbable run to the postseason, they'd need to nearly run the table. And even if they do get to 10 wins, there is no guarantee that it would be enough.

A bit of a melancholy edition of the one play this week, but one that certainly warrants the title.

In the first game of the Sunday slate, the Detroit Lions and New England Patriots faced off in Germany. At the end of the opening drive, Lions star pass rusher Aidan Hutchinson sacked Patriots quarterback Drake Maye. The 2025 MVP runner-up remained on the turf as Hutchinson jumped in the air to celebrate the sack, and then he, too, went back down to the ground.

It was revealed that Hutchinson tore his ACL on the celebration, ending his season. Maye suffered a fractured ankle, and his season looks like it could be over, as well. Two teams seeing their respective campaigns severely altered, one of which will now need to figure out what it will do at quarterback for the rest of the year. For the Lions, it's the second time in three seasons they've lost Hutchinson to a season-ending leg injury. For a defense that was already inconsistent at best, this will serve as yet another massive obstacle for them to overcome. As for the Patriots, their season was a whirlwind before the Maye injury. Now at 5-5, their playoff hopes are all but dead and gone.

Seattle's offense has just flat out been better with Lock under center. Of all quarterbacks with at least 100 dropbacks, Lock leads the NFL in EPA per play.

Read that again. Drew Lock leads the NFL in EPA per play. He's also yet to throw an interception, and has seven touchdown passes, including a Ben Roethlisberger-esc play that resulted in Cooper Kupp hauling in a long score this past Sunday against the Raiders. Sam Darnold has looked a lot more like Jets and Panthers Sam Darnold than the quarterback we saw in each of the last two seasons. Will Lock keep up this level of play? It feels unlikely, but right now he is the best option for Seattle. Keep riding the hot hand until it stops being hot.

For what it's worth, everyone has been selling on Parker since Week 1, but Sunday was a never-ending charade of inept defensive play. The Cowboys allowed 584 yards of offense to the 49ers, the most yards ever allowed at home in franchise history. The 46 points allowed puts Dallas in second-to-last in points per game (28.2), just 0.7 points ahead of the Cardinals and Raiders. It's rare to see a coach or coordinator last less than one season, but this should be a case where Dallas gives Mike Pettine the interim DC title for the rest of the season.

Kirk Cousins going back to the Vikings is much more than just a good story. In his first start back in the Purple and Gold, Cousins threw two touchdowns and led the Vikings' offense to a 30-10 win over the Packers, its first 30-point outing since Week 1 against… the Packers. With their defense, the Vikings don't need Cousins to be superhuman, they just need him to not be the worst starting quarterback in the NFL, which, collectively, Carson Wentz and Kyler Murray were. We saw the Seahawks win a Super Bowl with this formula a season ago. Who's to say Minnesota can't do it, too?

This is a lifeless team with a soft defense. Credit where it's due, Jacoby Brissett came in and gave the Ravens a chance to win. However, their defense, under the guidance of a quote unquote "defensive genius" Jesse Minter, allowed the Chargers' league-worst scoring offense to put up 23 points – tied for their most in a game this season – en route to Los Angeles finally winning their first game. Now at 4-6, and with Lamar Jackson still a way's away from being able to come back, stick a fork in the Ravens.
