Chargers vs. Ravens: 4 bold predictions for Week 10 matchup

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Chargers weren't so quiet this past week during the trade deadline.

After reports surfaced that they would stand pat, the Bolts made one trade. However, it was rescinded.

Daiyan Henley is back with the Chargers after the trade with the Broncos fell through. After all that, the Bolts have the same team heading into Week 10 when they face the Ravens.

Los Angeles is the league's only winless team. They are 0-8, but they have a great opportunity to get their first win over the hobbled Ravens.

If these four things happen, the Chargers could possibly get a win.

It was a whirlwind of a week for the Chargers linebacker. First, Henley finds out he was traded from the team that drafted him four seasons ago to their division rival, the Broncos. And a little over 24 hours later, the trade fell through after a failed physical. Henley is back with the team now, and I think he will be out for blood. Last week, Henley recorded a season-high 10 tackles against the Texans. I think he set another season-high in primetime against the Ravens.

The Chargers' run defense ranks in the bottom half in that category, allowing an average of 122 rushing yards per game. Through eight games, the Chargers have allowed 11 rushing touchdowns. Los Angeles will face their toughest test yet with running back Derrick Henry. It's been tough sledding for Henry over the past few weeks, and I think that trend continues. Henry has rushed for under 60 yards in four consecutive games.

The Ravens will have yet another quarterback under center: veteran Jacoby Brissett. The Chargers' defense has been the team's only bright spot. Their offense and defense haven't provided enough complementary football this season, but forcing turnovers could help change that. Brissett has had a mixed -to-rough time taking care of the football this season. Los Angeles will take care of that.

Justin Herbert is playing the worst football of his NFL career. He's coming off another disappointing performance against Houston, throwing for 193 yards and one touchdown in a 44-22 loss. However, Herbert has a chance to play his best game yet against an inconsistent Ravens defense. Baltimore is allowing 329.7 passing yards per game, and while Herbert has yet to reach 300+ passing yards, that will happen in front of a national audience.
