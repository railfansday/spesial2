SEC Heisman Hopes Take a Hit as Three National Stars Pull Away Before Rivalry Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

BIRMINGHAM, Ala. — The Southeastern Conference remains firmly involved in the national championship race.

However, the league faces a much different situation in the competition for college football's most prestigious individual award.

As Rivalry Week approaches, the 2026 Heisman Trophy race has shifted heavily toward players outside the SEC.

Ohio State wide receiver Jeremiah Smith has emerged as the overwhelming favorite.

Meanwhile, Utah quarterback Devon Dampier remains his closest challenger, with Miami's Darian Mensah and Notre Dame's CJ Carr among the longer-shot contenders.

For a conference accustomed to producing Heisman winners, the current situation is unusual.

Smith has delivered one of the nation's most impressive individual seasons.

Through Week 12, the Ohio State receiver recorded 86 catches for 1,482 yards and 17 touchdowns.

Furthermore, he strengthened his case with six receptions for 165 yards and two touchdowns against Nebraska.

Despite Ohio State's 31-28 defeat, Smith continued to separate himself from the competition.

His combination of production, consistency and national recognition has made him the clear frontrunner.

Dampier has established himself as Smith's strongest challenger.

The Utah quarterback continued his impressive season with four touchdowns against TCU, helping the Utes maintain their national championship ambitions.

Meanwhile, Mensah and Carr have both generated significant Heisman attention throughout the year.

However, their candidacies suffered setbacks during Week 12.

The two quarterbacks combined for one touchdown pass and three interceptions, allowing Smith to widen his advantage.

Consequently, the national race appears increasingly difficult for anyone outside the top two.

Georgia quarterback Gunner Stockton and Alabama quarterback Keelon Russell have helped their programs remain among the nation's championship contenders.

However, neither has established a realistic position near the top of the Heisman race.

Stockton entered Rivalry Week as a considerable long shot, while Russell faced even longer odds.

Their situations illustrate an important distinction between team success and individual award recognition.

Georgia and Alabama can compete for national championships without producing serious Heisman finalists.

Furthermore, other SEC standouts have struggled to maintain the national attention necessary to challenge Smith.

A Nov. 22 FanDuel snapshot illustrated the gap separating the leading candidates.

Russell remained substantially further back in the market.

The numbers show that Smith has become the clear favorite, while Dampier retains a plausible opportunity to challenge him.

Mensah and Carr would need exceptional finishes and help from the frontrunners.

Rivalry Week offers another opportunity for Stockton, Russell and other conference standouts to deliver memorable performances.

However, a single impressive game would be unlikely to erase the enormous gap in the national Heisman race.

For now, the SEC's strongest argument remains its collective championship credentials rather than an individual award candidate.

The conference could send multiple programs into the College Football Playoff while failing to produce a leading Heisman contender.

And unless something extraordinary happens during the final weeks, Smith appears increasingly likely to take home the trophy.
