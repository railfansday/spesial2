Even in a lost Eagles season, greatness can happen

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

I'm going to tell you something you already know.

The Philadelphia Eagles are not going to win the Super Bowl this year. In fact, they are almost certainly not going to make the playoffs, even though their epic 39-38 back-from-the-dead victory over the Cowboys in Dallas on Thanksgiving afternoon increased their odds of doing so just a tad.

We've known for more than a month that this was a lost season. When Jalen Hurts was lost for the year with blood clots, Lane Johnson retired, half the team got injured and the front office traded away Cam Jurgens and Jordan Davis, it became obvious the franchise was punting on 2026 and looking to the future.

After winning their second Super Bowl two years ago and reaching the postseason five straight seasons under Nick Sirianni, that's OK. Lost seasons happen.

Not every season has to be about winning the Super Bowl. I know that's easier to say as fans of a franchise that now has two of them in the last decade, but it's the truth. Fans of prior generations who had to live through not just lost seasons, but lost decades, had to learn how to unearth greatness while trudging through the muck of a depressing campaign.

The Eagles' scintillating victory over the Cowboys yesterday was just such a moment. It probably won't result in the team making the playoffs, but it is a victory that will be remembered by every fan of this team for the rest of their lives.

Their rally from 21 points down is the largest deficit overcome in NFL history on Thanksgiving, and they did it against their most hated rival, in their building. They did it with incredible plays from the unlikeliest, and likeliest, corners of the roster.

Consider Joe Milton, III, the Eagles' fourth quarterback of the season, who began 2026 as Dallas' third-stringer and was signed by Howie Roseman off their practice squad back in October. After being thrust into the starting job following Tanner McKee's struggles as the back-up, Milton has thrived in the team's two games after the bye.

On Thursday afternoon in Dallas, he was electric.

Milton finished 21-of-31 passing for 331 yards, 3 TDs, one interception, and one rushing score on a 15 yard scramble to the end zone. He's making quite a case to be considered the team's back-up QB once Hurts is back next year.

His favorite target on the day, DeVonta Smith, had the game of his life. Connecting on multiple deep hook-ups against an awful Dallas secondary, Smith caught 11 balls on 14 targets for a career-high 200 yards and two TDs. It's unclear if he'll be available for the Eagles' next game in Arizona after being landing hard on his tailbone late in the 4th quarter of the Birds' comeback, but his efforts on Thursday were nothing short of heroic.

Ty Robinson, a name no one could have been expected to utter much heading into the game, was responsible for two of the afternoon's biggest plays. Trailing 31-17 early in the 4th quarter, Robinson's blocked punt allowed Kenny Moore to pick the ball up inside the five yard line and waltz into the end zone for a momentum-shifting score.

And then, with less than a minute to play and Dallas going for a game-sealing 4th-and-1 conversion, Robinson (with the help of Quinyon Mitchell's terrific defense on Cee Dee Lamb denying a slant pattern that was Dak Prescott's first option) sacked Prescott at midfield to put the Eagles a mere 10 yards out of field goal range.

Robinson finished with three QB pressures, two hurries, the aforementioned sack and blocked punt. The trade of Jordan Davis earlier this month has allowed Robinson to get more playing time, and he's taking advantage of it.

And how about Jake Elliott? After struggling over the last two seasons, especially from long distance (5-for-15 from beyond 50 yards), he has suddenly re-discovered his deep stroke and is enjoying a late-career renaissance. Overall, he's 13-for-14 this season, including a perfect 5-for-5 from beyond 50.

If you haven't heard the Dallas radio call of Elliott's 60-yarder yesterday, as well as the final two seconds of the game, enjoy this.

There were other performances worth remembering, too. Will Shipley stepped up and rushed for 46 yards on 10 carries after Saquon Barkley left the game with a stinger. Makai Lemon hauled in five balls for 51 yards, including a terrific 24-yard touchdown catch in the 4th quarter. Elijah Moore caught three balls for 57 yards. Jeremiah Trotter, Jr. led all players with 12 tackles (no other player on either team had more than nine, Byron Young). And Braden Mann continued pounding punts into the stratosphere, consistently helping the Eagles with field position whenever possible.

And kudos must be given to the much-maligned head coach and offensive coordinator. After failing to score more than 24 points in a single game this year, the offense put up 22 in the 4th quarter alone and 39 overall. Yes, it was against a porous Dallas defense, but we've seen Sean Mannion's offense struggle against the worst defenses in the league, including this very same Cowboys squad in late October when they lost 38-21 at the Linc.

I didn't love Sirianni's histrionics after the game. Stop jawing with fans, get yourself under control, and go give the opposing head coach a proper post-game handshake. Also, the decision to go for two points after drawing to 38-30 made no sense to me, either. But all that being said, Sirianni's players never folded the tents after getting behind 31-10 in the second half, and credit must be giving to the head coach for that.

Eagles fans haven't had to deal with many lost seasons over the last quarter century. Thankfully, we're not used to this. So it's easy to forget that, even in a season where nothing has gone your way, a legendary moment can be birthed from its ashes.

Sometimes, sports is just about moments and memories. And while there likely won't be a banner raised at Lincoln Financial Field at the beginning of next year, The Joe Milton Thanksgiving Game of 2026 will be remembered as an Eagles classic forever.

And that is something to be thankful for.
