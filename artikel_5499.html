Top tips for long flights from frequent travelers

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Make your next long-haul flight more comfortable with expert tips for better sleep, smarter packing, in-flight snacks and an easier arrival

A long-haul flight can test even the most enthusiastic traveler. After hours in a cramped seat, the cabin starts to feel stuffy, your legs need a stretch and the snacks you packed suddenly seem less appealing. While you can't control every part of the experience, a little planning can make a big difference between arriving ready to explore and stumbling off the plane feeling completely wiped out.

Frequent travelers have learned to make those hours more manageable. Some rely on practical comforts, such as packing a personal amenity kit or choosing a seat with sleep in mind. Others have built routines around staying hydrated, getting up to move or adjusting to a new time zone before landing. A few even treat long flights as a chance to catch up on work without the usual interruptions.

Reader’s Digest spoke with frequent travelers to gather their best advice for making long flights more comfortable. Their suggestions cover everything from what to wear and pack to how to spend your time in the air. Whether you're crossing the country or heading overseas, these tips can help you settle in, make the most of your seat and arrive feeling a little more human.

A long flight is no time to assume you'll be hungry at exactly the moment the airline serves food. Reader’s Digest recommends bringing a mix of snacks, including something salty and something sweet, along with a drink purchased at the airport. In-flight service can be delayed, and it's easy to sleep through the beverage cart or simply not feel like asking for something between scheduled services.

Having your own snacks gives you more control over when and what you eat. It also means you're not stuck waiting if you get hungry during a long stretch without service. Spending a little extra at the airport may feel unnecessary, especially when you've already paid for your ticket, but having food within reach can make the flight more comfortable. Think of it as a small investment in your own in-flight peace of mind.

Airplane cabins can get cold, and a stylish outfit won't do much for you if you're uncomfortable halfway through the flight. According to Reader’s Digest, wear breathable, cozy layers if you can. Wearing an oversized hoodie and thick socks, and bringing a neck pillow and eye mask, can help create a warm, dark space for sleeping.

Prioritize comfort over an outfit designed just for the airport. A soft layer is easy to add or remove as the cabin temperature changes, and it can save you from relying on a thin airline blanket.

There's nothing worse than needing a toothbrush, face wipe, or change of clothes, and realizing the item is buried in your overhead suitcase. Keeping a well-stocked amenity kit in your personal item, where you can reach it throughout the flight, can be extremely helpful.

Your kit can include basics such as a toothbrush, toothpaste, makeup-removing wipes, and hair products. Also, according to Reader’s Digest, packing a change of underwear can go a long way in case checked luggage goes missing. or doesn't arrive when you do.

Rather than rummaging through your carry-on or waiting until you reach your hotel, you have the things you need to freshen up close at hand. Put together a small kit before you leave home and keep it in the bag that stays under the seat in front of you. It's one less thing to worry about when you're tired and ready to land.

Long flights come with plenty of familiar cabin smells, from reheated meals to stale air. For example, notes Reader’s Digest, scented balms can make the environment feel more pleasant.

The scents can be subtle, so as not to overwhelm nearby passengers. If you want to try the idea, keep any scent you bring understated. A strong fragrance can quickly become unpleasant for people sitting nearby in a confined cabin. The point is to create a small comfort for yourself, in an otherwise somewhat stale environment.

The hours spent in an airplane cabin can leave some travelers feeling less than refreshed. Keeping up with those small routines can go a long way to feeling good upon arrival. Additionally, according to Reader’s Digest, a few familiar products can make it easier to freshen up during a long journey, especially if you're heading straight into sightseeing, a meeting or another connection.

You don't need to bring your entire bathroom cabinet. Choose a few travel-size products you already use and keep them somewhere accessible in your personal item. That way, you can freshen up without unpacking your luggage or waiting until you reach your destination.

Shoes that feel fine at the airport may become uncomfortable after hours in the air. According to Reader’s Digest, choose slip-on shoes without laces and go up half a size. Tight sneakers became increasingly uncomfortable as your feet will possibly swell during lengthy journeys.

Easy-to-remove shoes also make it simpler to get comfortable in your seat without wrestling with laces. Just remember that you'll want footwear that's practical for walking through the airport, too. The goal is to avoid shoes that feel restrictive after several hours, not to sacrifice comfort as soon as you reach the gate.

If you're planning a long-haul trip, think about how your shoes will feel near the end of the journey, not just when you first put them on. A little extra room can make those hours much easier.
