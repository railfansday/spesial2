ESPN hints at potentially massive contract extension for Caleb Williams

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Chicago Bears quarterback Caleb Williams could be about to rewrite the contract record books this coming offseason.

At this point, it goes without saying that the third-year signal-caller will get a monster bag as Chicago's franchise quarterback when he becomes eligible for a contract extension this coming spring. ESPN's Courtney Cronin suggested as much in her latest blurb on the Bears and the top extension candidates for each team (aka Williams).

But her projection makes my preseason estimates sound tame.

"Chicago's front office has an incentive to get ahead of a rapidly inflating QB market, which will see the likes of C.J. Stroud and Lamar Jackson come to the table, along with others in Williams' draft class. Though Williams said in September that "money's not a motivating factor for me," his top-10 play on a contending team could lead to him earning around $70 million per year. Signing Williams early would allow the Bears to keep his 2027 cap hit low while prorating a massive signing bonus into future years," she wrote.

Then again, perhaps it shouldn't be surprising.

Right now, Patrick Mahomes boasts the highest average annual value among NFL quarterbacks, earning $63.09 million per year on his reworked contract, followed by Dak Prescott ($60 million) and Josh Allen ($55 million). At least four quarterbacks—Jackson, Stroud, Williams, and Drake Maye—are in line to potentially break those records this offseason as the salary cap continues to climb. (Jayden Daniels is a sneaky candidate as well, though his struggle to stay healthy could hold him back slightly.)

Of the five names listed above, only Jackson is definitively a better player than Williams. Moreover, Williams might be the only one of those players to lead their teams to the playoffs this season, which would be his second trip to the postseason in a row. Throw in his major improvement as a passer, and you have the recipe for a gargantuan payday.

What will be curious, however, is how Williams handles the contract situation in reality.

The young quarterback, who has plenty of endorsements and outside investments, isn't hurting for cash. While he shouldn't take a lowball offer simply for the sake of a hometown discount, it wouldn't be shocking to see him hold back on breaking the bank just enough to make sure the Bears can keep players in town. After all, quarterbacks are important, but they can't win Super Bowls alone.

With the race to $70 million AAV likely to be intense, maybe paying Williams that amount is inevitable for the Bears. But when you have a franchise quarterback, you pay him like one. Fortunately for the Bears, Williams seems like the kind of player who can be worth the price.
