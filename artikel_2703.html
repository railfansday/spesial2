The NFC South is football’s annual participation trophy

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The belief that the NFC South would be better in 2026 was completely delusional. If we look back to the close of the 2025 season, there was a lot to look forward to when it came to the division. The Falcons looked like they were going to take a step forward with Kevin Stefanski and Bijan Robinson. Carolina looked like a team that had begun to put it together on the back of the Bryce Young/Tetairoa McMillan handcuff. The Saints had a big free agency that appeared to set the table for a Tyler Shough breakout. While the Buccaneers, steady as ever, had excellent defensive talent and a good enough offense.

Fast forward to today, and once more the NFC South is hilarious. The NFL's only remaining division that doesn't have a single team above .500, it will again host a home playoff game where a much better team will have to play visitor, and will continue to both be laughed at, as well as stoke the flames of frustration in the playoff process.

Someone has to win this division, so who's it going to be?

The Falcons decided to punt on the season when they traded away Jessie Bates, and it's probably smart that they did. This team is just not in a position to compete, and there's more soul searching to be done at the quarterback position moving forward.

There's only one real bright spot for Atlanta on offense, which is Bijan Robinson. We know Drake London is a hell of a receiver, but he just can't show it with the horrible quarterbacking he has to work with. Sure, he'll finish with more than 1,000 yards receiving because he's a beast, but right now he only has one receiving touchdown because this team can't pass the ball in the red zone. Some of that is a focus on running the ball, but a receiver with London's frame should be able to do more.

We just didn't see the defense take the step forward this season we assumed they could. Atlanta ranks 23rd in the NFL in points allowed, they're 25th against the pass, and 19th against the run. There's nothing this team does well, and without a consistent passing game there's no mechanism to generate enough explosive plays to get back in games.

This has been a watershed year for Bryce Young, who really put it all together in his fourth season, showing flashes of being a top-10 quarterback when things are meshing. The main issue for the Panthers is a lack of consistency, which has been fueled by myriad injury issues at key positions.

Carolina's offensive line has been ravaged by injuries, thrusting rookie Monroe Freeling into a starting gig before he was ready, and having a turnstile of mediocrity at left tackle. Sporadic injuries at guard have caused issues too, ruining any chance of putting some momentum together. This team is being dragged to wins kicking and screaming by Bryce Young, Chuba Hubbard, and Tetairoa McMillan.

The defense was a mess for much of the year until Carolina fired defensive coordinator Ejiro Evero. The unit has improved since then, but this team is down its three top cornerbacks, and has been for several games now. Pair that with the preseason injury to pass rusher Nic Scourton and again, there's just been no room for momentum.

Carolina won't meet its expectations this season. Embarrassing losses earlier in the year to the likes of Cleveland and Tampa will come back to haunt them as we get to final rankings.

This Saints team is a mess. It's not that the talent isn't there, but something is just rotten inside the organization. There is no team in the NFL who has less of an identity than the New Orleans Saints, who are seemingly good at absolutely nothing. They don't run the ball well, the passing game is inconsistent, playcalling is uninspired, the defense is mediocre. It's resulted in a season where the Saints aren't so much playing, as just existing.

Something had to have happened inside that locker room. Tyler Shough is playing good football, but nobody else seems prepared to play on Sundays. The team is constantly surprised by its opponents on both sides of the ball, and seems lost when it comes to strategizing against an opponent. We have seen the high water marks this team can reach, shown in gutsy wins over the Lamar Jackson-led Ravens, and the Giants — but that's about it.

It's beginning to feel like Kellen Moore's days in New Orleans are numbered.

The Buccaneers' defense is astonishing. One of the best units in the NFL, it's been very difficult to score against Tampa, almost as if they're the baby brother of the Vikings in the NFC North. Similarly, their offense has been awful as well.

Losing Mike Evans in free agency transformed the potential of this team, and nobody is hurting more because of it than Baker Mayfield. Barely gaining over 150 passing yards per game, Mayfield has struggled to generate any consistency with the weapons at his disposal. The saving grace for the Bucs is that both Bucky Irving and Kenneth Gainwell have been running the ball well, assuming the mantle for the entire offense.

This is a team that is capable of beating anyone in ugly games because of their defensive prowess. Those wins haven't often come, but they have an incredibly easy schedule down the stretch that should allow them to string some Ws together. It will all come down to Monday Night Football when the Buccaneers host the Panthers, and right now I have the edge going to Tampa Bay, who will win the NFC South and move onto the playoffs.

The thing about this Tampa team is that between Vita Vea in the middle and their incredible group of linebackers, the Bucs really could shock a much better team in the playoffs with their blitz aggressiveness. If they can get anything out of Mayfield, then don't be surprised if this is a team that turns some heads.
