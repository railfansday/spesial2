Minnesota coach Chris Finch also said the split was amicable, though he admitted beforehand he knew the Nets forward would be out to punish his old team. "[I] loved coaching Julius. We did a lot of winning here, certainly in the playoffs. He was a huge, huge contributor there," Finch said. "He came here in less than ideal situations, 48 hours before the season started, trying to figure it out on the fly. "It was his selflessness in the middle of that first season that really ignited our run, learning that this team needed him to be a primary playmaker. … The first two rounds of the playoffs that year, he was probably one of the top-five players in the league and helped us get to the Western Conference finals. So I know exactly the mindset he'll be in. I've seen him go out against his previous teams."
