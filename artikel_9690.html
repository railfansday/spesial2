Investigating Suspected CVE-2026-79856 Exploitation

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The HPE advisory reports no known public exploit code for CVE-2026-79856 as of its release date. An investigation still needs a factual starting point. Begin with the AOS-8 release, PAPI reachability and evidence of administrative or configuration activity that conflicts with approved operations.

CVE-2026-79856 is a buffer overflow in the PAPI protocol of HPE Networking ArubaOS (AOS-8). HPE rates it 9.8 Critical under CVSS 3.1 with the vector CVSS:3.1/AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:H/I:H/A:H. The assessment records no required privileges and no user interaction, while confidentiality, integrity and availability impact are all high. HPE published the defect in advisory HPESBNW05162 on October 22, 2026.

HPE describes a buffer overflow in PAPI, Aruba's access point management protocol. The overflow can let an unauthenticated remote attacker circumvent existing authentication controls. Successful exploitation can provide administrative access, allow changes to system configuration and permit access to or manipulation of sensitive data. The advisory does not provide a packet layout, the exact packet-processing function or a public proof of concept. Defenders should preserve that technical limit and avoid claiming that a port scan alone proves exploitability.

The immediate security concern is control of the AOS-8 management plane. An attacker who crosses the authentication boundary could change how the wireless infrastructure enforces policy, trusts clients or exposes operational data. Configuration changes can outlive the initial intrusion and affect connected systems. The CVSS vector also scores high availability impact, so disruption is a plausible outcome. These are possible consequences of successful exploitation; HPE's release advisory does not confirm that any customer was attacked.

HPE assigns CVE-2026-79856 to the AOS-8 branches 8.13.0.0 through 8.13.3.0 and 8.10.0.0 through 8.10.0.23. The fixed releases are 8.13.3.1 and 8.10.0.24 or later versions in the applicable supported branch. HPE's advisory covers Mobility Conductors, Mobility Controllers, Mobility Gateways and AOS-8 Instant Access Points. AOS-10 is outside this CVE's listed affected product scope.

A reachable device in an affected range satisfies an exposure condition. It does not establish that the overflow occurred. HPE says successful exploitation can bypass authentication and provide administrative access. Investigators should look for unauthorized sessions, changed accounts, configuration divergence or sensitive-data access and state which source supports each finding.

Preserve device logs and evidence from upstream firewalls, AAA systems and central management before a reboot or reconfiguration removes context. Record the source networks that could reach PAPI. If the device is affected and unexplained privileged activity exists, isolate the management plane and begin the organization's incident process. Do not wait for a public payload signature that the vendor has not supplied.

ZoomEye provides inventory context, not a vulnerability verdict. A successful Search for app="ArubaOS" returned 210 product-fingerprint matches. The selected Filter vul.cve="CVE-2026-79856" returned 0. Exposure is the larger selected count, 210, and is not a sum. The Search can include ArubaOS systems on unaffected branches or versions, and the zero Filter result does not prove that no vulnerable device exists.

HPE directs customers to upgrade AOS-8 8.13.x deployments to 8.13.3.1 or later and AOS-8 8.10.x deployments to 8.10.0.24 or later. Until the update is applied, HPE recommends restricting the CLI and web-based management interfaces to a dedicated Layer 2 segment or VLAN and controlling access with firewall policy at Layer 3 and above. HPE also recommends accounting controls for tracking and logging user activity and resource use. The vendor reported no known public discussion or exploit code as of the advisory release date.

Containment can remove unapproved routes, narrow management access and upgrade to a fixed AOS-8 release. Those actions reduce further risk while the investigation continues. Compare configurations with trusted backups, trace account and session activity and determine whether sensitive data was accessed or changed. Keep the exploitation hypothesis open until release, reachability and activity evidence support a conclusion.
