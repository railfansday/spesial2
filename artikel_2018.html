Amorim discusses Cardinale’s ‘Top Gun’ remark, Scudetto aim, Ramos’ struggles and injuries

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

AC Milan head coach Ruben Amorim has responded to Gerry Cardinale’s words about him being his ‘Top Gun’ and the Scudetto being an aim.

For Milan, there is once again the need to bounce back after a negative result. On Wednesday night at San Siro they led at half-time against Bologna, before conceding three times in the second half to eventually lose 4-2.

Not only did that knock the Rossoneri out of the top four but it put them seven points behind Inter and Lazio who are the league-leaders at the moment. So, it means that a victory is needed in the derby to keep pace at the top, but there is at least some good injury news: Diego Moreira, Mario Gila and Adrien Rabiot are all back.

Amorim spoke to the reporters who gathered at Milanello for his pre-match press conference on the eve of the game. MilanNews relayed his words.

“I truly appreciate Cardinale’s support. We already knew – and everyone here has the clarity to understand – that we can’t build a solid foundation if we have to deal with these kinds of issues in the middle of the season.

“It will take time, it’s inevitable, but at the same time, we want to win every single game: when we fail to do so, we’ll continue to feel it, making it our own with great pain. It will be like this for a while. We need time to grow and improve the team, and we know that good things take time to find the perfect balance and hit the mark.

“I think it’s a good analogy and it makes perfect sense, but that doesn’t take away the pain of defeat. That’s still there, it still stings, but we need to be extremely clear with our fans, making them understand that it will take time.”

What differences do you feel between this derby and the ones in Manchester and Lisbon?

“I think it’s a special match because it’s being played in the same city, and yesterday we saw the plans for the new stadium. It’s a unique rivalry, but there’s also a deep bond: two great teams in the same city sharing a stadium.

“I don’t think you can find a context like this anywhere else; it’s truly special. But matches like this… well, if we look at Portuguese culture, the feeling is the same: the sense of responsibility for victory, the enthusiasm of the fans, are very similar. The football culture here is closer to that of Portugal.

“In England, it’s slightly different: there’s a great battle, but the spirit remains a bit more sporting. Here, you can feel that Latin element that we also experience in Portugal, so I really think it will be a special match.”

“They’re ready to join the team. We’ll have to evaluate tomorrow morning whether they’ll be able to start or what the game plan will be. Of course, I already have an idea, but we’ll see tomorrow.”

Has the team regained the energy it didn’t see on Wednesday?

“Everyone knows it’s a special match, a special moment, and when you get to play it, the context doesn’t matter, it doesn’t matter if you’re tired. You have to go out there and give it your all, and we’ll be there.

“I said it: I felt it from the first minute of the match against Bologna, watching the game from the bench. We can’t lose a game like that. We can’t afford to concede two goals in two minutes. We can do much better, and there are so many small details that I’ve already discussed with the team.

“But since we returned from international duty three weeks ago, there hasn’t been any real time to train. And I’m not saying other teams are in a different position, I’m speaking for ourselves: I feel like there are so many small things we’re missing simply because we don’t have the time to address them in training.

“So it’s not just a question of energy, although I’ve noticed that, because with energy you can also mask a lot of shortcomings, but there are so many aspects we need time to work on if we want to become a better team.”

Cardinale says that the Scudetto is possible this year and that there will be some signings in January…

“You always have to talk about the Scudetto as the main objective when you’re at Milan, but we’ve already dropped points and we need to fix things. We just have to focus on improving the team step by step.

“What we did wrong in the last match needs to be corrected for the next one. Because if we start thinking too far ahead, about the standings and long-term goals, we risk forgetting what’s really important to us. We want to win consistently, year after year.

“We don’t want to win a season just because we had a bit of luck or because we took shortcuts in the foundations of the project, only to find ourselves back where we started the following year. That’s why we have to fix things day by day, addressing both needs in parallel.

“But when you’re Milan, the moral obligation is to declare that the objective is to win. And at the same time, we have to be aware that next week we have a match that could allow us to close the gap at the top.

“Taking all this into account, we just have to focus on winning the next match, and everything else will calm down. But the objective of the club is always and in any case winning Serie A and that remains our final goal. How long will it take? I don’t know.”

“I have zero envy for Chivu because I’m already in the right place, that is, at Milan and not at Inter. That’s the way I am, that’s the way I am.

“I haven’t learned the lesson from my previous experience: I chose more or less the same situation, a club in the midst of rebuilding, which is trying to launch young players like Cisse to build something from scratch.

“It’s a challenge that excites me, I like it, and that’s why I chose this project: I’m really happy with my choice. At the same time, however, I’m not naive: I know very well that you have to win games to buy yourself the time necessary to build something lasting.

“Everyone chooses their own path, and this is mine. Whether it will work or not, no one can know anything now, but I’m really happy because this reflects exactly who I am and the type of work I want to do.

“I understand the questions you ask me: there are days when, in the morning, I think that perhaps I could have had more experience, more calm, a larger squad, fewer injuries. But at the end of the day, I was the one who chose this job, so I’m very happy with it and I’ll go all the way, straight down my path.

“You already know: if I embrace an idea, I certainly don’t change my mind. I can change the tactical system, sure, but I won’t change my conviction, and I’ll do everything I can to win and be successful here.”

What do you think of Inter? Did you speak to Chivu yesterday?

“He’s very nice. We were complaining a bit about the timing of the presentation because we’d played the night before: he won, so he was happier than me, but we had a match to prepare for, and that was our priority.

“But I think we were all really enthusiastic about the new stadium, I think everyone can say that. Inter, for me, is that team we were talking about: a team that can play with their eyes closed, that rotates, like in the last match in midfield, and continues to express themselves in exactly the same way.

“Certain individual characteristics change, but they already know what to do on the pitch even without needing to practice too much. They have an extraordinary ability to read the moments of the game.

“You can clearly sense that, even if they find themselves behind late on, they have the maturity to understand that they can win any match: it’s an awareness you gain when you win so many matches and so many titles.

“They have a lot of talent in their squad, but we’re there to fight against that too and we’ll be ready for the match.”

Goncalo Ramos scored a brace against you in a derby between Benfica and Sporting CP with you on the bench. Could he also be the man for the Milan derby?

“I think it could be. I think all the problems and difficulties Goncalo Ramos is experiencing are primarily a team issue. I think we need to push forward… We control games well, but we play too far from the opponent’s goal.

“We need to raise our centre of gravity and the team on the pitch, spending more time in that area of ​​the pitch to allow Ramos to play his game. He’s playing far from the goal because the team can’t push the opponent in the final thirty metres. I think we need time to correct this aspect as well.

“We want to do better and we will improve in this area. But in any match, a player like Goncalo Ramos, who has already shown he can break the deadlock at any moment and score in any situation, can be the man of the match in the next one.”

Why did you reduce the squad so much at the start of the season? Do you regret it?

“When it happens, you think about it, but these are things you can’t control. There are factors that are beyond your control. Think about Saelemaekers, who had that physical problem practically at the end of a training match during the international break.

“Then Chukwueze comes along with another problem, and as a result Moreira ends up being overworked because he’s forced to play more minutes since Chukwueze and Saelemaekers aren’t there. Imagine having three players for a single position and all three get injured: at that point you overload that department.

“And then you take that player and make your rotations, and you find yourself fielding Tomori… well, I’m not saying as a winger, but adapted to emergency situations, maybe having him do an extra 400-meter sprint.

“Then obviously I take the risk because I want to win against Bologna, I know perfectly well that we can’t afford to lose other players and that certain characteristics were needed for that specific match. So I pushed for Tomori and we found ourselves with this problem.

“I think that sometimes Some things simply can’t be controlled; you have to imagine them. In my head, at the beginning, I had three options for that role, and all three had physical problems: when that happens, you can’t manage the impossible.

“Unless you have a squad of forty players, but then you have to deal with financial management, with each player’s playing time, or you have to accept taking some risks, taking a gamble. Today, if I could go back, maybe I’d want a few more players.

“But if we look at the situation rationally – and we have to be rational – the numbers at the start of the week told us we were fine with the rotations; but then in the last week, with three games in a few days, we found ourselves with six or seven starters or key players out of action.

“It’s a situation you can’t control. So, to answer the question: yes, maybe we’re short on resources right now, but there’s always a rational logic behind every decision made at the start of the season.”

Ramos gets fouled a lot. Should he become a bit more aggressive too?

“I like the way we’re going here in Italy, we’re on the right path. More speed, fewer fouls: I think it’s an evolution that’s improving the quality of the game. Sometimes you might think that maybe that could be a foul, but I like it that way.

“And then, I’m not going to talk about the referees: just take the images of the Udinese match and compare them with those of other games. I won’t say a single word more and you’ll understand exactly what I mean. But I won’t comment on the referees, I want to be crystal clear.

“I like this kind of approach, I like that in Serie A we see results like 5-1, 4-4, 3-3: I think it’s really a great advertisement for our country and for our movement. However, if you look at what happened with Udinese and compare it to other matches, well…

“Maybe it’s becoming difficult for Ramos to have more touches of the ball, maybe it’s difficult for Ramos to move freely inside the penalty area. Take the images, do the comparison, and then make your own evaluations and decisions.”

A bit of defensive fragility has also been evident in recent matches…

“In Bournemouth, we conceded two goals from penalties and one from a free kick, having been down a man for the entire match.

“Against Bologna, however, we conceded the fourth goal in seven minutes: we were completely out of position, and instead we had to maintain our identity until the last second, because we were buying time to reopen and turn the game around.

“Conceding a 3-2 is not the same as conceding a 4-2, and we still had plenty of time to recover. My feeling is that after the 3-2, we became anxious, starting to lose our composure and overturn our positions on the pitch.

“The central defenders going up with the ball, Pavlovic running forward with the ball, all these little flaws are details that we absolutely must correct in training, talking about them openly.

“It takes that collective maturity to understand that yes, the momentum of the game has changed, but we had 40 minutes left to fix it because we would have created chances anyway. Different situations, different dynamics. on which we must inevitably grow as a team: this is a clear fact.”

“Injuries and muscle problems have nothing to do with the medical staff, the gym staff, or Milan Lab. The responsibility lies with me, the coach, and the way he trains the team.

“What I can say is that there are several situations to consider: overloading certain areas forces us to take risks, and then we need to thoroughly analyse the medical history of individual players.

“Take, for example, those who say that a player like Cisse doesn’t play, scores a goal, and then misses the next match: we need to look at his history, the pubalgia problems, the serious injuries he’s suffered in the last year, and it’s my duty to manage him accordingly.

“We need to consider how many consecutive matches these players have played in the past, how many meters of sprint they complete, whether it’s the same type of effort or whether the game has changed.

“It’s the combination of all these factors that causes injuries. And then there’s bad luck: when you suffer a foot problem, it’s pure and simple bad luck. I’ll tell you: the episode that happened to Saelemaekers was just bad luck.

“When you find yourself With one less option at a certain position, you can’t rotate the players as you’d like, and then things happen. But I want to say this very clearly: the medical staff is extraordinary, they’re doing an exceptional job and they work hard every single day.

“Workload management is my job, it’s my responsibility. I know the medical history of every player on the roster, I know the daily workload, and I try to manage it as best I can. Sometimes, however, I’m forced to take risks, or there are guys who aren’t used to playing so many games in a row.

“Take a player like Jashari: he’s played three games, two of them 90 minutes, without any problems. Others, however, play a half and a half and are already feeling the fatigue.

“We try to monitor and manage all of this with the utmost attention, but I repeat: I’m very happy with our medical staff and they’re doing an incredible job.”

Why these ups and downs in performance and results?

“It’s a combination of factors that combine. Of course, we need to develop the maturity needed to control matches and manage the various moments of a match, and we’ll get there with time.

“Then there’s the busy schedule and the simultaneous injury crisis: a situation that doesn’t make our game any easier, because to perform at our best, we need certain technical and physical characteristics. I think it’s truly a combination of all of these.

“We need to pay attention to every single detail; we need to train more, spend more time talking and working on these aspects. But right now, we don’t even have time to fully recover, let alone do targeted training sessions.

“That’s why we’re having so many difficulties in some matches: to find that consistency of performance, we need to address and correct all these issues during the week, but we don’t have the time. Despite everything, I assure you that we will become a great team.”

Will the substitutions be one of the keys to the match? How is the team’s spirit?

“Regarding the second question, I sensed the right mentality in the players. Yesterday we were bitter, we were angry: more than bitter, we were truly furious. I myself was very angry yesterday, and it’s difficult to start over the next day.

“Today, however, I saw them completely focused on the match against Inter, concentrating on technical and tactical training, because that’s all that matters now. They were extremely attentive and have a clear understanding of the game plan and what needs to be done.

“In terms of attitude and mentality, this team is on the right path. If we analyze our performances, various types of difficulties emerge. There are games where we start terribly and finish on a high, like against Lazio, and others where we play excellent 66 minutes, like against Atalanta, only to then drop in intensity.

“Sometimes it’s more a question of psychological momentum than chronological playing time. The key is to find consistency throughout the entire 90 minutes. We don’t yet have the same experience and maturity as Inter: it’s It’s an obvious fact, and we must acknowledge it pragmatically.

“When Inter find themselves in trouble towards the end, they tend to fill the penalty area, pushing many players forward and using crosses to overload the opposition’s defenses. Our playing style is different; we’re not that type of team.

“So both tactical and technical aspects come into play. We simply need to focus on being consistent throughout the match, because so far we’ve shown different sides depending on the game: sometimes we start strong and then fade, other times we start poorly like against Lazio and improve in the second half.

“We need to become a better team, more mature and capable of reading and interpreting the various moments of the game.”
