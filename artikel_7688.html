No right to Vegemite in prison, Australian judge rules

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

A judge in Australia ruled Monday that a convicted murderer does not have the right to get Vegemite in prison because of security risks. (Photo from Shutterstock)

Vegemite, the ubiquitous savory spread beloved by Australians, is too much of a security risk to allow a convicted murderer to eat it in prison, despite his claims that it’s a human right, an Australian judge has ruled.

Justice Claire Harris of the Supreme Court of Victoria in Australia ruled Monday that Vegemite’s scent had the potential to throw off drug sniffing dogs and could be used to brew alcohol, thus outweighing Andre McKechnie’s right to have it in prison, the New York Times reports.

McKechnie, serving a life sentence, filed a lawsuit last year in the Supreme Court of Victoria, arguing that the state’s ban on serving the salty, yeasty condiment, denied him his “culture as an Australian.”

In his suit, McKechnie noted that neighboring New South Wales did not ban Vegemite from its prisons and argued that not eating it made him feel “sad and disconnected from my family and community,” according to the New York Times.

“The limitation on Mr. McKechnie’s ability to enjoy Vegemite in community with other persons of Australian background is primarily that he is a prisoner,” she wrote. “It is difficult to accept that the inability to consume Vegemite, rather than the fact of his imprisonment, is a material cause of his sadness and disconnection from his family and community.”
