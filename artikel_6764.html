Samantha Morton on Being a Woman in Hollywood: ‘We Are Here as a Family’

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Odyssey costume designer Ellen Mirojnick delivered some of the actress’s remarks on her behalf.

Yesterday evening, The Odyssey star Samantha Morton was unable to attend ELLE’s 2026 Women in Hollywood celebration, remaining at home in London instead. But the honoree had the film’s costume designer Ellen Mirojnick deliver some of her moving remarks on her behalf. Morton reflected on the honor of playing Circe in The Odyssey—and the greater lesson she has learned about what being a woman in Hollywood means, decades into her acting career.

She wrote, “Thank you so much to Nina and ELLE for this amazing honor. And to Ralph Lauren and Bulgari for supporting this wonderful event. My huge thanks to Alex White, Norman Jean Roy, and the wonderful team who made me feel so fantastic at the cover shoot. To Jenny, Nicki, Kat, Billy, Scott, and Ashton for being there for me every step of the way and for helping to make my dreams come true.

“To be even a small part of a phenomenon like The Odyssey was an experience that cannot be matched. Circe is a role that I felt deeply blessed to play, to bring her voice to life for Christopher and Emma and their incredible team, John Papsidera, Ellen Mirojnick, Luisa Abel, and Andrew Jackson.

“And to get to work with my favorite actor in the whole world, Matt Damon, the peerless Matt Damon…the most suave, sophisticated, talented, handsome, generous, kind, and gifted Matt Damon. Thank you for helping me to find Circe on that Scottish hillside and for standing in for me this evening. It takes a village and I am certain this wouldn’t have happened without all of these wonderful people and more…I am so desperately sad not to be able to be there with you all to celebrate.

“I want to congratulate the other amazing women being honored tonight. What an incredibly talented and formidable group. Rosalind, my Harlots sister…you inspire me so much.

“And as women in this industry, we know what we do isn’t easy. Yes, we love it, we are passionate about it and we are fortunate to do it; it’s great when it all comes together. But we know this isn’t always the case and there can be many obstacles and pitfalls along the way in an ever-changing and sometimes cruel business.

“I keep asking myself what it means to be a ‘Woman in Hollywood.’ It was a childhood dream for me to be here. To work in this business. To act in films that would travel the world and be remembered forever. For almost 30 years I have visited and worked here and for a time I called this city home, returning every year to the same house in Beechwood Canyon, a house built by Dolores del Río, the first Mexican movie star, yet I never fully felt that I belonged, that I had ‘made it.’

“Tonight shows me that to truly be a woman in Hollywood is not about location but community. It doesn’t matter where you come from…your race, your faith, your sexuality, your social or economic background. We are here as a family, a community of creatives, and we must support each other, help each other, and fight for our right to work in a safe environment. To be treated fairly and respectfully. To keep pushing for fair and equal pay and parity for female actors and filmmakers. We will get there.

“And ultimately to pass on the baton and because of this I want to thank my beautiful daughters Esmé and Edie for their brilliance and support and the difference their outspoken and courageous generation is making to our glorious industry.

“I dedicate this award to all the women in our community, fighting to carve out their own path and to create, free from fear and exploitation.
