Strong ‘I Play Rocky,’ ‘How to Rob a Bank’ KO and More: Amazon MGM Distribution Head on 2026 Highs and Lows

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Kevin Wilson tells The Hollywood Reporter about the 'Rocky' origin story's "great start," 'How to Rob a Bank' underperforming ("Still trying to figure it out") and the 'Ali G' strategy: "That theatrical hurdle is just tough."

Amazon MGM Studios‘ team is relishing the thrill of the theatrical fight as I Play Rocky looks to capitalize on its muscular start.

Peter Farrelly’s fact-based underdog drama stars Anthony Ippolito as Sylvester Stallone amid his battle in the 1970s to get his script for Rocky into production with himself in the lead role. After two weekends in limited release, I Play Rocky rose to No. 2 at the domestic box office as it expanded wide last weekend. It is expected to gross roughly $9 million for the five-day Thanksgiving frame, which would bring its North American cume to around $22 million through Sunday. I Play Rocky is the latest release in a buzzy year for the studio, following the mammoth success of this spring’s Project Hail Mary — set for rerelease Dec. 11 as awards season comes out swinging — and a winning path for Verity. Amazon MGM also sparked recent conversation with its unusual release plan for Sacha Baron Cohen’s Ali G: Who Iz I?, which received a two-day theatrical run just before hitting Prime Video, and for its tough turnout for David Leitch’s How to Rob a Bank, the $40 million-budgeted heist film that opened earlier this month with just $5.1 million domestically.

During a conversation with The Hollywood Reporter conducted ahead of Thanksgiving, Amazon MGM head of domestic theatrical distribution Kevin Wilson shares insight into I Play Rocky’s broad appeal and the hopes that it continues to find younger audiences after that impressive A+ CinemaScore. Wilson also weighs in on the releases for How to Rob a Bank, Ali G and David O. Russell’s Madden, not to mention the company’s big James Bond casting news.

How are you feeling with I Play Rocky now playing wide?

It’s a great start. From the first time we saw the movie — and really from the first time I read the script — I knew we had a special story. What Pete [Farrelly] and the filmmaking team were able to put on screen, coupled with Anthony Ippolito’s performance, we got magic. As we went [wide last] weekend, we saw the audience response, which is always good just to validate our own intuition. With that A+ CinemaScore and a 97 percent in the top two boxes [on PostTrak] and an 88 definite recommend, we always hoped that this thing would have legs. We’re pretty confident.

The movie was the subject of Oscar talk ever since premiering at TIFF.

Something we’re consistently seeing is the ability of Anthony to portray Sly, [and] that is not the easiest character in the world to play. It’s one of those feel-good, perseverance stories that doesn’t come around that often. The unique thing about this one is, we rolled it out, and we saw good numbers in New York and L.A. and, of course, Philly, but it’s interesting to see [now that it’s wide]. I’m not super surprised, but the movie’s playing broad from a geographical standpoint. It’s playing solid across the country. We’ve got theaters in Redlands, California, in the top 10 — Goodyear, Arizona [and] Santa Clarita [in California]. It’s performing really well in the South Central part of the country and those Texas regions. We are getting a nice percentage of the Hispanic audience in.

I know that Hollywood loves movies about Hollywood, but it’s sometimes hard to predict if broad audiences will, too.

I think it just goes to [show], these are Rocky fans. They’re fans of Sly, and they’re fans of the Rocky franchise. We’re definitely playing a little bit older right now, which is not a surprise — 53 percent of our audience is 45-plus [as of last weekend]. As we get into Thanksgiving, the hope is that maybe that age playability comes down a little bit. I do think it could be one of those Thanksgiving films in the marketplace that’s a good choice for adult families to all agree on a movie.

Another movie focusing on a public figure and sparking social buzz is Madden. Was there talk of putting that one in theaters?

That movie was always greenlit as a streaming movie. That was always the plan, to release it in that Thanksgiving corridor at home for audiences. Christian Bale’s performance is unbelievable in that movie.

Speaking of Rocky, your year got off to a great start with Project Hail Mary, which continues to be part of the Oscar conversation.

We’re really excited. I came over from the MGM team, so I’ve always been releasing theatrical movies. But it’s our first big year at Amazon MGM putting a pretty robust slate together, and to have that movie pop the way it did early on in this theatrical journey, obviously, was a really good thing. Between [directors] Chris [Miller] and Phil [Lord] and [star] Ryan [Gosling], what they were able to put on screen was really something special. We’re rereleasing it back in theaters on Dec. 11th, and we’ll have some IMAX screens and back in 70mm in locations. Just get that audience back out to see it in a theater where it deserves to be seen. We’ll see what happens.

Any thoughts on what happened with How to Rob a Bank?

Tough one. Obviously, not what we were hoping for and certainly not what our expectations were [with] releasing this film theatrically. It’s a tough marketplace. That’s the unique thing about the marketplace right now, is there’s no floor on these movies. It was tough to connect with a younger audience on this movie, and we’re still trying to figure it out a little bit internally, why this thing didn’t at least click for that younger audience in a bigger way. Have you seen the movie?

The movie’s fun. Nick [Hoult] and Pete [Davidson] and that crew, it’s fun to watch, but then Zoë [Kravitz] and John C. Reilly, that relationship is really good in the movie. It’s a little bit of a head-scratcher, and we’re still living in that world where the theatrical hurdle is not small. Trying to convince audiences to get off their couch, to get off YouTube, to get off all the streaming services — it’s not an easy thing to do. For this particular movie, it was just tough to get audiences into a theater. The unique thing about Amazon MGM, comparative to some other studios — and it’s not an excuse or deflecting the underperformance theatrically — but we have the second biggest streaming platform on the planet. The life cycle of our films are a little bit different than other studios. So I’m really hopeful that that’s where audiences are going to find this movie.

How did the team decide on the unique strategy for Ali G: Who Iz I?, with just the two-day theatrical run?

We always wanted to do some sort of a theatrical event with that movie. That was always the plan. We know he has this rabid fan base, so we wanted to create an event in theaters. We decided just to do that two-night event in theaters and then get it to Prime Video as quickly as possible. It’s just like we’ve seen with the Jackass movie and some of these other comps: That theatrical hurdle is just tough. To convince a really wide audience that they need to go see a film like that in theaters is not the easiest thing to do. We came up with that event-style release and got it to Prime Video as quickly as we could, and the performance on Prime Video has been solid.

Your team certainly dominated headlines recently with Robert Pattinson’s casting as Bond.

Super, super excited for that one. I got to release No Time to Die, which is Daniel [Craig’s] last movie [as Bond]. This one’s going to be really fun — a reinvention of the franchise, and it’ll be super interesting as this cast starts to fill out.

I’ve gone full circle with Rob Pattinson. I was at Summit Entertainment and released the Twilight movies way back in the day. God, I think he was 17 at the time. It’s been a fun journey to see his career, and now his Bond will be great.
