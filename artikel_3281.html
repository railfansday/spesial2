8 Great Anime Movies Worth Watching Again and Again

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Anime has become one of the most popular media in the world because of its revolutionary storytelling, creative ideas, and mesmerizing animation. From classics such as Akira to modern sensations like The Boy and the Heron, anime films are regarded as some of the most innovative. With big budgets and gorgeous animation, these stories are a step above everything else that is put out.

But when an anime movie is so good, fans can't just watch it once; they need to view it over and over again. That is why this list will feature eight great anime films that are worth watching again and again based on aspects such as rewatchability, entertainment value, easter eggs, details, themes best understood a second time, critical acclaim, fan opinion, and overall quality.

Studio Ghibli is arguably the greatest animation studio of all time, meaning they have a few entries on this list, starting with one of their more underrated films, Porco Rosso. Taking place in 1930s Italy, the titular character is a retired war veteran cursed with the head of a pig. He makes his money as a bounty hunter, but when an arrogant American pilot challenges him to a duel, both his honor and pride are at stake.

It may be one of Studio Ghibli's lesser-known gems, but it is one of the most rewatchable. Porco Rosso has an incredibly relaxed vibe, letting viewers soak in the Italian sun and coast with amazing views. This atmospheric style alone makes it worth a rewatch, but the anime film goes a step further with its dogfights and mature themes that deal with grief, survivors' guilt, and anti-fascist wit.

Shinichiro Watanabe is one of the greatest anime directors of all time, and while he is best known for his TV shows, he also has a film of his most popular work, Cowboy Bebop. The film, Cowboy Bebop: The Movie, is set between episodes 22 and 23 and follows the crew of bounty hunters on yet another mission. In pursuit of a mysterious terrorist, they must take him down before he releases a deadly nanobot pathogen on Mars.

Cowboy Bebop is one of the greatest anime series of all time, and while the movie can't live up to that, it is still an amazing story. Cowboy Bebop: The Movie doesn't try to deliver a grand story like most film adaptations of shows, instead acting as a cool, moody, and stylish film. The setting is absolutely gorgeous, and the animation is among some of the best hand-drawn in the medium, creating an endlessly rewatchable jazz fest.

Anime became so popular because of its remarkable animation, and one film that embodies this better than most is Promare. Decades after humanity gained the ability to control fire, it has split into two factions. The firefighters clash with the militia group, each pushing their ideals on the other, which uncovers a dark conspiracy hiding underneath the city.

From a visual standpoint, there aren't many anime movies better than Promare, which is a striking masterpiece and color fest that displays its vibrant palette and over-the-top style. It isn't known for its story, but fans can still watch it over and over again because of its flawless animation. The distinct geometry, neon colors, and cel-shading meld together to create one of the most gorgeous anime films that fans can't help but watch a few times.

This list features some critically acclaimed masterpieces, and one of the most famous is Ghost in the Shell, which has received a few different adaptations, including a modern one, but none compare to the original. Motoko is the leader of an anti-terrorist group that stops cyber threats, and their newest mission involves tracking down a killer that uses other people's bodies to commit crimes.

The newest show is already one of the best anime series of the year, but while it is more accurate to the manga, the original film is still viewed as the best. Ghost in the Shell is a philosophical masterclass that explores identity through the lens of the shell of a human, learning about the world and their part in it. The dense, atmospheric, and meticulously designed world is a hallmark of the cyberpunk genre, and fans can't get enough of it. Not only that, but the complex narrative requires multiple viewings to truly appreciate.

Anime is no stranger to weirdness; in fact, it is a core trait of the medium, and some of the best films excel at this, like Mind Game. This unique anime movie is about an aspiring manga artist who is killed by the yakuza. But after arguing with god and escaping heaven, he rewrites his own past and death, but this only leads to weirder situations, like being stuck in the belly of a whale with his childhood crush.

Be prepared for one of the most chaotic and nonsensical experiences, because Mind Game is a cluster of bizarre imagery and distinct scenery. However, this unique identity is exactly its appeal, depicting a hidden message that is quite profound and well portrayed when fans analyze it through the animation. However, to do this, viewers will need to watch this movie over and over again, finding something new each time.

As mentioned, Studio Ghibli is home to some of the greatest anime movies, and one of their more recognized masterpieces is Howl's Moving Castle. Sophie is a young girl, but when her beauty catches the attention of an evil witch, she is given the body of a 90-year-old. Sophie seeks the titular Howl, a vain wizard fighting in a massive war that is threatening to destroy the entire kingdom.

Almost every single Studio Ghibli film is perfect to watch again and again, but Howl's Moving Castle is something even more special, creating a cozy vibe that fans can keep going back to as a comfort movie. However, this is more than just a relaxing film, because the painterly art style provides an unmistakably mesmerizing animation style that lets viewers get lost in its beauty. More than that, Howl's Moving Castle is a timeless coming-of-age story that made it one of the best animated films of the 2000s.

Satoshi Kon is a legendary anime director with an impressive catalog of films that are all perfect to watch over and over again, but his most rewatchable is also his magnum opus, Perfect Blue. After leaving the idol business to become a movie star, Mima starts suffering from illusions, not being able to tell reality from fantasy after being under immense stress from the job, her former friends' success, and a stalker who is threatening her life.

Perfect Blue is one of the most engaging thrillers you will ever watch, progressively getting more and more desperate, haunting, and riveting as it goes on. It keeps viewers on the edge of their seats as they ponder what's real and what isn't; the final plot twist completely recontextualizes the film. After it, fans have to watch Perfect Blue again to further analyze its perfection, and after that, they realize how flawless it is, and most watch it again and again.

Animation is usually the main appeal of anime and what makes it so rewatchable, and that is why Redline is at the top of this list. The titular race happens every so often, pitting the greatest racers in the galaxy against each other. This year, it is being held on a totalitarian planet adamant about keeping the race out of the way. With the protagonist finally getting his chance at stardom, he and the other racers battle against the planet's military.

Redline is a universally loved movie, with all those who have seen it claiming it to be one of the best animated works ever. This movie is a fever dream that fans won't want to wake up from, delivering an exhilarating ride with high-octane action that refuses to take its foot off the gas. Dialling it up to eleven, Redline boasts a spectacular visual style and impeccable animation that viewers can get lost in, enjoying it even more every time they watch it.
