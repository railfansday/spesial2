A “Bad Call” Or “Can’t Wait”? Industry & Media React To Robert Pattinson’s Casting As James Bond

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Refresh for updates… When it came to who should play the next James Bond, everyone had an opinion.

Now that the seventh Bond has been revealed as Robert Pattinson, plenty of people are sharing new opinions. We’re still waiting to hear from some of the bigger names connected to the franchise but below are some of the early reactions to the news.

Amazon’s President and CEO Andy Jassy said he was “excited to have” Pattinson as the next Bond and described him as “one of the most versatile actors of his generation, who consistently makes fearless choices, and keeps surprising people with his performances.”

Excited to have Robert Pattinson as the next James Bond. The Ian Fleming Estate took to social media to congratulate the actor on his selection, saying they were “delighted to welcome Robert Pattinson to the James Bond family.” Watch on Deadline

“As Bond himself reflects at the end of Moonraker when facing the fact that the object of his affections is engaged to another man, ‘He must play the role which she expected of him. The tough man of the world. The Secret Agent. The man who was only a silhouette.’ We look forward to Pattinson stepping into that silhouette and making it his own. We have no doubt that Ian Fleming would be amazed at the longevity of his creation and the fact that the world is still hungry for tales of the world’s greatest spy. As the custodians of Ian Fleming’s literary work, we wish Pattinson and everyone associated with the film the very best of luck. And as die-hard fans, we can’t wait for Bond 26.”

Never afraid to wade in, presenter Piers Morgan said casting Pattinson was “a bad call”, primarily because the actor is also starring as Batman. “Batman can’t be Bond too,” Morgan wrote. “They should have gone for an unknown. Bad call.”

Ralph Fiennes — who plays M in the latest Bonds — and Jesse Plemons were live on with Andy Cohen and others on SiriusXM’s Front Row channel when the Pattinson news broke. “What are our reactions?” Chen asked the pair. “Jesse’s upset because he was up for the role.” Plemons replied, “You saw it all over my face.” Fiennes then said: “I’m sorry, I’m in love with Daniel Craig still. So it’s a hard, it’s hard.” Cohen said, “You’re from the Daniel Craig class.” Fiennes replied about Pattinson: “So no, he’s a great actor. Christopher Hogg, Senior Lecturer in Screen Studies at the University of Westminster, had some interesting thoughts on the subject, calling it “a clear shift in casting strategy for the iconic character.”

“The announcement of Robert Pattinson as Bond marks a clear shift in casting strategy for the iconic character in the franchise’s new Amazon MGM era. Whilst previous actors cast in the role had established star profiles, their fame has never been on this scale prior to donning the tuxedo and pointing the Walther PPK. Pattinson is unquestionably a talented actor with varied screen credits that demonstrate his abilities in both emotional complexity and full-throttle action. However, the background resonances of Twilight and Batman will ring loud when he reaches the screen as Bond. Pattinson’s casting confirms Amazon’s ‘commercial metrics’ approach is now being applied to Bond’s screen future.”

He continued: “The choice also makes sense in terms of established working relationships, with the new Bond director Denis Villeneuve having very recently worked with Pattinson on Dune: Part Three. The casting choice certainly marks a decisive change of approach. People were very quick to criticise the casting of Daniel Craig in the first few months, however, and what followed was both critical and commercial success. Let’s keep an open mind, for now.”

The Night Agent and The Shield showrunner Shawn Ryan is a fan of the casting, saying “the best actor is never a bad choice”:

Missing in this discussion is that Pattinson has just proven himself lately to be an extraordinary actor. Essentially stole The Odyssey. So great in so much stuff lately. Other options are wonderful but RP is on a different level right now. Best actor is never a bad choice. https://t.co/dyRtgDTvY9

Some have noted that Pattinson, who turns 41 next May, will be one of the older Bonds by the time his first movie comes out. The Walking Dead actor David Morrissey acknowledged the age factor but enthused that the former Twilight and Tenet star will be “a brilliant Bond”:

Bill Patterson will be a brilliant James Bond! Tad older than usual but the twinkle in the eye is a winner!

A number of critics and online film commentators are also having their say. Online critic and YouTuber Will Jordan, known on X as The Critical Drinker, expressed a level of bemusement over the casting, calling it “an odd choice. Predictable and safe in some ways (high profile, established actor) but weird in others (already 40 years old, not especially physical or masculine, and already committed to another franchise with Batman). Hmm…”

An odd choice. Predictable and safe in some ways (high profile, established actor) but weird in others (already 40 years old, not especially physical or masculine, and already committed to another franchise with Batman). Hmm… https://t.co/nNqpG92L33

South American critic and festival programmer Diego Lerer disagreed with the notion that Pattinson was unsuited for the role because he also portrays Batman, noting that Harrison Ford juggled two iconic characters in Han Solo and Indiana Jones:

Para los que se quejan de que Robert Pattinson no puede ser Batman y James Bond a la vez les recuerdo que un tal Harrison Ford fue Han Solo e Indiana Jones al mismo tiempo y no fue problema para nadie… 🤣

Many are starting to speculate on who could play the Bond villains or love interests. Trade veteran and LA Times writer Tim Grierson playfully suggested he’d like to see an on-screen reunion for Pattinson and Kristen Stewart:

There are many reasons why this will never happen or should happen. But I can't stop thinking about how intriguing it would be to cast Kristen Stewart as James Bond's love interest — someone who's his equal, like Eva Green in CASINO ROYALE. Or let her be the super-cool villain.

Roger Ebert associate editor and writer Robert Daniels wasn’t alone in predicting that we won’t know whether Pattinson works or not until we see him on screen as Bond for the first time:

I’m old enough to remember when people hated Daniel Craig being cast a Bond. Bond is probably the one role where people routinely don’t know who’d actually work until they see it.

Meanwhile, as often happens, the internet is having fun with a cultural moment. Aggregation site Discussing Film points out that the Twilight movies “predicted” Pattinson’s casting in a scene where a character name drops James Bond and the camera immediately cuts to Pattinson’s character Edward Cullen.
