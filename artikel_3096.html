Bloom Energy Stock Has Crashed Nearly 60%. Is It a Once-in-a-Decade Opportunity at $145, or Should You Keep Waiting for a Bigger Drop?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Bloom Energy has plunged about 60% from its peak, even as revenue and profits surge. Is it a screaming buy or still expensive?

By Matt DiLallo – Nov 22, 2026 at 9:16AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Bloom Energy ›

Bloom Energy (BE -15.30%) has gone from market darling to one of its biggest losers. After surging earlier this year as investors bet on its fuel cells to power artificial intelligence (AI) data centers, the stock has crashed about 60% from its peak and recently traded around $145 per share. The decline came even as the business hit new records. Bloom's revenue more than doubled last quarter, and its profits surged.

So, has the sell-off created a once-in-a-decade opportunity to buy this energy stock? Or should you keep waiting for a bigger drop? Here's what the numbers say.

Bloom's third-quarter results were its best yet. The fuel cell maker's revenue jumped 138% to $1.2 billion, and it posted a net profit of $250.2 million, up from a $23 million loss in the year-ago period. Management raised its full-year guidance for the third time this year. Bloom now expects $4.4 billion to $4.6 billion of revenue, about 122% growth at the midpoint, and adjusted earnings of $3.10 to $3.40 per share.

The demand story is simple. AI data centers need reliable electricity, and waiting years for grid upgrades isn't an option. Bloom's modular fuel cells can generate power on site, and customers including Oracle, CoreWeave, and Equinix already use them. With more data center developers turning to on-site power solutions, Bloom has a massive growth opportunity.

Bloom's skyrocketing revenue and insatiable customer demand had sent its stock soaring until earlier this summer. However, several things hit the stock at once. The Federal Reserve's rate hike in September made it more expensive to finance new data center projects, a delay at Oracle's Project Jupiter AI campus raised questions about the project's timing, and the Democratic sweep of the midterms also raised the prospect of tighter data center regulation. Most recently, the AI sell-off that followed Anthropic's IPO dragged down nearly every AI infrastructure name.

A nearly 60% drop sounds like a bargain, but Bloom's valuation is still demanding. At $145, its market cap is about $42.9 billion. That has it trading at roughly 9.5x this year's revenue guidance and about 45x adjusted earnings, neither of which screams "bargain." The stock is also still up over 60% for the year, so this is a correction of a very large run-up, not a collapse in the business.

The risks are also real. Cash flow from operations was negative $27 million last quarter, before capital spending. Bloom also faces a potential slowdown in AI spending, or tighter financing for data center projects, which could hit demand.

As the bull case shows, Bloom could deliver extraordinary returns if it reaches its full potential. However, a more conservative base-case payoff isn't very attractive for an investment with this much uncertainty. That's why I'd rather wait until shares drop to $120, when the return potential would be more enticing.

Bloom has made tremendous progress, and its opportunity to power data centers and other electricity-hungry customers could support years of growth.

However, a roughly 60% decline doesn't automatically make the stock a bargain. At $145, Bloom still carries a valuation that assumes rapid growth and improving profitability. I wouldn't call it a once-in-a-decade opportunity at today's price. I'd rather wait for a larger margin of safety, closer to $120, before buying more.
