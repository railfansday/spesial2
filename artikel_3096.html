Not Micron, Not Sandisk. This Is My Top Memory Stock for 2027.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

SK Hynix is bigger than Micron and Sandisk in the DRAM and NAND flash markets, which is why it has the potential to outperform them next year.

By Harsh Chauhan – Nov 18, 2026 at 2:01PM EST

Micron Technology and Sandisk have sizzled on the stock market in 2026. Both stocks have delivered impressive gains this year, despite witnessing a significant pullback in recent months amid the broader correction in tech stocks.

Micron stock, for instance, has gained 128% in 2026, as of this writing. Sandisk's gains are even bigger at 194%. Importantly, both memory companies can step on the gas once again in 2027, driven by the artificial intelligence (AI)-fueled demand for compute and storage.

However, I think that Micron and Sandisk could be overshadowed by South Korean memory giant SK Hynix (SKHY -0.88%) next year. Let's look at the reasons why.

SK Hynix sells both dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) and NAND flash memory. Counterpoint Research points out that the company controlled 29% of the DRAM market in the third quarter of 2026. It is second only to Samsung in this market. Micron, for comparison, has a 21% share of the DRAM market.

Meanwhile, SK Hynix's 19% share of NAND flash puts it ahead of both Micron and Sandisk, which have market shares of 15% and 11%, respectively. The South Korean company's healthy market shares in both DRAM and NAND flash explain why it reported phenomenal growth in Q3. SK Hynix's revenue surged 328% year over year to $79 billion, easily beating the consensus estimate.

The company's operating profit jumped 632% from the year-ago period and exceeded analysts' expectations. SK Hynix is well-placed to sustain such impressive growth. That's because the company has already sold more than half of its production capacity for 2027 and 2028 under long-term agreements with 14 customers.

Moreover, SK Hynix anticipates that memory demand will outstrip supply through 2030, indicating that the favorable environment fueling the company's stunning growth is sustainable. Also, SK Hynix is ramping up production of its next-generation high-bandwidth memory (HBM) chips, known as HBM4.

It points out that HBM4 has twice the bandwidth of the previous-generation HBM3E chips and 40% higher power efficiency. SK Hynix notes that HBM4 revenue and shipments will surpass those of HBM3E in the current quarter, which should bode well for the company's margins. That's because HBM4 reportedly carries a 24% premium per gigabyte (GB) over the previous generation, HBM3E.

What's more, SK Hynix controls a third of the global HBM market, higher than Micron's 18% share. This is another reason to buy SK Hynix, considering that the global HBM market's revenue could surge to a whopping $641 billion in 2030, according to Bloomberg Intelligence. The firm has significantly upgraded this guidance from its earlier estimate of $260 billion, noting that the average price of HBM will more than double over the next four years.

So, there is a strong likelihood that SK Hynix's revenue growth will outpace Wall Street's expectations.

SK Hynix's lead over Micron and Sandisk in DRAM and NAND flash should ideally give it greater pricing power over its rivals. This probably explains why analysts expect SK Hynix's earnings per share to increase at a much faster pace in the long run.

Also, SK Hynix's valuation is in line with Micron and Sandisk's multiples.

So, even though SK Hynix stock hasn't made a bright start on the U.S. stock market following its listing on the Nasdaq this year, it remains worth buying on account of its red-hot growth, cheap valuation, and the ability to outperform Micron and Sandisk. In the end, I won't be surprised to see this AI stock's fortunes turn around in the New Year, and buying it while it trades at cheap multiples seems prudent.
