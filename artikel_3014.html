Why Isn’t Alphabet Stock Taking Off After Reporting Record Cloud Growth?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The company's impressive Google Cloud gains and soaring profits weren’t enough to impress investors because the AI build-out is eating its cash.

By Patrick Sanders – Nov 18, 2026 at 2:47PM EST

You would think that in today's "show me" era of artificial intelligence and the stock market, Alphabet (GOOG +1.32%) (GOOGL +1.46%) would be flying high. The third-quarter earnings report it delivered on Oct. 28 showed $30.7 billion in revenue from Google Cloud, a 103% gain from the prior-year period.

But that bounce didn't happen. Alphabet stock actually slipped in the next day’s trading session, and has posted only a modest 2% gain since that earnings report.

The explanation for Wall Street’s relative lack of enthusiasm may be buried in Alphabet's cash flow statement, where the cost of building a hyperscale AI data center business apparently is raising some red flags among investors.

Google Cloud has become a substantial profit generator for Alphabet. The division produced $11.1 billion in operating income during the third quarter, more than triple the $3.6 billion it reported a year earlier. Its 36% operating margin was an impressive figure for a business growing at triple-digit rates.

Demand for Google's AI infrastructure is driving much of that growth. The company cited increased demand for enterprise AI solutions and its custom Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), which offer customers a more cost-effective alternative to traditional graphics processing units.

Meanwhile, Alphabet's advertising business remains healthy. Google Search revenue rose 12% to $63.3 billion, helping push total company revenue to $127.6 billion.

The problem isn't finding customers. It's paying for the infrastructure needed to serve them.

Alphabet spent $51.4 billion on capital expenditures during the third quarter, more than double the $24 billion it spent a year earlier. That spending exceeded the $42.3 billion the company generated in operating cash flow, resulting in negative free cash flow of $9.1 billion.

And this wasn't an isolated quarter. Alphabet reported negative free cash flow of $5.9 billion in the second quarter, following positive free cash flow of $10.1 billion in the first.

The company is investing heavily in data centers, servers, and infrastructure to meet demand for AI capacity. And while those investments will likely produce substantial returns over time, the bill needs to be paid today — and investors apparently are balking.

Alphabet's cash flow problems shouldn't be confused with a struggling business. Operating income increased 30% during the third quarter to $40.6 billion, while the company finished September with $265.1 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities on its books.

That's considerable financial flexibility. But Alphabet's trailing-12-month free cash flow has fallen to $19.7 billion, despite record revenue and operating profits.

Investors have good reason to appreciate Google Cloud's extraordinary growth. They also have reason to wonder how much of that growth will eventually translate into profits.

Until Alphabet can demonstrate that its infrastructure investments are generating sufficient returns to justify their cost, even another outstanding quarter of cloud growth might not be enough to move the stock.
