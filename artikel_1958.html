Newcastle stadium meeting reveals ‘very positive sign’ as PIF’s finance plan clear – Ex-CEO

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Newcastle United are set to fund the building of a new stadium using “debt from the market” rather than PIF’s own funding into the project.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that Goldman Sachs’ involvement in the project is a “very positive sign.”

This comes after the news that representatives from the American investment bank were present on Tyneside recently for a meeting with the club hierarchy over the new stadium plans.

Wyness outlined that the debt Newcastle are set to take on to fund the project is nothing to worry about for fans, given that will be invested into new revenue streams.

Recent reports have estimated that the Magpies’ new ground is likely to cost around £1.3billion to build, with a planned capacity of around 65,000.

The North East outfit have played at their current St James’ Park home since 1892, but are now in line to move to a new site at Leazes Park within the next few years.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy business advising elite clubs – believes that Newcastle’s new ground will become a “showpiece stadium” for the North East.

The Scottish businessman is pleased to see the club planning to use Goldman Sachs to help fund the project, rather than solely relying on PIF’s own finances.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider’s Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained how the debt from loans for the stadium would be good debt to take on.

“Well it's interesting with Goldman being present because it does point to the fact that this would be financed with debt from the market rather than PIF actually funding it themselves,” he said.

“So that means that this would not be PIF investment purely into the club which is what we talked about last week. It's actually a very positive sign that this would be debt on the club.

“Because, they could afford it with a new stadium and we've always discussed the fact that when you go take on more debt, as long as you're putting it into things that are increasing your revenue streams, that's okay.

“It's when you start taking on debt to pay costs is the real problem. In this case, I'm sure it is going to be a showpiece stadium. It's been 18 months I've been talking about this now. So we're all keen to see it.”

Former Manchester City financial advisor Stefan Borson recently told Football Insider that Newcastle could lose around “£75million” each year just from interest rates on their loans for the stadium project.

The football finance expert pointed to the 2.5 per cent interest that Tottenham paid when building their new stadium, and outlined that Newcastle would be lucky to pay less than double that in today’s financial climate.

Borson believes that the loan needed for Newcastle’s stadium plans could creep up to the £1.5bn mark by the time yearly interest is factored into the total.

The Magpies’ hierarchy are maintaining that a new ground is a necessary step to help the club reach the next level, but it is set to cost an eye-watering sum of money.
