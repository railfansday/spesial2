Ariana Grande Says “Insane Perception” of “Petal” Music Video Felt Like ‘Black Mirror’ and Intention Was “Me Sharing a Vulnerable Thing”

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

"That was just really interesting. But I feel so proud and grateful. I wouldn't change a thing. I would put it out again today exactly as is. I wouldn't change a thing," the Oscar nominee said of the music video that sparked conversation when it was released in late July.

Ariana Grande is addressing the online public discourse that erupted following the release of her “Petal” music video, admitting the commentary felt akin to Black Mirror and she hoped it would be received as her “sharing a vulnerable thing.”

The Oscar nominee appeared on Bowen Yang and Matt Rogers’ Las Culturistas podcast Wednesday, where they discussed the video for the title track of her eighth studio album Petal. Yang brought up the character Grande plays in the video, an auditioning actress named Pepper who uses a chainsaw to kill people in the end after receiving repeated criticism from those she’s auditioning for, and said: “There’s something about that that is so modern about someone in your position or really anyone who is being told to be more authentic.” Related Stories Movies 'Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping' to Dominate Thanksgiving Box Office as 'Focker In-Law,' 'Hexed' Join Fray Movies 'Focker-in-Law' Filmmaker John Hamburg Talks Ariana Grande's Comedic Timing and the Actor Who Made Ben Stiller Laugh Hardest on Set

“But that’s the distortion, right? It’s like, the distortion is not your own. It was given to you. It was by another’s hand,” the former Saturday Night Live star added, as the singer responded, “It’s kind of like, oh, when will it be about the art?”

“When will it be about the work? That was largely what the video was about is like, oh, here comes this girl, and there’s always some distortion around the performance or art, whatever it is,” Grande added.

Grande, Yang and Rogers spoke more about Pepper’s evolution as a character by the end of the video, as they shifted into discussing the public reaction to the video, which at the time heavily centered around her body.

“It was a fun thing for then that video with the intention of really spelling it out,” Grande said as Yang added, “got its own meta test it on.” The Focker-in-Law actress agreed, continuing, “had its own meta insane perception, and that was just really interesting. But I feel so proud and grateful. I wouldn’t change a thing. I would put it out again today exactly as is. I wouldn’t change a thing. I think my experience of putting art out and having people receive it is so different than how it used to be even just a few years ago.”

Grande announced in August that she’d be taking a step back from the public eye following the finishing of her Eternal Sunshine Tour. “Ariana will be taking a step back from visibility after she completes the Eternal Sunshine Tour,” a statement shared with The Hollywood Reporter read.

“She looks forward to finishing the tour and ending it on a high note, both healthily and happily, and then taking a much-deserved break from public-facing work and appearances, which has led to endless, ongoing public scrutiny,” the statement continued. “This tour has been a beautiful experience for her. She loves her fans and has loved every minute of this tour so much.”

A source close to Grande also told THR at the time that her album Petal is “a fight song.” They added that it’s an “anthem about the love-hate relationship with the public and their toxicity. This album is not about any romantic partner or breakup in that way.” She also exited the West End revival of Sunday in the Park With George, which was later axed completely after Jonathan Bailey also dropped the project.

On Las Culturistas, Rogers asked Grande if she thinks that she “achieved something even more because of that conversation” that sparked online because “what you set out to do with the album [was] to make a comment about that, and then it had its own meta conversation.” He added, “Or does it feel like it really impacted and maybe commenting on that phenomenon to see that phenomenon happen as a result of the comment?”

“It’s Black Mirror,” Grande said. “It’s so funny and Black Mirror and bizarre. I really thought it was going to be a reveal, like me sharing a vulnerable — like, OK, here’s this thing. And then it happened and then sometimes 10 back. It was very funny and weird, but like I said, I love that project. I love that video.”

The Grammy winner continued, “Those are two of my favorite videos, ‘Hate That I Made You Love Me’ and ‘Petal.’ I remember how it felt to be in the room, too, with all of those women dancers and all of us discussing what it felt like and what it held for us and we’ve all been told some kind of interesting thing about ourselves that has nothing to do with the art or our talent or our work or what we’ve given to our audition or whatever. That has nothing to do with anything.”

Grande is currently starring in Focker-in-Law alongside Robert De Niro and Ben Stiller.
