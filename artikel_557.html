France Knights ‘Autobiography,’ ‘Tiger Stripes’ Producer Yulia Evina Bhara at JAFF Market

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Indonesian producer Yulia Evina Bhara, whose credits include Venice title “Autobiography” and Cannes Critics’ Week winner “Tiger Stripes,” was named a Chevalier dans l’Ordre des Arts et des Lettres at a ceremony at JAFF Market in Yogyakarta.

Fabien Penone, France’s ambassador to Indonesia, Timor-Leste and ASEAN, presented the insignia for France’s culture minister. The ceremony doubled as the opening of the Indonesia-France Film Lab booth at the market, which runs Nov. 25–30. France created the order in 1957, and its cinema honorees range from Agnès Varda, François Truffaut, Martin Scorsese and Isabelle Huppert to Indonesia’s Garin Nugroho. Filmmaker Joko Anwar received it last year.

“When I read the names of the people who received this before me, I felt very small,” Bhara said. “They are writers, painters, musicians, directors, and many of them are my heroes. So when the letter came, my first thought was, maybe this is a mistake. My second was, this is not only for me, and this is true. A film producer never works alone. A producer is like a person who builds a boat, but the boat only moves when people row together.”

In his address, Penone traced Bhara’s path from Way Kanan in Sumatra’s Lampung province through seven years of producing historical theater, a craft she taught herself, before she turned to film in 2012 with a foundation that used art to revisit the past. She set up KawanKawan Media in Jakarta in 2016, with international co-production at the core of the business from day one. The company’s name is Indonesian for “friends.”

Its first feature, Yosep Anggi Noen’s “Solo, Solitude,” bowed at Locarno that year and later took the Golden Hanoman Award at the Jogja-Netpac Asian Film Festival. Makbul Mubarak’s “Autobiography” premiered in Venice in 2022 and went on to become Indonesia’s Oscar submission. “Tiger Stripes” won the Critics’ Week Grand Prize in 2023, and Bhara co-produced “Renoir,” which played in competition at Cannes in 2025. That same year she sat on the Critics’ Week jury.

This year she co-produced Next Step Studio Indonesia, which launched as the Critics’ Week’s new talent initiative and paired four young Indonesian filmmakers with four from across Southeast Asia. The result premiered at Cannes to a standing ovation. Bhara also heads international cooperation at the Indonesian Film Board (BPI), is active in producers’ body Aprofi and has been one of the Film Lab’s driving forces since its launch.

“Without question, you rank among the most respected producers in Indonesia,” Penone said. “Your work has helped Indonesian cinema reach new audiences and gain new recognition.”

Bhara, who said she grew up in a small village by a forest and that her family was very poor, recalled that one of the first films she ever watched was Nugroho’s “Cinta dalam Sepotong Roti,” and that nearly every film she has produced was a multi-country effort, with partners from Singapore, Malaysia, the Philippines, Japan, Taiwan, Germany, Poland, Qatar and France.

She told the room that independent cinema now faces three pressures, the first being big money and content, with young directors often told their stories are too small and too local. “I believe the opposite: the more local a story is, the more universal it can become,” Bhara said. “Second, attention. Fifteen seconds, then we swipe. But cinema is the art of staying. The third, the machine. AI can make images, the voices in a second, but a machine cannot remember what it feels like to lose someone.”

The Film Lab is run by the French Embassy and Institut Français d’Indonésie together with Indonesia’s Ministry of Culture, BPI and Aprofi, backed by French film agency CNC and Paris film school La Fémis. Six Indonesian director-producer teams were picked this year, with Éric Collin, Vincent Wang, Thibault Amti and Sébastien Chesneau among the mentors. The lab continues for the next six months.

“A first edition is an experiment. The second one is a test. A third edition tells us that something has become a habit, an institutional relationship,” said BPI chair Fauzan Zidni, whose organization joined the initiative for the first time this year. A producer who has chased international co-productions for 14 years, Zidni said he had gone to markets alone in search of partners, and pledged that BPI would keep supporting the projects after the lab ends.

Penone said most projects from earlier editions will head to Paris early next month for the Southeast Asia Film Forum’s inaugural co-production meetings, staged with CNC backing and the Centre Pompidou as partner. BPI members and filmmaker Edwin will also attend.

“Chevalier means a knight. A knight promises to protect something,” Bhara said. “So today, I promise I will keep protecting the fragile voices, the local films, the human films, and I promise I will never do it alone.”
