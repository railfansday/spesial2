Live Nation’s 17,000 Gather Brisbane Project Is Approved

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Live Nation, OVG and Plenary will build the arena, which will have an 18,700 capacity in concert mode and which, the team behind it say, should bring more major live events to the city.

BRISBANE, Australia – The capital of the Sunshine State, Brisbane, will become a two-arena city when a new 17,000-capacity venue is completed ahead of the 2032 Summer Olympic and Paralympic Games.

A years-long wait for approval came to end this week when Gather Brisbane was rubber stamped, a major new live music and entertainment project for the inner-city suburb of Woolloongabba. Live Nation, OVG and Plenary will build the arena, which will have an 18,700 capacity in concert mode and which, the team behind it say, should bring more major live events to Brisbane.

According to forecasts presented by Live Nation, the arena is expected to welcome more than 1.2 million people each year, support upwards of 1,500 ongoing jobs and generate almost A$844 million ($568 million) annually for the city’s visitor economy.

“Brisbane has the audience and the ambition to welcome more of the world’s biggest tours, but it needs the venues to match,” comments Michael Coppel, chairman of Live Nation Australia and New Zealand. “This arena will help bring more shows to Queensland, strengthen Australia’s appeal as a touring destination and give artists and fans an arena they can be proud of for decades to come.”

Brisbane won the rights to host the Olympics in 2021 when the IOC declared the city as the “preferred candidate,” thanks to its proven track record for organizing international events, with the Commonwealth Games (two, including the Gold Coast), an Expo and the Goodwill Games in its good books.

And critically, the region already had up to 90% of the essential pieces of infrastructure in place — although a cornerstone stadium, bettering the aging Brisbane Cricket Ground (The Gabba) at Woolloongabba, and the Queensland Sport and Athletics Centre at Mount Gravatt, has been both a sore point and a political football.

The creation of a new, world-class arena, one to complement, and outlive, the Brisbane Entertainment Centre, which opened its doors in 1986, was part of the bargain.

Gather Brisbane beats several rival bids, including the so-called Brisbane Live Arena, a proposed 18,000-capacity venue at a derelict spot on Roma Street, a short stroll from King George Square in the heart of the CBD.

On completion, the new space “will be transformational for the area and generational for millions of people who’ll come to live, visit and enjoy this new precinct,” comments Jarrod Bleijie, deputy premier and minister for state development, infrastructure and planning.

Oak View Group CEO Chris Granger, meanwhile, says the “landmark arena will help secure Brisbane’s place as a leading destination for major entertainment and live events.”

The facility is being designed by Blueprint Studio, Live Nation’s in-house design and development group, in collaboration with Cox Architecture and 3XN.

Gather Brisbane will be Live Nation’s biggest venue in these parts, and the jewel in a growing crown of venues across Australia and New Zealand. Just last month, the concerts giant announced it would deliver and operate a purpose-built, 5,000 capacity venue at Arden Central in North Melbourne. Currently, LN operates The Palais Theatre (2,900 capacity) and Festival Hall in Melbourne; Fortitude Music Hall (3,000) and The Triffid (800) in Brisbane; Hindley Street Music Hall in Adelaide (1,800), Anita’s Theatre in Wollongong (1,200); and the Northbridge Music Hall in Perth, which is expected to open to the public in the summer of 2026-27. LN also operates the 13,000-capacity Spark Arena in Auckland, New Zealand.

“Having played to home crowds in Brisbane throughout our careers,” comments Brisbane pop act The Veronicas, “we know how much live music means to this city. This arena will give the next generation of Queensland artists a bigger stage to aspire to and give local fans more chances to see the artists they love.”
