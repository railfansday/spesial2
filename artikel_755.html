Will the Kennedy Center Honors Go Full Trump?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The once star-studded D.C event is moving to new digs next month. Will the president’s name come along?

By Benjamin Svetkey and Julian Sancton, Editors

Never mind who’s onstage at this year’s Kennedy Center Honors. Keep an eye on the banners.

Donald Trump’s efforts to put his name on the performing arts institution continue to be thwarted by a federal court. But this year, while the Kennedy Center’s main building is closed for renovations — and still, after five months, remains shrouded by a tarp hiding the building’s original signage — the Dec. 7 honors ceremony is moving to Washington’s Capital One Arena, where the judge’s no-name-changing order may not apply. “The injunction may only block Trump from affixing his name to the physical building that’s owned by the federal government,” says a source familiar with the Kennedy Center’s inner workings, noting that it remains “a very good question” as to whether there will be “Trump-Kennedy Center” branding at the Capital One event. After all, we know how much the president loves top billing.

If Trump’s name does end up on posters and programs, it’s unlikely the recipients will object too strenuously. This year’s crop of honorees — David Mamet, Paul Anka, David Foster, Andy Garcia and Barbara Mandrell — ranges from outspoken Trump admirers to, at least publicly, Trump agnostics. Mamet has enthusiastically championed the president; Anka performed a modified version of “My Way” at Mar-a-Lago (“When we vote your way”); and Foster hosted a fund-raiser for Trump-backed L.A. mayoral candidate Spencer Pratt.

“In normal times, the board was 50-50 Democrat and Republicans,” says the source. “But Trump is now chairman of the board, so he’s making the final decisions.”

Also in normal times, honorees were traditionally announced in midsummer. But this year, like last, they came months later. The holdup in 2025 was apparently because of Tom Cruise, who kept the board dangling while considering the invitation before finally deciding he had “scheduling conflicts.” It’s unknown who, if anyone, kept the board hanging this year, but they still haven’t announced a lineup of presenters and performers. Those names could provide an even more revealing measure of Hollywood’s willingness to participate in the Trump-Kennedy Center Honors — or whatever they end up being called.
