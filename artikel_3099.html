BioStem (BSEM) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Cost cuts and hospital growth drive BioStem's path to profit.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

BioStem Technologies, Inc. (BSEM -11.56%) reported a shift in revenue mix toward the hospital channel while implementing cost-reduction initiatives to reach profitability. Management stated that the organization completed its transition to internal operations in August and is now focused on improving margins by moving manufacturing in-house in the first half of 2027. The company reported that full ownership of commercial systems and data is expected to drive more precise customer targeting and operational efficiency. Management indicated that strengthening the balance sheet remains a priority, supported by recent private placements, anticipated tax refunds, and executive option exercises.

As we look ahead, we remain committed to driving efficient growth by maximizing the potential of our commercial footprint and product portfolio while exercising disciplined expense management. This approach allows us to prioritize our highest impact growth opportunities while improving profitability and extending our cash runway as we build a sustainable business.

Following the uplist, we have taken several steps to strengthen our balance sheet, and that remains our top priority. Over the long term, we believe the facility will provide an additional source of capital to strengthen our balance sheet and support our strategic priorities. Additionally, at the end of the quarter, we closed a private placement with multiple institutional investors generating gross proceeds of approximately $3 million and the potential for up to approximately $5.6 million in additional gross proceeds from the exercise of accompanying warrants. Just after the quarter ended, Andrew Van Vurst, our Chief Operating Officer, and I, each exercised options, which in total contributed approximately $1 million in cash to the company.

We are currently in the process of evaluating additional financing alternatives with a clear preference for nondilutive structures. More broadly, our objective is to accelerate our path to profitability and to extend our cash runway through efficient growth and disciplined expense reduction. Our strategy is focused on removing spending from areas that are not directly contributing to revenue generation, which Brandon will walk through in a few minutes. As part of these cost reduction and cash preservation efforts, Andrew and I have offered to reduce our annual cash salaries to $250,000 in 2027 and to receive the balance of our salaries in BioStem stock, subject to Board approval. Like our option exercises, this reflects our conviction in the company, and it lowers our cash expenses at a time when it matters most. Moving forward, we are focusing our resources on initiatives that have a direct impact on revenue generation, driving revenue growth and expediting the path to profitability.

Critical to this effort will be to transition Neox and Clarix production into our Pompano Beach facility. We expect products we manufacture to carry a meaningfully higher gross margin, and our goal is clear: move toward profitability as quickly as possible while maintaining sustainable revenue growth. We're simultaneously working to reduce the near-term demands on our cash. We are in active discussions with BioTissue to restructure the terms of the clearance payment, which remains outstanding, with the goal of lowering our near-term cash obligations, and we look forward to sharing more details once those discussions are complete. In the meantime, supply of Neox and Clarix continues without interruption. Now turning to the quarterly results.

During the quarter, we increased hospital revenue sequentially to $6.8 million. I will now provide updates on our strategy to prioritize resources to drive growth in the most efficient way possible. Effective at the beginning of August, the transition services agreement with BioTissue concluded, and responsibility for sales logistics, operational support, invoicing, collections and inventory management fully transferred to BioStem, giving us better control over working capital. With the transition now complete, we have full ownership of the systems and data that underpin our commercial organization.

This was not a small task, and I'm proud of our team for integrating several complex systems in such a short period of time. Our customer relationship management platform is now live and integrated with our enterprise resource planning system. We will use this available data to more precisely target our highest value customers. Specifically, we will be able to leverage our commercial infrastructure to drive growth and improve productivity without proportionally increasing costs. After a learning curve of just over a month, the organization had fully adapted by September, and we now have dramatically improved insight into account activity, inventory and ordering behavior than we had before the transition.

Looking at our commercial progress, we continue to drive adoption of our product portfolio, including the Neox, Clarix and VENDAJE brands in the hospital market. Our commercial strategy in the hospital channel remains centered on 2 complementary objectives: deepening utilization within facilities where our products are already approved and expanding our footprint with the addition of new accounts. A significant portion of our growth opportunity lies within the hospitals we already serve. Having already made the investments required to establish contract access and relationships with key customers, we are now well positioned to expand utilization within these facilities at a meaningfully lower incremental cost.

In September, the VENDAJE product family was added to our existing GPO agreements with 2 major GPOs, the 2 largest group purchasing organizations in the United States. These additions represent the first GPO coverage for the VENDAJE product family and allow clinicians across those GPO member facilities to procure VENDAJE through established contracting channels. As a result, deepening penetration within our existing customer base through GPO coverage expansion represents one of the most efficient avenues for growth available to BioStem. We remain pleased with the progress of our sales organization as we continue to focus on our most productive territories to gain new accounts.

We are seeing that our broad product portfolio and GPO access continues to attract new customers and expand our account base. Just as important, existing customers are expanding their use of our portfolio. From the second quarter to the third, the number of accounts buying 2 or more of our product lines grew by 14%; customers buying 3 or more lines rose by more than 40%. That depth matters. Customers who buy across our portfolio, meaning buy more than one product, account for more than half of our revenue. These metrics demonstrate that our commercial strategy is working and that clinicians are seeing value for their patients across a variety of specialties and procedures.

We believe these metrics will continue to trend in a positive direction. Clinical evidence remains foundational to our approach in wound care. Our VLU study is on track, and we are scheduled to report top line results at the Innovations in Wound Healing meeting in early December. This clinical evidence is helping to drive physician adoption and deepen hospital penetration, and we believe it's important -- we believe its importance will only increase as coverage and product selection decisions become more evidence driven. Our field organization integrates this evidence into every customer engagement, and we believe that it adds another point of leverage for our technology.

With the combination of targeted execution around key use cases, strategic accounts, clinical evidence and new products coming in on to our GPO agreements, we believe we are well positioned to broaden adoption of our portfolio and support our growth expectations over the coming years. Now an update on the technology transfer of Neox and Clarix. The transfer of Neox and Clarix manufacturing to our Pompano Beach facility remains a significant value driver for BioStem. Bringing production in-house is expected to deliver significant improvement in gross margin and cash generation, and our existing infrastructure allows us to accomplish this with modest capital investment.

We remain on track to complete the tech transfer and begin in-house production in the first half of 2027, subject to process validation and applicable regulatory requirements. In the interim, BioTissue continues to manufacture these products on our behalf under our manufacturing and supply agreement. We've experienced no interruption in product availability or customer service, and we expect continuity of supply to be maintained throughout the transition. Brandon Poe: Thank you, Jason. For the third quarter, revenue totaled $7.6 million compared with $7.9 million in the second quarter of 2026. The sequential decrease primarily reflected higher transfer pricing under the BioTissue supply agreement following the expiration of the initial discounted pricing. We expect margins to remain in the 50s until we execute a successful technology transfer next year. Total operating expenses were $14.1 million compared with $13.2 million in the prior quarter. The sequential increase was driven primarily by higher general and administrative expenses related to uplisting and financing activity, partly offset by lower research and development spend. Sales and marketing expenses were $5.3 million, flat when compared with the prior quarter. The majority of our new sales reps were hired in Q2 of this year.

So we saw the full cost of those adds in Q3, offset by lower sales training and recruiting costs. General and administrative expenses were $7.5 million compared with $6.5 million in the prior quarter. The increase was largely driven by higher payroll tax expense due to the vesting of certain and higher legal and accounting fees related to our Nasdaq uplist process, registration statements and financings, which we anticipated. Research and development expenses were approximately $500,000 compared with approximately $700,000 in the prior quarter as we continue to wind down our current clinical studies. Other operating expenses, primarily from intangibles amortization, were approximately $700,000, in line with the prior quarter.

GAAP net loss for the quarter was $11.4 million or $0.64 per share compared with a $9 million or 52% -- sorry, $0.52 per share loss in the second quarter. The GAAP net loss for Q3 included a $1 million payment to BioTissue to extend the clearance payment deadline. The adjusted EBITDA loss was $6.4 million compared with a $4.6 million loss in the prior quarter. The increase in the adjusted EBITDA loss reflected the additional extension payment to BioTissue, lower gross profit and additional G&A costs as I described. Cash and cash equivalents totaled $1 million as of September 30, 2026 and compared with $7 million as of June 30, 2026.

At the end of the quarter, we closed a private placement with multiple institutional investors, generating gross proceeds of approximately $3 million with the potential for up to approximately $5.6 million in additional gross proceeds if the accompanying warrants are exercised in full for cash. We continue to assess other financing alternatives with a preference for non-dilutive structures.

We believe that these will be positive steps toward improving our liquidity position, and we will continue to evaluate additional options and take further actions as needed. Turning to our outlook. Within the hospital channel, we continue to expect sequential growth in the fourth quarter, supported by fully operational internal systems, increasing productivity from our focused sales organization, VENDAJE availability under GPO contracts and general surgical seasonality. We continue to focus our resources in the hospital, and we expect minimal contributions from the physician office channel.

Reflecting our confidence in the trajectory of the hospital business, we are raising the low end of our full year 2026 revenue guidance and now anticipate revenue to be in the range of $28 million to $29 million compared with our previous range of $26 million to $29 million. In 2027, with our cost actions in place and in-house manufacturing targeted to begin in the first half of 2027, we expect our operating expenses to decline substantially and our adjusted EBITDA loss to narrow across 2027.

Looking closer at our cost reduction plan, late in the quarter, we identified cost reduction initiatives intended to right size the business, extend our cash runway and accelerate our path to profitability while remaining focused on sustainable growth. To start, we have moderated hiring. In addition, we discontinued certain third-party contractor engagements and renegotiated agreements to reduce ongoing costs. These initiatives are designed to improve operating efficiency without impacting our commercial momentum. We believe our existing commercial infrastructure can support continued growth and broader portfolio adoption with limited incremental investment. By aligning our cost structure with our current priorities, we expect to improve operating leverage and preserve resources for our highest impact growth opportunities.

Building on these steps, we are implementing a broader cost reduction plan that we expect to lower our annualized cash operating expenses by approximately $10 million to $12 million compared with our current run rate. The plan, which is being finalized, still includes focusing our sales organization around our most productive territories and accounts, streamlining our support functions and ending a range of outside services, media and sponsorships that do not directly contribute to revenue. We expect to finalize the plan and implement these initiatives over the coming weeks with the full benefit from these changes beginning in the first quarter of 2027.
