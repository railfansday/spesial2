Haissem Hassan on Celtic goal drought, Maloney guidance & one area for improvement

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

In football, end product is the defining trait of any winger. From Jimmy Johnstone to Jota, Celtic have a storied history of successful wide men, with Haissem Hassan the latest man eager to make his mark.

Hassan joined the club for an initial fee of £6 million from Real Oviedo this summer following an impressive World Cup campaign with Egypt that ended in a Round of 16 defeat to eventual finalists Argentina.

So far in Glasgow, though, Hassan has struggled to perform consistently, unable to fully replicate the international displays that brought his name to the attention of several European clubs.

If anything, the 24-year-old has shown his talent in bursts. He breezed past Celta Vigo's Marcos Alonso last Thursday, carrying Celtic 80 yards up the pitch and winning a corner.

An apex showing in the 7-1 shellacking of Hearts stands out too, but Hassan has yet to find the net for Celtic.

Indeed, two assists in 15 appearances across all competitions suggests a winger still searching for that crucial end product.

"Defensive-wise I need to improve. On the attacking side I need the numbers on goals and assists," Hassan said. "I should not be losing my head about it. I just need to focus on training.

"When I get the minutes, I need to try to produce and contribute to the team's performance. I will focus on doing this.

"In terms of defending, the team in Spain asked me a lot as well. The teams you play against are also good teams and they asked me to defend more."

Haissem Hassan in action for Celtic. (Image: Stuart Wallace / Shutterstock)

Hassan's lack of goals isn't for a want of trying. He forced Dundee United goalkeeper Jack Walton into a fine save after coming off the bench during Wednesday evening's 2-2 draw and unleashing a rasping effort from range.

He also rattled the left post after cutting inside and leaving three Hearts players for dead a fortnight ago.

"I am really keen to score my first goal for Celtic and also add assists," Hassan continued. "The goal feels closer and closer.

"The other day I was close. The game before that I assisted goals so hopefully it will come soon."

Seven of Hassan's 15 Celtic appearances have come from the start.

Manager Martin O'Neill isn't short of wide options, with Jota, capable on both flanks, recently returning from an 18-month anterior cruciate ligament injury.

Camilo Duran, the club's current top scorer, can play on the right while Hyun-jun Yang and James Forrest remain viable picks.

Callum Osmand has also started featuring on the left-hand side. Hassan relishes the competition for places.

"The wingers in this team are at a really good level," he said. "When I go on the pitch, I have to give my best.

"But the competition is good for us all. If one player comes out and does really well, the rest of us know we have to do even more. It's been really good so far."

Assistant manager Shaun Maloney guides Celtic's wingers, having featured out wide during his own playing career.

"Shaun helps me a lot," Hassan explained. "He is close to the players and always tries to help the players. He had a good career as a winger.

"Whenever he comes to me and speaks to me, I always try to listen and take it into account. Having him as a reference point really helps me."

Hassan qualifies to play for Egypt through his parents, though he grew up on the outskirts of Paris and speaks fluent French and Spanish. The transition to life in Glasgow, he says, has been relatively straightforward, even if a slight language barrier exists.

"I am happy here. It's a nice city," he adds. "I live here with my family and they are happy here, too. It is cold but it is good! It's a big city that has it all so I'm happy here.

"It happens in Glasgow that I meet fans. It's good to feel the love of the fans. I try to stop as much as possible and take a photo with them.

"I like the culture. But I have been living in France and Spain where people have dinner quite late. Here it's very early so it's the only thing I need to get used to!"

Despite the cold weather, for Hassan, joining Celtic was a simple call: "I made the decision myself to come over here. It's a big club and I thought it would help me be a better player and improve my career.

"I didn't have to speak to anyone else for those reasons."

Although still transitioning to life at Celtic, Hassan played for Egypt during the recent international break, starting two crucial AFCON qualifiers against Angola and South Sudan.

A double-header against Malawi lies ahead later this month.

"It feels good to play for the national team," Hassan stated. "You represent your country and it's a great honour.

"There is a bunch of great players in the national team and I enjoy playing with them.

"The games against Malawi are really important. If we can win, it would take us top of the table and give us the opportunity to play at the next AFCON."

For now, though, Hassan's focus remains on getting off the mark at Celtic.

O'Neill's side travel to face Falkirk on Sunday afternoon before welcoming Besiktas to Glasgow in the Europa League on Thursday evening.

Pointless from their first three fixtures, Celtic realistically need a win against the Turks.

"We still have five games to go," Hassan asserted. "A win over Besiktas would give us confidence and hope.

"If we win the rest of the games or get as many points as possible, we definitely hope we can qualify for the next stage.

"This game is at home. We have the fans with us and hopefully we can get a good result and move forward.

"I don't think the Europa League has been a disappointment as such. We have a good team, good management but the results have cost us.

"We are struggling a bit now. But we can improve and we'll try to play in the best way possible."
