Manchester United appear to have suffered an early setback in their pursuit of Lewis Hall, with Newcastle United determined to block any January approach for the England international.

The 22-year-old has emerged as a serious long-term option for Mauricio Pochettino as United consider strengthening at left-back.

Luke Shaw’s contract situation and injury history have created uncertainty in that position, while Patrick Dorgu has also endured fitness issues this season.

Hall would provide youth, Premier League experience and the technical quality Pochettino could build around, but convincing Newcastle to negotiate looks extremely difficult.

According to The Telegraph, Newcastle will not entertain offers for Hall when the January transfer window opens, despite suggestions Man United could revive their interest.

The Magpies consider the defender a crucial part of their long-term plans and strengthened their position earlier this season by securing him on a new long-term contract.

United have monitored Hall for some time, and their interest appears unlikely to disappear simply because Newcastle have closed the door on a winter sale.

A CaughtOffside report suggested United have been encouraged to pursue the England international, with a potential valuation of more than £60million being discussed. The report described him as an attractive option to eventually succeed Shaw.

There is plenty to like about Hall from a United perspective.

He is homegrown, comfortable carrying and progressing the ball, defensively improving and capable of contributing high up the pitch. At 22, he could potentially solve the left-back position for many years rather than provide another short-term fix.

His growing international standing also demonstrates his development. Hall played the full 90 minutes as England beat world champions Spain 3-2 on Sunday, another indication that he is increasingly comfortable at the highest level.

However, United should recognise when a January deal is unrealistic.

Newcastle have just extended Hall’s contract, regard him as an important player and have no obvious reason to sell halfway through the season. Attempting to force a deal could simply result in United paying an inflated fee.

Pochettino will need reinforcements, but centre-back and striker currently appear more urgent priorities.

United should therefore maintain their interest and revisit Hall next summer. If Newcastle’s position changes, he would be an excellent long-term target. For January, though, spending £60m-plus to test a club determined not to sell would be difficult to justify.
