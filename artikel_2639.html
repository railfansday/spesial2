Ravens reach new low in primetime loss to previously-winless Chargers

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

There are teams that would announce firings the morning after a game like the Ravens' loss to the previously winless Chargers on Monday night.

That will not happen in Baltimore. Not from a franchise that has long prioritized continuity and stability, often at substantial opportunity costs. The Ravens will maintain their stance that they can keep their postseason chances alive until Lamar Jackson comes back – if that even happens. The idea seemed plausible when he first went down, but still a long shot.

Now, it is simply preposterous. Book it: the Ravens will be closer to a top-five draft pick at the end of the season than they will be to the playoffs. (They currently sit at No. 14.) And they have only themselves to blame.

Lamenting injuries and refereeing only goes so far. The fact is, the Ravens never should have been in a position for close calls going against them to matter.

Monday night put all of Baltimore's core flaws on display. It started from the very first play from scrimmage – a zero-yard run by Derrick Henry that matched the team's first-half rushing total. The Ravens openly talked about new quarterback Jacoby Brissett's ability to push the ball downfield to open up previously-nonexistent rushing lanes for Henry. That only happens if a defense respects the opposing passer's arm – which can only happen if said passer completes a few throws downfield.

Those circumstances would lend themselves to the under-center, play-action look on early downs that have propelled Baltimore's offense this year. But Declan Doyle rarely pulled that lever on Monday night, and almost never on early downs. Instead, he insisted on trying to power through a Chargers defensive line that was having their way with Baltimore's blockers.

The result was a grand total of 16 rushing yards and endless obvious passing situations for Brissett that allowed Los Angeles' pass rush to tee off on Baltimore's weak offensive line. He dropped back 51 times with a 35.3% pressure rate (via PFF's initial stats) despite a relatively conservative blitz approach from Chargers defensive coordinator Chris O'Leary. He had no fear of a run on second- and third-and-long, often rolling out dime personnel to lock down passing lanes. Former Raven Del'Shawn Phillips, the Chargers' No. 2 off-ball linebacker, played just 28 snaps, representing roughly 40% of the team's defensive total and a massive amount of disrespect for the Ravens' run game.

Despite the negative game script and opportunity set, Brissett found a way to complete 33 of his 49 passes (67.3%) for 340 yards. He somehow managed to take just two sacks, but did throw a crucial interception that set up the Chargers' second touchdown. Overall, the veteran gunslinger deserves some credit for a decent debut in purple and black after just 10 days in Baltimore.

So, too, does Mark Andrews. He has been under siege by Ravens fans this year, but he reeled in all eight of his targets on Monday night for 82 yards and five first downs. ESPN's broadcast revealed that the veteran tight end was playing through a broken hand earlier this season, explaining his frequent drops but not why he continued to take the field instead of letting it heal.

The rest of the Ravens' playmakers chipped in, but Zay Flowers struggled to shake loose for his usual explosive gains with a long of 17 yards on the night. He also committed yet another fumble in the worst possible moment, two minutes left, down three points – that sank any chance of a late comeback. Once again, one of Baltimore's best could not be at their best when their best was needed.
