Suns’ Devin Booker gets 100% on ‘worst loss of the year’ to Mavericks

PHOENIX– As the Dallas Mavericks hyped themselves up exiting the Mortgage Matchup Center with a 113-109 win on Friday, the Phoenix Suns did not feel that, especially Devin Booker.

Although he did all he could by posting 31 points and shooting over 50% from the field and 50% from deep, it wasn't enough. The problems, though, go beyond him or some of his teammates.

Much of it had to do with them letting Dallas back into the game. While the Suns' road trip seemed to dispel the true weaknesses, they crept back onto their home floor.

A 35-14 point discrepancy in the fourth quarter is unacceptable, and certainly with leading by as many as 21. For a team that has their eyes set on making the playoffs, Dallas is the perfect litmus test.

A big, physical team that can defend at all five positions and truly be disruptive. Even with Phoenix's lack of size, Friday could've been good to measure where they are.

Instead, it left many feeling a legitimate uncertainty about where they are against some of the upper echelon teams in the Western Conference.

Booker didn't hold back with what Friday's loss is making him feel.

"Just a meltdown," he said in the locker room. "It's our worst loss of the year so far."

The loss is bad in the context of what came before it. As mentioned earlier, there was the 21-point blown lead. However, much of it was the rotations and adjustments.

Ryan Dunn and Khaman Maluach didn't touch the fourth quarter at all, despite having a positive +/- rating in the first three frames.

Collin Gillespie played nearly the entire fourth quarter and had five of the team's 14 points. He had it going all game, but eventually, the gas tank was on empty.

Rolling with the hot hand is one thing, but letting it fizzle out isn't good. But that's not the most important thing.

As simple as it sounds, it was how, inch by inch, piece by piece, and even fraction by fraction, the Mavericks snuck back into the game. Consistent 8-0 and 10-0 spurts throughout the fourth quarter put them on track to win.

This gives deja vu to the Suns' disaster against the Atlanta Hawks last season when they squandered a 20-point lead.

Nonetheless, it'll be tough to stomach, but cleansing their palate against a struggling Utah Jazz can be just what the basketball doctors prescribe.
