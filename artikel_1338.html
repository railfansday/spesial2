11 Best Number 9s in World Football Right Now (2026)

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The number nine shirt went out of fashion in the opening two decades of the 21st century. Traditionally the squad number of choice for each team's starting striker, the Premier League's three most prolific centre-forwards since 2000 - Wayne Rooney, Harry Kane and Sergio Aguero - all opted for the number 10.

Lionel Messi underscored the enduring popularity of that figure, while Cristiano Ronaldo's rise to stardom spawned a flood of acolytes demanding the number seven shirt. But there has been a shift back to nine in recent years.

Through a mixture of preference and circumstance, arguably three of the four best players on the planet right now turn out for their club side draped in a curved number nine. There are plenty of other potent forwards outside that elite bracket of Ballon d'Or contenders following in the long tradition of prolific number nines.

Best Number Nines in World Football Right Now (2025)

Lionel Messi is only the third-best player on the planet wearing the number 10 shirt right now.

The role of the striker - namely, the team's number nine - is woven into the very fabric of Athletic Club de Bilbao. La Liga's award for the top scorer at the end of each season is called the Pichichi in honour of the former Athletic legend, the first in a long line of prolific centre-forwards for the Basque giants.

Inaki Williams has carried the burden of the club's number nine shirt since 2018 and represents perhaps the most selfless incumbent. No cause is lost for the tireless runner, no goalkeeper's heels are safe from his snapping tackles.

Williams channelled that generosity of spirit into a hat-trick of assists against Las Palmas in 2024, becoming the first Athletic player to achieve such a feat since his former manager, Ernesto Valverde, three decades earlier.

Dominic Calvert-Lewin is everything that a classic No.9 should be. He's physical, hard-working and scores most of his goals in between the six-yard box and the penalty spot. He is true throwback and Leeds United have benefitted ever since his arrival in 2025.

Some believed that the Englishman's career was destined to nosedive after Everton failure. They have been proven wrong every week ever since. The forward is now one of England's best strikers and could be next in line to take Harry Kane's place once the Bayern Munich star bows out at international level.

England's 20 best players right now have been ranked, including Harry Kane, Jude Bellingham and Bukayo Saka.

If Calvert-Lewin is a classic No.9, then Evanilson is very much the epitome of a modern-day forward. The Brazilian's goals have helped fire Bournemouth to European heights and could keep them there. Back to full fitness in 2026, Evanilson enjoyed a sensational run of five goals in five games in October.

It's not just the goalscoring side of his game that impresses, either. The Bournemouth star is almost like Roberto Firmino with the way he can also bring others into play. It's been a major benefit for the likes of Marcus Tavernier at Bournemouth and could see the Cherries continue their sensational rise.

To say that Alexander Sorloth has bounced back from Crystal Palace failure many years ago would be quite the understatement. The forward has become an important player for Diego Simeone at Atletico Madrid, with his most impressive season coming in the 2024/25 campaign, when he hit 20 La Liga goals.

Atletico Madrid triggered Sorloth's modest £26.7m release clause in 2024 and have benefitted ever since. Where there is a big moment, there has been a big goal. The Norway international scored three goals against Barcelona and Real Madrid in the 2025/26 campaign, with one of those goals coming in the SuperCopa final which Atletico eventually lost against Real Madrid.

Ferran Torres will forever have his place in history as the man who took the winning touch to hand Spain the 2026 World Cup, defeating Argentina in extra-time. The forward has been a man reborn since then, too. From an under-performing, nervous star, Torres has become a key man at PSG.

The only way is up from here for a player who may finally take his place as a starter for Spain in the coming years.

PSG boss Luis Enrique was clearly delighted with his arrival in the summer, telling reporters after the Spaniard's debut against Rennes: "He wasn't bad, was he? He scored two goals, but I'm happy with everyone who came on in a very difficult match because we had a lot of trouble controlling Rennes in the first half.

"They were good on the counter-attack and scored two wonderful goals. It was complicated, but it's important for me to see that so many players are ready. We sign players to go on a long journey with them. It's very positive for him and we're very happy."

When Inter Milan sealed the club's 20th Serie A title with a derby win over AC Milan in April 2024, earning a second star on the shirt, Marcus Thuram celebrated by draping his number nine jersey over a corner flag which had sported their rivals' flag. Rather than a pointed jab at Milan, this has been the Frenchman's signature celebration for years.

During his time at Borussia Monchengladbach, Thuram would even request his teammates' kit, singling out the star player from that particular contest for celebration by bouncing around the pitch post-match with a billowing makeshift flag.

While his legendary father Lilian rarely ventured away from the number 21 at club level, Marcus is forging a reputation as one of the modern game's best number nines.

Serhou Guirassy somehow remains one of the most underrated players in Europe. The Borussia Dortmund forward is so often as prolific as they come. His best goalscoring campaign arrived in the 2024/25 season, when he netted a stunning 38 goals in 50 games in all competitions. The numbers have not slowed down since, either.

If Dortmund are to ever overcome Bayern Munich's dominance, then it will be Guirassy who spearheads it. For now, though, his goals remain unrewarded as the Bavarian giants drive towards more success in the Bundesliga.

Harry Kane, Kylian Mbappe, and Zlatan Ibrahimovic all rank among the greatest strikers since 2000.

The Chelsea No.9 curse is as unforgiving as it is prolific. It has stood in the way of success for the likes of Fernando Torres, Alvaro Morata and Radamel Falcao. All thrived elsewhere, but instantly struggled to find the back of the net at Stamford Bridge. So, when Joao Pedro arrived, there would have been some natural doubts.

It turns out, however, that the only way to stop the curse is to splash out on Brighton & Hove Albion's star striker. Pedro has put any doubts to bed in emphatic fashion. Not only is he among the best players in the Premier League, but he's also among the best No.9s in the world. Curse? What curse?

The game of football has seen a wide variety of players over the years, but who have been the most skilful people to ever grace the beautiful game?

There was no one more relieved when Alexander Isak commenced the 2026/27 campaign in emphatic fashion than Andoni Iraola. The Liverpool boss knew that, if his tenure was to stand any chance, he had to get the Swede back to his best form after such a disappointing debut campaign full of disruption at Anfield.

Had Isak failed to find his feet, then Liverpool would have been left looking at a £125m flop. As it is, however, they are looking at one of the best No.9s in world football and the Premier League who is proving to be worth every single penny. It may not be too long before he takes his place at the very top of this list, either.

Erling Haaland is the ultimate in number-nine efficiency. Since the start of his Manchester City spell, the unrelenting talisman has scored more than 150 goals at club level. Add on the fact that it took him just 61 games to score more than 60 international goals and it's clear that there are not many better No.9s on the planet.

A record-breaker, an athletic freak and so much more, Haaland is a generational star. It won't be too long before he's chasing down Alan Shearer's all-time Premier League record if he sticks around in English football. The Ballon d'Or may also be in the Norwegian's future if all goes to plan. The sky is the limit.
