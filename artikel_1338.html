David James Names Best Atmosphere He Ever Played In - Not Anfield or Wembley

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

David James had a fruitful career playing for Liverpool, Aston Villa and England, among others, in some brilliant stadium atmospheres.

Wembley Stadium is the national stadium, but the Premier League also has incredible stadiums such as Liverpool's Anfield, Manchester United's Old Trafford, Arsenal's Emirates Stadium, Man City's Etihad Stadium, Chelsea's Stamford Bridge, Newcastle's St James' Park, and Aston Villa's Villa Park as part of it, where players are either backed to the hilt by home fans, or face a hostile welcome when appearing as an opponent.

English football is the home of some of the greatest stadiums in world football, and as a result, it often produces some of the best atmospheres too.

When playing at the highest level too, players will get to travel around the world and compete in continental competitions or at international level, meaning they get to play at other iconic venues and soak in the atmosphere there too.

One player who was able to do that during his 26-year career in professional football was former goalkeeper David James.

James represented top clubs such as Liverpool, Man City, Portsmouth, and Aston Villa during his career, while he also earned 53 caps for the senior England national team, appearing at Euro 2004, the 2006 World Cup, and 2010 World Cup.

The 6ft 5' star made a name for himself as a fine shot-stopper, and even had a brief stint playing as a striker under Stuart Pearce for 6 minutes in a Premier League game as they chased a victory. He is best remembered for his time as Liverpool number one, where he made 277 appearances between 1992 and 1999.

Having played at the top level, James played all over the world and in some of the greatest stadiums. He also played in some huge matches with great atmospheres, including four FA Cup finals, a League Cup final, and World Cup knockout stage games.

But while speaking exclusively to GIVEMESPORT, on behalf of LiveFootballTickets, the 56-year-old revealed that while he loved playing regularly at Anfield and Wembley in his career, neither of those venues was the best atmosphere he ever played in.

“'Best' is a very subjective term," he said.

"Of course, winning at Anfield is special. I was actually thinking about playing games at Anfield, but my experience in India with Kerala Blasters stands out.

"The team had just been created for the Indian Super League. We played a game in Kochi, and it was the loudest football match I’ve ever played in. There were 60-odd thousand people making noise two hours before the game, all the way through it, and then even more after we won.

"It was incredible to understand how football had gripped a part of the nation that wasn’t renowned for football, and how much passion the supporters had.

"Anfield and Wembley are obviously massive stadiums and massive occasions, but the atmosphere at Wembley or Anfield is a frequent thing. What we had in Kochi was the first time for that team, and that was part of the reason why.

"I’ve said this to many people: experiencing that made me fall even more in love with football."

James spent one season playing professionally in India, having been signed by the club's owner and cricket legend Sachin Tendulkar as player-manager.

The team finished as runners-up in the league, before James retired and left the club at the end of his contract. He returned for a short stint as manager once again in 2018. But his time in Asia was clearly very special to him and left a lasting impression, as he still recalls his time there very fondly.
