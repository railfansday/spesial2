2 Up, 2 Down: Solid Sunderland Earn A Point At Villa

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

With two clean sheets in a row and four points from six against two difficult teams, it's starting to look like Sunderland are getting back to the dogged formula of last season.

There's still room for improvement but there are signs at least that we're becoming hard to beat again, and that means we'll have a chance in games.

All eyes are going to be on Ahoka in the coming weeks and there's a lot of pressure on the lad, but to play like that on your first Premier League start is a brilliant achievement.

He's stepping up well so far and deserves loads of credit.

Chemsdine Talbi was a fraction away from winning it.

He worked hard to create the chance but the only reason I have it as a negative is that he couldn't quite find the target.

Règis Le Bris knows the squad better than me, and I can see why he didn't want to risk unbalancing a team that was well organised, but it does worry me slightly that we only saw one substitution being made.

Nilson Angulo battled hard and we might get to see him through the middle again, but given the workload, I assumed there would have been a couple more switches as things went on.

It is more an observation than a criticism I think, and getting the result was the important bit, although it does make me think that we'll see a different XI on Thursday night.

Having started the season looking very shaky at the back, individually and collectively, we've been really solid defensively for the last two games against string attacking sides.

A welcome return to last season's defensive foundations. Robin Roefs, Nordi Mukiele and O'Nien were very good.

The midfield three of Jules Ahoka, Noah Sadiki and Enzo Le Fée was very strong and gave as good as they got from their Villa counterparts.

It's unbelievable to think how few games Ahoka has played.

He covered the defence well and showed some attacking intent, especially in the first half. Maybe less involved in the second half but was still doing his job by sitting and protecting the back four.

Sadiki covered a lot of ground and Le Fée showed some lovely touches — although a couple of attempted through balls didn't quite come off.

Just about every team in the Premier League would miss Brobbey, as he's such a presence and gives us a platform to play off. The lack of a similar back up is a glaring gap in our squad so when he's not playing it has a huge impact as Wilson Isidor's hold up play is poor.

We need to find a way of making the most of Isidor's own attributes as it looks like Brobbey could be out for a while — or at least in and out a bit.

Earlier this week, we were tentatively linked with the Dinamo Zagreb striker who scored against us in the Europa League, Dion Beljo, and it's that kind of strong player who can play the lone striker role that we need.

Every week — it doesn't matter who it is — the referee is always a talking point.

This one, Andy Madley, wasn't actually all that bad but it's a low bar we're judging him on and the booking for Nilson Angulo was just embarrassing.

At least he didn't make any game-changing decisions.

A really good point against a very decent side away from home.

The fight was there, the game plan was there and we could've nicked a winner with Chemsdine Talbi's effort at the death. It's another boost after a tough period and a definite step in the right direction.

Enzo was class and everything positive seemed to come through him.

There were some really good performances today and we operated as a team but he was the spark. We need to look after the little maestro as he'll be pivotal for us in the months ahead.

I sound like a broken record but I'm still scratching my head as to why we didn't bring in a proven striker in the summer.

With European football and the league, we needed more depth and it shows. We just have to hope Brian Brobbey is back soon. Wilson Isidor can't carry the burden alone.

If I am being really picky given our performance there were too many passes which went astray when we weren't being put under pressure.

We work so hard off the ball so when we're in transition and looking to counter, so we need to protect the ball better.

Granted, this Villa team was woeful in front of goal but we put in a terrific defensive display despite players being missing from our back line.

The midfield also looked solid and gained in creativity as the game proceeded — Enzo Le Fée was again his creative self.

I was at the unlucky 4-3 reverse last season when we levelled with two goals from Trai Hume and Wilson Isidor late on, but this was a much more controlled team performance and we came away with a point.

Again, the team showed great togetherness and determination, and more so than in nearly all of our away games this season.

This represents our first clean sheet away from home — remember that Luke O'Nien started last season well down the pecking order after the signing of several new defenders? Here, he put in another disciplined performance, and captained the side for a great away point.

O'Nien is a legend; a great example for the whole team, and especially the younger players.

We didn't lack ambition and essentially matched a team with a Villa top six ambitions.

This was great after our poor start, and we forced out third clean sheet in four games — all against tough opposition.

All this bodes well to our beloved club moving up the table.

We are light on strikers, as illustrated by the fact that we played with two wingers and nobody in the middle for the last fifteen minutes of the game.

Who doesn't love Wilson Isidor? But as a lone striker, he needs support, and a new signing of a proven goalscorer is required.

I do find it hard to understand why we had ten men behind the ball many times when Villa were on the attack. This left no rapid outlet and nobody to aim for up front in Brian Brobbey's absence.

Despite all this, Chemsdine Talbi came very close to a winner after a quick break late on.

Nicholas Jackson got the better of him a few times early on, but the Frenchman is such a steadying influence in the centre of that defence.

We miss his threat in the attack immensely on the right side, but he still found a way to contribute here and there offensively as best he could. He's Sunderland's best and most important player.

Rigid defensively, toothless offensively, using only one sub, getting a hard-fought away point and almost nicking three at the death…this is the Sunderland that got promoted and the Sunderland that was fourth in the league early last season.

Régis Le Bris, the players and fans should all be ashamed for trying to be something we're not.

Everyone wanted the team to evolve; to not just rely on grit and determination, but to be expansive, entertaining and fun.

Boring road points are fun. Winning at home is fun. Good to see everyone seems to be remembering who we are.

That said, we've got to find a way to be occasionally threatening — the right side has devolved instead of evolved.

Wilson Isidor, who I love, spent most of the match fouling Leon Goretzka for some reason. Chemsdine Talbi worked his butt off, but can't be the only outlet.

The return to solidity is welcome, but Le Bris has to find a way to squeeze more threat out of this squad.

For all Aston Villa had the lions' share of the possession, an xG below 1 for the opposition shows just how good a job the defensive unit did for the second game in a row.

Although there were a few hairy moments, it's fair to say that Villa were well-marshaled, controlled and ultimately frustrated by a Sunderland side defending with the vigour of last season.

It's frustrating to a point that it's taken this long to get here, but it's a welcome return should it remain the bedrock of how we go about our business.

Still with under ten professional appearances to his name and playing like that for the second time in as many games is just beyond description.

Once again, Ahoka thrived in the middle of the park and with his defensive responsibilities increased and the inevitable pressure of Granit Xhaka's absence to boot, it was a genuinely mind-bending experience.

Never looking in trouble, looking after the ball with subsequent ease and gliding past players as if they weren't there when he was allowed the opportunity to get explorative with the ball at his feet, it's almost impossible to not get carried away.

With someone of Thomas Meunier's experience likening you to Sergio Busquets, you're on the right track alright.

This wasn't Isidor's day and whilst I'll mitigate his performance with his style of play not quite matching what we are trying to achieve, it did bother me how ineffective he was.

Simple things like niggly fouls, going down overly easily and not really challenging for headers really made a tough job harder. There was plenty of huff and puff about what Isidor got up to but even if he isn't onto a winner, he really has to start making defenders work harder to win the ball than what he has done in his limited time this season.

Perhaps if he was to make more of a nuisance of himself, he would give the midfield a springboard to allow him to play off the shoulder of the last man.

We know he has the pace and the finishing, so it's not a lack of ability doing the damage here — more so a lack of wising up and remembering the offside rule.

It may or may not have worked in our favour but once again, the man in the middle managed to make an entire game about himself.

It was always going to be an "in-the-trenches" job for us but the game was dragged from The Somme to Passchendaele by the referee who managed to sufficiently kill the game any time a game of football threatened to break out.

In the end, it helped us solidify a good point gained but with the game breaking up in the last fifteen minutes, there was more than one "what could've been" moment from our whistle-happy friend in the middle.
