'Losing respect for him' - Pundits react to stunning Rodgers claims

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Pittsburgh Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers claims that head coach Mike McCarthy was 'snoring' in a quarterback meeting in an explosive interview; Steelers president Art Rooney II comes out in "100 per cent" support of McCarthy after team releases Rodgers in light of comments

Has Aaron Rodgers played his final game in the NFL? Was he wrong to publicly criticise Mike McCarthy? The four-time MVP quarterback is 'losing respect' following his explosive claims about the Pittsburgh Steelers head coach, according to Sky Sports NFL's Jason Bell.

Rodgers sent shockwaves through the NFL on Thanksgiving Eve as he used an interview on The Pat McAfee Show to claim McCarthy had been "falling asleep in meetings" while suggesting his entire team believed a change was required on the sideline.

It came after Rodgers was benched for Friday's matchup with the Denver Broncos following two straight defeats, with Mason Rudolph due to start under center.

The Steelers later released Rodgers 11 weeks into what is set to be the final season of the 42-year-old's career.

"First of all, I don't want to be on Aaron Rodgers' bad side. Let's start there. We all make mistakes, right? We all do things that aren't necessarily right in the moment," said Sky Sports NFL's Bell.

"But when I look at him as a player, he wasn't playing well. He wasn't helping his team.

"And you could could see him and his head coach arguing on the sideline with with each other. They weren't getting along.

"Whatever happens behind closed doors, that goes down, but the response to it is where I'm losing a little respect.

"In that locker room is a sanctuary. Some things we just don't let out. We don't have those conversations in public - it's a respect thing.

"If Rodgers was playing MVP football like he did before, he could say all these things and get away with it, but you have to know where you are in your career."

Rodgers suggested in his appearance on The Pat McAfee Show that the prospect of being benched had never been mentioned during recent conversations with McCarthy.

He accused McCarthy of snoring during meetings and rebuffing Rodgers' suggestions that he needed to make more of an effort of connecting with the Steelers' defensive players.

Rodgers added that the "final straw" came during half-time of a game when McCarthy closed a team talk by shouting 'one, two, three, Packers!'.

Rodgers and McCarthy spent 13 seasons together with the Packers from 2006-2018, reaching the playoffs nine times and winning one Super Bowl. They were reunited this past offseason when the Steelers hired McCarthy following the departure of Mike Tomlin.

"I'm not sure what's next for Aaron Rodgers. I think he will figure out that for himself over the next handful of months, or whatever his particular process is," said Super Bowl champion and Sky Sports NFL expert Ndamukong Suh.

"But I want to go back to what Jason [Bell] said in the way that you handle your business, especially from the perspective of a star. There's a way you go about handling things.

"Those things should be handled behind closed doors before trying to get ahead of the story - on either side. Have a conversation, have an understanding, and then go from there and address it.

"Going out to the media and doing all of these different things, The Pat McAfee Show, it's great for them and great for ESPN, but it's not the proper way I would want to handle that particular scenario."

Responding to Rodgers' comments, McCarthy admitted to his "embarrassing" moment when mentioning the Packers during half-time of their defeat to the Philadelphia Eagles in Week 11.

Steelers president Art Rooney II defended McCarthy on Thursday in an interview with the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette, insisting he supports him "100 per cent".

"I just want to say that I have 100 per cent confidence in Mike McCarthy and I expect he'll be our coach this season, next season and beyond," Mr Rooney II told the Post-Gazette.

"The big thing, what happened this week, is insubordination can't be tolerated in any organization. When you start getting ultimatums, you can't live with that. There's maybe a time and place after the season if he wanted to kind of unload.

"He talks about wanting to be a good teammate, but you don't do that to your teammates a couple days before the game, that's for sure."

Rooney II underlined his belief that events in the locker room should be kept within the locker room, and downplayed Rodgers' comments regarding concerns for McCarthy's health.

The Steelers are 6-4 as they prepare to face the Denver Broncos (8-2) on Black Friday.
