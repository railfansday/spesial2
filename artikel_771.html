Explosive Fury vs Klitschko canvas row revisited

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

On November 28 in 2015 Tyson Fury toppled the man who had dominated the heavyweight division when he beat Wladimir Klitschko in Dusseldorf; But he almost had to scuttle the contest on the day of the fight in an explosive row over the ring surface, which saw Vitali Klitschko weighing in

On this day in 2015 Tyson Fury scaled his Everest. He dethroned Wladimir Klitschko, the dominant heavyweight of that era, to win the unified WBO, IBF and WBA world heavyweight championships.

It was the goal Fury had targeted throughout his career. Klitschko had ruled the division for a decade and was selling out stadiums in Germany.

Fury travelled to Dusseldorf as the underdog but utterly confident he had the tools to unravel Klitschko.

However on the day of the fight, an explosive row at the Esprit Arena threatened to scuttle the whole event.

Peter Fury, Tyson's uncle and then trainer, decided he wanted to inspect the ring in advance. Then they spotted something they hadn't expected. Feeling the canvas, they realised that the layers under it were spongy.

Tyson Fury climbed through the ropes to bounce on the balls of his feet on the canvas where he was due, a few hours later, to stage a remarkable win.

"No way you can fight on that," his father John Fury said.

"You know what they've done that for," the then heavyweight title challenger said, "so I can't move."

Speaking to the camera, Tyson Fury declared: "They must have half a foot deep of sponge on the canvas. It's like training on a beach on the sand. It's absolutely ridiculous and I can tell you now, Sky Sports viewers, there's no fight if that ring ain't sorted.

"I'm a mover and I move, that's the way I fight. I can't move in that ring."

History could have been very different. If he'd gone ahead with the fight, he could - probably would - have lost. He certainly wouldn't have been able to box in the way he did so successfully later that night.

"My fighter is not fighting in quicksand," his trainer Peter Fury declared at the time.

They undid the ring canvas to look underneath; there were two soft layers that they insisted had to be removed.

Vitali Klitschko, Wladimir's older brother and a fearsome former world heavyweight champion himself, was on hand to respond. He stood in the ring himself, towering over the top rope and glowering down at Fury's team on the floor of the arena.

Mick Hennessy, Fury's promoter, told him: "You're trying to control this but it's not in the interest of health and safety. So we need it sorted.

"Your ankle or your knee could go on this, it's too thick."

Vitali, a formidable advocate for his brother, argued fiercely, gesturing animatedly as he said: "Both fighters have the same conditions. Same gloves, same ring and you're unhappy always.

"Everything you say is excuses," he roared. "Do you want to fight or not?"

Fury's team weren't bluffing though. They were prepared to walk away.

John Fury raged at Bernd Boente, Klitschko's manager, in a corridor at the arena. He told him directly: "The fight's off, forget about it." Then he stormed off.

He later said: "If they hadn't replaced it, there wouldn't have been a fight."

Fury's team got their way. The offending layers were removed and Fury did indeed use his footwork and mobile long-range boxing to defuse Klitschko, a dangerous puncher, so adept normally at controlling distance but who just couldn't pin down an inspired Fury.

That blazing row did change the course of the heavyweight division. It was an important element in a monumental victory for the Englishman. Fury became the dominant figure in the weight class. He was unable to meet Klitschko again in a rematch; struggling with depression, he stepped away from the sport.

But Fury's mental health improved and he was able to make a famous comeback. He cemented his legend against Deontay Wilder, becoming a two-time heavyweight world champion without losing a fight.

Now he has the chance to become a three-time champion when he fights his great rival Anthony Joshua for the first time with a vacant WBA title on the line.

That climactic fight is two weeks away as Fury looks to start the final chapter in a championship story that began 11 years ago today.
