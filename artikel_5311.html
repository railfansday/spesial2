12 numbers that define Denny Hamlin’s NASCAR career as he weighs retiring

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Denny Hamlin has 64 Cup wins and no championship. 12 numbers show why he is torn about retiring after 2027.

NASCAR fans tend to assume the Cup Series champion is the driver who was best all season. Under the sport's playoff format, the title goes to the driver who runs best in the final stretch of races, and that is not always the same person. In 2026 it wasn't. Denny Hamlin posted the best average finish of any Cup driver that year, according to FOX Sports, but Kyle Larson won the championship. Larson made no claim to the stronger season. "This was statistically probably one of our worst years," Larson told The Associated Press. Under these rules, a driver can win races for two decades and never become champion, and that is what has happened to Hamlin.

Hamlin is still winning, still without a title and now close to the end of his career. Age has not yet shown up in his results, and 2026 was the most consistent season of his career. His time to add a title is short, though, because his current contract runs through the 2027 Cup season, according to Heavy. Hamlin has acknowledged that a driver cannot take back a retirement once he announces it, so he has to decide while he is still winning.

NASCAR.com summed up Hamlin's career through its key figures as he considered whether to retire. Those figures cover his record at Pocono Raceway, the Daytona 500 he has won more than once, the championship he has never won, and his own words about aging and when to step away. Some of the numbers argue for staying and others argue for leaving, and the slides that follow weigh which side each one supports. The question behind every slide is whether one more season can bring Hamlin the title that two decades of winning have not.

Denny Hamlin has 64 Cup wins, ninth in Cup Series history, according to NASCAR.com, and he reached that total while still racing in his mid-40s. Richard Petty holds the record with 200 wins, a total from an earlier era that no current driver can expect to reach. Hamlin already belongs among the sport’s great winners. What he lacks lies elsewhere.

Hamlin is still adding to that total. His count began with his first Cup win in 2006, and after the late races of 2026 he still appeared as an active driver among the sport's all-time winners, according to NASCAR.com. His winning has also held up with age, and he has won more than once in each of the last eight seasons. That record shows steady success year after year rather than a late burst. Whenever he walks away, he will leave while the wins are still coming, not after they have dried up.

For a driver with this record, staying on makes sense only if he can still win. Hamlin rejects the idea of hanging on just to fill a seat. "I'm just too much of a competitor to just go out there and make laps," Hamlin told NASCAR.com. He judges any extra season by whether he can contend, the same standard that produced his career total. The wins prove he can still compete, and the decision turns on what they haven't brought him.

A career built on this much winning usually ends with a championship, and a championship is the one thing missing from Hamlin's. For all those victories, he has won zero Cup Series titles, according to NASCAR.com. He has come close enough for the zero to hurt, finishing second in the standings in 2010 and again in 2025. Kyle Larson won the 2025 title at Phoenix, according to The Associated Press. One near miss came early in Hamlin's career and the other came late in it, so the missing title is a pattern rather than one unlucky year.

NASCAR's format rewards the driver who is sharpest when the title is decided, and one driver of Hamlin's era has managed that again and again. Larson has won three Cup titles, according to The Associated Press. His record shows that a driver of Hamlin's generation can win the championship more than once under this format. The question about Hamlin, then, is not whether he is good enough. It is whether he can put his best stretch of the season into the 10 races that decide the title.

In 2026, the title was settled before Hamlin could do anything about it in the last race. Larson clinched the championship in the finale's opening stage, The Associated Press reports, and Hamlin was not even his closest challenger in points going into that race. Hamlin judged the year as a strong one with a single clear failure. "I guess the lows (are) not winning it all. Other than that, we sustained pretty good performance through the whole year," Hamlin said.

His wins are already in the record book, but the title is not, and the chance to change that zero gives him his strongest reason to race one more season.

Hamlin went into the 2026 playoff as the top seed and still ended the year third in the standings, NASCAR.com reports, with Kyle Larson taking the championship. He wasn’t even the strongest title threat on his own team. Christopher Bell, his Joe Gibbs Racing teammate, entered the finale as Larson’s closest challenger, according to The Associated Press.

Hamlin lost the championship over several weeks, not in one bad night. Larson took the points lead from him after the third playoff race, RACER reports, and Hamlin never got it back. By the last race he trailed by 70 points and needed an outright win, and Larson clinched the title in the finale’s first stage.

Hamlin traces Larson's title to how hard Larson drives. "There is a little bit more variance in the results because of how hard he pushes it, but he reaps the benefits because he gets lots of wins," Hamlin told RACER. By his own account, he lost to a driver who accepts more risk and collects wins because of it. Hamlin didn't treat the result as a wound either. Asked how losing the title felt, he said he felt nothing.

For a driver weighing one more year, the result supports both sides of the decision. He showed in 2026 that he can still earn the top seed, which suggests another title run is possible, but the same season shows how quickly the playoff can take that advantage away.

A driver weighing retirement has usually stopped winning, and Hamlin's 2026 shows the opposite. He won four races, NASCAR.com reports, with an early victory at Las Vegas and then three straight wins in a summer run at Nashville, Tenn., Michigan and Pocono. Winning more than once a year is routine for him, and 2026 extended his streak of multi-win seasons to eight. That makes this season part of a pattern that has run for most of a decade. The real question is when he won those races.

Hamlin still had the speed to win in 2026. Of the four races in which he ran the fastest laps, he won three, according to MotorBiscuit. On the days he was quickest on the track, he usually finished first. For a driver deciding whether to step away, that kind of pace is a sign that he hasn't slowed down.

Hamlin started the title fight as the favorite on paper, after he won the regular season and took the No. 1 seed. He then went without a win across the 10-race Chase, according to MotorBiscuit. Over that many races, a winless stretch means a real dip in form, not one bad afternoon. Hamlin admitted the drop himself. "It did drop a little bit in The Chase; there's no doubt about that if you look at average running position, things like that," Hamlin said.

Four wins prove he can still win races. What 2027 has to show is whether he can do it when the championship is at stake.

Hamlin averaged an 8.9 finish in 2026, the best of his career, according to NASCAR.com, and the best of any full-season driver. Tyler Reddick came closest with a 12.2 average, according to FOX Sports.

Hamlin could only average that low by running near the front almost every weekend, and he did. He placed in the top 10 in 24 races, more than in any season since 2021, so his average came from good results week after week rather than from a handful of standout days. Hamlin felt that consistency from behind the wheel. "This was the most put-together year I think I've ever had," he said. For a driver weighing retirement, this steadiness is the strongest part of the record, because it shows he was complete across the whole year and not only fast now and then.

Hamlin earned those finishes during the races rather than relying on where he started them. His average starting position was 9.8, according to FOX Sports, and on a typical weekend he finished ahead of where he began. A driver gains those spots through strategy, passing and staying out of trouble, and that work depends on him more than on the raw speed of his car.

In 2026, Hamlin raced as a driver at the top of his craft, not one hanging on.

Most drivers have a track that suits them, and Hamlin's is Pocono Raceway, where his eight Cup wins are the most any driver has earned at the track, according to NASCAR.com. That record is more than a personal statistic, because it is the standard every other Cup driver at Pocono is chasing. For a driver who has never won a championship, the Pocono mark is one record that belongs to him alone.

Pocono is where Hamlin's winning began, and it is still where he wins, which matters to a driver deciding when to stop. He won his first Cup race there in 2006. He earned his eighth win at the track in June 2026, according to Motorsport.com, in the season he is now weighing as a possible farewell. If age were wearing down his driving, a decline would likely show at his best track, and his latest result there shows none. That gives the case for staying one of its strongest arguments: he is still winning where he has always won.

Hamlin didn’t just win at Pocono in 2026. He won comfortably, finishing 1.678 seconds ahead of runner-up Tyler Reddick, according to Motorsport.com.

At Pocono, Hamlin has shown he can still win where he has won before. He has yet to show he can win when a championship depends on the result.

A missing championship hasn't stopped Hamlin from collecting the sport's biggest single-race trophies. NASCAR.com reports that Hamlin took the Daytona 500 in 2016 and again in consecutive years, 2019 and 2020, for three wins in all. His trophies from the sport's other crown-jewel races include three Southern 500s and a Coca-Cola $KO 600. Whatever has kept him from a championship, it isn't an inability to win when a single race carries the most prestige.

Hamlin very nearly lost the first of those Daytona wins. He beat Martin Truex Jr. by 0.010 seconds in 2016, the closest finish in Daytona 500 history, according to CBC. Hamlin gave much of the credit for that win to the drivers around him. "But this wouldn't be possible if it wasn't for Toyotas sticking together all race long," he said. At Daytona, a driver needs other cars to push him through the draft.

As Hamlin weighs one more season, his Daytona record gives him reasons both to stay and to leave. He has the skill and composure to win the sport's biggest single day, and that is a reason to keep chasing major prizes. But winning one race by a margin that small, with help from other drivers, is a different test from winning a title over a full playoff.

Hamlin has shown on three occasions that he can win the Daytona 500. What he has never done is carry that kind of form through a full playoff to a title.

Denny Hamlin will have raced 22 Cup Series seasons if he drives through the end of his current contract in 2027, according to NASCAR.com. Heavy reports that he has driven for Joe Gibbs Racing throughout his Cup career. The 22 is a measure of loyalty as much as longevity, because every one of those seasons would belong to the same team.

Hamlin left himself room even after he signed. He agreed to the extension in July 2025, and when he was asked about it after the announcement, he said it would probably be his last contract, according to Heavy. That makes 22 seasons the earliest plausible stopping point, not a farewell he has scheduled. A driver who hedges about his final deal is saving the decision for later, so the last year of the contract settles nothing by itself.

Hamlin will base his exit on his ability to win races, and the number of years behind him plays no part in it. "I want the ability to know I can win my last race," Hamlin told Heavy. His test and the contract's end date are at odds, because as long as he keeps winning, reaching the end of the deal becomes a reason to continue rather than to stop. Joe Gibbs Racing President Dave Alpern judges him by the same standard, and he says Hamlin can win in any given week and that he can't tell yet when age will slow him down. With the driver and the team both measuring him by wins, and neither seeing the end, the count could climb past 22.

The 22nd season is less a scheduled goodbye than the year that shows whether he still passes his own test.

Most drivers retire when their results force them out, but Hamlin has set his own rule. At 46, he will keep racing only as long as he can contend for wins, NASCAR.com reports, and he is weighing an exit now because of the aging he notices in his daily life, not in his finishes.

Hamlin expects age to catch up with him even while he believes he is driving as well as he ever has. "Father Time is undefeated. I can feel it," Hamlin said. He says the changes are already plain to him in everyday life, and he expects them to reach his racing eventually. For him, the open question is when the decline arrives on the track, not whether he is good enough today. Part of his decision, then, rests on his own sense of how age is affecting him, not only on what his results show. Waiting for his results to fall off could mean breaking his own rule, because by then he might already have raced a season without contending.

Any 2027 season is a bet that he can make one more real run at the title before the decline he feels reaches the race track.

The strongest push for a longer career is coming from Hamlin's team rather than from Hamlin himself. Owner Joe Gibbs has begun pressing Hamlin to keep racing, NASCAR.com reports, and the team hopes to have him behind the wheel in 2028. That season comes after his current deal ends, so the team is asking for more than he has agreed to give. Another season is a standing offer, not a hypothetical. The pressure from his owner shows that Joe Gibbs Racing still sees him as a driver worth keeping in the car.

Hamlin measures his remaining time in chances to win, while his team measures it in seasons, and the two do not produce the same answer. He also describes what lies ahead in narrow terms. "But we've only got one more shot at this," Hamlin said. He sees his future as a single chance, while his team is already planning for 2028. The case for staying depends on whether a title is still within reach, not on how long he can keep racing.

Hamlin does not plan to settle the question inside his own garage. He intends to consult Kevin Harvick, a retired driver who has already made the choice Hamlin faces now, so he will weigh the team's push against advice from outside it.

Joe Gibbs Racing can make the offer, but only Hamlin can judge whether one more season is a real shot at a title or just more laps.

Hamlin reached the part of 2026 that decides the championship with less energy than at any other point in the year. He didn’t use fatigue as an excuse, but he didn’t hide it either. “I think I certainly probably burned myself out by the end of the year,” Hamlin said. By his own account, he used up much of the energy he needed to turn the strongest regular season in the field into a title.

The dip in his form was slight, but it came in exactly the weeks when NASCAR awards its championship. The open question is whether he can reach the final stretch of a season with enough left to contend.

For a driver weighing one last season, stamina at the end of the year matters more than speed at the start of it.

Hamlin is asking his team to share responsibility for his final push for a title. He wants Joe Gibbs Racing to improve his No. 11 team for 2027, according to NASCAR.com, and that makes whatever the organization does this offseason part of how his career ends. He did not present the request as a complaint. He paired it with a promise to raise his own game. "And it's no secret — I definitely want Joe Gibbs Racing to look in the mirror as well and figure out how they can make my (No.) 11 team better next year and what we need to do to be better, because I'm certainly going to do that as a driver," Hamlin said. By tying his effort to the team's in public, he has asked owner Joe Gibbs's organization to match him. The question for 2027 is less whether Hamlin still has the skill and more whether the people around him will give him a car worthy of his last contracted season.

Hamlin’s request concerns performance, not pay. He has said he would not race two or three more years unless he could win every weekend, which rules out a long, gradual exit.

A better car won’t decide his future by itself, but without one, there’s no 2028.
