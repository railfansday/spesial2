Brian Schottenheimer on failed fourth-down try: "I trust our quarterback and our offense"

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Eagles’ path to erasing a 31-10 third-quarter deficit included many favorable twists and turns. The last one was a gift from the Cowboys.

With 55 seconds left — and with the Eagles having no timeouts — the Cowboys faced fourth and one from the Philadelphia 39. After attempting to get the Eagles to jump offside, the Cowboys took a timeout and decided to go for it.

The Cowboys lined up in shotgun formation. Quarterback Dak Prescott looked to the right, presumably to connect on a slant to receiver CeeDee Lamb. Lamb was jammed hard by cornerback Quinyon Mitchell. Prescott waited for Lamb to get free. When Prescott decided to pursue another option, he was sacked.

The play lost nine yards, giving the Eagles the ball at their own 48.

Why not punt? Why not force quarterback Joe Milton III to drive into field-goal range, with less than 50 seconds to play and no timeouts?

Coach Brian Schottenheimer was asked about the decision after the game.

“Really felt good about the drive we had going, and felt really good that we were gonna put the game away there,” Schottenheimer told reporters. “And I wanted the ball in Dak’s hands. And, unfortunately, we weren’t able to make it work.”

Does Schottenheimer ever second-guess himself?

“All the time,” he said. “Not in that situation. Absolutely not in that situation.”

“I trust our quarterback and our offense,” Schottenheimer said.

That’s fine. But it’s not about “trust.” It’s about whether it makes sense to assume the offense will execute in that moment, and what the consequence will be if the defense executes a little better.

It’s about making an assessment, based on years of watching, playing, and coaching football, as to whether the risk of not converting outweighed the risk of the Eagles driving from their own 20 (or perhaps deep inside their own 20) into field goal range.

Maybe the truth is that Schottenheimer didn’t trust his defense to keep that from happening. Regardless, his “trust” in the offense was ultimately misplaced. And the Cowboys lost another game when they scored 30 or more points.
