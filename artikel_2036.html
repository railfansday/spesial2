What happened weeks before Ian Holloway's Swindon Town sacking is revealing

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Ian Holloway has been dismissed as Swindon Town boss following their 2-0 defeat at Newport County, but the search for a new manager has been ongoing at the County Ground for several weeks now.

Holloway was one of the managers who came into the new season under a little bit of pressure, mainly due to how last season ended. The Robins were sitting second in mid-February but fell out of the play-offs completely after four wins in their final 15 games.

The former Blackpool and Crystal Palace boss still had some credit in the bank after guiding Swindon from relegation danger in his first half-season at the club, but after picking up just 19 points from 16 games to start this term, the higher-ups at the County Ground have pulled the trigger.

This hasn't been a rash decision, though, and apparently one that they've been preparing for for around a month.

According to the Moonraker, Swindon's search for a new manager to replace Ian Holloway has been ongoing "in some form or another" for over a month.

The Robins endured a mixed start to the 2026/27 campaign. They won each of their opening three games at the County Ground, but paired that with terrible away form, picking up just one point from their first five on the road.

Before that 1-0 defeat at Rochdale, which made it four losses from five away, Holloway labelled himself a "dead man walking" if he couldn't help turn their form around amidst a terrible injury crisis. It turns out, from this report, that he was starting to become one.

In fact, a shock 3-1 win at league leaders Barnet just over a fortnight later, in turn handing them their first league defeat of the season and their first league loss since March, seemed to give him an extra bit of time, especially as that was followed up by a draw at Bristol Rovers and a win over Rotherham United on Halloween.

It's been four straight defeats in all competitions since then, though, albeit two away against League One sides MK Dons and Plymouth Argyle in the FA Cup and Jersey Mike's Trophy, respectively.

That's been enough to earn him the sack, and that work which has been done in the background will now need to be the full focus for the higher-ups at Swindon.

It's probably not an unpopular belief that Holloway may have been dismissed after that Barnet game if the result went the way many expected it to go. Not only were Swindon winless in their first five on the road, but they hadn't scored, either.

In the end, while the win against the high-flying Bees was impressive, it was done by unsustainable means. The Robins were 3-0 up at half-time, having scored all their shots on target. Meanwhile, Dean Brennan's side created 2.54xG in that first half, with Conor Ripley having to make eight saves before the break. He ended the game with 11.

A win against a hapless Rotherham side isn't exactly convincing, either, and, ultimately, their other wins outside of that Barnet game haven't aged that well at all.

Cheltenham Town aside, they prevailed against Port Vale in September, who took a while to shake off their relegation hangover, and Colchester United, who are among the bottom four. They've also not scored away from home in the league since that Barnet game.

In the end, it seemed as if Swindon wanted Holloway to turn things around, but had seen enough after another defeat to nil on the road at Newport.

An exchange with a fan after the game that the former Robins boss needed to be restrained by stewards for meant that the writing was on the wall.
