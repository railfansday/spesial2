If You’d Bought Oklo at Its SPAC Debut, Here’s What You’d Have Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Oklo stock has had a rocky life on Wall Street.

By Steven Porrello – Nov 23, 2026 at 2:15AM EST

Let's not beat around the bush: If you had invested $1,000 in Oklo (OKLO -4.75%) when it debuted on May 10, 2024, your investment would be worth about $1,332 as of Nov. 2.

That's roughly a 33% gain in about two and a half years, assuming you bought Oklo at its opening price (about $15.50) and held on until today.

If you had purchased Oklo near its debut closing price, your investment would be worth more: about $2,443, or a roughly 144% return.

Oklo, which is designing a small nuclear reactor, has had a short but eventful performance on Wall Street. Since its debut, the nuclear energy stock shot up to an all-time high of $194 before a brutal sell-off brought its share price crashing back to roughly $20 today.

The company's meteoric rise and spectacular fall can be traced, in part, to its connection to artificial intelligence (AI) and data centers.

Its small nuclear reactors, which promise to supply round-the-clock power, have often been seen as a solution to a potential energy crisis brought on by electricity demands outpacing supply. That narrative worked in its favor last year when Wall Street was generally bullish on AI. Lately, however, the tables have turned, and deep uncertainty around the technology has made Oklo lose its shine.

Amazingly, the stock still trades at a premium: It carries a $4 billion market valuation with almost no revenue to show for it.

As such, Oklo remains a high-risk, high-reward play on the future of energy. Investors bullish on nuclear power but wary of betting on a single company might want to consider a nuclear energy exchange-traded fund (ETF) instead.
