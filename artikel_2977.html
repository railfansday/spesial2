Why Didn't Tilray Brands Make a Bid for Aurora Cannabis?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Multiple companies have made offers for Aurora's business this year, but Tilray hasn't shown any interest.

Aurora Cannabis (ACB -6.68%) has been a surprisingly hot stock of late. Its value has been on the rise as both Curaleaf Holdings and Organigram Global have made offers for its business. Aurora has been encouraging investors to reject Curaleaf's takeover attempt and to instead agree to a friendly deal the company recently reached with Organigram.

What may be surprising, however, is that Tilray Brands (TLRY -3.70%), which has been hungry for growth and no stranger to acquisitions in recent years, didn't make a bid or appear interested. Here's why I think it didn't bother with a move that could have grown its market share in both Canada and international cannabis markets.

In recent years, Tilray has been in acquisition mode, but there are a couple of things to note about its recent moves. The first is that the Canadian-based cannabis company has mainly been acquiring beverage companies, and the second is that many of them operate in the U.S. market, as it has now become one of the largest craft brewing companies in the country.

I believe it's doing this for a couple of reasons. In addition to growing sales, it appears eager to build a presence in the U.S. market however it can. Beverages are a logical way to diversify its operations, and in the future, it could transform those facilities should it need to. Plus, cannabis-infused beverages are one of the fastest-growing areas of the entire marijuana industry, so there is some potential overlap there.

Meanwhile, investing in a Canadian producer such as Aurora in a market where competition is intense may simply not be all that enticing for Tilray. Recently, the company announced it was consolidating some of its operations in an effort to focus on profitability. Acquiring one of the larger cannabis companies in Canada may simply not make sense given its long-term strategy.

Aurora's stock is up this year due to multiple acquisition attempts for its business. Otherwise, it might be on a trajectory similar to Tilray's stock, which is down more than 70% this year.

It's been a downright horrendous year for Tilray. While investors may be tempted to take a position in the company because, if the U.S. moves forward with legalization in the future, the stock could take off, it's a highly risky position.

A better move may be to simply take a wait-and-see approach and put the stock on a watch list. If legalization takes place in the U.S., there will be plenty of upside for Tilray; there's no need to rush to buy the stock today, when there's still nothing on the horizon.
