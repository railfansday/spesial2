Downton Abbey’s 3 Butlers & How Long They Served The Crawleys

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Three faithful butlers served Downton Abbey in its six seasons and three feature films. Created by Julian Fellowes, Downton Abbey depicted the trials and triumphs of the Crawley family from 1912, when the hit ITV and PBS Masterpiece series began, to 1930, the year Downton Abbey: The Grand Finale closed out the beloved saga.

Downton Abbey itself has stood for centuries, with the Crawley family owning the estate since before 1772, when the first Earl of Grantham was created. We know that Violet Crawley (Maggie Smith) became the Countess of Grantham before 1860 after marrying Patrick Crawley, the 6th Earl of Grantham. In the late 19th century, Downton Abbey's butler was Mr. Mountjoy, who was frightened of Lady Grantham.

Only one butler served Downton Abbey throughout the series, until a changeover took place in the sixth season's Christmas special, which was also Downton Abbey's series finale. Remarkably, the three Downton Abbey movies featured all three butlers serving the Crawleys at various points, throughout the King and Queen's visit and a movie being filmed at Downton before Lady Mary Talbot (Michelle Dockery) formally took over running the great house from Robert Crawley (Hugh Bonneville).

Charles Carson (Jim Carter) is Downton Abbey's definitive butler, and the longest-serving we've seen throughout the series. Along with Batman's butler, Alfred Pennyworth, Carson is perhaps the most famous butler in pop culture. It's unclear when, exactly, in the late 19th century Carson succeeded Mr. Mountjoy, but Charles was Downton's butler until he retired in 1925 due to palsy.

The Gilded Age takes place over twenty years before Downton Abbey even begins, but Aunt Ada is forward-thinking in terms of modern technology.

A former song and dance man, Carson joined Downton Abbey's staff as second footman in 1875 and rose through the ranks. Following his retirement in 1925, Carson returned as butler temporarily during King George (Simon Jones) and Queen Mary's (Geraldine James) visit to Downton in 1927. Carson then served two more years as butler from 1928 to 1930 as he trained Andrew Parker (Michael Fox) to succeed him.

Thomas Barrow (Robert James-Collier) succeeded Charles Carson as Downton Abbey's butler in 1925, and he served about 3 years before resigning in 1928. Barrow then moved to California to work for film star Guy Dexter (Dominic West), whom he was also romantically involved with, as seen in Downton Abbey: A New Era.

Barrow lived and worked at Downton Abbey for 18 years as a junior footman, first footman, valet to the Earl of Grantham, and under-butler. Nefarious for many years, Barrow had a change of heart after he briefly worked as the butler at Dryden Park, a job he intensely disliked. Barrow was asked to succeed Carson as butler in Downton Abbey's series finale.

In 1930, Andrew Parker became the Crawley family's butler, as seen in Downton Abbey: The Grand Finale. Andrew joined the Crawleys' staff as a temporary hire during Lady Rose McClare's (Lily James) 1924 wedding in London. Andy then became second footman and moved to Downton Abbey in Yorkshire.

Andrew and Daisy (Sophie McShera) married in between the first and second Downton Abbey movies.

After Barrow gave his notice in 1928, Carson returned as butler to train Andrew as his replacement, a process that took about two years. Carson then retired for good, and Andrew formally became Downton's butler. If Downton Abbey ever returns for a sequel series, Andrew Parker will most likely still be the butler for Lady Mary and her heir, George Crawley (Oliver and Zac Barker).
