Tony Gonzalez calls Aaron Rodgers 'selfish' after Steelers flame-out

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Pittsburgh Steelers face the Denver Broncos on Black Friday in their first game since their stunning, midseason breakup with Aaron Rodgers.

The Steelers released Rodgers after the 42-year-old quarterback went scorched-earth against his now-former coach, Mike McCarthy, in an appearance on "The Pat McAfee Show." Rodgers accused McCarthy of sleeping in team meetings and breaking down a Steelers huddle with a "1, 2, 3, Packers!" chant among a litany of grievances following his benching in favor of Mason Rudolph.

Rodgers publicly blasting McCarthy has been met with the condemnation of many former NFL players, so it was little surprise to see Amazon Prime's studio panel rail against the quarterback's comments ahead of the Steelers vs. Broncos game.

Tony Gonzalez was particularly perturbed by Rodgers' actions, referring to them as "selfish" and the entire situation as "sad."

"It's really sad for everybody," Gonzalez said. "For me, for Aaron Rodgers to do what he did, it's a selfish thing to do. It makes no sense. You're talking (out) two sides of your mouth when you say it's insane the way Mike McCarthy's acting, but you're the one who vouched for him to come over here and get him in the building."

"Then, you nuke everybody," he added. "You nuke your teammates, a playoff football team. I just think it makes no sense."

Gonzalez, who played 17 NFL seasons before transitioning to broadcasting in 2014, also acknowledged he could not recall a blowup as explosive as Rodgers' across any sport.

"It's one of those things that's gonna go down in history. I've never seen anything like this in my life," Gonzalez opined. "And we've played this game for a long time, and not just in football, basketball, hockey, it is unprecedented in what went on."

"It's like a little hint of narcissism on his point, to come out on air on 'The Pat McAfee Show' and do what he did," he added. "And there's no excuse for it."

Meanwhile, Ryan Fitzpatrick referred to Rodgers as both "one of the greatest quarterbacks of all time" but also noted the 42-year-old is "one of the most complicated athletes of all time."

What did Fitzpatrick mean by that? He outlined that Rodgers has almost universally been loved and appreciated by his teammates; but at what may be his final stop, he left them out to dry following his comments on "The Pat McAfee Show" before also effectively trying to dispose of McCarthy to save himself.

"The complicated part is, what did he do? He blew everything up. He sees the locker room like a chessboard is what I feel like. All the pieces he can manipulate and move – they're very precious to him as long as they're protecting the king. But as soon as they don't have value and they can't protect the king, then all of sudden they become expendable. He throws them out, and that's what happened with Mike McCarthy."

"You look back to Week 1 – the moment they had – when he gave him the game ball and how much raw emotion there was and how much it meant to him and how much he knew it meant to Mike," Fitzpatrick added. "Fast forward three months later and we have what we have. He sacrificed him as a second thought in his career and I don't think it was the right thing to do."

Richard Sherman was in agreement with both Fitzpatrick and Gonzalez about Rodgers making the wrong choice about the manner in which to air his grievances.

However, Sherman also acknowledged Rodgers' criticism of McCarthy as being warranted, and the former Seattle Seahawks star openly wondered how that might impact the 63-year-old's job security moving forward.

"I don't think it was the right thing to do at all," Sherman said, referencing Rodgers' comments. "I also know that a child that isn't embraced by the village will burn the village down to feel its warmth. And I think that's what Aaron Rodgers did."

"He felt betrayed by the front office, the coaching staff and Mike McCarthy," Sherman explained. "But I think even if you say, 'Hey, this is an indictment of Aaron Rodgers,' (it's) terrible what he did, I agree. It is a sacred place – the locker room. But that's true. The things he said are true. It is also an indictment on Mike McCarthy and the culture that he's built."

"You talk about the Pittsburgh Steelers and what that logo stands for," he continued. "It stands for stability. It's regal. It's respected in the National Football League and you never think of anything like this happening. Dysfunction. What did Tomlin say? It's a dumpster fire? You've never heard those things associated with the Pittsburgh Steelers. Their head coaches coach for 15, 20 years – each one of them. I don't see how Mike McCarthy follows in those footsteps after what's happened."

McCarthy will get a chance to state his case to remain the Steelers' leader as the 2026 NFL season continues. His first opportunity to right the ship will come on Black Friday against the Broncos.

As for Rodgers, he could consider looking for another opportunity after his flame-out, but more likely, the soon-to-be 43-year-old will ride into the sunset to close a 22-year NFL career.
