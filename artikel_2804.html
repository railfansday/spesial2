Packers By The Numbers: Stats from the Week 10 loss to the Vikings

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Did you all know that they keep numbers even in games where your team loses by 20? Feels pretty rude to me.

As we do every week in this space, we're looking at some stats from the Green Bay Packers' most recent game, courtesy of SumerSports. Today we're looking at the 30-10 loss to the Minnesota Vikings in Week 10.

The Packers wanted to operate out of a heavy offense this week. Coming into this game, the Packers used 11 personnel (1 RB, 1 TE, 3 WR) on 62.0% of their offensive snaps, the 10th highest rate in the league. They clearly wanted to try to find ways to protect Jordan Love, so they were only in 11 personnel on 35.2% of their snaps. That was their lowest rate of 11 personnel since the 47.8% mark they posted in their Week 1 game against the Vikings.

That 5.6% personnel in the "Other" category is their use of a 6th offensive lineman. They hadn't used that personnel group since their Week 1 game (they had 4 snaps with a 6th offensive lineman in that game).

As I thought when watching live, their pass game looked to be operating best when they were out of 11 personnel. This doesn't show the breakdown between their tempo and non-tempo snaps, but I would expect their EPA when going tempo out of 11 was their best way to operate.

Did going heavier help protect Love? Not really! He was pressured on 56.5% of his dropbacks, despite posting an absurdly quick 2.21 seconds to throw. The Vikings averaged 2.3 seconds to pressure.

I did look to see what the pressure rate was when the Packers were in 11 personnel to see if he had any more time. Perhaps that personnel helped to spread out the defense and slightly mitigate the rush. Nope! Love was pressured on 72.2% of those dropbacks with an average time to pressure of 2.25 seconds. The quickest pressure on a snap in 11 personnel was 1.4 seconds, which is insane. So Love seemed to be operating at his best when they could spread-and-shred, but he was under immense fire on those snaps.

Matthew Golden had another brutal day in terms of drops, but he was also the most productive receiver for the Packers, posting an EPA of 9.28 on a 31.0% target share. He did some nice work on late downs, posting a +3.6 EPA. He did most of his damage from the outside, with +2.12 EPA when lined up wide and +0.82 when in the slot.

Kaleb Johnson continues to show promise as a pure runner. He's not much of a receiving back, but he shows good burst and power when running as a straight-ahead runner.

The main scheme that Johnson had success with was when the Packers were pulling linemen. It's something we haven't really seen much from them over the past few weeks, but it worked pretty well for them today when Johnson was running.

If he can continue to hold onto the ball, I like what I've seen from Johnson this year.

The Packers worked hard to get pressure on Kirk Cousins, blitzing on 44.4% of snaps. And it worked when they were able to get pressure, but Cousins was getting rid of the ball quickly (2.35 average time to throw) so the Packers only notched a pressure rate of 30.6%.
