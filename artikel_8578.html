Personal Finance (Not Investing) • Re: Big house, cheap mortgage rate, ongoing deficit: stay or sell?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Some bigger picture questions I would be thinking about: How much do you spend per year? Does your current income cover it? If not, how much do you need to withdraw from your portfolio to meet your spending needs? Is it more than 4%? What’s your plan for stopping work altogether? Is your portfolio large enough to support you when you do that?

I think this is less about the specific details of how to rent out the house optimally, and more about the overall portfolio, what it needs to support, and how the equity in the house it not doing much for you right now and creating cashflow issues.

Personally, If it were me, I would sell the house, buy something cheaper in cash, and invest the rest according to my investment policy statement. But I don’t like cashflow problems and I do like money compounding in the market. If you sold the house, bought something for 800k, that would be 700k going into your existing 1m portfolio putting you at 1.7m. Can you let that compound and live off what you earn and the 500k e fund? If your 1.7m doubles in 10 years you’ll have 3.4m, 136k spend per year at 4%, plus social security.
These are all great questions I would say I spend at least $120,000 a year but I’m actively trying to cut my expenses closer to $100,000 a year. Housing itself is about 60,000 a year plus a $600 car payment plus all the other essentials I would say I am roughly at a $10,000 a month all in essentials Number while my Social Security and disability income is closer to 4500/mo before any part time work or rental income. So my gap on a monthly basis that I need to close is $5000 a month or $60,000 a year. With about $1 million and Investments I would say 4% is about 40,000 a year so leaves a short fall of about 20,000 but I could easily close that by renting a room.
My dilemma is I did not plan to start withdrawing 4% at the age of 59. I thought I would start that at 65. I want to be sure I don’t run out of money in my 401(k) by doing that I am going to do some modeling with a financial advisor at Fidelity. Hopefully can shed some light on how long my retirement money would last if I start withdrawing it early.
My Social Security amount is the same as my Social Security disability amount so when I turn 67 my Social Security disability basically just converts to Social Security so they amount doesn’t change. It will be about 3200 a month I will lose my $1100 a month private disability policy when I turned 65 it disappears so my income goes down yet again.
