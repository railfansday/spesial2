Sebastian Stan’s New Movie Officially Hits Digital After Being Removed From 500 Theaters

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

He has covered everything from Marvel to the Oscars, and Marvel at the Oscars. He also writes obsessively about the box office, charting the many hits and misses that are released weekly, and how their commercial performance shapes public perception. In his time at Collider, he has also helped drive diversity by writing stories about the multiple Indian film industries, with a goal of introducing audiences to a whole new world of cinema.

A host of Oscar contenders are now playing in theaters, and this week, two of them are slated to make their digital debuts. The films have had very different box-office runs: one exceeded expectations and broke records, while the other didn't quite capture the mainstream audience despite massive early acclaim. A24's epic documentary You Can See Everything, a mind-bending experiment by directors Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim about the disgraced tech entrepreneur Elizabeth Holmes, broke out against all ods with its massive runtime. The second is a more conventional awards contender that won the prestigious Palme d'Or.

The movie comes from the acclaimed Romanian filmmaker Cristian Mungiu, who has previously won the top honor at Cannes for 4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days. In fact, the new movie marks the seventh Palme d'Or winner in a row to be distributed domestically by NEON, after Parasite, Titane, Triangle of Sadness, Anatomy of a Fall, Anora, and It Was Just an Accident.

The movie was released domestically after building buzz for months, but it wasn't quite able to translate all the attention into a major box office haul. Starring Sebastian Stan and Renate Reinsve — two hugely popular stars who are having particularly impressive years — the film grossed less than $2 million in its domestic box-office run. At its peak, it was playing in 540 theaters nationwide — lower than the threshold needed for a film to be categorized as a "wide" release.

We're talking, of course, about Fjord. The movie follows a family that is ostracized by a progressive community for their perceived regressive ways. It's now sitting at a "Certified Fresh" 91% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, where the consensus reads, "Presenting a knotty moral dilemma and refusing to provide easy answers, Cristian Mungiu's superbly well-acted Fjord has a chilly surface that belies a heated humanity beneath the frost."

Fjord is now playing in only around 30 locations nationwide and is slated to debut on digital platforms on November 24​​​​​​​, the same day as You Can See Everything. The viral sensation has had an entirely different theatrical journey, having been made in secret and revealed in a surprise screening at the Telluride Film Festival. It has now grossed more than $100 million worldwide, breaking records for documentary features.

Also releasing on the PVOD market on November 24 are Aaron Sorkin's critical and commercial underperformer The Social Reckoning and the acclaimed romance fantasy sleeper hit Wicker, starring Olivia Colman and Alexander Skarsgård. Stay tuned to Collider for more updates.

Fjord follows the Gheorghius, a Romanian-Catholic family who move to a remote Norwegian village hoping to build a quieter life. As they grow close to their neighbors, the Halbergs, cultural differences and local suspicion begin to turn that welcome into hostility, leading to accusations that threaten both families and expose the fragile line between trust and fear.
