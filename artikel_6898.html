Duchess Meghan and Prince Harry Have Reportedly Moved Into a New Home

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

They have been living in the Cotswolds since late August, according to an insider source

A few months after Duchess Meghan and Prince Harry made their surprise move across the pond, it seems they’ve finally found a more permanent residence.

According to a new report from The Daily Mail, the pair have been living with their two children—Prince Archie, 7, and Princess Lilibet, 5—in a friend’s house in the Cotswolds since they arrived to the United Kingdom in late August. The friend is allegedly a wealthy businessman who Harry became acquainted with through their mutual military charity work.

Originally, the Duke and Duchess of Sussex were “living in the main house,” but now, they’ve “moved to a property on the estate,” per an insider source. Further details about the residence remain under wraps, though.

Meghan and Harry shocked the world when they revealed at the end of the summer that they would be returning to the U.K. for an extended period of time, six years after they stepped down from their senior positions as royals and moved their family to Montecito, California. (Currently, the pair are still holding on to their $14 million mansion in the starry West Coast neighborhood.)

The move precedes Harry’s 2027 Invictus Games, which are set to take place in Birmingham, England, and which may have partly inspired the move. Meanwhile, Meghan has already been busy at work too, teaming up with Malala Yousafzai for a project tied to International Day of the Girl, and joining Junior MasterChef Australia as a guest host.

In a recent interview with GQ, Harry opened up about why he and Meghan decided to move their family back to the U.K., saying: “Being able to put the kids in a British school has meant a lot to me. I didn’t know how important it was to me.”

He added: “I think that having been [in the U.S.] for six years now and only being able to go back to the U.K. for short periods of time, to actually have my whole family there with me, the place that I grew up, wanting to be able to show my kids where I grew up and have them have the experiences to some extent that I had—the good rather than necessarily the bad—provided real, proper, serious thinking as to, ‘This is an opportunity, let’s do it now.’”
