Lions’ late owner Martha Firestone Ford will be on Matthew Stafford’s mind as he watches ex-team play on Thanksgiving

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

For years, Thanksgiving in the Stafford household meant watching Dad play quarterback for the Detroit Lions. Even after Matthew Stafford joined the Rams, his wife and daughters still had to share him with football.

At some point every Turkey Day, he was due at practice.

That was Thanksgiving for Stafford. He was either playing in a game or preparing for one.

Matthew Stafford throws a pass during the Rams' 26-13 win over the Packers on Nov. 25, 2026,at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, Calif. Getty Images

This year, the Rams got their work done Wednesday night against the Packers in the NFL's inaugural Thanksgiving Eve game. For the first time in as long as he can remember, Stafford gets the whole day with his family.

And when the Lions come on, his wife and daughters can slide over and make room for Dad on the couch.

"I'll be tuning in just like everybody else," Stafford said.

Except there's someone he'll be thinking about when his old team takes the field. Martha Firestone Ford, the Lions' owner and chair emeritus, died this week.

And for all the pleasure Stafford takes in the prospect of a Thanksgiving at home, he said he'll be "a little heavy-hearted" watching the Lions play.

He was a kid from Texas by way of the University of Georgia when the Lions made him the first pick in the 2009 NFL draft. Ford had been part of the organization since 1963, when she and her husband, William Clay Ford, purchased the team. By the time Stafford came along, she had spent nearly half a century welcoming people to Detroit.

Detroit owner Martha Firestone Ford, who died this week, plays with Sawyer Stafford, held by her mother Kelly Stafford, before a home game against the Falcons. USA TODAY Network via Reuters Connect

He remembers how warmly she welcomed him and his family.

"She's a special lady to me, to my family, and to that organization to the city of Detroit," Stafford said.

He spent his first 12 NFL seasons there. Along the way, he got to see another side of the woman who had made him feel at home. When her husband died in 2014, Ford took over the Lions herself.

The relationship Stafford built with Ford has made her passing all the more painful. Even as he prepared to play the Packers, his heart was with his second family in Detroit.

"I've reached out to them a little bit," Stafford said. "Just an unbelievable lady, a great leader, and she'll be missed."

Even after the trade to Los Angeles, Thursdays in November generally meant practice for the next opponent. It took the NFL putting the Rams and Packers in its inaugural Thanksgiving Eve game Wednesday to give Stafford the first full Thanksgiving off he can remember in years.

Matthew Stafford waves to the crowd after the Rams' 26-13 win over the Packers on Nov. 25, 2026, at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, Calif. Getty Images

Of all the things he gets to do with that time, maybe the sweetest is the simplest.

He gets to sit with the family Ford welcomed all those years ago and watch the team that gave them a reason to always call Detroit home.
