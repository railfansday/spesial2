Steve Kerr rightfully beginning to tinker with Warriors' Steph Curry-less lineup

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Steve Kerr rightfully beginning to tinker with Warriors' Steph Curry-less lineup originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

They knew the road would be treacherous, that their win streak likely would end, and that their once-stout bench was shrinking. And when injury struck Stephen Curry minutes into the first game of the trip, it was a given that hard times would visit the Warriors.

Being smacked to smithereens by the mighty San Antonio Spurs without Curry might be understood, a case of the NBA's hottest team spanking a diminished, uncertain squad.

But Monday night was supposed to bring some clarity to the Steph-less Warriors. If ever there was a team that would enable them, it is the New Orleans Pelicans, a reeling crew they annihilated one week earlier at Chase Center.

The Warriors now know annihilation after a 115-94 loss they hope was an anomaly and not indicative of a sharp downward post-Steph spiral.

"We're going to have to change some combinations and find some things that work for us," coach Steve Kerr told reporters at Smoothie King Center.

The first-time starting lineup – LJ Cryer, Brandin Podziemski, Yaxel Lendeborg, Draymond Green and Al Horford – was a change from San Antonio and the fourth in the last five games. It failed miserably.

Golden State allowed the Pelicans to race to a 23-2 lead inside the first eight in minutes on the way to snapping their six-game losing streak and, for one evening, living like kings.

Listless and soft and afflicted with curious decision-making, the Warriors didn't make a field goal until De'Anthony Melton drained a triple with 4:13 left in the first quarter and shot 2 of 18, including 1 of 13 from deep, to end the quarter trailing 35-11.

"That's on me," Green said. "Because I'm the leader, and I came out like that."

Falling behind big in the early minutes is not new. This was the ninth time in 14 games that the Warriors trailed after one quarter, but first-quarter margins are growing. They were minus-24 against the Spurs and minus-15 – and lost Curry – in the first quarter against the Rockets.

"We've got to bring a sense of urgency right from the beginning of the game," Kerr said. "I've got to do my job as coach and find the right starting group and to make sure we have a good five-man combinations coming off the bench.

"It's kind of the early part of the season, where you're experimenting, you're looking at different combinations that sort of thing. That's our job as a staff. But it's the players' job to find that energy and that juice."

The Warriors missed Curry, to be sure, but they also lacked the persistent tenacity they'll need to be a force when he's active and at least competitive when he is not. They were not competitive. The very beatable Pelicans played with evident gusto, while the Warriors took their third consecutive loss looking as if they were new to the game.

In one way, they are. There's bound to be a period of disarray when a team loses the hub of its offense.

This was the Warriors' second full game without Curry, their 14th without Jimmy Butler III, their second-best player. The dropoff from those two is steep, with the next level occupied by veterans Green and Podziemski, and the rookie Lendeborg.

Green's scoring, a bonus in the best of times, goes into the freezer without Curry. Podziemski has averaged 11.5 points on 5-of-18 shooting, including 5 of 12 from deep; he is scoreless in the paint. Lendeborg is averaging 15.5 points, shooting 10 of 41 from the field, including 2 of 11 from distance.

Lendeborg was ineffective Friday against the Spurs and muddled through the first half against the Pelicans, scoring eight points on 2-of-9 shooting from the field, missing all three attempts from deep. He recovered after halftime, scoring 20 points on 7-of-14 shooting, including 2 of 6 from beyond the arc.

Green, however, made it clear that it's asking too much to think Lendeborg should grab Curry's torch – even temporarily – in his first month in the league.

"Depend on him, but lower the expectation," Green said. "It's not fair to him to try to make step into Steph's role 13 games into his career. Depend on him like we need to, but lower what we're expecting out of him, from to allow him to just flow into it.

"Right now, we're putting too much pressure on him."

He's right. It's only two games, but at least one conclusion can be made: The non-Steph minutes that went so smoothly before Curry was injured suddenly are a disaster.

The supporting cast should be capable of being more competitive, but it already has discovered that it's considerably tougher to flourish for 15 minutes than for 48.
