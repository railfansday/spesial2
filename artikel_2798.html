Texas remains in contention to play in the SEC championship game

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

On December 5, Mercedes-Benz Stadium in Atlanta will host the 35th and final SEC championship game after 33 years in the ATL after the conference announced the end of the event with the sport moving to a 24-team playoff next year.

"The SEC Football Championship Game has been a defining event in college football for more than three decades, producing unforgettable moments and crowning legendary champions," SEC Commissioner Greg Sankey said in a statement released last week.

"What began with the vision and leadership of Commissioner Roy Kramer in 1992 helped reshape the landscape of college football and established a tradition that has endured for 35 years. As the sport evolves with an expanded College Football Playoff, we look forward to celebrating the remarkable legacy of this event with its 35th and final edition and recognizing the city of Atlanta, Mercedes-Benz Stadium and AMB Sports and Entertainment, and the many partners who have contributed to its success."

With two conference rivalry games remaining, the Texas Longhorns remain in contention for a berth in the championship game.

On Monday, head coach Steve Sarkisian called the motivation to make it to Atlanta "the life of a competitor."

"We don't do anything just to do it, and the idea that this is maybe the last year ever of an SEC championship game, and the fact that we maybe have a chance to be part of that, gosh, why would we not be striving for it?" Sarkisian asked.

The answers, of course, put into perspective why conference championship games are going extinct — the format became outdated when the playoff expanded to 12 teams, forcing the College Football Playoff selection committee to ignore losses in the SEC championship game, and it adds extra wear and tear to teams that are already facing longer seasons and more difficult schedules with the SEC playing nine conference games this season.

Why would Texas want to go through 60 minutes of utter warfare against No. 1 Georgia?

The Longhorns are already without four injured starters who are out for the season and have numerous other players who are banged up to various degrees.

Fortunately for Texas, the Longhorns could win out and still not have to face the Bulldogs as the SEC goes through the convoluted tiebreaker scenarios necessitated by a 16-team conference that illustrates the silliness of these conference championship games — if Alabama, Tennessee, and Texas all finish 7-2, the conference would go to its fourth tiebreaker, cumulative conference winning percentage of all conference opponents among tied teams.

Plenty will change there over the next two weeks, along with the possibility that any one of those four teams could lose a conference games, a scenario only slightly diminished because Alabama and Georgia both play non-conference games in the final two weeks, and Tennessee plays Vanderbilt, which only has one SEC win this season.

As a competitor, Sarkisian has to say the right things publicly about his team's desire to play in Atlanta, but this space is a safe one from which to point out the fact that missing the conference championship game and having another week to prepare for the College Football Playoff is the ideal scenario for the Longhorns.

The bottom line for fans who want to see Sarkisian's team avoid playing that extra game? Hope that Alabama and Tennessee both finish 7-2, because if Texas wins its final two games and either of those teams suffer a conference loss, the Horns will be playing in that final SEC championship game at the Benz.

"Notwithstanding the playoffs are a whole 'nother opportunity to be a champion, but to me, when you can hoist an SEC championship trophy knowing the caliber of coaches and players and venues in this league, that's a pretty special thing," Sarkisian said.
