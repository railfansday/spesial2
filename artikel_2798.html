‘It Almost Felt Like (Aaron Rodgers) Was Calling Out The Guys’ — CBS Reporter Pulls Back The Curtain on QB’s Steelers Meetings Complaint

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Aaron Rodgers' latest message to the Pittsburgh Steelers came with a dash of nostalgia. During CBS's broadcast of Sunday's 19-16 loss to the Philadelphia Eagles, sideline reporter Melanie Collins shared what the veteran quarterback has been asking of his teammates in meetings.

Collins pointed to the tablets that now fill those rooms, explaining, "He said it's tough nowadays with these iPads. Back in the day, you could check guys' notes and make sure they were writing things down." However, the iPads were only part of what she passed along.

The whole thing came up when, during CBS' broadcast, Kevin Harlan revealed that Rodgers had been seeking more interaction from teammates during meetings. Collins then explained that Rodgers had expressed frustration with the difficulty of monitoring players' engagement now that they take notes on tablets instead of paper.

"He called for more interaction," Collins reported. "He said it's tough nowadays with these iPads. Back in the day, you could check guys' notes and make sure they were writing things down. It almost felt like he was calling out the guys a little bit for a lack of details. He wants everyone to feel like they're contributing to what's happening on game day, even when they're not on the field."

The subject isn't a new one in Pittsburgh this season. It surfaced in early October, after a Thursday night game put the quarterback and his head coach on national display.

Early in a 27-24 loss to the Cleveland Browns, Rodgers wanted the play calls coming in quicker, and the national broadcast picked up a profane back-and-forth between him and head coach Mike McCarthy.

When he met with reporters on Oct. 7, Rodgers downplayed the moment, quickly answering "It's always ironed out" when asked if everything was settled with McCarthy.

Sunday brought a different kind of frustration, and the afternoon went sideways early. The Eagles, who arrived on a seven-game losing streak, led 13-0 after the first quarter on two Jake Elliott field goals and a 38-yard Saquon Barkley touchdown run.

Pittsburgh converted 5 of 17 third downs and managed 255 total yards, leaning on three Chris Boswell field goals until Rodgers found Max Hurleman for a 20-yard touchdown with 1:21 left. Philadelphia ran out the clock from there, and Rodgers finished 30-of-44 for 227 yards while taking five sacks. He also led the Steelers in rushing with 29 yards on five carries.

Rodgers' PFN QB Impact Grade now clocks in at 67.0, good enough for only 29th in the league.

The loss dropped the Steelers to 6-4, third in the AFC North, and extended a drought, as Pittsburgh hasn't won a road game in Philadelphia since 1965. There is little time to regroup, with the Denver Broncos visiting Acrisure Stadium on Black Friday, Nov. 27, at 3 p.m. ET, a short week for the Steelers to put Rodgers' message into practice.
