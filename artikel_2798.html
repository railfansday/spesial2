Tanggal: 2026-11-14

All you need to do is see my mailbag post on The Feed and then make a comment about what we should talk about! We had a lot of questions this week. I'll try to answer some of them:

I don't mind parity; I do think it's being taken a biiiiit too far, however. I'm against a top team having a first pick in the draft—that's where you get all the NBA controversies with lotteries and such. That said NBA draft "lottery" was originally implemented to prevent all-out tanking, but somehow elite prospects get picked up by specific teams, defying the odds.

Here's what I WOULD like. I agree with your salary cap worries, but I don't think it should be removed—instead, adjusted. Several companies that have remote workers adjust salaries based on cost of living. For example, I live in Seattle, Washington, so my salary for a position would be significantly higher than someone living in Jackpot, Nevada. I think income tax can be a key decision-maker for players, and teams that have to deal with income taxes should adjust accordingly. Washington has no income tax; California does. So adjust the salary cap accordingly based on the tax structure of that team's state. How do you do this? I have no idea. I am the worst at numbers.

My other suggestion for the salary cap is that for any player drafted by a team who accrues x years on that team and is up for an extension, a percentage of their new deal doesn't count against the salary cap. This lets them get the deal they want without crippling the team. This also rewards teams for drafting well rather than penalizing them. It's like a franchise tag you can use once a year on a newly signed contract. Oftentimes, players don't want to leave their team, but the business side of things gets in the way, and the team can only offer so much. Having a rule like this in effect would create roster consistency and ensure the talent gets the salary they deserve. You can go even further, like any pick in the second round-on, while the first round has the fifth-year option.

The whole idea is that teams are rewarded for investing their resources and time into a prospect rather than the player's contract being up and priced out of whatever team wants to keep them.
