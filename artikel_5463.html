Sound Ventures turned $435 million into a $7 billion Anthropic stake. Ashton Kutcher left months before the IPO

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

When Anthropic, the company behind the AI assistant Claude and a suite of large language models and coding tools, went public this morning, the numbers were eye-popping. The San Francisco AI firm raised about $81.3 billion, the most money ever raised in an initial public offering, and began trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT.

Anthropic priced its shares at $83, above its expected range of $70 to $78. They opened at $111.50 and had cooled to around $98 by mid-afternoon, per Yahoo Finance.

That’s a windfall for Anthropic’s earliest backers, and one of them has a Hollywood pedigree. Sound Ventures, the venture capital firm cofounded in 2015 by talent manager Guy Oseary and actor Ashton Kutcher, owns roughly 0.5% of Anthropic, a stake worth about $7 billion, according to Bloomberg.

For context, the previous record belonged to SpaceX’s June launch, which raised $75 billion. Third, distantly, is South Korean memory-chip maker SK Hynix, which raised $26.5 billion in a U.S. listing in July, per Renaissance Capital.

At its IPO price, Anthropic was valued at about $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis, nearly on par with Meta Platforms and Tesla, worth about $1.58 trillion and $1.52 trillion, respectively, per recent data from The Motley Fool. (SpaceX still holds the record for the richest IPO valuation, at $1.77 trillion, which is why Anthropic’s lower-than-expected pricing drew scrutiny last week.)

That valuation exceeds the GDP of many industrialized nations, including Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, and Switzerland, per Worldometer. Anthropic was founded in 2021.

Sound Ventures first invested in 2023, putting $35 million into Anthropic’s Series C round, TechCrunch reported. It kept writing checks in later rounds and has invested about $435 million in total, per Bloomberg. That same spring, the firm launched a $240 million fund dedicated to AI, with stakes in both Anthropic and OpenAI.

Bloomberg’s $7 billion figure was based on Anthropic’s $1.4 trillion target valuation, so at Thursday’s trading prices the stake is worth more.

How much of that belongs to Kutcher hasn’t been disclosed; most of the stake belongs to the fund’s investors. And the former That ’70s Show star is no longer running the firm. He stepped away from Sound Ventures this year to start a new venture firm with Morgan Beller focused on AI infrastructure, energy, and deep tech, the Wall Street Journal reported in July. He remains an advisor to Sound.

The biggest individual winners are Anthropic’s seven cofounders, including CEO Dario Amodei and president Daniela Amodei. Together they hold about 1.69 billion shares, or roughly 10% of the company, according to its S-1, as Fortune reported last month.

At the $83 IPO price, each cofounder’s stake was worth roughly $19 billion to $20 billion. At Thursday afternoon’s price of around $98, each is worth roughly $23 billion to $24 billion. The cofounders have pledged to give away most of their wealth.

Still, investors big and small have reason to stay circumspect, cautions Jay Ritter, an emeritus professor at the University of Florida’s Warrington College of Business who studies IPOs.

“SpaceX and Anthropic are in a league of their own, with valuations at the time of the IPO approximately 10 times bigger than any prior U.S. company going public,” Ritter said.

He offered a historical yardstick. “United Parcel Service (UPS), the prior record holder, went public in 1999 at a valuation, in 2026 dollars, of $162 billion at the end of the first day. It jumped by 36.25% on its first day of trading, but then declined by 10% in the next six months,” Ritter told Fortune. “UPS was 92 years old at the time of its IPO, with annual revenue of $26.3 billion. On an inflation-adjusted basis, Anthropic’s $35.2 billion trailing 12-month revenue is almost that high less than six years after its founding.”

That doesn’t promise a smooth ride. “As with SpaceX, for which the stock price varied by over $27 on its first day of trading and by over $120 since going public in June, with a low of $104.83 and high of $225.64, Anthropic’s stock will be volatile,” Ritter said.
