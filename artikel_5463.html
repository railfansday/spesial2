Nvidia stock falls as board investigates Jensen Huang's affair with alleged spy

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The company said the board has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised; earnings are due Nov. 17

Samuel Boivin / NurPhoto via Getty Images

Nvidia $NVDA’s board of directors launched an independent investigation on Sunday following Bloomberg reports that a foreign intelligence effort had singled out CEO Jensen Huang, and Nvidia shares dropped roughly 5% on Monday.

A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside independent counsel, will oversee the inquiry, the company said. The board said nothing it has seen suggests that national security or Nvidia's trade secrets were put at risk, and that the inquiry will examine all available information. Jensen Huang backs the review and has pledged his cooperation, and the board said it has confidence in him and his leadership team.

Jensen Huang apologized in a message to employees. "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry," he wrote. He said he remains committed to Nvidia and intends to rebuild trust with his coworkers, directors, business partners and investors.

Reports name Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed International executive suspected by U.S. intelligence agencies of spying for China, as the person who targeted him, according to Bloomberg. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun rejected the "so-called Chinese spy narrative" on Monday as lacking any substantiation.

Jensen Huang described Nvidia's business with Megaspeed as normal and not unusual last month. He said Nvidia had verified that Megaspeed did not ship its AI chips to China in violation of the law. Alice Huang is no relation to the CEO.

President Donald Trump said on Sunday that Jensen Huang is "an honorable guy" and that he had not talked with the CEO regarding the probe. "He's done an amazing job for our country," Trump said, according to CNBC.

Three House Republican committee chairs asked Jensen Huang last week to appear for a private session with lawmakers on Nov. 18. Nvidia is scheduled to report third-quarter earnings on Nov. 17.
