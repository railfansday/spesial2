Inside the Unraveling of the Tollman Family Travel Dynasty

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The company was doing fine. The family wasn’t.

For 104 years, The Travel Corporation was owned by a single family, and that ownership was a key selling point of the company.

The Tollmans built TTC into one of the world's largest guided-tour companies and kept full control. It presented itself to travel agents and customers as a family business with no outside investors to answer to.

Then, in July 2024, Brett Tollman, TTC’s chairman, announced a sale to Apollo. He gave only one reason: there was no next generation to lead the business. While that was true, it wasn’t the whole story.

Credit rating reports, UK filings and interviews with more than half a dozen former executives and advisers point to a profitable tour business that was underfunded, while the family’s luxury hotel chain, Red Carnation, lost money and relied on financial support from TTC on cheap terms. Ultimately, the family sold the tour business and kept the hotels.

Skift's review of credit rating reports, UK filings, and interviews with more than half a dozen former executives and advisers fills in what sat behind The Travel Corporation's 2024 sale to Apollo. Brett Tollman publicly cited the absence of a next generation to lead the business. The documents show a profitable but underfunded tour group — roughly $190 million in EBITDA on an implied $1.3 billion in revenue — sold for about $1.1 billion, less than six times earnings, despite Uniworld's roughly 45% share of luxury river cruise and Trafalgar's 15% of guided tours. S&P and Fitch both described reduced marketing spend and agent commissions, weak direct sales, and outdated systems, with an EBITDA margin of 14% against more than 20% at competitors. Apollo set aside $50 million to upgrade operations. The family kept Red Carnation Hotels, which UK filings show had been funded by a loan of more than £50 million from Travcorp Financial Services at the Bank of England base rate — terms the hotel group's own audited accounts call below market. The London hotel group's losses widened from £1.6 million in 2023 to £7.2 million in 2025, while capital spending rose from £2 million to £7.6 million over the same period.
