Tweedy, Browne Mutual Funds Q3 2026 Commentary

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Global equity markets continued their seemingly inexorable advance during the third quarter, shrugging off persistent inflation, geopolitical turmoil, mounting government deficits, and concerns about the cost of capital. Buoyed by solid corporate earnings, continued enthusiasm surrounding artificial intelligence, and a seemingly insatiable appetite for risk, investors appeared largely willing to look past these gathering clouds. With US equity valuations already at historically elevated levels, the old adage that markets climb a wall of worry seemed particularly apt.

An encouraging development in 2026 has been broader market participation beyond the large-cap US technology companies that dominated returns in recent years. After more than a decade of extraordinary US market leadership, investors appear to be rediscovering the merits of attractively priced businesses outside the United States. We continue to find a wider assortment of opportunities in international markets, particularly among established companies with durable competitive positions, sound balance sheets, and meaningful free cash flow. That said, a rather narrow and familiar group of large U.S. technology companies once again led global equity returns during the quarter.

Against this mixed backdrop, all four Tweedy, Browne Funds produced positive absolute returns, ranging from 1.29% to 3.37%, and three outperformed their respective benchmarks. During the quarter, we established several new positions and added selectively to an existing holding, while trimming or selling other investments. These purchases spanned a range of businesses and markets, reflecting our continuing search for opportunities where the relationship between price and underlying value appears attractive.

Among the new positions were Aptiv (APTV), the automotive technology and components supplier; LVMH (LVMUY), the French luxury goods group; Minth Group (MNTHF), a manufacturer of automotive exterior and structural components; Puregold Price Club (PGCMF), a Philippine supermarket operator; and Toyota Motor. We also initiated or added to the position in an undisclosed Taiwanese manufacturer of composite products used in sporting goods and other applications. At purchase, we believed these companies were trading at discounts to conservative estimates of their intrinsic value, were financially strong, and had attractive prospects for long-term growth.

On the sell side, we trimmed Computacenter following its strong advance and eliminated Teleperformance (TLPFY) from two portfolios after it contributed positively during the quarter. We also reduced or eliminated positions in Transcosmos (TRCLF) and Alliance Global Group (ALGGY). Other activity included sales of Kuraray (KURRY), Adris Grupa, and LX Holdings, and reductions in holdings such as UniFirst (UNF), Vertex Pharmaceuticals (VRTX), Pets at Home, and Okamoto Industries (OKMIF).

As mentioned in the last two quarters, the investment strategy for the newly renamed Tweedy, Browne Buybacks. Dividends + Value Fund (formerly the Worldwide High Dividend Yield Value Fund) was modestly adjusted to emphasize investment in companies that pay an attractive dividend and/or have initiated share buybacks when their shares trade at discounts to underlying value. Our assessment of undervaluation incorporates proprietary combinations of numerical value-oriented investment characteristics, a value score, and qualitative analysis. The Fund no longer requires an above-average dividend.

We believe a sea change may be underway in global equity markets, driven in part by the rising cost of money and elevated equity valuations, particularly in the United States. If we are right, the years ahead could look considerably different from the recent past. Higher interest rates should, in our view, restore some of the relevance of price-sensitive investing after a prolonged period in which investors were often rewarded for paying almost any price for anticipated growth. With many equity valuation measures at or near historical extremes, and corporate earnings expectations increasingly ambitious, we believe investors may be assigning too little weight to the possibility that future earnings and investment returns could prove less favorable than currently anticipated.

The extraordinary capital spending associated with artificial intelligence also raises questions about the ultimate economic returns on these investments. While AI holds considerable promise for improving productivity, it remains uncertain whether the enormous sums being committed to infrastructure will generate satisfactory returns on capital, particularly as competition intensifies and some historically asset-light technology businesses become increasingly capital intensive. At the same time, elevated levels of margin borrowing and growing investor enthusiasm for speculative investment products suggest a degree of complacency about downside risk. None of these developments necessarily signals an imminent market reversal, but collectively they give us reason for caution.

Despite these concerns, we continue to find attractive opportunities in global equity markets, particularly among established businesses with durable competitive advantages, strong balance sheets, and the ability to generate meaningful free cash flow. Should today's exuberance eventually give way to a more discriminating, price-sensitive market environment, we believe our Funds are reasonably well-positioned to weather the resulting volatility and potentially gain ground on their benchmarks. Conversely, should animal spirits continue to propel equity prices higher, we expect to participate in the advance, although perhaps not to the same extent as more aggressively positioned growth-oriented portfolios. We are comfortable with that trade-off. Our objective remains to compound our shareholders' capital at attractive rates over time while remaining mindful of the risk of permanent capital loss.
