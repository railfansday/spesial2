If I Could Only Own 1 Trillion-Dollar Artificial Intelligence (AI) Stock Through 2030, It Would Be Anthropic, Nvidia, or Alphabet. Here’s My Top Pick.

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Nvidia's falling valuation and Anthropic's IPO may make them tempting buys right now, but Alphabet presents an interesting long-term investment opportunity.

By Adam Spatacco – Nov 20, 2026 at 1:00PM EST

The artificial intelligence (AI) revolution has created some of the largest companies in stock market history. Nvidia (NVDA -10.83%) has evolved into an indispensable supplier of AI computing infrastructure, while Anthropic (ANT -9.54%) finally made its highly anticipated initial public offering (IPO) on Nov. 19. Meanwhile, Alphabet (GOOGL -0.44%) (GOOG -0.61%) is rapidly integrating AI services throughout its ecosystem of consumer and enterprise products.

All three companies offer exposure to the AI industry, but in different ways. This makes their respective risk profiles considerably different. Nvidia is facing an unprecedented leadership transition, while Anthropic must justify an enormous valuation while financing hundreds of billions of dollars in infrastructure commitments.

For investors looking to buy one trillion-dollar AI stock among this trio and hold it through 2030, Alphabet stands out as the most compelling choice.

Nvidia's financial performance makes it difficult to dismiss as an investment. During the company's fiscal third quarter, revenue surged 103% year over year to $115.8 billion while adjusted earnings per share (EPS) climbed 111% to $2.68. Management projected fourth-quarter revenue to land around $130 billion. This demonstrates how demand for Nvidia's accelerated computing platforms remains exceptionally strong.

Nevertheless, investing in Nvidia now comes with considerable uncertainty following founder Jensen Huang's unexpected resignation amid allegations involving ties to Chinese intelligence. Huang spent decades shaping Nvidia's technological roadmap, developer ecosystem, and relationships with critical supply chain partners. His departure raises legitimate questions about whether the company can maintain its founder-driven culture of innovation under new leadership.

Competition presents another challenge. Alphabet, Amazon, and other hyperscalers are developing custom AI accelerators to reduce their dependence on Nvidia's graphics processing units (GPUs). Although Nvidia's CUDA software platform creates switching costs, the expansion of custom silicon into the broader chip landscape could eventually pressure the company's market share and pricing power.

Although Nvidia could overcome these obstacles, its long-term investment thesis increasingly depends on a successful leadership transition and continued technological edge. For investors prioritizing durability through 2030, those uncertainties are difficult to ignore.

Anthropic presents a different investment proposition. The developer of Claude has experienced historic growth, with its annualized revenue run rate reaching $81 billion in October. As I write this (Nov. 19), Anthropic stock is trading around $95 and boasts a market cap around $1.2 trillion.

Anthropic needs to prove that accelerating revenue growth can actually translate into sustainable profitability. According to its S-1 filing, Anthropic has $900.9 billion in infrastructure requirements extending over the next two decades. These obligations create financial risks because developing and operating frontier AI models requires enormous amounts of computing power, electricity, and capital.

Anthropic's disclosed arrangements include commitments of $121.3 billion to Google through July 2033, at least $110 billion to Amazon, and approximately $47.2 billion to Microsoft through June 2033. Many of these commitments require minimum payments regardless of actual compute usage, meaning Anthropic still needs to generate sufficient revenue and maintain access to financing to support its expansion, even if customer demand falls short of expectations.

Although Anthropic's growth is impressive, its business remains comparatively young and capital-intensive. Competition from Google's Gemini, OpenAI, and open-source models could pressure pricing and profitability. At a valuation already in the trillions of dollars, investors are already paying an enormous premium for financial results that have not yet materialized.

Alphabet's competitive advantages extend across Google Search, YouTube, Android, Chrome, Google Workspace, and Google Cloud. These products create a powerful distribution ecosystem for introducing new AI capabilities.

Importantly, Alphabet isn't simply defending its legacy businesses against the proliferation of generative AI. The company is incorporating its Gemini models into Search, productivity applications, smartphones, and enterprise software, creating unique opportunities to improve customer engagement and introduce new revenue streams.

Alphabet's latest financial results suggest the company's AI investments are already producing meaningful benefits. During the third quarter, total revenue increased 25% to $127.6 billion. The company generated $26.2 billion in net income and maintained a companywide operating margin of 32%. This underscores Alphabet's ability to fund AI development through existing profits.

Perhaps the most exciting opportunity is Google Cloud, which generated $30.7 billion in quarterly revenue, representing 103% year-over-year growth. That exceeded the growth rates of Amazon Web Services (AWS) and Microsoft Azure. Google Cloud's growth demonstrates how Alphabet is increasingly becoming a formidable competitor in enterprise AI infrastructure.

Alphabet also has a technological advantage through its custom Tensor Processing Units (TPUs). These chips are helping the company optimize capacity costs for its own AI services while offering customers alternatives to Nvidia's infrastructure. Combining proprietary chips, Gemini models, cloud services, and consumer distribution gives Alphabet multiple levers to capture more value as AI adoption accelerates.

If only one trillion-dollar AI stock among this cohort could be bought today and held for the long term, Alphabet would be my strongest choice. While Nvidia and Anthropic could deliver outsize returns, Alphabet offers a particularly attractive combination of innovation, financial resilience, and durable competitive advantages.
