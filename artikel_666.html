Is Carly Rae Jepsen’s ‘Run Away With Me’ About to Hit the Hot 100 for the First Time After Its ‘GTA VI’ Bump?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

This week's Trending Up looks at some 'GTA'-related bumps, as well as a CMA Awards-driven breakthrough.

This week, it’s all GTA (with a little CMAs), as we look at revived favorites from Carly Rae Jepsen and The Cars, an ascendant hit from an all-star cast, and a new artist of the year winner at age 47. It’s one of the most beloved pop songs of the past 15 years — but never one of its biggest chart hits. Carly Rae Jepsen‘s “Run Away With Me” became a cult classic almost immediately upon its single release (from her enduring E•MO•TION album) in 2015, but Billboard Hot 100 success totally eluded it in its moment. (In 2023, Billboard named it the 21st greatest pop song of the Hot 100 era to never reach the chart.) But in 2026, a great pop song still has a chance to reach the Hot 100 no matter when it was originally released — and “Run Away With Me” now has the best opportunity it’s had in a decade, thanks to the release of Grand Theft Auto VI.

“Run Away With Me” plays in the record-breaking new game during a scene when its dual protagonists, Jason and Lucia, are enjoying the Vice City high life after the latter’s release from prison. With its cinematic sax and propulsive synth-pop shuffle making it an ideal soundtrack for just about any action-packed sequence, the song has become one of the breakout musical moments from the game’s heavily music-focused universe — and led many pop fans to wonder if it could result in the storied track finally reaching the Hot 100 for the first time.

The answer? Maybe — but it still has a long way to go.

The song’s streaming breakout has unquestionably been real: It was pulling daily official on-demand streams in the low 20,000s in the days before the game’s release, but that number had broken six digits by Sunday (Nov. 22, three days after GTA VI‘s release), and was all the way up to 281,000 by Monday, according to early data provided by Luminate. That’s a gain of nearly 1,100% over the total the song posted last Wednesday, just before the game’s release, but it’s still a little low for a streaming-driven-song on the Hot 100.

The question is if the song can continue its viral growth throughout this week, and maybe even into next week. It’s got a bit of a late start for this tracking week, which runs through Thursday — and as of Tuesday, the song still wasn’t streaming enough to enter the daily top 200 on either Spotify or Apple Music — so its best chance of breaching the chart might come next week, especially if it continues to grow and then stays stable, with daily U.S. streaming numbers more in the 500k-600k range.

However, it will be getting late early on the charts for songs like “Run Away With Me” by next week — since the yearly avalanche of Christmas-related titles, which has already provided a pretty heavy snowfall on the Hot 100 in November, will really hit following Thanksgiving. At that point, non-holiday-related space on the chart will become increasingly limited, and the bar that “Run” needs to clear in order to reach the listing will climb higher and higher.

It will be a challenge for “Run Away With Me” to broach the Hot 100 — but it definitely could happen, if its moment continues to spread and attract new fans while re-activating old ones. Could it get extra momentum through new TikTok trends? Will a still-unreleased remix from Overmono — featured in the game, but not yet available on streaming — end up as an important wildcard? Might pop radio belateedly decide to embrace it, in a way it never quite did a decade ago? Could its best chance of breaking the chart not actually come next week, but next year?

It’s too early to say on any of this, but one thing is for sure: Its longtime fans will be there rooting it on, supporting as much as they can and air-saxing the whole way. — ANDREW UNTERBERGER

Outside of Morgan Wallen’s “Last Thing You Need,” the all-star jam “Flower in a Hurricane” was already the closest thing to a pre-minted hit from the Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album soundtrack, having already spent multiple weeks on the Hot 100. But following the game’s release, the song from Justice, Tame Impala, SZA and King Gizzard & The Lizard Wizard has reached a new level of popularity — and may soon challenge or even eclipse even the commercially bulletproof Wallen’s latest hit single on the Hot 100.

In the four days following the game’s Nov. 19 release, “Flower’ racked up nearly 7.6 million official on-demand U.S. streams, according to early data provided by Luminate — a 165% gain over the 2.9 million streams the songs racked up over the previous Friday-Monday period (Nov. 13-16). While doing so, it also topped Spotify’s Daily Top Songs USA chart and reached No. 3 on the Apple Music realtime chart. It all portends another huge jump for “Flower” on next week week’s Hot 100, with the song already reaching a new peak of No. 45 this week, and looking to shoot into the top 20 or possibly even the top 10 next week.

But that’s not all for Justice — the French house duo also responsible for composing the game’s epic score — who has two additional new original collabs on the game’s official soundtrack. One of those, the album-opening “Starting From Zero” — a teamup with yacht rock icon Michael McDonald — is also off to a strong start on streaming, earning 1.8 million streams in its first four days of release (Nov. 20-23), and potentially pushing for an outside chance at a Hot 100 debut of its own if its numbers keep climbing. (The duo’s third GTA song, “Pusher” with Gesaffelstein, is also up 53% to 464,000 streams.)

And Wallen’s “Last Thing”? That’s up about 31% too over that four-day period — albeit with a lower streaming total now (around 6 million) than “Hurricane” — and should also be due for a chart rebound from its current No. 17 perch, possibly back to the chart’s top 10. — AU

After The Cars‘ “Drive” reached No. 3 on the Hot 100 during its initial 1984 run, the dramatic rock classic is back in the cultural mainstream following its key sync in the newly released Grand Theft Auto VI video game.

In the Friday-Monday period (Nov. 13-16) preceding the game’s Nov. 19 release, “Drive” collected 629,000 official on-demand U.S. streams. In the Friday-Monday period following the game’s release (Nov. 20-23), that total leapt 83% to over 1.15 million official on-demand U.S. streams, according to early data provided by Luminate.

Even though Rockstar Games teamed up with Atlantic Records for a gargantuan, genre-spanning, 57-song compilation soundtrack, “Drive” remained the developers’ North Star. Billboard’s latest cover story revealed that Rockstar first tried to clear “Drive” for GTA in 2002, but this year’s long-awaited sync “was a testament to the company’s unwavering prioritization of music, the relationships it has cultivated throughout the industry and the meticulous curation that has transformed Grand Theft Auto into one of the past 20 years’ most culture-saturating, riveting and lucrative products.” With first-day sales of $3.79 billion, “Drive” now soundtracks one of the biggest monocultural moments of the decade — and indeed, is the first song players hear play on the in-game radio.

“Drive” also experienced a smaller resurgence in 2019, following the death of frontman Ric Ocasek. The song returned to No. 6 on Hot Rock & Alternative Songs back then, and to No. 27 on Digital Song Sales. – KYLE DENIS

Despite being born in the 1970s, Stephen Wilson Jr. was named country music’s new artist of the year at the Country Music Awards last Wednesday (Nov. 18) — capping an incredible journey that’s taking Wilson from the science lab at Mars Inc. to songwriting rooms in Nashville, to now his own success as a solo singer-songwriter. The 47-year-old singer-songwriter was one of the biggest non-Ella Langley presences at the ceremonies, not only winning new artist of the year and giving a memorably emotional speech following the win, but also performing his new single “I’m a Song” alongside fellow country powerhouse (and 2026 CMA Award winner) Chris Stapleton.

And it seems like the awards might only be the beginning of Wilson’s mainstream breakthrough, as his streaming numbers shot through the roof following his big night. His catalog drew 8.7 million official on-demand U.S. streams over the three days following the awards (Nov. 19-21), more than tripling the under 2.9 million streams he amassed over the prior Thursday-Saturday period. The biggest gains from that period were of course reserved for “I’m a Song” (up a staggering 1,089% to 1.4 million streams), as well as his breakout cover of Ben E. King’s “Stand By Me” (up 135% to 1.1 million) and his CMA Award-nominated 2026 single “Gary” (up 281% to 753,000).

And of course, Ella Langley also saw a bump from her five wins — including for her big winners, record-breaking Hot 100 smash “Choosin’ Texas” (up 11% to 8.6 million streams) and Billboard 200-topping album Dandelion (up 16% to 24.2 million), as well as for her acoustically performed “Loving Life Again” (up 17% to 2.5 million). – AU
