Magic forward Paolo Banchero opens up about ankle injury recovery

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Paolo Banchero is eager to return to the court, but the former All-Star is taking a cautious approach to his ankle injury recovery to ensure he is fully healthy before joining the Orlando Magic.

Banchero suffered a low-grade right high ankle sprain in the second game of the season on Oct. 23 after it was hit by a teammate. While the Magic didn't offer a timetable for his return, the initial hope was that Banchero would only be sidelined for approximately 4-6 weeks.

The 24-year-old missed his 12th straight game on Wednesday, a 121-116 loss against the Philadelphia 76ers at the Kia Center. He didn't offer many specifics on what work he can do, but he is hopeful he'll return sooner rather than later.

"It definitely is a complex injury," Banchero said in the locker room Wednesday, just under four weeks after his initial diagnosis. "You just don't want it to linger, so you want to let it fully heal because there are cases of guys who came back too early and it bothered them later in their career, and there are guys who let it heal and were fine.

"I definitely just want to let it heal fully. Obviously, it is early in the season. You want to be there later in the season and postseason. But I do want to play very badly. I'm itching to get out there, but at the same time, the guys have been playing great, and it has been fun watching them."

The Magic (6-8) have gone 6-6 without Banchero in the lineup, including a 2-0 record in the NBA Cup. The group recently reeled off four straight wins behind the stellar play of Franz Wagner, a streak that ended Wednesday after they blew a 17-point first-half lead.

Orlando has been without a couple of other key players, including Jalen Suggs (eight games) and Anthony Black (three games), while Jonathan Isaac (knee) and Noah Penda (hamstring) have yet to suit up. Given their injuries, Banchero has been pleased with their recent play.

Anytime you get a whole new staff and a whole new way of playing, there is going to be a little bit of an adjustment and some ups and downs. Seeing us work through that and guys start to find their rhythm and find their feet has been great to see. It makes me excited for the rest of the season and for the future in general because I think the continuity we're starting to show is some of the best I've seen, especially offensively.

After receiving his diagnosis, Banchero consulted with Wagner, who dealt with a similar injury last season that forced him to miss significant time. Wagner even tried to come back too soon and had to miss more time, which Banchero is trying to avoid.

Entering the season, Banchero had missed 46 total games over the past two years, including an oblique injury that cost him 34 games during the 2024-25 season. He is using his past experience and is working diligently with the team to put his latest setback behind him for good.

"I think, in the past, especially the oblique, I really just kind of floated through that three-month period and was just kind of out of it and upset and stuck in my own head about my own personal problems," Banchero said. "So I learned that the more I'm engaged when I'm out, and the more I'm paying attention to what we're doing and pouring into the guys and watching film and watching film on how I can be better when I come back, it's just going to make it better for when I do come back."
