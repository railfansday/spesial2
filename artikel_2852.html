Steelers owner breaks silence on ‘insubordinate’ Aaron Rodgers — and reveals Mike McCarthy’s fate

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy has the support of team president and owner Art Rooney after Aaron Rodgers accused his now former coach of sleeping on the job.

Rooney made it clear that McCarthy's job is safe in a statement to the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette on Thursday — and he called out Rodgers for "insubordination" regarding the quarterback blasting McCarthy during Wednesday's installment of "The Pat McAfee Show."

"I just want to say that I have 100% confidence in Mike McCarthy and I expect he'll be our coach this season, next season and beyond," Rooney said. "The big thing, what happened this week, is insubordination can't be tolerated in any organization. When you start getting ultimatums, you can't live with that."

Pittsburgh Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy speaks during a news conference after an NFL football game in Philadelphia, Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. AP Photo/Chris Szagola

Rooney explained that Rodgers was not being a "good teammate" by publicly bashing McCarthy two days before their Black Friday game against the Broncos.

"There's maybe a time and place after the season if he wanted to kind of unload," Rooney said. "He talks about wanting to be a good teammate, but you don't do that to your teammates a couple days before the game, that's for sure.

"In general, I think, historically, what's in the locker room stays in the locker room. That's the way it's always been. That's the way it should be as far as I'm concerned."

Art Rooney II, owner of the Pittsburgh Steelers, looks on before the game against the New England Patriots at Gillette Stadium on Sept. 21, 2025, in Foxborough, Massachusetts. Getty Images

McCarthy said he benched Rodgers after the former four-time NFL MVP presented an "ultimatum" that was "clearly outside of his job description."

He didn't elaborate on their "private conversations."

According to the Post-Gazette, Rodgers gave McCarthy an ultimatum in a meeting Tuesday night: "Either let me call all the plays or just release me."

McCarthy told Rodgers he doesn't get to make demands, and then had an impromptu meeting with Rooney and general manager Omar Khan that night, sources told the Post-Gazette — adding that it was decided by McCarthy to make Rodgers inactive for the Broncos game with the intention of releasing him.

The Steelers announced Tuesday night that Rodgers would be inactive against Denver this week, and that Mason Rudolph would replace him.

After that, Rodgers did a surprise TV appearance Wednesday and accused McCarthy of sleeping during team meetings — and he was released shortly after his interview.

Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers and head coach Mike McCarthy chat prior to a game against the Eagles at Lincoln Financial Field on Nov. 22, 2026, in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Getty Images

He also said McCarthy missed a walk-through Friday and a meeting Saturday morning before the Steelers' 19-16 loss to the Eagles on Sunday — adding that it's allegedly a known behavioral pattern exhibited by McCarthy in the Steelers' building.

During his appearance on "The Pat McAfee Show," Rodgers said he confronted McCarthy about dozing off in meetings as a "concerned friend" because it seemed "unhealthy."

Rooney said Rodgers never expressed concerns about McCarthy's health with him.

"Mike's as healthy as the rest of us," Rooney said. "Health was not an issue. I'm not sure where that part comes."

Rodgers explained that "the nail in the coffin" occurred during Sunday's game in Philadelphia, when McCarthy broke up the halftime huddle by saying, "1-2-3 Packers."

Rodgers also said he found out he was going to be inactive from tight end Pat Freiermuth on a phone call Tuesday night, and that being benched was never a discussion with McCarthy.

Rodgers was released Wednesday shortly after the interview.

During a press conference Wednesday, McCarthy did not confirm or deny sleeping in meetings — but he did concede to the "1–2–3 Packers" moment, describing it as embarrassing.

"A change had to be made, and I certainly agree with the change and the way it was handled."

Rodgers returned to Pittsburgh on a one-year, $22.5 million contract for what he said is his final NFL season.

"I'm disappointed the way it came out," Rooney said when asked if he regrets bringing Rodgers into the fold. "We certainly had hopes that he would be successful here, and I thought at times we certainly got on a roll. We had high expectations.

"It's easy to say, knowing what I know today, what would I do? But we had a good experience last year, and so I kind of wasn't expecting it to go this way this year."

McCarthy coached Rodgers with the Packers from 2006 through December 2018 and they won a Super Bowl by beating the Steelers to cap the 2010 season.
