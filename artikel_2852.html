What we learned as Kings' dreadful home streak continues in loss to Thunder

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

What we learned as Kings' dreadful home streak continues in loss to Thunder originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SACRAMENTO — It's been 219 days since Kings fans have seen the beam after a home win at Golden 1 Center.

Sacramento on Sunday dropped its eighth straight home game, extending the team's longest home winless streak to begin a season in franchise history with a 125-103 loss to the Oklahoma City Thunder.

The Kings' last victory at G1C came in April 10 last season against the Golden State Warriors.

The Kings are in danger of dropping all seven games of their season-long homestead if they don't beat the Phoenix Suns on Wednesday. They fell to 1-12 on the year, and remain on pace for one of the worst records in NBA history.

But they still have company at the bottom of the standings. The Utah Jazz and Milwaukee Bucks each fell to 1-13 with losses on Sunday.

The Thunder, who came in with a three-game losing streak, were playing without four key rotation players: Chet Holmgren, Cason Wallace, Jalen Williams and Jaylin Williams.

Which meant the stage was set for OKC's two-time NBA MVP to attack Sacramento's 28th-ranked defense.

Gilgeous-Alexander had 21 points in the first half and 16 in the third quarter. The Thunder went into the fourth quarter with a 14-point lead they extended to 27, and Gilgeous-Alexander didn't have to re-enter the game.

He finished with 37 points on 13-of-20 shooting – included 4 of 5 from 3-point range – and got wherever he wanted on the court. Rookie center Aday Mara finished second on OKC in scoring with 14 points and 13 rebounds in 26 minutes.

Mara had more points and rebounds than Domantas Sabonis, who finished with 11 points, 12 rebounds and three assists in 29 minutes.

Kings prized rookie Darius Acuff Jr. came into the game leading all rookies in scoring, assists and minutes. The 19-year-old has been the focus of opponents' scouting reports and game plans throughout his young NBA career.

It shouldn't be entirely surprising Acuff had one of his most challenging performances against the Thunder, who came into the game with the NBA's fifth-ranked defense.

Acuff had a career-worst nine turnovers. He reached his seventh early in the fourth quarter while no other Kings player had more than one. He led the Kings in scoring with 26 points but also had the worst plus-minus at minus-24 in just under 33 minutes.

Acuff had another volume shooting night, going 10 of 25 from the floor. He's had at least 19 shot attempts in every game this season.

The Kings have made it clear that Acuff is their centerpiece going forward and will take the lumps typical for rookies. But the team will continue to harp on avoiding turnovers.

The Kings were without three players who had started at least nine games in Zach LaVine (illness), DeAndre Hunter (hip soreness) and Precious Achiuwa (left ankle soreness).

Doug Christie added Malik Monk, Dylan Cardwell and Keegan Murray in their places joining Darius Acuff Jr. and Domantas Sabonis.

It didn't get off to a good start. The Thunder made 5 of their first 7 shots and took a 13-4 lead at the first timeout three-plus minutes in before the first substitution.

Christie made a change to start the second half, going with Leaky Black at forward instead with Cardwell on the bench serving as Sabonis' backup. Christie didn't play two bigs together again until garbage time late in the fourth quarter.

Murray, who has been dealing with left ankle capsulitis, was listed as questionable entering Sunday's game before being announced as available roughly 90 minutes before tipoff.

He struggled to get going, making just 1 of his first 5 shots in 17 minutes, adding just a single rebound and no assists. Murray didn't hit his second shot until 2:00 in the third quarter when he made a 3 from the right wing. He went on to airball a contested corner 3 in the final seconds of the period.

Murray finished with just 6 points and 1 rebound in 21 minutes. He made 2 of 9 shots.
