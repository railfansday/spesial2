President Donald Trump's Investment Team Dumped McDonald's in September and Bought Up to $25 Million of a Time-Tested Dividend ETF

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The president disclosed 751 trades in September, with one low-cost exchange-traded fund (ETF) topping the buy list.

By Sean Williams – Nov 24, 2026 at 4:06AM EST

10 stocks we like better than McDonald's ›

Some of the world's most successful money managers oversee hundreds of trades per quarter -- but that doesn't hold a candle to the jaw-dropping activity in President Donald Trump's investment account. The president's independent, third-party investment team has executed in the neighborhood of 30,000 trades since January 2025, according to reports filed with the U.S. Office of Government Ethics (OGE).

According to the latest OGE filing, Trump's investment team executed 751 trades in September. This included dumping up to $5,000,000 in McDonald's (MCD +1.15%) stock and piling up to $25,000,000 into the Vanguard Dividend Appreciation ETF (VIG +2.18%).

President Trump's independent, third-party investment team oversaw 751 trades in September. Image source: Official White House Photo by Daniel Torok.

Although Donald Trump is a connoisseur of McDonald's food, his third-party investment team isn't. Though several small trades were executed in McDonald's stock during September, the $1,000,001-$5,000,000 sale on the 23rd ties for the largest disposition of the month.

Despite shares being historically inexpensive, the company is navigating numerous headwinds that aren't easy fixes.

For example, McDonald's is being hit on both ends due to inflation (rising prices). On the one hand, the company's value menu risks pressuring its margins amid persistently elevated inflation. Conversely, raising prices to protect its margins could be off-putting to the low- and middle-income customers that have long been the lifeblood of the company's customer base.

Furthermore, while McDonald's NEXT strategy offers promise of a turnaround, the company's plans to spend up to $8.5 billion through 2036 to assist its franchisees provide minimal near-term benefits. In a stock market that's been dominated by growth stocks, the last thing investors want to hear is that it could take years before the company's investments really start to pay off.

At the other end of the spectrum, the president's independent, third-party investment team scooped up between $5,000,001 and $25,000,000 of the Vanguard Dividend Appreciation ETF on Sept. 23.

Vanguard's dividend-focused ETF closed October with nearly $136 billion in assets under management and a portfolio containing a blend of 333 value and growth stocks. The common theme is that these are large-cap companies that have increased their annual payouts for at least 10 consecutive years. In other words, these are time-tested businesses with rich histories of profitability and transparent growth outlooks.

Additionally, the Vanguard Dividend Appreciation ETF removes companies in the top 25% of yield to avoid potential yield traps. While this exchange-traded fund's 1.5% yield isn't jaw-dropping, its payout is incredibly safe.

Since this is a passive ETF with minimal turnover, it has a minuscule net expense ratio of 0.04%. Only $0.40 of every $1,000 invested goes toward management/marketing fees, enabling investors to retain more of their principal and long-term gains.

Best of all, dividend stocks have an extensive history of outperforming publicly traded companies that don't offer a regular dividend. In "The Power of Dividends: Past, Present, and Future," the analysts at Hartford Funds, in collaboration with Ned Davis Research, found that dividend growers and initiators averaged a 10.22% annual return from 1973 to 2025. By comparison, the non-payers trudged their way to an average annual return of 4.21% over the same time frame.

Dividend stocks are a smart way to hedge in a historically pricey stock market.
