A 12% Dividend Yield Worth A Look From TPG Mortgage Comes With Risk

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Everyone would like a 12% yield that comes with little to no risk. With MITT-B (MITT.PR.B), investors can get that 12% yield.

Unfortunately, the risk decided to come along.

MITT-B is a preferred share from TPG Mortgage Investment Trust (MITT). Shares are currently trading around $16.94 and offer a stripped yield of approximately 12.10%.

That's a massive yield for a preferred share. However, it does come with our highest risk rating. MITT-B carries a risk rating of 5 on our scale from 1 to 5. Of all the preferred shares we cover, only two companies have preferred shares carrying that rating. MITT is one of them.

Is it bad? Well, in a way, yes. However, we're giving MITT-B a buy rating.

Because there's a difference between a risky preferred share and a bad investment. At the right price, even some of the riskiest preferred shares can be worth a purchase.

At the end of the day, a preferred share isn't bad just because it carries significant risk. It's a bad investment when investors arenât getting compensated for the risk they are taking on.

MITT-B has a fixed coupon rate of 8%. That's not an absurd coupon rate for the preferred shares we cover. It isn't even unusual.

Investors are getting such a high yield because of the price. At $16.94, investors are purchasing shares at a significant discount to the $25 call value. Even though the price is down so low, investors are still entitled to the same dividend amount. The current yield is around 11.81%. However, we are using the stripped yield of 12.10% because it accounts for dividend accrual.

Look at the common equity to preferred liquidation ratio: 0.95.

Itâs even uglier if you use market cap instead of common equity.

We like to see a substantial amount of common equity protecting the preferred shares. Generally speaking, the common equity absorbs losses before the preferred shareholders. The bigger that cushion, the better.

For most of the preferred shares we cover, this ratio is substantially higher. That's especially true for preferred shares with our lowest risk ratings.

As an example, we recently covered DX-D (DX.PR.D) from Dynex Capital (DX). Dynex preferred shares have a coverage ratio around 11. Remember, MITT preferred shares have a ratio of 0.95.

One of these preferred shares has enough cushion to survive a fall from a roof.

The other is hoping gravity gets distracted.

MITT-B is offering investors a stripped yield around 12.10% and is in our buy range.

However, MITT-B carries our highest risk rating. The weak coverage ratio is a large part of that. We believe there are other preferred shares currently trading at a much better risk/reward profile. For investors with a high-risk tolerance, MITT-B is worth considering.

Everyone would love a 12% yield from a preferred share issued by Dynex Capital or Annaly Capital.

MITT-B offers the yield but comes with our highest risk rating.
