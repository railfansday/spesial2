Fantasy football trade targets: 10 players to buy or sell before Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

In yet another sign that the NFL does not care about making fantasy football convenient, the league scheduled the most bye weeks of any week this season – six – in Week 11. That's the trade deadline in many leagues and that timing could muck up some deals.

Keep in mind that with Atlanta, Cleveland, Green Bay, New England, Seattle and the Los Angeles Rams all on bye, you might need to trade for a player sitting out in Week 11 if that's what it takes to get a deal done. You're here to win your league, not just win the week, and depth is never at more of a premium than during the stretch run of the playoffs.

If your deadline is this week, send some trade offers out. Get a deal done. You'll miss this feeling in April. Good luck and may the odds be in your favor.

Here are 10 fantasy football trade targets for Week 11:

This is really a move to buy any starting Bears player – of Chicago's seven games to end the fantasy year, only two of them are against above-average defenses. There should be plenty of opportunities to feast. You could also go for receiver Luther Burden or running back Kyle Monangai, but Loveland might be the cheaper option after a miserable start to the season. Buy before the deadline and feel confident about starting a TE1 down the stretch.

I'm not going to pretend to know what's going on with Detroit and Sam LaPorta, but the tight end has been invisible since publicly complaining about his contract a month ago. Whenever one of LaPorta and Williams are down, the other is usually up. That, along with Detroit losing its best defensive player in Aidan Hutchinson, means it's a good time to buy Williams. We love big-time playmakers in shootouts during the fantasy playoffs, when a huge individual week can potentially win you a title.

You'll need to hope the Coleman manager in your league is anxious about the rookie not yet clearing 60 rushing yards in a game, while potentially dealing with the return of RJ Harvey off IR. If they are, buy Coleman before the deadline. He's significantly more explosive than Denver's other options and talented rookies coming out of their byes are usually fun bets to end the year.

In just his second game of the season in Week 10, rookie Jordan Tyson led Saints pass catchers with 11 catches for 80 yards. Olave settled for just three catches for 30 yards on four targets. This is just a simple buy low on the Saints' best player. Tyson looks the part but that should only help open things back up for Olave over time.

A 2025 undrafted free agent out of UC Davis, Larison is the kind of prove-it player that teams only give a chance to if things are bleak. Well, here we are. New England's offense becoming Tommy DeVito throwing to Mack Hollins means that veteran Rhamondre Stevenson might not be rushed back from an injury, which would leave plenty of backfield opportunities for Corey Kiner (who has played on four NFL teams in two years) and Larison, who scored a touchdown and had another called back during Week 10.

Indianapolis' backup QB should be free right now and it's worth taking a shot on the 2023 No. 4 overall pick before he inevitably gets a chance to start for the Colts. This is a lost season and Daniel Jones has been arguably the worst starting QB in the NFL. At the very least, Richardson offers a high rushing floor that could make him useful in certain fantasy leagues.

Johnson is a nice player to have on the bench for the playoffs, so you should only make this move if you can find another manager willing to really overpay for Johnson's big breakout game vs. the Falcons (101 rushing yards, plus six catches for 70 yards and two touchdowns). But Kenneth Walker should be back in Week 11, and even if he's not, he's going to continue getting a workhorse role if healthy in the fantasy playoffs. Selling high means trading an injury-dependent handcuff with upside for a starting RB return.

CMac was on the sell list last week and promptly ripped off 223 all-purpose yards and three touchdowns against the Cowboys. Yikes. However, those huge numbers also came on just 17 touches. Rookie Kaelon Black has earned a consistent role and San Francisco doesn't get to play Dallas every week. You should think about selling high at overall RB1 prices if you need depth another manager wants to overpay.

If scoring a touchdown in Week 10 helps get you anything at all in return for Strange, you should sell at the deadline. He now has an ugly two catches in each of his past three games. Jacksonville spreads the ball around too much on offense for Strange to be reliable enough to start, and while he's going to catch more than two balls again soon, his upside isn't high enough to match the low floor.

This might be too reactionary, as I am a huge Wilson believer and the Jets play some rough defenses to end the year. However, if Cade Klubnik replacing a potentially injured Geno Smith is New York's plan at QB to end the year, I want no part of Wilson. Let someone else deal with a rookie QB who looked erratic at best against a bad Bills defense in Week 10.
