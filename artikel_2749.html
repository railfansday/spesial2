KIRK SPEAKS: Northwestern

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

It's time to declare the 2026 Iowa Football team as the Jekyll and Hyde Hawkeyes. I have no clue what to think of this team, and I have no clue what version of the team is going to show up on any given Saturday. When they're good, they're really good, and when they're bad, woof, they're really bad.

The fate of the program's hope for a playoff appearance this season now rides on this weekend's trip to Evanston for a huge matchup against -checks notes- -checks notes again- the Northwestern Wildcats, who are having a breakout season in head coach David Braun's fourth season at the helm of the program. The Wildcats are a talented team and Iowa will have its hands full, particularly if the Hyde version makes its appearance on the road once again, under the lights at Northwestern's incredible looking new stadium.

But let's not focus on what I think, let's see what Kirk Ferentz had to say about things in his weekly press conference, shall we? Check out the full transcript HERE and my highlights and snark below. -snort-

We'll start with this, something bigger than football:

KIRK FERENTZ: Good afternoon. So, a little bit last weekend and move on to this game coming up.

You know, with Jack Campbell, we all became aware on Sunday shortly after what was going on there, and I want to share our support certainly to the family and, most importantly, to Jack.

Been in contact with the family, expressed that to them, and they've kept us updated, but we're going to respect their wishes right now. They'll handle the information that comes out. When they think it's appropriate, they'll release that information and go from there.

I think all of you know just how impactful Jack was in our program, what a tremendous person he is, what a tremendous player he's been for us, and now he's moved on and done the exact same thing in Detroit. Not only playing really well, but just a tremendous leader and tremendous addition.

We're all keeping him in our thoughts, and certainly hope everybody that's a Hawkeye fan does the same.

Sending all the best vibes possible to Jack Campbell and his family. This is a situation that goes beyond football in every way. I don't give a shit if he plays another down of football again, but I care deeply about what kind of life he gets to live moving forward. Things seem positive, which is great, but let's keep the positive vibes flowing for an all-time Hawkeye.

Q. I wanted to ask about the month of November. The calendar has turned, and this is a month that you guys always seem to play your best football. How and why does that happen? Do you think this team has the characteristics to have other teams that have done well in this month?

KIRK FERENTZ: We're going to find out. That's one of the great things about it because this is where things get interesting. We have a high number of guys not practicing right now. Not just, like, in the two-deep, but guys in general.

So, you start getting stressed a little and stressed in different ways in November typically. Emotionally, physically, there's a lot of things that beneath the surface that are taking place. Ultimately, you have to be able to perform, find a way to get it done, and that's really what it gets down to if you are going to have a successful season.

I've talked to our players about this every year. If you are going to be successful in a conference, you have to win on the road. You've got to find a way, because you're going play half your games — and I understand we play an odd number of games, so it's not technically half, but over two years it's half your games on the road. If you want to use that as a crutch, you can't. You're not going to be very successful.

Same thing about November. It's just meeting the challenges, whatever they may be, and every November has got them. Having the right mindset and then you got to go out and play, and we're going to found out. Hopefully it's positive.

There truly is something special about Kirk Ferentz teams in November…but can that trend hold up? This team's inconsistencies are not something we normally see ahead of the more polished November Iowa teams. The fact that this team also seems to be a little worse on the road makes this a particularly difficult month for the team on paper. With the playoff still very much in play this late in the season, we're not really used to having November be so consequential to the season overall in additional to rivalries, and it's going to be interesting to see how it plays out.

Q. For Hank, and I know your quarterback situation has been a little different this year. Can you speak to what it's been like for a guy like Hank to not perform great at times and then come out there and stay steady and earn a Big Ten award this season?

KIRK FERENTZ: Yeah. We talked about Deshaun, TJ, and Kamari. It's part of being a young player, and I've been trying to say that since January probably that whoever is going to be a new player playing in a position, you have to understand it's not going to look like it will probably as a senior.

We just talked about the quarterback from Northwestern. I would venture to say he's playing at a much higher level than he was two years ago. Played really good against us. We witnessed that firsthand, but you can't hand experience to player.

Sometimes occasionally guys come in and just play really well right off the bat and go, but usually most good players I've been around are better a year later and two years later after they got started.

Hank has just had a great attitude. He's worked hard, and he's been here two years now, just like Jeremy. A little less time with Jeremy, but still a long time.

We double-listed him this week for two reasons. I wanted to hear what the public wanted. We've heard it's gone back and forth ample times. I'm joking there, but my point of emphasis is we like both guys. They're both good players.

You can only play one, and obviously what Hank did the other day was really impressive. Right now he's our starter, but we like both players. They both are improving with every week hopefully, and that's what we're seeing at least.

We did it folks, an Iowa quarterback won Big Ten Player of the Week. What a time to be alive. Hank has had his good moments and some really bad moments, but the number of good moments continues to climb. Hank is clearly QB1 going forward (again) barring injury, but we have a new drinking game: drink every time Kirk Ferentz says he likes both of his quarterbacks. We'll all be getting sloshed fast.

Q. First, it seemed like Tony might have gotten banged up. Is he doing okay health-wise?

Thank God. Tim Lester did a fantastic job getting Tony Diaz more involved in the offense last weekend, and it would definitely be concerning to lose him for any of the remaining games on the schedule. Feed the ball to this man in any possible way. The coaching staff needs to do everything possible to keep Diaz on the roster next season.

Q. You mentioned Cael Winter is likely out. Does that mean we're going to see Myslinski?

KIRK FERENTZ: Yeah, Mike jumped in and did a really good job Saturday, not surprisingly. I think he's our most veteran player, quite frankly. Just had a 25th birthday last week.

He did a great job Saturday, and every confidence he'll play really well this weekend.

Not a great week to be without the starting center, but I guess we'll have to do. Mike Myslinski did a nice job coming into the game on short notice last week, so hopefully this doesn't become a huge issue.

Let's end with this (not a super newsy press conference week):

Q. When you look at the film, they're second in the country in red zone efficiency. What do you see from this unique offense that you need to prepare for maybe uniquely?

KIRK FERENTZ: I probably could talk for like an hour about him. Just tremendous respect. Let me start there. I've gotten to know him a little bit over the last several years. I think a really good person. Just a brilliant football coach.

If you look at his career, what he's done in his career, the multitude of things and going back to Oregon, time in the NFL, UCLA, he's done a lot of different things in a lot of different ways. You have to be adaptable to be good at anything you do, and he certainly is that. He's got a great football mind.

He's in a new situation. I don't want to speak with great authority, but I would think it's probably a little bit unique and different than what he's been in at several places. Good coaches find a way to make it work, and he's certainly had a real good impact with the program.

We've played against the quarterback, and he played really well against us. Probably wasn't as consistent that year. I don't want to speak for the coaches there, but he's playing really consistent now, and I think that's him being older too and being more experienced. He's with a really good coaching staff. So I can't say enough.

Again, you go that record, that 6-1 record, and they played really well up at Oregon, which a lot of people have not, again, it just speaks to the way they're coached. They're coached really well in all three phases, and Chip is a big part of that.

We are truly living in crazy times when Northwestern is one of the best red zone teams in the country. Thankfully I feel like the Hawkeyes are one of the best red zone defenses in the country, so we'll see what happens here. I like our odds…as long as the prepared, disciplined version of this team shows up.
