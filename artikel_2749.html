Werder Bremen vs Hamburg preview: Derby Day in the North

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Despite all the worry going into the campaign, this season has been a fantastic one so far for Werder Bremen.

They were tipped as relegation candidates in the summer. It was a fair claim considering how badly the previous year went, and the amount of turnover that took place in the offseason. An injury to Jens Stage and an opening day blowout to Freiburg only further cemented those beliefs.

However, things turned around very quickly for Bremen. They beat down RB Leipzig on matchday two, which began a seven-match unbeaten run. The team was scoring for fun, especially a returning Niclas Fullkrug who found the back of the net in every game during that spell.

While there have been some slip-ups defensively, the back four and new number one Karl Hein are doing a solid enough job at that end of the pitch. Marco Friedl and Amos Pieper have formed a strong relationship. Both can be world class on their day, and if they are at their best then Werder are in pretty safe hands.

This will be a big test for the club, though. While supporters are still thrilled with being far ahead of the bottom three, avoiding relegation is no longer the goal. Their sights are set much higher. The Green-Whites are in the thick of the European race, and the pressure is on to finally return to the international stage.

Bremen are confident that their best would be more than enough to pick up a derby day win. However, they also know how often the team can fall flat, especially to start out games. If the hosts have a slow start, they'll set themselves up for real disappointment.

Then again, this is the exact contest the players and staff will be incredibly excited to start.

Werder are still without long-term absences like Felix Agu, Moussa N'Diaye, Jens Stage, Salim Musah, and Keke Topp. The real issue is with how many players are doubtful for the match, with Senne Lynen, Mitchell Weiser, and Marco Grull potentially missing out.

Hein, Arthur, Pieper, Friedl, Deman, Reeger, Reis, Chuki, Njinmah, Fullkrug, Dincki

It seemed like Hamburg were set for a nightmare season based on their opening few weeks.

They were historically bad, losing back-to-back games 5-0 after a loss in their first match. Many wrote off HSV right after, not expecting much of a turnaround unless their coach was fired. Instead, manager Merlin Polzin received a contract extension.

The risky move paid off, at least for now. Hamburg made a remarkable turnaround, immediately winning three games on the bounce. They beat Koln at home, and then grabbed late winners against both Hoffenheim and Stuttgart. That not only dug themselves out of the hole, but it put them pretty clear of the bottom three.

Things have been up-and-down since. Die Rothosen did lose to Paderborn and then twice to Eintracht Frankfurt, but they secured one more win before the international break, defeating Borussia Mönchengladbach. They're doing it mainly by scoring screamers, with a number of long-distance strikes earning their goals. Miro Muheim leads them with three goals so far.

What's especially fascinating is that Hamburg could vault themselves into the European picture with a win of their own here. They'd go level with their neighbors as a result, and end up between a number of different teams. However, a loss would keep them back, and a further slide means relegation worries are back on the cards.

This derby was fruitful to the home sides last year, but a road victory would be quite the reward for their supporters.

Hamburg will be without a number of key players, as Jordan Torunarigha, Albert Sambi Lokonga, Yussuf Poulsen, Bilal Nadir, and Nicolai Remberg are all out, while Fábio Vieira and Miro Muheim are doubtful as well.
