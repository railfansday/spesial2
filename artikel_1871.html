ProRef's double standards were infuriating in Brentford vs Everton, it's so obvious it's embarrassing - verdict

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Senior Correspondent AUTHORITY Senior football journalist specialising in refereeing and officiating; former contributor to The Football League Paper, Late Tackle, and the Premier League; University of Derby graduate. FOCUS Refereeing controversies, football finance and governance, PGMOL decisions, and officiating analysis across the Premier League, EFL, and SPFL. Craig Pawson was ultimately let down by those around him as Brentford thrashed Everton at the Gtech Community Stadium.

Keith Andrews' side fired four unanswered goals past their opponents on Monday, 23 November, who were down to 10 men for much of the game.

James Tarkowski was sent off for his studs-up challenge on Yehor Yarmoliuk in the 40th minute, and even that could be argued.

Sure, the challenge was high and probably quite painful for the Bees' midfielder, but it was evident that he tried pulling out at the end.

Pawson, the on-field referee, appeared to see the incident perfectly in real time, but it was only when it was slowed down for him that he was pushed into sending the player off.

That should not be what VAR is used for. It's there as a support tool, not something to re-referee a game, which it is far too often.

The officiating got more frustrating, though. Later in the game, in the 59th minute, Everton fans wanted the playing fields evened up.

Michael Kayode went flying studs-up into Jack Grealish, who had skipped past the full-back, before crashing to the floor in pain.

While it was maybe not as dangerous as Tarkowski's, there was just as much malice in the challenge and was deserving of a punishment.

However, after VAR decided not to intervene, it was clear that Pawson simply hadn't seen it properly, and Kayode avoided even a caution.

Why do the rules stop VAR from suggesting that a yellow card would be appropriate? What if Kayode makes another cautioable foul later in the game? That should be a sending-off.

Fans up and down the country are continuously frustrated by the Premier League's officiating, whether that be on the pitch or at Stockley Park.

There's no accountability, double standards in the same game, and the only ones actually being punished are the players.

What happens if a referee or a VAR official make a mistake? Sure, they get taken out of the limelight, but that's Howard Webb trying to take the heat off them.

There so much wrong with the officiating in the sport, and it couldn't be any more evident when rugby's Nations Championship is going on.
