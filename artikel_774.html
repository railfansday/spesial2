Selena Gomez No Longer Facing Lawsuit Over Mental Health Startup Wondermind

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A lawyer for Gomez, who was sued alongside her mother and former company exec Daniella Pierson, says she "looks forward to putting this matter behind her."

Investors in Selena Gomez’s mental health startup Wondermind have dropped their fraud lawsuit against the star and company.

A Monday (Nov. 16) court notice, obtained and first reported by Billboard, says that five investors are permanently dismissing the claims they brought in August against Wondermind, Gomez, her mother Mandy Teefey and former company executive Daniella Pierson. Gomez’s attorney, Mathew Rosengart, told Billboard on Monday, “Selena is pleased by the dismissal of the case on the heels of our motion to dismiss on her behalf, and looks forward to putting this matter behind her.”

A lawyer for Pierson, Scott Balber, told Billboard on Monday that they did not pay any settlement in the case. “Ms. Pierson did nothing wrong,” said Balber. “Apparently, the plaintiff recognized that and dismissed the complaint against her. Unfortunately, Ms. Pierson’s reputation has already been damaged by these false allegations. We are hopeful that the dismissal of the lawsuit will be the first step in restoring her good name.”

Teefey and the investors’ attorneys did not immediately return requests for comment.

Wondermind launched in 2021 with Teefey and Pierson as co-CEOs and public support from Gomez. The plan was for the company to host a suite of mental health media initiatives, including a digital magazine, mobile app and podcast.

An investment vehicle run by five men — Brent Saunders, Marc Roberts, EJ Solimine, Andrew Resnick and Mark Peikin — put $1.2 million into Wondermind in 2022. In the August lawsuit, the investors alleged they were duped by false promises that Gomez would be actively involved as Wondermind’s head of marketing, thus leveraging her huge fanbase and 400 million Instagram followers. They were also allegedly told that Wondermind had a “potential future valuation” of more than $4 billion and solid brand partnerships in place already.

Last year, The Cut published a bombshell investigation reporting that Gomez had distanced herself from Wondermind and that the company’s finances were in dire condition, due in part to Teefey’s alleged substance abuse issues and erratic behavior. According to the lawsuit, this article led the investors to believe they’d been defrauded. They asked for their money back and, when it didn’t come, resorted to litigation.

Gomez’s team was quick to argue that she did not belong in the lawsuit. Rosengart called the case “absurd” in court papers, saying Gomez had no involvement in Wondermind’s fundraising operations and “should never have been dragged into this action.”

The other defendants had not responded to the lawsuit in writing by the time of Monday’s dismissal. In the Cut article, Teefey denied allegations of company mismanagement and said Wondermind remained viable: “I started Wondermind because I wanted to help people with mental illness,” Teefey said in a statement to the magazine. “It’s unfortunate that a few disgruntled employees with an ax to grind can spread lies about me and distort the truth. Even more disappointing that the media is willing to amplify their lies.”
