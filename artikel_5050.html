Bitcoin’s $67,000 crash puts $57 billion holder cluster at risk as ETF investors flee

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Bitcoin has reached a major historical cost-basis zone, with selective whale accumulation facing ETF withdrawals and renewed inflation pressure.

Bitcoin‘s drop to $67,000 is testing a $57 billion holder cluster as ETF outflows surpass $1 billion.

The cryptocurrency traded at $67,105.61 at 12:31 UTC on Nov. 14, down 11.6% over seven days and 3.04% in 24 hours, according to CryptoSlate data. The decline places it inside a $65,000–$68,000 range where Coinbase Institutional estimates 855,000 BTC last moved.

Those last-moved prices serve as a proxy for investors' acquisition costs, creating a potential break-even zone for a substantial group of holders. Some investors may defend their positions against further losses, while others could sell into a recovery to recover their initial investment.

In its Nov. 13 commentary, Coinbase identified $66,000–$68,000 as potential support, citing an overlap between the cost-basis concentration, historical pivot levels and Bitcoin's 200-week moving average.

US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded $185.4 million in net outflows on Nov. 13, according to Farside Investors, bringing withdrawals over five consecutive sessions to $1.065 billion.

The sustained outflows suggest a deterioration in demand from a major investment channel at a time when Bitcoin needs fresh capital to absorb selling from existing holders. Continued redemptions could leave the cryptocurrency increasingly dependent on spot-market buyers to prevent a deeper decline.

Despite the institutional withdrawals, on-chain data suggests some large Bitcoin holders have been increasing their exposure during the correction.

Glassnode's Nov. 11 research identified accumulation among wallets holding 100–1,000 BTC and 1,000–10,000 BTC, while other wallet-size groups continued reducing their balances.

The research firm also observed more resting buy orders than sell orders within 5% of Bitcoin's spot price on Binance and Coinbase, suggesting traders were positioning to purchase further declines.

However, Glassnode warned that some buy orders had already been withdrawn during the selloff, raising uncertainty about how much of that liquidity would remain available if selling intensified.

The competing signals leave Bitcoin facing a demand imbalance, with selective accumulation among larger wallets running against sustained ETF withdrawals and selling by other investor groups.

October's inflation data has added another constraint to the recovery outlook.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Nov. 13 that US producer prices excluding food, energy, and trade services rose 0.5% month-on-month, seasonally adjusted, and 4.9% annually, unadjusted.

Coinbase warned that additional Federal Reserve interest-rate increases could disadvantage Bitcoin because the cryptocurrency generates no yield. Higher borrowing costs could also discourage leveraged investors from providing liquidity during the downturn.

Bitcoin's ability to hold the $65,000–$68,000 range now hinges on whether selective accumulation can offset sustained ETF withdrawals and selling by existing holders.

A sustained breakdown below $65,000 would weaken Coinbase's support thesis and expose investors who accumulated within the cluster to deeper unrealized losses.
