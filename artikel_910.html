Playoff picture taking shape and Mendoza belongs - NFL Sunday Review

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

NFL Sunday Review - we reflect on Week 11 in the NFL as Travis Kelce moved past Rob Gronkowski for touchdowns all-time among tight ends, Las Vegas Raiders' No 1 overall draft pick Fernando Mendoza gave evidence he belongs and the Pittsburgh Steelers look in danger in the AFC playoff race

It's an oft trotted out cliché that 'football season starts after Thanksgiving'. Well, with the American holiday only three days away, the NFL playoff picture is truly starting to take shape going into the stretch run.

The San Francisco 49ers (9-1) further asserted their dominance in the NFC with a thumping 35-10 victory over the Minnesota Vikings and their much-vaunted defense in Mexico City - the final International Series game of the season - while the Buffalo Bills (9-1) share the same record as the AFC top seeds, albeit they were far less convincing in their 26-9 dispatching of the one-win Miami Dolphins.

Josh Allen threw two interceptions to allow the Dolphins to hang around until the Bills pulled away late. He and the team will need to sharpen up for their mouthwatering Thanksgiving night meeting with the Kansas City Chiefs (8-2) - live on Sky Sports NFL from 1.20am, Friday - that could ultimately end up deciding where the road to the Super Bowl runs through in the AFC.

However, it's below the Bills and the Chiefs, and the 49ers on the other side of the conference divide, where things are really starting to get interesting.

The Denver Broncos (8-2) are also in the mix for top billing in the AFC, after claiming a sixth straight against the Las Vegas Raiders, while the Cincinnati Bengals (7-2) have the opportunity to match that mark with a Monday night victory at the Washington Commanders.

The Jacksonville Jaguars (7-3) stumbled at the New York Giants (6-3-1), whose impressive victory strengthened their grip on the NFC East division, which the Dallas Cowboys (5-6) are still in the hunt for after hanging 51 on the Tennessee Titans defense.

The Jaguars' loss was far from fatal, with the Titans, along with the Indianapolis Colts and the Houston Texans all floundering in their division, though the Pittsburgh Steelers' (6-4) shock loss at the Philadelphia Eagles might well have opened the door to the latter in the race for a wildcard berth.

The Texans, who started the season 0-4, now sit 4-6 and - after a meeting with a Baltimore Ravens team in the midst of a six-game losing streak - will likely have the opportunity to match the Steelers' record when the two teams meet in a fortnight, following Pittsburgh's tricky assignment against the Broncos where they'll try to avoid dropping three in a row.

Meanwhile, back in the NFC, the greatest intrigue lies in the NFC North and the four-way battle unfolding for the division, with the Detroit Lions (8-2), Chicago Bears (8-2), Vikings (7-3) - even with their chastening loss to the Niners - and Green Bay Packers (6-4) all currently playoff bound.

Mind you, they all have the looming threat of the wildcard-hunting Los Angeles Rams (6-4) and defending Super Bowl-champion Seattle Seahawks (6-4) from the NFC West.

It makes the Thanksgiving night clash between the Lions and Bears in Detroit - live on Sky Sports NFL from 6pm, Thursday - even more crucial.

Lost in amongst how historically bad their defense is - even the one-win Titans scored on five of their first six possessions, one week on from the 49ers managing to score 46 points and a staggering 584 yards against them - is how impressive their offense is, and needs to be!

The Cowboys are just the fourth team in NFL history to allow at least 20 points in each of their first 11 games and still win at least five of those games. They have allowed 20 points in 18 straight games dating back to Week 12 of last season.

But Dak Prescott threw four touchdown passes and ran in another score as Dallas found the end zone on each of their first six drives in their 51-30 thumping of the Titans to somewhat mask their continued defensive woes.

There's still teething issues as the No 1 overall draft picks eases his way into NFL life - most notably the game-sealing pick-six he threw that ensured he starts his career with back-to-back defeats in his first two starts. But there was a lot to like too.

Mendoza and the Raiders hung with the Broncos far longer than many expected, with it being just a one-score game when the rookie QB tossed that costly interception.

Prior to that, he completed 18 of 37 passes for 267 yards and three passing touchdowns - his first in the NFL.

"I felt more comfortable than last week," Mendoza admitted afterwards. "I was able to settle in and able to see the entire field better.

"If you just do your job, your momentum is going to swing your way eventually."

Are the Steelers in danger of dropping out of the AFC playoff picture?

It certainly looks like they're in trouble. The 19-16 reverse in Philadelphia not only was the Eagles' first win in eight games, it was the Steelers' second straight loss, with Aaron Rodgers, who turns 43 on December 2, looking every bit his age as he limped to 95 passing yards when going 17 of 20 in the first half.

That turned into 227 yards by the end of the game after some garbage-time stat-padding, but it's looking a little rough for the Steelers, who currently occupy the seventh and final playoff berth in the AFC and have a dire run of stellar defenses to come over the next three-week stretch in the Broncos, Texans and Jaguars.

Helping their cause is the fact that the AFC isn't exactly flush with challengers. The New England Patriots (5-5) are currently their closes rivals but they're in the midst of their own crisis with starting quarterback Drake Maye out for the year, then there's the Texans (4-6) and that's pretty much it.

Though Houston's record looks pretty ugly at the minute, they won nine straight from 3-5 to get into the playoffs last year, so are well capable of going on a run.

Their visit to Pittsburgh in two weeks' time could prove pivotal.

David Bailey: The New York Jets rookie edge rusher had two sacks in their win at the Los Angeles Chargers that broke their five-game losing streak, giving him nine-and-a-half sacks on the season to go with six forced fumbles, including one against the Chargers.

The No 2 overall draft pick generated five pressures, meaning he has now recorded 45 pressures this season, the third-most by a rookie defender through Week 11 since 2018, trailing only Jared Verse (50 in 2024) and Nick Bosa (49 in 2019).

Travis Kelce: The veteran Chiefs tight end had 74 receiving yards to go with two receiving touchdowns in their win over the Arizona Cardinals, his first game with multiple receiving touchdowns since Week 11 of the 2022 season against the Chargers.

The 37-year-old's eight-yard score on Kansas City's opening possession also saw him pass Rob Gronkowski into third place for the most total touchdowns (including playoffs) by a tight end in NFL history with 109.

Only Pro Football Hall of Famers Antonio Gates (118) and Tony Gonzalez (115) have scored more.

DK Metcalf: The Steelers receiver was ejected from their game in Philadelphia after committing two unsportsmanlike conduct penalties 10 offensive plays apart, with the second of those appearing to see him reach into Cooper DeJean's facemask and poke the defensive back near the eye, sparking a melee between the two teams.

The ejection is Metcalf's third for his career since his debut in 2019. That's tied with 49ers linebacker Dre Greenlaw for the most in the NFL during that span.

"It's emotional on a number of fronts," Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy said afterwards. "We lost a game, a tough game. You take all that in, and everything will be addressed. We can't have players getting ejected from games."

Tennessee Titans: "All gas, no brake" is Tennessee head coach Robert Saleh's much-quoted mantra.

Well, the Titans took that sentiment a touch too literally on Sunday with someone seemingly cutting the breaks as they went careering off a cliff against the Cowboys.

And the coach, renowned for his defensive acumen, was NOT happy about the performance as his defense let up 51 points.

"It was disgusting, embarrassing football," Saleh said afterwards. "That starts with me. I got to figure it out.

"We have no choice but to get better. It can't get any worse."

Jeffery Simmons has been everything the Bears could've hoped for following their blockbuster mid-season trade for the former Titans defensive tackle which saw Chicago cough up two first-round draft picks.

He was the standout from Dennis Allen's unrelenting defense in their victory over the New Orleans Saints in which Chicago racked up a season-high 23 quarterback pressures and six sacks - Simmons recording three of those, tying his career high in a single game.

In doing so, he also became the first Bears defensive tackled to have three sacks in a game since 2014, taking his tally to five in three games since arriving in Chicago.
