Vikings explain what went wrong on two costly turnovers in 49ers loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The story of the Vikings' blowout loss against the 49ers on Sunday night in Mexico City was the way the NFL's No. 1 offense marched up and down the field against the NFL's No. 1 defense. Kyle Shanahan against Brian Flores was the matchup that received all the attention leading up to this game, and San Francisco won it handily.

At the same time, for as ineffective as the Vikings' defense was, they might've had a chance to hang around in a shootout if it hadn't been for two critical turnovers from their offense. A pick-six from Kirk Cousins in the second quarter was a massive swing in the game. Then, with the Vikings hoping to spark a comeback early in the third quarter, a costly Justin Jefferson fumble all but put that idea to bed.

Those turnovers were a big part of the difference in a game where the Vikings won the time-of-possession battle and played the 49ers relatively even in total yards and first downs. Afterwards, Kevin O'Connell and the two players who committed the turnovers were asked about what went wrong on those plays.

The Vikings had just gone down 21-10 late in the first half after another long 49ers drive ended in a Mike Evans touchdown (that arguably should've been reviewed more closely). But they weren't out of it. Getting points in the final two minutes before halftime could've made it a one-score game with the Vikings getting the ball to start the second half.

That plan never had a chance. On the first play of the ensuing drive, with the Vikings at their own 13 after a penalty on the kick return, Cousins threw a pass that was jumped by 49ers safety Ji'Ayir Brown for an easy pick-six, making it a 28-10 game.
