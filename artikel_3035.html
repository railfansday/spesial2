Why Did Redwire Stock Pop on Friday

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

After a 74% drop, Redwire stock could be a buy.

By Rich Smith – Nov 27, 2026 at 1:49PM EST

On a holiday-shortened Friday in a holiday-shortened week, space company Redwire (RDW +9.17%) stock was one of the market's strongest performers, rising nearly 10% at one point before closing the day up 7.6%.

And here's the strangest part: There doesn't appear to be any news to explain it. No analyst upgrades, no price target changes -- not so much as a press release from Redwire itself to explain the move.

So what got investors so excited about Redwire on Friday? My best guess is that they're looking ahead to next week, when Redwire will present Thursday and Friday in Wiesbaden, Germany, at the LANDEURO 2026 international defense symposium and exposition.

Redwire is scheduled to showcase "robust multi-domain capabilities," including its propane-powered fuel cells, Octopus sensors, and Stalker and Penguin UAVs, at the exhibition, and could potentially announce sales concluded at its end.

That's just a guess, however, and the mere potential for contract announcements seems a pretty thin reed to support a 7.6% increase in Redwire's stock price. That said, a lot of risk has been squeezed out of this stock in 2026.

Since peaking in late May, shares of the space and defense stock have shed 74% of their value to just $6 and change. That's well below the $10 per share Redwire cost at the time of its 2021 SPAC IPO.

Although free cash flow-negative and unprofitable (analysts polled by S&P Global Market Intelligence don't expect to see GAAP profits before 2029), Redwire trades below what I consider a fair valuation for unprofitable space stocks at just 3.1x trailing sales.

While not a sure thing, investors betting on Redwire Friday are correct: At this price, Redwire could bounce higher on just about any positive catalyst.
