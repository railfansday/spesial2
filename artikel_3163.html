If You'd Invested $1,000 in an S&P 500 ETF Ahead of the Dot-Com Bubble, Here's What You'd Have Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

See how much the S&P 500 returned even with 13 years of zero returns.

By David Dierking – Nov 18, 2026 at 1:00PM EST

10 stocks we like better than SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust ›

Imagine investing in the stock market at one of the worst possible times.

On March 24, 2000, the S&P 500 closed at a record high. It wouldn't hit another one until May 30, 2007, more than seven years later.

It didn't last long, though. Shortly after this, the financial crisis started and led the S&P 500 into another multi-year bear market. Ultimately, it wouldn't hit a new all-time high that it stayed above until March 28, 2013.

It's not the way you want your investment to start -- 13 years of essentially zero returns.

But that's not the end of the story. The end of the financial crisis ushered in one of the best stretches ever for U.S. stocks. Fueled by several years of near-zero interest rates, an unprecedented liquidity boom following the COVID-19 pandemic, and the beginning of the artificial intelligence (AI) revolution, the S&P 500 has kept rising for well over a decade with few interruptions.

Since March 24, 2000, the S&P 500 has returned a total of 705% with dividends reinvested. If you had invested in the State Street SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY -0.22%), a $1,000 investment would have grown to $8,050, or an average annual return of just over 8%.

This goes to show you that you don't need to have perfect timing to build long-term wealth. Even with 13 years of essentially no return on investment, long-term investors would still have achieved returns that would have built substantial wealth over time. It's often not about finding the right entry and exit points. It's simply about being invested, period.
