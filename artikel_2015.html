West Ham told not to fear £8m Leeds United, Joel Piroe transfer twist

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Despite a change in the dugout, West Ham’s woes in the Championship are continuing.

Scott Parker is yet to taste victory since replacing Nuno Espírito Santo, with a 1-0 defeat away at Portsmouth in midweek meaning it’s now six second tier games without a win.

The Hammers are now slumped in mid-table after 17 league games, with it difficult to see how and when the East London outfit are going to get back on an upward trajectory.

A main cause for their dip in form has been a lack of goals in the team, particularly from the forward line, with none of their striking options getting on the scoresheet consistently enough.

It’s been a big surprise to see Joel Piroe, in particular, so out of form at this level, with the Dutchman in previous Championship seasons a goalscoring menace for both Swansea City and Leeds United.

But the 27-year-old has been unable to set the second tier stage alight again so far this term, leaving sections of the Hammers fanbase frustrated by his displays of late.

Meanwhile, West Ham would have to pay a seven-figure obligation fee to make Piroe’s stay at the London Stadium a permanent one should they gain promotion, raising fears about having a player on their books in the Premier League that is currently struggling in the Championship.

When West Ham managed to secure the signature of Championship marksman Joel Piroe on loan during the summer transfer window, it was originally seen as a big statement of intent about their ambitions to get back to the top-flight at the first time of asking.

Piroe has known where the back of the net is throughout his time in the second tier, spending two full seasons each at Swansea City and Leeds United.

In that time, he notched 73 goals, returning two 19-goal campaigns, while his best return came in his debut Championship season with the Swans where he netted 22 times.

But this term, it’s been the complete opposite, with Piroe scoring just four times in 16 league appearances so far at the London Stadium.

It’s a complete surprise to many avid Championship watchers, particularly when you look at the quality the Hammers’ squad has in the final third on paper.

But the Dutch frontman just hasn’t managed to put any form together, with that a common theme across the striking options that West Ham have at their disposal.

A revitalised Niclas Füllkrug may give the Hammers a decision to make in January.

However, while West Ham are in a worrying slump, there is every chance they could get back on track in the coming weeks and months and charge their way back into the promotion mix.

If they were to return to the top-flight at the first time of asking, then the Hammers are believed to be obligated to pay £8m to Leeds United to make Piroe a West Ham player permanently, a deal that is currently worrying a lot of supporters given he is struggling so much in the second tier.

So, with this in mind, FLW’s West Ham fan pundit on whether he thinks Piroe will come good or not and if he fears about having the 27-year-old on their books next season.

Speaking to Football League World, West Ham fan pundit Ryan Horn admits he holds little faith in Joel Piroe to turn around his current form for the Hammers, but insists that the club ‘shouldn’t worry’ about keeping him permanently as the East London outfit, in his opinion, are unlikely to gain promotion come the end of the campaign.

Ryan told FLW: “If West Ham go up, do I fear we’ll be stuck with Joel Piroe, who isn’t even impressing in the Championship? I do. But also, I shouldn’t worry as I don’t see us going up this season.

“If it goes to the play-offs, I don’t think we will win it, and I don’t know how we get automatics.

“Loads of teams are dropping off then picking up points. It’s a really entertaining promotion fight, and I’m loving the Championship for that reason and watching all those teams, but our strikers are just not performing. Our centre-back has more goals than our strikers, which is mental. Just stick Mavropanos up front at this point.

“I don’t have any faith, which I hate to say, in Joel Piroe, if we go up, because he’s struggling in the Championship. Now I do think, ironically, that he will be the highest scoring striker we have this year, just because of going off of the fact he’s done it (in the Championship) before.

“Scott Parker is still working out his starting XI, which I think he’s getting there a little bit, I mean, we’ve had close to 70 shots in the last three games and scored only two goals, so I think when they start banging in the goals, we’ll be fine.

“But honestly, I don’t know. I don’t think we’ll go up, personally, but if we do go up, I think we only have to pay a few million for him (Piroe), so maybe it’s one where we loan him back to the Championship?

“But football is a very fickle thing and anything could happen at this point.”

West Ham supporters' concerns over Joel Piroe are completely understandable, particularly with the striker struggling to find any consistent goalscoring form. There is a question over whether he would be able to make the step-up to the Premier League if the Hammers secure promotion, but there are also reasons why his current return should not necessarily be taken as a fair reflection of his ability.

One of the biggest factors has been his lack of regular minutes. Piroe has had to compete with Taty Castellanos and Pablo for a place in the starting XI, with Scott Parker yet to fully settle on his preferred centre-forward. Having to fight for a starting spot and regularly come in and out of the side can make it difficult for a striker to build rhythm, confidence and an understanding with the players supplying him.

It is also worth remembering that Piroe is still relatively new to the club and has had to adapt to a different environment, new teammates and most recently, Parker's methods, that are bound to be different to Nuno’s.

Building relationships with the players around him takes time, particularly for a striker who relies on good service and intelligent movement to get into goalscoring positions. He has not necessarily had the consistent run of games needed to fully settle into West Ham's attack.

There is also the issue of the Hammers getting the best out of his natural qualities. Piroe is a striker who tends to thrive when he can find space in the penalty area and finish chances that come his way, rather than being asked to do too much of the build-up work himself. He needs good service, intelligent movement around him and players capable of picking him out in dangerous positions. If West Ham are not creating those opportunities consistently, it will be difficult for him to find the back of the net regularly.

There is certainly a case for giving him more time, though. Piroe has already shown that he can be a prolific Championship goalscorer, winning the division's Golden Boot during Leeds United’s promotion-winning campaign in 2024-25. That record should not be overlooked, and it would be premature to write him off based on a difficult spell in a team that is still trying to find its best attacking formula.

However, Piroe is not the only West Ham forward struggling to deliver consistently, suggesting the problem is not simply down to one player being out of form. Parker needs to find a more effective attacking approach, improve the service into his forwards and establish some consistency in his selections. There may also be scope to explore how Piroe can complement the other attacking players, rather than simply treating him as one of several options competing for the same position.

But right now, the Hammers have more immediate problems to solve, with getting their attack firing a far greater priority than worrying about whether Piroe will be good enough for the top-flight next season.

Ultimately, Piroe still has time to prove his worth at West Ham. A more sustained run in the side, a clearer role under Parker and better service could help him rediscover the goalscoring form that made him such an effective Championship striker in the past.
