Final Demand battles back to win Punchestown thriller

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

In a John Durkan Memorial Punchestown Chase dominated by Willie Mullins-trained runners, Final Demand showed real determination to fend off last year's runner up Fact To File

Final Demand emerged best of the army of returning Willie Mullins stars on show after a thrilling finish to the John Durkan Memorial Punchestown Chase.

With Henry de Bromhead's mare The Big Westerner a non-runner due to unsuitable ground, the champion trainer saddled each of the nine remaining runners, with last year's winner and subsequent Cheltenham Gold Cup hero Gaelic Warrior the 10/11 favourite in the hands of Paul Townend.

Kitzbuhel and Daniel King took the field along for much of the two-and-a-half-mile journey, closely pursued by 8/1 shot Final Demand and Patrick Mullins, with Fact To File and Gaelic Warrior next in line.

With the latter unable to quicken sufficiently to get himself into a challenge position, it was the 2024 winner and last year's runner-up Fact To File who looked most likely to prevail when taking over the lead on the run-in, but Final Demand battled bravely to get back up by a neck.

"It was a fantastic race. Very few of my horses were disappointing, they all ran well enough and I was happy with them," said Mullins.

"What a result, for the Mullins family, not just Closutton. To see Patrick riding a Grade One winner and dare I say it, maybe outriding Harry Cobden from the last home.

"Patrick said he thought he had the race won but he just missed the last and he had to win it a second time. So that just shows how much this horse has improved from last year.

"In against those top-class horses, he was good the whole way around. I was amazed at what turn of foot he showed from the last home. I thought when he made that little error that was enough and Fact To File would go on and win. He got down and ground it out. For a second season novice, that's a huge result."

Bookmakers cut Final Demand's Cheltenham Gold Cup odds to 7/1 from 14/1, while easing Gaelic Warrior to 5/2 from 13/8.

Gaelic Warrior's owner Rich Ricci was not too disappointed with the defeat of his star chaser.

He said: "Paul was happy enough, he obviously needed the run. He said he blew up a couple of times and got boxed in a little bit, but couldn't go fast enough to keep with them.

"He rides like more of a three miler these days, but Paul was very positive and said he wouldn't swap him for anything yet. Let's see how it goes.

"For him it's all about the spring, I think. Last year he won here, lost at Christmas, lost at the Dublin Racing Festival and then won the last two at Cheltenham and Punchestown, so hopefully he can repeat his spring successes."
