Ones to watch: Alessia Russo will undoubtedly be one of Arsenal’s biggest attacking threats when the sides meet again. The England international has developed an impressive record against Lyon, scoring three goals in five WCL appearances against the French side. Only Marie-Antoinette Katoto, with four, has scored more against them in the competition. Russo has also contributed four goals across her last four meetings with Lyon, with three goals and an assist. Having started the campaign relatively slowly, the striker has rediscovered her prolific touch, most notably with a hat-trick against Benfica that saw her become the all-time leading English goalscorer in WCL history.

Caitlin Foord is another Arsenal player who could prove important. The Australian has scored three goals in seven European appearances against Lyon, including a memorable brace during Arsenal’s 5-1 victory in France in October 2022.

For the visitors, Melchie Dumornay remains a major threat. The Haitian international has already scored four times against Arsenal and has been in excellent form this season, registering 11 goals across all competitions. She also scored twice in Lyon’s 2-1 victory at Meadow Park last season.

Katoto is another player Arsenal must keep quiet. Her seven goals in this season’s European campaign make her the competition’s leading scorer after four rounds, while Ada Hegerberg’s experience and record of 69 European goals provide Lyon with another attacking option.

Head-to-head record: As mentioned, this will be the 13th meeting between Arsenal and OL Lyonnes, making it the joint-most-played fixture in WCL history, level with Lyon’s encounters against Wolfsburg. The French giants have historically enjoyed the upper hand, winning eight of the previous 12 meetings, with Arsenal recording three victories and one draw. However, the rivalry has become increasingly competitive in recent seasons, with all three of the Gunners’ wins coming since 2022.

The clubs met three times last season, beginning with a league phase encounter at Meadow Park. Despite Alessia Russo opening the scoring after just seven minutes, a Melchie Dumornay double secured a 2-1 victory for Lyon.

They were subsequently drawn together in the semi-finals, with Arsenal taking a 2-1 advantage from the first leg thanks to Olivia Smith’s winner. However, Lyon responded in France, claiming a 3-1 victory to progress 4-3 on aggregate. Jule Brand scored in both legs, while Wendie Renard also found the net in the return fixture.

Interestingly, Arsenal have won just three of their 17 matches against French opposition, with all three victories coming against Lyon. Meanwhile, the French side’s first-ever defeat on a trip to England came against Arsenal in April, ending an impressive 10-game unbeaten away record.

Arsenal XI (4-3-3): Misa; Fox, Williamson, De Almeida, Hinds; Stanway, Maanum, Cooney-Cross; Baum, Russo, Ford.

Lyon XI (4-2-1-3): Grohs; Brand, Sangaré, Renard, Bacha; Egurrola, Yohannes; Dumornay; Becho, Katoto, Chawinga.

Two European heavyweights meet tonight as Arsenal welcome OL Lyonnes to Meadow Park for a showdown in the Women’s Champions League. With 10 European titles between them, this is a fixture worthy of a final, but there is plenty at stake as the league phase reaches its closing stages.

The sides will play their 13th meeting in the competition, making this the joint-most-played fixture in Women’s Champions League history. While Arsenal have enjoyed some recent success against the French giants, Lyon have won seven of their previous 11 encounters and have proved particularly difficult to overcome on English soil.

Arsenal currently sit fourth in the league phase table, two points behind leaders Lyon, knowing a top-four finish would secure automatic progression to the quarter-finals. However, they face a side in formidable form. Lyon are unbeaten in Europe this season, having scored 20 goals without conceding a single one in their opening four matches.
