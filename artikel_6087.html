United Airlines has stepped in to help the Pride of Pearland Band and Color Guard to travel home from New York to Texas, after the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade was postponed. This came as the parade was postponed for the first time in the parade's 100-year history, due to a severe coastal storm that was forecast to impact New York City.

The new parade has been rescheduled from Thursday, November 26, to Saturday, November 28, with kickoff expected at 8:30 AM EST. These last-minute changes have affected thousands of participants' travel plans, and for the Pearland Band and Color Guard, from Pearland High School in Pearland, Texas, United has stepped in with three dedicated aircraft to transport the members and their chaperones home.

As reported by Click2Houston, the community from Pearland, Texas came together to raise $350,000 that was required to keep the high school's band and Guard in New York due to the postponement of the parade. The former Pearland Mayor, Kevin Cole, led the effort to ensure all students in the group could remain in New York, and Star Alliance carrier United has now stepped in to ensure everyone will get home safely.

Initially, there was a plan to bring all participants home by bus, due to existing flight availability only providing options on Monday or Tuesday. Following support from United, all members will now get to fly home on Saturday evening.

Simple Flying reached out to United for comment on this support to Pearland High School on Saturday morning; however, an airline spokesperson could not immediately be reached.

The three extra flights put on by United are set to depart on Saturday from LaGuardia (LGA) and Newark (EWR). These will arrive back into Houston Intercontinental (IAH) between 9:30 PM and 11:00 PM local time. Each plane will carry approximately 170 travelers back to Texas. Members of the Pearland community have also been invited to gather at Pearland High School to welcome back the group.

With many other groups affected by the postponement of the Thanksgiving Day Parade, this has left many struggling to change travel plans. As reported by the Star Advertiser, this included the 200-member Na Koa Alii Hawaii All-State Marching Band from the state of Hawaii. This sudden change saw the group looking for donations, and they were able to raise $500,000 in a matter of hours.

Hawaiian and Alaska Airlines stepped in on Thursday evening, announcing that they would help support the group and its chaperones' trip home to Hawaii. The group will travel from New York to Seattle (SEA), before connecting onto flights back to Hawaii.

The once-in-a-lifetime opportunity for many to participate in the 100th edition of the parade has made much of the sudden postponement all worth it.
