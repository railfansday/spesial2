Einstein’s Equations Reveal an Unusual New Way Space-Time Can Transfer Energy

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Einstein’s equations suggest that gravity may be capable of a process resembling the one behind powerful solar explosions. In a new theoretical study, physicists show how gravitational field structures can change their connections and transfer energy into matter. They call this process “gravitational reconnection.”

Luca Comisso of Columbia University and Felipe A. Asenjo of Universidad Adolfo Ibáñez in Chile describe the discovery in Physical Review D. Their work reveals a mathematical connection between gravity and the behavior of plasmas, electrically charged gases found in stars and throughout space.

In plasmas, magnetic field lines can become stretched and twisted until they rearrange through a process called “magnetic reconnection.” This releases stored magnetic energy and drives powerful events such as solar flares.

“Magnetic reconnection is when these field lines, in some specific configuration, break and reattach in a different configuration,” Comisso said. “When they break and reconnect, they release energy.” He added, “It takes energy from the magnetic field and gives it to the particles in the plasma, producing explosions, energetic events.”

Gravity operates differently because Einstein’s theory describes space-time as a four-dimensional structure rather than three-dimensional space.

“Because space-time is four-dimensional rather than three-dimensional, gravity involves something like field ‘sheets’ rather than field lines,” explained Comisso. “Adding one dimension to a line gives you a plane. You can think of it as putting many lines together to form a ‘sheet.’”

These sheets are mathematical structures, not physical surfaces. The researchers found that when the conditions preserving their connections break down, the sheets can reconnect, allowing energy to transfer from the gravitational field into matter.

The researchers uncovered this behavior by expressing Einstein’s equations using different mathematical variables, revealing similarities with equations that describe plasmas. Their study builds on earlier research with Maricarmen A. Winkler that identified previously unrecognized constraints on how space-time can evolve.

“Einstein’s equations contain constraints on how space-time can evolve that are not immediately apparent,” said Asenjo. “By revealing these hidden new properties, we can begin to understand the fundamental rules governing how this gravitational reconnection process behaves.”

“Some implications are hidden in the equations, and they are not straightforward to capture just by looking at them,” Comisso said. “You need to rewrite them in a different way, and then you can exploit that behavior.”

One potential application involves merging black holes, where extreme gravity severely distorts space-time. Scientists rely on powerful computer simulations to predict the gravitational waves produced by these collisions, which detectors such as the Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory (LIGO) are designed to measure.

The newly identified constraints could help researchers determine how gravitational fields must behave without calculating every detail of their evolution.

“It helps us predict some patterns of the space-time,” Comisso said. “Even without doing the full calculation, we know that space-time needs to behave in a certain way, that it needs to satisfy those constraints.”

Gravitational reconnection remains a theoretical discovery. Researchers must now investigate whether it occurs in astrophysical environments, how much energy it could transfer, and whether it might produce detectable signatures in gravitational waves.

Reference: “Reconnection of gravitational fields” by Luca Comisso and Felipe A. Asenjo, 28 October 2026, Physical Review D. DOI: 10.1103/lmqy-pf9k
