Vision's Entire Family Tree Across All Marvel Lore

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Without a doubt, the Vision has one of the most complex and strange family trees in all of Marvel Comics lore. Not only does the synthezoid have one family born of magic and one of science, but he also has an impressively unique array of "siblings" as well.

Going beyond the normal rules and limitations of typical organic biology, the Vision has a father who's an evil genocidal robot, a grandfather who accidentally created that robot, two wives with very different origins and backgrounds, and so many more siblings, children, and more "family" members (artificial, magical, and otherwise).

In honor of the MCU's recently concluded VisionQuest series, here is Vision's entire family tree in the comics explained, and how each member relates to the heroic synthezoid.

It all starts with Hank Pym, aka Ant-Man, aka Giant Man, aka Yellowjacket. In the comics, Pym created the artificial intelligence known as Ultron (not Tony Stark and Bruce Banner like in the MCU). Ultron was formed from Hank Pym's own brain patterns. However, when Ultron went rogue and became a genocidal robot hell-bent on humanity's extinction, one of his most classic schemes involved Vision's creation. As a result, Hank Pym has long been considered Vision's "grandfather" in terms of the lineage in Marvel Comics.

Janet van Dyne, The Wasp, is connected to Vision via her marriage to Hank Pym, making her Vision's grandmother of sorts. This is largely due to Ultron viewing Janet as his own mother. However, that does make Ultron's creation of his robotic wife Jocasta even stranger, seeing how he used Janet's brain patterns to create his artificial soulmate (though it does help explain Jocasta's name).

Ultron is Vision's creator and the closest thing he has to a father. After rebelling against Hank Pym, Ultron chose to build Vision, a powerful new weapon to use against the Avengers, as seen in 1968's Avengers #57. However, just as Ultron rebelled against his father, Vision also rebelled and joined the Avengers against Ultron, eventually joining Earth's Mightiest Heroes long-term along with other superhero teams over the years.​​​​​​​

Dr. Phineas Horton is another important figure as it relates to Vision's origins and creation, despite not technically being the person who created him. Instead, Horton was the scientist responsible for building the original Synthetic Man, otherwise known as the original Human Torch or Jim Hammond. It was because of Horton's early work that Vision's construction was possible. Likewise, Ultron used spare parts and the template used to create Jim when creating Vision.

Technically, Jim Hammond's Human Torch is the closest thing Vision has to a physical twin or brother. Originally, it was believed that Ultron repurposed Jim's dormant android body to create Vision, which would have made Vision a reincarnation of sorts. However, it was later retconned that Ultron merely used the body template and spare parts from Horon to create Vision, making the synthezoid and his predecessor separate beings who eventually met one another and fought side by side on the West Coast Avengers for a time.​​​​​​​

When creating Vision, Ultron used the brain patterns of Simon Williams, aka Wonder Man, meaning much of Vision's personality and emotions were inspired by Williams, making the two of them brothers of sorts (and have often referred to each other as such). However, their relationship has at times been complicated, seeing as how Simon also developed the same feelings for Wanda Maximoff's Scarlet Witch, who would become Vision's first wife.​​​​​​​

Victor Mancha is another artificial child of Ultron, which technically makes him Vision's younger brother. Having debuted in the beloved Runaways series, Victor is part human and part machine as a unique cyborg. Like Vision, he rejected Ultron's dark plans for him and became a hero instead, and was largely inspired by Vision himself. However, things got messy after Victor accidentally killed Vision's son, Vin, which in turn led to Vision's second wife, Virginia, murdering Victor (though Victor was eventually brought back to life).​​​​​​​

Like Vision, Jocasta was created by Ultron, which, from one perspective, would make her his sister. However, she was also designed to be Ultron's robotic bride, meaning she can also be considered Vision's stepmother. To make things even more complicated, Jocasta was based on the brain patterns of Janet van Dyne (Vision's "grandmother). However, Marvel fans choose to view her familial connection to Vision, she too rejected Ultron and became a hero just like her brother/stepson/grandson.
