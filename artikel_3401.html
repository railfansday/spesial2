‘The Gilded Age’ Officially Gets Massive Expansion at HBO

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Season 4 of The Gilded Age has now debuted on HBO, with all the social striving, romantic yearning, and vintage frocks that its fans have come to expect. However, now that HBO's parent company, Warner Bros., has become part of a massive merged conglomerate with Skydance and Paramount, will the series' large and lavish budget make this season the end of an era? The decision has been made, and fans will probably be excited.

The Gilded Age had a somewhat difficult conception. The series is the brainchild of Julian Fellowes, the creator of Downton Abbey; in fact, it was originally conceived of as an America-set prequel to that series, depicting the clash between the New World's old money and new money, and the latter's intermarriage with the aristocracy to gain prestige. It was also originally in development with NBC; however, the scope of the series soon grew to a point where it would not be sustainable on network TV. Instead, it moved to HBO, and the arrangement has proved to be a fruitful one.

In fact, it's so fruitful that HBO has renewed The Gilded Age, a mere three episodes into its fourth season. The series will now return for a fifth season next year, thanks to some excellent ratings. According to HBO's own numbers, the series is now averaging 5.9 million US cross-platform viewers per episode, a number that's still growing. That's enough to outpace last season by nearly 20%, and making this the show's highest-rated season yet.

Four love stories, four completely different ideas of what it means to be somebody's person. This is the one that matches how you actually fall, and what it would ask of you.

You do not fall for strangers. You fall for the person you have already been talking to for years, which is the slowest and by some distance the most durable way anybody has found to do this.

You want a partner, not an audience. Somebody who wants their own life as badly as you want yours, and who would never ask you to be smaller to make the relationship easier.

The intensity was never the problem. The secrecy is. You are drawn to a love that lives entirely in private, and to the particular ache of wanting somebody you are not supposed to want.

You do not do this cautiously. When it happens for you it is immediate and total and it rearranges everything, and you would not accept a smaller version of it.

The marriage of robber baron George Russell (Morgan Spector) and his social-climbing wife Bertha (Carrie Coon) is still fractured, but there's some new joy in their lives, as after a difficult delivery, their daughter, Gladys (Taissa Farmiga), gave birth to a healthy child. Bertha's now in a cold war with Agnes van Rhijn (Christine Baranski) over hosting US President Grover Cleveland; more consequentially, Agnes' niece Marian Brook (Louisa Jacobson) had a night of passion with Bertha and George's son, Larry (Harry Richardson), that seems to have derailed their on-and-off romance. Meanwhile, Marian's friend Peggy (Denée Benton) has married the man of her dreams, affluent doctor Dr. William Kirkland (Jordan Donica), despite some friction between their families.

The Gilded Age has been renewed for a fifth season; season 4 is currently airing on HBO. Stay tuned to Collider for future updates.
