Two Tiny Fixes That Can Save Your Putting Strokes

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Most golfers think they need a miracle swing change to stop three-putting. A recent lesson with PGA Tour putting coach Stephen Sweeney proves that fixing the setup fixes the stroke. The writer of a Yahoo Sports report struggled with putting this year until Sweeney spotted two small errors that were costing strokes.

The first mistake happens right before the putter moves. The writer tended to keep eyes inside the ball, closer to the feet instead of directly over it. This tiny tilt changes the whole path of the club. Sweeney noted that when eyes sit inside the line, players tend to aim out to the right. "Where your eyes are right now, I tend to see if players' eyes are inside the ball line; they tend to look out to the right," Sweeney said.

This setup forces the putter to move from inside to out. That path might work for a full swing, but it kills short putts. The fix is simple but feels weird at first. The coach told the writer to hinge at the waist more and get into a "tabletop" position. A good rule of thumb is to keep two putter heads of space from the ball to the toe line. Then bend forward until the eyes sit right over the golf ball.

The second error is a right-aligned putter face at address. Because the eyes were looking from the inside, the writer subconsciously aimed the face right and tried to pull the ball back to the line. Once the eyes moved on top of the ball, lining up the face became much easier. Sweeney showed a clever trick using a phone to check the aim. "Just go ahead and set up and aim your putter face," Sweeney said. "If I go ahead and set the back of my phone to your putter face, and you come back and look down that line, you can see the face aimed on the right edge."

The writer felt like a new player on the greens after just these two adjustments. Confidence returned, and more putts dropped. Sweeney explained the mechanics clearly: "As the putter goes back, the putter is moving a little on the inside — but pretty square to where you aimed it."
