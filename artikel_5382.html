U.S. crude and gasoline inventories rose far more than expected, sending oil lower

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Crude stocks rose 3.4 million barrels and gasoline stocks 5.2 million in the week ended Nov. 13, the EIA said

U.S. commercial crude oil stockpiles grew by 3.4 million barrels, reaching 417.8 million barrels for the week ended Nov. 13, the Energy Information Administration reported on Wednesday. Gasoline stocks gained 5.2 million barrels and now total 215.9 million barrels, the EIA said.

Both builds exceeded forecasts. Analysts surveyed by The Wall Street Journal projected a 1 million-barrel crude build and a 1.4 million-barrel gasoline build. A Reuters poll anticipated a 58,000-barrel crude increase and a 1.6 million-barrel gasoline increase.

Oil futures kept sliding once the figures were released. Brent crude futures changed hands at $91.85 per barrel, a decline of $1.29, as of 10:38 a.m. ET. West Texas Intermediate futures fell $1.81 to $84.20 a barrel.

The crude increase was the second consecutive weekly build, according to The Wall Street Journal. Crude stocks were running 3% under their five-year seasonal norm, the EIA said. Gasoline inventories were 3% above that average.

The EIA said stocks of distillates, a category covering diesel and heating oil, grew by 1.6 million barrels to reach 104.9 million barrels. That left them 7% below the five-year average. The Wall Street Journal's survey called for a 500,000-barrel increase. Reuters' poll called for 1.3 million barrels.

The EIA said inventories at the Cushing, Oklahoma, delivery hub dropped 1.2 million barrels, leaving 26.1 million barrels in storage. Oil in the Strategic Petroleum Reserve was drawn down by 3.2 million barrels, leaving the stockpile at 276.4 million barrels.

U.S. crude production averaged 14 million barrels daily, an increase of 19,000 barrels daily over the week before, the EIA said. Crude imports dropped 1.1 million barrels daily, settling at 5.6 million barrels daily. Exports climbed 1 million barrels daily, reaching 4.5 million barrels daily.

Refineries operated at 95.7% capacity, up from 95.2% a week earlier, the EIA said. Crude input to refineries slipped by 129,000 barrels daily, landing at 16.9 million barrels daily. Gasoline demand totaled 8.8 million barrels daily, an increase of 23,000 barrels daily.
