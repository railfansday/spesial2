Resistance driven by ESR1 mutations continues to be a challenge for metastatic breast cancer patients, and ER degradation is an important therapeutic strategy for addressing these mutations.

Oral SERDs degrade estrogen receptors by binding them and causing a confirmational change, which indirectly results in degradation of estrogen receptors. In a preclinical study, the scale of ER degradation was 8% to 68%. PROteolysis Targeting Chimera, or PROTAC, is not a SERD and is a very different mechanism to eliminate estrogen receptors. PROTAC is heterobifunctional, binding to the estrogen receptor on one end and the E3 ligase on the other, bringing them close together to initialize polyubiquitination, directly degrading the receptor through the proteasome. In addition, the PROTAC can then release and degrade multiple estrogen receptors until it's metabolized. This ability to recruit the E3 ligase, remove the estrogen receptor and repeat the process differentiates PROTAC from SERD.

In a preclinical study, the PROTAC VEPPANU degraded 79% to 86% of estrogen receptors. This is why we believe VEPPANU represents an important therapeutic advance for ER-positive HER2-negative ESR1 mutated metastatic breast cancer. On Slide 23, you can see the current treatment landscape for this indication.

MDS is a clonal disorder of hematopoietic stem cells that leads to ineffective hematopoiesis with consequences that include anemia, infections, iron overload and organ dysfunction and possible progression to acute myeloid leukemia. The primary goal of therapy is to reduce or eliminate transfusion dependence. In the first-line setting, patients typically receive erythropoiesis stimulating agents, or ESAs, or luspatercept. If the patient has a 5q deletion, lenalidomide is eligible. After ESA failure or for ESA ineligible patients, luspatercept and imetelstat are approved. In the second-line setting, these treatments each have a response rate of approximately 38% to 40%, respectively. For more aggressive disease, hypomethylating agents can be used with response rates of approximately 18% to 20%.

There remains a clear need for new treatments for previously treated transfusion-dependent patients who are relapsed or refractory to or ineligible for ESAs. If the data for R289 plays out and it earns FDA approval, we see the potential for R289 to be an important therapy option for these patients. On Slide 24, we summarize the value proposition for R289 in lower-risk MDS. There are approximately 12,200 previously treated lower-risk MDS patients in the United States and effective therapies for transfusion-dependent patients in this setting are limited. As I mentioned, R289 offers dual targeting against IRAK1 and 4.

Since dysregulated inflammatory signaling in the bone marrow is associated with MDS, co-targeting of IRAK1 and 4 may suppress inflammation and LSPC function, restoring normal blood cell production. R289 blocks toll-like receptors and IL-1 receptor signaling in vitro and has been active in preclinical models of inflammation. We established clinical proof of concept in a randomized controlled trial in healthy volunteers where R835, the active moiety of R289 markedly suppressed LPS-induced cytokine release. The FDA has granted R289 Fast Track designation for previously treated transfusion-dependent lower-risk MDS and orphan drug designation for MDS.
