Why Firefly Aerospace Stock Dropped on Friday

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Investors are taking a second look at Firefly's groundbreaking AST SpaceMobile deal.

By Rich Smith – Nov 20, 2026 at 1:05PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Firefly Aerospace ›

Firefly Aerospace (FLY -9.66%) stock tumbled 9.6% through 12:50 p.m. ET Friday after investment banker JPMorgan cut its price target on the space stock by 20% to $32 per share.

That's the bad news. The good news is that there doesn't appear to be any specific catalyst behind JP's price target reduction. The write-up on StreetInsider.com contains no worrisome details that would support the idea that Firefly stock is worth 20% less today than it cost yesterday -- and JP still believes the stock should be "overweight" in investor portfolios.

Of course, the other bad news is that, even without a full explanation from JPMorgan, investors may reasonably question whether Firefly is worth the $32 the analyst says it is -- or even the $18 per share Firefly stock costs today!

Recall that part of the reason Firefly stock is so expensive right now is that two weeks ago, Firefly inked a deal to sell 36 rocket engines to AST SpaceMobile (ASTS -8.69%) and help AST learn how to build rocketships. In exchange, AST would pay Firefly $1.3 billion.

As I've already pointed out, however, this deal isn't all it's cracked up to be.

For one thing, only the first $400 million of this $1.3 billion appears to be guaranteed to be paid in cash. At least $400 million more will be paid in AST stock -- stock that is already down 28% since this deal was announced.

The form of payment for an additional $500 million, due upon delivery of engines and provision of engineering services, is unclear. It's probably supposed to be paid in cash, but the companies didn't state this outright in their press release.

If any more of this is payable in rapidly devaluing AST stock, this deal could be worth far less to Firefly than investors expect.
