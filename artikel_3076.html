Agility Robotics Opened at $11.26 and Dropped to $6. Here’s the Price Where I’d Buy the First Humanoid Robot Stock.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The SPAC-backed robot maker flopped after its market debut.

10 stocks we like better than Agility Robotics ›

Agility Robotics (AGLT -2.65%), a developer of humanoid robots for warehouses, logistics hubs, and manufacturing plants, went public through a merger with a special purpose acquisition company (SPAC) on Nov. 5. Its stock opened at $11.26, but it now trades at about $6.60. Let's see why Agility fizzled out -- and what price would make it a compelling buy.

Agility's main product is Digit, a bipedal humanoid robot that can move heavy items while navigating aisles, ramps, and stairs on its own. Its top investors include Nvidia (NVDA -2.79%) and Amazon (AMZN +3.56%).

Agility's Digit v5 robot. Image source: Agility Robotics.

Agility has already signed multi-year contracts with Toyota (TM -1.17%), GXO Logistics (GXO +1.24%), MercadoLibre (MELI -0.84%), and Schaeffler, and its backlog has swelled to more than $300 million in committed orders for its newest robot, the Digit v5.

Agility's backlog gives it plenty of room to grow, but it only generated $1.78 million in revenue in 2025 and $360,861 in revenue in the first nine months of 2026. It posted a net loss of $138.1 million in 2025 and an even wider net loss of $458.7 million in the first nine months of 2026.

Agility has only shipped a handful of its Cassie research robots to research institutions, and earlier versions of the Digit to a few commercial and industrial customers. For the full year, analysts expect it to generate only $846,200 in revenue with a net loss of $583.7 million.

That red ink made it tough to invest in Agility's market debut, especially as elevated interest rates and other macro headwinds drove investors away from riskier growth stocks. However, Agility claims it can deploy its first Digit v5 robots by the end of 2026, and scale its annual deployments to 800 units in 2027, 7,000 units in 2030, and 25,000 units in 2035. Its customers will need to pay recurring "robot as a service" (RaaS) subscription fees to keep them running.

Agility's $300 million backlog includes orders for 1,000 robots with bundled RaaS fees. If it successfully fulfills those orders, analysts expect its revenue to rise to $37.8 million in 2027 and $108.9 million in 2028. But with a market cap of $1.74 billion, it already trades at 16 times its 2028 revenue -- and it's expected to remain unprofitable for the foreseeable future.

Another major problem is Tesla's (TSLA +1.01%) Optimus. Tesla plans to produce a million Optimus robots in 2027 and start selling them to external customers by the end of the year. That's an ambitious goal, but Tesla has the manufacturing capacity, supply chain, experience, and AI ecosystem to support that expansion. Agility lacks those core strengths.

Tesla is also firmly profitable, so it can easily subsidize its robot production with its higher-margin EVs. Tesla plans to sell the Optimus at $20,000-$30,000 after it ramps up its commercial production, while the Digit v5 will initially cost $150,000.

Agility's stock still looks expensive after its post-merger plunge. I believe it should trade at a discount to Tesla -- which trades at 10 times its 2028 sales -- since it still has a lot to prove.

Agility's stock price would need to drop nearly 40% to about $4 to match Tesla's valuation. Therefore, I wouldn't touch it unless it gets cut in half -- or more -- from its current price. But after that plunge, it could look like a compelling investment if you believe it can carve out its own niche in the nascent market for humanoid robot workers. If its market cap drops below $1 billion, it could also become a compelling takeover target for Tesla or Amazon.
