TSMC gains $158 billion in value in one day as Jensen Huang returns as Nvidia CEO

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Asian stocks rallied on Tuesday after the board of Nvidia—the chipmaker at the center of the AI boom—voted Monday to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, ending a leadership crisis that had battered the region’s AI supply chain. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. (TSMC), which makes Nvidia’s chips, surged 9.9%, the daily limit, adding 5.2 trillion New Taiwan dollars ($158 billion) to its market value in a single day.

Japan’s benchmark Nikkei 225 rose 3.9% as the market returned from a three-day weekend, while South Korea’s KOSPI surged 6.2% and Taiwan’s TAIEX climbed 9%.

Tuesday’s rally marks a rebound after a brutal stretch for the AI trade. The TAIEX fell by 17.5% last week, its worst such drop since 1991, pushing regulators to impose market stabilization measures, such as banning short selling, over the weekend.

Foxconn and Wistron, which assemble AI servers around Nvidia’s chips, rose almost 10%. SK Hynix, the world’s leading manufacturer of high-bandwidth memory, climbed 6.7%, while Samsung Electronics rose 6.6%. In Japan, Kioxia, Toshiba’s former memory chip unit, surged 10.0%, and Advantest, which makes chip-testing equipment, jumped 12.5%.

“This looks like a powerful relief rally, amplified by short covering and the sharp selloff that preceded it,” Charu Chanana, chief investment strategist at Saxo Bank, says. “The initial rebound could extend over the next few trading sessions, particularly in semiconductor-heavy markets such as Taiwan and South Korea, where earnings visibility has improved following [Jensen] Huang’s return.”

Yet the relief from Huang’s return will likely peter out as broader concerns about the AI ecosystem persist. “I would expect markets to become more selective over the coming weeks, as investors shift their focus back from Nvidia’s leadership to AI spending, earnings delivery and the Fed’s policy outlook,” Chanana adds.

Not every Asian market is having a good day. Hong Kong’s Hang Seng Index fell by 0.6%. “The Hang Seng and mainland Chinese markets have struggled to find support,” says Chris Weston, head of research at Pepperstone, a currency and derivatives broker. “Without a meaningful rotation back into Chinese AI and technology stocks, sustained upside looks challenging.”

Huang’s return comes less than two weeks after his resignation. He stepped down on Nov. 12 after reports of multiple affairs, including one with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive who some U.S. intelligence officials believed was working with Chinese espionage, according to the New York Times.

Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud computing company, is at the center of allegations that Nvidia chips were diverted to China. The company has denied the allegations.

Last week, the Commerce Department expanded export controls on AI processors to Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia and Brazil; it also closed a loophole that allowed Chinese companies to access Nvidia chips housed overseas.

On Monday, the department added seven companies to its Entity List, including Megaspeed. In a Federal Register notice, the department said the companies posed a “significant risk” and acted contrary to the “national security or foreign policy interests” of the U.S. The added companies include firms in Thailand, Malaysia, and Singapore, including several Megaspeed affiliates.

The same day, Nvidia’s board voted unanimously to reinstate Huang. “In the process of reaching its decision, the Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk,” the company said in a statement.
