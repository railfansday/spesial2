ETF Investors Are Rethinking What “Diversified” Really Means

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Here's how to shift your focus to what really counts.

By Dana George – Nov 22, 2026 at 12:30PM EST

10 stocks we like better than iShares Core S&P 500 ETF ›

Exchange-traded funds (ETFs) have been around since 1993 and have long been treated as a quick way to diversify. You buy one fund and immediately own a basket of investments.

While that diversification remains a positive feature of ETFs, today's investors are increasingly asking about more than how many holdings a fund has. They're asking what those holdings are and how much an ETF's holdings overlap with the rest of their portfolio.

Let's say you invest in the iShares Core S&P 500 ETF (IVV -3.52%) for access to hundreds of large U.S. companies. For example, IVV's top holdings included giants such as Apple (AAPL -1.90%), Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%), Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%), Amazon (AMZN -4.10%), and Alphabet (GOOG -2.59%) (GOOGL -2.70%). You can't get much bigger than these companies, and IVV's largest positions give you the sense that you're betting on winners.

With its low expense ratio, broad market access, transparency, and flexible trading, you can't find anything you don't like about IVV. In fact, you're so happy with the fund that you decide to invest in another ETF. While it's easy to believe that another fund will help you create a more diversified portfolio, that may not be the case.

For example, what if you added the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -3.42%) to your portfolio alongside IVV? You'd add some new holdings, but you'd also end up with a lot of overlap. That's because IVV and VOO both track the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) index and are heavily weighted toward Apple, Nvidia, Microsoft, Amazon, and Alphabet.

Instead of branching out and diversifying, you end up owning more shares of the same companies. The Securities and Exchange Commission advises anyone who holds several ETFs to check the top holdings of the funds to make sure they're different and provide the diversification you seek.

A truly diversified portfolio spreads risk across investments that don't all respond to market events at the same time or in the same way. One way to achieve that is to include different regions, company sizes, sectors, and asset types.

When investing in more than one ETF, look under the hood of each. Find out about underlying holdings, the percentage of each fund invested in the same companies, and how much of your total portfolio those shared holdings represent.

While you're there, compare sectors, countries, and investment styles, since funds that hold different stocks can still be concentrated in similar areas.

One of the easiest ways to compare funds is to use the ETF Research Center's fund overlap calculator. The calculator shows not only how many holdings overlap, but also the overlap by weight. This is vital information when you're aiming for a truly diversified portfolio.
