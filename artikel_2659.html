Christian McCaffrey and his ‘psychotic tendencies’ quietly becoming underrated

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Christian McCaffrey is reaching the point in his career where he defies conventional thinking, over and over again.

The San Francisco 49ers star turned 30 last June, making him the running back equivalent of an AARP member. He's playing in an NFL that prioritizes ball carriers and receivers at significantly younger ages, yet McCaffrey is continuing to prove he's a rare player.

McCaffrey enters Week 11 ranked 11th in the league in scrimmage yards with 1,012, and he's the only player older than 28 within the top 11. Additionally, McCaffrey and Baltimore Ravens running back Derrick Henry are the only over-30 players in the top 40.

McCaffrey is also quietly third in the NFL in touchdowns scored with 14, behind 24-year-old Jahmyr Gibbs (18) and 26-year-old Javonte Williams (15).

Christian McCaffrey of the San Francisco 49ers carries the ball against the Minnesota Vikings in Mexico City, Mexico, Nov. 22, 2026. Getty Images

And McCaffrey has somehow become underrated when discussing the 49ers' 9-1 start to the season that's made them the odds-on favorite to land the No. 1 seed in the NFC.

"He takes care of himself and prepares like no other," San Francisco coach Kyle Shanahan said this week ahead of Sunday's game against the Seattle Seahawks.

"That's why we call him a psycho, because it's not normal."

The discussion surrounding the 49ers these days is more often focused on Brock Purdy's MVP candidacy or how future Hall of Fame receiver Mike Evans has given the offense a new dimension. Evans has proved vital in San Francisco's two biggest wins of the season against the league's best defenses in the Seahawks Oct. 11, and last week against the Minnesota Vikings.

But McCaffrey's impact has remained constant to the way the 49ers run their offense. The consistency has fallen to the background because he's no longer the shiny new weapon Evans has become. He's still often used to pull defenders towards him in passing plays and create space for his teammates in the middle of the field.

What's new for the offense this season is McCaffrey's improved efficiency. He's averaging 4.5 yards per carry, up from 3.9 in 2025, his lowest clip since 2020. It's a big reason San Francisco's running game ranks second in the NFL in success rate after being uncharacteristically average, ranking 16th last season.

Christian McCaffrey of the San Francisco 49ers warms up before a game at Levi's Stadium in Santa Clara, Calif., Sunday, Nov. 8, 2026. Getty Images

Part of the improved efficiency stems from a decreased workload. McCaffrey this season is getting 16.9 touches per game – down from 25.8 in 2025 – a 34% difference.

The 49ers in the offseason made it a priority to make things easier on their aging running back. They were heavily criticized for drafting Indiana running back Kaelon Black in the third round this spring after he was projected to go much later. Black wasn't even invited to the NFL scouting combine and was widely considered a Day 3 pick by draft analysts.

But Shanahan said he thought Black was the second-best running back in the entire class. Black has since become the 49ers' best backup they've had to complement McCaffrey since acquiring McCaffrey midway through 2022. Black's also made Shanahan look prescient. He ranks second among all rookies with 364 rushing yards.

San Francisco 49ers running back Christian McCaffrey (23) celebrates a touchdown against the Dallas Cowboys in Arlington, Texas, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

There were legitimate reasons to have concern when it came to McCaffrey before the start of the season. He led the NFL in touches last year with 413, and added 37 in two playoff games.

McCaffrey led the league in touches twice before, but things didn't go well the following seasons in both instances. He appeared in just three games in 2020 due to ankle, shoulder and thigh injuries after a 403-touch, 2,392-yard and 19-touchdown season with Carolina in 2019.

McCaffrey in 2023 had another 2,000-yard season and won Offensive Player of the Year while helping San Francisco reach the Super Bowl. But he entered the following season with bilateral Achilles tendinitis that caused him to miss the first eight games before sitting the final five to have surgery to repair a torn PCL in his right knee. The 49ers went 6-11.

This year, McCaffrey has played in all 10 games, and has acknowledged adding Black to the offense has been beneficial.

"I always tell him that's what I'm trying to do," Shanahan said. "Christian's very smart, so he understands what's good about that. He's also a dog and a competitor, and tells me I should never have to worry about anybody else because he can handle everything.

"Which, he's not wrong. But he also knows the benefits of it too. I don't know if he always likes it, but it's not something he's complaining about because he knows it can help him. He also respects Kaelon. When someone goes in there and runs like he does, I think that makes him feel better too."

Black had double-digit carries in three straight games before getting just six in the win over Minnesota. He might have gotten more if not for third-string running back Jordan James getting inserted in fourth-quarter garbage time.

Shanahan has often called McCaffrey a "psycho" for his maniacal approach to the game. His offseason workout regimens are constantly changing with the times, while the extreme difficulty remains the norm. His approach has helped set the tone even in the 49ers' veteran-heavy locker room.

San Francisco 49ers Christian McCaffrey #23 is congratulated by George Kittle #85 after a touchdown in Mexico City, Mexico, Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. Getty Images

"Everything he does, he's preparing to play, whether it's how he sleeps, how he walks down the hallway, everything," Shanahan said. "So it's amazing in that way. … With all our guys who have gotten older, who are Hall of Fame players, that some guys are like that because they're just so talented and different than everybody. You can't be a Hall of Fame player if you aren't like that.

"But our group of guys, they're also really special in just their awareness and how they play the game. From both of our wideouts, to our tight end, to our linebacker, to our rusher. They're very smart and the game comes very natural to them. Even if you aren't exactly the same as you were at 23, they still have a lot of other special qualities about them."

McCaffrey's locker room is near tight end George Kittle's and the two have grown close since becoming teammates. It's led to them collaborating on ideas to keep their bodies in shape during the offseason – like taking trips to Germany to try things they aren't willing to discuss on the record (Kittle has been known for doing stem cell treatments in Panama during the offseason).

"When you sit down there and bounce ideas off each other, because you're both a little out there in the head, respectfully, you just get new ideas and find things you've never heard of," Kittle said before declining to offer specifics of their Germany trip.

George Kittle, left, and Christian McCaffrey of the San Francisco 49ers celebrate a touchdown on Sunday, Nov. 2, 2025, in East Rutherford, N.J. Bill Kostroun/New York Post

Shanahan made it clear his use of the word "psycho" when describing McCaffrey was meant to be endearing and a compliment.

"I've never seen it that way," McCaffrey said about his approach being called psychotic. "I think it's a compliment. I understand that, so I'm appreciative of it. But I just love football, man. I love it so much. I feel very blessed and fortunate to be able to do it. And I want to respect the game and respect how you do it.

"And if that means you have psychotic tendencies, then that's fine."
