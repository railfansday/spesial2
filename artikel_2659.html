Rams Winners & Losers: LA beats Packers to bolster playoff odds

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

It's no secret that Wednesday's pre-Thanksgiving matchup between the Los Angeles Rams and Green Bay Packers carried immense weight for both teams' playoff chances.

The Rams took care of business behind Blake Corum's big rushing day on his birthday. They dealt a near-death blow to Green Bay's postseason odds.

The schedule to get to the playoffs is still long and littered with stiff competition. That is a tomorrow problem. For today, let's take inventory of all the individual contributions that led the Rams to their most important win on the season yet.

Corum made it seven-straight games with a touchdown. He has officially taken the starting running back job from Kyren Williams and continues to outshine his counterpart in terms of production. The third-year back rushed 32 times (!) for 184 yards (5.8 average) and a touchdown. He also added two catches for 21 yards through the air.

The Rams have needed an offensive spark nearly all season. It's clear that Corum has been the answer and is someone the team must rely on moving forward in order to make due on their sky-high expectations.

Jordan Love is not a quarterback that gets sacked often, even if his pass protection is often porous these days. Donald and Garrett were key to generating pressure. Love was only brought down a single time by Byron Young. The pressure helped force Love into some very curious decisions—of which resulted in key takeaways by the defense.

Donald and Garrett are as advertised, even if they are hitting their stride later into the season than originally expected.

You don't rush for 241 yards without a strong performance from the big guys up front. The Rams got the job done even with injuries that caused linemen to play out of position. Warren McClendon, Bill Murray, Tyler Higbee, and Davis Allen all dealt with injuries at some point. Rookie Keagen Trost, Justin Dedich, and David Quessenberry all played parts in relief.

The perseverance from this group in an all-important game was impressive.

With no Puka Nacua for the rest of the season and Davante Adams' return to health timeline currently uncertain, the only pass catcher who seems interested in stepping up is second-year receiver Konata Mumpfield. He added four receptions for 69 yards on Wednesday night. He also snagged a ball at the goal line that was originally called a touchdown and later reversed to incomplete. It was probably at least a completion even if the touchdown call was borderline and the ball short of the end zone.

Mumpfield's step forward is an encouraging sign for the offense. Their passing game is not where it needs to be. Mumpfield's production with Adams back in the fold should help matters.

Wesley Bailey and Josaiah Stewart are still playing sizeable rotational roles on defense. Last week Bailey snagged an interception. Against the Packers, Stewart made multiple key plays in coverage to prevent big gains. You won't see Garrett in coverage often—the Rams want him moving towards the quarterback. Someone has to do the dirty work and play the linebacker role. Stewart did an excellent job in this facet versus Green Bay.

With the Rams up one score late into the third quarter, Matthew Stafford threw an inexplicable first-down interception right to a Packers defender. It was a chance for Green Bay and Love to regain momentum, although Love made an even worse throw for a turnover just two plays later. The Rams stubbed their toe. The Packers let them off the hook.

It's still an open question whether the Rams have enough juice in the passing game to keep up with the likes of the Chiefs, 49ers, Cowboys, and Seahawks—all teams they will see in the coming weeks.

Stafford must be better both in terms of production and in his decision making. It's tough to win games when your leading rusher has more yards than your quarterback (184 versus 140).

But the passing game struggles do not rest entirely on Stafford's shoulders. The receiving corps aside from Mumpfield is not inspiring confidence. The injuries at the tight end position from this game spell concern moving forward. Sean McVay's play calling leaned more towards "don't lose" then "go for the win". LA was reliant on screen passes that often went for minimal gain and there were a few close laterals that could have resulted in disaster.

Something needs to change. Do the Rams have the right players in order to turn things around, or will the passing attack be the reason they fall short of Super Bowl expectations?
