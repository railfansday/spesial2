AI Token Prices Are Tumbling. Is It a Red Flag for Anthropic Stock?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Token prices are falling just as Anthropic goes public. Is it a warning sign for the IPO stock?

By Jeremy Bowman – Nov 20, 2026 at 12:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

Anthropic (ANT +17.07%) just finished its IPO day with the AI lab jumping 17% in its blockbuster debut.

Anthropic's debut exposes stock market investors to AI labs for the first time, and Anthropic may have a broader range of potential outcomes than any stock before it. The company has identified a market opportunity valued at $40 trillion, and its technology is so powerful that CEO Dario Amodei thinks it could cure diseases like cancer and Alzheimer's within a decade. In contrast, a researcher for the company said there's more than a 10% chance that it "could kill all humans."

Likewise, Anthropic bulls, including Goldman Sachs, projected $440 billion in revenue by 2029, and Morningstar's bull case put its share price at $188.

Anthropic bears, on the other hand, have argued that the company doesn't have a sustainable competitive advantage and faces too much competition from cheaper models and the threat of distillation.

Among the top critiques is that token prices are falling sharply, as Bloomberg's chief economist argues below.

In today’s piece @TheTerminal, I write about why Anthropic IPO may mark the end of the AI trade. https://t.co/BhCWhrbIbM Two charts summarized it the argument: 1) Its effective price per million token has come down sharply of late (its s-1 cutoff date gives financial statements up to 9/30) 2) yet its token usage share didn’t pick up, and even edged down That reveals the kind of market structure that it operates in. It seems to be an oligopolist who does not have durable pricing power. Growth has to rely on price cuts to drive volume. That is a tough business.

The usage-weighted Silicon Data LLM Token Expenditure Index, which tracks prices per million tokens, fell more than 40% between early June 30 and early October, and prices have continued to fall since then.

Anthropic has even cut its prices by 20% on its newest frontier model, Fable 5.2.

Token prices are falling for a number of reasons. First, competition from cheaper models is pushing prices across the industry lower. Second, customers are shifting their consumption habits and using cheaper models for more routine tasks, and third, costs to generate tokens are falling due to more efficient computing usage, allowing Anthropic to lower prices without necessarily losing profit.

The consequences of lower token prices aren't fully clear. Superficially, lower prices would seem to be bad, as it would lower Anthropic's revenue, but lower prices can also drive increased usage, a relationship known as the Jevons paradox, which is a benefit over the long term.

Anthropic's strong growth through the third quarter indicates that the company can grow even while token prices are falling, as it reported 47% sequential growth in the third quarter. Additionally, the company has managed to scale revenue faster than its computing costs, resulting in an adjusted operating profit.

Still, falling token prices could put pressure on Anthropic, especially if rising competition and usage shifts are driving them.

CEO Dario Amodei has called for a crackdown on industrial-scale distillation operations, referring to a method of training AI models that uses existing models, rather than training on data from scratch. Distillation is comparable to counterfeiting in other industries. It lowers prices, but does so at the expense of undercutting the investment and research and development that market leaders have made, disincentivizing it in the long run. Amodei also sees it as a national security threat, as distillation allows China to build much better models than it normally would be able to.

In technology, prices often fall over time due to competition and as the technology evolves and gets more efficient.

Amazon invented cloud computing and remains the market leader, even though the company has cut costs hundreds of times due to economies of scale, competitive pressure, and incentivizing customers to stay on its platform. AWS continues to grow and has become one of the most valuable businesses in the world, even on a stand-alone basis.

Anthropic has a number of similarities to AWS as a leader in a new technology with a massive addressable market ahead of it, and pricing is just one part of the equation.

Demand for artificial intelligence could be nearly limitless, and investors shouldn't lose sight of that. While token prices are worth monitoring, if Anthropic can continue to grow profits, falling token prices aren't the red flag that bears seem to think they are.
