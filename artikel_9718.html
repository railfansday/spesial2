Guardz Integrates Palo Alto Networks Prisma Browser Protection to Address Growing Workspace Security

The browser has become one of the most important tools in the modern workplace. Employees use it to access business applications, communicate with colleagues, manage sensitive information, and increasingly interact with generative AI platforms. As more business activity moves into browser-based environments, the security risks associated with these interactions are becoming harder to manage through traditional endpoint and email protection alone.

A phishing email, for example, may appear to be an isolated threat. But when an employee follows a malicious link, the consequences can extend to compromised credentials, stolen session tokens, unauthorized access to cloud applications, and potentially an exposed endpoint. Understanding the full scope of such an incident requires security teams to connect activity across multiple parts of the workplace.

As shared with this writer through a press release, Guardz is addressing this challenge through a new integration with Palo Alto Networks, bringing browser-level protection to its security platform.

Guardz, a cybersecurity platform built for managed service providers (MSPs), has announced the integration of Prisma Browser for Business from Palo Alto Networks. The integration brings browser protection into Guardz's broader security environment, connecting browser activity with signals from email, identity, endpoints, data, and user risk.

The objective is to give MSPs and security teams a more connected view of threats affecting the organizations they protect, particularly small businesses that may lack dedicated security operations resources.

Through the integration, Prisma Browser for Business can block malicious websites, including fraudulent login pages designed to capture employee credentials. Guardz can then correlate the browser event with the original phishing email, related Microsoft 365 activity, and endpoint telemetry.

Rather than treating each signal as an independent security alert, the platform can bring the associated activity together to support investigation and response as a unified incident.

For Guardz CEO and Co-Founder Dor Eisner, this approach reflects a broader change in how organizations should think about cybersecurity.

“Identity is the new center of gravity in cybersecurity, and the browser has become the primary gateway to the modern workspace. Traditional security tools operate in silos, leaving critical gaps in visibility and response,” Eisner said.

The integration introduces several browser security capabilities into the Guardz environment.

Prisma Browser for Business uses inline machine-learning phishing detection and Advanced URL Filtering to help identify and block malicious websites. This includes pages designed to impersonate legitimate services and attacks intended to steal active session tokens.

The integration also incorporates WildFire file analysis to help detect malicious files, alongside browser security scanning for uploads and downloads. Risky browser extensions can also be identified, addressing another potential entry point for attackers.

These capabilities become particularly relevant when considered alongside the wider incident investigation process.

If an employee encounters a malicious login page, browser protection can prevent access to the site. Guardz can then connect that event to other activity involving the same identity, giving the MSP and Guardz's 24/7 managed detection and response (MDR) team additional context for determining whether the incident extends beyond the initial click.

Eisner described the broader objective as connecting security signals through an identity-centered architecture.

“Together with Palo Alto Networks, we’re advancing a new era of identity-centric security operations, connecting signals across identity, browser, email, endpoint, and data into a unified security fabric that is powered by agents. Guardz enables MSPs to autonomously investigate, correlate, and respond to threats across the entire workspace, while giving small businesses the freedom to embrace AI securely and thrive. This is the future of cybersecurity: identity-centric, unified data fabric, and built for autonomous defense at scale,” Eisner added.

For MSPs, security technology must also be practical to implement across multiple customer environments. Complex deployment requirements can make otherwise useful tools difficult to introduce, particularly when supporting organizations with limited IT resources.

Guardz says its integration streamlines provisioning, setup, and default configuration, allowing MSPs to deploy browser protection within minutes once the required tenant approvals are completed.

Early-access feedback from Datawise Networks highlighted the deployment experience. Kevin Kehoe of Datawise Networks said, “The rollout through our RMM was super smooth. Within minutes, the Prisma Browser for Business extension was on every Chrome and Edge profile, and it was quick and easy to configure and deploy.”

The simplified deployment process is designed to help MSPs extend browser security across their customer environments without introducing unnecessary operational overhead.

The announcement also addresses the growing use of browser-based AI tools. As employees increasingly turn to generative AI services for everyday work, organizations need visibility into which platforms are being used and how company information is being shared.

Guardz's AI Usage and Control capabilities allow technicians to establish client-specific policies governing activities such as copying, pasting, uploading, and downloading files. Restrictions can be applied by website or category.

The company has also introduced an AI usage report that gives MSPs a per-client view of AI tool adoption based on available security signals.

Shivam Srivastava, VP Product at Palo Alto Networks, sees the collaboration as an opportunity to bring browser protection to organizations that may not otherwise have access to the same level of security coverage.

“For most small businesses, the browser is now the office. Working with Guardz brings Prisma Browser for Business into the security these organizations receive from the providers they already trust. It connects our browser protection with the wider coverage and support their MSP delivers, helping safeguard the applications and information employees use every day,” Srivastava said.

As business applications and AI tools increasingly operate through browsers, securing that environment is becoming a more central part of protecting the modern workplace. Guardz's integration with Palo Alto Networks reflects an effort to bring browser activity into the wider security picture rather than leaving it as another isolated source of risk.
