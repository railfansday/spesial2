Besides the safe driver discount, careful drivers can claim several other car insurance discounts, but your insurer applies only the ones it knows you qualify for. Before your policy renews, ask it to check each: a defensive driving course, paying in full, paperless billing, and a group rate through your employer or alumni association.

How soon a clean record earns that safe driver discount depends on your insurer. At GEICO, one accident-free year can earn a discount of up to 22% on most coverages, while State Farm gives its version to new customers after three years without a moving violation or at-fault accident.

That discount comes from your record. The others depend on what you tell your insurer or sign up for. GEICO, for one, adds a discount once you’ve given it the details it needs, such as a group you belong to, and it has checked them.

Start with a defensive driving course, since some states make insurers give a discount for one. Minnesota law sets it at 10% or more for drivers 55 and older who finish a four-hour approved course. New York requires one for three years for any car’s main driver who completes a DMV-approved course, whatever their age. Say you’re 60, live in Minnesota, and pay $1,800 a year. The course saves you at least $180 a year, and a refresher course every three years keeps the discount.

Pay-per-mile policies and driving-tracking apps set your price by how far or how you drive, so weigh each as a separate choice. If your insurer comes up short, shop other insurers before your renewal date, since some of them discount a policy bought before your current one ends.

Send your insurer any course certificate the day it arrives. In New York, a certificate your insurer gets within 90 days of the course starts the discount on the day you finished it.
