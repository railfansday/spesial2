Bryson 'box office' for DP World Tour...but what about other LIV golfers?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Over 30 ex-LIV Golf players will feature on the DP World Tour next season, with Bryson DeChambeau set to feature full-time after accepting membership; What impact will it have on past members and why have some earning cards divided opinion?

Bryson DeChambeau may offer a ‘box-office’ addition to the DP World Tour next season, but opinions remain split on the impact of other ex-LIV Golf players being welcomed to the circuit.

Membership categories for the 2027 DP World Tour season were announced on Tuesday, with DeChambeau applying before the November 15 deadline and taking category one status - the highest priority ranking - for his 2024 US Open victory.

DeChambeau's future was uncertain after LIV Golf's collapse earlier this month, with him ineligible on the PGA Tour next season, but the two-time major champion is one of over 30 players from the breakaway circuit now set to feature on the DP World Tour.

Many already had DP World Tour status, including nine taking category 11 membership via the career money list, while nine - including Dustin Johnson and Cameron Smith - signed agreements to receive invites to a specific number of tournaments in 2027.

"I think he [DeChambeau] is going to be a great addition, he's going to bring interest and I think it's good, but I think he's very fortunate and I think all the LIV Golf players are incredibly fortunate," former Ryder Cup captain Paul McGinley told Sky Sports News.

"They [LIV players] basically are not skipping a beat here. They're coming back into the fold again having worked for LIV, that worked very strongly against the DP World Tour in terms of stealing away a lot of the business.

"They are very fortunate that they are getting an opportunity to play in the DP World next year, which is an excellent tour, with excellent prize funds, and obviously an opportunity to play the following year on the PGA Tour with all the riches over there."

Abraham Ancer, Lucas Herbert, Michael La Sasso, Joaquin Niemann, Carlos Ortiz and Thomas Pieters have joined major winners Johnson, Smith, Bubba Watson in signing an invites-based agreement, which will exclude Rolex Series events.

The DP World Tour admit the decision will bring 'different viewpoints' but insist it will not reduce playing opportunities for existing members, with invites designed to 'add value to tournaments'.

"The players to have accepted this invite rule, they're going to be sent to these places like the Magical Kenya or the China Classic or whatever it might be," Sky Sports' Tim Barter explained on the Sky Sports Golf podcast.

"Those tournaments sometimes didn't have anyone in the top 50 in the world in them, occasionally only one player in the top hundred in the world, so they are going to really to bring some star power.

"I think Guy Kinnings took it very seriously when players started to say to him, maybe that the rank-and-file players on the DP World Tour, 'we shouldn't be missing out on opportunities to play because these guys are being invited'.

"What he's doing is obviously putting them [invites to ex-LIV Golf members] into events where it goes through most of the categories. Nobody who won a card at tour school or the HotelPlanner Tour or the main tour will actually miss out, it looks like."

Winners of major championships since 2022 earn category one membership, with Jon Rahm joining DeChambeau in that list, while Tyrrell Hatton - winner of the DP World Tour Championship on Sunday - in category two.

Laurie Canter and Adrian Meronk hold category three membership for a victory in the previous two seasons and David Puig is in the category below, with priority access given to the lowest numbers when finalising a tournament field.

Thomas Detry, Tom McKibbin and Victor Perez are Category 10 members, while Ryder Cup legends Sergio Garcia, Lee Westwood, Paul Casey, Graeme McDowell and Martin Kaymer all qualify via the Career Money List.

Branden Grace, Charl Schwartzel, Ian Poulter and Louis Oosthuizen also hold DP World Tour membership via the money list, albeit in a slightly lower category, although any who previously resigned from the circuit are understood to face a 10-tournament suspension before being eligible to return to action.

Josele Ballester, Byeong Hun An and John Catlin progressed through Qualifying School to claim category 18 status, while those agreeing a specific number of invites can secure DP World Tour membership with a victory. Others ex-LIV members may secure spots in events co-sanctioned with different tours.

It remains unclear how many PGA Tour cards will be available via the Race to Dubai after next season, with a significant reduction from the 10 currently on offer expected, leaving it tougher to progress from the DP World Tour.

Those further down the rankings may find it harder to keep their DP World Tour membership, with LIV Golf additions adding additional depth to a circuit where only the top 100 at the end of the campaign retain full playing privileges.

"We've seen two ex-LIV players winning in the last two weeks and we're likely to see a lot of ex-LIV players winning in the coming months on the DP World Tour," McGinley added.

"They [ex-LIV golfers] are probably going to take 20 or 30 of the 100 DP World Tour cards that are available, so there's really 20 or 30 less spots for the current players to play for next year in terms of keeping their status.

"That's to be a difficult scenario for them and they didn't get the riches that the guys went to LIV did, so I can understand why they're feeling aggrieved.

"I'm just hoping that the DP World Tour players, who have remained loyal, come out next year and play well with all these LIV players in the field. Somebody is going to lose out in the years to come and that's what's the unfortunate about this whole sorry mess."

The earliest LIV Golf players would currently be able to feature on the PGA Tour is the FedExCup Fall in the autumn, depending on their based membership status, leaving DP World Tour events with stronger fields during the bulk of the 2027 season.

DP World Tour chief executive Guy Kinnings said the forthcoming season could 'be one of the most keenly contested campaign' in their history, following a 'significant change in the landscape of men's professional golf'.

"There's going to be lots of eyes on the DP World Tour, Sky Sports' Andrew Coltart admitted. "There's some huge names playing a full season and maybe more than four of them will be coming back for the next year.

"Obviously it [LIV players featuring] is going to put the nose out of joint for some members. This is specifically designed to enhance the strength of tournaments and not have a massive impact on them.

"The DP World Tour is going to be as strong as it's ever been next year and certainly very strong in 2028."
