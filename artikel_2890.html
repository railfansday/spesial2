Cowboys turn into turkeys during epic Thanksgiving Day meltdown | Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

ARLINGTON, TX – Give the people what they want, Dallas Cowboys. Or not.

Points. More points. Drama. And a holiday meltdown for the ages.

Hail to the depleted Philadelphia Eagles, capping a wild rally from a 21-point deficit with a 59-yard field goal from Jake Elliott with two seconds left that lifted them to a 39-38 victory – the largest Thanksgiving comeback in NFL history.

Joe Milton, the Eagles' fill-in quarterback signed off the Cowboys' practice squad in mid-October, got some serious revenge on the team that didn't keep him again as Dak Prescott's backup. Milton passed for a career-high 331 yards and three TDs. And boy did he live up to his "Bazooka Joe" moniker, using that cannon arm to connect with DeVonta Smith for a 50-yard touchdown in the first half and a 45-yard scoring strike in the second half.

Yet Milton's big day was only a part of the stain and shame left with the Cowboys.

They blew it again. Wasn't this the team that collapsed in crunch time in Week 8 against Arizona?

Indeed. Only this might have been even worse.

"Pretty stunning," said Cowboys quarterback Dak Prescott, a man who has way too much practice in trying to explain these embarrassing losses. "I guess I said it a couple weeks ago after the Arizona game, how stunning that was. This definitely takes the cake."

And the turkey, stuffing and sweet potato pies, too.

"Numbing is the word," Cowboys owner Jerry Jones offered.

He, too, has seen this season of promise for the Cowboys dissipate into disaster. Again. They are 5-7 and with road games against the Seahawks and Rams up next on the docket, this does not have the look of getting better. Yet, when Jones went on to hype the skill level of his coaching staff as he held court during his typical postgame briefing outside of the locker room, it was like the spin cycle repeating itself.

Which brings us to Brian Schottenheimer, in his second season as just the second coach in franchise history with a losing record (12-16-1), and a man losing, too, is trying to explain it.

"Every situation is different, each game is different," Schottenheimer said.

Maybe so, but there are common denominators. They lost to the Cardinals when Gardner Minshew came off the bench in the fourth quarter to throw his only three touchdowns of the season. They were upset by the Bucs, starting undrafted rookie quarterback Jalon Daniels on a night that started with the defense getting pushed around on a game-opening, 15-play touchdown drive. They lost in Week 3 in Rio with bone-headed blunders in crunch time, as the Ravens scored 10 points in the final 90 seconds.

Different situations, yet early or late in games, Dallas finds ways to lose.

This time, it started with a bang. The Cowboys had five possessions in the first half and scored four touchdowns. This flowed with recent trend. A few days earlier, when the Tennessee Titans came to town, Dallas had eight drives and seven TDs. And the week before that, in losing a shootout against the mighty Niners, they scored touchdowns on four of the last five possessions. In Cowboys math, that's 15 TDs over 18 drives during the three-game homestand (until country music star Miranda Lambert's halftime concert), which was nothing less than a certified roll.

Then poof. For all the feel-good flow of a 28-10 halftime lead enabled by Javonte Williams' 3 TDs, Dallas gagged against an injury-depleted team that used Aaron Rodgers earlier in the week to snap a seven-game losing streak.

The Eagles, fighting on for weeks after losing Jalen Hurts to blood clots, had one setback after another on Thursday. Star cornerback Cooper DeJean suffered a concussion on the game's first snap. Saquon Barkley had just five carries before getting sidelined by a stinger. Tight end Dallas Goedert was knocked out with a shoulder injury.

And Philadelphia (4-7) was still game enough to lay it on Dallas. Besides a huge, career-high 200-yard game from Smith, who torched Dallas on 11 catches as Milton's go-to man, the Eagles got a blocked punt from Ty Robinson early in the fourth quarter that Kenny Moore scooped and returned 3 yards for a touchdown. And then a 55-yard kickoff return by Britain Covey set up a 15-yard TD run for Milton. Throw in a 40-yard missed field goal by usually reliable Cowboys kicker Brandon Aubrey and yeah, words like stunning and numbing surely apply.

Reality check: The Cowboys, with all that explosive offense, still have a defense capable of imploding, of giving up huge chunks of yards, of allowing quick scores, of allowing opponents to stay in the game.

Of, well, allowing a castoff come back to haunt you.

"You never know when your opportunity is going to come," Milton said. "You never know when, where, how. The only thing you can is prepare yourself."

And if you're a quarterback, pray that the opportunity comes against the Dallas defense.

Remember when the Cowboys stripped first-year defensive coordinator Christian Parker of his play-calling duties? Like two weeks ago. They gave the reins to recently hired consultant Mike Pettine. Well, look at where that got them.

Still, for all of that, the Cowboys, with that red-hot, star-studded offense that produced 416 yards, could have bagged a victory by gaining 1 more yard.

The Eagles would have never had a chance for Elliott's big kick if Dallas had converted on fourth-and-1 from the Philadelphia 39-yard line with 55 seconds remaining. One yard.

Prescott was sacked for a 9-yard loss as he looked for CeeDee Lamb. Then it was left for Dallas' defense to protect the slim lead. And you know where that has gotten them.

Schottenheimer said he went for it because he trusted his offense to get a yard. Of course, there was no reason to trust the defense. And as it turned out, he couldn't trust the offense, despite a 100-yard game from Williams, to pound it inside to move the chains. Prescott took a shotgun snap. Then was eaten alive.

"You can't put the game on one play," Prescott said.

Yet sometimes, all it takes is one more play. Prescott acknowledged that part of his frustration was that his unit scored just 10 points after halftime. No, the defense's lapses and the special teams blunders could not be pinned on the quarterback, who has been on a tear with an 11-2 TD-to-INT ratio during the homestand.

Someone asked Prescott, fairly and pointedly, whether he'd expect to win after putting up 38 points.

"I'm not going to be the guy that's going to put things on other people," he said. "And I shoulder this one because of that last play."

Rest assured, there's plenty of embarrassment and shame to go around for the Cowboys. And little hope that this will be the season for a different type of finish.
