‘You Can See Everything’ Gets Digital Release — Here’s Where You Can Stream It

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

If you purchase an independently reviewed product or service through a link on our website, Rolling Stone may receive an affiliate commission.

You Can See Everything was one of the biggest breakout movies of the year, racking up positive reviews, big box office numbers, and awards buzz — a rarity for a nearly three-hour documentary.

Despite the fact that it’s still showing in theaters, You Can See Everything is available to stream at home; the film is available to rent for $19.99 or buy for $24.99 on PVOD platforms like Amazon, Apple TV, and Fandango at Home.

Distributor A24 has not yet announced a Blu-ray or subscription streaming release. The film will eventually land on HBO Max because of A24’s pay-1 deal with the streaming service.

You Can See Everything, directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, ups the ante on Fielder’s brand of intimate, awkward interactions. In the film, he spends over a month living with Elizabeth Holmes and her partner, Billy Evans, just before Holmes heads to prison for financial crimes related to her failed biotech company, Theranos. The documentary also marks a major year for Oppenheim, who helmed this year’s acclaimed Robert Pattinson-starring Primetime.

You Can See Everything features a surprisingly significant appearance by Amanda Seyfried, who played Holmes in the Hulu series The Dropout. The series was one of a number of films and books about Holmes, whose case became one of the most notorious financial crimes of the 21st century. Yet somehow, You Can See Everything managed to frame the story in an entirely new light, in part thanks to Seyfried’s appearance.

In an extensive interview with Rolling Stone, Seyfried discussed her eagerness to be a part of the film with Fielder and Oppenheim. “When you needed me, I came because of how invested you were, and how I felt like we were all on the same page at a point,” she said to the co-directors. “We were like a band. We were like a team. It felt like part of the team. And when I felt part of the team, it made me want to go even further. It’s not necessarily about being safe, but about doing something different.”
