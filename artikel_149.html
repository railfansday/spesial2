‘Zero’ evidence that recent cascade of tech outages are linked, says Rubio

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The US secretary of state was speaking after Microsoft, major banks, and the National Security Agency were all affected by technical failures in recent days

US secretary of state Marco Rubio has sought to assure Americans that there’s “zero” information that the cascade of service outages – which have grounded flights, cut off work access, limited bank access and disrupted classified communications – are related to each other or to a malign actor.

In recent days, Microsoft, major US banks, American air traffic control systems and the National Security Agency have all been affected by technical failures and outages. Donald Trump has faced a groundswell of criticism as friends and foes alike demand the White House answer for the disruptions.

“We have zero evidence or any reason to believe that any of these isolated or individual instances have anything to do with nefarious actors, with cyber hackers, with criminals, with another nation state, with a malign actor of any kind,” Rubio said.

He made the comments as he travelled with Trump for key meetings in the Philippines on Wednesday, before flying to the tech hub of Shenzhen in China for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) summit.

Trump was dismissive when asked whether he was concerned about the outages on Tuesday. “It’s life. When you’re big and powerful and you have all the money, things like that happen. It’s fine,” Trump said in Manila.

The series of outages began on Thursday, when Microsoft’s email and other tools went down, paralysing millions of workers who rely on them. Microsoft traced the issue to “an earlier network capacity upgrade”.

Flights across the US were grounded the next day in the longest so-called ground stop since the September 11 attacks. The US Federal Aviation Administration blamed a router upgrade.

By Monday, the banks were in the spotlight, as customers reported difficulty accessing online accounts at several major financial institutions and the money transfer service Zelle after a technical issue at JP Morgan Chase caused ripple effects throughout the financial system. Chase said it was caused by “a software bug” and was “not cyber-related”.

The same day, United Airlines said it had resolved an internal system outage that delayed departing flights.

Then on Tuesday, an internal network that the National Security Agency uses to communicate classified information was offline after what the agency described as “routine maintenance”.

Karoline Leavitt, Trump’s former press secretary, said the White House needed to do more to explain the situation.

“What I would say to the White House is: transparency is key,” Leavitt said on Fox News. Later, she praised the White House for having director of national intelligence Jay Clayton address the outages in an interview on Fox Business Network.

“Looking very diligently across the landscape, we are not seeing this as a cyber-attack incident,” Clayton said, explaining that the US government believes the incidents were caused by security upgrades that took longer than planned or encountered unexpected hiccups.

Democrat Mark Warner, the chair of the Senate intelligence committee reiterated that message, saying “there is no indication that these incidents are the result of a cyberattack, foreign interference, or some coordinated effort.

“AI is making it possible to identify vulnerabilities across enormous numbers of systems at unprecedented speed, creating enormous pressure on companies and government agencies to patch those vulnerabilities immediately, often all at once, putting tremendous strain on systems that weren’t built to handle that pace of change.

“The evidence so far points toward legitimate efforts to secure our systems, not malicious activity.”
