Last Beats First, Canucks Outlast Wild in Shootout

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Malhotra wanted the Canucks to play well during stretches of nothingness and simplicity, and he got his wish. There were long stretches of low-event hockey as the Minnesota Wild were as mild-mannered as they could be against the Vancouver Canucks on Saturday night. The Canucks looked like they could get their hearts broken in the third period, giving up a late goal, but showed tenacity in their response, ultimately defeating the top team in the NHL, 4-3 in a shootout.

Quinn Hughes avoided a second return to Rogers Arena this season after being ruled out for Minnesota's road trip. It was clear just how much Hughes means to Minnesota, as they did not look nearly as dynamic offensively without him. You can imagine how much the team wants him to sign an extension, as their competitive window could very well rest on his decision.

For the Canucks, forward Elias Pettersson was sidelined with an upper-body injury, while Brendan Gallagher got hurt after falling awkwardly in Thursday's game in Seattle. The Canucks' center depth took a big hit as the ill-timed Max Sasson trade exacerbated the issue.

Jacob Quillan and Ty Mueller got called up from Abbotsford, with the latter making his season debut. Luke Schenn also drew back into the lineup, marking his first game since Nov. 9.

With the win on Saturday, the Canucks got their second win at home this season. About time the fans got their money's worth at Rogers Arena. For the first half of the game, it looked like a snoozer, but the game heated up after that.

After Yakov Trenin boarded poor Ty Mueller on his first NHL shift of the season, Luke Schenn and Marcus Foligno dropped the gloves to spark the game. That marked the 11th fight between the Schenn and Foligno families and the seventh fight between Luke and Marcus. Given that Schenn had not played in two weeks, he needed to prove he deserved to stay in the lineup.

The Canucks kept the pressure on, outworking Minnesota, who did not get a shot for an 11-minute stretch. Through the first three quarters of the period, the shot attempts were 15-2 for the Canucks.

Minnesota scored with 1:58 remaining, as the Foligno brothers connected on a good passing play to give the team the lead, with Nick scoring the goal. It looked like it could be a heartbreaker in Vancouver, but…

The Canucks did not go quietly into the night, as they tied the game 35 seconds later. Marco Rossi capitalized, scoring his second goal of the night. Brock Boeser got his third assist of the night on that play.

Both teams went end to end, with a post hit by Filip Chytil and a highlight-reel save by Meriläinen. It is refreshing to see the teams go right at one another, instead of circling backwards over and over.

Meriläinen stayed as calm and composed as he always is, and Jake DeBrusk was the only scorer in the shootout, pulling out his signature forehand flip after coming in with speed. It is such a clever move that it is hard for goalies to read.

4-3, Canucks, the final via shootout, at Rogers Arena.

The first graph from NaturalStatTrick.com is the shot attempt advantage. The second is the expected goals advantage. Minnesota had the shot advantage in the first 40 minutes, but after that, it was mostly the Canucks taking over the game. The expected goals were 2.82-2.05 for Minnesota, which meant it was a relatively low-event game, which it was.
