Manchester City were left fuming on Tuesday night because they felt referee Istvan Kovacs blew the final whistle too early at the end of their Champions League clash against Napoli.

The two sides played out a 2-2 draw at the Etihad Stadium, but City were convinced Kovacs ended the game before the allotted two minutes of stoppage time had elapsed.

Enzo Maresca’s side were pushing for a winning goal after Rayan Cherki equalised in the 86th minute. However, those associated with the Premier League club were left incensed when the referee blew his whistle for full-time.

The Athletic’s Manchester City correspondent Sam Lee wrote on X (Twitter): “The ref blows the final whistle and points to his watch to tell the angry City players that the game is over. The big screen clock showed it was 91:47, and two minutes had been added on. They're still arguing now.”

Man City goalkeeper Gianluigi Donnarumma, one of the best goalkeepers in the world right now, was shown a yellow card for his protestations as boos rang out around the Etihad Stadium.

City currently sit sixth in the Champions League table with 10 points, but could find themselves out of the top eight by the end of Wednesday night if results don’t go their way.

A win would have left City on 12 points, level with the likes of Paris Saint-Germain (who face AS Roma on Wednesday), Como and Real Betis.

An explanation has now emerged, explaining why the ref appeared to blow the full-time whistle early.

The TV broadcast shows the Romanian match official actually blew the final whistle at 92:04 - not 91:47, as the big screen inside the Etihad Stadium claimed.

TNT Sports commentator Darren Fletcher said: "There's a problem here, the Manchester City bench are furious. I think they're saying he's blown before time, and so are the City players on the pitch.

"The clock on the screen was actually 20 seconds behind our clock, so if his watch tallies up with our clock he's got it right - just to make that point while all this pandemonium breaks out."

While it remains unconfirmed at this stage, it seems likely that the issue was caused by the Etihad Stadium big screen’s clock being slightly behind.

Kovacs’ timing tallied with the TV broadcaster’s on-screen clock, which suggests the match was brought to an end at the correct moment.
