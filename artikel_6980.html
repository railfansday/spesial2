A night cream is any cream that you use at night before bed to hydrate your skin. The idea is that night creams are thicker and made with rich emollients that work well to seal in hydration overnight versus your standard moisturizer that could be applied day or night. But a night cream can be a cream for nighttime, even if it’s not explicitly called a night cream. FWIW, that’s more of a marketing term than anything.

You absolutely need a moisturizer before bed, even if you have oily skin. But do you need a specific cream that’s called a “night cream”? Not necessarily, so long as your nighttime moisturizer is doing its job of hydrating and locking in moisture while you sleep.

How to choose the right night cream for you

If you have dry skin, the night cream selection process will probably be a breeze for you. Most night creams contain a plethora of ingredients to moisturize your skin. But some of those can be too heavy for oily and/or acne-prone skin. If you want something on the lighter side, avoid anything with a lot of butters and oils, like shea butter or cocoa butter. Lightweight hydrators include squalane, jojoba oil, hyaluronic acid, and panthenol.

Look for a mix of hydrators and sealants.

The key to moisturizing your skin is two-fold: Hydrate, then lock in moisture. Hydration comes from humectants, aka ingredients that pull water into your skin. That’s glycerin, hyaluronic acid, panthenol, and aloe vera. But once you’ve hydrated, you want to keep all that water in your skin, which is where sealants come in. That’s oils and butters. They create a barrier over your skin to keep moisture from seeping out. Together, the two create a formula that offers lasting hydration, not just a quit hit of plumpness.
