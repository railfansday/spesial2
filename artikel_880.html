F1 drivers back return of 'very cool' V8 engines in the future

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Several Formula 1 drivers have backed the possibility of V8 engines returning to the sport as early as 2030; watch the Las Vegas GP weekend on Sky Sports F1, with Friday's practice sessions at 12.30am and 4pm

Formula 1's drivers appear to be unanimous with the prospect of V8 engine returning to the sport in the 2030s.

Earlier this week, ahead of this weekend's Las Vegas Grand Prix, FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem teased the possibility of V8 engines with the sound of a screaming F1 car with a caption: "You spoke, we listened. V8s coming soon."

The FIA also published graphics on social media which suggested an agreement in principle had been reached by themselves, Formula 1 management and the engine manufacturers for V8 engines to return from 2030, but the post was deleted.

The current regulations are set to run until 2031, which could be an explanation for the deleted post, while further details of the overall power unit, including whether an electrical element and a turbo will remain, need to be finalised.

"I'd love to see it. When I look at the old videos of the old engines, they give me goosebumps," said Mercedes driver George Russell.

"I think it's about just that high-pitched squeal that I'm missing from this era. The purists will say take away all electrical power, but I think when you look at how a lot of these cars have evolved in recent years, and the power output from some of these electrical motors these days, Formula 1 is going to continue to be the fastest race cars on the planet, you need to have it, that's just the reality.

"You need to have some sort of hybrid, because the power you get, you can't really produce that just solely on internal combustion.

"If we go back to V8s, having some sort of electrical power as well, to give you that extra 400, 500 kilowatts to bring the cars to 1000+ horsepower, that's what we want.

"I think it's worth remembering as well actually, the final V8 era, they sounded awesome, but the cars, they actually weren't very fast. I don't think many people realise that."

New world champion Kimi Antonelli agreed with his Mercedes team-mate and claims "many people would look forward" to the return of V8 engines.

"It's going be very cool to go back to more old style. Also, the cars will get lighter even further, probably 70kg, which is immense," he said.

"Definitely, I think it's a very exciting regulation change. Of course, we have got to first do well in this one."

Max Verstappen has been critical of the current power units which are around 53 per cent battery powered and led to an unusual style of driving and racing at some tracks, with the drivers sometimes unable to push flat out in a traditional manner.

Verstappen, who warned driving in Las Vegas will be "painful" due to the long straights, feels the FIA have listened to the drivers and realised the 2026 regulations are "not what you want".

The four-time world champion stated he would get rid of the turbo in future power unit regulations, and effectively have a fully normally aspirated engine.

"In some tracks then you're not allowed to race because it will be too loud because if it's up to me I would take the turbo off," he explained.

"I would get rid, maybe you can use it as a push to pass, but not too much of the battery side. But then on some tracks, you're not allowed to race, so fuel consumption goes up a lot without a turbo, so you need a bigger tank if you don't want to do refuelling in the races, so I understand that you want to have a turbo.

"If it's fully up to me, of course you go back to like a V10 or whatever. That sounds even better, but I know that not something realistic.

"This [news] is good. This is for sure in the right direction, but again, it's not confirmed yet. So there's still a few things I guess that they need to agree on."

Lewis Hamilton, Fernando Alonso, Nico Hulkenberg Sergio Perez and Valtteri Bottas are the only drivers who drove in the previous V8 era which ended in 2013.

Alonso said it's "frustrating" F1 has not had V8 engines since 2014, while Hamilton is keen to know more details about the proposed return of V8s.

"I think it's ultimately dependent on… it's all well going to V8. It depends on how they design it, regulation-wise, and going back to the good sound because that's I think been lacking since we've been in the V6 era," he said.

"Yeah, bring back the smaller exhaust and get that sound back.

"But you could have that, and as long as we don't have the engine dying halfway down the straight, then I think that'd be better."
