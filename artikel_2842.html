Double Trouble: Teremoana Teremoana In Action In Australia

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

• Popular Australian heavyweight prospect Teremoana Teremoana will be back in action in front of his devoted fans at home in Melbourne on a mid-week card on Wednesday.• Teremoana will face regional rival Hemi Ahio in an all-Tasman showdown.• North American fans can watch live on DAZN Boxing at 3 a.m. ET/Midnight PT, or catch the replay.

A light week on the boxing schedule kicks off with a heavy-duty matchup of Australian heavyweights early Wednesday for North American fans.

Heavyweight prospect Teremoana Teremoana will headline his first card in front of his Australian fans in Melbourne against New Zealand rival Hemi Ahio.

The vacant IBF Intercontinental Heavyweight belt will be at stake in the trans-Tasman showdown between the undefeated Teremoana (12-0, 12 KOs) who collides with the veteran New Zealand rival Hemi "The Heat" Ahio of Auckland (25-1, 19 KOs).

Fans with insomnia or who are awake for other reasons can watch live in the U.S. starting at 3 a.m. ET/12 midnight PT on DAZN Boxing. Everyone else can enjoy a replay with their morning coffee.

All fighters made weight for the card. Ahio weighed in at 105.3 kg, or 232 pounds. Teremoana weighed in at 122 kg, or 269 pounds.

The affable Teremoana is one of the young-gun generation of heavyweights steadily replacing champions and contenders aging out.

He became the first Australian boxer to win an Olympic match in the super heavyweight division, knocking out Dmytro Lovchynskyi. Unfortunately, he then ran into eventual gold medalist Bakhodir Jalalov in the second round, and his Paris 2024 Olympics came to an end.

Teremoana still managed to make a big impression on fans by performing a traditional Haka dance representing his Cook Island heritage at the end of his bouts. He has continued to do so as a pro, including his last victory in March. With a record of early knockouts, Teremoana and his team have plenty of time left to entertain.

At this week's final pre-fight news conference, Teremoana enjoyed every minute in the limelight, happy to be home. But he made his intentions toward his opponent Ahio clear. He wants to make a quick night of it, and talk his way onto the Fury vs Joshua card in December. But he has to get through Wednesday first, and without injury.

"He obviously knows what is coming, and I expected him to train hard. He knows the task that is in front of him. It's not an easy task. I believe that I have all the attributes and all the advantages to get the job done," said Teremoana. Although he admitted Ahio has far more experience, he will be dangerous everywhere.

"He's had that experience, and that is what he has on me. But youth, speed, size, I have all of that on my side. I'm not overlooking him but I do expect to be punching holes through him.

"In this division, everyone punches hard," explained Teremoana. "I've been hit by smaller fellas and, of course, it hurts if you get caught. But we have trained so hard in the gym, we do the rounds and we have prepared so well.

"He's got a big task ahead of him and he's got to get past the reach. Up close, I'm powerful and explosive too, so there's no safety zone for him."

Ahio isn't a big personality or big talker like his opponent. But he made his case.

"I don't have too much to say about it. I kind of like Teremoana. I have met him a few times before. We've fought on the same card twice. We both know what we're doing and are both respectful of each other.

"But come fight night, I know he's going to come and try and take my head off and I will be trying to do the same thing … For Teremoana, I think I am going to come out and hurt him because I do know he likes to be in the firing zone and I like to come in too on the inside. If he makes the mistake of being in there too long, then I am going to get him."

Fans outside Australia may not know much about Ahio, but they might be more familiar with his assistant trainer and sparring partner, Australian heavyweight Junior Fa. Fa, who retired from active competition in 2023 with a record of 20-3, 11 KOs, faced top competitors including Lucas Brown, Joseph Parker, and Frank Sanchez.

Fa promised Ahio is taking the fight seriously and has been training nonstop.

"I've seen him – because I have trained alongside Hemi for a long, long time and done countless camps together and been sparring partners, now joining him as an assistant coach – where I have seen him physically at his peak and has matched it mentally as well. He's in a very good place," said Fa.

"The way that this fight has developed and to be for the IBF Intercontinental Title, that things are just lining up. So, we are looking forward to a very big night and a very big performance from both boys, but especially our boy Hemi Ahio."

Teremoana Teremoana took time out this week to attend a Melbourne United soccer match before his fight on Wednesday. Photo: Matchroom Boxing

Matchroom Boxing CEO Frank Smith is in Australia overseeing the event. Australia is an important boxing market for Matchroom, and it is working to build a presence with domestic talent.

"On Wednesday night we are going to see, who I believe, is going to be a future heavyweight star in Teremoana Teremoana in what is his toughest test to date, alongside what is a great undercard.

"These are the kind of fights that we want to make. Teremoana, we believe, is someone who will go to the very top of this sport and rule the heavyweight division for a long, long time. But, as we've seen, especially in heavyweight boxing, if you make the wrong steps at the wrong time and don't build yourself through those levels, then it is a tough sport."

Smith said Matchroom is moving Teremoana through the division ranks with care. He sweetened the pot for Teremoana with a personal bet on Wednesday's results.

"I've got a bet on the line with Teremoana that if he knocks him (Ahio) out in the first round, then I've got to pay for his new golf membership. He has taken up golf as a new side quest."

"I know I say this every time, but it is such a privilege and honor to represent us on the world stage," said Teremoana. "I am a proud Australian and a proud Cook Islander. I just hope that people see a bit of themselves in me as I fly those flags worldwide.

"When we get in the ring, it will be just me and Hemi and nobody is there to back you up, so I can't wait. Come Wednesday night we will let it all out in the ring and go hard."

The undercard features a boxing box of chocolates lineup of Australian domestic talent in what can charitably be called club-level fights. These can be a lot of fun if you temper your expectations accordingly.

Joel Camilleri of Melbourne (31-8-1, 15 KOs) takes on Mitchell Leek of Melbourne (14-0, 8 KOs) for the vacant IBF Australasian Middleweight title. Camilleri lost twice to Koen Mazoudier and once to Tim Tszyu in 2019 by decision.

Super welterweights Ky Hodgson of Melbourne (7-1, 5 KOs) vs Victor Adis-Iosifidis of Victoria (3-1, 1 KOs).

Super middleweights Omar El-Abdallah of Melbourne (6-0, 5 KOs) vs Nicholas Benn of New Zealand (5-2, 5 KOs).

Middleweights James Noonan of Melbourne (1-1) vs Matt Faron of Victoria (2-0).
