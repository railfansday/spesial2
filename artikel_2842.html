Knicks Bulletin: ‘It’s a matter of time in my opinion’

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

As everyone already knew before the game, the Knicks beat the Nets.

Here's the latest from the only people that play meaningful basketball in New York's five boroughs.

On the Knicks' bench performance against Brooklyn:

"Our bench was fantastic. I thought Landry came off and gave us a big lift on both ends of the floor. Jordan as well. And then Tyler — I thought Tyler's minutes were tremendous. He did a fantastic job running the team, getting us some easy looks, and then he was solid defensively."

On searching for a lineup combination to stop Brooklyn's momentum:

"When we went into a little funk was at the beginning of the second. Us not scoring gave [Brooklyn] a little hope because they were able to shrink that lead and tie it up, even take the lead. When you give a young team confidence, now it's hard to turn the water off … So I just started searching at that point and went to Tyler, went to Landry, tried JC, and then we found a group that caught a little groove and helped us take the lead back and create separation."

On Jordan Clarkson accepting a reduced role and being an exceptional teammate:

"You have a veteran that's been there, done that. and he's willing to accept the role that most guys wouldn't accept – even guys younger that haven't proved as much as he's proven in this league. And he's willing to accept it. So, in my opinion, if he's willing to accept it, which is what he did last year, and still try to uplift everybody, connect everybody while he's going through what he's going through, then everybody else should. That's that's the ultimate team player, I will say this about JC. I've been around some good teammates. He's got to be the best. He is the definition of ultimate pro."

"A lot of (his minutes) are based on the feel."

On Mikal Bridges' early-season struggles and what he needs to do to regain his form:

"Yeah, he's just got to keep playing. Everybody goes through those ups and downs throughout the course of the season, and obviously, it's the beginning of the season right now, so he's got to find his way. Not just with him but with everybody, when you're in a funk you gotta find one or two things to hold your hat on. Mikal is one of the best runners in the NBA.

"In both directions – offensively and defensively. So he's just got to try to hang his hat on that as well as his cutting and trying to do the other little things – rebounding and driving and touching the paint and spraying it. Then this shot will come eventually."

On his confidence that Bridges will eventually rediscover his shooting touch:

"It's a matter of time in my opinion, before Mikal gets going."

On not penalizing players, including Alavarado, for missing games because of injuries:

"I try not to take a guy out [of his rotation spot] due to injury and the reality is Jose got hurt."

On his conversation with Jose Alvarado and Tyler Kolek about the backup point guard rotation:

"Jose got hurt, and Tyler gave us some good minutes. But at the end of the day, I sat them both down and I told them, 'Hey, Jose, you're our backup because you ended the season as our backup.'

"Both you guys helped us win last year. Jose in the finals, and Tyler in the Cup final. Both you guys got to be ready because a change can happen anytime."

On Karl-Anthony Towns' increased playmaking responsibilities and turnovers:

"KAT's touches are going up, but his turnovers have gone up. KAT is a load making plays with the basketball. He's just gotta finish the play. And not get it deflected or turn it over."

On the Knicks attempting too many risky passes and Towns' offensive fouls:

"Obviously, they have to do better [with the turnovers] and we talked about it. We will continue to talk about it. We've tried to make the home run pass. That's happened a few times. And obviously some turnovers from KAT are offensive fouls. He's got to be better in that area. But for the most part, we have to keep trying to hit singles as opposed to triples and home runs."

On preferring dead-ball turnovers to mistakes that lead to fast breaks:

"Obviously, you don't want your opponent to have the opportunity to get out on the break, so if it's a situation where we have a chance to get back or the ball is dead, I'd prefer that. We've had some turnovers, like [with] Philly in the first half, it felt like we paid the price for it. So to try to just control that a little better at times will help us. But we'd rather have our defense set up than not."

On making better use of his increased touches and playmaking responsibilities:

"I just care about wins. So I gotta do better, making sure with that time [holding onto the ball] and translating it to more wins."

On the need for keeping the Knicks' offense moving:

"I think we just all gotta be together, figure it out. Things just can't get stagnant, and just gotta keep moving."

On Jordan Clarkson accepting a smaller role to help the Knicks win:

"I think it shows his character more than anything else. He's someone that if he was on a different team, he could play 20, 25 minutes every night. He could score double digits. He could be that spark, that microwave off the bench. And he's here. He was willing to sacrifice and take a smaller role, be a part of something bigger than himself. So it shows its character.

"It shows his professionalism. To be able to go out and do those things. And when he's not doing it, he's out there coaching, not on the court, he's coaching guys up, talking to them, letting them know what he sees. So he's the ultimate professional. He's someone that you want on your team. And whenever he's given an opportunity, he'll go out and play well."

On the Knicks finally increasing their pace in the fourth quarter against Brooklyn:

"We finally played fast, got stops. We've been playing very slow, very stagnant. We finally got out, we ran and we got good looks."

On why the Knicks' strong fourth quarter against Brooklyn did not erase their earlier problems:

"No. No, the issues are still there. That [fourth quarter] doesn't erase the problem or erase the issue. So you gotta figure that out and get better."

On the Knicks' offensive struggles persisting despite their victory over the Nets:

"The issues are still there. I think defensively, we were solid. I think this year, for the most part, our defense has been pretty good. Obviously there have been some lapses. … But our offense has to get in a better flow, and that's kind of what's holding us back right now. So a win doesn't erase the problem or erase the issues. We got to figure those out and get better."

On the contributions of the Knicks' reserves against Brooklyn:

"We talked about [Clarkson] earlier, Landry (Shamet), I mean, he had big threes. That third quarter kept us in it. Jose (Alvarado) played well. Ty (Kolek) played well. [Andre Drummond], I think he had to win like nine rebounds, ten rebounds in, like, 11 or 12 minutes. It shows you the depth that we have, that it could be anybody's night on any given night."

On Mike Brown's confidence in him and his willingness to use different players:

"I think it shows his (Brown's) confidence in me. I think he has confidence in every game on the roster. As he's shown, he'll throw any guy in at any time, to do the spark."

On Mike Brown's willingness to confront the Knicks' problems directly:

"We have to be real with ourselves, and Mike's great with that, assessing things in film, calls it as it is. Our group's great with that. We all understand that by no means — we're not taking this as any potential fool's gold. Like I said, the whole goal right now is to continue to get better, and we want to do that. So that's the thought process."

On his early experiences playing against Jordan Clarkson:

"When I was younger, my only thought of him was that he was really hard to guard and it felt like he always went at me. I used to hate it. He'd score and talk a little s—."

On Clarkson's team-first attitude and willingness to accept substitutions:

"Knowing him now, knowing who he is, it's not crazy. The guy just wants to win. He wants to do whatever he can for this group, his teammates. He'll play a stint out there for two minutes and get subbed out. A lot of guys in the NBA, the reality is, if they get subbed, they're pissed. The whole thing. He never has any of those moments."

On the physical toll of the Knicks' championship run:

"We definitely played until June last season and I think everyone still feels it."

"When I got to Utah, I was trying to think of a number. The only player I remembered as a kid who played for the Jazz (whose number wasn't retired) was Greg Ostertag. So, when I got to New York, I kept it."
