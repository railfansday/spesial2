Will anyone else sign Aaron Rodgers after he torched Steelers? Breaking down 5 questions, including most logical match

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The cognitive dissonance was high and, yet, unsurprising.

Few around the NFL were surprised that the Pittsburgh Steelers released quarterback Aaron Rodgers after he lambasted head coach Mike McCarthy on "The Pat McAfee Show."

And also: Few were ready to say with certainty that the 42-year-old four-time MVP would not get another chance to play in the NFL.

There is a laundry list of reasons why other teams would not sign the man who just aired quite a bit of dirty laundry.

And yet: NFL teams have operated off far less logical desperation before. Rodgers' arm strength has not fully dissipated, nor has his line-of-scrimmage command lost its argument among the best in the game.

So multiple sources weren't ready to fully rule out a Rodgers return to the NFL this season.

One high-ranking AFC executive said they "doubt" Rodgers gets another gig but left the door a crack open, noting "we all are trying to get to the postseason."

It's doubtful Aaron Rodgers and head coach Mike McCarthy will be exchanging holiday cards this year.

An NFC talent evaluator responded to whether they'd sign Rodgers if they were general manager not with a "no" but by asking in this hypothetical "of what team?"

And an NFC assistant coach whose team has realistic odds to make the playoffs did not dismiss the upside of signing Rodgers for a team with strong pieces to support a quarterback.

"If it means we win a Super Bowl? Yes," the assistant said. "Not sure who is in that mode right now."

So could another NFL team sign Rodgers for the rest of the regular season and/or a playoff run? Here are five questions to keep in mind surrounding Rodgers' possible next steps.

1) The Steelers released Rodgers on Wednesday, and he went unclaimed on waivers Thursday. Doesn't that already mean no team wanted to sign him?

Claiming Rodgers off waivers feels like trading for receiver A.J. Brown last year. Sure, any team could have tried to pry the three-time Pro Bowler from the Philadelphia Eagles. But Brown's vocal disruptions in Philadelphia indicated that he would not go quietly to wherever he was told. The New England Patriots knew Brown wanted to reunite with his former Tennessee Titans head coach Mike Vrabel and thus they felt confident that Brown's willingness to accept coaching and embrace his teammates would be stronger with Vrabel in New England than in a place he landed against his will.

And then there were the financial implications to claiming Rodgers off waivers rather than signing him as a free agent: Off waivers, Rodgers' original contract had $8.56 million left this year. As a free agent, a team could sign Rodgers for the prorated veteran minimum and leave the Steelers responsible for nearly all of his pay. It's not hard to guess whether Rodgers would appreciate taking up as much space as possible on the Steelers' books.

Now that he's cleared waivers, teams can pursue Rodgers as a free agent at a lower cost and with a higher ability to research whether they want him.

2) Would Rodgers add value to another team?

Determining Rodgers' net value is complicated: How does one weigh decades of schematic and technical knowledge against the risk of another public embarrassment for which teammates and coaches must answer? How does one project Rodgers' willingness to accept play-calls from a play-caller other than himself — after his ultimatum to McCarthy about the head coach no longer calling plays appears to have first and foremost triggered his benching?

What's indisputable: Rodgers' performance this year. He led the Steelers to a 6-4 record on track for the playoffs (there is plenty of time, and reason, to wonder if they'll go off the tracks now), but Rodgers' individual statistics haven't topped charts. In 10 games, Rodgers completed 60.9% of passes for 2,241 yards, 16 touchdowns and six interceptions. His 86.6 passer rating ranks 19th among 32 qualified quarterbacks, per Next Gen Stats, while Rodgers' 6.2 yards per attempt ranks 26th. Rodgers' EPA/dropback, a measurement for the average amount of expected points added on dropbacks from a quarterback, ranks 29th among 32 quarterbacks.

Rodgers' completions have primarily featured dump-offs. Rodgers' 7 air yards per attempt ranks fourth-lowest in the NFL this season, per Next Gen Stats, and his 36.4% rate of downfield passing (10+ air yards) ranks last in the league.

How much will other clubs view this as a sign of Rodgers' age or unwillingness to run plays? How much will they view this as reflecting the Steelers' cast around him and perhaps the coaching and play-calling deficiencies of which he's accused McCarthy?

Earlier this month, a high-ranking executive from a team that played Rodgers this season characterized the quarterback as "pretty good."

"He wasn't against us, but he's pretty good," the executive told Yahoo Sports. "He can still throw it as good as anybody in the league. And he's got a quick mind and still moves around a little bit. I didn't see anything that would tell me he can't play anymore."

Expect that executive to not stand alone in his assessment.

This question seemed of most interest to executives, talent evaluators and coaches after Rodgers unleashed his allegations. To consider what teams would sign Rodgers, let's start with which teams will not. Teams with established and healthy starting quarterbacks will not sign a declining, disgruntled player on the cusp of his 43rd birthday. Teams outside the range of realistically making the playoffs are also unlikely to waste their time managing a quarterback who plans to retire after this season — nor would Rodgers likely want to join a team with whom he cannot contend.

So which teams on track to make the playoffs, or contend for a final spot, could make sense? Six of the eight division leaders as of Thursday night do not currently need a quarterback: That's the Buffalo Bills, Denver Broncos, Jacksonville Jaguars, Cincinnati Bengals, Chicago Bears and Carolina Panthers. (An injury could make those teams interesting.) The San Francisco 49ers certainly don't need a quarterback when Brock Purdy is healthy. And even if Purdy needs to rest his sprained ankle, there's reason to believe the 49ers' second-string Mac Jones has more to offer than Rodgers, particularly for a team that notoriously revolves around head coach Kyle Shanahan's play-calling system.

The New York Giants are currently patching together a system with J.J. McCarthy and Jameis Winston, but at least some in the league don't believe Rodgers and Giants head coach John Harbaugh would mesh. Harbaugh's team-first messaging hardly aligns with Rodgers' McAfee sound-off.

The Cleveland Browns and Minnesota Vikings are each on track for wild-card positions without clear stability at quarterback, while the New England Patriots are on the bubble and fading in the aftermath of quarterback Drake Maye's injury. The Baltimore Ravens also are intriguing given they've lost two quarterbacks for the season and play the Steelers twice more this year (imagine Rodgers wanting revenge!), but Baltimore's playoff chances currently rest at 6%, per Next Gen Stats, and could fall to 2% if it loses on Sunday to the Houston Texans.

4) Given all of that, who is the most likely to sign Rodgers?

There's a reason to take the field against any team if betting. But executives and coaches across the league seem more suspicious of the Patriots' interest than they are of any other team.

Sources believe newly acquired Kirk Cousins fits the Vikings better than Rodgers, and Rodgers' distraction level and subpar play would be a tough sell for a Browns team that's exceeding expectations with Deshaun Watson at the helm.

Expect the Patriots to give in-house Tommy DeVito a full game with practice reps before looking outside their organization for help. But DeVito's 12-of-24 passing (50%) for 81 yards, one touchdown, one interception and four sacks in a 35-10 loss to the Detroit Lions two weeks ago did not inspire confidence. The circumstances were unideal, in the aftermath of Maye fracturing his right fibula, per ESPN, while abroad in Germany.

The Patriots visit the 1-9 Los Angeles Chargers on Sunday. While that should challenge DeVito less than an eight-win Lions team did, the Chargers' defense is feisty and a loss could further create questions around Vrabel's recent comments surrounding winning culture. Add to that Rodgers' Green Bay overlap with now-Patriots general manager Eliot Wolf and Patriots receiver Romeo Doubs … front offices around the league are curious, if by no means sure that any move would happen imminently.

5) Say a team wants to consider Rodgers. What would his references say?

Calling the references of a prospective employee who burned his last employer is dicey. Calling McCarthy, in particular, seems like an unproductive task. But Rodgers' head coach immediately prior, longtime Steelers leader Mike Tomlin, shared his perspective Thursday night as part of NBC's halftime show during the Chiefs-Bills game.

Tomlin characterized his primary emotion as "sadness," for multiple reasons including this possibly marking the end of Rodgers' prolific career. Tomlin agreed that Rodgers' McAfee appearance "certainly" shocked him — while also giving Rodgers credit for the relationships the quarterback built last season upon arriving in Pittsburgh.

"He was beloved and I'm not overexaggerating that," Tomlin said. "He worked at it and people appreciated his openness to getting to know and understand the Steeler culture, to be engaged with teammates. And particularly from my perspective, I worked with a lot of veteran players over the years, and at times they lose a little patience for the process or they lose patience for relationships with younger guys.

"Man, he seemed to embrace all of that and really embrace it in a real natural way."

That didn't keep Tomlin from acknowledging the unprecedented nature of Rodgers' week.

"On the macro level, whether it's organizationally or for the game of football at large," Tomlin said, "to see laundry aired out that way in a public forum, those are things that kind of run counterculture to professional football."
