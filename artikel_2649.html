Inconsistent Nets crushed by lousy Raptors two days after encouraging Bucks win

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

TORONTO — Every Nets step forward gets followed by two steps back. And Friday they got knocked all around in a soft showing, bullied 124-100 by the woeful Raptors at Scotiabank Arena.

Two nights after having won in Milwaukee, the Nets let Toronto hit first and they never hit back. Against an injured, offensively challenged team, they couldn't buy a bucket or beg a rebound in a sorry effort.

"The most physical team won. It's plain and simple. They were the aggressors," coach Jordi Fernández said. "After a good performance in Milwaukee, then we respond with this. It's not about the result, it's how you fight. If the other team has a level, just to match that level, keep fighting and responding — and we just didn't.

Toronto Raptors' Immanuel Quickley, right, steals the ball from Brooklyn Nets' Keon Ellis during an NBA Cup game in Toronto, Friday, Nov. 27, 2026. Frank Gunn/The Canadian Press via AP

"As far as our urgency to contest and rebound, we were not there. We were not winning 50-50 balls. I addressed it at halftime, and then we responded with the worst quarter of the game. So, a lot of work to do."

That's for a Nets team that clearly hasn't worked hard enough.

They got out-hustled and outmuscled by the injury-riddled Raptors. Scottie Barnes torched them for a game-high 34 points while Immanuel Quickley scored 21 of his 24 points in the third quarter en route to a triple double.

But it wasn't the numbers so much as the knowledge they got pushed around. Again.

"We've got to match physicality. There's really no excuse for it," Michael Porter Jr. said "We're a young team, but yeah, if you're going to be on the floor, you've got to be able to match the physicality of our opponents."

Noah Clowney of the Brooklyn Nets handles the ball against the Toronto Raptors on Friday, Nov. 27, 2026, at Scotiabank Arena in Toronto. NBAE via Getty Images

The Nets (4-14) didn't come close to matching it. And that's facing a Raptors team that had dropped seven of their last eight, and was playing without star Kawhi Leonard (left knee injury management) and RJ Barrett (right hamstring strain).

They allowed 49 percent shooting and were outrebounded 44-30.

Brooklyn was whistled for seven more fouls because the Raptors (7-11) beat them to the ball. The Nets sent Toronto for a parade to the charity stripe, 30 of 33 at the free-throw line as they got punished for not being the aggressors.

Scottie Barnes (4) of the Toronto Raptors plays against the Brooklyn Nets during an NBA Cup game in Toronto on Friday, Nov. 27, 2026. Anadolu via Getty Images

Other than rookie Mikel Brown Jr.'s 21 points, the Nets had few bright spots. Julius Randle had 21 points, five assists and five rebounds, while Porter added 17 points but just two boards and finished a minus-16.

The Nets were within 38-37 after an Egor Dëmin 3-pointer with 8:12 left in the second quarter. But the young Russian was just 2 of 8 in another off night, and the Nets immediately conceded eight unanswered points to fall behind by nine.

Whatever Fernández said at halftime didn't land. Down just eight, the Nets got outscored 39-24 in the third.

"They were the aggressors coming out starting the game," Brown said. "We just let teams get comfortable early on in the game, and then it just becomes a snowball effect."

That's something that we have to get better at."
