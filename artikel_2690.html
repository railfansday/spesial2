V.J. Edgecombe Reveals How He Motivates Joel Embiid Amid Dreadful Shooting Slump

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

The Philadelphia 76ers defeated the Toronto Raptors 123-112 tonight after a team offensive effort led by a 27-point night for Tyrese Maxey. But the most noteworthy storyline was Joel Embiid's health and performance.

The former MVP went down late in the first quarter, clutching his knee and clearly in pain. He came back out midway through the second quarter, to the relief of 76ers fans.

But he finished tonight's game with 20 points, four rebounds, and three assists, shooting 7-17 from the field (41.2%) and missing all three of his three-point attempts. This extended his shooting slump to 2-29 from the three-point line over the last 10 games (6.8% 3P).

In the postgame locker room media scrum, V.J. Edgecombe spoke to reporters about his initial reaction to Joel Embiid's injury scare and how he constantly motivates Embiid.

"It sucks. It sucks to see, but I tell him all the time that I don't like seeing him on the floor. I'm always kind of pessimistic at times because you never know what happened when someone's just lying on the floor, especially when I didn't see what happened."

"But for him to go back and then come back out shows his heart. He could have just sat out, but that shows his heart. I think he needed a collision, so that's something."

"Obviously, thank God it wasn't anything like that. So that's on them now. But yeah, I hate seeing him on the floor. He knows that. I always tell him, 'Please don't just fall. Please don't,'" Edgecombe initially said.

The only way that Joel Embiid can come out of this three-point shooting slump is by seeing the ball go through the basket, even if it's from inside the paint. So the 76ers believe that Joel Embiid needs to consistently stay aggressive, despite the three-point shooting slump.

"Yeah, I tell him all the time, you were the MVP of the league," V.J. Edgecombe said. "Obviously, injuries and all that play a part, but it's more of a mental thing. You know, I just want to know that he hasn't lost a step for real. You saw it tonight. He can dunk; did you see that tonight?

"But I always tell him, 'Yeah, you were MVP of the league, bro; you're that good. You're that good.' I always tell him, 'Nobody can stop you. There's not one person out there who can guard you. You can face up; you can play with your back to the basket. Bro, nobody can guard you.' So, he knows it."

"And as I said, he's starting to find his rhythm. I said he had to get his focus and timing back, but he's starting to find his rhythm," Edgecombe concluded.

The 32-year-old is currently averaging 17.9 points, 8.8 rebounds, and 3.7 assists while shooting 37.4% from the field and 11.1% from beyond the arc. Clearly, his averages don't reflect an MVP-caliber start to the season.

Joel Embiid had recently admitted that the shooting slump was taking a mental toll on him, and he was considering stopping his three-point shots forever. But I am certain he knows the only way out of this slump is to shoot more, not less.

Despite his ongoing shooting struggles, Embiid still has his team's support, who believe he can rediscover his MVP form. Edgecombe's words reflect the 76ers' confidence in their superstar, but Embiid will need to turn that encouragement into consistent performances.

For now, the 76ers will hope his knee is fine and that this latest outing marks the beginning of a much-needed turnaround. But it remains to be seen whether the former MVP can still perform consistently at his peak level.
