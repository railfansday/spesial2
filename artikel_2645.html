Michigan football wide receiver Andrew Marsh charged with indecent exposure

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Michigan football wide receiver Andrew Marsh has been charged with indecent exposure for an off-campus incident in early February.

According to a release from Pittsfield Township Police & Fire Department on Thursday, Nov. 19, the Washtenaw County (Michigan) Prosecutor's office authorized a misdemeanor complaint of indecent exposure on Oct. 14.

A notice to appear was generated on Wednesday, Nov. 18 and Marsh is scheduled for a pre-trial hearing on Dec. 4.

"We were just made aware of an incident involving Andrew Marsh," Michigan football spokesman Dave Ablauf said in a statement to The Detroit News, a part of the USA TODAY Network. "We will continue to monitor the situation as more information becomes available, but we are unable to comment further due to the on-going case."

According to the Pittsfield Township Police & Fire Department release, the department was contacted on Feb. 2 by a female food service delivery driver who claimed that during a delivery, the door was answered by a man who had a blanket draped over his shoulders and was unclothed beneath it. As the complainant turned to leave, the man pulled the blanket apart, exposing his genitals.

After she left the residence, the complainant said she received a message from the customer in which he said that she was attractive and asked if she would return to the address.

According to online court records, the incident occurred on Feb. 1. Pittsfield police officers contacted the male, who confirmed he received the delivery and subsequently was identified as the suspect. The male is listed as 18 years old at the time of the incident. Marsh turned 19 on Feb. 2.

As a freshman last season, Marsh was Michigan's leading wide receiver, catching 45 passes for 651 yards and four touchdowns. This season, the Texas native has 48 receptions for 459 yards and a touchdown.

The charge against Marsh is the latest bit of bad news for a Michigan program that is enduring a disastrous first season under coach Kyle Whittingham. Heading into their Week 12 game against No. 4 UCLA, the Wolverines are 5-5 and have the eighth-worst scoring offense at the FBS level, averaging just 18.3 points per game. If Michigan is unable to beat UCLA or No. 17 Ohio State, it will finish a full season with a losing record for the first time since 2014.
