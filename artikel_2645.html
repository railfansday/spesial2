‘College athletics will all have to take notice’: Former LSU head coach predicts that Tigers will be expelled from SEC

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

On Monday, the SEC announced that it had "filed an amended complaint and motion for a temporary restraining order in its federal lawsuit against Louisiana State University, stating that the presidents and chancellors of the SEC will meet on Thursday, December 3, to deliberate LSU's ongoing membership in the Conference."

The LSU Tigers football and basketball programs have both attempted to roster former professional players, and while football head coach Lane Kiffin ultimately waved the white flag and didn't roster the players, basketball coach Will Wade played RJ Luis in a game before ultimately deciding it was everyone's best interest for him not to remain on the roster.

With the vote looming, former Tigers head football coach Brian Kelly, who was fired from the team last season and replaced by Lane Kiffin, believes that his former program is going to get the boot from its conference.

"This has gone to the point where they will have a meeting on December 3rd. The presidents will vote, and I'll actually be surprised if they don't vote them out," Kelly said on SiriusXM.

"Now, look, you know, they've asked him to come, you know, and meet them halfway a couple of times. I think, I think the Luis situation and playing him in the game went against any kind of good faith that they had. And I think this is going to go right down to a vote. And I think college football, college athletics will all have to take notice of where we are and where we want to go."

In September, the SEC had a formal meeting "to discuss recent matters involving conference expectations and responsibilities" and released a statement saying that "those discussions will continue as the conference and its member universities work toward an appropriate resolution," but decided at the time not to punish LSU.

However, Wade playing Luis in a game seems to have reinvigorated the other members of the conference to pursue punishment.

"Monday's filing advances the Conference's obligation to defend its governance framework and the shared interests of its member universities," the SEC's statement concluded.
