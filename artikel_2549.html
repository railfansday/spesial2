Which of Ole Miss football's 3 injured RBs Pete Golding said could return

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

OXFORD − Ole Miss football isn't expecting to have to rely entirely on fourth-string running back Joshua Dye for the remainder of the season.

A loss to the Sooners eliminated the Rebels (6-4, 4-4 SEC) from the College Football Playoff picture.

Because of injuries, the Rebels were missing their top three running backs against OU. No. 1 running back Kewan Lacy is out for the season with a shoulder injury. Coach Pete Golding said Nov. 17 that Lacy has already had a procedure done to get fixed.

No. 2 Makhi Frazier was out with a thumb injury against OU, and No. 3 Shekai Mills-Knight exited in the middle of the game with a high-ankle injury.

The Rebels will host Wofford (5-6) on Nov. 21 (11 a.m., SEC Network+) and Mississippi State (4-6, 2-6 SEC) on Nov. 27. Golding said Frazier is likely to return from his thumb injury vs Wofford.

"Hopefully that shouldn't be an issue this week and all and I would expect him to be available," Golding said.

The timeline for Mills-Knight to return is less clear. The sophomore has led Ole Miss with 163 rushing yards over the past three weeks. Golding said Mills-Knight got an X-ray at halftime against the Sooners and was ruled out.

"We're still evaluating the Shekai situation," Golding said. "It's a high-ankle. Week-to-week. I think he's a game-time decision at best."

Dye had just seven carries in 2026 before the game against OU, when he rushed for 58 yards and a touchdown on 11 carries. Golding said Dye brought a spark.

"That's a guy that's consistently done it right, and he's a guy that we know we can count on to do things right both in protection and the run game," Golding said. "I think it was good for him to get in, and he came in and responded. Didn't look different, didn't feel different."

Dye is a transfer from Southern Utah. He was a star for the FCS Thunderbirds, and rushed for 1,831 yards and 28 touchdowns. He said he's always had confidence that he could produce at the SEC level.

"I'm not the type of person that judges the level of play that you are in," Dye said. "If you are a baller, you are a baller. At any level. It's really just the confidence and momentum that you have."
