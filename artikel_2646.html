Serena Williams’ former coach has noticed two big changes in Alexander Zverev as he becomes world number one

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Alexander Zverev finally reached the summit of the ATP rankings after years of being a nearly man in men's tennis.

Zverev had cemented his status as the greatest player never to win a major up until Roland Garros earlier this season, where he secured his first Grand Slam title. After his triumph in Paris, the German reached his first Wimbledon final before clinching major number two at the US Open and ultimately becoming the world number one, rising above injured Jannik Sinner.

Zverev has faced criticism from pundits and fans alike since becoming the world number one, with some claiming he doesn't deserve to sit atop the rankings due to his poor record against top-ten players and being winless against Carlos Alcaraz and Jannik Sinner in 2026.

Others have come to the defence of Zverev, however, including Serena Williams' former coach Patrick Mouratoglou. In a post on Instagram, Mouratoglou said: "Is Zverev better now than he was one year ago? Oh yes.

"For two reasons. First, his serve has progressed a lot. It's huge now; huge! The percentage of first serves is huge, the accuracy is huge, the speed is huge, the second serve is huge, everything is huge.

"Secondly, yes, he is not the guy who has the ability to accelerate suddenly and make a big difference like Alcaraz, but if you look at the pace of the rallies, I think that Zverev is playing way faster than he was one year ago, and the big reason for that is because of his position.

"He was two metres behind the baseline, and now he is just behind the baseline. He didn't do this overnight, but he worked on it a lot to be able to position himself way closer to the baseline, and he still doesn't make a lot of unforced errors, so he is super consistent while playing very close to the baseline at the same pace as before. So technically, because he is closer to the baseline, the rhythm of the rallies is way higher than before."

Zverev became the first German player in men's tennis to reach the top of the rankings since Boris Becker in 1991. He also set the record for the fewest titles won by a year-end number one in a season with two.
