Felix Auger-Aliassime speaks after losing for Canada at the Davis Cup and confirms plans for 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Felix Auger-Aliassime has suffered defeat for Canada in their Davis Cup Finals quarterfinal against Czechia.

Tomas Machac beat Canada's Liam Draxl in the opening match of the tie, before Auger-Aliassime lost to Jiri Lehecka. After a gruesome two-hour battle in Bologna, the Czech ace won 7-6(7-4), 6-2. It followed the opening one-hour, 55-minute clash, which Machac won 6-2, 3-6, 6-2.

Czechia now meet Korea or home favourites Italy in the Davis Cup semifinals, with Auger-Aliassime and his teammates left wondering where it all went wrong. The Canadian has since spoken about the defeat, having also confirmed his plans for the 2027 season.

Speaking to the media in Bologna, Auger-Aliassime was asked if he felt physically fit going into the second set after a first set that lasted over an hour, and indeed in light of his recent knee injury.

"I mean, first credit to him," he said of his opponent. "He played a great match. This is not an individual competition; it's a team competition. You try your best to represent your country.

"I've had a lot of joy, a lot of luck in the past with Team Canada. I feel like things fell my way a lot of times. It wasn't the case today. Of course, I mean, I can't deny the reality, which is that I need to keep working, get back into rhythm.

"Yeah, it was a gruelling first set. Maybe there was just a bit of a dip on my end at the start of the second. I tried to fight back, but he was pretty locked in. Yeah, tough to recover after that."

And when asked to look ahead to 2027 after "ups and downs" throughout 2026, he commented: "I mean, first physically there's some work to do. I think a lot of those ups and downs, as you said, are due to physical issues at times. Some big ones, some minor ones. I think the challenge for me is first there.

"In terms of my game, I want to see real changes in my game. I'm hungry to make the right adjustments in my game going forward. I know if I just do the same thing, my best ranking will be whatever it was in the past, but it won't get any better. There's nothing to lose for me there. It's just trying to push, trying to improve, trying to see areas where I can do better."

The 26-year-old was making his first appearance since the Shanghai Masters in October, when he retired hurt during his last 16 meeting with Casper Ruud. Incidentally, Auger-Aliassime struggled with a back injury earlier in the season too.

Climbing to ATP number four in June this year, the Canadian will end the 2026 season 17th in the world. He will hang up his racket for 2026 with a 37-18 win-loss record, and with one title to his name from Montpellier.

At Grand Slam level, the ATP superstar fell in the first and second rounds of the Australian Open and the US Open respectively, but reached the quarterfinals of both the French Open and Wimbledon. He ended his 2026 major season with nine wins and four losses.
