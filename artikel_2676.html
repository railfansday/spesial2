Kansas State Football: Depth Chart/Stats/Grades vs TCU – Offense

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Dillon Duff: Snaps – 59 – Comp – 22/33 – Yds – 244 – Td – 1 – Rush – 10 – Yds – -7

Duff looked much better in this game than he did in his first start against Colorado. The game plan focused mostly on short and intermediate routes, but he found a wide-open Jaron Tibbs for a 53-yard touchdown. I was surprised, like most people who have followed Kansas State, that Duff was asked to throw 33 passes, while the backs only received a combined 14 carries.

For a quarterback starting his second game, Duff was perfectly functional. Was it enough to convince me that he's "the man" moving foward?

No, but I'm still willing to listen after this performance.

Rodney Fields Jr.: Snaps – 39 – Att – 9 – Yds – 33 – Rec – 1 – Yds – 5

Joe Jackson: Snaps – 25 – Att – 5 – Yds – 33 – Rec – 1 – Yds – 4

If Kansas State had faith in their running game, they would probably use it more, given the quarterback injuries. Needless to say, that faith doesn't exist. Kansas State can't win games with replacement-level running backs, and that's what these guys have been this season, with a few exceptions.

After watching Deuce, DJ, and even that little guy on Kansas turning nothing into something, and something into touchdowns, this group gets what the play is blocked for and not much else. This program was built on dynamic running backs, and these guys don't have the explosive runs that make this offense hum. Sure, Joe Jackson may have one or two games a season where he slips into a fugue state and looks like an NFL back; he tends to follow up those eruptions by disappearing for several games. He's also one of the worst pass blockers I've ever seen at running back. I miss Deuce Vaughn.

I think Fields was supposed to be the lead back, but he hasn't shown me the burst and wiggle to make it work. Yeah, it's cool to watch him truck a safety, but you know what I'd rather see? I'd like to see him make a safety miss and turn an 8-yard run into a 20+ yard run. When Fields manages to get some space, he looks for someone to run into instead of looking for a path to the endzone.

This position group needs an upgrade in the offseason.

Josh Manning: Snaps – 52 – Targets – 4 – Rec – 2 – Yds – 2

Jaron Tibbs: Snaps – 59 – Targets – 12 – Rec – 8 – Yds – 115 – Td – 1

Derrick Salley Jr.: Snaps – 52- Targets – 5 – Rec – 2 – Yds – 32

Tibbs is steady. For me, he's a poor man's version of former NFL wide receiver DeAndre Hopkins. He can make a catch in traffic and doesn't mind taking a big hit for a big gain. What he lacks is Nuk's ability to shake off tacklers and turn a 5-yard reception into a 15-yard reception. His stats are a bit inflated because TCU forgot he was on the field, and Duff managed to loop a 53-yard touchdown reception into his waiting hands for a walk-in touchdown. When he's not left completely alone by the defense, he's a guy who makes the catch and gets tackled. He doesn't create much separation, but his size and catch radius allow him to make contested catches. I'd like to see him work on his route running this offseason, which is also what made Hopkins an elite NFL receiver despite his lack of top-end speed or agility.

This wasn't a 2-touchdown performance from Salley Jr., like last week, but I think he's another piece of the puzzle for next season. Like Tibbs, he's not going to run by corners, or even around corners, but he can run over them. When Kansas State isolates him as the boundary receiver, he puts the defense in a bind. I'd love to see Duff give him a couple more chances on deep balls when he's single-covered.

Manning, on the other hand, hasn't convinced me he's a starting-caliber receiver. He's on the field the majority of the time, but I don't really notice him. It looks like the coaching staff is trying to use him as their speed guy, but I haven't seen any indication that's who he is on the field. I wouldn't mind keeping him, but out of the 3 Wildcat wide receivers, he's the one I'm the least sold on heading into next season.

This group needs a massive infusion of speed. Actually, I take that back. They need a massive speed infusion paired with a receiver who doesn't have ping-pong paddle hands. This group is too one-note for my taste. Someone needs to be able to make a play in the open field.

Garrett Oakley: Snaps – 49 – Targets – 8 – Rec – 6 – Yds – 60

Linkon Cure: Snaps – 16 – Targets – 2 – Rec – 2 – Yds – 26

After one of his worst games in a Kansas State uniform last week, Oakley returned to his reliable self in this one. He caught everything that hit his hands, with 3 of his 6 receptions ending in a first down. Much like the receivers, he'll get what's available on most plays, but he's not a tight end who can get deep and stretch the field. This is a team full of possession receivers.

That brings me to Linkon Cure. After a brief glimpse of his potential a couple of games ago, he's returned to his role as an afterthought in the offense. I don't get it. This team desperately needs a playmaker, and Cure gets paid to make plays. He's not a good blocker or a great route runner, but if you can get the ball in his hands, he can make a play. I've been disappointed with how he's been used this season. I was expecting a little more creativity from the coaching staff in getting the ball into Cure's hands with space to operate. I get that Oakley is the clear-cut starter, but they're paying Cure a bunch of money to put up TE2-level stats.

The pass blocking was much better. Duff took 3 sacks, but 2 of those were a product of Duff holding onto the ball too long.

The issue is that Kansas State needs to run the ball, and this group isn't opening enough holes for backs. In theory, all 5 of these guys could return next season. Normally, that's a good thing, but I'm not sure I trust this group to improve their run blocking enough to make this offense work.

Again, maybe this isn't so much on the offensive line as it is on the running backs? Deuce and DJ made the offensive line look good, and Fields and Jackson haven't. Still, I loved diagramming the Wildcat's gap-scheme runs with multiple back-side pullers blasting holes in an opposing defensive line, and I haven't seen a need to do that this season.

It'll be interesting to see how the staff handles a functional line that struggles to open holes in the run game. This group isn't bad, but are they good enough to run it back, hoping experience and an improved running back stable will fix the run game next season?
