UNC Football has its worst report card of the year against Virginia

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

It's been a season of close calls for UNC Football, whether the Tar Heels came out on the right side or the wrong one, from Game 1 onwards… but in Charlottesville this Sunday, that ended. The University of Virginia continued to be a house of horrors for UNC, as some mistakes, penalties, and breakdowns all combined to give the Heels a beatdown at the hands of the Wahoos and keep UNC one win away from bowl eligibility for at least one week longer. Predictably, in UNC's worst loss of the year, the gradebook also looks as bad as it has all year (this column's been absent for a few weeks, but I'm not sure any group other than defensive backs would have earned a grade worse than C+ for any of the previous 4 games). For Bill Belichick and his team's penultimate game of the season, here it is.

Travis Burgess got the start this week, after Billy Edwards was deemed unable to go following his injury last weekend. In his first full game of college action, Burgess was… uneven. Things started off poorly for him, as he missed a couple of throws over the middle and then tossed what I think the kids are calling a "meatball sub" to a UVA defender who was trailing Jelani Thurman near the end of the first quarter. He did settle into the game a little as the first half went on, headlined by a bomb down the sideline to Jordan Shipp that allowed him to settle down in the Cover-2 hole. He started the second half off with a bomb down the middle to Mason Humphrey that might have been his best throw of the day, but didn't have the consistency he needed throw-to-throw, placing balls either slightly behind or slightly too high for his receivers too often, and eventually, Bobby Petrino took a lot of the responsibility out of his hands and dialed up mostly throws to the perimeter. Some of that could have been jitters, more of it was clearly caused by a lack of reps with his pass-catchers (on several occasions, you could see the ball beating receivers' hands to the spot, showing they didn't know the velocity his passes were coming with), but it was there nonetheless.

He finished the day 25/45 for 354 yards, a touchdown, and two interceptions, though his last pick was basically meaningless. While the actual execution left a little to be desired, he also showed a lot of tools and traits that make it easy to see why the program was so excited about him: the ball comes off his hand effortlessly even as he fires it into tight windows, he moves well in the pocket, and he showed a pretty mature understanding of the game. Burgess also ran for 112 yards on 12 non-sack carries, and it was as a runner that he was most electric. Every time he carried the ball, he was the fastest player on the field, destroying pursuit angles and converting first downs when it looked like he might be dead to rights.

Benjamin Hall had 7 rushes and 6 of them constituted wins for the offense, and Jaylen McGill wasn't far behind with his 6 rushes for 5 successes. They combined for 95 yards on those 13 carries, finding seams and working through UVA tackle attempts in one of their better efforts on the season. Because Burgess had so many carries himself, they didn't get the ball as much as they might have in any of UNC's other games (and also, this was a three-possession game quickly into the 2nd half, which made rushing the ball kind of a lost cause). Kaleb Jackson saw his first action of the season as a short-yardage back and looked capable, scoring a touchdown after two attempts from the 2-yard line. Demon June failed two short-yardage 4th down attempts, but he was met by unblocked defenders in the backfield both times, so it wasn't really his fault.

Just like it was hard on Burgess not knowing his pass-catchers' tendencies and preferences, it was clearly hard on the pass-catchers not being on the same page as their quarterback. A group that's been so good at erasing their quarterback's mistakes all season just wasn't able to do it this time. There were more than a handful of balls that might have been slightly behind, or ahead, or too high, but catchable, but while these receivers have been able to anticipate and adjust to that kind of miss all season, they didn't have that same sense and ability with another quarterback at the helm. That didn't stop them from making a few plays. As is the case every week, Jordan Shipp led the group with 9 catches on 16 targets for 149 yards. With Jelani Thurman exiting the game due to injury after his only catch of the game, Shipp was the only go-to guy on the offense; nobody else was targeted more than 5 times. Mason Humphrey's catch on the aforementioned deep ball was his best play of the season, as he went up over a defensive back to grab it in stride.

A couple of key mistakes don't help here. Shipp was called for not being set while a running back was in motion on a play that should have been a touchdown, and Carson Sneed and Shemar Easter completely whiffed on blocks that allowed fourth-down conversion attempts to be stopped in the backfield.

On one hand, Burgess was sacked just once and the two primary running backs combined to average over 7 yards a carry, so overall the offensive line performed pretty well. I'd ordinarily blame them for the Heels failing as many short-yardage opportunities as they did, but even those were more down to tight ends in jumbo packages than the offensive linemen themselves. On the other hand, the play on the field, down to down, wasn't quite as good as all that sounds, and a lot of those yards came in what was effectively garbage time. In relief of guard Aidan Banfield, out of the game with injury, Christian Alvarez played most of the game. I've thought he stood out negatively in most of his rotational appearances this season, and while he was a little better with prolonged action, it wasn't by much.

UNC was playing without Melkart Abou-Jaoude, but that still isn't really an excuse for how stonewalled the UNC defense was in the trenches. Xavier Lewis, Leroy Jackson, and Jaylen Harvey racked up tackles, with 10, 7, and 7 apiece, but a lot of those came after they'd been blown 3 yards downfield. Harvey had UNC's only two hurries of the day; UVA quarterback Beau Pribula had all day most of the game besides a few plays and, as we'll see below, he made the Heels pay. Xavier Brown ran for 83 yards on 13 carries, which was plenty enough damage, but the real mess was in the passing game, where the UNC defensive line just did not make an impact.

I'm simply baffled by Virginia's second touchdown, a 75-yard catch-and-run by Jekail Middlebrook, a running back lined up out wide. Derek McDonald was lined up opposite him, and as Middlebrook went deep, McDonald squared back to the quarterback as if he thought there was either a safety behind him or a corner outside him… but he was both the furthest outside and the deepest player on the field before the snap. That's not the kind of mistake that you expect McDonald, whose best ability is his field awareness, to make, but it also wasn't the only time something like this happened to a UNC linebacker.

The same thing happened at least once to Timir Hickman-Collins. It's one thing that UNC's linebackers got beat a lot by running backs coming out of the backfield; that's an issue but it's something that'll happen to a lot of teams. Not knowing your assignment, though… that's linebacker responsibility #1 with a bullet. Combine that with a mediocre tackling game from most of the guys, particularly Peyton Seelmann, and the middle of UNC's defense caused a lot of problems for the Heels.

Some people thought that Jaiden Patterson was going to be these Heels' defensive Jordan Shipp; a player who had shined in spots his first two years as a Tar Heel and was ready to be a plus starter. I was a little bearish on that prospect; Shipp looked like a really good player who was just stuck behind some other guys, while Patterson was only ever about replacement-level in the snaps he got. Replacement-level was impressive for a player in his position, but now that he's a full-time player, he's had it rough. Louisville targeted him all game last week, and Pribula, too, found quite a bit of success going after him. He gave up Virginia's first score and a few big catches besides. But he wasn't alone. A rotating cast of Heels struggled mightily to replace safety Greg Smith in the first half, and once Smith returned to the field after serving his suspension, he, too, missed a couple of plays, including leaving a running back alone out of the backfield for the touchdown that broke the game open. This group has been UNC's weakest for several weeks after looking like a strength early on, and combined with the pass rush not getting home, it's not a winning formula.

Rece Verhoff has regressed badly from his form a season ago, when the only miss of his season inside 50 yards was a blocked kick. Blocks can be somewhat on the kicker, but he was accurate and reliable all year on pretty decent volume. This year's been rough for him, and that continued this game. His first attempt of the game was blocked, he made his second one, and then he badly missed a 36-yarder. The rest of UNC's special teams units didn't have a lot to do. Aeron Burrell's only punt of the game wasn't very good, but it wasn't really notable, either.

At one point, the analyst on the broadcast said that Middlebrook's 4 catches for 120-odd yards (he finished with 5 for 140) was a "Randy Moss-level" statline, and that kind of sums it up. Again and again UVA showed their intention to use running backs as weapons through the air, whether it was out of the backfield or lined up out wide, and again and again UNC defenders at the second and third levels looked completely unprepared for it. The lack of coaching adjustments was pretty shocking for a staff that's been pretty good, defensively at least, at self-scouting in-game. Offensively, I think they probably went away from the run a little too quickly; I've said it before, but running in college is significantly more efficient than running in the NFL (averaging 7+ yards a carry over 13 carries is only going to happen in college). Asking Burgess to throw 45 times in his first game was probably a little much.
