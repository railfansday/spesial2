Tyson Foods is restoring beef production at its Texas plant ahead of a planned rancher deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The company expects the Amarillo plant's second shift to be in full operation by summer 2027 and has begun restoring some operations

Tyson Foods is moving forward with plans to reopen the second production shift at its Amarillo, Texas, beef plant ahead of a planned long-term partnership with Allied Beef Company, a consortium of American ranchers.

The company has begun restoring select second-shift operations and expects the shift to reach full operation by summer 2027. Tyson said the reopening will raise output and bring additional jobs to the area. It did not give job figures, according to Bloomberg.

Tyson said it intends to establish the partnership, which would link the company with American ranchers and create "a more economically sustainable model across the supply chain."

"The future of our beef business depends on strong partnerships across the supply chain," Tyson Chief Operating Officer Wes Morris said in a statement. Allied Beef said the planned partnership offers an opportunity to create long-term value for ranchers.

An Allied Beef spokesperson told Bloomberg the company "works across all stages of cattle production, including both multigenerational ranch and feedlot operations." Packers such as Tyson generally get their cattle through feedlots, which in turn obtain them from other ranchers at auction sales, according to Bloomberg.

Tyson scaled the Amarillo plant back to one shift in January, laying off 1,700 workers and trimming its expected daily throughput by upwards of 2,500 head of cattle. The company also closed its Lexington, Nebraska, beef plant this year. Its beef segment has posted an operating loss every quarter since early 2024, according to Bloomberg.

In August, Tyson announced plans to close two beef facilities and pursue the sale of a third. It said it would consolidate its beef business around plants in Dakota City, Nebraska; Holcomb, Kansas; and Amarillo, and would add a second shift at Amarillo as cattle supply allows.

Tyson CEO Donnie King cited U.S. Department of Agriculture data showing limited signs that ranchers are expanding their herds. He said that indicated supply constraints are likely to persist. Tyson projected an adjusted operating loss of $500 million to $650 million for its beef segment in fiscal 2026.

Tyson is scheduled to report fourth-quarter earnings on Nov. 16, according to Bloomberg.
