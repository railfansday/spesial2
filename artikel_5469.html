Central banks bought gold at fastest pace since 2024, WGC data shows

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Purchases reached 321 tonnes in the third quarter, lifting year-to-date buying 6% above last year's level

One kilogram gold bars and a five hundred gram gold bar in April 2025 (Angel Garcia/Bloomberg via Getty Images)

Central banks bought 321 tonnes of gold in the third quarter, the World Gold Council said on Tuesday.

That was the fastest pace of central bank buying since late 2024, according to The Wall Street Journal. Purchases for the first nine months of the year total 675 tonnes, up 6% from a year earlier, the council said.

The council said third-quarter buying exceeded recent typical quarterly levels. Central banks bought 226.3 tonnes in the same quarter of 2025, according to Mining Zimbabwe.

China and Poland led the way among buyers that disclosed their purchases, according to The Wall Street Journal. "Central banks remained an important pillar of demand," said Louise Street, senior market analyst at the WGC, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Other parts of the market moved differently, the council said. Total gold demand, including over-the-counter trading, slipped 1% from a year earlier to 1,369 tonnes. Gold-backed exchange-traded funds added 211 tonnes after outflows in the second quarter. The council said the inflows appeared to reflect worry that public finances may prove unsustainable and that prices could keep climbing.

Jewelry demand fell 17% to 313 tonnes, the lowest reading for any third quarter in the series the council tracks, as high prices remained a headwind. Bar and coin demand slid 19% to 268 tonnes as buyers held off until the path of prices grew clearer. Technology demand rose 3% from a year earlier, driven by AI-related manufacturing.

Gold has slipped under $4,000 per troy ounce over the past few days. Prices stood at more than $5,000 an ounce ahead of the Iran war's outbreak in late February.

The WGC expects central bank purchases for the full year to equal or exceed last year’s total, after buying in the second and third quarters came in stronger than it had forecast, according to Mining Zimbabwe.
