What is an EGOT? 10 stars who've won an Emmy, Grammy, Oscar and Tony

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Meet 10 EGOT winners who earned an Emmy, Grammy, Oscar and Tony, and learn what the rare entertainment grand slam means and what each star won

An EGOT means one person has won an Emmy, a Grammy, an Oscar and a Tony. Each of those four awards covers a different part of the entertainment business. Emmys go to television, Grammys to recorded music, Oscars — formally the Academy Awards — to film, and Tonys to Broadway theater. Collecting all four requires success in fields with separate audiences, separate production systems and separate voters.

Only competitive wins count toward the title. An honorary award does not complete the set. That rule keeps the group small. It also explains why some performers with shelves of trophies still fall outside it. An artist can lead one field for decades and never earn a qualifying win in another.

The routes to the milestone vary. Some winners are composers and songwriters whose work moved between the stage, the screen and the recording studio. Others are actors who added a Grammy for a spoken-word recording or an audiobook to their acting honors. Producing credits count too. Jennifer Hudson completed her set in 2022 with a Tony as a producer of A Strange Loop.

The timeline stretches across more than 60 years. Richard Rodgers is generally recorded as the first person to complete an EGOT, finishing in 1962. Rita Moreno and Helen Hayes both completed their sets in 1977. John Legend finished his in 2018 and became the first Black man to reach the milestone. Viola Davis followed in 2023, and Elton John completed his set with an Emmy in 2024.

This slideshow profiles 10 EGOT winners, a selection rather than the complete list or a ranking. Each slide sets out the four awards behind one person's set, with titles and years where the record is clear. The group includes film actors, a Broadway composer, a comedy writer and director, and pop musicians. Their wins span Broadway musicals, film dramas, live television concerts and audiobooks.

Richard Rodgers is generally recorded as the first person to win an EGOT. The Broadway composer completed the set in 1962, when he won an Emmy for his music for Winston Churchill: The Valiant Years.

The Emmy was the final piece. Rodgers had already won the other three awards through work in film, recorded music and the stage. Nearly all of it grew out of the same body of songwriting.

His Oscar came first. Rodgers won the Academy Award for best original song for the 1945 film State Fair. The movie was a Hollywood musical with a score by Rodgers and his lyricist partner, Oscar Hammerstein II. It was the only musical the pair wrote directly for the screen.

His Tony Awards came from Broadway, where he built his career. Rodgers wrote the music for a long list of stage shows. With Lorenz Hart, his first major collaborator, he wrote musicals including The Boys from Syracuse and Pal Joey.

With Hammerstein, he wrote Oklahoma!, Carousel, South Pacific, The King and I and The Sound of Music. South Pacific also earned Rodgers a share of the Pulitzer Prize for Drama. That fifth major honor places him in a smaller group sometimes called PEGOT winners.

The Grammy traced back to the stage as well. Rodgers won it for the original cast album of The Sound of Music. Cast recordings carried Broadway scores to listeners who never saw the shows in a theater.

The Emmy came from a different kind of project. Winston Churchill: The Valiant Years was a television documentary series about the British wartime prime minister. Rodgers composed music for it.

He had worked in documentary television before. Rodgers wrote the music for Victory at Sea, an NBC series from the early 1950s about naval combat in World War II.

His later stage work changed after Hammerstein died in 1960. Rodgers wrote both the music and the lyrics for No Strings, which opened on Broadway in 1962 — the same year he completed his EGOT.

Rodgers was born in New York City in 1902 and died in 1979.

Helen Hayes completed her EGOT in 1977, when she won a Grammy for a spoken-word recording. By then she already held two Oscars, one for The Sin of Madelon Claudet and one for Airport. She also had Emmy and Tony wins from her television and stage work.

Her two film awards sit at opposite ends of a long career. The Sin of Madelon Claudet, released in 1931, earned her the Academy Award for best actress at the ceremony held in 1932. In the film she plays a woman who makes years of personal sacrifices to pay for her son's upbringing and education.

Airport brought her a second Oscar, for best supporting actress, at the ceremony held in 1971. She played Ada Quonsett, an elderly woman who sneaks onto flights as a stowaway. The two Oscar ceremonies took place 39 years apart.

The theater was her main base. Hayes was born in Washington, D.C., in 1900 and began acting as a child. She became known as the "First Lady of the American Theatre." Her title role as Queen Victoria in Victoria Regina, which opened on Broadway in 1935, became one of her best-known stage performances.

Her Tony record reaches back to the start of the awards. She won best actress in a play at the first Tony Awards in 1947 for Happy Birthday. She won a second Tony in 1958 for Time Remembered. The award is named for Antoinette Perry, a stage actress and director who died in 1946.

Television added the Emmy. She won the award for best actress in 1953, in the medium's early years, when much TV drama aired live.

The Grammy came last. It honored a spoken-word recording rather than music. She was 76 when the win arrived. It made her the second person to complete an EGOT, after composer Richard Rodgers.

Hayes died in 1993. A Broadway house on West 44th Street was renamed in her honor in 1983. It now operates as the Hayes Theater.

Rita Moreno completed her EGOT in 1977, when she won an Emmy. Her run began with an Oscar for West Side Story (1961). A Grammy followed in 1972, and a Tony came in 1975.

The Oscar recognized her performance as Anita in the film version of the Broadway musical. She won in the best supporting actress category. Moreno was born in Puerto Rico, and the win made her the first Latina actress to receive an Academy Award.

Her Grammy came from children's television rather than film or the stage. Moreno was a cast member on The Electric Company, a PBS series built to teach reading skills to young viewers. A recording tied to the show earned her the Grammy in 1972.

Broadway supplied the third award. Moreno won a Tony in 1975 for The Ritz, a Terrence McNally farce set in a New York bathhouse. She played Googie Gomez, a nightclub singer with large ambitions and limited talent. She returned to the role in the 1976 film version, directed by Richard Lester.

Television completed the set. Her 1977 Emmy came for a guest appearance on The Muppet Show. A year later, she won a second Emmy for The Rockford Files, the detective series starring James Garner.

The acronym lists the awards in a fixed sequence, but winners can collect them in any order. Moreno's sequence was Oscar, Grammy, Tony and then Emmy. Each came from a different kind of work: a film musical, a children's recording, a stage comedy and a network television guest spot.

Her honors extend beyond the four competitive awards. President George W. Bush presented her with the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2004. She has also received the National Medal of Arts, and she was a Kennedy Center honoree in 2015.

West Side Story returned to her career decades later. Steven Spielberg's 2021 film adaptation cast her as Valentina, a character written for the new version. She also served as an executive producer on that film, 60 years after the original adaptation that won her the Oscar.

Audrey Hepburn is an EGOT winner. She won an Emmy, a Grammy, an Oscar and a Tony.

All four of Hepburn's awards were competitive wins, which places her among the performers who hold the full set.

Mel Brooks is an EGOT winner whose Oscar and Tonys trace back to one story: The Producers. He won the Academy Award for the film's screenplay. He later won Tonys for the Broadway musical adaptation, which opened in 2001.

Brooks wrote and directed The Producers as a 1967 film comedy. It follows a failing Broadway producer and a timid accountant who scheme to profit from a guaranteed flop. Their chosen show is a musical called Springtime for Hitler. Zero Mostel and Gene Wilder played the two leads.

The film was Brooks's first feature as a director. His Oscar recognized the original screenplay he wrote for it. That made it a writing award rather than a directing or acting honor.

Brooks adapted the story for the stage decades later. He wrote the music and lyrics and co-wrote the book with Thomas Meehan. Nathan Lane and Matthew Broderick starred. Susan Stroman directed and choreographed the production.

The Broadway production won 12 Tony Awards. No show has won more. Brooks took home Tonys of his own for his work on the musical. That tied the theater leg of his EGOT to the same title as his film award.

His Emmys come from television, a medium that shaped his early career. Brooks wrote for Sid Caesar's Your Show of Shows in the 1950s. He later co-created the spy spoof Get Smart with Buck Henry. His Emmy wins include awards for guest appearances as Uncle Phil on the NBC sitcom Mad About You.

He also holds a Grammy, drawn from a long recording history in comedy. That body of work includes the 2000 Year Old Man routines he performed with Carl Reiner.

Brooks also made the film comedies Blazing Saddles and Young Frankenstein, both released in 1974. The Producers itself returned to movie screens in 2005. That year the stage musical was adapted into a film, with Lane and Broderick reprising their roles.

Whoopi Goldberg is an EGOT winner whose four competitive awards came from comedy recording, film acting, daytime television and Broadway producing. She won her Oscar for the 1990 film Ghost and completed the set in 2002.

The first of the four was a Grammy for recorded comedy. Goldberg won the 1986 Grammy for best comedy recording for the album of her one-woman Broadway show. Mike Nichols produced that show. It was a series of character monologues, and it introduced her to a national audience in the mid-1980s.

Her first major screen role came in the same period. She played Celie in Steven Spielberg's The Color Purple, released in 1985. The performance won her a Golden Globe for best actress in a drama. It also earned a best actress Oscar nomination, though she did not win that award.

Ghost brought the Academy Award. Goldberg played Oda Mae Brown, a fraudulent psychic who discovers she can hear a murdered man's spirit. Patrick Swayze and Demi Moore played the couple at the center of the story. The role won her the Oscar for best supporting actress, along with the Golden Globe in the same category. Ghost was also the highest-grossing film worldwide in 1990.

That Oscar made her the second Black woman to receive an acting Oscar. The first was Hattie McDaniel, who won for Gone with the Wind.

The final two pieces of her EGOT arrived in the same year. Goldberg won a Daytime Emmy in 2002 for Beyond Tara: The Extraordinary Life of Hattie McDaniel, a documentary special she hosted. The film profiled the same actress whose Oscar had come before hers.

She also won a Tony in 2002 as one of the producers of Thoroughly Modern Millie. The musical, starring Sutton Foster, took the Tony for best musical. That award goes to a show's producers. As a result, Goldberg's name went on a Broadway trophy without a performing credit.

Daytime television became a larger part of her career afterward. Goldberg became moderator of ABC's The View in 2007. In 2009, she and her co-hosts shared the Daytime Emmy for outstanding talk show host.

John Legend completed his EGOT in 2018, when he won an Emmy as a producer of NBC's Jesus Christ Superstar Live in Concert. That win made him the first Black man to hold competitive Emmy, Grammy, Oscar and Tony awards.

The Emmy was for outstanding variety special (live). NBC aired the production on Easter Sunday, April 1, 2018. Legend played Jesus in the live staging of the rock opera by Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice. He was also one of its producers. That credit put his name on the Emmy. The award was presented at the Creative Arts Emmys in September 2018.

Lloyd Webber and Rice shared that same Emmy as producers. The award completed their EGOTs as well. Three people reached the milestone through a single award on the same night.

Legend's path to the four awards began in music. He won the Grammy for best new artist in 2006, after the release of his debut album, Get Lifted. He added more Grammys in R&B categories in the years that followed.

His Oscar came from film. Legend and Common shared the Academy Award for best original song for "Glory." They wrote it for Selma, Ava DuVernay's 2014 drama about the 1965 voting rights marches in Alabama. The Oscar was presented in February 2015.

The Tony arrived in 2017. Legend was one of the producers of the Manhattan Theatre Club revival of August Wilson's Jitney. The production won the Tony for best revival of a play. Jitney belongs to Wilson's 10-play cycle about Black American life, with one play set in each decade of the 20th century. Its story follows drivers at an unlicensed cab station in Pittsburgh.

Two of Legend's four awards came through producing credits. The Tony and the Emmy both recognized his role in backing a production. His Grammy and Oscar recognized his own work as a singer and songwriter.

Jennifer Hudson completed her EGOT in 2022 with a Tony as a producer of the Broadway musical A Strange Loop. Her run of competitive wins began with an Oscar for Dreamgirls in 2007 and closed 15 years later.

Hudson first reached a national audience on American Idol in 2004. She finished seventh in the show's third season. Fantasia Barrino won that season. Hudson's major awards all came after that early exit.

The Oscar came for her first film role. In Dreamgirls, Hudson played Effie White, a singer pushed out of the lead spot in her own group. The film adapted the 1981 Broadway musical of the same name, and Bill Condon directed it. The Academy named her best supporting actress.

Her Grammy followed in 2009, two years after the Oscar. It went to her self-titled debut album, Jennifer Hudson, which won best R&B album. Its lead single was Spotlight.

Her other two awards came through producing rather than performing. Her Emmy was for Baba Yaga's Heart, a project she worked on as a producer. Her Tony came as one of the producers of A Strange Loop.

A Strange Loop is a musical by Michael R. Jackson. It follows Usher, a young Black queer writer who works as a Broadway usher while trying to finish a musical of his own. The show won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama in 2020, before it opened on Broadway. It won the Tony for best musical in 2022.

On Broadway, the Tony for best musical is presented to the show's producers. Each credited producer can count that win toward an EGOT without appearing on stage.

Hudson has continued to work in film and television since her Oscar. She played Aretha Franklin in the 2021 biopic Respect. She also hosts The Jennifer Hudson Show, a daytime talk program that premiered in 2022.

Viola Davis completed her EGOT in 2023, when the audiobook of her memoir Finding Me won a Grammy. That win joined an Oscar, two Tonys and an Emmy she had already earned for acting.

The Grammy came in the best audio book, narration and storytelling recording category. Davis narrated Finding Me herself. The memoir, published in 2022, recounts her childhood in poverty in Central Falls, Rhode Island. It also traces her path into acting.

Her Oscar came for Fences, the 2016 film adaptation of August Wilson's play. Denzel Washington directed the film and played Troy Maxson. Davis played his wife, Rose, and won best supporting actress at the Academy Awards ceremony in 2017.

Fences also accounts for one of her Tonys. Davis played Rose in the 2010 Broadway revival, again opposite Washington. That performance won her the Tony for best leading actress in a play.

Her first Tony came nine years earlier, for King Hedley II, another Wilson drama. That 2001 award was for best featured actress in a play. Both Tony-winning roles come from Wilson's cycle of 10 plays. Each play in the cycle is set in a different decade of the 20th century and depicts Black American life.

Television supplied the Emmy. Davis won outstanding lead actress in a drama series in 2015 for How to Get Away with Murder. She played Annalise Keating, a law professor and defense attorney, in the ABC legal drama. Davis was the first Black woman to win that Emmy category.

The 2017 Oscar gave her competitive acting awards from film, television and Broadway. That combination is often called the Triple Crown of Acting. Davis was the first Black performer to complete it.

The Grammy arrived six years after the Oscar and turned that acting triple crown into a full EGOT. Every award in her set recognizes her work as a performer. Her Emmy, Oscar and Tonys honored characters she played. The Grammy honored her reading of her own life story.

Elton John completed his EGOT in 2024, when Elton John Live: Farewell from Dodger Stadium won an Emmy. That television win joined the Oscar, Tony and Grammy awards he had already won.

The special captured the final North American concert of his Farewell Yellow Brick Road tour. The show took place at Dodger Stadium in Los Angeles in November 2022 and streamed live on Disney $DIS+. The venue had history for him. He played two sold-out shows there in October 1975, a run remembered for the sequined Dodgers uniform he wore on stage.

The program won in the outstanding variety special (live) category at the Creative Arts Emmys in September 2024. The award went to the special's producers, and John was among them. A producing win is a competitive Emmy. It counts toward the four-award set.

His first Oscar came from animation. "Can You Feel the Love Tonight," written with lyricist Tim Rice for Disney's The Lion King, won best original song at the Academy Awards in March 1995. He won a second Oscar in the same category in 2020. That one was for "(I'm Gonna) Love Me Again," written with his longtime lyricist Bernie Taupin for the biopic Rocketman.

Broadway supplied the Tony. John and Rice wrote the score for Aida, a stage musical built on the story of Giuseppe Verdi's opera. The show opened on Broadway in 2000 and won the Tony for best original score that year.

His Grammy wins include one for "Candle in the Wind 1997." The track reworked his 1973 song "Candle in the Wind," with new lyrics by Taupin, as a tribute to Diana, Princess of Wales. John performed it at her funeral at Westminster Abbey in September 1997. The recording won the Grammy for best male pop vocal performance in 1998.

Television was the last of the four categories he needed. The Emmy arrived 24 years after his Tony for Aida. It also came from a filmed concert rather than an acting role or a scripted series. His EGOT rests on songwriting for film and stage, a pop vocal recording and a live performance broadcast to a streaming audience.
