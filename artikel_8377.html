My Gear Is Ready. My Mind is Stoic. My Body? It’s Still in Negotiations!

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Preparing for a thru hike on Appalachian Trail truly begins with just one burning question;  “What will I need to walk 2200 miles?”   Pretty simple and straightforward right?  Theoretically, yes……that is. of course, until you discover the multitude of gear list videos.

Suddenly, you’re comparing sleeping bags by temperature rating, researching water filters like you’re prepping for the apocalypse, and obsessing over the ratio of weight and R-rating of every single sleeping pad in existence. Your hike is still months away, and you’ve already downloaded four GPS trail apps, scheduled transportation,  meticulously planned your daily mileage to the foot and plotted every daily campsite  down to the hour of  arrival.

Relax……You’re definitely not the first or the last hiker to become immersed (obsessed?) in the vortex that is thru hiking prep!  Perhaps I can break it down and make it a little more palatable

First comes the backpack. It should be light, durable, comfortable, and large enough to hold everything you need—which, according to differing opinions, is both nothing and several highly specialized pieces of equipment. First, try the pack on in the store. Bring your gear, (yes, really do this) and then look at the in the mirror to see how it sits.  Ask yourself, . Do you appear ready to summit a mountain or flee a small country?  Adjust your choice accordingly.  Lastly, never, ever choose your pack because of its popularity, the aesthetics, or because of any opinion from some internet blogger.

Then there’s your shelter. You want something weatherproof, spacious, easy to set up, and light enough that you won’t question your life choices by mile four. The salesperson assures you it’s simple to pitch. You proudly take it home and spend twenty five minutes just trying to decipher the pitching instructions, and end up with what, oddly, looks like a very expensive kite.!  Research. research, research!  Take your time and decide solely based on what YOUR hiking style looks like. With so many options out there, you’ll find that perfect home!

The choice of  deciding on a sleeping bag or quilt…..down or synthetic …….or temperature rating, is another personal decision.  When and where are you starting?  Average temperatures where you’ll be at that time of year?   Lightweight or no?  Inflatable pad or closed foam?  Answer these questions, and the decision should be much easier.

Food actually takes up surprisingly little space…..that is,  until you remember you’ll need to eat it every day. Focus on a balance of carbs, protein, and pleasure.

As a novice , I got some of those fancy dehydrated meals labeled “creamy pasta primavera,” or “Spaghetti with meat sauce”.  I added the correct amount of boiling water, and soon discovered that the phrase “creamy” or “meat” was creative writing at work. Still, in all fairness , it did have calories, and, in the end, calories are the fuel of the trail.

Clothing……now this decision becomes just another expedition into the unknown. Basically, you need layers for warmth, rain protection, sun protection, and the full understanding that your clothes may never again smell like laundry.

I pack two shirts; one for hiking, and one for sleeping, two pair of socks, two underwear, and one pair of long pants and one pair of shorts.  That’s it, because apparently I’m going into the wilderness, not to a four-day business conference.

Finally, you weigh your pack. It weighs enough to make you reconsider your plans, your friendships, and the person who told you hiking was “just walking.” You remove a book, a camp mug, and a backup pair of pants. Then you add them back because what if you want coffee while reading in clean pants?

Pick the gear you bring and the total weight of your full kit solely on what you’re honestly willing to carry.

The key, you’ll learn, is to pack what you need, test it before you leave, and accept that your first big purchase may be a tiny scale. The Appalachian Trail doesn’t care whether your gear matches. It cares whether you can carry it, use it, and keep walking when your feet have begun filing formal complaints !

So you shoulder your pack, take a few determined steps around the block, and feel like you’re ready for anything……..then you remember your food bag is still on the kitchen table.  Ugh!
