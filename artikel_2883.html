The Justice Department Is Investigating the Company at the Center of Nvidia’s Jensen Huang Scandal

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Justice and Commerce departments have confirmed a criminal investigation into Megaspeed.

The Justice Department and the Commerce Department confirmed in a joint statement on Monday that they are running a criminal investigation into Megaspeed, the Singapore-based AI company at the center of a scandal that led to the ouster of now-former Nvidia (NVDA -1.57%) CEO Jensen Huang.

The agencies said Commerce opened the case all the way back in 2024 after spotting "suspicious activity" surrounding the company, suspecting it may be involved in chip diversion -- getting Nvidia's most advanced chips to buyers who aren't allowed to have them under U.S. export rules.

Then, in May 2025, investigators asked Nvidia for records of its sales to Megaspeed. A month later, Nvidia put the company on its own internal "no sell" list. Commerce agents, along with Singaporean law enforcement, inspected Megaspeed that June, interviewing its then-CEO, Alice Huang. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing, and its CEO, Tan Yong Pong, said multiple inspections -- by Commerce's investigators, Malaysia's trade ministry, and Nvidia itself -- found no evidence of illicit chip transfers. He said the company will cooperate fully with U.S. authorities.

While Commerce was heading up the investigation, the Justice Department has taken the lead on what it says is now a criminal investigation.

Jensen Huang's recent departure came after reports of multiple affairs, one with an Nvidia direct report, and one with Alice Huang, the Chinese businesswoman who had served as Megaspeed's CEO and chairperson, though she has since left, according to the company. U.S. officials suspect that she has ties to Chinese intelligence, though the exact nature of that relationship is still not clear.

Whether or not she is proved to be an asset, she was reportedly instrumental in getting more than $7.5 billion worth of Nvidia chips into China, according to The Information.

After just days on the outside, it seems Huang may want back in. In his first official statement since his ouster, Huang said this morning that "I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO."

Huang has wide support within and without the company. A petition with more than 190,000 signatures -- including SpaceX (SPCX +3.03%) CEO Elon Musk's -- was sent to the board demanding he be reinstated while the board's investigation is underway. Separately, a letter to the board from a senior employee warned that without Jensen at the helm, "irreplaceable talent at Nvidia is now contemplating departing."

Sources have told both Reuters and CBS that despite the support, the board is not considering a return. Interim CEO Colette Kress will lead her first earnings call tonight.

It's speculation, but I would be surprised if Jensen Huang was reinstated. At least not until more details come to light. There is too much at stake for the company. If any wrongdoing is found, or any evidence that Huang or senior Nvidia officers were "looking the other way" while chips flowed into China in defiance of U.S. export laws, Nvidia could face some serious consequences.
