Microsoft is retiring To Do, and personal users get no replacement

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Microsoft To Do’s work users move to Planner, but their tasks won’t follow. Wunderlist’s founder, whose app Microsoft bought and closed, says he would love to buy To Do back.

Microsoft’s support page covers To Do on the web, Windows, Mac, iOS and Android. Its advice to personal users is blunt.

“Choose another task-management application that meets your needs,” Microsoft’s guidance says.

The company announced the change at the end of a long Planner blog post on Wednesday, during its Ignite conference. Windows Latest spotted it the same day.

Existing To Do tasks and lists will not move to Planner. Work users can still see them in Planner until the June deadline, and then lose access. Shared lists, their members and their permissions will not transfer either.

Microsoft has promised a tool to export To Do data before the apps close. It has not published instructions yet. It also warns that exported data may not import into Planner or any other app. Retiring the apps does not by itself schedule the data for deletion, the company says.

Planner also lacks several To Do features. Microsoft does not plan native Planner apps for Windows or Mac. Its web and Teams versions do not offer the same offline access. Private tasks cannot take file attachments for now. List groups have no equivalent, and Microsoft has no plans for printing or emailing a list. The old Tasks feature in classic Outlook, meanwhile, will keep working.

The To Do service and its links to other apps will retire later. Microsoft says it will announce those dates separately.

Microsoft is merging To Do and Planner’s plan types into one system it calls unified plans. One system lets it offer the same features everywhere, the Planner team wrote. It also lets users connect their tasks to Copilot and other agents. At Ignite, Microsoft also put Office inside Copilot, pitched Copilot as cheaper than Claude and launched MAI Code 2.

A Microsoft spokesperson acknowledged the gap for home users in a statement to Windows Central.

“We recognize this change will affect customers differently, particularly those using personal Microsoft accounts, and we’re committed to helping them prepare,” said Howard Crow, Microsoft’s director of product for agents and AI.

For many users this is the second move. Microsoft bought 6Wunderkinder, the Berlin startup behind Wunderlist, in 2015 for a reported $100m to $200m. Its team built To Do, and Microsoft shut Wunderlist down in May 2020.

Wunderlist’s founder, Christian Reber, now runs Superlist, which started in 2020. It called itself “the natural successor” and already offers a To Do importer, GeekWire’s Todd Bishop reported. Reber also replied to the news on X.

“I’d love to buy it back anytime!” Reber wrote.

Rivals moved fast. TickTick posted a migration guide, and Todoist’s chief executive, Amir Salihefendic, called the shutdown “pretty unexpected” on LinkedIn, GeekWire reported. Brad Silverberg, who led Windows 95 development, said he had used To Do daily. He is now looking at TickTick and Todoist.

Microsoft says it will add export instructions, the supported data and any limits to its support page once they are final.
