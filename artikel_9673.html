Almost Is the Most Expensive Word in Any Startup

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Almost done. Almost launched. Almost profitable.

If you've built anything, you know these phrases. You've probably said them in a standup, to a cofounder, or to yourself at midnight. I've said all three. They sound like you're nearly there. Very often they mean you're stuck and comfortable about it.

The trick of almost is that you stay busy. Every day there's a bug to fix, a screen to polish, a small feature that would make the launch better. You go to bed tired. That tiredness feels like proof you're moving.

But the work in the almost stage is often the safe work. You polish what you already have, because finishing means stepping into the parts that can hurt: setting a price, showing it to strangers, asking for money, hearing no.

As a developer I know this pattern well. A feature that's 90% done can stay 90% done for weeks. The last 10% is edge cases, error messages, docs, deployment. That's not the fun part, and nobody claps when you finish it.

This isn't only a startup thing. Look around your home.

The bathroom that's almost renovated. It works fine, apart from the missing tile or the light switch with no cover. After a few weeks your brain stops seeing it. Almost has turned into the normal state.

That's the expensive part. Almost isn't just unfinished, it's invisible. You stop noticing what it costs: the subscription still running for the half-built product, the evenings, the mental tab you keep open.

To be fair, not everything needs to finish. Some projects are for learning or for fun, and almost is a perfectly good place for them to live. A half-built hobby can still be a good hobby.

The problem is almost that you keep reporting as progress while it eats your time and money. What matters is being honest about which kind you have.
