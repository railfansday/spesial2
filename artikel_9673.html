Quote and contract from the same numbers: a wedding vendor's spreadsheet + the 11 clauses that hold up

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Every wedding vendor I have talked to has the same story. Somewhere around booking number twenty, a client cancels six weeks out, asks for the deposit back, and the vendor realises the "contract" was a WhatsApp message that said "ok booked!". The second-most-common story is the quote that got sent as a photo of a notes app, with one line item missing, and the argument that followed on the wedding day.

Both problems have the same fix: a quote and a contract that come from the same numbers. Here is the structure I use, with the spreadsheet logic that keeps the two in sync.

The mistake most vendors make is writing percentages in three places: the quote says "30% deposit", the contract says "40% second payment", and the payment reminder you send says something else. Put every rule in one place and let everything reference it:

The final-balance cell is a formula, not a number, so it can never fail to add up. A one-line check next to it (=IF(ROUND(dep+second+final,4)=1,"OK","CHECK")) catches the day you change the deposit and forget the rest.

A pricelist with a short code per item (PKG-03, ADD-06) means the quote sheet needs only two inputs per line: code and quantity. Name, unit and price come from a lookup:

INDEX/MATCH rather than XLOOKUP because clients open your PDF, but you might open the sheet in Google Sheets, LibreOffice or an older Excel, and all of them evaluate this one. A COUNTIF on the code column flags duplicates before they bite.

The totals block then reads Subtotal → Discount → Tax (only if a Yes/No setting says you collect it) → Total → Deposit to book → Remaining balance. The deposit line is =ROUND(Total * Setup!Deposit, 2) — the same cell the contract will quote.
