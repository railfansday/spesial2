Vistra Is Down 41% From Its High. History Suggests This Is What Happens Next.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The leading power producer faces near-term headwinds.

Vistra(VST -2.02%), the largest competitive power producer in America, reached a record high of $217.02 per share on Sept. 22, 2025, marking a 556% gain over its previous two years. The rapid expansion of the power-hungry data center market fueled most of that growth.

But today, Vistra's stock trades at just $128 per share. Let's see what caused that 41% drop, and whether it's still a good long-term play in the AI infrastructure and electrification markets.

Vistra operates natural gas, nuclear, coal, solar, and battery energy storage facilities with a combined capacity of 49 GW. Its retail subsidiaries -- including TXU Energy, Dynegy, Homefield Energy, Ambit, and other regional power companies -- serve over five million customers. It's also exposed to the data center boom through its power purchase agreements (PPAs) with Meta Platforms (META -0.53%) and Amazon(AMZN -0.07%).

Vistra's growth looks lumpy, but several temporary issues distorted it. Its revenue fell in the third quarter of 2025, due to a tough year-over-year comparison against the record-breaking summer heat waves in Texas in 2024. Its decline in the second quarter of 2026 was driven by unrealized losses on its open derivative positions to lock in future power prices.

Vistra shut down a major portfolio of its Moss Landing battery storage facilities after they were damaged by fires in early 2025, rather than reopening them. PJM Interconnection also extended its existing cap on electricity capacity prices across the Mid-Atlantic and Midwest.

But despite all of that pressure, Vistra continued to grow as the cloud infrastructure and AI markets expanded. That acceleration turned the slow-growth utility into a high-growth AI play.

For the full year, analysts expect Vistra's revenue and adjusted EBITDA to rise 24% and 25%, respectively. With an enterprise value of $64 billion, it looks like a screaming bargain at nine times next year's adjusted EBITDA.

However, Vistra trades at that discount valuation because it faces some unpredictable near-term challenges in Texas, which accounts for over 40% of its total capacity. Texas' governor-elect Gina Hinojosa, who will take office on Jan. 19, plans to freeze residential electricity prices and issue a new moratorium on all new data center approvals until tighter laws are passed. Several other Tier-1 data center states -- including Georgia and Ohio -- are adopting similar strategies.

That pressure, along with the threat of rising interest rates curbing AI spending, could throttle Vistra's growth over the next few years. That's why analysts expect its revenue and adjusted EBITDA to only grow at CAGRs of 6% and 8%, respectively, from 2026 to 2028.

Those growth rates remain stable, but they make Vistra look more like a traditional utility than a higher-growth AI infrastructure play. That's why its stock pulled back sharply from its all-time high, and why its upside could be limited for at least the next 12 months.

Vistra's downside could also be limited at these levels, but I don't think it's a compelling investment yet. Its forward dividend yield of 0.7% is paltry compared with other utility stocks, and it lacks the momentum of higher-growth AI infrastructure stocks. I wouldn't touch it unless the regulatory headwinds dissipate and the data center market heats up again.
