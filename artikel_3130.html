3 Reasons I'm Not Selling a Single Share of VXUS No Matter What the Market Does in 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The future looks bright for international stocks, even if the U.S. stock market has a bad year in 2027.

A lot of investors might be feeling nervous right now.

Investor enthusiasm about artificial intelligence (AI) stocks has turned to skepticism. The tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 index recently entered "correction" territory, with a decline of about 10.5% from its Oct. 6 record high. As of Nov. 5, that index was down about 14.7% since Oct. 6 -- about two-thirds of the way to a bear market (20% downturn).

What if the worries spread beyond tech stocks and the entire U.S. stock market enters a correction in 2027? What if there's an S&P 500 bear market that lasts a year or longer?

If so, there's one move I'm going to make: I'm going to hold on for dear life to my shares of the Vanguard Total International Stock ETF (VXUS -1.13%). This international ETF holds more than 8,900 stocks from all over the world. It's delivered about 20.3% annualized returns over the past three years. And I'm going to keep buying more.

Here are three reasons why I'm bullish on VXUS and other international stock ETFs in 2027 and beyond.

Vanguard research says international stocks are likely to outperform U.S. stocks for the next few decades. As of Sept. 30, the Vanguard Capital Markets Model projects that "global ex-U.S. equities (unhedged)" will outperform U.S. equities for the next 10 years and 30 years.

According to this Vanguard model, global stocks are expected to deliver annualized returns of 5% to 7% over the next 10 years, and 6.2% to 8.2% over the next 30 years. Meanwhile, U.S. stocks are forecasted to deliver annualized returns of only 4.8% to 6.8% (10 years) and 4.9% to 6.9% (30 years).

Why is Vanguard more bullish on international stocks than U.S. stocks? According to the company's 2026 economic and market outlook, Vanguard believes that in the future AI-powered economy, "non-U.S. developed market equities" (international stocks in advanced economies) are likely to be among the biggest winners from future AI-related productivity gains. As for U.S. stocks, Vanguard's analysis suggests that U.S. growth stocks may already be overvalued in anticipation of high future earnings that might not materialize.

If companies in other countries get good at using AI to boost their profits, that's good news for international stock investors. And if America's tech-heavy stock market is already "priced for perfection," or if the biggest gains from AI don't go to American AI companies, that's another reason to diversify beyond U.S. shores.

Even if Vanguard's research predictions are off-target and the U.S. stock market keeps going strong, I want to stay diversified. I don't want to bet everything on one country, even if it is a country like America, which has many impressive strengths as a place to invest.

I believe that in life, all good things must come to an end -- and that might include this recent bull market in U.S. stocks, when American companies have strongly outperformed the rest of the world.

What if the rest of the world catches up? What if the U.S. dollar weakens against other currencies? What if the world economy changes in ways that could be disadvantageous for American companies and give other countries' stock markets a boost?

I'm not rooting against America or betting against America, but I want to own international stocks in case those possible futures come to pass.

At the risk of sounding flippant: As an investor, I don't care about 2027. I care about 2047 and beyond. I'm in this for the long run. If the S&P 500 crashes tomorrow and goes into a bear market for all of 2027 and beyond, I accept that risk. It won't be fun or painless. There's a lot of anxiety that comes with a bear market. ("What if stocks drop forever? What if the stock market never goes up again?")

But in the long run, even the worst crises and stock market crashes look like temporary blips on a stock market chart. The longer-term trend in the global economy is toward growth, innovation, collaboration, and prosperity. I don't want to bet against that.

Every day, billions of people around the world wake up, go to work, and try to improve their lives. That universal human impulse to collaborate and create is what makes the global economy work. We can all make each other richer in the long run. Owning international stock ETFs, such as the Vanguard Total International Stock ETF, is a good way for me to invest in a brighter future for the world.
