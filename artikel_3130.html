Intuitive Machines vs. Rocket Lab: Which Space Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Intuitive Machines trades at a far lower valuation than Rocket Lab, but each carries very different risks tied to customers, cash burn, and launch development.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 20, 2026 at 1:00PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Intuitive Machines ›

The commercial space race is expanding as private enterprises take over roles once reserved for governments. Investors weighing Intuitive Machines (LUNR -10.63%) against Rocket Lab (RKLB -9.47%) are looking for the best long-term winner.

Both companies represent the growth of the modern space industry. Intuitive Machines focuses on lunar landing services and data infrastructure, while Rocket Lab provides reliable launch capabilities and satellite manufacturing.

Intuitive Machines provides lunar spacecraft, communication networks, and infrastructure-as-a-service. It serves a mix of civil and national security clients, including NASA and the U.S. Space Development Agency. NASA accounted for roughly 78% of revenue in 2025, and customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $707 million, representing revenue growth of approximately 220.9%. The company reported a net loss of $128.0 million for the period. This resulted in a net margin of negative 18%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was approximately negative 0.5x, indicating that total liabilities exceed shareholder equity. The current ratio of nearly 5.0x shows the company has sufficient short-term assets to cover its short-term debts. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was roughly negative $56 million, and many defense stocks are currently competing for similar infrastructure contracts.

Rocket Lab is an end-to-end space provider that designs rockets and manufactures spacecraft components. It serves diverse markets including NASA, the National Reconnaissance Office, and various commercial satellite operators. Its top five customers accounted for nearly 49% of 2025 revenue, which requires careful monitoring by investors.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $881.9 million, about a 59% increase over the prior year. Rocket Lab reported a net loss of around $226.9 million during this time. These results produced a net margin of negative 25.7%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was roughly 0.1x, which compares total debt to shareholder equity to show reliance on borrowing. The current ratio of nearly 4.1x shows strong short-term liquidity, even as free cash flow for the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, was approximately negative $321.8 million. This cash consumption reflects the capital-intensive nature of building global launch infrastructure.

Intuitive Machines faces significant customer concentration, as a vast majority of its revenue comes from NASA. The company also deals with operational hazards inherent in space missions, including potential landing anomalies or mission failures. Additionally, the recent acquisition of Lanteris presents integration challenges and increased overhead costs.

Rocket Lab must manage development risks associated with its new Neutron launch vehicle, which recently suffered a tank failure during testing. The company is also exposed to operational hazards at its specific launch sites and relies heavily on key leadership. Finally, the pending acquisition of Iridium Communications (IRDM -2.66%) involves substantial financial risk and potential shareholder dilution.

Intuitive Machines appears cheaper, carrying a lower Forward P/E relative to future earnings estimates and a lower P/S ratio based on sales over the past twelve months.

Intuitive Machines has been posting superb results in fiscal 2026, most recently posting sequential quarterly growth of 31%, to $269 million in the third quarter reported in early November. Management says they have an order backlog of $1.9 billion, up $1.2 billion from the end of 2025. NASA is moving toward steady access-to-space flights and deliveries, too, which bodes well for the company's longer-term sales. Revenue for fiscal 2026 is expected to more than quadruple to $935 million, though net loss is expected to widen to about $100 million. Analysts expect the business to turn a profit for the first time in 2029.

Rocket Lab, meanwhile, made a huge splash in the market with its pending $8 billion acquisition of Iridium Communications this year. The combination promises to make Rocket Lab a space powerhouse by combining its launch technology with the communications spectrum offered by Iridium. In short, Rocket Lab could very well be a serious competitor to Space Exploration Technologies Corp (SPCX -6.26%). Don't overlook Rocket Lab's expertise in sending small payloads into orbit, and it is closing in on the same reusable rocket technology that SpaceX has used to lower its customer prices.

Stand-alone Rocket Lab is seen boosting its revenue by more than 50% this year to $983 million, a figure analysts have raised throughout the year, with a net loss just about equal to 2025, at $199 million. The Iridium acquisition is a big meal to swallow, but the combined business should generate $1.8 billion in revenue in 2028, the first full year of the merged companies (The Iridium acquisition is slated to close mid-2027).

These are two exciting space-age stocks. Intuitive Machines, with its much more reasonable P/S and forward P/E ratios, gets the nod, under the adage of buying good companies at good prices for the long term, rather than paying extremely high premiums for future growth with Rocket Lab.
