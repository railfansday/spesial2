15 Most Expensive Wingers in Football History (Ranked)

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

High transfer fees have become something of a norm in football. With the growth of the game in recent decades, hundreds of millions of pounds have been invested into the sport and as such, standard aspects such as transfer fees have skyrocketed. Long gone are the days of amazement at a £1 million player in divisions like the Premier League, Bundesliga and La Liga.

More often than not, the highest fees are reserved for more attack-minded players, at least in comparison to goalkeepers and defenders. At a very basic level, players in attack are more likely to contribute to goals and as such, demand a higher fee if a team is looking to buy them.

There have been many wingers in football to move for extortionate fees, but which 15 wingers in the history of world football have transferred for the highest amounts of money?

The greatest left-sided attackers the Premier League has ever seen have been ranked.

A 2025 signing, Bryan Mbeumo made the big-money to Manchester United from Brentford more than 12 months ago. The Cameroon international has been a prolific performer in English football's top-flight for a number of years now, and has been a key man at Old Trafford since arriving.

That said, only time will tell whether that £65m transfer fee proves worthwhile, as Man United continue to fall disastrously short of their high expectations.

Luis Diaz's at Liverpool was characterised by some electric displays that would see him cut inside from the left wing before unleashing a powerful shot into the corner, helping the Reds to a famous Premier League title in the 2024/2025 season under Arne Slot.

After helping to deliver the top-flight for the Reds, however, the Colombian made the move to German giants Bayern Munich, where he has taken to the Bundesliga like a duck to water ever since.

Diaz is among the best wingers in the world right now and has been worth every penny since arriving in Germany.

An Ivory Coast international, Nicolas Pepe began his professional career in France, the country of his birth. After four years with Angers, Lille moved to sign the wide man in 2017, where he would spend two further seasons. His form in Ligue 1 saw Arsenal come calling who, in 2019, made him their club-record signing at the time, for a fee of around £72 million.

Expectations for Pepe were immensely high, not only for his quality, but for the price tag that was on his head which of course, he had no control over. Pepe scored just five goals in his debut Premier League season, notching six assists alongside that. He doubled his goal tally in the following campaign but unfortunately for Pepe, that would be his peak for the Gunners.

Across the 2021/22 term, Pepe netted just one Premier League goal, which came in late February. Pepe would spend the following season back in France, on loan with Nice, before departing North London on a permanent basis in 2023 for Trabzonspor, his transfer firmly viewed as a bust and he viewed as one of the biggest flops in Premier League history.

After spending his youth career with Watford and Manchester City, Jadon Sancho made the decision to move to Germany in 2017, signing for Borussia Dortmund with whom he would establish himself as one of Europe’s most promising young wingers. After four years with the Schwarzgelben, Sancho returned to England, signing for Man United in a deal worth £73 million.

Sancho enjoyed a steady couple of campaigns to begin his time with the Red Devils, though never found the consistency that you would perhaps expect of a player that was so expensive. Early in the 2023/24 season, an outburst on social media in response to comments from then-manager Erik ten Hag saw Sancho excluded from the first team.

He returned to Dortmund in that term’s winter transfer window, reminding the world of how good he is at his brilliant best. Since then, however, that form has not reared its head once, despite spells at Chelsea and Aston Villa. Now a free agent, Sancho looks destined to wonder what might have been.

Many questions have been asked of Neymar, his career and his ambitions, particularly stemming from his decision to move to Paris Saint-Germain while still in his mid-20s. Neymar would spend six years in the French capital, though saw his last season or two impacted heavily by injuries.

In 2023, Neymar decided to move to the Saudi Pro League, joining Al-Hilal for just under £77.6 million. The Brazilian, who is his country’s all-time top scorer, still feasibly had a number of years left at a higher level, but could not have foreseen that the injury niggles he’d faced with PSG would worsen significantly.

Across 18 months as an Al-Hilal player, Neymar scored just one goal and featured in only seven games. The winger was in a constant battle for fitness, seemingly never earning a reprieve from injury troubles. In 2025, Neymar agreed to mutually cancel his contract with the club, paving the way for a return to his boyhood club, Santos.

The inflation of the transfer market for young players is evident year after year.

Though he drifted centrally as he got older, Cristiano Ronaldo was an out-and-out winger for much of his career, particularly in his prime. His incredible eye for goal meant that converting to a centre-forward was almost a foregone conclusion, but only after he established himself as the world’s best winger.

Startling form with Man United, that saw him win a Ballon d’Or and Champions League, saw the elite start to circle Ronaldo, who made the move to Real Madrid in 2009. Ronaldo signed for the Spanish giants for £80 million which, at the time, was a world-record transfer fee, though the Portuguese would more than prove his worth.

Across nine years in Madrid, Ronaldo won two La Liga titles and two Copa del Rey trophies. Most impressively, though, were the four Champions Leagues that he helped the club win, including three successive tournament victories between 2015 and 2018. Remarkably, when he left for Juventus in 2018, Madrid made profit on the sensation.

After spending two years in his native Brazil representing Sao Paulo, Antony moved to Europe in 2020, signing for Ajax soon after the club had reached the semi-finals of the Champions League. The Brazilian would spend another two seasons in Amsterdam, winning a KNVB Cup and two Eredivisie titles in that time.

Antony’s performances saw many sides register an interest in the winger and in 2022, in a move that saw Antony reunite with manager Ten Hag, he joined Man United. The Red Devils paid just over £80 million to acquire Antony’s services, the second-highest transfer fee, behind Paul Pogba that the club have ever paid.

Despite the fee and expectation, Antony’s tenure with Manchester United was anything but good. The winger ended his time at Old Trafford with just 12 goals in 96 appearances. For context, in that time, he accumulated 14 yellow cards, receiving two more bookings than goals.

The winger joined Real Betis on a permanent basis in 2025, where he has slowly faded out of the spotlight.

When Gareth Bale joined Spurs from Southampton in 2007, he did so as a promising, young left-back. By the time of his departure six years later, he was one of the most exciting wingers on the continent. Having been moved further up the pitch by Harry Redknapp in the 2009/10 season, Bale spent the rest of his tenure in North London developing as an offensive threat.

Bale’s undeniable quality saw Real Madrid pay what was then a world-record transfer fee of just over £85 million for his services in 2013. Bale was a frustrating figure for many Madrid fans, given that often, he struggled for consistent minutes and had his priorities in the wrong order, according to some.

What is impossible to deny, however, is the ability that Bale had. The Welshman spent nine years with Madrid–eight, if you factor in his 2020/21 season-long loan back at Spurs–and formed a fearsome attacking triumvirate with Cristiano Ronaldo and Karim Benzema.

Bale was a crucial cog in the team that won three successive Champions League trophies between 2015 and 2018, three of the five he won with the club.

There are some incredible dribblers in football right now.

It is feasible to think that, were it not for injuries, Eden Hazard would still be playing football, given he's only in his mid-30s. The Belgian spent four years with Real Madrid between 2019 and his retirement, having signed for the club for a base fee of just under £90 million. Hazard had major struggles with injury, however, limiting him to just 76 games in his time at the Bernabeu.

Prior to Hazard’s move, he had represented Lille but most predominantly, Chelsea, who he joined in 2012. Across the next seven years, Hazard consistently put in incredible performances each season, with the Belgian considered one of the best wingers to have ever played in the English top flight.

His form for the Blues more than justified his big-money move to Madrid and while the numbers do not make Hazard’s time there look great, or even good, it would feel unfair to label the move a complete bust, given his long-term struggles for fitness served to continuously disrupt any form he was building.

One of the first wingers in the history of world football to have cost at least £100 million, Jack Grealish made the switch to Manchester City from Aston Villa in 2021, having spent eight years as a professional with his boyhood club. With Villa, Grealish established himself as a creative outlet, full of flair and dynamism.

What followed was an incredibly difficult run to judge. While some argued that Pep Guardiola nullified Grealish's flair, it's also worth pointing out how crucial the Englishman was in the 2022/23 treble-winning campaign at the Etihad. That, alone, was worth the £100m spent to secure his services.

Now on loan at Everton, the former Aston Villa man is looking to get back to the form he initially showed to earn a £100m move to Man City in the first place.
