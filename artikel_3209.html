MNTN (MNTN) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Sales turnover and a reset outlook marked the quarter, with a rebuild underway.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

MNTN, Inc. (MNTN -54.58%) announced a leadership transition and a downward revision to its 2026 financial outlook following significant execution challenges within its sales organization. Management reported that a rapid shift to a new compensation and organizational model led to substantial turnover among experienced sales personnel, impacting the acquisition of new customers. The company appointed a new chief executive officer and several senior executives to address operational rigor and stabilize the go-to-market strategy. Despite these internal disruptions, management noted that retention and expansion among existing customers remained stable throughout the period.

Brinlea Johnson: Good afternoon, and thank you for joining us for Mountain's Third Quarter 2026 Earnings Call. With me today are Joe Kaiser, Mountain's Lead Independent Director; Tony Weisman, CEO; and Patrick Pohlen, CFO. Just to remind everyone, today's call includes forward-looking statements that are subject to risks and uncertainties, and actual results could materially differ from those anticipated in the forward-looking statements. For the risks and uncertainties that may affect future results, please see our most recently filed periodic report, which is also available on our website. With that, I will turn the call over to Joe.

Joseph Kaiser: Thank you for joining us. I'm Mountain's Lead Independent Director. As you surely saw in the press release we issued a little while ago, Tony Weisman has been appointed Mountain's new CEO effective November 2. The Board determined it was time to bring more operational rigor to the business and asked Tony to step in as CEO. Tony joined Mountain's Board earlier this year, and he has the unwavering support of Mountain's Board to lead the company at this time. As a career marketer, Tony brings the voice of the customer to Mountain. From his time leading Digitas and serving as Chief Marketing Officer of Dunkin', Tony understands the decisions our customers make about where to spend their advertising dollars.

With that, please join me in welcoming our new CEO, Tony Weisman.

Tony Weisman: Thanks, Joe. Let's start with the numbers. Third quarter revenue was $80.3 million compared with the company's guidance of $86 million to $89 million. Adjusted EBITDA was $19.9 million compared with guidance of $22 million to $25 million. For the fourth quarter, we expect revenue to be between $75 million and $78 million and adjusted EBITDA to be between $8 million and $10 million. Our Q3 results and outlook are disappointing. We have much higher expectations at Mountain. Before accepting this role, I reviewed the business, including product market fit, customer demand, our technology, and our sales motion. I wanted to understand what had changed and whether we had a practical path to address it.

I concluded the issue was exclusively go-to-market execution. The company recently made significant changes in the sales team that did not work out, most notably a new compensation structure modeled after the biggest platforms in our industry like Google and Meta. They were implemented too quickly, resulting in sales force attrition. We lost about 40% of our experienced quota-carrying sellers, reducing our ability to bring in new business. We've since corrected the compensation plans. We've also since hired 16 new sellers, and our first new hire class starts later this month. Additionally, we've added two proven executives, Lynn Knaak from Salesforce, leading brand sales and Frank Lee from Snap leading agency sales. They both report to me.

It will take time for these sellers to fully ramp, and our updated outlook assumes that the disruption we saw in Q3 will continue into Q4. Our new leaders have instilled more discipline around hiring, training and supervision, resulting in increased new starting budgets. We've changed the leadership behind the decisions that got us here. Chris Innes and Garland Hill are no longer with Mountain. I have spent my career on the advertiser side of the table. Mountain does what marketers have always wanted - reach the right people and measure the return on their advertising spend. In a category full of complexity, I love how Mountain keeps it simple.

We provide one frictionless platform that allows customers to invest in CTV channel with clarity, simplicity, and accountability. Our technology, experience and data help them decide which messages to run, how to allocate their budgets, and how to measure results all in one place as easily as they buy on Meta or Google. And we give them the attribution they've come to expect, tying their TV spend to site visits and sales. I believe our opportunity for growth remains strong. Mountain is a performance channel like search and social. Combined with the power of television, it is the ultimate business-driving platform. What Mountain needs is operational rigor to achieve our goals. I plan to take a three-fold approach.

One, identify the opportunities and the weaknesses early and address them quickly. Two, get the right leaders in charge of each team. Three, make sure everyone is aligned on common metrics and working together for measurable results. I'll rigorously review pipeline growth, conversion of demos into paying customers, and starting budgets with the team. I plan to judge our progress by whether those improvements translate into sustained customer spending and revenue. I plan to move quickly and decisively. I've also begun to make changes to our leadership team. I'm pleased to announce that Anna Sayre, who led marketing at The Trade Desk for seven years, joined us this week as Mountain's Chief Marketing Officer.

She'll make our offering salient to generate new customer growth. Finally, I excel at building cultures of accountability where the teams are focused on execution. I've been delighted to learn that some of the best industry talent is already inside our company, and I'm committed to giving them the support and guidance to perform at their best. Let me wrap up by saying that I know we have to earn back your confidence. While we're hiring and onboarding salespeople now, it will take time for these sellers to fully ramp. Our outlook reflects this.

When we report fourth quarter results, I'll lay out our 2027 operating plan and outlook, the measures we use to assess progress, and what our changes have delivered to that point. Patrick, over to you to walk through the numbers and the outlook, and then we will take your questions.

Patrick Pohlen: Thank you, Tony. Our third quarter revenue increased to $80.3 million, up 15% year-over-year. Revenue growth this quarter was impacted by unforeseen execution disruption in our go-to-market organization. Third quarter gross margins remained consistent at 79%, flat with the prior year period. Our expansion rate, which measures the spend of our current customers as compared to those same customer spend a year ago, remains quite healthy and is still well north of 115%. Total operating expenses for the third quarter decreased by $4.7 million sequentially to $54.5 million.

This was primarily driven by a decrease in personnel costs as a result of the reduction in force in the second quarter and a one-time stock-based compensation award that also occurred in the second quarter. For the third quarter, we achieved positive net income of $7.7 million for a GAAP EPS of $0.10 compared to a net income of $6.4 million and GAAP EPS of $0.09 in the prior year period. Adjusted EBITDA for the quarter increased to $19.9 million, up from $16 million in Q3 of 2025, an increase of 24%. The company's adjusted EBITDA margin grew to 24.8%, up 200 basis points compared to 22.8% in Q3 of 2025.

We plan on continuing to make disciplined investments in sales and marketing, specifically in experienced hires to drive broader customer adoption. We expect sales and marketing expenses to increase approximately 10% sequentially as we add qualified salespeople. Our balance sheet remains strong, and we ended the quarter with $252 million in cash and cash equivalents with no borrowings outstanding. We ended the quarter with 75.8 million shares outstanding. We are reducing our fourth quarter and full year outlook to reflect ongoing changes to our go-to-market organization. It will take time for sellers to fully ramp, and our guidance assumes that the disruption we saw in Q3 will continue into the fourth quarter.

For Q4, we expect revenue to be between $75 million and $78 million. We expect adjusted EBITDA to be between $8 million and $10 million. Our fourth quarter guidance includes additional non-recurring general and administrative expenses related to our executive personnel changes. For the full year 2026, we are lowering our revenue guidance range to $311 million to $314 million, representing 10% year-over-year growth at the midpoint, excluding the impact of the Maximum Effort divestiture. We expect adjusted EBITDA to be between $66 million and $68 million. We are basing the revised outlook on our expectation for how the business will perform as we realign the go-to-market organization around our priorities.

We'll provide more detail on the operating plan and the outlook for 2027 when we announce our fourth quarter and fiscal year results. With that said, we'll open the line for questions.

Matthew Weber: I guess just to start off, you've seen the business from the Board, so you've had a view from the inside. What gives you the confidence that this is an execution problem rather than a demand or a competitive one? And then I have a follow-up.

Tony Weisman: I've studied this business for quite some time, and I know that it's delivering value to our customers, many of whom have been with us for a very long time. And it has proven to be one of the most effective ROAS channels. And of course, the growth in the market underscores the overall interest in this space. I'm confident that Mountain has terrific product market fit, a terrific team, a great brand, and certainly the heritage of having essentially invented this space. The more I dug, the more I realized that it was decisions that were made in our own sales organization, primarily leading to attrition that caused this slowdown.

And those strike me as problems that are and are being fixed and can be addressed quickly so that we can get back to the historical growth we've seen.

Matthew Weber: And then just..., is there any incremental color that you can share with us just in terms of what specifically those changes were to the compensation structure and sort of what the thought process was going into them and sort of how you've corrected today?

Tony Weisman: Sure. I mean when Garland joined the company from TikTok, there was a desire to move the sales organization to models that are underway at bigger platforms like TikTok, Google, Meta. Those had things to do. There was a verticalization that was deployed poorly and too quickly. There was a change in incentives from revenue to activity, and there was a change in the frequency with which sellers were paid.

These were told to the team in late Q2, but implemented at the beginning of Q3, and it just didn't work for most of the -- for many of the sellers who felt disaffected and they felt like their opportunity for earning had been diminished, and that's what led to the attrition.

Andrew Marok: Two, if I could. So maybe again on the kind of culture reset and the operational rigor that you were talking about. Tony Weisman: My analysis has determined that is the #1 issue and the first thing that needs to get addressed and the thing I'm most focused on most immediate. Across the board, I believe there is an opportunity for the company to operate with greater focus on outcomes, a higher level of accountability, and more transparency across the company on the metrics that matter and they're all going in the same direction. So I started to put some of those in place so that we have common dashboards reporting real-time insights that are relevant to the growth of the business. I think that just sort of falls in general under an approach of accountability, transparency, and holding people responsible.

By contrast, many of our current customers retained and continue to expand, and we continue to see very high levels of satisfaction and performance in that group. I think the dynamics in the industry that you referred to, some of our competitors being acquired by larger platforms, is going to play out in such a way that the potential customers are going to wonder about the difference between Mountain (which is a purely independent platform that only cares about the customers' growth) and some of these others and wonder where their data is going and whose interest they're serving.

So I believe that's an opportunity for us in the future to even grow greater share.

What we did had the best -- had the highest impact on the non-self-serve opportunities where sellers and demos that were brought in from the team themselves fell well below the volumes that we had seen historically in the past.

So that's the area that we're focusing on the right hiring and the right training to get those folks on board so that they are quota carrying going forward, and we're going to continue to operate both motions going forward.

Andrew Boone: And then I'd love to ask around just the direction that's implied with the hires.
