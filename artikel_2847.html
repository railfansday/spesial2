Eagles take confusing stance on breakout DT drawing major trade interest

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Eagles take confusing stance on breakout DT drawing major trade interest originally appeared on The Sporting News. Add The Sporting News as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

Heading into Week 9 of the 2026 NFL season, the Philadelphia Eagles are a surprising 2-6 on the year. Jalen Hurts is out for the year, Lane Johnson has retired, and this team has lost six straight games.

Now, as the Eagles are under a week away from the NFL trade deadline, it should be clear that this team is acting as a seller. While this might not be a fire sale, plenty of players on expiring contracts should be traded.

That's why the report from Sports Illustrated's Albert Breer on breakout defensive tackle Moro Ojomo is so confusing. Instead of Ojomo being a premier trade chip, the Eagles and Howie Roseman are taking the stance that Ojomo is a part of the Eagles' future.

"Ojomo, also in a contract year, is a different story," Breer writes. "In fact, Howie Roseman has given other teams the impression that he'll try to sign Ojomo, who's gotten very strong interest, long term, even though he's already got DTs Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis on big second deals."

The Eagles drafted Ojomo four years ago, and now, following the 2026 NFL season, he is going to hit free agency. That is, unless the Eagles re-sign him or trade him to a team that extends him.

From the Eagles' perspective, sitting at 2-6, trading Ojomo would make a ton of sense. Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis are signed long-term, so why wouldn't the Eagles want to get something back for Ojomo, whom they might let walk?

While the Eagles might be able to pick up a third-round compensatory pick if Ojomo leaves in free agency, that would come after pick 96 in the 2028 NFL Draft, and it's not even a guarantee.

There's a real chance the Eagles could get something much better in a trade this season with Ojomo, especially when an older, more expensive DT in DeForest Buckner went for a fourth-round pick.

This trade stance from Roseman and the Eagles is confusing. Signing Ojomo doesn't make sense with Davis and Carter signed, and letting him walk in free agency isn't worth the risk when a trade would make more sense.

Not trading Ojomo might only make sense if the Eagles decide to trade Carter, which has been a rumor floating around recently prior to the trade deadline, albeit with no substantial reporter backing such a rumor.

But if the Eagles decide to keep Ojomo, it would be hard to imagine they keep Carter and Davis as well. That's a lot invested in the interior defensive line, which would be an odd allocation of money, especially with some big contract negotiations coming up.

It wouldn't be a surprise if this was all posturing from Roseman to try and drive up the price of Ojomo, as a trade makes too much sense for the breakout DT. But, for now, taking it at face value, this is a confusing stance from the Eagles on Ojomo this Fall.
