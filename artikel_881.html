Hemmings unfazed by rise at Villa and eager to take next step

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Sky Sports sits down with Aston Villa prospect George Hemmings to discuss his rise to the first team; Hemmings started for seven games before an injury curtailed progress; watch Ipswich vs Aston Villa live on Saturday Night Football, kick-off 5.30pm

Football Journalist @PatrickRoweSky

Two days after being thrown into a cauldron in Istanbul during Aston Villa's loss to Galatasaray in the Champions League, George Hemmings strolls into the media centre at Bodymoor Heath, sliders on, looking entirely at home in the senior surroundings that should be new to him.

The nineteen-year-old is quiet and reserved to start but soon settled into proceedings when quizzed on that atmosphere and what the experience was like.

"It was mad," he says with a smile on his face. "When we had the ball, you couldn't hear anything but whistling."

The mentality to take to an occasion such as that is largely expected by prospects in the modern game. A lofty standard set by the likes of Max Dowman and Rio Ngumoha.

But it does not mean we should lose sight of it.

For comparison, the journalist typing this was pulling on the ASDA shirt at the same age.

Hemmings, meanwhile, has been fast-tracked into the senior squad at Villa, thrust into an industry where the scrutiny and pressure is unmatched, and has impressed in the early stages of his career.

Unai Emery has an eye for talent and a knack for helping prospects tap into their potential. He wouldn't provide the platform for Hemmings if he didn't see something in the versatile midfielder.

"He is really good with me. He is always just looking for the best and always trying to improve me," he says when asked about the guidance Emery offers.

A quick glance out the window and you can see his team-mate, Taylor Harwood-Bellis, leaving. But it is two chairs and a spotlight for Hemmings, rather than an early exit.

His answers are short and snappy. A young prospect still getting to grips with the added responsibilities that come with being a Premier League footballer. But just like on the pitch, he takes it in his stride.

I move the conversation onto his quick rise into the first team. Following a summer of change at Villa Park, where Morgan Rogers, Ollie Watkins, Youri Tielemans, Ezri Konsa and more all departed, Hemmings was a shock inclusion in the starting line-up against Brighton on the opening day of the season.

A chastening 4-0 defeat would follow at the Amex Stadium, but in the brief spells of positive play from 10-man Villa on that day, Hemmings showed the flashes of talent that have caught the eye of Emery since day one.

He went on to start all seven games to begin 2026/27 before a knee injury curtailed his progress, with a first goal for the senior side still eluding him. He is yet to start a game since returning but Villa have reaffirmed their belief in the player after he penned a new five-year deal at the club.

"I am excited! It is five years, so I have a long time to improve myself and improve my game." I then pose the question of where he may see himself in his career in five years' time, where he adds: "We will have to see! I just have to keep working."

It is clear he is firmly in the plans of Emery and also revealed conversations about a potential loan exit before the season began never occurred.

"I always look at what is best for my career and once I saw that I could get a few games, I was always going to stay," he continued.

The 2-1 win against Coventry, arguably his strongest performance in a Villa shirt so far, offered a glimpse into what Emery is trying to coerce out of the young talent.

Tammy Abraham's opener at the CBS Arena came courtesy of a moment of real quality from Hemmings. His perfectly placed pass with the outside of his right boot dissected Premier League opposition and made the finish easy for his team-mate.

It offered an insight into the quality Villa hope he can one day produce regularly. But the celebrations also outlined why he is in the perfect environment for that necessary development.

Abraham quickly pointed to Hemmings and embraced his team-mate. Captain John McGinn arrived with a tap on the head, with Ross Barkley and Ibrahim Mbaye following suit.

The next opportunity to impress arrives on Saturday as Villa face Ipswich, live on Sky Sports, with a six-game winless run looming over the squad.

Hemmings was already around the squad prior to the major changes that occurred over the summer. Should he continue his current trajectory, he could become an increasingly important part of Emery's plans for years to come.

At 19, he still has plenty to learn but if his early steps in senior football are anything to go by, he is unlikely to be overawed by whatever the game can throw at him. It was a short encounter with Hemmings - but you can see why he is rated so highly at the club.
