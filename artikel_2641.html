Nets blow another opportunity, collapse in 124-100 loss to Raptors

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

It's hard getting a grasp on who the Nets are these days.

Some nights, they look like they're making progress even if it doesn't equal a victory. Building momentum is the issue. After taking down the two-win Bucks on Wednesday, the matchup marked the start of a lighter patch of Brooklyn's schedule, playing a 6-11 Raptors team without Kawhi Leonard and RJ Barrett.

These are the games the Nets need to get. Instead, it's players like Immanuel Quickley — who dropped 21 points in the third quarter en route to a 24-point triple double — that ultimately doom teams like the Nets. Or they just do it to themselves.

They struggled to build a potential win streak and fell 124-100 at the hands of the Raptors, outscored 39-24 in the third quarter.

Right out of the gate, both teams applied heavy ball pressure. Toronto has a reputation for doing just that and the Nets turned the ball over four times, compared to the Raptors' two.

There were a ton of lead changes, but by the end of the frame, the Raptors were ahead by four. The Nets' offensive engine was simple from early on: Julius Randle and Michael Porter Jr. In the first quarter, they combined for 17 of Brooklyn's 27 points, with Porter Jr. posting nine points in nine minutes while Randle finished the quarter with eight points in 10 minutes.

Jordi Fernández went to the bench earlier than normal, with nine players logging minutes in the first quarter. It feels as if he has changed his philosophy recently, feeding more into the rhythm of the game and making changes based on that rather than a predetermined notion of how minutes are distributed.

Entering the game, the Raptors had established a reputation for doing the tiny things right, like applying ball pressure and winning the rebounding battle, but they just couldn't shoot well enough to finish close games.

However, the tone of the game shifted completely by halftime. It turned into an offensive shootout, even as Brooklyn still trailed 63-55. Randle and MPJ remained the Nets' top offensive threats, combining for 27 of Brooklyn's 55 first-half points.

At the half, both teams were shooting over 50% from the field, while the Nets shot a whopping 42.1% from 3-point range. Toronto's mark from downtown wasn't too shabby either, at 38.9%. The Nets struggled to take advantage of the freebies, though, shooting just 58.3% from the charity stripe, leaving five points on the table in an eight-point game by the break.

Brooklyn's toughest task in the first half was containing Scottie Barnes, who dropped 22 points in 17 minutes on 7-of-12 shooting, including a 2-of-4 mark from downtown. He led Toronto's offense early before finishing the night with 34 points.

It was a close game throughout the first half. Coming out of the break, though, that narrative flipped in an instant. Call it the Nets continuing to fulfill that "two-face" identity. You don't even have to look at it game by game to get that takeaway; it's quarter by quarter, as it was on Friday.

After the third quarter, Toronto led 102-79. All it took was one quarter, in which the Nets lost 39-24, to really put themselves in a tough hole after a pretty solid first half. On top of Barnes, Immanuel Quickley emerged as another offensive problem for the Nets, scoring 21 points in the third quarter, leading the Raptors' 39-point frame.

The damage was already done. Toronto maintained its 23-point advantage for most of the fourth quarter before closing out the 124-100 victory.

If you're looking for positives as a Nets fan, Mikel Brown Jr. set a career high with 21 points in 25 minutes and arguably put out his best game as a pro yet. Other than that, the lack of defense continues to crush Brooklyn, especially playing a team like Toronto that possesses offensive firepower.

Because of that, the Nets are now 4-14 on the season after Friday's loss.

Final score: Toronto Raptors 124, Brooklyn Nets 100

The Nets will evaluate Brown Jr. to see whether he'll play in his first full back-to-back.

The rookie had a career-high 21 points in 25 minutes.

It's a quick turnaround for the Nets, who head to Charlotte to play the second leg of their back-to-back tomorrow. After the Hornets tomorrow, they'll face Thunder two times in a row, Celtics, Pistons, Mavericks, Magic, Wizards, Blazers, and Spurs.
