Crisis engulfing Australian cricket divides the nation and parliament

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

CANBERRA — The crisis engulfing Australia's cricket team is dividing parliament, with Deputy Prime Minister Richard Marles backing captain Pat Cummins and a Liberal leadership aspirant praising Cameron Bancroft's testimony as "compelling."

The crossbench Greens Party has also demanded sporting whistleblower protections for sport be written into Australia's criminal code, with leader David Shoebridge arguing those attempting to reveal wrongdoing "carry all the risk, while administrators avoid accountability."

Bancroft, who was banned for nine months for using sandpaper on the ball during a 2018 test match, has put Cummins' leadership under threat by claiming the entire team were aware of his cheating.

Bancroft was one of only three players, also including then-captain Steve Smith and then-vice-captain David Warner, sanctioned for the saga after Cricket Australia concluded no one else was aware of the plot to tamper with the ball.

Cummins labeled claims he was aware of sandpaper being used as "100% false" on Friday, before Bancroft's account directly contradicted his statement.

Speaking to News24 on Thursday, Marles described Cummins as a "great captain" who was doing a "great job."

"He is a fantastic representative of our country as well as being a fantastic captain and bowler, and he's not so bad with the bat either," Marles said.

But Liberal frontbencher Andrew Hastie, viewed by many as a potential future leader, described Bancroft's account as "pretty compelling."

Hastie, a former SAS soldier, referenced his own history of speaking publicly after members of his regiment were accused of war crimes in Afghanistan.

"Of all the people who've spoken thus far, I'm probably most sympathetic to Cam Bancroft, who suffered the most. … He became the fall guy along with Steve Smith and Dave Warner," he told 2GB radio on Friday.

"Having lived something similar myself through the military, I'm conscious that the truth always comes out."

The scandal was brought back into the spotlight after former player Usman Khawaja, a close friend of Warner, published an autobiography allegedly the entire team was aware of efforts to tamper with the ball.

While Khajawa subsequently refused to confirm whether he was alleging Cummins knew specifically that sandpaper was being used, drawing an angry and personal response from the captain.

Khawaja has argued he did not speak up before his retirement in January because of potential consequences for his international career.

Facing another media storm eight years after the original incident, Cricket Australia announced it would introduce a private "forum" for players to relay their experiences, but would not reopen an investigation or issue additional sanctions.

While Warner and Khawaja broadly welcomed the announcement, they called for its findings to be made public.

"The public want … the truth," Warner told Fox Sports.

Shoebridge said on Thursday that the Greens will introduce an amendment to the criminal code in parliament, making it unlawful for sporting bodies to punish whistleblowers for speaking up.

"If you see cheating in sport right now the only place to report it is often the same club or sporting body that controls your career, which is a system designed to produce cover-ups," Shoebridge said.

"Whistleblowers can keep sport honest, yet right now they carry all the risk, while administrators avoid accountability."

Shoebridge did not provide specifics on how the plan would work, instead calling for Labor to legislate a scheme by the end of 2027.

Sport Integrity Australia, a federal body, was set up in 2020 to counter doping and match fixing. Whistleblower protections were not written into the legislation establishing it.
