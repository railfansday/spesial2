Former Honduran Consul General Charged and 3 Others Arrested in Alleged Bribery and Money Laundering Scheme

Ada Evila Serrano Nuñez (Ada Serrano), the former Consul General of the Honduran Consulate in Atlanta, was arrested in Atlanta on charges alleging her participation in a bribery and money laundering scheme. The indictment, which was unsealed after her arrest, includes one count of conspiracy to violate the Foreign Extortion Prevention Act (FEPA), one count of violating FEPA, and one count of conspiracy to commit money laundering. In addition to Ada Serrano, three other individuals were arrested for their alleged roles in the scheme, including her two sons, Luis Euceda Serrano (Luis Euceda) and Angel Euceda Serrano (Angel Euceda), both residents of Suwanee, Georgia, and Mario Antonio Castellanos Velasquez (Castellanos), a resident of Atlanta, and former employee of the Honduran Consulate.

“Today’s charges, the first ever brought under the Foreign Extortion Prevention Act, make clear that the Department will not let foreign officials off the hook when they abuse their positions to extort businesses,” said Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Department of Justice’s Criminal Division. “Companies should be able to play by the rules and compete based on the strength of their products and services, not based on extortion or their willingness to pay off corrupt officials. By passing this Act, Congress provided a critical enforcement tool to protect American interests, and the Criminal Division will pursue appropriate cases just like this one.”

“Foreign diplomats operating in our country may not exploit their power to extort bribes from innocent people,” said U.S. Attorney Theodore S. Hertzberg for the Northern District of Georgia. “Serrano allegedly did just that, stuffing her pockets with hundreds of thousands of dollars laundered by her sons. My office will vigorously prosecute any bribery occurring in our district, no matter how highly placed the criminal is.”

“The arrest of a former Honduran Consul General for allegedly exploiting her diplomatic position to extort bribes from the very people she was entrusted to serve marks a significant moment in the fight against foreign corruption,” said Special Agent in Charge Marlo Graham of the FBI Atlanta Field Office. “This first-ever prosecution under the Foreign Extortion Prevention Act demonstrates that no one is above the law, and the FBI will pursue those who use positions of public trust to exploit others, regardless of their title or influence.”

According to court documents, beginning at least in or around August 2024 and continuing through at least in or around March 2026, Ada Serrano, in her role as Consul General, and her co-conspirators engaged in a criminal scheme in which they corruptly demanded and received bribes in exchange for allowing clients of a company located in Tennessee (Consular Services Company) to access appointments for services at the consulate.

As alleged, Consular Services Company scheduled appointments for its clients at the consulate and provided them with transportation to and from the consulate. After Ada Serrano’s appointment as Consul General, she required that Consular Services Company pay a bribe for each person transported to the consulate. In exchange for the bribes, Ada Serrano allowed Consular Services Company to gain access to appointments at the consulate, thereby enabling its clients to attend the appointments necessary to secure passports, identification documents, and other services. Ada Serrano directed others, including Castellanos, to coordinate directly with people associated with Consular Services Company to ensure that its clients were allowed into the consulate after they paid the bribes.

To conceal the proceeds derived from the scheme, from in or around July 2023, through at least in or around December 2024, Ada Serrano allegedly directed that the cash bribes collected from Consular Services Company be paid to Luis Euceda and Angel Euceda, who then allegedly made cash deposits of between $100 and $6,000 into their personal bank accounts in the Northern District of Georgia.

The FBI’s Atlanta Field Office, with assistance from the FBI’s International Corruption Unit, is investigating the case.

Assistant Chief Derek J. Ettinger of the Criminal Division’s White Collar and Corporate Enforcement Section and Assistant U.S. Attorney Garrett L. Bradford for the Northern District of Georgia are prosecuting the case.

The Criminal Division’s White Collar and Corporate Enforcement Section is responsible for investigating and prosecuting Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) and FEPA matters. Information about the Justice Department’s FCPA and FEPA enforcement efforts can be found here.
