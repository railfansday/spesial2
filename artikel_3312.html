Bob Seger Mourns ‘Extraordinary’ Silver Bullet Band Bassist Chris Campbell

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Earlier this week, Silver Bullet Band bassist Chris Campbell, who played alongside Bob Seger from 1969 through his final tour in 2019, died of natural causes at 78. Tributes poured in from across the music world, and now Seger himself has weighed in.

“With the passing of our bassist and my friend for over 55 years, Chris Campbell, it’s taken me a couple of days to put my thoughts together, such a huge and sad loss,” Seger wrote in a statement. “Chris was with us as we started out traveling to gigs in station wagons and vans and for the next forty plus years - all the way through our last show in 2019.

“He was a great musician,” Seger continued. “Chris understood well the roots of rock and roll, rhythm and blues.  His ability to lock into a pocket with a drummer was extraordinary and he was remarkably consistent show to show.  Off stage, his sense of humor kept us laughing and entertained.  Chris was like family; we raised our kids together.  It was a blessing to share this journey with Chris, and I’ll cherish the memories forever.  RIP my friend.”

Campbell joined the Bob Seger System in 1969, just one year after “Ramblin’ Gamblin’ Man” became their first nationwide hit. “I was hired in what I thought was a temporary job as bass player because Dan Honaker had just broken his wrist about a week before the Mongrel tour [in a motorcycle accident],” Campbell told Rolling Stone in 2011. “I had a week to prepare. I had to learn this album frontwards and backwards.”

The Bob Seger System dissolved a few years later, but Seger kept Campbell in mind when he formed a new group not long afterwards. “He said, ‘I’m thinking of putting a band together,'” Campbell told Rolling Stone. “I said to him, ‘That’s kind of bizarre, I have a keyboard player and a drummer.’ He goes, ‘I got a guitarist, Drew Abbott.’ That was the start of the Silver Bullet Band.”

“This is my life, and I’m very happy with it,” Campbell told Rolling Stone. “I have wonderful kids and a good life, and we’re all friends. We’re all very fortunate. There’s no animosity and no fighting. Bob is our rock, our patriarch. We all look up to him. He’s so easy to work with. He’s a wonderful man, just as a human being.”

A couple years after a farewell tour ended in 2019, Campbell sued Seger over alleged unpaid royalty and touring payments. The majority of the case was ultimately thrown out by a judge.
