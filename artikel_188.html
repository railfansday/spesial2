National Security Agency network for internal communications goes down

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

NSA issues ‘code yellow’ as NSANet, used by staff to share classified material, including top-secret intelligence, fails

The National Security Agency said on Tuesday that it had experienced “temporary degradation” during scheduled routine maintenance, after reports that its classified internal communications network had gone down.

In a statement, the agency said some internal systems were affected but that “mission-critical capabilities remain fully operational” and its ability to defend national security continues. It said its infrastructure is built with “multiple layers of redundancy” to keep operations running during planned maintenance or unexpected events.

The statement did not name the network, say what caused the problem or how long it lasted, or say whether service had been fully restored.

The New York Times and CNN had earlier reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that NSANet, the network the agency uses to send and receive classified information including top-secret material, was offline on Tuesday. They said staff at the agency’s Fort Meade, Maryland, headquarters had been told to stagger their arrivals while it assessed the problem. The Wall Street Journal also reported the outage, citing one person familiar with the situation, who said it stemmed from a software update to patch the system and that it would only be down temporarily.

The Times reported that a recorded message on an employee information line described a “code yellow”, the second-highest level in a three-tier alert system, for the day shift. Non-emergency staff were told not to come in before noon, while emergency personnel were to report as scheduled, the paper said.

Both CNN and the Times, citing people familiar with the matter, said there was no sign so far that the outage was caused by a cyber-attack or other malicious activity. The Times, citing one person, reported that the network had been unstable for weeks, and had been a cause of frustration among staff.

A former senior NSA cybersecurity official, who has no knowledge of the incident beyond public reports, speculated that several software patches may have been applied at once and something had gone wrong.

The outage follows a run of technology failures in the US in recent days, including a nationwide ground stop of flights, a Microsoft outage and disruption to banking services. None of those have been tied to a cyber-attack, though the causes of some have not been fully explained. Officials said the ground stop was not the result of malicious activity by a foreign actor, and the transportation secretary, Sean Duffy, said they were investigating whether contractor error or a faulty router was responsible.

Trump has not commented directly on the NSA outage. Asked in Manila on Tuesday about the constant outages, he played them down, saying such problems were inevitable: “Things like that happen.”

He said he had spoken with banks and that the situation would be “very much under control”, and that Americans travelling before Thanksgiving were safe.

Hakeem Jeffries, the House Democratic leader, called for the Trump administration to send officials to brief lawmakers on Capitol Hill about the recent nationwide technical and infrastructure outages, telling reporters that the public deserves more transparency around the widespread disruptions.

While none of the incidents have been classified as a cyber-attack, Mike Wiacek, a former US Department of Defense analyst, said the run of failures in a single week was a reason for caution and that early assurances may have come too quickly.

“Without data, I can’t say for certain whether this is or isn’t a cyber attack,” Wiacek said. “But four major outages in under a week (Outlook, the FAA, the banks and now the NSA) look suspiciously like what I’d expect one to look like.

“What concerns me most is how quickly officials ruled out an attack,” he said. “You can’t compress months of forensic work into a few hours.”
