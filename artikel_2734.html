Kentucky tumbles to No. 13 in updated AP poll

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Some losses you carry home. Others carry you, in the dark, up the long road out of Knoxville, with the game film already replaying behind your eyelids.

Kentucky football took the second kind on Saturday night. Tennessee 34, Kentucky 6. By Sunday the voters had finished the arithmetic. The Wildcats, No. 8 a week ago, are now No. 13 in the AP Top 25, down five spots at 7-2. They have 897 points and the quiet of a team that learned what it is not yet.

Will Stein's ball club walked into Neyland Stadium with the SEC standings in its pocket and a chance to do what Kentucky teams have chased across generations: win in Knoxville, a place the Wildcats haven't won since 1984. The Volunteers answered like a program that remembers every year of that drought. Kentucky's offense managed six points. By the time Tennessee stretched its lead to 20-6 early in the fourth quarter, the game was a formality. Safety Qua Moss forced two fumbles, Dejuan Lane picked off a pass, and every time the Wildcats reached for momentum the Vols' defense took it back.

The poll made it official. Tennessee, No. 16 before kickoff, vaulted seven spots to No. 9 with 1,177 points. That is 280 more than Kentucky, a gap that was a footnote on Friday and is a canyon now.

Elsewhere, the SEC has its king back. Georgia is No. 1 again at 8-1, with 1,729 points and 53 first-place votes, the clear choice of the room. Kirby Smart's Bulldogs climb one spot into the driver's seat and have Missouri next. Utah, unbeaten at 9-0, sits second with 1,675 points and 12 first-place votes, just 54 behind. Notre Dame is third at 8-1 with five first-place votes, and unbeaten UCLA is fourth. Miami, No. 1 a week ago, falls four spots to fifth at 8-1. Indiana is sixth.

The rest of the league is thick in the poll. Eight SEC teams are ranked, and a ninth, Missouri, collected 50 votes on the outside.

No. 13 Kentucky (7-2), home against Florida

Nobody in that group can look past the next game, and Kentucky least of all.

That makes Saturday night the one that matters. Florida comes to Kroger Field without a vote in this poll, unranked and unlisted among the others receiving votes. That is the kind of opponent that should be easy to dismiss and is dangerous for exactly that reason. A wounded team gets a visitor with nothing to lose, under the lights, in a stadium that has not been kind to anyone who forgot where they were.

Kentucky has to rebuild its margin, and it is thinner than it looks. The Wildcats sit 144 points behind No. 12 BYU and only 94 ahead of No. 14 Houston, which is 7-2 and not finished climbing. A poll is a rumor the voters agree to believe, and the rumor on Kentucky changes fast.

But a season is not one night in Knoxville, and Stein's team did not become a bad football team in the span of three hours. This is a group that went through a stretch of the schedule most programs would have signed for. It needs the same stubborn thing that built the 7-1 start: a clean week, a loud crowd and a first quarter that belongs to the home team.

Caturday night, in Lexington, is a good place to start.
