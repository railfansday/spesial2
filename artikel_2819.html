AP Top 25 poll discussion: Louisville is now the beneficiary of the UConn win boost, rising to No. 3 in the rankings

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

This year's UConn team doesn't (yet) look like UConn.

And yet, a victory over the Huskies still comes with a guaranteed jump up the rankings.

After Michigan moved from No. 5 to No. 2 in Week 1 of the AP Top 25 poll, Louisville leaps from No. 7 to No. 3 for Week 2, soaring after sending UConn, now No. 7, to a second loss with a resounding 69-51 win.

Imari Berry led the Cardinals with 21 points, draining five triples. The Cardinals also overwhelmed the Huskies on the glass, with 5-foot-7 Zam Jones grabbing nine rebounds to go with her 10 points, five assists, three steals and three blocks. Mackenly Randolph also had 10 points and nine rebounds, while Taj Roberts posted 10 points and eight boards.

The Cardinals' four-spot rise was the highest of the week, with Oklahoma State's fall from No. 19 to No. 25 representing the biggest drop at six spots.

Much has been made of Michigan's junior of trio of Olivia Olson, Syla Swords and Mila Holloway remaining in Ann Arbor, none seduced by transfer portal promises. Fellow junior Te'Yala Delfosse, an emerging star who originally was less heralded than the other three, also has worn maize and blue for her three seasons of college basketball.

Louisville likewise sports a junior class that has stuck together in Imari Berry, Taj Roberts and Mackenly Randolph, while senior Elif Istanbulluoglu also has spent her entire career with the Cardinals.

Both programs have benefitted from the portal. Yet, they have layered other talents on top of their core, rather creating new team identities to accommodate transfers.

After now-senior Ashley Sofilkanich, previously at Bucknell, joined the Woliveriens last season, Courtney Ogden has arrived from Stanford appearing poised to add some off-the-bench experience. Louisville has added in transfers Zam Jones (NC State), Carys Baker (Virginia Tech) and Amiya Joiner (LSU) to complement their existing core.

There's no right way to build a women's college basketball program, especially in this still shifting and uncertain landscape. In the end, the team with a lot of talent, a little luck, will probably be raising the trophy.

It's worth noting, however, that two teams that entered the season as questionable contenders but now look like solid bets to threaten for the Final Four, have striking continuity.

Last year, Duke began the season ranked No. 7.

When they played their first ACC game, a visit to Virginia Tech on Dec. 7, they were 3-6, ousted from the top 25 and considered college basketball's biggest disappointment. The Blue Devils eventually rebounded, winning both the ACC regular-season and conference titles before advancing to the Elite Eight.

A tough non-conference schedule largely explained Duke's lackluster start. At one point, they lost consecutive games to then-No. 2 South Carolina, then-No. 3 UCLA and then-No. 5 LSU. The difficult slate, while painful, steeled the Blue Devils, making them stronger for when the games really mattered in March.

This season's UConn squad might be setting up for a similar fate.

The Huskies have already accumulated two losses, and, if not for a pair of Sarah Strong masterclasses, might have one or two more. And as Zack Ward recently noted, the schedule is NOT becoming more friendly. UConn's Thanksgiving week is sandwiched with games against No. 1 South Carolina and No. 5 Duke. Back-to-back dates with No. 18 Maryland and No. 13 Notre Dame follow in early December before a pre-Christmas clash with No. 9 LSU.

If recent performances are any indication, UConn's record might be pocked with more than a few Ls before they begin Big East play.

The hope is that those (potential) losses become lessons that, eventually, help lift UConn back to their expected perch near the very top of the women's basketball world by March.
