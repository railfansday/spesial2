UWF tops out Darrell Gooden Stadium, remains on track for 2027 opening

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

For former UWF president Judy Bense, Wednesday's topping out ceremony for Darrell Gooden Stadium shows the power of football in Pensacola.

What else would get hundreds of people to show up on Wednesday morning to sign the final steel beam placed on a new campus project?

It's what Bense, who led the university from 2008-16, envisioned when they added football in 2016 under her leadership. From what started with makeshift bleachers on a practice field is now one step closer to transforming into a stadium for an NCAA Division I team. The final structural beam for Darrell Gooden Stadium, which is being built to expand PenAir Field, was put in place just after 11 a.m. on Nov. 18.

"I knew that as a president I knew that nothing else would be the draw if we would just start," Bense said. "And boy we started. So I feel very happy and satisfied."

The ceremony marked the end of construction for the building's framework, signifying that the highest point of the building is in place. People were invited to sign their name on the beam, which was raised into place with an American flag on one side, a UWF flag on the other and then an evergreen tree in the middle to symbolize growth and good fortune.

Officials broke ground on Darrell Gooden Stadium on Feb. 11, 2026, which is estimated to cost $55 million, expand seating capacity from 3,800 to 7,500 with a total capacity of more than 10,000. The new facility will add bleacher and stadium seating to the bleachers currently at PenAir Field as well as premium seating areas that can be used not just for home football games but other on-campus events.

Construction remains on schedule even after Hurricane Isaias ripped through the campus last month, with everything set to be up and running for the Argos first home football game of the 2027 season. UWF is currently 4-5 overall and 1-5 in the United Athletic Conference in its first season as a Division I program.

"My office is literally right out over there, (so) looking out the window every day I get to see literally every day of growth," UWF football head coach Kaleb Nobles said. "So it just puts it into a tangible thing of we're growing this university. You talk about growing in enrollment, we're growing also in facilities. So this what's needed and what we want. A Division I football program, Division I athletics, you got to start growing and this is the next step in it."

Darrell and Debbie Gooden got the project off the ground with a $9 million donation in April 2024, which was the largest gift from a living donor in the history of the university. The donation later grew to over $10 million, which was on top of Darrell Gooden's lead gift to build the Darrell Gooden Center, which included a new football locker room and sports performance center for all student athletes.

A 1973 UWF graduate, Gooden died on June 30 after a battle with cancer.

"I will never come out to this field without thinking about Darrell," associate vice president of athletics Dave Scott said. "I think he'll make an impact on a lot of young men and women here at the university as this facility progresses. I had some opportunity to spend some time with him right before he passed and this was his dream as a graduate and alum of the University of West Florida, to make this come true.
