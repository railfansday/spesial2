4 Ways The Lakers Were Outplayed By The Jazz In 109-97 Defeat

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Los Angeles Lakers suffered a frustrating 109-97 loss to the Utah Jazz, failing to be efficient on offense while allowing Keyonte George to take control of the game.

Despite entering the matchup with enough firepower to compete, the Lakers never established a rhythm and fell short against a Jazz team that executed much better on both ends.

George led Utah with 30 points and 10 assists, while Ace Bailey contributed 19 points and Svi Mykhailiuk added 15 off the bench. The Jazz also received a massive rebounding performance from Mo Bamba, who finished with 16 boards.

Luka Doncic scored 29 points and added seven assists for Los Angeles, while Quentin Grimes chipped in 20 points. However, Doncic's nine turnovers and the Lakers' poor shooting prevented them from building any sustained momentum.

Here are four reasons the Lakers were outplayed by Utah.

The biggest difference was the quality of each team's shooting. The Jazz finished 37-77 from the field, shooting an impressive 48.1%, while the Lakers managed just 34-89 attempts, finishing at 38.2%.

Utah also had a significant advantage from beyond the arc. The Jazz knocked down 14 of 37 three-point attempts (37.8%), compared to just nine of 33 (27.3%) for Los Angeles.

George was the biggest problem for the Lakers, scoring 30 points on 9-17 shooting and connecting on five three-pointers. Mykhailiuk added another four triples, finishing with 15 points on 4-10 shooting from deep.

Meanwhile, Doncic struggled to find efficiency despite his 29 points. He shot 10-28 from the field and just 2-12 from three-point range. Grimes was more productive with 20 points on 8-20 shooting, but the Lakers needed considerably more efficiency from the rest of the lineup.

Los Angeles simply took too many difficult shots and failed to convert enough opportunities. Against a Utah team shooting nearly 50% from the field, that was a recipe for defeat.

George delivered the type of performance that makes life difficult for any opposing defense. The Jazz guard finished with 30 points and 10 assists, shooting 9-17 from the field and 5-10 from three-point range.

His scoring was impressive, but his playmaking was just as important. George consistently created opportunities for teammates, helping Utah finish with 29 assists compared to Los Angeles' 24.

The Lakers struggled to contain his combination of scoring and distribution. When defenders gave him room, he punished them from the perimeter. When the defense focused on stopping his scoring, he found ways to involve his teammates.

Bailey also benefited from Utah's offensive rhythm, adding 19 points and eight rebounds. Mykhailiuk provided another important scoring option with 15 points off the bench.

The Lakers had individual defensive contributions, including four steals from Matisse Thybulle, but they could not slow down Utah's overall offensive execution. George was the primary reason.

Doncic finished with 29 points, five rebounds, and seven assists, but his nine turnovers were a major problem for Los Angeles.

The Lakers committed 18 turnovers overall, compared to 23 for Utah. While that number suggests Los Angeles actually protected the ball better as a team, Doncic's individual mistakes came at a costly rate for the team's primary ball-handler.

Utah scored 29 points off turnovers, compared to 26 for the Lakers. The Jazz also recorded 11 steals, matching Los Angeles, and converted defensive pressure into opportunities to maintain control.

Doncic's offensive production was still valuable, but his inefficiency made it difficult for the Lakers to establish consistent possessions. He shot just 35.7% from the field and 16.7% from three-point range.

The Lakers need Doncic to be aggressive, but they also need him to make better decisions with the basketball. Against a team that was already shooting efficiently, turnovers were a recipe for disaster.

The Lakers actually had a 14-7 advantage in offensive rebounds, but Utah controlled the overall rebounding battle and did a much better job protecting its defensive glass.

The Jazz finished with 45 rebounds compared to Los Angeles' 42. More importantly, Utah collected 38 defensive rebounds, while the Lakers managed only 28.

Bamba was particularly effective, finishing with 16 rebounds, including 15 defensive boards. His presence helped Utah limit second-chance opportunities despite Los Angeles' advantage in offensive rebounding.

The Jazz also recorded five blocks compared to just one for the Lakers.

Both teams scored 46 points in the paint, so Utah did not necessarily dominate the Lakers inside offensively. Instead, its advantage came from protecting the rim, securing defensive rebounds, and limiting Los Angeles' ability to generate additional possessions.

The Lakers had opportunities to make the game closer through offensive rebounding, but their poor shooting and inability to consistently convert second chances hurt them in the end.
