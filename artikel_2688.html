‘A fake problem’: Rachel Maddow calls out ‘pitiful’ trans-athlete panic

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Republicans suffered major losses in last week's midterm elections, even as voters in several states approved bans on transgender athletes in women's sports. Now, longtime cable news personality Rachel Maddow thinks that it's time to call the trans-athlete debate what it is.

During an appearance on the Pablo Torre Finds Out podcast, the MS NOW host called the trans-athlete debate a "fake problem" invented by the right. She also labeled the political campaign around it "repulsive" and said that it has been exposed as a sign of "profound political weakness."

Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate in last week's midterm elections, dealing a major blow to President Donald Trump and the GOP. Pablo Torre took a shot at JD Vance after the results, and he welcomed Maddow onto his show days later to break down the election.

Still, voters in four states approved ballot measures banning transgender athletes from girls' and women's sports, including left-leaning Washington and Colorado. Arizona and Nebraska passed bans of their own.

"The reality is undeniable: fearmongering about transgender people is a losing strategy," GLAAD president and CEO Sarah Kate Ellis said in a statement after the election, via ESPN.

The issue made a lot of headlines over the summer as the conversation made its way into the WNBA after Indiana Fever guard Sophie Cunningham became a conservative favorite after telling ESPN that she wanted to protect "young girls in sport who shouldn't have to go against biological men."

Cunningham's comments sparked a media firestorm lasting months. The controversy led to protests from both sides of the issue and publicity stunts from right-wing personalities while the players and coaches were turned into political pawns. Despite all of this, it's worth noting that there has never been a transgender woman in the WNBA.

Torre brought up the topic by pointing to Jay Feely, the former NFL kicker who has been outspoken on trans athletes. Feely ran for Congress in Arizona as a Republican, but he lost to Democrat Amish Shah, who was once his team doctor with the New York Jets, per AZFamily.

"I'm wondering how that issue feels to you as it was, as many have said, an 80-20 winner for the Republican Party and simultaneously the thing that did not work," Torre asked, in a clip shared by the podcast.

Maddow started by pointing to the real-world impact that the issue has had on trans people.

"It turns out the trans athletes thing, I mean, there's a lot of actual very serious elements of it. And number one is just seeing this national, insistent, now yearslong political project to scapegoat and vilify and try to direct violence and hate towards a tiny, disfavored minority, which is trans people," Maddow said.

"Like, that is something that has profound, profound impact on some of our fellow Americans. And it is repulsive, and it is politically as transparent as any other exercise like this has been from any other authoritarian movement in politics in any country in the last century and a half," she continued.

Maddow then argued that the issue simply doesn't affect most Americans.

"And politically, it is a fantasy to invent a fake problem. To invent something that has literally no contact with the real lives of 99.9999% of Americans and say this is the problem that the American government must be reorganized to prioritize and to never solve for you, so we can continue to run on it forever, is pitiful, a sign of profound political weakness, and something that I think has now been exposed as the fantasist BS copout that it is," Maddow said.

Torre also pointed to a poll his show commissioned, which found that a combined 15% of Americans thought that more than 5% of NCAA athletes identify as trans. When the show reached out to the NCAA, Torre said that the number of trans athletes was "effectively zero."
