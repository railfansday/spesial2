Eagles News: Moro Ojomo is drawing “real interest” ahead of the NFL trade deadline

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Welcome to The Linc, the daily Philadelphia Eagles links roundup at Bleeding Green Nation!

I just don't believe the Eagles are seriously interested in paying Moro Ojomo after signing Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis to big-money contract extensions. Unless they move on from Carter or Davis … or plan to do so in the offseason. Seems to me like they'll trade Ojomo ahead of the deadline. If there isn't a great offer, they can just keep him and hope he factors in to their compensatory pick formula. Now, what can they get for him? Hard to say for sure. I think some might overstate his value considering he has zero sacks this season and only six in his career. But he's better than those numbers would indicate. Maybe a third-round pick?

Let's get to the Philadelphia Eagles news and links …

NFL Week 9 latest intel, buzz questions, fantasy tips and trade deadline news – ESPN

This could be for edge or interior help, in part because the Jaguars have versatile players such as Travon Walker who can play multiple spots up front. Eagles defensive tackle Moro Ojomo is a person of interest here, though Philly is inclined to keep and potentially extend him. […] Speaking of Philadelphia, my sense is the Eagles could be trading some players but likely won't engage in a rebuild. As one source pointed out, that's not in the Eagles' organizational DNA. We already know they aren't trading running back Saquon Barkley. Look, let's not be naive — general manager Howie Roseman will take advantage of good value if it's there, and supporting players are surely on the radar. But dealing big names would be a mild surprise. Ojomo will be a test of the Eagles' transitional state. There's real interest in him (from Jacksonville and others). Whether Philly acts on that interest will tell a lot.

Eagles thoughts: Nick Sirianni's staff stays put, questions at QB and Moro Ojomo's trade value – The Athletic

Moro Ojomo is a fascinating player to watch during the next week leading up to the trade deadline. The Eagles don't like letting talented homegrown defensive linemen out the door — and they're feeling the pain of seeing Milton Williams and Josh Sweat go elsewhere — but it's hard to see a scenario where Ojomo returns next season with Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis still on the roster. Sports Illustrated reported last week that the Eagles are telling teams they're not interested in trading Ojomo and want to keep him, but will they pay three defensive tackles top-of-the-league money (assuming that's what Ojomo commands on the open market)? They seem to have made their choice when they signed Carter and Davis. "Howie (Roseman) and (Jeffrey) Lurie mean so much to me for giving me this opportunity to play for this team," Roseman said. "I think I was the 23rd DT picked up? If we could make it work, I love playing for the city; I love working hard for the city. I want to produce results for the city. I hate letting them down and being 2-5 like we are. … I mean, I don't think any door is shut. I wouldn't say that. You don't know what the future holds." It's a nice thought. It doesn't seem realistic. The Eagles would be smart to get compensation for Ojomo now, especially in what could be a seller's market at defensive tackle. If they can land a third-round pick, it would be better than any potential compensatory pick in 2028. Perhaps they can find a way to get a second-round pick. Ojomo said he doesn't know what will happen — "I just work here," he said — but he has the talent to help a contending team down the stretch. It hasn't shown in his statistics this year. I believe in the player, and he's also a value-add in the locker room. The Eagles will miss him if he's gone. It's the reality of the NFL. "I like the way I started the season a little bit," Ojomo said. "The last couple games being banged up and all that, I want to get back to kind of playing confident, fast. I think that 'get off' is one of the things I do at a really high level. That's one of the things I want to kind of lock in on these next however many games."

Eagles-Commanders Film Review: The idea that Jalen Hurts was holding this offense back should be finished by now – BGN

I'm struggling to get particularly angry about this offense anymore. There were times when watching the Eagles lose 27-10 to the Commanders bored me. We keep seeing players asked to do things that don't suit them. Every week, I sit down hoping to see something different, and every week, it feels like I'm writing about the same problems. The Eagles scored their final points with 4:27 remaining in the first quarter. There was an awful lot of football left to sit through after that. The idea that Jalen Hurts was holding this offense back, and that everything would look dramatically different with a quarterback who preferred playing under center and throwing over the middle, should be finished by now. Tanner McKee hasn't transformed anything. It's the same offense, with the same limitations, and very little downfield passing game. That doesn't mean Hurts was playing perfectly or that McKee has played well. He hasn't. It means changing the quarterback was never going to solve all of this.

Bowen: As the Eagles lurch further into the throes of disaster, shouldn't somebody down there get at least a little upset about this?- PHLY Eagles

I would not make a good NFL head coach or coordinator for many reasons. High on that list would be, I can't imagine being at ground level of a disaster like we currently are experiencing with the Eagles, and not reacting with urgency, frustration, and alarm. The Eagles' coordinators held their weekly media briefings Tuesday. I'm smart enough to know that the manner they convey to reporters doesn't necessarily mirror their private reactions. They don't want their players to think they are panicking. Even so, the matter-of-fact, "we just need to tweak this or that" patter at this point in the season is jarring to the ear. Six losses in a row? The last three by double figures? All to teams that currently have losing records? Defensive coordinator Vic Fangio has been known to get acerbic when his players aren't performing to his standards. Not this season, which is shaping up as the worst by far in Fangio's three years with the Eagles. Vic seems a bit distant these days, almost sedated. When asked to comment on specific players, he either praises them, as he has done repeatedly when asked about Cooper DeJean's uncharacteristic struggles, or refers to injuries they are working through.

In Roob's Eagles Stats: A big pile of misery broken up by tiny encouraging blips – NBCSP

The Eagles have had fewer than 350 yards of offense in all eight games so far this year, with a high of 341 at Tennessee. This is the first time in more than half a century they've opened a season with eight games under 350 net yards. Last time it happened was the first 11 games of the 1971 season, when their yardage high was 315 yards in a 37-14 loss to the Bengals in the season opener at Riverfront Stadium. Going back to last year, the Eagles have gone 10 straight games with fewer than 350 yards, their longest streak since an 11-gamer over the last nine games of 1977 and the first games of 1978. This is not good.
