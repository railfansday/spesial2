Tanggal: 2026-11-14



In order to make a comparison to sell yourself rather than do a trade in there is often a tax calculation on the trade value - at least in the states that we have bought and sold. In those cases if the tradein has a relative large value the potential tax on that trade value becomes part of the decision of whether or not to sell privately.
Factory warrantees which are transferable can sometimes be extended for reasonable amounts (if you shop them) which makes a private resale even more appealing.
Something I only recently learned is that the tax on a vehicle in my state is - as of a new law effective July 1 2026 - now based on the price you pay for it (according to the receipt), while previously the tax would be based on book value if the price you (allegedly) paid was below book value. The previous law prescribed the exact procedure for determining your tax bill.

However what hasn't changed is that tax is based on the full price of a vehicle and doesn't take trade-ins into account.
In NY it is an 8.75% - that would mean the private sale would need to be 8.75% above trade value to come out ahead. Other states have varied rates and rules.
