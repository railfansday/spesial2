Personal Finance (Not Investing) • Re: So many mistakes in our trust documents, mention AI analysis or not?

Tanggal: 2026-11-13



I guess my concern is that I know that excellent estate planning attorneys are in high demand (at least in CA) and they have a lot of discretion about whom they take as clients. The same is true for excellent CPAs. I chose our current attorney when she was a new associate at the firm and within a few years she became partner with an essentially closed practice. If I become a nuisance client complaining about “what Claude said” I may find myself relegated to communicating with whomever is least busy at the firm. That’s how we ended up with an amendment that introduced contradictions and other inconsistencies. At the time we requested the amendment our attorney was overwhelmed with tax work and asked if her colleague could handle the amendment for us.
To the extent you're concerned about coming off as a pain-in-the-rear client, I recommend not trying to conceal use of AI (because you're probably not going to be able to succeed in concealing it). Instead, focus on asking for help figuring out if these really are issues and correcting them if necessary. You might also want to keep the more minor nits to yourself until you have a turn at the documents where you can propose the line edits yourself rather than paying a partner to interpret the AI output and make the changes herself.

The complaints about clients using AI are most often complaints about clients raising scattershot or disproportionately voluminous issues where there's no evidence that the client herself actually thought about or cares about the issues. That doesn't sound like your situation so it shouldn't be hard to avoid looking like that kind of client.
This is good advice, thank you. Doing a restatement of the trust at a new firm is probably the best way forward now, as suggested by bsteiner. We'll be able to use the information from the AI summary as a building block, instead of trying to repair the existing document.
