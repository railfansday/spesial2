I'm one of those investors, and I have pretty much a Boglehead portfolio. I've mentioned this elsewhere in this thread that I am fairly equity-heavy, in stark contrast to what the OP of this thread speculated that VHNW investors do.

This is for two major reasons.

1) My spend is very low relative to my NW (my yearly pre-tax spend is around 1% of my portfolio value, add taxes and charitable contributions and my withdrawal rate is around 2%), so I can stomach volatility.

2) My equity portion has a very low cost basis (and my portfolio is almost entirely in taxable accounts), so selling stock to get to even 20% fixed would be a major tax blow (I'd have to realize a few million in LTCG to go up another 10%). It's taken me years to even close to 10% fixed from what had been a 100% equities portfolio, since I was trying to stretch the taxes out over time. And because I can stomach the volatility in my stock index funds, I haven't felt the need to accelerate the process and take the major tax hit.

Also, if you lurk around Reddit in the FIRE forums, you'll see a lot of VHNW people advocating self-management, index investing, avoiding "alts," etc. In fact, when you see people in those subreddits recommending working with wealth managers for access to private equity, etc., you'll see that those accounts are often wealth managers astroturfing for their products.

A lot of VNHW people are basically Bogleheads.

It's not universal, but we're well-represented. And there is a wide variety of what people do in terms of investing approaches and asset allocations at VHNW levels, just like there is a wide variety of risk tolerances and approaches at "normal" levels.
