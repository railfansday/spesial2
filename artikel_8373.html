The classic LTC response. It is a valid concern, and I don’t want you to think that I am poo-pooing it by the following comment. Long-term care is very expensive for the select few that need it for more than just a couple of years. But, if we are starting off with millions, such as we are discussing here, and we are adjusting spending based on the reality of market returns, that should mean that we come to the point of our lives when and if we need long-term care, we still have a huge chunk of money to pay for it. Not only do we have our portfolio, we also have the value of the house that we eventually will not need. And lastly, it is OK to use up some of that portfolio before we die. It is OK that our heirs inherit a bit less money.

Realize, by the time that they do inherit this money, they will already be retired or close to it. If they had any reasonable success in their lives, this money that they will receive, will likely not be necessary and will only be a boost to what they have already accumulated. So yeah, they will be the ones flying 1st class because you chose not to.
Oh, we definitely chose to!
Maybe I misunderstood you or made assumptions about you that were incorrect. My apologies if that is the case.

If you have plenty of money and spending it to your desire, then odds are even if LTC expenses are going to last longer than just a few years, you and your portfolio are in a perfectly fine shape to handle those costs. Why even worry?

My dad is not a big fan of bonds, but after explaining the need to have some "safe assets" for potential end of life healthcare needs, my dad agreed to set aside $1 million in bonds. My parents also get SS of about $85k. Plus they have the rest of their portfolio, and, if needed they have their house. I am very confident that they can now spend down their portfolio to their hearts content and I implore them to do so. When they do pass away, any inheritance I receive will not impact my life all that much so why not spend it. Sadly, they probably don't have another 30 years which means they can spend more than 4% with a low probability of running out of money.

I think some people add LTC costs on top of current expenses which is very unrealistic. Sure, groceries, insurances, and home upkeep costs will be unchanged unless the house is sold. But if one needs extensive nursing needs, that person is not traveling, not going out for shows, not driving, and spending a lot less on discretionary items. All that gets funneled towards nursing needs.
