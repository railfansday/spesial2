SFP+ transceivers and DACs for your homelab: coding, compatibility, and the traps

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

10GbE at home is cheap now. A used dual-port Intel X520 or Mellanox ConnectX-3 costs less than a decent mechanical keyboard, and a second-hand SFP+ switch (MikroTik CRS309/CRS317, Dell N4032F, Brocade ICX6610, Arista 7050S) has come way down. The part that still bites people is the little module you push into the cage. Here is what actually matters when you buy transceivers and DACs used.

The cage is physically the same across SFP (1G), SFP+ (10G) and SFP28 (25G). That does not mean any module works in any port.

Check the switch datasheet for per-port speed, not just "has SFP+ ports". Some cheap switches have one or two SFP+ uplinks and the rest are 1G SFP.

Direct Attach Copper (DAC) is a fixed-length twinax cable with the modules molded on each end. For a rack where switch and server are within ~3 m, this is the right answer: cheapest, lowest power (~0.1 W vs ~1 W for an SR optic), no fiber to keep clean, near-zero latency. Passive DAC tops out around 3 m for 10G, 5 m for active DAC.

AOC (Active Optical Cable) is the same molded-end idea but with fiber in the middle, for 5-30 m runs without dealing with connectors.

Do not mix module types on the two ends. SR talks to SR, LR to LR, and the fiber must match (OM3/OM4 for SR, OS2 for LR). Mixing OM and OS2, or SR with LR, is the most common "no light" ticket.

Every transceiver has an EEPROM that reports a vendor name and part number. Many enterprise switches check it against a whitelist and refuse or error-disable an "unknown" module. This is the single biggest reason a working transceiver "doesn't work".

If you buy from a specialist, ask for modules coded for your switch brand (Cisco-coded, Dell-coded, etc.). Vendors like FS.com, 10Gtek and ProLabs sell the same optic coded to order, and a used batch is often already coded for whatever fleet it came out of. A Finisar or Avago OEM module (e.g. FTLX8574D3BCL) with generic coding will link on MikroTik/Arista/Juniper and may need the override elsewhere.

10G optics support DDM/DOM (digital diagnostics). After it links, pull the readout:

You want Tx power and Rx power inside the module's spec window (roughly -1 to -7 dBm for an SR at short range), temperature under ~60 C, and no "high/low alarm" flags. A module reading -12 dBm Rx on a 2 m patch is dying or the fiber end is filthy. Clean with a proper one-click cleaner, never canned air.
