Import once, rebuild everything: why a nightly Apple Health rebuild loses your deltas

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The nightly rebuild is the pipeline almost everyone writes first. Drop the export, recompute every day from scratch, write it out. It is idempotent, it is easy to reason about, and it quietly throws away the one thing a health feed needs: which day changed, and why.

The longer version, with the live proof, is on the blog: Import Once, Rebuild Everything.

A full rebuild earns its place. It is stateless: delete the output, re-derive it, and you get the same answer every time. No cache to invalidate, no partial state to repair, no ordering to get wrong. If your export were immutable, it would be the right answer forever.

It is not immutable. Late-arriving samples are normal, not an edge case. A watch uploads a workout hours after you recorded it, a ring or scale syncs overnight, a manual entry lands after the fact. So a day you already read can change, and the model stops paying for itself.

On a cache written by the app, supported is true and the block names the days rebuilt after late-arriving samples, with any day too old to rebuild called out separately. When the file cannot answer the question, the server says supported: null and tells you why, instead of returning backfilled_days: 0 as if nothing had changed.

A count of zero and an unknown are different answers. A health feed should never confuse them.
