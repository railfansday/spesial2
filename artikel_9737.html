Why Stripe is not what you need to charge for your AI product (kind of)

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Like everyone else, you build a product and need to charge your customers. Your first instinct is to go to Stripe. Stripe is the GOAT, so of course you'll use it.

This is true and untrue, let's say you're building an AI product. You probably won't charge your customers a flat monthly fee that covers all usage as they have a really different cost for your business depending on how they use your product. Instead, you might want to have pricing based on usage. Sounds good. Stripe covers that, case closed... Well, yes and no. There are two types of usage-based billing. One lets customers use the product and pay later, which exposes your business to unpaid bills, and we don't want that... The other uses credits purchased up front.

Credits are what most businesses are moving toward nowadays. They give security on both sides, for the business and the customer. They also show you not only how your customers use your product but more importantly how they spend in your product.

So you're sold on credits, great, let's set them up in Stripe. This is where things start to get complicated. There's a new account system to figure out, confusion between usage-based billing and credits, meters to set up, and that one credits tab to find in Stripe's whole suite of products to monitor them. Then you have to make sure all the pieces work together, figure out how to keep your customers' credit balances in sync. Oh, and if you want to calculate your margin on every credit spent... good luck...

It's doable, but far from trivial. It is similar to deploying your app on Vercel in a minute versus taking a course on how to deploy on AWS and rearchitect your whole app to match AWS architecture. You have better things to do, right? You're building an AI product, not a credit system. You want an abstraction layer on top that makes your life easier, not harder.

By now, I hope you're convinced there's an easier way (even if your agent does most of the work). If you've used something like Vercel, it's hard to imagine going back to DevOps and the low-level infrastructure of AWS. This is where a platform built for credits makes sense. It handles a lot of those architectural decisions for you so you don't have to stitch everything together yourself, losing a bit of confidence with every stitch. You want to know which features customers spend their credits on, how much each of those features cost you and if your margin are protected even if you give away promotional credits.

Of course, managing credits and taking payments for them are two different jobs. You still want an established solution for payments, and Stripe is the best at that. That's why this credit layer and payment solution should work together and integrate all the best things from Stripe.

That's what we're building with Orbyte Labs. We've built credit systems too many times and taken too many shortcuts in the past that came back to hurt the business later. We want to do for credits what Vercel did for application deployment. Keep the power of Stripe without having to deal with the complexity of building your own credit infrastructure.
