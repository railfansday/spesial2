The U.S. economy grew at a 3.4% annual rate in the third quarter and inflation picked up to 3.6% in September, the Bureau of Economic Analysis reported on Thursday, October 29, hours before crypto stocks Coinbase and Strategy report earnings.

The sector was already down before the data and barely moved on it. Coinbase traded 2.4% below Wednesday’s close before the open, Strategy 2.3% and Circle 2.5%. The bigger story is the month: bitcoin miners are down 31% to 37% in October and Coinbase 15%, against a 4% drop for Bitcoin itself.

So what did the data show, why are the shares falling so much harder than the coin they track, and what do tonight’s results need to show?

Because they carry Bitcoin’s risk plus their own. From the September 30 close to Thursday’s premarket, Bitcoin is down 4.2% and the Nasdaq 100 tracker QQQ 2.9%. Most of the sector has lost several times that:

Circle is the exception. Its revenue comes from interest on the reserves behind USDC, so higher rates help it, and it has held up better than Bitcoin this month.

For Coinbase, that trading held up better than Robinhood’s. For Strategy, that its Bitcoin bet still sits above cost. Both report after the close, with Coinbase’s call at 5 p.m. ET.

That cost basis is the number to watch. At $80,108, Bitcoin is about 6% above Strategy’s average price, and down from $83,624 at the end of September, so the fourth quarter has started with a paper loss of roughly $3 billion on the company’s holdings.

Bitcoin itself is holding its October floor. It trades just below its 50-day EMA at $80,799, with support at $79,221, the October 24 low, and resistance at $84,898, the October 21 high.

Thursday’s data gave the Fed more reason to hike in December, and crypto stocks have spent October pricing that in at several times Bitcoin’s pace. Tonight’s results will show how much of the drop was earned. The next test after that is the October jobs report on November 6.
