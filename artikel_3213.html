Philip Rosenberg Dies: Oscar-Winning Production Designer On ‘All That Jazz’ As Well As ‘Network’ & ‘Moonstruck’ Was 91

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Philip Rosenberg, the production designer who won an Oscar for his work on All That Jazz and also worked on films such as Network, Moonstruck and The Hurricane, has died. That, according to his nephew, Alan Rosenberg. No cause of death was given. He was 91.

Rosenberg was raised in Brooklyn before studying scenic design at Yale University with Donald Oenslager. About that time, he was hired as an assistant at the Metropolitan Opera where he worked with Ita Maximovna and Eugene Berman. His first film was The Seagull in 1968, on which he was a design assistant.

Among Rosenberg’s first starry projects was Herbert Ross’ The Owl and the Pussycat in 1970. Rosenberg was the art director on the film starring Barbra Streisand and George Segal. He transitioned to production designer on James Toback’s The Gambler before making a splash in 1976’s new Hollywood landmark Network, becoming known for the realism which made that period’s films stand out. After Network, Rosenberg would go on to work with direction Sidney Lumet on Daniel, Garbo Talks, Running on Empty and many more.

He likewise had a long-running working relationship with Norman Jewison. The two collaborated on Moonstruck, Other People’s Money, The January Man (which Jewison produced) and The Hurricane.

Rosenberg’s breakthrough came on All That Jazz in 1978. His work on Bob Fosse’s film earned him an Oscar, which he shared with Tony Walton, Edward Stewart and Gary J. Brink. He would be nominated again in 1978 as an art director on The Wiz.
