Mercer (MERC) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Debt-for-equity swap and $400 million in new capital reshape Mercer's balance sheet.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Mercer International Inc. (MERC -1.40%) entered into a restructuring support agreement to equitize $1.1 billion in debt and secure $400 million in new capital through a prepackaged Chapter 11 process. Management reported that the restructuring involves only the parent company and one subsidiary, with all operating mills continuing business as usual. The company is focusing on the One Goal One Hundred program to achieve $100 million in cost savings by the end of 2026. Strategic initiatives include modernizing the Peace River mill with $26.5 million in government support and restructuring the Torgau mill to reach cash-positive operations by mid-2027.

Relative to the second quarter, pulp and sawmill fiber costs were down slightly overall. With slight increases in German pulpwood costs and decreases in Canadian pulpwood and German sawlog prices. Our German fiber costs are at historically high levels driven by tight supply and strong demand. These market dynamics are a direct result of the war in Ukraine, which stopped the inflow of Russian wood into Europe and caused energy costs to reach record levels.

High energy prices coupled with legislation that allows wood burning for energy purposes and German government subsidies for home wood burning solutions have resulted in a high demand for wood pellets. As a result, our German pulp mills are now in direct competition with pellet producers for pulp fiber. The higher demand from pellet producers coupled with reduced sawmilling output across the country, limiting the amount of residuals available, thus creating increased price pressure for the fiber that we use to feed our pulp mills. In contrast, we are seeing pulp fiber cost coming down meaningfully in Canada as we renegotiated contracts with our suppliers taking advantage of the reduced chip demand resulting from pulp mill curtailments.

We expect German sawlog prices to be flat in Q4 due to weak European lumber demand offsetting ongoing supply constraints.
