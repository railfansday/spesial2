The fourth official often takes plenty of flak from managers, players and fans on the sidelines. But what does the official standing between the dugouts actually do?

First introduced in 1991, the International Football Association Board (IFAB), the body responsible for the laws of the game, brought the role into the game as a replacement for any match officials if any were not able to continue their duties during a match.

Here, we go over exactly what a fourth official does, what their duties are and how they assist the on-pitch referee during play and stoppages and some of the most famous moments involving the official on the touchline.

Premier League referees are paid handsomely for refereeing matches, earning a substantial salary and a bonus per match.

Official guidance from the Football Association explains how and why a fourth official is appointed:

"A Fourth Official will be appointed to certain rounds of FA and County FA Competitions and all matches in the English Premier League, English Football League and National League Premier. The Football Association may also appoint a Fourth Official to a Contributory League match. The Fourth official actively officiates if any of the three match officials are unable to continue."

In practice, the fourth official's role is to assist the main on-pitch referee at all times whilst maintaining control of the touchline and technical areas. The technical areas are where each team's manager, coaching staff and substitutes are based before, during and immediately after a match.

The fourth official also helps with match control. Responsibility for the running of the game ultimately lies with the referee, but the fourth official can alert them to significant incidents missed by the referee and assistant referees, such as violent conduct away from the ball or a card shown to the wrong player. With three other officials, and VAR at the elite level, watching the action, these interventions are rare, but they can prove decisive.

More often, the fourth official is focused on the technical areas, acting as the point of contact for coaching staff who want to question decisions. That makes them the first target for frustrated managers and coaches, and heated exchanges can end with the referee showing a yellow or red card to the offending team official.

FA's official guideline lists the duties of the fourth official as follows:

"(The fourth official) shall keep a full record of the game, including details of any players cautioned or sent-off.

"(The fourth official) shall be in charge of substitutions and whether the substitutes equipment is in order. They shall indicate substitutions by holding up, to display the numbers, the number board provided for this purpose (if available). The Fourth official shall also record the exact times that substitutions are made.

"(The fourth official) will supervise the Technical Area to maintain order and inform the Referee of irresponsible behaviour by any occupant of the Technical Area. Irresponsible behaviour may be defined as, but not exclusive to: - any person who leaves the Technical Area to remonstrate with the Referee, an Assistant Referee, opponents or members of the opposition Technical Area - Area; any act of abusive, insulting, offensive or threatening language and/or gesture - gesture; persistently failing to comply with the Laws of the Game, League Regulations, Competition Rules or the request of the Fourth official."

Law 6 of the Laws of the Game adds further responsibilities, including supervising the replacement balls, approving a player's re-entry to the pitch after a signal from the referee, and indicating the minimum amount of added time at the end of each half.

There are two main pieces of equipment a fourth official uses during a match. The first is the electronic board, which shows players and supporters inside the stadium which players are being substituted, as well as the minimum amount of added time the referee intends to play at the end of each half.

The second is a headset. At the elite level, the fourth official is connected to the officiating team's communication system, alongside the referee, the two assistants and, where it is in use, the VAR. That link allows them to relay information to the referee instantly, which has proved crucial on some of the sport's biggest stages.

The most famous intervention by a fourth official came in the biggest match in world football. With the 2006 World Cup final between Italy and France level in extra time, Zinedine Zidane headbutted Marco Materazzi in the chest.

Referee Horacio Elizondo and both of his assistants missed the incident. It was the fourth official, Luis Medina Cantalejo, who saw it and informed Elizondo through his headset. Zidane was sent off in his final match as a professional, and Italy went on to win the trophy on penalties.

In January 2017, Burnley were awarded an injury-time penalty against Arsenal at the Emirates. Arsène Wenger was sent to the stands by referee Jon Moss for his reaction towards fourth official Anthony Taylor.

The Frenchman then appeared to push Taylor in the tunnel area. The FA handed Wenger a four-match touchline ban and a £25,000 fine. Wenger later admitted he should have "shut up" and gone home.

In December 2020, a Champions League match between Paris Saint-Germain and Istanbul Basaksehir was halted after fourth official Sebastian Coltescu allegedly referred to Basaksehir assistant coach Pierre Webo as "the Black one".

Players from both sides walked off the pitch in protest, and the match was resumed the following evening with a completely new officiating team. UEFA suspended Coltescu until the end of the 2020/21 season for "inappropriate behaviour" and ordered him to attend an educational programme.

Ex-Premier League referee Mark Clattenburg admitted Craig Bellamy was a 'nightmare', while he also wasn't very complimentary of Roy Keane and Pepe.
