Day 28: Charging Up on the Alternative Route

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

It took me over an hour to walk out of León’s city limits. And as I did, I noticed the frost on the cars.

And I truly didn’t want to be getting close to the end of this walk.

Like an angel sent to shift my mood, I crossed paths with a local who said,

By the time I was two and a half hours into my walk, I was on a dirt road, with nothing and no one around.

The sound of the road was barely audible in the distance, a necessary reprieve from the city atmosphere I’d been surrounded by for days.

I had chosen this alternative route out of the city intentionally because the main way followed a highway. Even though it was a few more kilometers, I didn’t care. There had already been so many road walks, and I was here to find space.

As I walked into the sleepy town of Chozas de Abajo, a quote graffitied on the sign hit me.

With the uncertainty of mine, I smiled at the play on words.

At the only bar in town, La Parada, I indulged my hunger. I ordered two tortillas and two coffees, and the kind woman working there added two complimentary pastries, all for €8.60. It was quite the feast, and surrounded by locals, I dove in.

With a full belly, caffeine, and pastries in my Ziploc for the road, I shouldered my pack and headed back out onto the Camino. Multiple locals wished me a “Buen Camino,” and as the only pilgrim I’d seen in hours, it felt extra sweet. If I hadn’t been only halfway done with my distance for the day, I would’ve loved to nestle myself in this little town for the night.

In the next town I came across, I was hoping their tiny grocery store would be open. Nope. But as I kept walking, a smiling Spanish man started talking to me. He told me the mountains were coming soon, and I told him I was ready. Once he asked where I was from, his response about my Spanish being good felt so validating.

He told me “buen viaje” (have a good trip), smiled, and let me on my way.

While I walked through the quiet fields, I had a brilliant idea. Crazy, but brilliant.

So I planned, in the way I loved to do, but had not been on the Camino. More to come about that plan later.

By the time I walked into Puente de Órbigo, I felt so charged up.

I walked through fields with barely any traffic.

The sun was out, and it was the perfect temperature.

The cherry on top? The town was large, with beautiful stone and brick everywhere. It was quiet. My favorite.

When I arrived at my albergue, Karl Leisner Pilgrims Hostel, I fell in love. As soon as I walked through the door, it opened into a beautiful courtyard with vibrant flowers and greenery, and a gorgeous mural on the wall. The ladies working were kind and welcoming. As soon as I walked into the bunk room, I saw my Italian brother.

A bit later, we were walking back from the grocery store, and I heard a whistle coming from the balcony above us. It was my Argentine brother. None of us had planned to meet here, but the group was reunited.

I cut up veggies and cheese to make myself a little salad. Then my Italian brother whipped up a delicious risotto that I got to try.

It was perfect. The people, the food, the day, all of it.

Rather than doing work on my laptop, I found a couch in the courtyard—the first I’d sunk into in weeks—and savored the peace all around me.
