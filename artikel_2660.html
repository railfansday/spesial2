Dodgers Sign Former Top Pitching Prospect to Minor League Deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Support Local Media - Click to Add LA Sports Report as a Preferred Source on Google

The Los Angeles Dodgers signed righth-handed pitcher Tink Hence to a minor league deal, per MiLB Central's Chase Ford.

The St. Louis Carindals drafted Hence, now 24, in the second competitive balance round of the 2020 draft with the No. 63 pick, and he was the No. 2 prospect in the system at one point.

St. Louis designated him for assignment in August before outrighting him to the Florida Complex League, and the right-hander elected free agency after the end of the season.

Hence pitched just eight times in rookie ball during the 2021 season, but put together a solid season the following year. He pitched 16 times in Class-A, allowing just eight earned runs in 52.1 innings pitched while striking out 81 batters.

He made his debut in High-A the following year, making 11 appearances as a starter with the Peoria Chiefs while posting a 2.81 ERA. The right-hander moved up to Double-A that same season, but struggled to post similar results with a 5.47 ERA through 12 appearances.

Hence settled in the following year, making 20 Double-A starts while slicing his ERA in half from the year prior, ending the season with a 2.71 mark. Several injuries impacted his availability, though, dealing with shoulder, chest and lat tightness.

The following year he suffered a rib cage strain which kept him out until May, and shoulder and pectoral injuries in the latter half of 2025 limited him to just eight starts. He returned in a bullpen role in 2026, and looked a shadow of himself in Triple-A. He had a 6.64 ERA and struck out 20 batters in 20.1 innings pitched.

Through 94 career appearances, Hence has a 3.50 ERA through 277.2 innings pitched, striking out 347 batters.

He still had a solid whiff rate in 2026, though his control suffered and his strikeout rate and fastball velocity dropped far lower than expected.

Hence likely won't appear for the Dodgers in 2027, though his involvement isn't impossible.

The Dodgers struggled with injuries to their pitching depth in 2024, 2025 and 2026, so if they find themselves in a pinch, Hence may be able to step in and do a job.

The right-hander's main focus with the Dodgers will be returning to his for before suffering his various injuries, and will hope a healthy season is enough to put him on the team's radar next year.
