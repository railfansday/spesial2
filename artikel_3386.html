Shia LaBeouf’s Forgotten ‘Watch Dogs’ Replacement Is Officially Leaving Streaming

For an original movie to gross nearly $200 million at the worldwide box office is seen as an anomaly these days. For instance, consider the recently released survival thriller Heart of the Beast, starring Brad Pitt and directed by David Ayer. However, things were very different before the streaming era. This was when a non-IP film with poor reviews and no A-lister attached could do just as well, if not better, at the box office. One of the best examples of such a film is currently streaming for free in the United States. At this point, it's an actual relic of how things used to be.

Unlike Heart of the Beast, which features a major A-lister coming off the biggest hit of his career — Pitt headlined F1 last year — and set to reprise his Oscar-winning role in a matter of days, the movie we're talking about features an up-and-comer. It was headlined by Shia LaBeouf, who, only a year prior, had starred in the blockbuster Transformers. LaBeouf, who worked with Pitt on the World War II film Fury, was famously groomed for stardom by none other than Steven Spielberg, who served as one of the executive producers on Transformers.

Spielberg was also involved in LaBeouf's other 2007 film Disturbia, which grossed nearly $120 million worldwide against a reported budget of $20 million. The 2008 movie reunited the star with his Disturbia director D.J. Caruso. It was released in the same year as Spielberg's Indiana Jones and the Kingdom of the Crystal Skull, which further cemented LaBeouf's potential.

But we're talking about the techno-thriller Eagle Eye, which has shades of Avatar, North by Northwest, and the video game Watch Dogs. It follows a man who is surveilled by a shadowy agency after becoming embroiled in matters related to his recently deceased twin brother. The film also features Michelle Monaghan, Billy Bob Thornton, and Anthony Mackie, while Julianne Moore provides the voice of an artificial intelligence agent.

Eagle Eye received poor reviews, but grossed $180 million worldwide against a reported budget of $80 million. Incidentally, it cracked the coveted $100 million mark domestically — a feat that Heart of the Beast hasn't achieved. The movie is sitting at a 26% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, where the consensus describes it as "a preposterously plotted thriller that borrows heavily from other superior films." You can watch the movie on Tubi until December 1, and stay tuned to Collider for more updates.
