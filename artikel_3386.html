178-Episode Urban Fantasy Series Has One Of TV's Most Iconic Lead Trios

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

This classic eight-season urban fantasy show introduced one of TV's most iconic central trios. Urban fantasy shows were all the rage in the 90s and early 00s, with shows like Xena: Warrior Princess, Buffy the Vampire Slayer, The X-Files, and Supernatural dominating pop culture. Xena, Buffy, and The X-Files explored contemporary themes through a paranormal lens, centering on female characters and their relationships, ambitions, and desires.

One show took this even further, introducing not one or two but three female leads: Charmed. Set in San Francisco, Charmed's Halliwell sisters aren't just bound by blood; they're also bonded through magic. Together, they're known as the Charmed Ones, wielding the collective Power of Three against demons, warlocks, ghosts, and other immortal supernatural beings hellbent on destroying their lineage. While their magic is important to them and they're passionate about saving innocents, the sisters always try to make time for their own lives, finding happiness in friendship, family, romance, and decidedly non-magical careers.

The show was certainly a product of its time, and not every episode has aged well, but the sisters' relationship is still as strong and meaningful now as it was when the series first aired in 1998. Even in death, their bond is unbreakable. They might fight, and certain demons or wayward spells might turn them against each other, but their love for each other always saves the day. They're stronger together, and the show proves it again and again.

The Power of Three is Charmed's most important piece of worldbuilding. That's why, even when one of the show's original cast members left, Charmed soon introduced a fourth Halliwell sister. Though the series started with the iconic trio of eldest sister Prue (the late Shannen Doherty), middle sister Piper (Holly Marie Combs), and youngest sibling Phoebe (Alyssa Milano), Doherty's exit after Charmed season 3 left a major hole to fill. Enter Rose McGowan as half-sister Paige.

The original sister trio was a triumph. Doherty was excellent as Prue, the burden of her responsibility as the oldest sister and her desperate desire to protect those she loved weighing on her heavily, especially after their mother's death. Combs was a grounding emotional presence as Piper, the perfect mediator between the eldest sibling and the youngest, while Milano captured Phoebe's free spirit and creativity with charm and wit. Changing that core dynamic should have sunk the show. And yet.

The divisive Charmed reboot may have diverged from the Halliwell sisters, but the iconic family coven could easily live on in a 2026 reimagining.

Though Doherty's Prue was certainly missed, and the series continued to honor her character — especially through Piper's and Phoebe's grief — McGowan's Paige fit in seamlessly with her new sisters. Suddenly, Piper was the eldest, Phoebe was the middle child, and Paige was the youngest, determined to prove herself as both a sister and a witch. Charmed did a great job exploring and building their new dynamic, and the show lasted for five more seasons, a testament to McGowan's performance and her chemistry with Combs and Milano.

Even now, two decades after its series finale, Charmed is still one of TV's best comfort fantasy shows. Though the series never shies away from serious topics like grief, death, heartbreak, parental abandonment, greed, or self-sacrifice, it always finds time for humor, romance, and heartwarming family moments. The Halliwell Manor is as iconic and nostalgic as Luke's diner in Gilmore Girls or Monica's apartment in Friends, even though the living room gets destroyed every episode (sometimes multiple times in one episode).

A Charmed reboot premiered on the CW in 2018 and ran for four seasons. The three lead sisters were called Macy, Mel, and Maggie.

Charmed balances darker episodes with light-hearted stories, including a groovy, 1970s time-traveling adventure and the Charmed Ones getting stuck in a medieval painting. Though it often switches between genres and storytelling tropes, the sisters' relationship keeps everything grounded. Everyone can find something to love in Charmed's iconic 178-episode run.

Charmed is streaming on Prime Video, Hulu, and Peacock.
