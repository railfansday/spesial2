South Korea education minister resigns after AI cheating scandal engulfs university exams

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Choi Kyo-jin said he ‘failed to prepare adequately’ for entrance exams, which this year had phones banned and checks for cameras hidden in clothing

South Korea’s education minister resigned on Sunday after an AI-assisted cheating scandal that erupted in the lead-up to the country’s key university entrance exams.

The country has been gripped by fears of AI-assisted exam cheating since last Sunday, when the top scorer in a national tax accountant exam confessed to using a miniature camera and smartphone to achieve a near-perfect score, just four days before the crucial university entrance exam known as the suneung.

The confession sent officials scrambling to roll out a slew of preventive measures for the entrance exams last week, including blocking public wifi in testing areas.

But the education minister, Choi Kyo-jin, struggled to reassure the public, and teachers’ unions criticised the government response as belated.

The annual eight-hour suneung determines which university a candidate can attend and, in turn, their future prospects.

“The exam concluded without any major incidents … However, as the minister in charge, I do not believe I am immune from public criticism that we failed to prepare adequately in advance,” Choi wrote on X.

“Now that the exam is over, I take full responsibility and hereby offer my resignation,” he added.

President Lee Jae Myung accepted his resignation, the presidential office said.

Asked during a parliamentary hearing on Monday whether he was confident that authorities could catch would-be cheaters on exam day, the 72-year-old Choi replied: “Due to my age, I am not confident I could.” His response quickly drew public backlash.

Choi did not appear at press conferences in the days leading up to Thursday’s exam, where a vice minister instead announced the preventive measures.

South Korea goes to great lengths to ensure the smooth running of the suneung. Aircraft take-offs and landings are rescheduled during the 25-minute English listening paper to minimise noise, while artillery fire and tank movements are suspended. The stock market opens an hour late and cars are stopped 200 metres from every centre.

In the aftermath of the cheating scandal earlier this month, the government announced that smart devices would have to be turned off and handed in for the suneung, while bags left in corridors would be checked for any devices that were still on. Exam officials would also inspect all candidates’ glasses and watches, as well as their collars, sleeves and buttons for concealed cameras.

The government also sent emergency alerts to every phone in the country, which sounded at full volume and could not be silenced, in the hope of catching out anyone who has not handed in their device.

Officials said 111 cases of misconduct involving electronic devices were identified during Thursday’s exam, nearly half of which were detected through the emergency alerts.
