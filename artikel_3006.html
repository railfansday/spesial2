Why GXO Thinks a Recession Could Actually Help It

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

GXO impressed investors with its 2030 guidance.

Fresh off a strong third-quarter earnings report, GXO Logistics (GXO +4.63%) held its first Investor Day conference since early 2023 on Monday, updating investors on its strategy and outlining its financial targets for 2030.

From 2027-2030, the company expects a compound annual growth rate of more than 7%, and it sees a 6.5% adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, and amortization (EBITA), which implies a higher EBITDA.

That forecast helped lift the stock 4.7% on Monday, signaling the company's growth was accelerating under new CEO Patrick Kelleher. The new forecast followed its third-quarter earnings report earlier this month that included 6.4% organic revenue growth, its highest in more than three years, lifting the stock nearly 10%.

In an interview with The Motley Fool, Patrick Kelleher explained why GXO's prospects were improving, and why the world's largest pure-play contract logistics provider is well-positioned to grow no matter what the economy throws at it.

Compared to other transportation businesses, which depend on fleets of trucks, planes, and ships, contract logistics tends to be relatively asset-light, and that gives the company an advantage at a time when rising interest rates have sparked fears in some capital-intensive industries like data centers, and as fuel prices, which GXO has no direct exposure to, have weighed on the transportation sector.

Kelleher saw little risk in the macroeconomic climate, noting the resilience of the contract logistics industry, which he said had not seen a downturn in 32 years. In an interview with The Motley Fool, he explained, "I always say macroeconomic headwinds are a catalyst to increased outsourcing, when interest rates are going up, when the macroeconomic environment gets challenging, when you look at things like tariffs and additional costs coming into business, inflation... those are drivers for customers to look to supply chains to reduce costs and save money, and we, and we are our customers' answer for that."

In other words, GXO is a cost-savings solution for its customers, and in times of macro pressure, those customers are more likely to look for alternatives to managing logistics in-house.

Currently, just 30% of contract logistics is outsourced, meaning there's a huge opportunity for growth as more of the business becomes outsourced, which is expected as GXO and its peers invest in technologies like AI and robotics. The industry is expected to grow at a CAGR of 6% over the coming years, and it represents a total addressable market of $1.1 trillion.

With a massive addressable market in contract logistics, a business model that potentially benefits from macroeconomic pressure, and growth driven in part by AI and robotics, GXO offers a unique way to benefit from growing demand for AI.

The company's 2030 guidance does not factor in any volume increases from its current customer base, so management is confident it can hit those numbers. With new customers accounting for the forecasted growth, the company could easily top that forecast, which should make the stock a winner between now and 2030, given the response to the forecast on Monday.

GXO was spun off from XPO in 2021, and the company has doubled its revenue since then with the help of acquisitions. However, the stock has mostly traded sideways, as there were high expectations at the time of the spin-off, and growth has been sluggish due to macro challenges and the integration of its earlier acquisitions.

Based on its guidance, GXO's adjusted earnings per share should improve to better than $5 per share by 2030, which puts the stock in position to double or more by then. If management executes on its vision, the company will be in a significantly stronger position then as well.
