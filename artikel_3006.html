SoFi Technologies vs. Toast: Which Financial Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

SoFi's member base is growing fast, but Toast's restaurant software moat and lower forward P/E may tip the scales for cautious buyers.

The fintech landscape is shifting as digital banks and specialized software providers battle for market share. Choosing between SoFi Technologies (SOFI -3.70%) and Toast (TOST +0.92%) requires weighing aggressive banking growth against restaurant-specific software dominance.

SoFi operates as a member-centric digital bank offering everything from student loans to investment accounts. Toast focuses on the hospitality industry, providing the backbone for restaurant operations and payments. Both companies are navigating high-growth phases, but their paths to profitability and market risks diverge significantly as they approach 2027.

SoFi acts as a one-stop shop for financial services, targeting high earners with a suite of lending and banking products. SoFi serves a concentrated number of whole loan purchasers and technology clients, and customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business. Recently, the company partnered with Mastercard (MA +0.08%) to settle stablecoin transactions, signaling an expansion into digital asset services for other financial stocks and fintech firms.

Reliable recent quarterly figures for at least one of these companies aren't available, so this comparison uses each company's latest full fiscal year. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, revenue reached nearly $3.6 billion, representing growth of approximately 35.1% over the prior year. The company reported net income of roughly $481.3 million during this period, following a substantial increase in its member base that reached 14.7 million users by mid-2026.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.2x, which compares total debt to shareholder equity to show reliance on borrowed funds. The current ratio, which measures a company's ability to cover short-term debts with short-term assets, is close to 0.2x. Free cash flow for the 2025 fiscal year was negative $4.0 billion, which equals cash from operations minus capital expenditures.

Toast provides a specialized cloud-based platform for the restaurant industry, serving over 170,000 locations. The company integrates point-of-sale hardware with software for payroll, inventory, and marketing. Recent business includes an agreement with Great Harvest to implement Toast Enterprise Solutions for its hospitality locations.

In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, revenue reached nearly $6.2 billion, an increase of roughly 24.1% compared to the previous year. The company reported net income of approximately $342.0 million, demonstrating a move toward sustained profitability. This growth highlights the company's ability to expand its footprint across both small businesses and enterprise-level hospitality clients.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is close to 0.0x, while the current ratio of 2.8x suggests the company maintains a cushion for immediate obligations. Free cash flow for the 2025 fiscal year was approximately $608 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 36.6% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

SoFi Technologies faces intense competition from traditional banking giants and digital rivals. A significant risk involves the concentration of loan purchasers and technology clients, as losing a partner could hurt liquidity. Regulatory oversight as a bank holding company remains a constant factor, while recent litigation from Citizens Bank concerning trade secrets adds legal uncertainty.

Toast is currently subject to an FTC lawsuit regarding how it handles certain business-to-business contracts. The company relies heavily on a limited number of payment processors and its bank partner for the Toast Capital loan program. Managing a global supply chain for hardware components also creates operational risks while competing against payment giants like Visa (V +0.16%) and other tech stocks in the restaurant space.

Toast appears slightly more attractive based on a lower Forward P/E, which measures the stock price against its future earnings estimates.

Toast stock has had a solid three-year run, returning more than 140% for investors. Its third-quarter report in November didn't disappoint. The company added approximately 8,500 net new locations, annualized recurring run rate (ARR) grew 24% to $2.5 billion, and net income was $169 million, compared to $105 million in Q3 2025.

SoFi has returned about 130% over the last three years. In its third quarter, members grew 35% to a record 17.1 million, and products grew 44% to a record 26.7 million. Net income grew 73% to reach $243 million.

The two companies operate in different industries. Toast is all-in on the restaurant space. It's investing in AI-powered services like Toast IQ and Toast IQ Grow, which could help restaurants streamline workflows and reduce customer churn. SoFi offers a full suite of digital lending and banking solutions. It's also harnessing AI in products like its SoFi Coach personal finance assistant and SoFi Automated Investing.

In this matchup, I'd choose SoFi, despite the fact that it's underperformed Toast over the last few years. And it boils down to the industry it operates in. The financials space certainly isn't without risk, but SoFi's range of products may allow it to pivot more effectively than Toast and arguably gives it a larger total addressable market.

SoFi also meets the core technical eligibility requirements for S&P 500 inclusion, though it hasn't been formally added to the index. Inclusion in the index isn't a be-all and end-all reason to own a stock, but it can provide a price boost as index-tracking funds buy the stock.
