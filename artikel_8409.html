Personal Consumer Issues • Re: Where to live as YOUNGER dementia caregiver. DH in moderate stages.

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

I am updating this post now that the move with my husband with frontotemporal dementia, or FTD (non-fluent variant - PPA - meaning with aphasia) is done.

We moved out in August, visited family for a month, and moved in, on September 1. This post is 9 weeks after that.

In September, we worked on converting this rental of ours to personal use. (it is a lovely home). Repainted the whole home, got all new floors, made ADA updates, new fixtures, plumbing repairs. Had no furniture so this was the time to do it. I was fortunate to get bids and execute it really fast.

MOVING IN and RENOVATING was extremely hard on hubby. He has declined A LOT in 7 months. His brother had this, and it was 4 years from diagnosis to death, so the speed is not surprising with this genetic variant. His agitation and waywardness has caused me huge stress, and his desire to fix things has resulted in him almost electrocuting himself, amidst other near-disasters.

In the last 2 weeks, I have gotten all our doctors set up, he is well established now at an integrated dementia care center, and HALLELUJAH - HE HAS STARTED ADULT DAY CARE. Without adult day care, I think his behavior might have given me a heart attack from the stress of watching him. Now, I can fix the home, and complete move-in while he's at day care. I have found caregiver groups and met quite a few wonderful neighbors. I pay $695/year for 24x7 caregiver support, and $101 per day for adult day care.

Next up: meeting planned with geriatric care navigator who can organize care ON THE SPOT for my hubby if I land in the hospital, and then coordinate with our daughter. You pay them a retainer fee and they act as a dispatch manager to handle emergencies and get immediate help for the dementia sufferer. (They can also coordinate legal tasks if you're not on top of that).

Next up: hubby's doctor insists I wear a med-alert button. I discovered on this journey that my husband could not deal with calling 911 when I had respiratory distress (onset of pneumonia), He just walked over me, to go eat, oblivious to my distress. I now no there is NO ONE looking out for me. He has forgotten how to use the phone, and even if he could, he can barely talk.

BOTTOM LINE: If you have a loved one with fast-moving dementia (like FTD in some variants), then get everything done before you hit moderate stages. It was the right decision to move, but - NO JOKE - a dementia patient does not handle moves well, no matter "how gentle" or passive they may be prior to the move. The impaired decision making in FTD adds real danger to getting established at a new location.
