Compass says it will cooperate with every broker, even off the MLS

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Compass introduced a Broker-to-Broker Cooperation Policy requiring agents to share listing details, allow showings to qualified buyers, and present offers, including for private exclusive and coming soon listings unless clients opt out in writing. The move cites Bright MLS cooperation language as Compass continues disputes and litigation with multiple MLSs.  AI Summary

Compass appears to be doubling down regarding its stance on industry cooperation.

On Wednesday, the RealTrends Verified brokerage announced its new Broker-to-Broker Cooperation Policy.

The policy requires all Compass agents to cooperate with other brokers and agents, including for listings in Private Exclusive or Coming Soon marketing phases, unless otherwise instructed by the client in writing.

As part of the policy, Compass agents must ensure that all agents, regardless of brokerage affiliation, can access listing details by request. They are also required to respond to inquiring from buyers or cooperating brokers in a timely manner, share all requested information, make the property available for showings to any qualified prospective buyer during any period when showings are being provided to buyers and present all written offers to the seller.

In a post on social media that was a collaborative post shared by Robert Reffkin, the CEO of Compass’s parent company Compass International Holdings, Compass president Neda Navab, who is a HousingWire Woman of Influence, and Compass, they said the inspiration for this policy came from Bright MLS’s rule governing cooperation when a home is marketed off of the MLS.

In the post’s caption, they wrote despite supporting cooperation and being willing to cooperate with “every broker,” Compass does not want to put all listings in the MLS because it has “become a system that restricts how homeowners can market their homes.”

“For example, 45 of our top 50 MLSs limit how long a seller can list a Coming Soon, prohibit showings for Comings Soons, or restrict accepting offers altogether. Support of Cooperation? Yes. Support of MLS Restrictions on Marketing? No,” the caption states.

Despite the brokerage’s commitment to is three-phased marketing plan, which includes a stage where properties are marketed as private exclusive listings, Compass has regularly stated that it is willing to cooperate with all agents even on the sale of properties that are listed as private exclusives. In May 2025, the company announced it was launching  the “Compass Private Exclusives Book,” which contains all of its private exclusive inventory. At the time of the launch, Compass said the books would be available for viewing by all consumers and agents at other firms at the largest Compass office in each of its markets.

The brokerage’s parent company is also currently involved in its own dispute with MLSs, with Reffkin threatening to sue any MLS that punishes or prohibits the company’s agents from publicly marketing office exclusive listings. So far, Compass International Holdings has filed one lawsuit against California Regional MLS (CRMLS).

CRMLS and eight other MLSs have filed suits of their own against Compass, asking the court for a declaratory judgment that their policies on listing cooperation are fair, pro-competitive and legally sound.
