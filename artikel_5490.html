Bitcoin falls to an 11-week low after hotter-than-expected October inflation

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October and core prices rose 0.3%, ahead of the Federal Reserve's Dec. 8-9 meeting

Bitcoin dropped 1.4% on Tuesday, touching a low of $73,588 that marked its weakest level in 11 weeks, after U.S. consumer prices climbed more than expected in October, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The consumer price index increased 0.4% from September, following a 0.6% gain that month. Economists expected a 0.3% rise, according to a survey by The Wall Street Journal.

Core inflation, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.3% on the month, matching September's pace. Economists had forecast 0.2%, according to The Wall Street Journal. Annual comparisons were unavailable because the government closure last year meant no October 2025 report was ever produced.

The data make additional Fed rate hikes look more likely, a development that tends to pressure riskier holdings, according to The Wall Street Journal. U.S. stock index futures slid once the report landed, with Nasdaq $NDAQ 100 contracts losing 1.2% and S&P 500 contracts shedding 0.5%. The 2-year Treasury yield rose 14 basis points to 4.69%. Traders are pricing a small chance of a 50-basis-point move in December, according to CoinDesk.

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said in a London speech on Tuesday that a 50-basis-point increase should be considered at the December meeting if October core CPI came in around 0.3% or higher. He said he was leaning toward a 25-basis-point increase if monthly inflation improved.

"Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take," Waller said.

Swaps moved to fully price a quarter-point hike for December after Waller’s remarks, versus roughly 85% before them, according to Bloomberg. The Fed’s next policy meeting is Dec. 8-9.

The Fed held its benchmark rate at 3.75% to 4% at its October meeting. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point increase. The central bank raised its benchmark rate by a quarter point in September, its first increase since July 2023.
