The 6 Most Bingeable Crime Shows You Can Finish In a Weekend

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Sometimes we seek out something with a darker tone and narrative as a means of escape. It’s why crime shows continue to be a go-to genre for television fans. The genre offers viewers a chance to dive into a gritty underworld and watch crime unfold before their eyes. Whether it be a murder mystery of a syndicate of unsavory individuals, the stories crime shows get to tell keep us glued to our screens. If you’re seeking out a crime drama and only have a single weekend to watch, we’ve got six shows perfect for you!

From award-winning masterpieces to ripped-from-the-headlines dramatizations, the six crime shows on this list offer a brilliant hook and a satisfying conclusion. Though there are countless long-running crime shows that are equally as good, you simply won’t be able to complete them in the hours allotted. These six you certainly can! Once you start, you simply won’t be able to stop until the final credits roll.

No crime thriller has captured our attention quite like the critically acclaimed Adolescence. Created by Jack Thorne and Stephen Graham, and directed by Philip Barantini, the four-part crime story follows 13-year-old Jamie Miller (Owen Cooper), who is arrested in a pre-dawn raid for the fatal stabbing of a female classmate. Filmed entirely in single, continuous takes, the series follows Jamie’s devastated family, a lead detective, and a clinical psychologist as they piece together what drove an seemingly ordinary boy to commit murder. Along the way, the story examines the real-world fallout of cyberbullying, social media pressure, and online "manosphere" radicalization on young men. Adolescence refuses to rely on sensationalized twists, opting instead to build natural real-time tension.

Each hour-long episode captures a single moment of the harrowing ordeal. By filming in a singular unbroken shot, the approach keeps the tension immediate and pulls viewers directly into the room during police procedures, interviews, and family confrontations. The narrative tackles modern themes through a mesmerizing, fresh lens that captures a wider chain of social and family pressures. The story stays concentrated on the immediate aftermath and psychological toll rather than stretching out a slow investigation. Adolescence is an acting showcase. Cooper’s breakout debut is sensational. It’s a raw, natural performance that deserved the massive award sweep. Graham as his father, Eddie, and Erin Doherty as Briony Ariston, the psychologist evaluating him, deliver stellar turns. Adolescence became a gamechanger in miniseries storytelling, reshaping how short-form narratives can be told.

The greatest multi-season crime drama of all time is Broadchurch. Created by Chris Chibnall, the three-season drama follows Detective Inspector Alec Hardy (David Tennant) and Detective Sergeant Ellie Miller (Olivia Colman) as they investigate major crimes in a close-knit English coastal town. The first two seasons focus on the murder of 11-year-old Danny Latimer (Oskar McNamara) and its legal fallout, while the third season centers on the investigation of Trish Winterman’s (Julie Hesmondhalgh) sexual assault. Throughout the series, intense police inquiries and media scrutiny expose hidden secrets that fracture the tight-knit community. A standout series with a brilliant acting ensemble, Broadchurch pairs a gripping mystery with grounded, character-driven drama and raw emotional fallout. From the very first episode, Broadchurch hooks you. The crime is established, the town is reeling, and the individuals investigating the shocking murder have engaging character flaws that may prevent them from doing their job at the best of their ability.

Broadchurch shows how a single crime shatters a tight-knit coastal community and reshapes everyday relationships over time. The series lingers on grief, paranoia, media scrutiny, and long-term consequences. Its grounded approach treats tragedy as something that permanently alters people. Filming on location in places like West Bay, Dorset, gives the show a moody coastal atmosphere where familiar neighbors suddenly look like strangers. Chibnall keeps viewers guessing with slow-burn suspense and a steady stream of suspicious leads rather than relying on obvious tropes. Led by Colman and Tennant, who have impeccable chemistry, the ensemble features a murderer’s row of UK stars including Jodie Whittaker, Jonathan Bailey, David Bradley, Alfred Enoch, Arthur Darvill, Jacob Anderson, Marianne Jean-Baptiste, Phoebe Waller-Bridge, James D’Arcy, Eve Myles, Shaun Dooley, and Lenny Henry. With three seasons of eight tight episodes, Broadchurch is absolutely a show you can achieve in a weekend because it’s addictive. Each episode introduces new clues, red herrings, and emotional cliffhangers that naturally compel you to hit "next episode."

One of the best self-contained American crime dramas of the decade is the brilliant Mare of Easttown. Written by Brad Ingelsby and directed by Craig Zobel, the seven-episode series tells the story of Mare Sheehan (Kate Winslet), a small-town Pennsylvania detective. While investigating the murder of a local teenage mother and reopening a year-old missing person's case, Mare struggles to keep her personal life from unraveling. As she uncovers dark secrets within her tight-knit community, she is also forced to confront her own deep-seated grief and family struggles. Mare of Easttown pairs a gripping small-town murder investigation with a deeply lived-in community where every individual is raw, authentic, and grappling with a broken reality.

Mare of Easttown is a masterpiece in storytelling. Ingelsby crafts a fictionalized version of Pennsylvania's Delaware County that feels immersive. The show grounds its mystery in local rhythms, community loyalty, addiction, and a carefully coached regional dialect. Across seven episodes, the show builds from a slow-burn setup into a twist-heavy final stretch that keeps you thoroughly engaged. And yet, beyond the investigation is a beautiful character-centric story. Winslet brings emotional exhaustion, rough edges, and quiet vulnerability to a woman navigating grief, family strain, and town expectations. Mare of Easttown features a stellar cast that elevates the narrative. With a supporting ensemble featuring Julianne Nicholson, Jean Smart, Evan Peters, and Guy Pearce, Mare of Easttown is built around the town's weighted, tangled relationships. Mare of Easttown is a show you’ll wish had a second season because it’s exceptionally executed.

HBO is a breeding ground for top-notch crime dramas. In 2016, The Night Of was proof as to why the premium cable network maintains a strong grip on sensational storytelling. Written by Richard Price and Steven Zaillian, and based on the original Criminal Justice plot by Peter Moffat, the eight-episode crime thriller follows Nasir "Naz" Khan (Riz Ahmed), a mild-mannered Pakistani-American college student in New York City who wakes up from a night of drinking and drugs to find the young woman he met stabbed to death. With no memory of what happened, Naz flees in panic, only to be quickly arrested and charged with her murder after police find the suspected weapon in his pocket. As streetwise defense attorney John Stone (John Turturro) takes on his case, the series examines the flaws of the American criminal justice system and how awaiting trial at Rikers Island transforms Naz. A truly remarkable story, The Night Of treats a murder accusation as a gateway into character psychology and institutional reality.

The Night Of prioritizes character over twist. The story tracks how a single night's events reshape Naz, his defense attorneys, his family, and the investigators. By examining courtroom friction, Rikers Island survival, institutional negligence, and cultural bias, the series is grounded with authenticity. From its Kafkaesque opening night to its cinematography and sound design, the pacing builds dread and immersion. The practical New York set pieces became a character of their own. Ahmed and Turturro are brilliant. Ahmed’s Emmy-winning turn is built on raw emotion, making him an underdog that you desperately wish to see earn justice. In a career-best turn, Turturro embodies Stone, down to his eczema-plagued exterior. Joined by strong performances from Michael K. Williams and Billy Camp, the series is a strong showing of realistic characters. Only eight strong episodes are needed to tell a complete, buttressing story. The story moves from a police procedural and murder mystery to a courtroom drama with seamless ease.

Perhaps we have to thank Christopher Nolan for allowing superhero films and series to move beyond the generic booms and pows. Spun off from Matt Reeves’ feature film The Batman, The Penguin drops the titular character into a bona fide criminal underworld that just so happens to have iconic IP. Created by Lauren LeFranc, The Penguin follows mid-level mobster Oswald "Oz" Cobb (Colin Farrell) as he attempts to seize control of Gotham City's criminal underworld. Following the death of crime boss Carmine Falcone (Mark Strong) and the catastrophic flooding of the city, Oz exploits the resulting power vacuum to push a new drug trade and dismantle rival factions from within. Along the way, he forms a fragile mentorship with a young street kid, Victor Aguilar (Rhenzy Feliz), while navigating a brutal turf war against Carmine's ruthless daughter, Sofia Falcone (Cristin Milioti), and the Maroni crime family. The Penguin prioritizes grounded mob storytelling, character psychology, and high-stakes power struggles over superhero spectacle for a fully-realized masterpiece.

Following the flooding of Gotham City and Carmine Falcone's death, the eight-episode HBO miniseries plays like a gritty crime drama in the vein of The Sopranos and classic mafia cinema. Rather than softening Oz into a neat anti-hero, showrunner LeFranc examines how manipulation, betrayal, and his fraught relationship with his mother, Francis Cobb (Deirdre O’Connell), shape a genuinely monstrous rise to power. Farrell delivers a transformative turn as the iconic character, balancing dark humor and calculated ruthlessness. Opposite him, Milioti brings a sensational spin on a woman with intense vulnerability. She finds a balance of menace and psychological chaos while still holding her rival’s feet to the fire. O’Connell is the ensemble's unsung hero, bringing fragile despair to a woman slowly losing control. Her ability to maintain quiet fury, manipulation, and heavy secrets tied to Oz's past makes her a brilliantly complex character. And then Feliz will absolutely break your heart, with his demise being the hardest moment of the entire series. The Penguin moves at a perfectly brisk pace, with Gotham's underworld playing like a gritty crime drama that goes beyond a traditional comic book adaptation. The Penguin is so good, you’ll keep begging for more. ​​​​​​​

Chances are you are familiar with The Staircase thanks to the infamous true-crime docuseries by Jean-Xavier de Lestrade. In 2022, the story of the documentary and its subject was dramatized in utterly brilliant fashion. Created by Antonio Campos, The Staircase centers on crime novelist Michael Peterson (Colin Firth), who becomes the prime suspect in his wife Kathleen’s (Toni Collette) murder after she is found dead at the bottom of a staircase in their North Carolina home in 2001. The eight-episode series chronicles the resulting 16-year legal battle and how deep-seated family secrets and shifting allegiances tear the Petersons' blended family apart. It also weaves in the behind-the-scenes story of the French documentary crew filming the defense, exploring how competing theories and true-crime storytelling shape the truth. The Staircase moves beyond the headlines, uniting high-caliber acting with a shifting, non-prescriptive structure that explores competing theories around Kathleen’s death rather than handing down a simple verdict.

Spanning timelines from 2001 to 2017, the eight-episode series examines the shocking saga through ambiguity and shifting perspectives. Incorporating the arrival of a French documentary crew filming the defense adds a layer to how true crime is constructed and manipulated. And let’s just say the actual individuals were none too pleased with some artistic liberties. That said, it’s a fascinating take that benefits those familiar with the documentary. Firth and Collette bring their exquisite dramatic skills to anchor a sensational ensemble where the taint runs deep. That includes Michael Stuhlbarg, Juliette Binoche, Rosemarie DeWitt, Patrick Schwarzenegger, Dane DeHaan, and Parker Posey. The compact crime thriller plays as a high-stakes family drama that zeroes in on how secrets, shifting trust, and a 16-year legal battle can destroy any semblance of normalcy. It's a perfect weekend watch.
