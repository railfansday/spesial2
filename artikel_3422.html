Apple seems to have the right approach to the first touchscreen Mac

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Steve Jobs famously dismissed the idea of a touchscreen Mac back in 2010 when he said that it “gives great demo” but turns out to be “ergonomically terrible” in real-life use.

It was inevitable that eyebrows would be raised when the company did a U-turn on this by launching the first touchscreen Mac. But while I have no interest myself, I do think the company’s counterargument some 16 years later makes sense …

We thought about this years ago. We’ve done tons of user testing on this and it turns out it doesn’t work. Touch surfaces don’t want to be vertical. It gives great demo, but after a short period of time you start to fatigue – and after an extended period of time your arm wants to fall off. It doesn’t work, it’s ergonomically terrible. Touch surfaces want to be horizontal […] that’s why we’ve perfected our multi-touch trackpads over the years.

Current CEO John Ternus addressed this change of heart directly. Specifically, he said that touch was intended to be a supplementary way of interacting with the machine rather than a primary one.

The Mac is first and foremost an indirect input device, built around the trackpad and keyboard. But I think there’s times when it’s comfortable and handy to have touch.

He suggested that it isn’t the headline feature of the new model.

All of these things, and, oh yeah, it has touch as well.

I’m a Mac guy through and through, right from the original Macintosh back in 1984. I’m also a massive trackpad fan, using the Magic Trackpad on my desk setup rather than a mouse. To me, it’s just the perfect way to control a Mac.

There was a period when I quite frequently used a 12.9-inch iPad with Magic Keyboard as a mobile alternative. Like the new MacBook Pro, that setup offered the choice of using the trackpad built into the keyboard or the touchscreen.

What I noticed about my own usage in this laptop-like configuration is that I almost exclusively used the trackpad. The only time I used the touchscreen was for scrolling, especially when I wanted to scroll a significant way down a page. That absolutely tracks with Ternus’ perspective of trackpad first, touchscreen sometimes.

However, when an M1 MacBook Air replaced my iPad with a Magic Keyboard, I didn’t miss the touchscreen at all.

One of the many things that’s changed over the years, in large part because of Apple’s launch of the iPhone way back in 2007, is that almost an entire generation has grown up with a touch interface. For them, the idea of a laptop without a touchscreen seems somewhat archaic.

However, I do very much suspect that even this generation will still use the trackpad as the primary control mechanism for the very reasons Steve outlined 16 years ago.

I use a 2 in 1 Yoga for years and loved it. So, I’d prefer that the MacBook had a hinge like the Yoga to allow it to fully fold into a “tablet” or “tent” mode. But a touchscreen Mac, even only being allowed to use vertical, is a Giant Leap for mankind! 😄

The nightmare scenario for Mac users was Apple compromising the interface in order to support touch. None of us wanted to lose the high resolution UI elements and see them replaced with giant cartoon-like equivalents. None of us wanted to lose the precise control we get from a trackpad. Thankfully, that hasn’t happened.

So no, I won’t be buying one personally, but I can see why Apple added the feature, and I do think the company has the right attitude to it.
