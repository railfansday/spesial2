Who will win the season-ending WTA Finals in Indian Wells?

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

The season-ending championship heads to Indian Wells for the first time, with new world No 1 Elena Rybakina leading the field along with Aryna Sabalenka and Coco Gauff; You can watch the WTA Finals live on Sky Sports from 8-15 November

Marion Bartoli, Martina Navratilova, Madison Keys and Laura Robson give their predictions ahead of the season-ending WTA Finals in Indian Wells.

Five players are returning to the year-end event, including reigning champion and world No 1 Elena Rybakina, while three are set to make their debut, but who will prevail?

Who will win the tournament in the California desert and who will be the dark horse? We've asked our panel of experts for their predictions.

I'm picking Aryna Sabalenka, just ahead of Elena Rybakina.

Sabalenka has won 85 per cent of her hard-court matches this year, 34 wins from 40, which is the best percentage of the eight qualifiers. The strongest argument for me is what she did in March. She won 12 matches in a row to complete the 'Sunshine Double', beating Rybakina in the Indian Wells final and again, in straight sets, in the Miami semi-final before beating Coco Gauff for the title. Add the Brisbane title and finals at both hard-court Grand Slams, and you can see how consistently she's put herself in a position to win on this surface.

November in Indian Wells is a different test. The cooler desert evenings could change how the ball comes through the court.

My dark horse is Mirra Andreeva. She's already world No 3 and a Grand Slam champion, so nobody in that locker room is overlooking her. But if the conversation starts with Sabalenka and Rybakina, she's the player I'd pick to break up that final.

Look at who she beat to win Indian Wells last year. She beat Rybakina, Svitolina, Swiatek and then Sabalenka, coming back from a set down in the final. That run tells you a lot about her. She dealt with Rybakina's ability to take time away, with Svitolina making her earn every point, and with the weight of shot that Swiatek and Sabalenka can put on you. Those are very different problems to solve in the same week, especially when you're 17.

This year she's won Adelaide outdoors on hard, Linz indoors on hard and her first Grand Slam at Roland-Garros. She also reached the final in Beijing, so we've seen her play well on hard courts deep into the season. What tips it for me is the quality of the players she's already beaten here and how well her game matches up against the two favourites.

What I love about her game is that she can absorb a very hard ball and change direction before her opponent has recovered. Against Sabalenka, watch for the backhand down the line after she's taken a heavy ball crosscourt. Suddenly Sabalenka has to hit on the move.

Against Rybakina, I'd look for a deep return through the middle. If Andreeva can take away the easy angle on that first groundstroke, she has a much better chance of getting into the rally. And when she's stretched, she can change the height of the ball to give herself time to recover. If the cooler evening conditions give her a fraction more time to defend, that helps, provided she gets enough depth on the next ball.

The test in her first WTA Finals is whether she can protect her own second serve and be brave enough to attack theirs.

Her movement and backhand will keep her in points. To beat the best players in this field, she'll also need to step forward when she earns the short ball. She's already shown she can do that against both favourites at Indian Wells. That's why I can see her winning the whole thing.
