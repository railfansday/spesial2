Florence Pugh Responds to Plastic Surgery Speculation With a Reminder About Aging: ‘Faces Change’

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

“The fact that people can’t even tell between what’s aging and what’s surgery is disturbing.”

After Evie magazine posted a video discussing whether Florence Pugh got plastic surgery, the actress shut down the speculation with a reminder of how aging works. “Guys. I turned 30,” she wrote. “I’m a woman. Body’s and faces change. It’s not that deep, the fact that people can’t even tell between what’s aging and what’s surgery is disturbing. Sadly puppy fluff doesn’t last forever ❤️”

Commenters also pointed out that the video featured older photos of Pugh, who has been acting steadily since her teen years. (She was just 18 when her first film, The Falling, came out in 2014.)

Pugh later posted an Instagram discussing the commentary about her body. She wrote that she was disturbed “for women who now think that I’m someone I’m not. For having my body dissected and pointed at again. I have spent my whole career defending and protecting bodies that just grow the way they grow. I was subjected to fat shaming in the business from the moment I started and I have loved every time I get to tell someone to fuck off when they are being inappropriate about my weight, and I’m not changing now just because you’ve all said so.”

She added, “I have never been on Ozempic. I have NEVER done any face surgery. And don’t EVER say that I didn’t make my own nose, I’ve always had that beautiful ski slope. Even as a baby!”

In her ELLE Women in Hollywood cover story, Pugh touched on aging and the comments actresses get about their appearance. It’s critical, she said, to be accepting of yourself amid all that noise.

“There’s so much scrutiny of how we look, how we dress, and how we age,” she said. “And then when we do those things, they’re like, ‘Oh my God, she looks like this, or she’s done too much of that, or she’s gained weight, or she’s lost too much weight.’ You can never get any footing right. I feel like the best work that I’ve ever done is when I truly feel ugly. When I’ve got shit makeup on and I’m either sick or ugly or having a hangover or a breakdown. I love that type of makeup because it allows me to be free.

“What I love about acting is, I don’t have the typical self-consciousness of just being a woman,” she continued. “When I’m acting, I get to be comfortable in the body that I’m in. If the camera picks up that I have a stomach roll when I’m sitting down, then I have a stomach roll when I’m sitting down. And if the camera picks up that I’ve got cellulite on my thighs, then yeah, I’ve got cellulite on my thighs. I just give it over to the art. So performing is very freeing for me, and it always has been.

“It is so crucial to be okay with how you are and who you are, because people are going to attack you regardless,” she added. “They’re going to say really shitty things, no matter if you’re too small, too big, too wrinkly, too spotty, too loud, too quiet, too opinionated. They’re going to find issue with you, because that’s just the nature of the internet and people and opinions. So it’s really important to find peace with what you want to represent and who you want to be. Because then you can just be like, ‘Ugh, those dickheads.’”
