The Cookie Cutter Method: Fast Estimates for Repetitive Work

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Posted on Nov 10 • Originally published at Medium

You look at the ticket. You look at the engineer. The engineer looks at the ceiling.

There's no shortage of estimation tools. Story points. Planning poker. Monte Carlo simulations for the particularly ambitious. They all work - in theory. They share one blind spot: they assume the project is new.

Think about what your team actually builds day to day. Integrating a new client. Rebuilding a feature on a new surface. Patching a set of servers. You've done versions of all of these before. The shape is familiar. You've just never kept a record of how long it took.

Break a repeatable type of project into its component tasks. For each task, estimate a minimum and a maximum delivery time. Save it somewhere sensible.

Next time a similar project arrives, you open the template, adjust for a few variables, and have a defensible answer in under five minutes. No planning poker. No collective ceiling-staring.

In the games and VR space, teams regularly create 3D environments inspired by the real world. New project, same basic shape. Every time.

Each task has a range - a fast version and a slow version. You're not pretending the work is perfectly predictable. You're capturing what you already know from experience.

In this example, the project lands somewhere between one and four months. And now the interesting conversations can start: do we have enough people? Do we have the right people? Is the scope realistic?

You're not scrambling for an answer any more. You're leading the conversation.
