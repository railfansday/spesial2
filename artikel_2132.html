West Ham have made a London Stadium admission that Birmingham City will be desperate to avoid

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

West Ham United and Birmingham City are two clubs that are hoping their current time in the Championship doesn't last much longer at all.

The Hammers were relegated from the Premier League six months ago after a 14-year spell amongst English football's elite, during which they lifted the UEFA Conference League in May 2023.

Blues, meanwhile, may have been relegated alongside West Ham in 2011, but haven't come close to ending such a lengthy exile from the top-flight barring a Championship play-off semi-final loss to Blackpool in 2012.

Both clubs are in healthy positions to mount serious promotion bids this term, although it is Chris Davies' side who, somewhat unexpectedly, lead the league at this moment in time on goal difference.

Recent developments at board level in East London will have certainly served as a reminder to Knighthead as to how running an ambitious football club with grand plans should be done, and the American firm will be hoping to avoid similar stadium-related issues in the years to come.

It has been well-documented for many years that West Ham have one of the largest and most passionate fanbases in England, especially when on their travels.

It was always the case, however, that such hostilities were evident at the club's former home, Upton Park, which they departed in 2016, three years after the move to the London Stadium on a 99-year tenancy was granted.

Situated amongst traditional terraced houses, Upton Park was famed for having the feeling that supporters were 'on top' of the opposition players, which often played to West Ham's advantage.

There have been some memorable times for the Irons since their stadium move, but even during the current campaign where over 50,000 season tickets have been sold, the ground still receives plenty of criticism for the overall matchday experience.

In a recent meeting with the Fan Advisory Board, members of the West Ham hierarchy acknowledged that a more 'homely feel' could be achieved by improving the atmosphere, recognising the club's heritage and identity, as well as commercial growth.

In September, it was confirmed that Czech billionaire, Daniel Křetínský, was set to become West Ham's largest stakeholder.

This move is yet to be fully confirmed, but it is one which will be welcomed by the majority of Hammers fans as the club looks to become more stable and forward-thinking yet again.

Kretinsky also made a strong impression on supporters in June, when it was confirmed that he wishes to, one day, remove the running track around the perimeter of the London Stadium pitch, with several athletics events still held at the stadium which West Ham do not own, some 14 years after the 'London 2012' olympics.

A year ago this week, Birmingham unveiled the 'Powerhouse' stadium, which had been designed by MANICA and Heatherwick Studio to the wider public.

Like West Ham, Blues are known for having one of the most loyal and vociferous fanbases outside the Premier League, with St. Andrew's @ Knighthead Park full to the brim these days after the club's current stadium was given a much-needed makeover two years ago.

Similarly to the London Stadium, the Powerhouse has been the subject of criticism through its 12-chimney design which pays tribute to the Second City's industrial heritage.

It has also been debated whether Birmingham, despite the club's large catchment area, will be able to fill the 62,500-seater stadium each and every match, but it feels rather apparent that Knighthead are desperate to avoid similar questions when it comes to the matchday experience.

Indeed, the stadium's South Stand has been designed to the maximum level of steepness to make it feel as hostile as possible for those visiting, rather than the stadium giving feelings of welcomeness and grandeur.

With Birmingham hoping to take many of the traditions from St. Andrew's to the Powerhouse in the coming years, Knighthead will instead be looking that the stadium has a similar feel to Everton's Hill Dickinson Stadium in terms of volume levels.

The Toffees have certainly made their new stadium feel like home in terms of results so far this season under David Moyes, but during the building of the 52,769-capacity ground, it was revealed that each stand climbs at 35 degrees, which is the maximum limit, to help retain as much of an atmosphere as possible.

Blues' new director of football, John Murtough, recently admitted how taken aback he was by the stadium design and the overall Sports Quarter project, which many are now waiting for with strong anticipation.
