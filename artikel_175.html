Car use in London forecast to rise by 10% in next decade

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Modelling in draft of 10-year London plan threatens Sadiq Khan’s promises on improving air quality

Car use in London will rise by 10% over the next decade with cycling and walking falling as a proportion of trips, according to an official forecast by the city’s mayoralty, putting into doubt Sadiq Khan’s promises on air quality and the environment.

Based on projections in the draft version of the latest 10-year London plan, drawn up by the mayor’s office and Transport for London (TfL), road congestion from a growing population would slow buses down, while packed tube services might require stations to close regularly to ease numbers.

Khan’s office said the figures were not a prediction but modelling, based on an assumption of no new transport policies, and did not take into account the impact of recent changes such as the pedestrianisation of Oxford Street.

The expectation was that active travel levels would increase and car use would fall, they added.

The draft plan for 2026-36 sets out three possible models of growth in population and jobs, and thus demand for transport, housing and other services. The central one of these, seen as the most likely, forecasts a population of 9.9 million people, up from the current 9.1 million.

If this happens, according to the plan, the number of car or other motor vehicle trips would rise by 10% between a baseline date of 2023 and 2036, with trips using public transport going up by 17%.

Journeys by active travel, such as walking and cycling, would rise by 6%, resulting in a slight fall as a proportion of all trips taken.

A relative decline in active travel would put London at odds with the trajectory of many other European cities, for example Paris, where bike use almost tripled as a proportion of all trips in just five years.

Mayoral officials, however, dispute that this will actually happen, pointing to London’s recent growth in cycling and walking trips, with new cycle lanes and hundreds of school streets that restrict motor traffic at drop-off and pickup times.

An increase in car journeys would also bring what the report called “significant challenges” on the roads, including a possible 7% reduction in average speeds, slowing down bus routes.

On public transport, overcrowding would become more common, while on the underground, severe overcrowding at some stations “indicates the possible regular need for intervention during periods of peak demand, such as closing stations to entering passengers or nonstopping trains”, it warned.

The modelling calls into question Khan’s progress on greening London’s transport network, given his pledge in 2022 for a 27% reduction in car use across London by 2030.

Ralph Smyth, a strategy adviser for the Transport Action Network campaign group, who uncovered the projections in the plan, said progress on shifting the capital away from car use appeared to have stalled.

He said: “The new London Plan was the opportunity for the mayor to leave a legacy of a cleaner, healthier and less congested city. Inexplicably, he seems to want to turn back the clock and increase cars across the capital.”

Khan has made progress in cleaning up London’s air, particularly with the expansion of the ultra-low emission zone to central and inner London in 2023, but the capital lags behind some other European cities and a shift towards more driving would further stall progress, even with the adoption of electric vehicles.

The transition to EVs will help, said Jenny Bates, a campaigner from Friends of the Earth, “but this will take many years – and it won’t reduce congestion”.

“London, like many cities, needs to reduce car use and reclaim space for safer walking and cycling routes, and more urban greenery to help cool the city during increasingly hot summers,” she said.

Simon Munk, the head of campaigns for the London Cycling Campaign, said the plan risked “modelling a future that is already out of date”.

He said: “TfL should not be modelling for ongoing heavy car use in London, but instead articulating how to do land-use planning and transport policy to get a lot less of it.”

A spokesperson for the mayor said: “City hall expects active travel will increase significantly by 2036 and car use will fall.

“The modelling in the London plan is based on a scenario in which no additional active travel schemes will be introduced over the next decade – which clearly is not the case, and the modelling will help identify what future transport schemes may be needed.”
