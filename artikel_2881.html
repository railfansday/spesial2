Meet Blades Brown, the teen golf phenom who just made PGA Tour history

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

The future arrived earlier than expected this weekend on the PGA Tour.

Blades Brown became the youngest champion on the PGA Tour since World War II when he rallied to win the World Wide Technology Championship in a playoff over Ben James on Sunday, Nov. 8, in Los Cabos, Mexico. The victory comes with wide-ranging implication for Brown's career, and cements his status as one of the rising stars in professional golf after turning pro out of high school nearly two years ago.

Brown overcame a seven-shot deficit, carding a 9-under 63 during the final round before James, a 23-year-old rookie who recently turned pro after a decorated college career at Virginia, forced a playoff late. Brown then won with a birdie on the second playoff hole, celebrating with a subdued fist pump as if he'd done this before.

"It hasn't set in just yet, I guess, the magnitude of being a PGA Tour winner," Brown told reporters afterwards. "I just go back to 5-year-old Blades that was watching the TV and watching Tiger Woods and all those great players win major championships, and to think that I'm now in a similar spot with having won a PGA Tour event is mind-blowing."

Blades Brown is 19 years old. He's the youngest PGA Tour winner since Bobby Dunkelberger won the St. Augustine Pro-Amateur in 1938 at age 18. The closest modern comparison is Jordan Spieth, who was 19 years, 352 days old when he won the 2013 John Deere Classic.

While still in high school, Brown joined Tiger Woods and Bobby Clampett as the only players to earn medalist honors in both the U.S. Amateur and U.S. Junior Amateur. He also became the youngest medalist in U.S. Amateur history in 2023.

Blades Brown grew up in the Nashville, Tennessee area and attended Brentwood Academy.

Blades Brown's mother is Rhonda Blades Brown, who was the first pick in the 1998 WNBA expansion draft for the New York Liberty. Brown is the first player in WNBA history to hit a 3-point shot in a game.

Brown's father, Parke, was in attendance as his son captured his first PGA Tour win. Parke Brown was diagnosed with leukemia three years ago and initially only given a short time to live. But he was misdiagnosed, according to the Golf Channel, and now manages the treatable disease.

Brown surprised the golf world when he elected become a professional as a high school junior, but his win Sunday further confirmed he knew what he was doing. Brown had already locked up his PGA Tour card for the 2027 season by finishing ninth in the Korn Ferry Tour standings this past season. But his win in Mexico on Sunday carries significant weight for 2028, when the PGA Tour debuts a new scheduling format.

Brown is now guaranteed a spot on the PGA Tour's Championship Series in 2028 thanks to his first PGA Tour win, and he also earned a two-year exemption into The Players Championship. It means Brown will be positioned with the top golfers in the world and have access to the PGA Tour's signature events and get to avoid the PGA Tour Challenger Series, at least initially.

What once seemed like a risky decision to bypass college has instead accelerated Brown's rise through professional golf. Less than two years after turning pro, he owns a PGA Tour victory and has already secured his playing status for years to come, validating a path few elite prospects have been willing to take.
