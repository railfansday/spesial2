Why isn’t the Las Vegas Raiders defense good enough

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Stop me if you've heard this before, but the Las Vegas Raiders defense has got a lot of growing to do to catch up with the offense. They have come within one score of, or surpassed, the 40-point threshold for the sixth time in 11 games.

Head coach Klint Kubiak said they win as a team and lose as a team; refusing to point fingers is noble and expected of the head coach. Defensive coordinator Rob Leonard elected not to take the high road, instead making a snide comment: "Fatigue makes cowards of us all…when you're on the field a long time, mistakes tend to happen more often." But here, we aren't limited by nobility; Leonard would have gotten a pass until he decided to open his mouth and speak on an offense ranking 29th in time of possession but 6th in total points.

So let us start by looking at who has been truly responsible for this lackluster defense. Leonard is the one calling the defensive plays and maintaining a league-worst defensive efficiency allowed, a paltry 48.1% defensive success rate and a 15.9% explosive play rate.

No one is doing jack up front for the Raiders defensive line outside of Maxx Crosby. Over the last three weeks, the Raiders defensive line has 2.5 sacks, five tackles for loss, seven quarterback hits, 1 forced fumble and two pass breakups. Crosby is the owner of every single one of those stats except one quarterback hit from Kwity Paye, Malcolm Koonce and Tonka Hemingway, along with the 0.5 sack, forced fumble and two pass breakups that Folorunso Fatukasi has produced.

Kwity Paye, a high-priced free agent acquisition, hasn't had a sack since Week 6 at home against the Buffalo Bills. Coincidentally, that happened to be the date of his last tackle for loss as well. His last quarterback hit was against Brock Purdy in Week 9. Hardly worthy of his lofty three-year, $48 million ($16 million average) contract.

Cutting him with a June-1 designation saves roughly $6.01 million, with $16.824 million (one year's salary) dead cap. At the rate of one sack every 5-6 weeks, one quarterback hit roughly every 2-3 weeks and a tackle for loss every six weeks, the rubber is going to have to meet the road at some point.

Malcolm Koonce, last year Koonce got a pass for coming off an injured knee and was kept over a more deserving K'Lavon Chaisson. After receiving a good-faith one-year $11.01 million contract, his good start to the 2026 season has faded into oblivion along with his productivity. Over the last three games, he has two tackles and a quarterback hit.

This season, he has accounted for 12 tackles, seven quarterback hits, three sacks, two forced fumbles and 1 tackle for loss. His last 0.5 sack was in Week 5 against the New England Patriots; the quarterback hit against the Denver Broncos on Sunday was his first impact as a rusher in six weeks.

Adam Butler, according to Pro Football Focus, ranks 84th out of 134 qualified interior defensive linemen. Strangely enough, Butler's best grade is as a run defender, ranking 49th of 134 and then comes the fall. His 21 total tackles are outside the Top 50 defensive tackles; his 0.5 sacks are outside the Top 75 defensive tackles; his seven total quarterback pressures are in the bottom third of qualifying defensive interiors.

Eric Stokes has played so poorly as of late that it is baffling how he is still starting. At this point, he has been every opponent's favorite target; more so than even the rookies. When he isn't giving up on plays, he is getting beaten like a drum. Stokes is allowing a passer rating of 115.2, making him the 9th-worst cover corner in the league coming out of week 10. What's worse is that number has been steadily climbing over the last three weeks. In weeks 8-10, he posted a passer rating of 116.1. Additionally, when it comes to penalties, he has four accepted pass interference calls resulting in 58 yards; the first being tied for 5th most and the latter being the 5th most in the league. Stokes may have five pass breakups this season, but he still hasn't recorded an interception since week 4 of his rookie season.

Hezekiah Masses, Jermod McCoy, Darien Porter: All three are rookie cornerbacks who must be afforded an opportunity to learn their craft and experience bumps and bruises along the way. Before blaming them for all the explosive plays generated, we have to understand they are being asked to cover for a league-worst 3.0 seconds to throw and 4th-worst in pocket time allowed, 2.72 seconds.

Individually, they have all experienced success in one way, shape, or form. All of these young defensive backs need to get in the weight room and transform their bodies from college to the pros. Getting acclimated to the league is a process and all of these young players have shown promise.

Masses has three interceptions this year but is still searching for a permanent positional home. Slightly undersized, he has the heart of a lion but needs a good NFL strength and development plan in the offseason. Out wide, he has three interceptions, but his productivity in the slot has been an improvement over veteran Taron Johnson, which offers positional versatility.

He is a better man-coverage corner than soft zone but can do both. A ferocious blitzer who can get home when activated but is still too small to be a nickel linebacker. One of the few blemishes has been his ability to hold contain when he's the last defender on the line.

Porter the second-year cornerback, is a bit of an enigma. Unable to keep his starting job locked down from Masses and McCoy, he hasn't been bad and has proven that, if utilized correctly, he can be an asset to the defense. Porter is a converted wide receiver with size, speed and length who specializes in press-man coverage.

However, the main issue is the press-bail and zone technique he is being asked to play is a style which isn't conducive to what he does best. He is a good and willing tackler, better suited to larger, less quick-twitch receivers. Opposite of Stokes, he is the second-best tackler out past the edges.

McCoy, when healthy, has the tools to be the best of the bunch. Watching him stay balanced, controlled and locate the football was a sight for sore eyes. Playing the ball all the way through the catching process and being able to punch the ball out is what the Raiders need. Tackling in the run game was a problem against the Broncos. All in all, for a rookie making his second start, there are some encouraging signs but also plenty of room for improvement.

Enough can't be said about how disappointing the run defense has been. This season, the 16 rushing touchdowns allowed are tied for the most in the league. The Raiders are allowing 149.5 yards per game, ranking 31st, and 4.82 yards per carry, ranking 30th in the league. Surprisingly, the 51 missed tackles are middle of the pack, so the reality is they are getting outmuscled.

Against the Broncos, the Raiders lost three defensive tackles to injury. Whether they are able to play in the Sunday Night Game against the Cleveland Browns remains to be seen. If they are not, the run defense stands to be even worse.
