Brian Brobbey has struggled to retain his fitness as Sunderland battle against Premier League relegation this season.

His impact in the final third couldn't have been anymore evident during the Black Cats' 1-0 win over Chelsea before the international break.

The centre-forward could only last 67 minutes, though, and it appears that he's picked up yet another problem after sustaining a hamstring injury before.

Brobbey was one of the key reasons for Sunderland's success last season, helping Regis Le Bris' side seventh-place finish in the Premier League.

His efforts didn't go unnoticed, with several clubs around Europe, including Chelsea and Manchester United, targeting the 24-year-old's signature.

Football Insider understood that even Nottingham Forest were interested, at least before they splashed £50million on Liam Delap.

The problem now, however, is that Brobbey has picked up another injury, and Le Bris says it is far too early to add a return timeline to the situation.

While the manager is confident that it's not a "big injury," it is another blow that the Black Cats didn't need, one that seriously needed avoiding.

With so many clubs interested during the summer, there undoubtedly would have been a chance to cash in, but the club didn't take it.

Now, the Black Cats are left wondering who they might have brought onboard using that money, while Brobbey requires more medical treatment.

Still, Football Insider understands that Sunderland will only consider selling if they are relegated from the Premier League.

Right now, Brobbey is valued at just over £41m, which is already lower than the potential price tag that the Black Cats had slapped on him.

That number could drop by at least £5m in the coming weeks, especially if Le Bris' not-so-bad news turns into something more serious.

If the worst is to happen and Sunderland finish in the Premier League relegation zone, there is no way that they'd be able to fetch a fee anywhere that high.

Maybe resisting the summer interest wasn't the best idea, but foresight is a wonderful tool, and not one that anyone has.
