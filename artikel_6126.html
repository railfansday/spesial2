CEO Dom Dragisich can claim a win in budget hotels, with Choice recently outperforming the industry average. But upscale brands grew at a much lower pace than the industry averages.

Choice Hotels' budget brands outpaced their U.S. industrywide benchmark in the summer, offering an early sign of progress for CEO Dominic Dragisich. But the company's higher-priced brands continued to lag.

U.S. revenue per available room (RevPAR) grew 3.8%, roughly a 1.5 percentage points above Wall Street's forecast. Dragisich said on a Monday earnings call that October's RevPAR was about 7%.

Dragisich's core problem in August was that Choice trailed in every price tier where it competes, from economy to upscale, according to CoStar data. The third quarter improved the picture without erasing it.

"We did narrow the gap," Dragisich said, referring to Choice's performance relative to industry averages.

Choice Hotels' third-quarter results gave CEO Dominic Dragisich an early sign of progress at the low end of the portfolio and little else. U.S. RevPAR rose 3.8%, about 1.5 percentage points ahead of Wall Street's forecast, and Dragisich said October RevPAR came in around 7%. But the gains were uneven: Choice's economy brands grew RevPAR 3.5% against an industry figure of about 2.5%, while its upscale and above brands grew just 1.1% against a roughly 4% industry average, and midscale and upper-midscale grew 3.6% against about 4.5%, according to Truist Securities citing CoStar data. Truist analysts C. Patrick Scholes and Gregory Miller wrote that the result implies Choice continues to lose market share, estimating fair share would have been closer to 5% to 6% growth. The U.S. hotel sector overall grew RevPAR 7% in the quarter, according to CBRE. Choice's room count is roughly flat year over year, and Dragisich said he wants net room growth of 2% to 4%. The company took a $24 million charge to terminate an option to acquire an unnamed European hospitality business after the option "eventually migrated to an obligation" and the target's growth became more lease-based. Choice said it is prioritizing organic growth, bought RV membership club Harvest Hosts for about $130 million on October 1, and sold two owned hotels for about $34 million in net proceeds while keeping the franchise agreements, leaving 17 owned hotels.
