The Best Volufiline Products for Subtly Plumper-Looking Skin

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Touted as a filler alternative, dermatologists say the effects of Volufiline are much more subtle.

As conversations around the beauty side effects of GL-P1s spread, and on the heels of the PDRN craze, a new player has entered the skin-plumping game with big promises. Volufiline, a patented cosmetic ingredient first developed by the French company Sederma, is an oil-based blend of hydrogenated polyisobutene (a synthetic hydrocarbon polymer) and an extract derived from the root of Anemarrhena asphodeloides, also known as Zhi Mu, a plant traditionally used in Asian medicine. Deborah R. Spey, MD, a board-certified dermatologist at Schweiger Dermatology in Livingston, New Jersey, says the ingredient has gained attention for its purported ability to subtly plump the skin and improve the appearance of volume loss, and for its claim to be a topical replacement for injectable filler.

According to Spey, the active component in Volufiline is believed to encourage existing fat cells to store more lipids and support the maturation of new fat cells, potentially increasing volume in the deeper layers of the skin. “However, there is very limited scientific data supporting its effectiveness, and any reported plumping effects have been quite modest,” she tells ELLE. “While the concept is promising, more research is needed to determine how effectively a topical formulation can deliver these results.”

Beverly Hills esthetician, Candace Marino adds that the ingredient is thought to encourage the fat cells just under the skin to store more lipids, which gives the area a softer, fuller look. “Think subtle improvement over time, not overnight change,” says Marino.

Volufiline has a completely different composition and mechanism of action compared to injectable dermal fillers. Marino says they’re not comparable. “Filler is injected, immediate, and can actually lift and reshape. Volufiline is a gentle topical option for someone who isn’t ready for injectables or wants to maintain between appointments.”

Spey further explains how the ingredient works compared to traditional filler. “Many traditional dermal fillers are made from cross-linked hyaluronic acid and are injected into specific tissue planes to restore lost volume and provide structural support,” Spey explains. Volufiline, on the other hand, is a topical ingredient intended to encourage the skin’s own plumping process. “There is a significant difference between injectable fillers, which can reach deeper structural layers, and a topical product, which has limitations in how deeply it can penetrate the skin. Volufiline should not be considered a replacement for injectable dermal fillers, particularly for individuals seeking noticeable volume restoration,” she clarifies.

Spey says that an ingredient like Volufiline may be a reasonable option for individuals looking for subtle plumping or hoping to improve the appearance of mild volume loss. However, she cautions that because it is oil-based, it may not be ideal for those with oily or acne-prone skin, as it could potentially aggravate these conditions. She says it also has not been adequately studied for use during pregnancy, so pregnant individuals should avoid using it unless advised otherwise by their health care provider.

Marino recommends the ingredient for anyone noticing early volume loss, hollowness under the eyes, flattening in the cheeks, or thinner lips. Drier and more mature skin tends to do especially well with it. If you’re acne-prone, pay attention to the oils in the formula.

Volufiline can be paired with other skin care ingredients, including peptides, ceramides, squalane, hyaluronic acid, niacinamide, and growth factors. However, Spey recommends avoiding layering it directly with retinoids, such as retinol, or strong exfoliating acids to minimize the potential for irritation. “If you use retinol regularly, you can incorporate Volufiline into your morning routine and reserve retinol for the evening, or alternate their use,” she suggests. In serum form, apply it to clean skin and follow it with a moisturizer. Cream formulations can serve as the moisturizing step in your routine.

There is a benefit to combining Volufiline with a retinol regimen (though not at the same time). According to Marino, retinol works on texture and collagen, and Volufiline works on fullness. “If your skin is sensitive, use retinol at night and Volufiline in the morning. Or, depending on the formula, you can layer them in the evening,” she suggests.
