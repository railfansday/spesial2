Texas botches execution of Reinaldo Dennes as staffers struggle to find vein

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Dennes, 70, was convicted of killing a Houston jeweler, and groaned for an hour after pentobarbital was administered

Texas executed Reinaldo Dennes on Thursday in a procedure that took over an hour as prison staffers struggled to find a suitable vein for the two intravenous needles carrying a lethal dose of pentobarbital, a sedative.

Dennes, who was found guilty of capital murder in the death of Janos Szucs, a diamond dealer found shot multiple times in his Houston office in January 1996, was the second person put to death in the US on Thursday, after Florida carried out a record-breaking 20th execution of the year, of Thomas Mitchell Overton.

Dennes gave a brief statement before the procedure began, then he told the warden: “Go, warden!”

Almost immediately he exclaimed over the pain, swearing and begging “the father” to “let me go”. He complained of more pain in his leg,

then said, “I don’t think this is normal. Take me, father. Don’t let me suffer like this. C’mon.”

The procedure was completed at 7.07pm, 63 minutes after he was strapped to the gurney. A minute later, after being examined by a physician, Dennes was pronounced dead at 7.08pm at the penitentiary in Huntsville.

Prison officials said all procedures were followed appropriately.

This took place at the same time Florida carried out a record-breaking 20th execution for the year, of a man convicted of killing a pregnant woman and her husband during a home invasion.

Thomas Mitchell Overton, 71, was pronounced dead at 6.19pm after receiving a three-drug injection at Florida state prison in Raiford. In a final statement, he denied ever breaking into the couple’s home and attacking them, saying: “I never killed anyone.”

The executions of the two septuagenarians in one day reflect the ageing of the US death row population.

Overton was convicted of killing Michael and Susan MacIvor and their unborn child during an August 1991 break-in at their Florida Keys home. Dennes was convicted of capital murder in the death of Janos Szucs, a diamond dealer found shot multiple times in his office in January 1996.

On Thursday, Overton woke up at 5am and remained compliant, a Florida department of corrections spokesperson, Jordan Kirkland, said during a news conference. He did not meet with any visitors or a spiritual adviser.

Florida has now carried out more than half the country’s 39 executions this year under Ron DeSantis, the state’s outgoing Republican governor who has made aggressive use of the death penalty like few other governors. Thursday’s executions come amid renewed scrutiny of capital punishment in the US after Tennessee’s failed execution of Christa Pike in October.

Texas set a record for executions there when 40 people were put to death in 2000. Dennes became the ninth person to be executed by the state this year.

The execution followed renewed scrutiny of capital punishment in the US in the aftermath of Tennessee’s failed execution in September of Christa Pike, who survived two doses of pentobarbital that her lawyers say probably did not enter her bloodstream correctly and instead pooled in her arms, leaving them burned and blistered.

On Thursday, the US supreme court denied a final appeal in the Florida case while an appeal remained pending in the Texas case.

Overton was convicted in 1999 of two counts of first-degree murder and received a death sentence for each. Defense attorneys had argued that investigators had planted the DNA evidence.

The MacIvors were found dead in their Tavernier, Florida, home in August 1991, according to court records. Autopsies determined both were strangled and that Susan MacIvor, who was eight months pregnant, had been raped. Overton, who had worked at a gas station near the MacIvors’ home, was arrested on an unrelated burglary charge in 1996. Authorities said a sample taken from him matched the sample collected at the MacIvors’ home, and he was charged with their killings.

In the Texas case, Dennes, a jewelry designer, had an office in the same building as Szucs, according to court documents.

A security guard, who was shot twice but recovered, identified Dennes as his attacker.

Police found the jeweler’s body upon entering his office, according to court filings. The documents said officers found the jeweler’s safe empty with no sign of the diamonds or the $200,000 in cash he was supposed to have on hand. The diamonds have never been recovered.

A year after Dennes was convicted, his brother, Jose Albert Dennes, pleaded guilty to murder in the case and was sentenced to 45 years in prison. He was released on parole in 2019.
