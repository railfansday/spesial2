Seattle Mariners Free Agency: New Wrinkle Added in Team's Pursuit of Seiya Suzuki

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Going into Thanksgiving, many Seattle Mariners fans would have been helpful with some more free agent new, but alas, the holiday went by without another major league transaction being made.

However, the day didn't pass without some news pertaining to the team's top free agent target, former Chicago Cubs outfielder Seiya Suzuki.

The Cubs opted to find Suzuki's replacement in the form of one of his countrymen, Teruaki Sato.

Sato was posted by the Hanshin Tigers of Japan's Nippon Professional Baseball this offseason, and signed a six-year, $120 million deal with Chicago. The contract contains the most money and the most years for a position player on their first deal out of Japan.

Sato's deal comes several years after Suzuki signed a five-year, $85 million deal with the Cubs before the 2022 season after he was posted by the Hiroshima Toya Carp.

And this time around, Suzuki will be looking for more.

According to a report Thursday from MLB insider Jon Heyman for the New York Post, Suzuki has informed teams that he wants more than the $120 million bench mark set by Sato.

Unlike Sato, who will make an average of $20 million a year, Suzuki is seeking a contract that's 4-5 years in length, bumping up the annual salary to $24-30 million a season.

Suzuki is coming off his best year in the major leagues. In addition to being a plus-defender in right field, he slashed .270/.368/.473 with an .841 OPS in 143 games and hit 31 doubles, a triple and 26 home runs with 86 RBIs.

As one of the top bats available in a weak free agency class for position players, Suzuki has been on the radar of several teams in need of a corner outfielder and a boost in their lineup. His profile as a right-handed hitter also is appealing to many teams, including Seattle.

In addition the Mariners, the San Diego Padres, Philadelphia Phillies and Toronto Blue Jays have all been reported to express interest in Suzuki.

Suzuki has been thought to be one of the top earners in free agency since the period began. This hasn't deterred Seattle's interest from being reported.

However, it's unclear whether or not the the Mariners anticipated Suzuki's final asking price being in excess of $120 million, and if the team will be able to meet that asking price, especially with big spenders such as the Blue Jays and Phillies reportedly interested.

It was reported earlier this week by Adam Jude of the Seattle Times that Seattle had shifted its focus on the bullpen. This could mean the Mariners have already put a final offer in front of Suzuki and are awaiting him to make a decision. If that offer came before Suzuki informed teams of his specific contract desires, then Seattle might already be out of the running.

The Mariners do not intend to bring back fellow right-handed outfielder Randy Arozarena, as evidenced by the fact that they reportedly did not extend him a contract offer.

Among the remaining free agent left fielders include Taylor Ward, who struggled for Seattle after the team acquired him in a trade this season with the Baltimore Orioles, Ian Happ, Ramon Laureano and Tommy Pham, among others.
