SEC proposed cap policy: League penalties could reach $10 million for spending over $70.5 million

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Details regarding the SEC's proposed cap policy emerged, per Yahoo Sports' Ross Dellenger. The league could penalize teams up to $10 million for spending over the cap on their rosters.

"Details of the SEC's proposed cap policy are emerging: The league would penalize schools in a tiered fine system reaching $10M+ in fines for those spending $70.5M+ on rosters, sources tell Yahoo Sports," Dellenger wrote.

As Dellenger pointed out, the SEC's proposal is far different than the Big Ten's cap rules which were passed in October. The SEC would consider some school affiliated, third party NIL deals as "institutional pay," implementing terms of the House settlement that have not been enforced previously.

"Financial penalties would begin low and gradually increase in a tiered system," Dellenger wrote. "Fines begin at less than 1% for those spending below $50.5 million on their rosters, and then increase to 1% for those spending $50.5-60.5 million, then to 3% for those spending $60.5-70.5 million and, finally, to 10% for those spending more than $70.5 million. This means a school could spend $70.5 million on its athletes with a fine of around $7 million.

"However, the financial penalties in each tier increase with each new cap year. Next year, for example, the model increases the penalty from 3% to 5% for those spending between $60.5-$70.5 million and from 10% to 15% for those exceeding $70.5 million. The following year, the penalty would reach 20% for the biggest spenders — a tax of roughly around $14-16 million."

Dellenger was told, by one Power Four athletic director, that the summary is the cap would be "basically" $70 million. This concept overall should be accepted by many, per Dellenger.

"In a concept widely supported by many college leaders, the SEC's proposal may remove the requirement for schools to gain approval from the College Sports Commission's NIL Go clearinghouse for NIL agreements with associated entities redirecting revenue from the athletic department to the roster," he wrote.

"This would eliminate what officials say are time-consuming and resource-draining measures to circumvent the existing cap by redirecting revenue to athletes in the form of concocted marketing and endorsement agreements. Many universities are paying as much as 20% to third party entities to "wash money," several power conference leaders contend, in an effort to effectively pass the contracts through the NIL Go clearinghouse."

A retroactive implementation date of October 1st is under discussion for the new fining structure as well. Third party NIL deals featuring redirected school revenue that were submitted on or after that date would be considered "institutional pay." According to Dellenger, only fully independent third party NIL payments are excluded from the new cap policy, which includes authentic marketing and endorsement deals that athletes strike on their own.
