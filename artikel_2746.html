Terry Maul’s coaching legacy spanned FSU, ATAC and generations of swimmers

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Terry Maul spent half a century coaching swimmers, from Florida State to the Area Tallahassee Aquatic Club. But the best measure of his career might not be found in victories, Olympic Trials qualifiers or the national teams he coached.

It can be found in the swimmers and coaches he impacted.

"Coach Terry helped many of us find joy and success in swimming and in life," former Leon High and ATAC swimmer Emily Kennedy told the Tallahassee Democrat. "He cared about who we became in and outside the pool and pushed us to be the best version of ourselves."

Maul, a former FSU swimmer and Seminoles head coach who became a longtime leader at ATAC, died Thursday, Nov. 12, following a battle with cancer, ATAC announced.

Tributes quickly poured in from former swimmers, coaches and parents, locally and across the country.

Bob Bowman, a former FSU swimmer (1983-85), assistant coach (1986-87) and ATAC coach who went on to coach Michael Phelps, the most decorated Olympian of all time with 23 gold medals, reflected on Maul's influence in a Facebook post.

"He sparked something in me that has defined my life and his influence has flowed through me to countless athletes and coaches," Bowman wrote. "He was the best definition of 'coach.' I'm forever grateful that Terry Maul was my coach, colleague and friend."

Current FSU swimming and diving coach Neal Studd said Maul's influence continues to be felt throughout the Seminoles' program and the larger swimming community.

"Terry holds a special place in our program's history," Studd said. "His dedication to Florida State, ATAC, and generations of swimmers leaves a lasting legacy in our sport and in the lives of those he coached."

Studd has also worked with Maul's son, John, who followed his father into coaching at ATAC.

"The true measure of a coach is the impact they have on people," Studd said. "Terry's influence lives on through the swimmers he guided, the colleagues he worked alongside, and the community he helped shape."

"Rest in peace, Coach Maul," Studd added. "Thank you for all you gave to Florida State and the sport of swimming."

Maul's accomplishments, detailed in his ATAC biography, reflected the reach of his 50-year coaching career. He achieved the highest level of certification from the American Swimming Coaches Association for both club and NCAA swimming and served as head coach of the U.S. National Team in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, in 1984 and the U.S. National Junior Team in Montreal, Canada, in 1992.

Maul coached at FSU for 18 seasons, from 1975-93, and over his career developed numerous Olympic Trials qualifiers, Senior and Junior National finalists and age-group swimmers ranked among the nation's top 16.

Yet the tributes following his death focused less on accomplishments than on the relationships he built during countless hours around the pool.

Maul didn't just coach swimmers. He helped shape coaches, lives and a swimming community whose influence continued long after athletes climbed out of the pool.

"He was such a great man, coach and friend to me, " Ann Belew O'Brien, who swam for Maul at FSU from 1983-87, told the Democrat.

For Kennedy, the memories return with something as simple as a familiar smell from long days at Trousdell Aquatics Center.

"There was a confederate jasmine flower that climbed up the fence bordering the pool that I would smell lap upon lap during the spring and the summer," Kennedy said. "I used to hate that smell because it reminded me of how tired I was swimming all those laps back then. Now, anytime I smell it, it reminds me of how grateful I am to have found swimming. ...

Maul also served as an assistant professor of statistics and sociology at Thomas University in Thomasville, Georgia, where his wife, Dr. Jodiann Yambor, is a sports psychologist and professor. The couple has two children, John, who followed his father into coaching and currently leads the ATAC program, and Alyssa.
