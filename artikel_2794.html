5 Things We Learned After Celtics Dismantle 76ers 123-101 Behind Hot Shooting

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The Boston Celtics delivered a statement performance against the Philadelphia 76ers, dismantling their Eastern Conference rivals 123-101 behind an outstanding shooting display and impressive contributions throughout the rotation. Paul George led the way with 36 points, while Jayson Tatum added 21 points and six rebounds. Payton Pritchard and Sam Hauser provided crucial perimeter scoring off the bench as Boston established complete control.

Philadelphia struggled to keep pace despite Tyrese Maxey scoring 24 points and Anfernee Simons adding 17 off the bench. The Celtics' combination of perimeter shooting, offensive efficiency, and defensive pressure proved overwhelming, allowing Boston to build a 29-point lead.

Here are five things we learned from Boston's convincing victory.

Paul George was unquestionably the biggest individual story of Boston's victory.

The Celtics forward exploded for 36 points on 14-22 shooting, including six three-pointers on 11 attempts. He also converted both free throws, grabbed five rebounds, and committed just one turnover in 30 minutes.

George's 63.6% shooting from the field was particularly impressive considering his substantial offensive workload.

His ability to generate efficient scoring from multiple areas of the floor made him virtually impossible for Philadelphia to contain. Whether attacking the basket or creating opportunities from beyond the arc, George consistently punished the 76ers' defensive coverage.

The Celtics need George to remain aggressive alongside Tatum, and this performance demonstrated just how dangerous Boston can become when both stars are operating effectively.

The Celtics' perimeter shooting was arguably the biggest difference between the two teams.

Boston converted 22 of 49 three-point attempts, shooting an impressive 44.9%. Philadelphia, meanwhile, managed only 11 makes on 37 attempts, finishing at 29.7%.

That created an enormous 33-point advantage for Boston from beyond the arc.

Sam Hauser was particularly impressive off the bench, converting four of six three-point attempts for 12 points. Payton Pritchard added four triples and 18 points, while George knocked down six shots from deep.

Tatum also contributed three three-pointers, helping Boston maintain constant pressure on Philadelphia's defense.

The Celtics' ability to consistently generate perimeter scoring opportunities created an offensive ceiling that Philadelphia simply couldn't match.

Boston's supporting cast deserves significant credit for the victory.

Payton Pritchard finished with 18 points, six rebounds, and five assists while shooting 7-15 from the field. His four three-pointers helped sustain Boston's offensive momentum, and he finished with a game-high plus-26 rating.

Sam Hauser also delivered an efficient performance, recording 12 points and five rebounds while shooting 4-6 from three-point range.

Their contributions were especially important because they prevented Philadelphia from gaining momentum whenever Boston's starters rotated out.

The Celtics received 30 combined points from Pritchard and Hauser, providing the kind of secondary scoring that makes their offense difficult to defend over 48 minutes.

Boston's bench production remains one of its most important advantages when the team is playing at this level.

Boston also dominated the turnover battle.

The Celtics committed only nine turnovers, compared to Philadelphia's 14. More importantly, Boston converted those mistakes into 21 points off turnovers while surrendering just nine.

That 12-point advantage was another significant contributor to the final margin.

Boston also recorded six steals and four blocks, demonstrating its ability to disrupt Philadelphia's offensive possessions without consistently giving the basketball away itself.

Philadelphia actually finished with 21 fast-break points compared to Boston's 10, but that advantage wasn't nearly enough to overcome its struggles in half-court offense and perimeter shooting.

The Celtics' overall offensive execution was outstanding.

Boston shot 50.5% from the field, converted 44.9% of its three-point attempts, and finished with 28 assists. Philadelphia managed only 41.8% shooting and 23 assists.

Boston also scored 46 points in the paint, compared to Philadelphia's 32, demonstrating that its offense wasn't exclusively dependent on perimeter shooting.

The Celtics finished with 48 made field goals on 95 attempts, while Philadelphia converted only 33 of 79.

Additionally, Boston grabbed 43 rebounds compared to Philadelphia's 42, ensuring that the 76ers couldn't compensate for their offensive struggles through second-chance opportunities.

The result was a complete offensive performance that allowed Boston to lead for 95% of the game and establish a maximum advantage of 29 points.
