Oregon football handles business in drubbing of Michigan State

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

It wasn't always pretty, especially in the first half of a cold night in East Lansing, Michigan − a place the Oregon football team had never won in − but the No. 13 Ducks ultimately did what they had to do in a 45-7 demolition of Michigan State Nov. 20 at Spartan Stadium.

All of the Ducks' (8-3, 6-2 Big Ten) postseason aspirations are, somehow, still in front of them after another dominant win, though this time against a downtrodden Spartans (3-8, 1-7) squad.

Previously dismissed as pretenders, UO is very much alive in the College Football Playoff race and put together another impressive result in Friday's expected drubbing.

"We're starting to play our best football towards the end," quarterback Dylan Raiola said. "That's what coach Lanning wanted. He told us that before the season even started, and hopefully we get in this thing, because ... I think we'll take advantage of our opportunity."

It didn't always look so dominant, especially in a first half littered with inconsistent play, and in particular in the offensive run game and in pass protection. Raiola was sacked three times in the first half and visibly frustrated with his offensive line, though eventually he settled in and completed 21 of 34 passes for 234 yards and a score.

The Ducks lost yet another running back in Dierre Hill Jr. to an early injury but saw freshman Brandon Smith reignite the run game with 14 carries for 121 yards and two touchdowns.

The defense, taking on a much-maligned MSU offense down to its third-string quarterback, was sometimes bend-but-don't-break but held the Spartans to just seven points and 200 yards of total offense. The Spartans averaged a measly 2.2 yards per rush attempt.

Still, Oregon couldn't help but lament Michigan State's lone touchdown drive after allowing just one touchdown the week previous against Michigan.

"Man, literally two plays away from two shutouts," senior defender A'Mauri Washington said. "It was just like, you want to get those plays back. It's the most simple things that we could have picked up and it could have led to a shutout. We just need to get over that hump."

With yet another running back going down with an injury in Hill, and Jordon Davison out over the last three games, the freshman took advantage of his opportunity yet again in more of a starring role Friday night.

"It's been great that Brandon's been getting more and more reps," Lanning said.

In a season marred by inconsistent play along the offensive line throughout the first half of the season and with a pair of banged-up starters, Smith had the first 100-yard rushing game by a Duck this season in the win.

"Coming in as a freshman, you always want to think, 'Yeah, I'm gonna play and do all these crazy things,'" Smith said. "But kind of just a reality check for me a little bit coming off a good senior year of high school and then coming in freshman year of college ... Just always tell myself to be ready. You never know when it's going to come, and my moments have been coming every week now."

It's unclear how long Davison and Hill will be sidelined for, though Lanning mentioned during his Monday press conference that Davison is out of his boot. Hill was seen in a boot and on crutches during the second half of Friday's win.

At the moment, though, Oregon has one confident freshman running back heading into late November.

The first act of business for Oregon, under any circumstance if it wants to make a third-straight CFP appearance, is to win. The Ducks did that Friday and have just one game left to show the committee that they belong in the 12-team field.

It also did so in dominant fashion, which it needed to continue to pass the proverbial "eye test" the committee has raved about in recent weeks, leading to the Ducks surpassing a two-loss Iowa in the latest rankings.

A few losses by teams ranked ahead of Oregon this weekend wouldn't hurt, either.
