Academy trust pays out after Prevent threat over children’s Palestine badges

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Lion Academy Trust apologises after threatening four pupils in east London with referral to counter-extremism scheme

An academy trust will pay out tens of thousands in compensation to four children who were told they could be referred to Prevent for wearing Palestine flag badges to school.

The school, part of Lion Academy Trust, has issued a formal apology after it sent letters to the children’s parents saying they might be referred to the counter-extremism programme or to local hate crime teams.

The issue dates back to November 2023 when the children’s school, Barclay primary school in Leyton, east London, held a non-uniform fundraising day for Children in Need.

The four children, all British Muslims aged between six and 10 at the time, attended wearing items displaying Palestinian colours or the Palestinian flag, in memory of children who had been killed in Gaza and those who continued to suffer during the conflict, which was ongoing at the time.

One child wore a Children in Need Pudsey sticker featuring a bow tie in Palestinian colours. Another wore a homemade badge bearing the Palestinian flag and colours.

Parents of the four children said they had repeatedly asked the school to apologise for their treatment of the children and had launched a legal challenge when none was forthcoming.

At a hearing at Central London county court on Thursday, the court heard that the academy trust had now made a formal apology to the children and their families and would pay them substantial compensation.

Lawyers for the children alleged direct discrimination, indirect discrimination and harassment on grounds including race, ethnicity, religion and belief, together with breaches of their rights to freedom of expression and freedom of thought, conscience and belief.

Shahid Achhala, the father of one of the children, said: “What happened to our son was deeply disturbing and should never have happened to any child. He was just eight years old, expressing compassion and solidarity with children suffering in Gaza, including members of his own extended family. Instead of being supported and protected by the school entrusted with his care, he was deliberately subjected to mistreatment that caused him considerable emotional distress.

“For almost three years, we have had to fight legally for accountability and an acknowledgment of the wrongs we believe were committed. While we welcome Lion Academy Trust’s apology, we cannot overlook the distress caused to our son and our family, or the fact that this matter could have been resolved long time ago.”

The families were represented by law firm Leigh Day and supported in their legal claim by Prevent Watch, which provides casework support to individuals and families affected by the Prevent duty.

Layla Aitlhadj, a director at Prevent Watch, said: “In this case, parents who were protecting their children’s rights were told that their concerns were ‘false and malicious’. They held firm through three years of pressure. Today the families, and the wider community who stood with them, have been vindicated.”

The academy trust has been approached for comment.
