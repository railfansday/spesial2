Sinner to miss Australian Open after undergoing knee surgery

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Jannik Sinner won the Australian Open in 2024 and 2025 but will miss the 2027 edition due to knee surgery; Tennis Australia confirmed his absence from the opening Grand Slam of the year; Sinner has not played since winning Wimbledon in July

Jannik Sinner will miss the Australian Open after undergoing knee surgery, tournament organisers have announced.

The world No 2 has not played since defending his Wimbledon title in July, seeing Alexander Zverev overtake him at the top of the world rankings.

Sinner confirmed on Tuesday he had undergone minor surgery on his right knee, extending his injury lay-off and leaving his participation in the first Grand Slam tournament of 2027 - starting January 17 - in doubt.

The Australian Open hinted at his likely absence by posting on social media that they would miss Sinner this summer, with Tennis Australia chief executive Andrew Abdo confirming that Sinner will not take part in Melbourne.

"We've been in regular touch with Jannik's team and they've confirmed he won't be joining us for the Australian Open in January following his recent surgery," Abdo said.

"Jannik loves competing here in Melbourne and is looking forward to returning for the Australian Open in 2028."

Sinner said earlier this month he was confident of returning in time for the Australian Open but would not rush recovery, with the 25-year-old since confirming the surgery on his knee was due to experiencing ongoing pain .

Sinner said in a statement earlier this week: "Over the past few months, together with my team, we've tried everything to treat my injury without surgery.

"After some very encouraging progress early on, my knee unfortunately didn't respond as we had all hoped, that's why today I underwent a minor surgery to improve the condition of my iliotibial band.

"I really miss being on court and will now begin my recovery process together with my team."

The iliotibial band is a thick band of fibrous connective tissue running down the outside of the thigh, and injuries associated with it are typically caused by overuse.

Sinner had made a stellar start to 2026 which saw him win the first five Masters 1000 tournaments of the year, becoming the second man after Novak Djokovic to achieve the Golden Masters.

Zverev and world No 3 Carlos Alcaraz supported Sinner's decision to undergo surgery, with his Grand Slam title rivals looking forward to being able to welcome him back to the court.

"The decision of making the surgery, I think his team made a good decision," Alcaraz, the defending Australian Open champion, said on Wednesday.

"He's young, has a lot of years ahead of him. I'm sure he's going to come back even stronger, even better."

Sinner won his first Grand Slam singles title in 2024 by beating Daniil Medvedev in five sets at Melbourne Park, then defended it by winning the 2025 final against Zverev.

Zverev added: "We know that he's mentally strong. I hope that he takes every step serious and that, in three, four months' time, he can be back to 100 per cent and be back playing."
