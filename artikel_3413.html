After 50 Years, Marvel Finally Gives A Fan-Favorite Avenger Their Own Joker

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Every comic book hero needs a villain to define them. It’s how an average character becomes something even greater than anyone could have expected. It’s doubtful that Batman, Superman, and Thor would be where they are without Joker, Lex, and Loki. There are exceptions to the rule, like Wonder Woman. Someone so well-written and important to a genre that they surpass what’s needed to make a god-tier character. Unfortunately, even the best-written heroes don’t always rise to the top. That’s where Marvel's Jessica Drew, Spider-Woman, comes in.

It may be hard to believe, but Jessica Drew has been around almost as long as Storm and Wolverine. Another fun fact about Jessica is that she, unlike many other Spider-People, doesn’t have a direct connection to Peter Parker. Other than being part of the Spider Totem, they aren't related in any way. Her power and skills come from having terrorist parents, being connected to the High Evolutionary, and working with Hydra. That backstory should have been enough to make her a star. Sadly, that isn't the case.

No matter how many amazing writers have worked on Spider-Woman comics, something seemed to be missing. It could be because, despite working for elite teams like S.H.I.E.L.D., S.W.O.R.D., and the Avengers, one thing eluded her. She doesn't have a major nemesis. Sure, Hydra and the Skrulls count, but everyone hates them. Thankfully, writer Dan Watters (Nightwing and Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Shredder) is about to change that.

At the end of Amazing Spider-Man/Venom: Death Spiral, it looked like Carnage would be out of the way for a little while. It was stuck bonding with rodents and insects, and without a human host, it would be aimless. At the end of Amazing Spider-Man: Spider-Versity, it’s revealed that the Carnage Klyntar bonded with Jessica Drew. She does this hoping that she could teach the symbiote a better way.

Since the Klyntar aren't inherently evil, this was possible. Sadly, it may be too far gone. In the first issue of Dan Watters’ Spider-Woman, Jessica is making jokes about murdered people and enjoying brutally hurting goons. It’s clear that the symbiote is affecting her, so she has a conversation over a flame (fire being a Klyntar’s weakness). This is to remind Carnage that she is in charge.

There’s good news for Jessica. She may not have to worry about Carnage for long. His original host, Cletus Kasady, is back and ready to take Carnage from Jessica. When that happens, there will be a struggle to see who the symbiote's next host is. Even if Kasady loses, he's still dangerous. That means, no matter how it ends, Spider-Woman has a new nemesis, and he's perfect for Jessica Drew.

Putting any random villain with Spider-Woman isn’t a good idea. It must be someone who can challenge her on multiple levels. Jessica is smart, crafty, and an amazing detective. Not to mention, she has a bunch of superpowers that make her one of Earth’s most reliable heroes. Using someone like Electro or Wrecker won’t challenge her in a way that draws readers in. However, Cletus Kasady is.

What makes Kasady special is that, as mentioned earlier, he’s a threat without the symbiote. Before becoming Carnage, he was one of the most notorious serial killers ever. Adding the Klyntar made him more creative, and the last thing a serial killer needs is something to feed their imagination. They certainly don’t need an alien suit that heals them and can make weapons with a thought.

It will be interesting to see what direction Dan Watters goes in during Spider-Woman. Considering all the amazing work he’s done, especially over the last couple of years, Marvel Comics fans can relax knowing that, whatever it is, it will be good. It will also likely be very violent.
