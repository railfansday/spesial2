American Airlines Launches 7 New International Routes: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

We are now firmly in the IATA Winter Season, which began on October 25. It will run until Saturday, March 27, 2027, before the summer schedule takes over the next day. During this time, American Airlines has launched or is launching seven new international routes, most of which will begin operations toward the end of the season. These include short and long-haul corridors.

American Airlines' international additions make for interesting analysis on account of their wide range of strategic implications. This is particularly true on the long-haul side, where we will see, among other things, Philadelphia International Airport (PHI) continue to grow as an intercontinental hub thanks to American's booming fleet of Airbus A321XLR narrowbody twinjets. Let's dive into the data using facts and figures from Cirium, an aviation analytics company.

American Airlines' first new international route this winter began on October 29, when it started flying from Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport (PHX) to Oaxaca in Mexico. The A320 was its jet of choice for this route, which it operated just four times between then and November 4. According to the airport, these flights served seasonal demand for the Day of the Dead festival.

More recently, on November 1, American began operations from Miami International Airport (MIA) to Cap-Haïtien International Airport (CAP) in Haiti. These flights are running daily year-round on Boeing 737 MAX 8 narrowbody twinjets, with American operating the first flight with an all-Haitian crew. The carrier's Vice President of International Operations, José Freig, spoke about the new route.

"As we celebrate American’s centennial year, reaching a milestone of 100 destinations in the Caribbean and Latin America highlights both our rich history and our unmatched footprint in the region."

As it happens, that route is not American's only new Latin American connection this winter. On January 15, it will begin flying from Miami to General José Antonio Anzoátegui International Airport (BLA) in Barcelona, Venezuela. These flights will be operated using Embraer E175 regional jets flown by wholly-owned regional subsidiary, Envoy Air under the American Eagle brand, with four departures a week.

Just over two months later, arguably American Airlines' most notable winter addition will come to life when, on March 19, it begins flying from Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD) to Narita Airport (NRT) in Tokyo. These flights will operate all year round on a daily basis using the 787-9 Dreamliner, and they will see American rival United Airlines on this transpacific route.

Recently, these two US legacy carriers have been locked in a heated rivalry in Chicago, as American looks to win market share from United Airlines, the dominant operator at ORD. The Star Alliance founding member already flies daily from ORD to Haneda Airport (HND) with the 787-10, and it also started flying daily to NRT using the 787-8 at the end of October.

10 A321neo aircraft bought from Alaska in American's fleet are forced to offer slightly reduced capacity.

American's additions will go right down to the wire, with the oneworld founding member's final three new international routes starting operations on Saturday, March 27, the last day of the winter season. Two will originate at New York-JFK, with their destinations being Amsterdam and Tel Aviv. These daily flights will respectively use the A321XLR and the 777-200ER.

Elsewhere on the East Coast, the A321XLR will also unlock another new long-haul route out of American's growing Philadelphia hub on the last day of the winter season. Its destination is the Portuguese city of Porto, and the A321XLR will also serve this route daily. Porto's other direct US destinations are Boston (TAP Air Portugal), New York-JFK (Delta Air Lines), and Newark (TAP and United).
