Rangers Daily: Zibanejad’s 2 goals ‘good for him … us;’ Bedard backs embattled coach

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Mika Zibanejad's reaction to a simple question asked by a Calgary reporter postgame Wednesday after the New York Rangers skated to a 3-1 road victory over the Flames spoke volumes about the veteran player and leader.

The reporter asked, "It was nice to get one, but how nice was it to get two your last time here at the [Saddledome]?"

"I'm hoping you're talking about the points we got as a team," Zibanejad said with a bit of a smile.

The question, though, was about Zibanejad's two-goal performance, but the 33-year-old alternate captain spun it to make sure that the emphasis was on an important victory and two points in the standings.

Of course, the two do go hand-in-hand, as they say. The better Zibanejad plays and the more he produces, the better the odds that the Rangers keep up their winning ways. And that victory over the Flames was a clear example because Zibanejad's two goals not only gave the Rangers a 2-0 lead, but were the only goals they scored until Tye Kartye's empty-netter in the final seconds.

"Obviously, it's nice to contribute, but this one feels good to get the two points from the win," he said afterward.

Zibanejad hadn't scored in the seven previous games and sat on three — only one at even strength — through the first 19 games. So, yes, those were two big goals, both on the micro and macro levels.

His first goal, less than a minute into the second period was a combination of brilliant thinking and execution. Off the rush, he flicked the puck toward the Flames goal crease, not so much looking for a Rangers stick nearby, but intentionally to bank the puck in off of surprised goalie Devin Cooley.

Ten minutes later he wired his trademark power-play one-timer from the left circle past Cooley.

Coach Mike Sullivan was quick to point out that Zibanejad's value to New York's success isn't simply tied to the amount of goals he scores.

"I think he's so important to our team," Sullivan explained postgame. "He drives offense in so many ways. He drives his line, he drives offense on the power play. He just impacts the game is so many different ways. So, obviously, when he's scoring goals, it's good for him and it's good for us."
