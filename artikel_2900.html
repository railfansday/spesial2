Nvidia Stock Just Had Its Best Day in Over a Decade. I'd Still Buy It Now.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The AI chip giant's shares climbed around a third in one session. Is it still a bargain?

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 24, 2026 at 12:43AM EST

Nvidia (NVDA +31.48%) said on Monday, Nov. 23, that it had brought back Jensen Huang as president and CEO, effective immediately. Shares surged on the news.

As I write, the stock's up about 34% for the day, at roughly $168. That'd be its largest one-day climb in over two decades, based on Yahoo Finance's daily closing prices. It'd also add about $1 trillion to Nvidia's market value in one session.

But the stock sits around 30% below Oct. 6, when it closed at a record $239.24. And I think Nvidia is still cheap for what it's expected to earn, especially with Huang back and sales rising faster than a year ago.

Huang stepped down on Nov. 11, after what Nvidia's board called personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct. Colette Kress, Nvidia's longtime chief financial officer, became interim CEO, and the board started looking for a permanent replacement.

Six days later, when Nvidia posted fiscal third-quarter results on Nov. 17, board chair Harvey Jones opened the earnings call by ruling out a return.

"The NVIDIA Board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," Jones said.

The selling kept going. Shares lost around 28% between the Nov. 11 close and Friday, Nov. 20, when the stock dropped 14% in one day. This was Nvidia's seventh straight losing week, its longest weekly losing streak since 2008.

Five days after the call, the board reversed itself. Nvidia said its directors voted unanimously to reinstate Huang after a series of meetings with him. The board also said it got input from senior levels of the U.S. government before deciding.

Sure, not everything is settled. A committee of independent directors is still investigating, and Nvidia said it'll keep cooperating with ongoing government investigations and inquiries.

Highlighting how strong demand stayed through all this, Nvidia's revenue surged 103% year over year to $115.8 billion in its fiscal 2027 third quarter, which ended on Oct. 25, 2026. Sales grew 20% from the previous quarter, a pickup from 18% sequential growth in fiscal Q2. What's more, earnings per share on a non-GAAP (adjusted) basis gained 111% year over year to $2.68, so profits more than kept up with sales.

Management sees around $130 billion in revenue for fiscal Q4, a period with an extra week. Kress also said on the call that Vera Rubin, Nvidia's latest computing platform, pulled in almost $20 billion in its first quarter of production shipments. And she lifted Nvidia's outlook for next year, saying the company now expects fiscal 2028 revenue (the year ending in January 2028) to rise around 75%, up from its previous 70% forecast.

In other words, the stock's drop after Huang's resignation was arguably about who would lead Nvidia, not about demand for its chips. With Huang back, much of that worry could fade.

At roughly $168, Nvidia's stock price is about 10 times expected fiscal 2028 earnings, based on analysts' average estimate.

In comparison, shares of Advanced Micro Devices, one of Nvidia's top rivals in AI chips, cost about 26 times the company's expected 2027 earnings. Even after Monday's rally, then, investors are paying less than half as much for a dollar of Nvidia's expected profit.

Nvidia is also buying back lots of its own stock. It returned $28.1 billion to shareholders in repurchases and dividends in fiscal Q3. And Kress said it still plans to spend the $227 billion or so left on its buyback authorization by the end of fiscal 2028 -- over 5% of Nvidia's market value at Monday's price.

Yes, a price-to-earnings ratio this low only holds up if the earnings forecast does. Nvidia sees gross margin of 71% to 72% in fiscal Q4, down from around 75% in fiscal Q3. And the board's investigation might still find something investors don't like. But at around 10 times expected fiscal 2028 earnings, I'd say the stock already prices in a lot of bad news.

In the end, Nvidia gets back the co-founder who ran it for more than three decades, and it gets him back with sales still more than doubling year over year. I'd still buy the stock after Monday's jump, but I'd likely add shares bit by bit over the next few months, given how volatile the stock is. There’s no guarantee it doesn’t go lower again.
