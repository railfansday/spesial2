Sprocket Security Named a Leader in GigaOm Radar for Penetration Testing as a Service

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

One year after debuting as a Challenger and Outperformer, Sprocket earns perfect scores on four criteria as it pairs expert pentesters with a fleet of AI agents.

MADISON, Wis., Nov. 20, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Sprocket Security has been named a Leader in the GigaOm Radar for Penetration Testing as a Service (PTaaS) v5. Sprocket placed as a Leader in the report's Agile Optimizers scorecards and earned perfect marks on test launch efficiency, attack surface integration, rules of engagement controls, and the testing audit trail.

The placement marks a clear step up from 2025, when GigaOm recognized Sprocket as a Challenger and Outperformer. In the year between, Sprocket expanded its capabilities with a fleet of AI agents that work alongside its pentesters, extending how much of the attack surface gets tested and how quickly.

Sprocket Security has been named a Leader in the GigaOm Radar for Penetration Testing as a Service (PTaaS) v5.

This year's report, authored by GigaOm analyst Chris Ray, maps four buyer profiles to four quadrants. It frames five market themes around the questions buyers should ask before signing: who performs the test, how it reaches internal networks, and what evidence an auditor will accept?

"Sprocket Security earns its Leader position by pairing AI testing agents with human validation of every finding, and wrapping both in machine-enforced scope, a customer kill switch and a checksummed audit trail, so its testing keeps pace with a changing attack surface without giving up control," Ray shared.

Sprocket's top scores reflect how the platform runs day to day:

Test launch efficiency (5.0): Teams scope and start testing without weeks of kickoff calls and paperwork.

Attack surface integration (5.0): Testing follows the attack surface as it changes, so new assets get tested as they appear.

Rules of engagement controls (5.0): Customers set what gets tested, when, and how, with guardrails built into the platform.

Testing audit trail (5.0): Every action is logged, giving security teams and auditors a record of what was tested and what was found.

Sprocket was founded because point-in-time pentests couldn't keep up with how companies change. Continuous testing closed part of that gap. Sprocket's AI agents close more of it, running alongside its pentesters on the Sprocket platform:

Apex: an agentic pentester for unauthenticated web applications, mapping each app and testing its own theories about where it might break.

Link: an agentic pentester for authenticated web applications, testing what sits behind the login.

Pinion: an agentic pentester for internal networks, falling the attack paths worth chasing.

Sprocket's pentesters direct the work and validate what the agents find, so customers get more coverage without trading away the judgment of an experienced tester.

"A year ago GigaOm called us a Challenger. Today we are a Leader, and agentic innovation is why. We put a fleet of AI agents to work next to our pentesters for all types of testing, and the results show up in these scores," said Casey Cammilleri, CEO and founder of Sprocket Security. "Buyers are asking who is actually testing, whether it reaches the parts of the network attackers go after, and whether the results will hold up with an auditor. Our answer is experienced pentesters with agents that extend their reach, on a platform that shows its work."

Organizations that work with Sprocket Security get:

Sprocket will keep expanding its AI fleet and testing coverage. Every new agent is held to Sprocket's Safety Framework for Autonomous Offensive Security Agents, which requires agents to stay within scope, stop at proof, log every action, and let humans step in at any point. The aim is to give buyers clear answers to the questions GigaOm says to ask: who is testing, how far the testing reaches, and what evidence it leaves behind.
