Democrats swept the midterm elections in a historic rebuke to Trump

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Republicans lost Senate seats in Texas, Kansas, and South Carolina, states Trump won by double digits in 2024

Democrats won majorities in both the House and the Senate in the midterm elections held Tuesday, taking each chamber away from the Republicans.

Democrats are forecast to capture 250 or more House seats, while Republicans are expected to hold 160 or more, according to CNBC. Democrats appear headed for a Senate majority of at least 54 seats, with Republicans likely to retain 42 or more.

Democrats won Senate seats in Maine, North Carolina, South Carolina, Texas, Ohio, Kansas, and Iowa. Democrats needed to pick up four more seats than they lost to take the majority, and President Donald Trump carried every one of those states except North Carolina by double-digit margins in 2024.

In Texas, Democrat James Talarico defeated Republican Ken Paxton, the state's attorney general, and became the first Democrat elected to the Senate from Texas since 1988. Talarico won even though Elon Musk, the SpaceX and Tesla $TSLA CEO and the richest person in the world, put $224 million behind Paxton's campaign.

In Kansas, Adam Hamilton defeated Sen. Roger Marshall, making him the first Democrat to take a Senate seat in that state since 1932. In Maine, Troy Jackson defeated Sen. Susan Collins. In South Carolina, Annie Andrews defeated Sen. Darline Graham, and in Ohio, Sherrod Brown defeated Sen. Jon Husted. Gov. Roy Cooper won an open Senate race in North Carolina.

Democrats also won governor's races in Georgia, Ohio, Texas and Iowa. In Texas, Gina Hinojosa unseated Republican Gov. Greg Abbott $ABT.

Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer of New York said Democrats had won the Senate and that "relief is on the way." House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries of New York, who is expected to become speaker, said the results were "a red, white and blue moment because America has won tonight."

Exit polls found that voters were unhappy about the economy, according to The Wall Street Journal. In a survey of over 79,000 registered voters, close to two-thirds said they believed Trump's policies had pushed inflation higher.

Trump told The Journal that Republicans' losses could be chalked up in part to the conflict with Iran, though he had no regrets about it. He said he was willing to cooperate with Democrats but would wield his veto if necessary. On Truth Social, Trump wrote: "I wasn't on the Ballot and if I was, we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE!"
