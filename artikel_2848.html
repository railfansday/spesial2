Sixers face young Nets squad with opportunity to extend win streak to three

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

No rest for the weary as the Philadelphia 76ers head to New York City to take on the Brooklyn Nets to close out their third back-to-back of the season.

This is a bit of a prolonged back-to-back with Sunday's game being a 1 p.m. matinee. However, it's still a back-to-back, which means no official injury report information until this afternoon. We will update you when the information becomes available, but pure speculation based on pattern suggests that LeBron James, who had a 15-point, 11-assist double-double yesterday, may be out for today's contest. Joel Embiid, who missed Sunday's game for plantar fasciitis management, may very well be back for this one — which could be key considering the Sixers' precarious backup five situation. Again, we'll let you know when we know for sure.

This is the Sixers' first meeting with Brooklyn this season, so let's talk about the Nets. The team is in a weird spot right now as the youngest team in the NBA this season with an average age of 24.8 years. This translates to nine of their 15 standard roster spots filled by guys younger than 25 and seven standard contract players having a year or less experience in the NBA. Their results so far have been what you might expect from such a young, inexperienced squad, with the Nets coming into Monday with a 3-9 record.

Brooklyn is led by two of the players that actually have significant league experience in Michael Porter Jr. and Julius Randle. Porter leads the way averaging 21.5 points per game and shooting 37.5% from long range on a 9.5 attempt per game clip. Randle is right there with him chipping in 19.9 points a night along with his 5.0 assists — the highest mark in the category on the Nets. The supporting cast features a number of younger guys still trying to find their footing in the NBA like Nolan Traore, Mikel Brown Jr., Egor Demin and Danny Wolf, all averaging double-digit points but none having played in every game this season.

Using some team stats to illustrate the Nets' struggles, they are a bottom-five team in offensive rating (26th, 109.4), field goal percentage (26th, 44.2%) and assists per game (26th, 24.2%). They are currently committing the third-most turnovers in the league at 17.7 per game and the most personal fouls at 26.1 a contest. As a Sixers' writer reading those last numbers, I immediately see two ways the Sixers can take advantage and hopefully make this an easy contest: get Embiid drawing fouls and taking trips to the line as often as possible, and get Tyrese Maxey or VJ Edgecombe out ahead running in transition to cash in on some easy buckets on turnovers.

This is another one of those games that, even on a back-to-back and even with James possibly sidelined for rest, the Sixers should expect to win. That being said, I wrote the same thing about the Sixers' contests with the Bulls. They did end up winning those, but both contests were a bit closer and more competitive than Philadelphia would probably like, especially on a night like Monday when bodies are likely a bit tired.

This is a golden opportunity to keep the good times rolling for Philly and extend their win streak to three, but we all know there's never guarantees in the NBA. Execution — as it always is — is still key.

The Sixers and Nets tip off at 7:30 p.m. ET.
