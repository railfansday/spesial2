Florida Vibe draw packed crowd ahead of Pensacola debut

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

An unexpected change in location turned into a welcome one.

The Florida Vibe were set to be introduced on their future home field at Blue Wahoos Stadium. But with rain in the forecast for the 5 p.m. event, they moved it to the Hancock Whitney Club, which sits mostly covered above the concourse on the left side of the stadium.

Fans packed the exclusive area, filling every seat and overflowing into every inch of deck. Quite the introduction for the latest professional sports team to move to Pensacola. The Vibe are members of the Professional Softball League, and will start playing at Blue Wahoos Stadium in June.

"Just seeing the people here just warms my heart to know that this is going to be a dedicated community to this franchise," PSL founder Ryan Moore said. "And I couldn't be more happy for the players and the fans."

Moore, Vibe owner Quint Studer and head coach Mike Scott led the crowd in "Let's Go Vibe!" chants. The six Vibe players were originally supposed to get a high-five tunnel onto the field, but instead got a tunnel through the pavilion. Hundreds of children rushed to the field afterwards to play catch. Newly appointed league commissioner Michele Smith, a two-time Olympic gold medalist and ESPN's lead softball analysts, stayed to chat and take pictures with fans too.

Exactly what everyone envisioned when they wanted to bring the team to Pensacola. The Vibe, who were an independent professional softball team, from 2021-25, were previously located in Bradenton.

"My heart is just melting to see the type of response that we're getting to be able to come down here and play," Scott said. "We're just really excited and blessed for that."

Studer initially became interested in the game through the Kansas City Monarchs, the independent baseball team he owns. The team would host a softball team, the KC Diamonds, at their stadium. He started thinking about what it would mean for young girls to go to Blue Wahoos Stadium and see women out there to serve as role models. Dream players would sign every single autograph after a game, then work to promote the team on their respective social media platforms.

So he started to looking to see how he could bring a team to Pensacola. The PSL was looking to expand and Moore connected with Studer and they decided to move the Vibe north. Scott, had also recently moved to Pensacola to be close to his new grandson. All the pieces for professional softball in Pensacola had fallen into place.

"I got turned on by professional softball, watching what it could do for young girls, what it can do for the community and just what a difference they can make," Studer told the crowd. "Not only are we committed to professional softball, we're committed to community softball."

The Vibe are part of the latest effort to find a foothold for professional softball in the U.S. The PSL and the Athletes Unlimited Softball League are the two main professional softball leagues, and both are recent developments. The AUSL had its first season in 2025, with former MIami Marlins general manager Kim Ng as commissioner and Major League Baseball investing in the league to try to make it sustainable.

The PSL finished its first season earlier this year and currently has teams in Knoxville, St. Louis, Atlanta, Kansas City and Birmingham. All teams will play 40 games, including 20 home games.

The Vibe will play in Pensacola when the Blue Wahoos are on the road and the stadium will be adapted for softball play through privately funded renovations.

Right now the league is a bit more regional, with teams in the south and midwest. But Smith thinks this is only the beginning. She mentioned that the American and National League both started as separate baseball leagues before combining under MLB in 1903 before growing into America's past time. Right now, softball has too much talent to disperse to all the professional teams across both leagues.

That means room to expand if the investment and community support are there.

"90 roster spots in one league is not enough for the amount of talent we have," Smith said. "So the PSL is filling the opportunity for the amount of talent we have in this sport for these women to continue to play at a professional level and then for these young girls to be able to dream about it. This is just the beginning. Six teams in this league is just the beginning. I think we really have the ability to grow this league."
