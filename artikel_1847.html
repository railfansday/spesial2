SFA could open ‘huge can of worms’ with ‘two-game’ Hyun-jun Yang ban after Celtic scenes – Keith Wyness

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Celtic winger Hyun-jun Yang could potentially face a “two-game” ban after he appeared to dive to win a free-kick during last weekend’s 4-1 win over St Mirren.

That is according to former Aberdeen chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the South Korean could potentially face retrospective punishment.

Yang went down midway through the St Mirren half in the 32nd minute, with referee Matthew MacDermid awarding a free-kick, which Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain eventually scored the opening goal from.

However, Wyness believes that the SFA must set a new precedent of banning players for dives outside of penalty incidents if they are to punish Yang.

Fans on social media have been in uproar over the incident, while former Premier League referee Dermot Gallagher has pointed out that VAR couldn’t intervene as it wasn’t a penalty incident.

Yang’s incident has prompted plenty of debate in recent days, but Wyness is sceptical as to whether the 24-year-old will face any punishment.

Aberdeen’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Pittodrie between 2000 and 2004 and now runs a consultancy business advising elite clubs – believes that the SFA would be opening a “huge can of worms” if they handed a ban to Yang.

The Scottish businessman pointed out that it would then set a precedent for dives to be reviewed all over the pitch, not just in the penalty area.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider’s Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained that it is not a straightforward decision as to whether Yang should face punishment.

“Well, this is probably a little bit more interesting in terms of the situation here because there have been retrospective actions on penalties, but this was for a free kick,” he said.

“He could see up to a two-game ban. But what that would mean is opening up a whole new situation of simulation for free kicks, which could be all over the pitch as opposed to penalty kicks.

“Now that's a big difference. And so we'll see how they actually handle that one. It may not be an area they want to go into in terms of opening up simulation for free kicks everywhere because that does open a huge can of worms.

“Penalty kicks I understand but I think this may be just too big a bridge for them to travel on this one.”

Wyness recently told Football Insider that Celtic fans will be unhappy to see the club exploit their “glorious past” with their latest release of watches ahead of the Christmas period.

The Bhoys are selling special edition Lisbon Lions watches at £299, with only 1,967 set to be sold, the year of their Champions League triumph.

However, Wyness only expects the club to make around “£100,000” in profit from the new scheme.

He also feels that Celtic may have handed their fanbase another stick to beat them with, as the discontent at Parkhead continues.
