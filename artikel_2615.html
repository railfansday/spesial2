The NY Giants have multiple paths to the playoffs

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The New York Giants had the postseason clearly in their sights in the dysfunctional NFC East…until they let victory slip through their hands against the Washington Commanders last Thursday evening. Now they have made life more difficult for themselves. If you experienced the Tom Coughlin era, you'll probably remember that this is nothing new. Let's take a little trip down memory lane:

2007: In Week 14 the Giants beat the Eagles at The Linc 16-13 to get to 9-4 and put the playoffs clearly in their sights. The the next week they laid an egg at Giants Stadium, losing to Washington 22-10. Not until Ahmad Bradhsaw's 88-yard TD run as the snow started to fall late in the game in Buffalo the following week did the Giants clinch a playoff spot.

2011: In Week 14 the Giants won in Dallas 37-34 when Jason Pierre-Paul blocked the Cowboys' last second field goal attempt to get to 7-6. Next week, at home against Washington, the Giants were embarrassed by a 4-9 Washington team at home, 23-10. Not until Victor Cruz's 99-yard TD reception to spark a win against the Jets and a final week 31-14 win over the Cowboys for the division title did the Giants clinch a playoff spot.

It just seems to be in the Giants' DNA to play poorly against Washington. Now we're three years and running that the Giants haven't beaten them (coinciding with the arrival of Jayden Daniels). Anyway, that's in the rear-view mirror, and the question is whether the Giants can bounce back as they did in 2007 and 2011 to get to the playoffs anyway. No one sees the Giants as a Super Bowl contender (more on that in a minute), but as 2007 and 2011 showed, you never know. Unlike those seasons, the 2026 Giants are in the driver's seat in the NFC East even after their loss. Unfortunately, the wins have all been against losing teams and they now face a stretch of some of the best teams in the NFL. What are their chances of reaching the post-season, and which games have the most leverage on their chances?

Three losses in the next four weeks, making it four of five defeats for the Giants, only reduces the playoff odds to about even. Why? It's because the Cowboys have their own gauntlet to run. After presumable gimmes against the Titans and Eagles in the coming two weeks, they face the Seahawks, Rams, and Jaguars after that, before playing the Giants and Commanders in their final two games.

And that's the key: Even if the Giants stink up the joint in the coming month, the odds are that they still face the Cowboys in Week 17 with the division title on the line.

So what is the Giants' most important game? Actually, it's coming up right after Jacksonville. The Giants absolutely must beat the Indianapolis Colts, who are in disarray after injuries, trading several of their best players, and Daniel Jones' carriage turning into a pumpkin in his second season as a Colt. All in all, I would say that the second scenario I presented above (lose to JAX, SF, SEA, beat IND) is the most realistic outcome for the coming month. That means that there are decent odds that it's a coin flip by late December whether the Giants will make the playoffs, and it will all come down to that last month. It could have been so much better if they'd beaten Washington even once, never mind twice as could have happened.

Still, things could be worse. You could be a fan of a team that only won its first game of the season on Monday night, or a fan of the team they beat that jettisoned its former head coach and may watch him in the playoffs while they stay home. (By the way, if the Los Angeles Chargers run the table they have a 52% chance to sneak into the playoffs.) Talk is cheap, though. Beating Jacksonville would be worth more.
