Forget True Detective, Netflix's 2-Part Thriller Masterpiece Is The Most Realistic Detective Series

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

True Detective has earned its place among the great success stories of modern crime thriller TV shows. The Golden Globe and multi-Emmy winning HBO series delivers unforgettable performances, deeply unsettling mysteries, and a sense that every breakthrough comes at a cost across four standalone anthology seasons. While its detectives might have a talent for making their own lives worse in melodrama-creating ways, the actual police work gives even the show’s wildest narratives' substance. True Detective is beloved for being so stylistic, but a huge part of the appeal also comes from making the investigative journey believable.

It’s an especially noteworthy achievement for a show that can make a crime scene feel like the entrance to a nightmare. However, beneath True Detective’s surreal imagery are investigations defined by missed opportunities, unreliable witnesses, and persons of interest who would rather keep uncomfortable truths buried. In other words, the relatively mundane obstacles of real-life police work wrapped into a cinematically presented package. However, Netflix’s Mindhunter manages to push that combination further, and gets better results.

Debuting on Netflix in 2017 and sadly only lasting for two seasons, Mindhunter follows FBI agents Holden Ford (Jonathan Groff) and Bill Tench (Holt McCallany) as they interview imprisoned murderers during the early days of criminal profiling in the 1970s and 80s. Their work means sitting across from real-life serial killers, with Ford and Tench’s findings being grounded in real forensic psychology. True Detective deserves its reputation for turning believable investigations into gripping entertainment, but Mindhunter beats it at its own game.

True Detective may make its investigations grounded, but Mindhunter manages to make even the most academic, office-based aspects of criminal investigation impossible not to watch. Interviews require preparation, research needs funding, and promising theories are intensely scrutinized, with all of it feeling just as gripping as a police chase.

The source material for Mindhunter definitely helps with this. The Netflix series draws on John Douglas and Mark Olshaker’s nonfiction book of the same name, with its central investigators inspired by real profiling pioneers. This foundation shines through in the show's commitment to detail, but it’s the presentation that’s the true masterstroke. Such heavy material could be borderline-dull without some action to break up the interview scenes and academic theorizing. Mindhunter somehow doesn’t need it.

This becomes especially apparent when comparing the narrative structure of Mindhunter to True Detective. For all its style, True Detective still follows a somewhat familiar path of connecting a drip-feed of revelations to eventually solve the central mystery. Mindhunter creates suspense from the difficulty of unearthing those revelations in the first place. The killers being interviewed lie, boast, manipulate, and contradict themselves, meaning no knowledge gained feels truly dependable. This embracing of uncertainty not only makes the realism so convincing, but an aspect that drives the plot rather than slows it down. Profiling offers possibilities, not supernatural insight. The series repeatedly revels in the huge difference between recognizing a pattern and proving who committed a crime.

What’s more, just like True Detective, Mindhunter never feels visually flat or ordinary. The precise framing of every shot and atmosphere created by cold and oppressive offices and shadowy interview rooms is deliberate. Se7en and Zodiac's David Fincher serves as an executive producer and directs several episodes, and his influence can be felt in Mindhunter having the same kind of cinematic polish that defines HBO’s anthology series. It may be a much more cerebral and grounded crime thriller than True Detective, but it’s just as difficult not to binge.

For all Mindhunter’s strengths, True Detective has one clear advantage: its anthology format. Each season delivers a largely self-contained investigation. Although connections can carry across installments, each detective's journeys are wrapped up across the True Detective season they’re the focus on. That means that the HBO series can keep reinventing itself, and its eventual conclusion is all-but-guaranteed not to leave any cliffhangers.

A long-form multi-season narrative, Mindhunter’s legacy never had that kind of safety net. Its two seasons are an ongoing account of the investigators’ careers and personal deterioration. When Mindhunter was effectively canceled when it was put on indefinite hold by Netflix in 2020 it didn’t just leave fans with a cliffhanger ending, it did so at the point when long-term arcs like the hunt for the BTK killer (Sonny Valicenti) were just getting started.

While it’s one of the best crime TV shows of the last few decades, even compared to True Detective, Mindhunter’s legacy is unfortunately now defined by its premature cancellation at a point when its plot was reaching full stride. At present, there are no plans to bring Mindhunter back, although demand has remained. However, it was hinted by star Holt McCallany that Ford and Tench could return in a movie trilogy (via Variety). Hopefully this project materializes, as Mindhunter deserves much more than being the poster child for Netflix series' that got the axe much too early.
