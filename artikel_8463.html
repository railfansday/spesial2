I would appreciate some advice on my election decision. Active federal employee; self & family.

Here is my question: would we be crazy to pick the Aetna HDHP over SAMBA? I hate to give up contributing to the HSA (currently through MHBP Consumer, which of course is no longer an option). We've also been happy with Aetna claims processing.

Virtually all of the Aetna costs are higher, but is there any rational argument that the difference is offset by: 1) the tax benefits of the HSA (assume 24% tax bracket), and 2) the dental coverage Aetna but not SAMBA offers (basically cleanings and xrays) ?
The tax benefit of the HSA is the difference between the plan contribution and the HSA limit, multiplied by your tax rate. This is money that you get that you would not have if you contributed the same after-tax amount to the Roth TSP or a Roth IRA, or the value of the tax exemption that you would lose if you contributed the same dollar amount to the traditional TSP.
All our docs appear to be in-network for both. I do take one speciality medication.

Here's what my comparison numbers look like (I focused on things I know will affect us, plus max OOP which I hope won't):

Annual Premium+Deductible-HSA contrib; Copays; Speciality Drugs; MRIs; Max Out of Pocket


Aetna HDHP $10,856.50; 15%; 50% up to $350; 15%; $15,000

SAMBA $8,197.16; $20/30; 35% up to $240; 20%; $12,000
If you use the full deductible with the HDHP, you will probably go beyond the deductible with the conventional plan, so that is an additional cost for SAMBA. And the tax benefit is close to covering the gap, especially if you get a further tax savings on state tax (not in NJ or CA),

But this doesn't give a clear advantage for one plan or the other; you will have to work out the estimated cost for the expenses you are likely to have. It usually works out that the HDHP comes out ahead of the conventional plan from the same insurer, which is likely to have similar benefits: Aetna HDHP costs less than Aetna's regular HMO plan, and GEHA HDHP costs less than GEHA Standard/Elevate. It's harder to compare apples and oranges.
