Packers Snap Counts: Micah Parsons played less than 40 percent of snaps

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Micah Parsons made his 2026 debut tonight, and he was certainly on a pitch count. Let's take a look at every player who saw action (or didn't) in the Green Bay Packers' 26-13 loss to the Los Angeles Rams tonight.

As a reminder, the scratches today were running back Josh Jacobs (finger), linebacker Ziare Franklin (shin), offensive lineman Donovan Jennings, offensive lineman Jager Burton and offensive lineman Mekhi Becton (all three were healthy scratches to get down to a 48-man game-day roster).

The Packers' top two backs in this game were Kaleb Johnson and Chris Brooks, after there was a three-way split last week. Johnson was given a more all-around back role, as he was targeted twice and ran the ball eight times. Last week, Johnson was the runner, Brooks was the blocking back and MarShawn Lloyd was the route-runner.

When Josh Jacobs returns to the lineup, assume that Lloyd is going to get knocked down to being a healthy scratch. The Packers haven't ever had more than three backs up on the 48-man game-day roster, even when they held five players at the position on the 53-man active roster.

Receiver Christian Watson dropped out of the game with a concussion, which is why Savion Williams jumped him in the pecking order this week. Williams actually started the game as the slot receiver, a role that Skyy Moore was playing until Moore's ankle got injured earlier in the year. In his two weeks back from injury, Moore has only played one offensive snap.

Tucker Kraft played every single snap of the game, and the Packers only played 1.3 tight ends per play, a big difference from their game plan against the Minnesota Vikings last week. Last week, the team heavily leaned into multiple tight end looks, while this week featured a lot more three-receiver sets, even when Christian Watson went down.

The starting offensive line in this game was Jordan Morgan, Laken Tomlinson, Sean Rhyan, Kevin Zeitler and Anthony Belton (left to right), a lineup that had never played together before. Zeitler dropped out of action in the middle of the game, though, which pushed Aaron Banks, who had been benched in recent weeks, into a guard spot.

Earlier in the season, rookie third-round pick Chris McClellan was a reserve defensive lineman, but because of injuries, the nose tackle played the most snaps of anyone in the unit against the Rams. Devonte Wyatt, who appeared to be on a pitch count in the first half (he hasn't looked right coming off his ankle injury this year), also dropped out of the game due to injury. It will be interesting to see if the team tries to get Warren Brinson, who is currently on the injured reserve, up to the active roster soon.

Micah Parsons' return to action featured him playing 39 percent of the defensive snaps in this game, about half of his playing time rate in 2025. For the most part, the snaps for Parsons, Barryn Sorrell (who got injured in this game, Brenton Cox Jr. and Dani Dennis-Sutton split snaps about evenly behind Lukas Van Ness.

With Zaire Franklin still out, Isaiah McDuffie played the Mike spot and Ty'Ron Hopper was the Will. When Franklin comes back, which should be soon, he should be playing the Mike. When Franklin was last healthy, McDuffie and Hopper split the Will position.

The only notable nugget here is that Kamal Hadden played one snap in the slot when Javon Bullard was getting looked at for an injury. Otherwise, Brandon Cisse played right cornerback and Kamal Hadden played left cornerback, as usual.

Because the Rams played such heavy personnel on offense, it forced the Packers to play their 3-4 base defense often. The result of that was Javon Bullard, the team's slot defender, only playing 31 percent of the defensive snaps today. "Nickel is the new base" is a fun catchphrase for NFL analysis right now, but it all depends on what the opposing offense puts on the field. Unfortunately, the Packers didn't get many three-receiver looks today, in part because of how the game got out of hand for them in the second half. That left Bullard on the bench for more than two-thirds of the plays tonight.
