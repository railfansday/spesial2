Edgewood's Vencills earn county POY, COY volleyball honors

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

There are various ways for a parent and child to connect.

For Kyra and Faith Vencill, that activity is volleyball.

Kyra is a long-time local coach who has been involved in helping youth programs and at SPIRE Academy.

Faith started playing volleyball at a young age.

For the last three seasons, the Vencills have joined forces at Edgewood High School.

The result has been three straight Chagrin Valley Conference Lake Division titles, two Division IV district final appearances and one trip to the D-IV regional tournament.

For a third straight volleyball season, Faith was voted the Ashtabula County Player of the Year, and Kyra the county Coach of the Year, as selected by the county coaches.

"I feel honored to be recognized, but I couldn't have done it without my team," Faith Vencill said. "The team is the reason where I am, and I loved playing with them the last four years."

She leaves Edgewood as one of the most decorated volleyball players not only in school history, but in the area.

This season, Faith recorded 525 kills, 62 aces, 76 blocks, 332 digs and 267 receptions in earning first-team All-Ohio honors for a second straight season, after capturing second-team all-state accolades as a sophomore.

"Coaching Faith was special," Kyra Vencill said. "She had such a huge range, huge presence and an unbelievable love for this game. She's been playing since she was 3, and her love for this sport rooted early in her life."

Faith Vencill's career numbers for the Warriors consisted of 1,735 kills, 243 aces, 76 blocks, 1,386 digs and 1,376 serve receptions.

"She made coaching easy because she had the drive to keep getting better, wanting to work in the next skill, and constantly improving," coach Vencill said. "I'm so proud of all of her accomplishments both as her coach and mother."

Faith Vencill said being coached by Kyra was rewarding and challenging.

"I wouldn't be where I am today without her," Faith said. "She motivates me and is always pushing me to be better. She has always been my biggest cheerleader, but she also critiques the things I need to fix, or pushing the next skill to make me better."

Faith Vencill led the Edgewood attack, but she certainly received helped over the years.

In the 2026 season, Summer Matthews and Haylee Rhodes were among her teammates to contribute heavily to the Warriors success.

Edgewood finished the season 20-3 overall, 12-0 in the conference.

"It was a great year, going undefeated in CVC and having another shot in districts a third year in a row," coach Vencill said. "I'm so proud of the girls and all the hard work they put in.

"Our area had competitive teams and our county girls continue to get better year after year. I am so happy with the level of play Edgewood had and the chemistry they shared."

Over the past three seasons, the Warriors compiled a 59-14 overall, 30-2 conference mark, with a regional appearance in the 2024 campaign.

"Our focus on taking care of the ball one point at a time, being aggressive on offense, controlling ball on serve receive, and being aggressive serving," coach Vencill said of the keys in the last three seasons. "We needed to have a faster tempo in offense, using our key hitters in playoffs, and we really focused on placing the ball offensively."

After a season-ending loss to Firelands in a district final match, Faith Vencill has moved on to basketball this winter and softball in the spring for softball.

But she will continue playing volleyball in college, choosing to attend St. Francis Fort Wayne.

"Coach [Scott] Haddix has been watching me for some time and he is so excited to have me," Faith Vencill said. "He allowed me to join the team over summer to play, and I got to meet the team and play with them."

St. Francis is a Division I National Association of Intercollegiate Athletics (NAIA) college, primarily competing in the Crossroads League.

"I couldn't be more happy for her to have found her next home," Kyra Vencill said. "The coach has been so welcoming, as have the girls, and she wanted a Christian-based program. We loved the campus, and are excited for her in this next venture."

Faith is currently undecided on an area of study.

Meanwhile, Kyra Vencill has decided to leave as Warriors head coach.

"I did retire this year, so I can concentrate more on being a mom and watching my kids play in college," she said. "Faith will be playing in college and we wanted more time to travel to watch [son] Zack [in basketball at Wright State Lake], [son] Noah [in volleyball at Wittenberg] and Faith play in college and travel a bit more."

Kyra Vencill, however, isn't finished teaching volleyball quite yet.

"I will still be around to help out, especially in summer with their conditioning and year preparations," she said.

Vencill will also remember the moments of coaching Faith and her teammates over the last three seasons.

"Coaching the team at Edgewood has truly been a blessing," she said. "I have had great kids who are vested in their school and want to put the time in the gym. The girls want to be pushed, and each of them embraced roles given and wanted to be better.

"Every girl on the team knew their role and worked hard to improve. They started speed and agility early in the summer with great turn out in participation, which showed their dedication early. They all had the drive and focus to get at it this year early on."
