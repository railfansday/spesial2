Stephen Sweeney Fixes Putt With Two Key Setup Tweaks

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Setup fundamentals often get overlooked by players chasing distance or power, yet they form the bedrock of consistency on the greens. A recent report from Yahoo Sports highlights how PGA Tour putting coach Stephen Sweeney corrected a struggling golfer's stroke using just two small adjustments. The lesson proved that a reliable setup allows a player to make fewer compensations during the actual stroke. When the eyes and hips align correctly, the putter face stays square, turning a miss into a make.

The golfer in the story arrived at the lesson expecting work on the stroke mechanics. Instead, Sweeney identified a fundamental flaw in the stance. "Where your eyes are right now, I tend to see if players' eyes are inside the ball line; they tend to look out to the right," Sweeney said. This misalignment caused the putter to move slightly on the inside on the backswing, even though it felt square to the player. The result was a subconscious aim to the right of the hole and a stroke path that moved from in to out.

Sweeney prescribed a "tabletop" setup to fix the eye position, similar to the stance used by Michelle Wie West during her prime. The coach instructed the player to hinge at the waist more aggressively. "As a general rule of thumb, I like to see a two putter head-(width) from the ball to the toe line," Sweeney said. The player then bends forward at the hips until the eyes sit directly over the golf ball. This simple shift in posture forces the putter to start back on a true path. "As the putter goes back, the putter is moving a little on the inside — but pretty square to where you aimed it," Sweeney noted. These changes eliminated the need for the player to manipulate the clubface during the stroke, leading to a more consistent roll.

Most amateur golfers spend hours practicing their putting stroke, trying to fix the path or the face angle while standing up too tall. The report from Yahoo Sports illustrates that the stroke itself was not the problem. The issue lay in the setup. When the eyes are inside the ball, the brain forces the hands to manipulate the club to get it back on line. This creates a shaky stroke that lacks repeatability. By moving the eyes over the ball, the body naturally aligns the putter face. This approach removes the variable of hand manipulation, allowing the pendulum motion to work as intended.

Coaches like Sweeney often find that players who struggle on short putts have a setup that forces them to aim right to compensate for an inside-out path. Correcting the eye position fixes the aim and the path simultaneously. The "tabletop" feel creates a stable platform that supports the stroke. This method works for players of all skill levels, from beginners to professionals. The margin for error on the greens is so slim that even a small setup error can lead to a miss. Fixing the foundation allows the player to trust the stroke without overthinking the mechanics.

Putting is the only part of the game where power is rarely the goal. Consistency and touch drive the score. The adjustments detailed in the Yahoo Sports report show how a simple change in posture can yield immediate results. Instead, the player sets up correctly and lets the stroke happen. This shift in mindset is crucial for breaking through putting slumps. The data supports this: when the eyes are over the ball, the stroke becomes more repeatable. The putter stays on the intended line, and the ball rolls true.

Without a proper setup, no amount of stroke practice will fix the underlying alignment issues. The two adjustments—moving the eyes over the ball and adopting a tabletop stance—provide a clear path to improvement. Players who adopt these changes often find their putting confidence returns quickly.
