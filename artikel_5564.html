Longbridge profit drop weighs on Ellington’s Q3 earnings

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Ellington Financial reported Q3 2026 net income of $40.4M, or $0.31 per share, down from Q2. Results were pressured by weaker Longbridge reverse mortgage earnings, while the investment portfolio produced $61.1M and net interest margin rose to 3.55%.  AI Summary

Ellington Financial reported net income of $40.4 million in the third quarter of 2026, down from $54.4 million in the second quarter, as lower earnings from its reverse mortgage subsidiary Longbridge Financial, weighed on results.

The Old Greenwich, Connecticut-based mortgage real estate investment trust reported earnings of 31 cents per common share for the quarter ending Sept. 30, compared with 43 cents per share in the second quarter.

Adjusted distributable earnings, a non-GAAP measure, totaled $58 million, or 45 cents per share, down from $74.6 million, or 59 cents per share, in the second quarter. The company said adjusted distributable earnings exceeded its quarterly dividends. Book value per common share was $13.53 as of Sept. 30, compared with $13.61 at the end of June.

Despite the lower earnings performance, Ellington president and CEO Laurence Penn said the company delivered strong results in the face of rising interest rates and widening yield spreads late in the quarter.

“We generated GAAP net income of $0.31 per share and an annualized economic return of 9.4%, preserved book value per share, and produced adjusted distributable earnings in excess of our dividends,” Penn said in a statement.

“Looking ahead, our diversified business model and proprietary sourcing channels, together with our flexible balance sheet and disciplined risk management, should position us well to capitalize on the attractive investment opportunities that we see developing in the wake of the recent spikes in interest rates and volatility.”

The company investment portfolio segment generated $61.1 million in net income attributable to common stockholders, down from $74.2 million in the second quarter.

Ellington said its investment portfolio benefited from retained tranches of non-QM and agency-eligible securitizations, commercial mortgage bridge loans and investments in loan originators, particularly LendSure Mortgage Corp. Strong credit performance across its loan businesses also contributed to results, with 90-day delinquency rates declining sequentially.

The company’s adjusted long investment portfolio increased 4.5% sequentially to $4.7 billion at the end of September. Growth was driven by purchases of residential transition loans, closed-end second lien loans, agency-eligible residential mortgages, prime jumbo loans and commercial mortgage bridge loans.

The portfolio’s net interest margin increased to 3.55%, up from 3.36% in the second quarter, as higher asset yields partially offset increased funding costs.

Longbridge reported $12.5 million in net income attributable to common stockholders, down from $30.2 million in the previous quarter. Adjusted distributable earnings fell to $8.6 million from $28.9 million.

The decline primarily reflected narrower origination margins and lower gains from proprietary reverse mortgage securitizations, according to the company.

During the company’s earnings call, Penn said that interest rates kept Longbridge’s margins compressed. “Although Longbridge’s contribution to earnings declined from the prior quarter, that prior quarter was extraordinarily strong, with two proprietary reverse securitizations completed,” he added.

Longbridge originated $676.3 million in loans during the quarter, up 36% year over year and 15% from the second quarter. Originations included $258.5 million in Home Equity Conversion Mortgages (HECMs) and $417.8 million in proprietary reverse mortgages.

The company’s net Longbridge portfolio increased 59% sequentially to $1 billion as of Sept. 30. Much of that increase was reversed following the October settlement of a proprietary reverse mortgage securitization that was priced in September.

“Despite higher rates, September was Longbridge’s strongest month ever for proprietary reverse submissions,” JR Herlihy, Ellington’s chief financial officer, said during the call.

Longbridge also reported a quarterly record for its share of the HECM Mortgage-Backed Securities (HMBS) market, reaching 31% and ranking as the market’s second-largest issuer, according to Bloomberg data cited in Ellington’s report.

During the quarter, Ellington completed its acquisition of a controlling interest in Statebridge Company, a diversified loan servicer. Statebridge serviced approximately 14,700 loans with a combined unpaid principal balance of $1.9 billion as of Sept. 30 — nearly all for third-party clients.

Penn said the acquisition is expected to help Ellington apply its data and workout expertise to delinquent loans and real estate-owned properties, with the goal of improving loss mitigation and recoveries.

Ellington also issued $150 million in additional 7.375% senior unsecured notes due in 2030, bringing the total outstanding in the series to $550 million. The company said strong institutional demand helped it secure a yield spread approximately 70 basis points tighter than its previous issuance last year.

The company had $269.5 million in cash and cash equivalents, along with $1.92 billion in total unencumbered assets, at the end of the third quarter.

Ellington repurchased 329,045 common shares at an average price of $11.44 per share after the quarter ended. During the third quarter, it issued approximately 2.5 million shares through its at-the-market program at an average price of $13.61 per share.
