A badly timed shock - the story of Matos' Swans exit

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Vitor Matos attempted to ingratiate himself with Swansea City fans by pointing out in a couple of press conferences how many days remained until the south Wales derby.

There is some irony, therefore, that confirmation of his exit has come a little over a week before Swansea's first meeting with Cardiff City in two seasons.

The timing of Matos' departure is far from ideal, and not just because the fixture which matters most to Swansea supporters is just around the corner.

The loss of Matos has come as a shock to a club sitting sixth in the Championship, while it has left many fans feeling the pain of separation.

With Matos gone, the Swans are unexpectedly left trying to figure out where to turn next.

Managers have come and gone with some regularity at Swansea in recent times, with the Welsh club now looking for an eighth permanent boss since they were relegated from the Premier League eight years ago.

Even during the glorious spell when Swansea went from the basement division in 2004-05 to seven seasons in the top flight between 2011 and 2018, there were numerous changes in the manager's office.

Yet few, if any, of the various managerial departures could be regarded as more surprising than Matos' swift exit.

A little over a week ago, Swansea chief executive Tom Gorringe spoke to the media at length and was fairly emphatic as he brushed off questions about the prospect of Matos being tempted away any time soon.

Matos, meanwhile, had been due to speak to BBC Sport Wales to assess his first year as Swansea boss on Monday of this week.

Vitor Matos' Swansea had 23 points from 10 league games this season - and were two points clear at the top of the table - after victory at Millwall just over a month ago [Getty Images]

The interview was arranged at the tail-end of last week. On Sunday evening, however, Matos told Swansea's media staff that it needed to be postponed.

Even then, there was no suspicion that the chat was being scrapped because he was not going to make it to the 12-month anniversary, which was due to come on 24 November.

By Monday evening, however, word began to get out that Strasbourg were keen on Matos – and that he wanted to go.

The 38-year-old spoke to players and staff about his decision at the training ground on Tuesday, with the suggestion being that he was emotional at times as he attempted to explain his reasons for walking away.

The loss of Matos was especially unexpected because everything seemed to be going along nicely at Swansea.

Usually there are signs of a managerial change on the horizon, either because a team are struggling or because things are going so well that rival clubs are circling.

Matos' exit falls somewhere in the latter category, but there is still surprise that he has gone at this particular point.

Yes Swansea endured a first difficult run of the season just before the international break, with four defeats in five games.

But only one of those losses came as the result of an alarming performance – at Queens Park Rangers – and Swansea might easily have taken points from their losses against West Ham United, Portsmouth and Birmingham City.

Had that happened, they might well be top of the league now – as they have been during two spells this season – rather than three points off the summit.

Would Matos have walked away had Ross Stewart scored late on at Fratton Park, or if Marko Stamenic had avoided a match-turning red card in what proved to be his final game in charge at Birmingham?

Did he not back himself to revive the sparkling form which earned Swansea seven wins in their first 10 league games of the season?

There was no sign of an imminent departure as Vitor Matos performed a chin-up gesture following Swansea's defeat at Birmingham on 7 November [Huw Evans Picture Agency]

They are questions which will never be answered.

What we can say for certain is that Swansea remain firmly in the mix to achieve something significant this season, and yet Matos - who was given a new deal running until 2030 as recently as July - decided he wanted out.

He has opted instead to take charge of a team who lie 13th in the French top flight, albeit one who expect better and who - crucially, perhaps - are a sister club of Chelsea, with all their financial might.

For Swansea's owners, whose Premier League ambition seems pretty clear, Matos' decision must be somewhat galling, particularly given the support he has been given since they took something of a punt on him last November.

The messy manner of his move, which was announced by Strasbourg long before it was confirmed by Swansea, has quite clearly not gone down well either.

Many of the managers who have opted to leave Swansea in recent memory – the likes of Roberto Martinez, Graham Potter, Steve Cooper and Russell Martin – could claim they were not given all the tools they wanted during their respective stints in Wales.

They could argue that they had to move on because Swansea's ambition did not match theirs, or that they would be given greater backing elsewhere.

But it would be hard for Matos to make that case given that Swansea have spent significantly in recent transfer windows, and that their squad – as the long-serving Jay Fulton suggested earlier this season – looks as deep now as it has any point since the end of the Premier League era.

Swansea's decision-makers are now left trying to figure out who might be able to maintain the momentum built up by Matos.

In the short term, Ashley Williams will hold the fort alongside three other long-serving former players, Joe Allen, Leon Britton and Kristian O'Leary.

The temporary coaching team will go straight in at the deep end, with fifth-placed West Bromwich Albion to come this Saturday before a trip to Middlesbrough, who are second, next Tuesday.

The south Wales derby follows at the end of a brutal Championship week.

Might a new permanent boss be in charge for Cardiff? That depends on how long Swansea need to identify their targets – and to get an agreement over the line.

As ever, Swansea's fans will expect a new boss who is committed to playing possession football.

It would also make sense, given the squad Matos built, to find someone who is as committed to a high-pressing game as the Portuguese was.

Who will that manager be? A string of names have already been linked, and Swansea will not be short of applicants for what is an attractive job.

Jamestown Analytics is likely to be involved in the selection process, given that Swansea's deal with the data specialists covers coaches as well as players.

For the moment, forecasting which candidate will emerge from the crowd is difficult.

The only certainty is that Matos' exit has left Swansea's hierarchy with a big decision to make.
