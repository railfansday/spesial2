Former Steelers coach Mike Tomlin calls Aaron Rodgers drama a 'dumpster fire'

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

It may be Thanksgiving. It may be a day removed from Aaron Rodgers' stunning divorce from the Pittsburgh Steelers, who will host the Denver Broncos for the league's now-annual Black Friday game on Nov. 26.

But the four-time NFL MVP and, suddenly, NFL free agent remains at the forefront of the discourse around the league and even the country in the aftermath of his very pointed exposure of Steelers coach Mike McCarthy's alleged issues and why they drove Rodgers to the point of issuing ultimatums and later detailing just about everything that had been occurring in the Pittsburgh locker room in a remarkable break from NFL protocol − and Rodgers' long-held belief that matters that swirl about a football team's inner sanctum should remain private.

But after a little more than 24 hours of pundits, reporters, social media, Rodgers' former teammates and a handful of current ones weighing in on the situation, a man with a perspective as unique and, for the most part, as informed as any shared his thoughts regarding the shocking meltdown that's occurred in the Steel City.

Mike Tomlin, the Steelers' coach for 19 seasons from 2007 through 2025, and perhaps the primary reason Rodgers came to Pittsburgh a year ago, discussed what has happened to the Steelers at halftime of the Kansas City Chiefs-Buffalo Bills game in his current as an analyst for NBC.

Speaking remotely with NBC's NFL play-by-play man, Mike Tirico, Tomlin said he felt a "general sadness" watching the public disintegration of Rodgers' and McCarthy's relationship and the quarterback's abrupt exit from the team.

"Certainly I had a range of emotions watching those course of events yesterday," Tomlin said of Rodgers' interview on "The Pat McAfee Show."

"I think the prevailing one is just general sadness – sad that we might be looking at the end of a legendary career, the quarterback, Aaron Rodgers. And just on a macro level, whether it's organizationally or for the game of football at large, to see laundry aired out that way in a public forum – those are things that kinda run counterculture to professional football."

Tomlin said Rodgers' remarks about McCarthy "certainly did" shock him, "particularly because I know both men involved."

He also characterized Rodgers, despite the scorched earth route he took Wednesday, as a "beloved" teammate.

"I'm not overexaggerating that," Tomlin continued. "He worked at it, and people appreciated his openness to get to know and understand the Steeler culture, to be engaged with teammates."

He also touted Rodgers' patience and outreach to fellow Steelers, especially younger ones.

As for McCarthy, Tirico asked Tomlin if his successor can effectively lead the Steelers in the wake of Rodgers' comments.

"It certainly created some challenges, and 'challenges' is probably an understatement if all those things are true," Tomlin said.

"But (McCarthy's) ability to overcome that or inability to overcome that, in my opinion, is based on relationships and the depth of those relationships and how those guys feel about him or how those guys buy into what he's selling.

"It makes you wonder the level of that challenge, but it will be relationship-based − his ability to overcome (this)."

Asked what it was like to see the scrutiny trained on his longtime employer, Tomlin said: "I'm upset about it. It's a dumpster fire. And I don't say that in a judgmental way − I've presided over a few dumpster fires in my 19 years (as coach). But it certainly is upsetting, and I'm sure Steeler Nation is upset."

While questioning McCarthy's dedication and efficacy at his job while speaking to McAfee, Rodgers took the opportunity to praise Tomlin anew. Both he and his former coach had long had mutual admiration before teaming up for the 2025 season. But Tomlin stepped down at the end of the campaign, which ended with a resounding defeat in the wild-card round to the Houston Texans in Pittsburgh. Per reports, Rodgers, who played quite poorly against the Texans, was in tears when Tomlin announced the decision to leave his post following the loss.

"Leader of men, he knew everybody's name, knew where they came from," Rodgers said of Tomlin on Wednesday, a description that also served to distinguish him from McCarthy.
