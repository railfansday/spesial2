Saints vs. Bears: Key Matchups

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The New Orleans Saints are walking into Chicago as double-digit underdogs against the Bears. The Saints have dropped two of their last three, and their season has quickly been derailed.

Now in desperate need of a win, New Orleans will face their former head coach and current Bears defensive coordinator, Dennis Allen, in a game they were completely outmatched in just a year ago. If the Saints want to steal a win, it will be by taking advantage of these key matchups.

Saints fans will be the first ones to tell you about Allen's capabilities as a defensive playcaller. The former Saints head coach was known for his ability to call up exotic looks at the line of scrimmage, something that has become even more relevant this season with the massive success of Brian Flores and the Minnesota Vikings defense.

Tyler Shough has been a bright spot for the Saints this season, but is coming off one of the more disappointing games of his career. Even worse for Shough and the Saints, this was against a lackluster Carolina Panthers defense. Now New Orleans will be facing a team that is top-10 in sacks and just made a massive trade for one of the best defensive linemen of the decade, Jeffrey Simmons.

The Saints have worked on getting the ball out faster, and it's improved over the weeks. But pass protection remains a major concern, and with a switch at center, right guard, and right tackle, the Saints could be in for a long day.

I don't think I even have to elaborate here. The Saints are a bottom-five rushing offense in the league, and are coming off a game where running backs averaged 1.5 yards per carry against one of the league's worst run defenses in the league. Now New Orleans will be facing a team that is top-five against the run.

Getting the run started has been a problem all season, and really, it's more of a Kellen Moore issue than it is a running backs issue. But if the Saints are going to have any chance at keeping up with Chicago's high-powered offense, it will be by controlling the game on the ground.

The Saints have two of the most dangerous pass rushers this season, with Carl Granderson logging 8.5 sacks and Chase Young getting 8 of his own. Fortunately for the Saints, they'll be facing a quarterback who holds on to the ball the longest in the NFL. Unfortunately for New Orleans, Caleb Williams' time from getting the ball and releasing it hasn't translated into sacks.

Over the last two games, Williams has been sacked just twice and has become a playmaker in the face of the pass rush. The former first overall pick's stats haven't looked great this season, throwing just 8 touchdowns to 5 interceptions, but he has grown into a leader and can command an offense with the best of them. If the Saints want a chance at winning this game, they'll likely have to take down the star quarterback three or more times and create impossible goal-to-go situations.
