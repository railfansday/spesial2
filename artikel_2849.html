84 days, four titles, one historic feat - Alcaraz's remarkable return

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The men's tennis season ended as it began, with a beaming Carlos Alcaraz holding aloft one of the sport's biggest trophies.

In January, he won the Australian Open to complete the career Grand Slam. Ten months later, he stood as the victor in Turin, having ended his wait for a first ATP Finals title.

Watching Alcaraz over recent weeks, it would be easy to forget that for a four-month period this year, the 23-year-old barely picked up a racquet.

He missed the majority of the clay and all of the grass-court season with a wrist injury, but he has made a remarkable return, culminating in a dismantling of world number one Alexander Zverev on Sunday.

It is 84 days since Alcaraz made his comeback at the US Open, where his lack of match fitness understandably showed. He came through the early rounds but lost a five-set quarter-final epic against Ben Shelton.

Since then, the Spaniard has won 21 of his 22 matches - including each of the past 14 - and captured titles at four of the five tournaments he has contested.

He continues to make history, too, becoming the youngest man to win all four Slams and the season-ending Finals.

This was the third straight tournament he has won, following victories at the Vienna Open and Paris Masters earlier in November, and he has now won his past nine matches against fellow top-10 players.

The former world number one, currently ranked third behind Zverev and Jannik Sinner, continues to make up for lost time as he seeks to quickly reassert himself at the top of the sport.

"I think everybody feels he's the number one," Alcaraz said, stressing each player in the game has to treat themselves that way.

"Me, Sascha [Zverev], Jannik [Sinner], [Ben] Shelton. Having that confidence in yourself is really important.

"But right now the number one is Sascha. He really deserves it."

Crowd-pleasing variety, devastating power and astonishing athleticism did not take long to return to Alcaraz's game.

Even with a heavily strapped left leg after injuring his hamstring on Wednesday, he could not be stopped by the world's top players in Turin.

That included Zverev, who this year rose to the top of the rankings for the first time after claiming his first two major titles and recording a tour-leading 69 match wins.

Alcaraz, already a seven-time Slam winner, feels his own growth in the mental side of the sport has taken him to a different level in recent months.

He has specifically pointed to the new sense of perspective he gained from his time away from tennis in inspiring his scintillating year-end form.

"The injury made me realise what's really important," Alcaraz said.

"It's not winning. It's just about playing tennis. I realised that it is a gift every time that I can step on the court and compete healthy.

"When you realise that, everything comes easier.

"I don't feel stressed most of the time. I think right now I'm more mature, or I feel that, on a tennis court, I'm facing the problems and difficult moments in a different way."

Carlos Alcaraz is the third Spaniard to triumph at the season-ending Finals - and first since Alex Corretja in 1998. Rafael Nadal never won the event. [Getty Images]

Alcaraz threw himself to the ground after ending his wait to win the season-ending tournament before racing into the stands to celebrate with his team and family.

It marked the 30th title of his career, drawing him level with rival Sinner and behind only men's record 24-time major winner Novak Djokovic (102) among active ATP players.

More could soon be around the corner - should he manage to stay injury-free.

Victory in Paris has left him two titles away from emulating Sinner and Djokovic by completing the career 'Golden Masters' - winning all nine Masters 1,000 events, the level of tournament below the Slams.

Should he achieve that next season in Canada and Shanghai, it would leave him only an Olympic singles gold away from completing the sport.

His main obstacle to achieving such dominance still appears to be Sinner, given Alcaraz's positive head-to-head against Zverev.

Italian Sinner has been absent with a knee issue since Wimbledon but is aiming to return at the Australian Open, where Alcaraz will defend his title in January.

"In today's final, I needed to put the three Cs - cabeza, corazon, cojones - that my grandpa always tells me I have to give every time I step on the court," Alcaraz said.

"The three Cs in English is head, heart and balls. Today, that was what it was about.

"Lifting this trophy is something I have been chasing for a really long time. It means the world to me."
