1 AI Stock I’d Buy Right Now Without Hesitation

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Every AI chip has to be built on machines that cost a fortune and take years to replace. Applied Materials makes those machines; it just had its best quarter ever, and the stock fell anyway.

Before an artificial intelligence (AI) chip answers a single question, it spends months in a factory being built one microscopic layer at a time. Applied Materials (AMAT -0.29%) makes many of the machines that deposit, etch, and polish these layers. If chipmakers want more AI chips, they have to buy more of its equipment first.

They're buying. On Nov. 12, Applied reported record quarterly revenue of $10.41 billion, up 53% from a year ago, and adjusted earnings per share (EPS) jumped 92% to $4.16.

The stock slipped 2.7% the next day anyway, closing about 50% below its 52-week high. I'll take that discount gladly.

The best part of this report was what customers want next. CEO Gary Dickerson expects spending on chipmaking equipment to rise more than 30% in 2027 and thinks Applied can grow faster than that. He also expects cloud companies to boost data center spending by more than 50% next year, bringing it to well over $1 trillion.

Management also added more than 10 new factory projects to its tracking list for the third quarter in a row, and many won't open until 2029 or 2030.

Then there's packaging, the step where memory is stacked right next to the processor so AI chips can grab data faster. Applied expects that business to grow about 90% this calendar year. Every AI chip needs piles of fast memory, and stacking it takes Applied's tools. So Applied is planning to double its systems output from last quarter's level by 2028.

Applied pays for its growth out of its own pocket. It produced record free cash flow of $2.6 billion last quarter and spent $2 billion buying back its own stock, its biggest quarterly buyback in more than eight years. Over time, it plans to return 80% to 100% of free cash flow to shareholders. Gross margin has also widened from a year earlier for 14 straight quarters. In a buying frenzy, customers pay up rather than shop around.

For the first quarter of fiscal 2027, Applied guided for $11.1 billion in revenue and $4.35 in adjusted earnings per share. That's an ordinary price-to-earnings ratio for a business growing profits this fast, and one of the cleanest ways to own semiconductor stocks without picking a chip winner. The real speed limit is how fast chipmakers can build clean rooms to house the machines, and that's a problem of too much demand, not too little.

Chip designers can fight all they want over whose AI chip wins. Every one of them still needs Applied Materials' machines to build it.
