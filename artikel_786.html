Instagram Testing Joke-Stealing Detection to Crack Down on Fake AI Comics

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Meta is introducing new features to help content creators go after people stealing their content: "We'll keep improving as fast as we can."

Stop them if you’ve heard this one before.

Meta is rolling out new features to help crack down on scammers stealing other people’s content.

The changes include a pilot program where eligible creators can enroll in a service where they’re notified if the company’s algorithm finds plagiarized versions of their content.

The move reported in The Hollywood Reporter and other outlets about comedians spotting their jokes being stolen, often word for word, by accounts featuring attractive AI comics. Often, these bogus comedians were linked to OnlyFans accounts. Instagram chief Adam Mosseri announced the measure on Threads on Tuesday: “This week we’re starting to roll out ‘Content Protection,’ a new way to see and review copies of your videos in your insights on Instagram. You can decide to hide the copy or add a link back to your original. We’re also working quickly on ways to catch copies that are harder to spot, like when someone takes scripts or jokes and uses AI to swap out the visuals. We will not get everything right at first, but we’ll keep improving as fast as we can.”

According to a spokesperson, the service “alerts eligible creators when it finds visual matches of their original videos and lets them decide what happens next, including tracking a copy, adding a credit link or hiding it. Once enrolled, creators’ new original videos are automatically protected, and they can manually select eligible older videos for protection.”

“Separately, we’re testing script detection with a small number of creators to help identify copies that reuse someone’s script or jokes while changing the visuals with AI,” the spokesperson added. “That remains a limited test, distinct from the broader content protection rollout, with plans to expand soon.”

Instagram says eligible creators will receive a notification in their activity feed allowing them to enroll.

In October, two comics posted detailed side-by-side breakdowns showing how their joke was taken by AI. “AI stole my joke and my outfit,” said George Brett Olson. “You might be saying, ‘I’ve heard comedians talk about this already,’ but this is new — very new.”

“So another AI bot just stole my joke,” added Julia Jasunas. “I’ve seen a couple other videos of other comedians posting about the exact same problem, and the thing that’s the most concerning is these AI bots are going viral, and people think that they’re real people. Who does comedy in Daisy Duke shorts?”
