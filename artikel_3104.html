Our results, and especially our same-store performance, reflect the dynamic flywheel that this approach creates as local CEOs and CCOs elevate others within their teams so they can spend time supporting transitions.

In many cases, these newly elevated leaders have driven improved clinical, financial, and growth results in our same-store operations, even as our CEO and CCO group have helped effectively integrate these new agencies. As a result of these efforts across the segment, total home health admissions grew 39.4% and Medicare admissions grew 38%, offsetting a 2.2% decline in revenue per episode due to lower rates in our new Southeast locations. In same-store locations, our total admissions increased 8%, Medicare admissions increased 12%, and revenue per episode increased 1.6%, each over the prior year quarter, as our operators drove accelerated growth and effectively offset the Medicare rate cut.

We also saw compelling hospice growth with total admissions increasing 31.8%, total average daily census increasing 34.6%, and revenue per day improving 2.1%, each over the prior year quarter. Same-store growth was equally strong, with same-store admissions up 3.1%, ADC increasing 13%, and revenue per day improving 3.5%, each over the prior year quarter. Our local leaders continued to manage the business effectively. And despite the significant increase in benefits costs that Brent described earlier, we saw same-store margin increase 80 basis points compared to the prior year quarter. Clinically, we saw continued outperformance as our potentially preventable hospitalization rate improved 10 basis points to 9.9% across the portfolio compared to an industry average of 10.8%.

And our CMS-reported hospice quality composite score increased to 98.3% compared to an industry average of 96.2%. We know our clinical outcomes drive our growth and financial performance and have made us an attractive partner for prospective referral, joint venture, and payer partners. Since the beginning of last year, our senior living business has acquired 16 communities, including seven in this calendar year. This growth created some short-term operating challenges during the quarter, concentrated primarily in two markets with a high number of transitions.

In these markets, our growth temporarily outpaced leadership capacity, and several transitions and same-store operations did not perform at the level we expected. Combined with the elevated benefits costs that Brent described earlier, this impacted segment margins and caused segment-level adjusted EBITDA to be roughly flat compared to the prior year quarter. It is important to keep these challenges in perspective. They are concentrated rather than systemic, and they come against a backdrop of substantial growth in the senior living business as a whole.

We are excited about each of our newly added communities, many of which were acquired at low occupancy and at a substantial discount to market rates, and the opportunity they present for organic growth for years to come. As we have discussed many times, we expect lumpiness in our short-term results, especially in communities we have acquired with low occupancy. Despite recent capital expenditures and an attractive building, River Centre's low occupancy and poor financial performance allowed us to acquire it at a significant discount to market rates.
