‘No hot seat’: Oklahoma backs Brent Venables, and his $51.67 million buyout explains why

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

The University of Oklahoma Board of Regents met in Norman on Wednesday morning, and chair Rick Nagel used the session to address a subject the board usually leaves to the athletic department.

The Oklahoma Sooners are 5-4 in Brent Venables' fifth season, with the No. 19 Ole Miss Rebels visiting Norman on Saturday. Oklahoma has never beaten Ole Miss, and the Rebels won in Norman last season. Oklahoma then hosts the Texas A&M Aggies on Nov. 21 and closes the regular season at the Missouri Tigers on Nov. 28.

"The board doesn't typically comment much on what's going on in the athletic department. That's maybe a smart thing. But I want to say this … our head coach, Brent Venables, is not on the hot seat. There is no hot seat. We just extended his contract," Nagel said, as reported by Joe Mussatto of The Oklahoman.

That extension, agreed to in August and approved by the regents in September, runs six years and $63 million through Jan. 31, 2032. Venables is making $10 million this season, with $200,000 raises each year after, and contract documents put his buyout at $51.67 million if Oklahoma moves on from him on Dec. 1. SoonerScoop's George Stoia calculated that the same decision would have cost roughly $26.2 million without the extension.

The deal also added incentives tied to College Football Playoff success. The board doubled the price of a coaching change in the summer, weeks before a season that has looked a lot like Venables' other even-numbered years.

Venables is 37-24 at Oklahoma and 0-4 in bowl and playoff games. His first four seasons alternated between 6-7 finishes in 2022 and 2024 and 10-3 finishes in 2023 and 2025, and the most recent of those 10-win teams lost in the first round of the College Football Playoff.

Oklahoma has followed the same rhythm week to week this fall. The Sooners beat the No. 1 Texas Longhorns 27-24 on Oct. 10 on a field goal as time expired, then lost 21-13 at home to the Kentucky Wildcats on Oct. 17. A 42-25 home loss to the South Carolina Gamecocks on Oct. 31 was followed by Venables firing offensive coordinator Ben Arbuckle, and the Sooners responded with a 23-7 win at the Florida Gators last Saturday that included a 20-play touchdown drive.

"A thing of beauty. Fabulous in every way," Venables said of that drive after the game.

The swings cut both ways for the board. Oklahoma has wins over Texas and at Florida, and it also has home losses to Kentucky and South Carolina. A program that can beat the top-ranked team in the country in October and lose at home to Kentucky the next week gives an administration reasons to believe in the coach and reasons to doubt him, sometimes inside the same month.

OU football head coach Brent Venables during the game against Houston on Sept. 7.Bob Nguyen/OU Daily

Nagel answered the hot-seat question by pointing to the contract, and that is the most direct explanation on the table. A board that committed $63 million in August and raised the cost of a firing to $51.67 million made its decision on Venables before the season started, and Wednesday's statement put that decision on the record before Ole Miss arrives in Norman.

Whatever the final three games produce, the Sooners' leadership has already told the public what it is willing to pay to find out.
