Senate Budget chair's Social Security fix might add trillions to U.S. debt

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget estimates the Stop Scaring Seniors Act would add $190 trillion over 75 years

Senate Budget Committee Chairman Ron Johnson, a Wisconsin Republican, proposed a bill on Wednesday that would prevent Social Security benefits from being reduced when the program's trust fund is depleted. Johnson calls it the Stop Scaring Seniors Act and asked on X $TWTR who would cosponsor it.

The Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget, a nonpartisan deficit-hawk group, estimates that over 75 years the bill would increase the national debt by $190 trillion after adjusting for inflation, equal to 165% of GDP, according to Fortune. The measure would allow the Treasury secretary to shift general-fund dollars into the retirement program's trust fund once its reserves are exhausted.

The retirement trust fund has under six years of reserves left, according to Fortune. Depletion would set off an automatic reduction of roughly 22% in benefits, a loss the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget estimates at around $500 monthly for the typical recipient.

Maya MacGuineas, president of the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget, criticized the proposal. "We usually say that inaction is the worst option when it comes to Social Security, but now we found something even worse," she said, according to Fortune. She called the bill "a cynical pander that ducks all the real decisions we need to confront."

MacGuineas also warned of market effects. "Even simply passing the legislation could spook the bond market enough to spark financial turmoil," she said, according to Fortune.

In an analysis published last November, the group calculated that paying for Social Security out of general revenue would lift interest rates roughly 2.6 percentage points above where they would stand if the trust fund stayed whole, according to Fortune. Under that scenario, the 10-year Treasury yield would climb from 4% to 6.6%, while mortgage rates on 30-year loans would go from 6.3% to 8.9%.

Johnson contends lawmakers ought to tackle the entire deficit rather than treat Social Security separately, according to Fortune. MacGuineas called that "a reasonable position," but said a law "that fails to fix Social Security while ballooning the debt" was not the way to do it. She suggested lawmakers consider a bipartisan commission.

A Senate Budget Committee report Johnson released Wednesday, titled "Social Security: The Reality… a Legal Ponzi Scheme," forecasts a gap of roughly $511 billion in the program's finances by 2032, according to Fortune.

The bill follows other recent remarks by Johnson. On Wednesday he called Social Security a "scam" that should be "shut down" during a Fox Business segment. A day earlier, he previewed a Senate Budget Committee hearing on the program in an X post that described it as a "legal Ponzi scheme."
