Shein is settling U.S. consumer-deception charges for $100 million

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The FTC alleges the fast-fashion retailer delayed orders, suppressed negative reviews and made unsupported supplier and cotton claims

Shein will pay $100 million to settle Federal Trade Commission allegations that it deceived U.S. consumers about shipping, product reviews, suppliers and cotton origin, the agency said on Tuesday. The FTC will use the money to provide redress to consumers.

The FTC filed a complaint and a stipulated final order in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California. The agency alleges Shein violated Section 5 of the FTC Act, the Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule and the Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials. Stipulated final orders have the force of law when the district court judge approves and signs them, the FTC said. The Commission voted 2-0 to authorize the filing.

"In reality, the company misled millions of consumers about its products, its suppliers and the authenticity of its customer reviews," Christopher Mufarrige, director of the FTC's Bureau of Consumer Protection, said in a statement.

The complaint alleges Shein marketed fast and reliable shipping, but tens of thousands of consumers reported orders that arrived days, weeks or months late. Some consumers never received their orders. The FTC says Shein ships close to one million packages a day to the United States. As of September 2025, it employed five customer service agents for telephone inquiries from American consumers and did not list a phone number for them on its website.

The FTC also alleges Shein published positive reviews while suppressing or deleting reviews from customers who criticized or returned its products. Internal guidance instructed staff to leave out "extremely bad comments," according to Reuters.

The complaint says Shein marketed its evoluSHEIN products as coming from suppliers with above-average ratings in social-compliance audits designed to protect against child and forced labor. The FTC alleges Shein could not provide a single audit supporting those claims. It also alleges that cotton Shein represented as U.S.-grown came from China, and that cotton in at least some products could not be traced to any country of origin.

Under the proposed order, Shein is prohibited from misrepresenting shipping and delivery times and from suppressing negative product reviews. It also may not refuse to provide adequate customer service or make deceptive claims about supplier and manufacturer audits or product traceability.

A Shein spokesperson said the company has not accepted legal responsibility and contests parts of the FTC's allegations, according to Reuters. As part of the settlement, the company said it agreed to keep strengthening its compliance work and how it treats consumers.
