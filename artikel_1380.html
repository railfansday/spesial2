Chelsea have not given up on signing Monaco midfielder Lamine Camara despite also pushing hard to land Bournemouth star Alex Scott in January.

The Blues came extremely close to bringing Camara to Stamford Bridge on deadline day last summer before Monaco unexpectedly pulled out of the agreement.

Rather than abandoning their interest, Chelsea have continued monitoring the 22-year-old and could now reignite a transfer battle with Liverpool.

According to TEAMtalk, Chelsea remain firmly interested in Camara and have stayed in regular contact with his representatives since the collapsed summer move.

A €55million agreement was in place with Monaco on deadline day, but the Ligue 1 club withdrew late in the window, leaving Chelsea frustrated.

Camara has responded impressively this season, playing every minute of Monaco’s Ligue 1 campaign so far.

Chelsea are also making progress with Scott, but sources have not ruled out the possibility of signing both midfielders. Their need for greater depth was highlighted again when Moisés Caicedo suffered an injury during the recent 4-1 defeat to Leeds United.

Liverpool are interested in both players as well, creating the possibility of two separate transfer battles.

There is logic behind Chelsea targeting both players, but spending heavily on two midfielders in January would be an aggressive strategy.

Camara offers defensive intensity, athleticism and ball-winning ability, while Scott provides more carrying, creativity and Premier League experience.

A recent CaughtOffside report revealed that Liverpool have already done considerable groundwork on Camara and are close to agreeing personal terms with his representatives.

That gives Chelsea a decision to make. They already know Camara is interested after agreeing terms with him in the summer, but Scott appears to be their current priority and would likely cost considerably more.

Trying to sign both could solve Chelsea’s midfield depth problem in one window, but it may also limit spending elsewhere.

The smarter approach may be to secure Scott first and only return for Camara if the finances remain manageable.

Still, Chelsea should not allow Liverpool a free run. After coming so close in August, walking away completely from Camara now would feel premature.
