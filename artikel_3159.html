1 High-Flying Stock to Buy Hand Over Fist if the Stock Market Crashes

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Moderna's cancer vaccine data were historic, but the stock prices in a flawless future.

By Prosper Junior Bakiny – Nov 20, 2026 at 12:21PM EST

Innovation often pays off. Moderna (MRNA -1.92%) has proven as much this year. The company's shares have soared in 2026 on the back of impressive clinical and regulatory progress. But is there any upside left for the vaccine maker? Not in my view. But Moderna would be a great stock to buy if the stock market crashes. Read on to find out more.

Moderna is pioneering mRNA-based personalized cancer vaccines. These products offer the potential to train patients' immune systems to recognize and destroy cancer cells based on specific mutations present in each patient's tumor. This approach could lead to much greater treatment efficacy than conventional cancer medicines. Personalized cancer vaccines may also more effectively prevent recurrence.

Moderna provided the first positive Phase 3 clinical trial data for an investigational personalized cancer vaccine, intismeran autogene, earlier this year. Moderna is developing intismeran autogene with Merck (MRK +1.40%), and in this Phase 3 study, the personalized cancer vaccine was tested as a combination therapy with Keytruda -- the best-selling cancer drug in the world -- versus Keytruda alone in certain patients with melanoma. Intismeran autogene, combined with Keytruda, led to meaningful improvements in recurrence-free survival. This clinical win sent Moderna's shares up by more than 100% overnight.

The market is seeing beyond Moderna's clinical win. The company proved that personalized cancer vaccines can reach significant clinical success. Intismeran autogene hasn't done so yet, but based on its Phase 3 results, it should eventually generate significant sales. Some analysts expect the personalized cancer vaccine to generate more than $10 billion in revenue at its peak, especially as it earns approvals across additional indications beyond melanoma.

It is being invested in as a treatment across several other forms of cancer. And the rest of the company's lineup and pipeline also looks robust. For instance, Moderna has earned approval for mFLUSIVA, an influenza vaccine, earlier this year. Its coronavirus vaccine is still on the market, and it is developing several other candidates in infectious diseases.

Some of the company's targets look ambitious. Moderna is developing a potential HIV vaccine, for instance. There is a long road ahead for this candidate, but if it is successful, it could be a game changer. That said, Moderna's most important strength isn't any single pipeline candidate or currently approved product.

It is the company's innovative mRNA-based platform that should produce significant clinical milestones over the next five to 10 years. However, there is a problem. The market may have already factored Moderna's success into its share price. Consider that the company's market cap is $82 billion, as of writing. Even assuming Moderna could generate $10 billion in sales next year (which it won't), it would be trading at 8.2x estimated 2027 sales, a fairly expensive valuation for a biotech stock of this size.

True, more clinical wins could jolt the stock, but even with that in mind, I am not convinced the valuation makes sense. After all, even intismeran autogene isn't approved yet, and some other advanced pipeline candidates may not earn U.S. approval for another two years. Adding commercial ramp-up times, we are looking at revenue far into the future even for the company's most advanced programs, and that's assuming they are successful at all.

The uncertainty of these future revenues (and cash flows) makes for a somewhat risky outlook at current levels: Too much could go wrong and sink the stock. I'd like a much more reasonable valuation and a significant margin of error -- considering we are discussing a volatile biotech stock -- before investing. If Moderna's market cap falls below $50 billion in a market crash, I'd definitely consider buying because the business looks strong, the science is impeccable, and the company's future looks bright.

But valuation is a major sticking point. I am keeping Moderna on my watch list for now.
