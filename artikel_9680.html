Faster Prediction Is Only Half the Loop. Faster Understanding Is the Other Half.

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

I keep thinking about the same line in Google DeepMind's new blog post: Antigravity for Science accelerating the search for novel antimicrobial peptides. It's a genuinely exciting direction — and a reminder of how fast computational biology now moves from sequence to candidate.

But here's the part that rarely makes the headline. Every one of those pipelines still ends with a human looking at a structure and asking, "does this binding pocket make sense?"

That act of looking has been stuck on flat screens for decades. Rotating a 300-residue protein with a mouse. Squinting at depth cues. Mentally reconstructing a 3D cavity from a 2D projection. It works — but it's a translation step, and translation costs insight.

NexoPeek is a Meta Quest VR molecular laboratory built for exactly that gap. Twelve narrated tours span six pillars — Biotechnology Basics, Foundations of Life, Pathways & Interactions, Medicines in Action, Genetic Technologies, Green Biotech — so a student can step inside hemoglobin and watch oxygen bind, or stand in a CRISPR-Cas9 editing complex. The professional PDB viewer resolves a gene name across RCSB PDB, UniProtKB, AlphaFold DB, SWISS-MODEL and PDBe, then loads the structure at human scale with one-tap Mol* presets: cartoon, ball-and-stick, spacefill CPK, molecular surface, B-factor heat, tube trace. Local .pdb, .cif and .mol2 files load too, with electrostatic field-line maps and electron-density volumes.

The analysis bench is where it earns a place next to the AI work. Grab and scale the molecule with your hands. Let AI pocket prediction highlight a druggable site. Superimpose a homolog with Foldseek. Trace hydrogen bonds in the Ligand Inspector, pull a 3D ligand from PubChem, or trigger-pick two atoms to read the distance in angstroms.

Prediction tools are getting extraordinary. Interpretation is still human. Giving interpretation a spatial workspace is the whole point.
