American soldier arrested by UK police on suspicion of spying taken into US military custody

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The US serviceman is understood to have been based at Imjin Barracks in Gloucestershire

A US Army officer arrested in the UK on suspicion of spying for Russia earlier this month has been taken into US military custody, British counter-terror police have said.

The serviceman has been identified as Lt Col Brandon Carroll Tyner, BBC News understands. The 41-year-old - who has served in the US Army since 2008 - was arrested near Gloucester last week by British counter-terror police.

Tyner was based at Imjin Barracks in Innsworth, Gloucestershire, the headquarters of Nato's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps (ARRC).

Counter Terror Policing said he is now in custody over suspected national security and espionage allegations. At the time of his arrest, Russia said they had "nothing to do with it".

British police previously said he was being held in London after he was arrested under section one and section three of the National Security Act 2023, which relate to obtaining or disclosing protected information, and assisting a foreign intelligence service.

The day after Tyner's arrest, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said he was "unaware" of what it was connected to.

"We have nothing to do with it, and it has nothing to do with us," he told reporters in Moscow.

Assistant Commissioner Laurence Taylor, head of Counter Terrorism Policing, said: "Subsequently, a decision was made to pass the investigation to them [their American partners] to progress."

"These are extremely serious allegations, and our collective response should send a strong message that we will act swiftly and robustly where we suspect this kind of espionage activity taking place," he added.

FBI director Kash Patel said an army officer was in custody "as the result of an espionage investigation and the close cooperation" between the bureau, US Army and various UK partners.

"Together we worked to identify a potential threat to national security and take swift action," he said.

"This FBI has zero tolerance for anyone who betrays their oath to the United States or puts our national security at risk."

US Brig Gen Sean Stinchon said the case should serve as a "stark warning to any service member suspected of violating the oath" they all take.

"Whenever we suspect somebody of betraying the trust of their fellow soldiers and jeopardising our collective defence, we will identify you, and we will ensure justice is served," he added.
