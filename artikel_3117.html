Is Credo Technology a Better AI Networking Buy Than Broadcom?

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Does Broadcom's massive scale make it the ultimate artificial intelligence stock? Credo Technology's triple-digit growth rate might offer a more lucrative alternative for risk-tolerant investors.

It's a chip giant in the trillion-dollar club, sporting a $1.65 trillion market cap as of Nov. 13. Trailing sales are $89.1 billion, $38.3 billion trickled down to the bottom line, and free cash flow of $39.4 billion is comparable to the gross domestic product of Iceland or Bolivia. Your smartphone probably has a few Broadcom chips, and the average corporate data center has thousands.

Credo Technology Group (CRDO -5.94%) is a different story.

This is a networking expert, best known for its active electric cables (AECs) and serializer/deserializer (or "SerDes") data converter modules. Credo's technology proved useful to large-scale data centers, where low power consumption matters as much as top-speed performance. As a result, trailing sales rose 869% over the last three years, and Wall Street is downright excited about the stock. However, Credo is a minnow next to Broadcom's bulk. After the massive sales jump, trailing revenues are still just $1.6 billion. And you'd have to be a network engineer or data center architect to come across its products in the wild.

But both companies are AI networking investments. Does Broadcom's gigantic scale make it a winner, or should you focus on Credo's high-octane growth instead?

Broadcom provides the picks and shovels for the AI gold rush at an industrial scale. In Q3 2026, the company reported AI semiconductor revenue of $16.7 billion, a staggering 221% year-over-year increase. More importantly, management's forward-looking visibility is almost unheard of in the semiconductor space.

CEO Hock Tan raised fiscal 2026 AI revenue guidance to $58 billion, projecting $115 billion in 2027 and $230 billion by 2028. This growth is heavily supported by custom AI accelerators (XPUs) co-developed with frontier labs such as Alphabet (GOOG -0.96%) (GOOGL -0.98%), Meta Platforms (META +2.90%), and Anthropic. Broadcom's Tomahawk Ethernet switches are also popular in AI data centers, meaning the company wins whether a data center needs processing power or data transfer tools. Trading at a forward P/E ratio of roughly 18, it gives investors a relatively safe, dividend-paying anchor for their AI portfolios.

Credo recently reported fiscal 2027 first-quarter revenue of $479 million. While that is a fraction of Broadcom's top-line haul, it more than doubled year over year. As noted earlier, that was Credo's seventh consecutive quarter of triple-digit revenue growth.

Beyond the ultra-efficient AECs, Credo's chief catalyst right now is a rapid expansion into the optical market. Driven by its optical digital signal processors (DSPs), silicon photonics, and innovative Zero-Flap Optics, Credo expects its optical segment alone to generate over $600 million in sales this fiscal year. As AI data clusters grow larger and more complex, Credo's focus on system-level reliability and power efficiency makes it a vital partner for hyperscalers with hyper-large electric bills.

Fun fact: Broadcom makes SerDes modules, too. Credo most likely collects royalties from those sales.

Choosing between these two networking plays ultimately comes down to your personal investing style and risk tolerance.

Broadcom is the clear choice for conservative investors. It is a foundational tech stock with a highly visible runway, not to mention decades of relationship-building across the tech sector.

But if you are looking for explosive, market-beating upside, Credo Technology makes a compelling case. Its specialized technological moat in both copper and optical connectivity is opening up massive new addressable markets. While it carries the typical volatility of a high-growth tech stock, Credo's sheer momentum makes it a top pick for growth-focused portfolios.
