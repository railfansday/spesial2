Gilmore Girls' "Worst" Season Officially Addressed By OG Stars

For years, Gilmore Girls fans have been in agreement about the final season of the series being the worst by far, and in a new documentary, the show's stars are finally addressing what led to the massive dip in quality.

Drink Coffee, Talk Fast: A Documentary About Gilmore Girls revisits the history of the long-running series, which began its run on The WB in 2000 and ended on the newly revamped network, The CW, in 2007. Several people involved with the series, including Kelly Bishop, who played Emily Gilmore, Michael Winters, who played Taylor Doose, and director Jamie Babbit shared their recollections of what took place after Gilmore Girls creator Amy Sherman-Palladino departed from the series, ahead of its difficult seventh season.

Said to be the show's weakest, Gilmore Girls season 7 often felt like an off-brand version of the hit's past life. While Sherman-Palldino and her creative partner husband, Daniel Palladino, weren't the only writers on Gilmore Girls, they brought a unique style to the series that the next showrunner, producer Phil Rosenthal, struggled to bring to the series.

Bishop offered a more reserved point of view, explaining that "it just wasn’t as strong a season, and that’s because [Sherman-Palladino] wasn’t there," but Babbit and others made it clear that it felt that there was a distinct lack of care that the show's original creator had brought to the series.

Sherman-Palladino's sometimes complex vision for Gilmore Girls, which was known for its fast-talking scenes that involved one steady shot and could take dozens of takes to get right, didn't make the cut when she departed the series. "The mandate was, ‘We don’t care if it’s good or not. We just want to go home...anything complicated was out the window," Babbit revealed.

According to Babbit, Sherman-Palladino's departure and changes to who was running the show were at fault for the quality dip of Gilmore Girls season 7. Rosenthal, a longtime producer of the series, came into the leadership role, but Babbit shared the higher ups "gave the leadership of the show over to the actors" who had spent years with grueling schedules that extended well beyond what had been scheduled. "The actors said, ‘Look, the thing that’s most important to us is going home on time.’"

While Babbit and others were interviewed to lay out an oral history of the series for Drink Coffee, Talk Fast, it's not a surprise to viewers of the long-running series to learn that there was a far different energy at play throughout the run of the show's final season. Gilmore Girls, which returned with Sherman-Palladino's original vision for the show's end with a Netflix revival in 2016, was ultimately able to come to a less hotly-debated conclusion.
