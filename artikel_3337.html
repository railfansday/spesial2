Disney+ Finds Its New #2 Show In The Perfect K-Drama To Binge

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

One of the best K-dramas of the year is deservedly climbing the charts on Disney+ and Hulu. Based on the 2018 web novel and later webtoon of the same name, The Remarried Empress follows titular Empress Navier (Shin Min-a), who is forced into a corner after her husband, Emperor Sovieshu (Ju Ji-hoon), throws away their marriage. The record-breaking premiere of The Remarried Empress started the series off with a bang, and the eight-episode K-drama has continually been a top performer for Disney+/Hulu since. In fact, the only title consistently outperforming it is Percy Jackson and the Olympians (via Flix Patrol).

Beyond having a built-in fandom from the hit source material, The Remarried Empress immediately attracted attention with its star-studded cast. Shin Min-a is easily one of the most renowned K-drama actresses in modern history, but pairing her with familiar heartthrob Lee Jong-suk (Heinrey) has been a true delight. Of course, there's more than enough to enjoy for viewers who aren't well-versed in the K-drama industry. Even if the basic premise doesn't instantly appeal, watching a single episode will reveal the true selling point of The Remarried Empress: a stunning, slow-burn fantasy romance that is meticulously crafted for every prospective viewer.

Despite the summary detailing major plot points like Sovieshu and Navier divorcing— and a strategic partnership with Heinrey making the latter the eponymous "remarried empress" in the first place— the Disney+ K-drama has taken an unconventional approach to adapting the illustrious webtoon. The bulk of the first season has been spent establishing its main characters and showing the audience the deterioration of Navier and Sovieshu's relationship in brutal clarity. Rather than breeze through the beginning and have Sovieshu leave the main character for Rashta (Lee Se-young) by the pilot episode's end, The Remarried Empress centers the familiar story on Navier.

Without question, each member of the extensive ensemble plays an important role in the overarching plot, but the titular character is the centerpiece of every dramatic revelation, shocking twist, and emotional decision that threatens to upend the entire empire. As the gripping narrative grows taut and tense, the stunning aesthetics of the show soften the blow, with sprawling landscapes and ornate interior shots building a beguiling setting while gorgeous costumes complement each character in their own unique color palette. Undoubtedly, the K-drama's every detail is utterly transporting, making it impossible for viewers not to fully immerse themselves in the story.

To that end, there's also incredible balance within the writing. The Remarried Empress refuses to give the audience perfect information, instead choosing deliberate moments to introduce pivotal characters, unveil transformative lore, and reveal just enough disruptive information to make the viewer question everything they know about a given subject. Consequently, The Remarried Empress wields the political drama and turmoil of a historical K-drama whilst it slowly but surely composes a love story that feels fantastic in every sense of the word. Irrefutably, the series is one of the biggest successes of the year— and it couldn't have come soon enough.

Nearly every major streaming service has invested in the production of original Korean content throughout the past decade, but Netflix K-dramas have overwhelmingly been the most popular. Be it dramas that went straight into the pop culture zeitgeist like Squid Game or multi-season favorites like All of Us Are Dead and The Trauma Code: Heroes on Call, Netflix is steadily creating K-dramas that eclipse almost anything comparable released on competing platforms. Obviously, Disney+ and Hulu have had their own successes, but titles like Moving, Made in Korea, and Perfect Crown simply don't have the same reach.

From harrowing depictions of real-life monarchies to modern twists on classic Cinderella stories, these K-dramas promise a royally good time.

As such, distributing something like The Remarried Empress— a long-awaited adaptation of a massive webtoon with a staggering cast to boot— is precisely what Disney +/Hulu needed to stay in the K-drama game. Admittedly, Netflix has dropped its fair share of amazing contributions this year, but very few can match the sheer scope of The Remarried Empress. Should the fantasy romance land a well-earned second season, Disney+ will have a multipart sensation on its hands soon enough. Yet, even if the drama isn't renewed, its eight-episode run is a worthy binge-watch that genre fans will fall head over heels for.
