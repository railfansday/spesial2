Here's My Top Stock to Buy for November

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The company has disappointed with its execution in recent years, but it looks like management's updated guidance has reset expectations such that the stock has significant upside potential.

Enter Boeing (BA +2.24%) stock in a beauty contest, and it wouldn't get many votes. But long-term investing isn't a beauty pageant; it's about finding good value stocks with plenty of upside potential. Boeing is a winner in that regard, even though its recent update might have been disappointing for some. Here's why its worth a closer look.

The manufacturing giant operates out of three segments, and the takeaways from the recent third-quarter earnings are that its global services (BGS) business continues to generate solid earnings to support the other two larger businesses, namely Boeing Defense, Space & Security (BDS) and Boeing Commercial Airplanes (BCA).

BDS has returned to profitability as it continues to work through problematic fixed-price development programs that have caused significant charges and losses in recent years. The update from Boeing's most important business, BCA, was disappointing. Specifically, the pushback in expectations for a ramp-up in 737 MAX production. Still, I think management has reset expectations low enough now that there's plenty of opportunity for Boeing.

Investors have been able to point to the massive backlogs at the company (currently $745 billion) and at BCA ($612 billion, including more than 6,300 planes), and, to a lesser extent, at BDS, for years. Still, the reality is that the company has had issues executing on them. However, there are real signs of progress here.

Starting at BDS, management has previously maintained that its issues stem from a few problematic fixed-price development programs (including Air Force One and the KC-46 Tanker) that represent only 15% of its sales, with the rest of BDS generating profit. It's taken time, but Boeing is working through key milestones on these programs. In July, CEO Kelly Ortberg told investors the KC-46 program "feels very low risk" now that "we're getting to the end of the production, the fixed-price production that we have," with repricing to follow.

Moreover, Ortberg recently raised expectations for BDS growth from low-single-digit annual growth to "middle-single-digit or higher." Ortberg also believes BDS is booking better orders than the problematic ones noted above, as evidence of an improving environment between government and defense companies. I would strongly recommend reviewing the provisions in Lockheed Martin's (LMT +0.97%) recent agreements with the DOD.

The game plan here is simple: Ramp up production of the narrow-body 737 MAX, and, less importantly, the wide-body 787 Dreamliner. Meanwhile, BCA is aiming for the new wide-body 777X, with certification in 2027 and first delivery to follow.

All three are crucial for free-cash-flow generation, as more deliveries mean more revenue and usually margin expansion as more units are spread around Boeing's fixed costs. Meanwhile, any delay in the first delivery of the 777X simply increases cash burn on the program.

Unfortunately, management pushed back expectations for 737 MAX deliveries, telling investors that it would now take until 2027 to stabilize the production rate at 47 a month before targeting "52 in the back half of 2027."

As a reminder, BCA started 2026 with a stabilized rate of 42 a month on the 737 MAX, and expected to hit a rate of 47 a month by the summer. In other words, the main condition (737 MAX) for $10 billion in FCF will be satisfied in terms of production (deliveries will follow) by the end of 2027, and management has set a target of increasing production from 52 per month in 2027 to the "mid to high 50s" in 2029. This may prove conservative.

Putting it all together, BGS is solid, BDS has returned to profitability, and there's evidence that the Department of Defense is being more accommodating in its contracts with defense companies. Finally, BCA guidance may prove conservative, and if the building blocks for $10 billion in FCF are met in 2027, the market won't waste any time pricing it in. As such, the stock is attractive for value-oriented investors.
