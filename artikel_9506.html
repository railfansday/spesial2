ChatGPT Ads Won't Tell You Which Clicks Became Customers. Here's How to Find Out.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Here's a conversation that's about to happen in a lot of marketing teams.

"We spent ₹1.5 lakh on ChatGPT ads last month. Did it work?"

"Well… we got clicks. And the CTR looks good."

That silence isn't a skill problem. It's a measurement problem, and it's built into how AI ad platforms work.

Now that ChatGPT ads have launched in India, many businesses are about to spend money on an AI channel for the first time. Most of them will set up the campaign carefully and the tracking as an afterthought.

This post is about doing it the other way round. No code required.

The problem: AI platforms see the click, not the customer

Someone asks ChatGPT for a recommendation They see your sponsored answer and click They browse your site, leave, and come back two days later They fill in a form Your sales team calls them They become a customer, or they don't

Connect the click to the CRM record. Everything below is just how.

UTM parameters are the tags at the end of a URL that tell your analytics where a visitor came from. They're the glue for this whole setup.

The most common way they break is inconsistency. One person writes ChatGPT, another writes chatgpt, a third writes openai, and your report now shows three different sources for one channel.

Write down a convention and pin it somewhere everyone can see:

Don't type UTMs by hand. Use Google's free Campaign URL Builder or a shared spreadsheet with a formula. One typo can split your data for a whole month.

Step 2: Give AI traffic its own row in GA4

By default, GA4 drops most AI visits into Referral or Unassigned. They're there, but buried.

Go to Admin → Data display → Channel groups → Create new Add a channel called AI Assistants Set the condition: Source matches regex chatgpt|openai|perplexity|gemini|copilot|claude Drag it above Referral. Order matters, because GA4 uses the first rule that matches.

Optionally, add an AI Paid channel above that, with Medium exactly matches ai_paid, so ad clicks and organic AI mentions stay separate.

That second group matters more than people expect. ChatGPT already sends traffic when it recommends you without an ad, and it currently tags many of those links with utm_source=chatgpt.com. Step 3: Stop losing the source when visitors click around

This is the step most setups skip, and it causes most of the "unknown source" leads in CRMs.

UTMs live in the URL of the first page a visitor lands on. If they click to your About page and then fill in the form, the UTMs are gone. Your CRM records the lead as "Direct", and the ChatGPT campaign gets no credit.

The fix is to save the source when the visitor arrives and drop it into hidden form fields later. You don't need a developer for this:

Using a form builder? Most (HubSpot, Zoho Forms, Typeform, Jotform, Gravity Forms) have a built-in "capture UTM parameters" or "hidden fields from URL" option. Turn it on. Using WordPress? Free plugins like HandL UTM Grabber store the UTMs and fill your forms automatically. Using GTM? Ask your developer for a 15-minute "UTM persistence" tag. It's a well-known pattern.

First touch: the channel that introduced the lead Last touch: the channel that brought them back to convert

For a brand-new channel like AI ads, first touch is the more honest number. Last touch tends to give the credit to Google search or direct visits.

Step 4: Track the actions that actually matter

Pageviews aren't conversions. Decide on three to five real actions and mark them as key events in GA4:

generate_lead: a form submitted successfully, not a button clicked book_call: a booking confirmed in your scheduler click_to_call: a tap on your phone number whatsapp_click: important for Indian audiences, and often forgotten

Set these up in Google Tag Manager, then test every one in GTM Preview mode and GA4 DebugView before launching ads. A broken conversion tag during week one of a new channel is an expensive way to learn nothing.

Step 5: Make your CRM remember where every lead came from

Create these fields on your lead record, whatever CRM you use:

CRM field Filled by First Touch Source / Campaign Hidden form fields Last Touch Source / Campaign Hidden form fields Landing Page Hidden form field Lead Status Sales team: New → Qualified → Won/Lost Deal Value Sales team, at close

Map the hidden form fields to these CRM fields once. From then on, every lead carries its origin through to the deal.

Make sure the sales team updates Lead Status. Attribution without outcomes is just a fancier click report.

Step 6: Judge the channel on qualified leads, not clicks

Now you can build the report the earlier conversation needed. Group by first-touch source and campaign:

Metric How to calculate Cost per lead Spend ÷ Leads Qualified rate Qualified ÷ Leads Cost per qualified lead Spend ÷ Qualified leads Revenue Sum of won deal values ROAS Revenue ÷ Spend

The pattern that surprises people: expensive leads often win. A campaign at ₹900 per lead that qualifies 40% of the time beats a ₹300-per-lead campaign that qualifies 8%. Work it out: ₹2,250 vs ₹3,750 per qualified lead.

This is also how to compare channels fairly. If you're weighing ChatGPT ads against Google Ads, put cost per qualified lead side by side, not CTR.

Give any new AI campaign a fixed test window (60–90 days) before deciding. Early costs on new ad platforms swing a lot, and two weeks of data will mislead you.

[ ] UTM convention written down and shared [ ] AI Assistants channel group set up in GA4 [ ] Forms capture first- and last-touch UTMs [ ] 3–5 key events tested in DebugView [ ] CRM fields mapped, and sales updating lead status [ ] Report built on cost per qualified lead Wrapping up

AI ad platforms will probably always show you less than Google or Meta did. That's partly by design.

So stop waiting for a better dashboard. Own the measurement yourself.

The click is theirs. The customer is yours.
