McKenzie: Fury-AJ loser has nowhere to go

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Former three-weight world champion Duke McKenzie believes whoever loses the Tyson Fury vs Anthony Joshua fight will have to retire; McKenzie worries that Joshua's changes of trainer will be detrimental; He believes Moses Itauma can still come back and take over as a new star heavyweight

Tyson Fury and Anthony Joshua are putting their future careers on the line, says former three-weight world champion Duke McKenzie.

Fury vs Joshua is a major event. It will fill a stadium, at 1am in Wales, and command a no doubt huge broadcast audience in Britain and across the world.

But it is happening a late stage for both. McKenzie thinks whoever's defeated in the fight would have to retire from competition.

"There's nowhere to go for the loser. Either of them, whoever loses, it's their career finished. And even for the winner he's going to be one fight away from retirement," he told Sky Sports.

"Let's hope that none of these boys get hurt, they both go home with their health and their wealth and they can live their lives."

Since losing to Andy Ruiz, in a shock upset in 2019, Joshua has brought in multiple difference trainers to his corner, most recently leaving Igor Golub, of Oleksandr Usyk's team, to return to Ben Davison for this fight.

McKenzie thinks he's still looking for something.

"Since [the Ruiz loss] he's had this coach, that coach, he doesn't know what time of day it is in terms of where he actually fits and what suits him. Five, six, seven years ago this happened and there's miles on the clock now with AJ," McKenzie said.

"That's what a defeat can do to you. When you lose for the first time in your career it robs you of something which you never get back. Once that '0' goes very few get it back. Unfortunately for AJ that's where he finds himself."

Fury and Joshua have been major stars in world boxing and British sport. But McKenzie does see a candidate waiting to fill the void when they do retire.

"Moses Itauma. He will be the next big thing in world boxing. What's happened to him [losing to Filip Hrgovic], it's like a wake-up call for him really," McKenzie said.

"He wasn't putting in the work, that was obvious. I think it's easy to get drunk on all the pats on the back that you're getting. Everywhere he turned, everyone was telling him how great he was because he was blowing people away and making it look easy. It's easy to get drunk on that sort of adulation.

"I don't think I've known a world champion, past or present, that hasn't had a shocker or had a fight before they'd won a world title that they should have won but they looked shocking in, or indeed they've lost it.
