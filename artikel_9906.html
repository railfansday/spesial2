New Release: Tor Browser 16.0

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The ongoing development of Tor Browser is made possible thanks to the support of our community. Every donation helps power the tools that let people browse freely and privately. Consider joining thousands of others who have already made a small contribution towards building a better internet for everyone.

Tor Browser connects millions of people to the Tor network (and by extension, the free and open internet) out of the box. However, for those who need to circumvent advanced censorship, boost their security level, or tailor their browsing experience, clear and easy-to-use Settings are essential. To that end, Tor Browser 16.0 introduces a complete redesign of the Settings experience based on a parallel redesign shipped in Firefox in June. Improvements include a cleaner layout, updated navigation, and clearer labels and descriptions aimed at making settings easier to find, understand, and manage. Other noteworthy changes include:

The Tor Browser manual, which can be found by visiting about:manual or through the application menu (â°) â Help â Tor Browser manual, has also received a complete revamp. Since the release of our new Support portal in 2025, the team has had to maintain two sets of documentation: one for the traditional manual that's available to read in Tor Browser, and another for the new Support site. With this release, we've solved that problem by replacing the older material in the Tor Browser manual with our latest and greatest help content from the new Support site. Like the previous iteration of the manual, the new version is bundled with Tor Browser for Desktop so it can be read offline, which comes in particularly handy when troubleshooting issues that prevent you from connecting to the Tor network.

Finally, after a year of testing, Tor Browser 16.0 now features native support for Linux computers with 64-bit ARM (AArch64) processors, such as Raspberry Pis and Apple Silicon Macs running Linux. If you have been relying on Tor Browser Alpha for support for these devices, we recommend switching over to the stable release channel unless you plan to continue testing Alpha releases. Meanwhile, Tor Browser 16.0 follows Firefox in ending support for 32-bit x86 Linux computers. Fortunately, very few people should be affected, as we currently receive around 200 update checks from this platform a day, down from a peak of around 4,000 in 2019. Most remaining 32-bit users should be able to switch to the 64-bit version unless they're running outdated hardware or versions of Linux.

Tor Browser for Android has also benefited from Mozilla's ongoing work to modernize Firefox, as seen in the Settings redesign on desktop. In this release, we've made significant progress applying the same changes to Tor Browser. Google Play limits the size of apps distributed as APKs to 100 MB. Since Firefox for Android continues to grow with each release, keeping Tor Browser within this limit has been an ongoing challenge â for example, this issue has prevented us from bundling Tor Browser's manual on Android so that it can be accessed offline. Nevertheless, we've found various ways to reduce the size of our Android package over the years and stay within the limit. Most recently, this involved carefully and creatively compressing some of the built-in resources Firefox uses to display its interface.

Tor Browser users can expect additional improvements over the next few months as we continue to refine the new Settings experience for desktop and Material Design 3 for Android. If you find a bug or have a suggestion for how we could improve this release, we'd love to hear your feedback.

The team is also working hard on a new and improved circuit display, coming soon to Tor Browser Alpha, which integrates site information directly into the circuit panel and can visualize more complex multi-path circuits. Multi-path circuits use additional relays to split your traffic into two paths that converge on a single exit, improving both connection speed and reliability. These circuits already exist for regular websites (but not onion sites) and are randomly assigned based on network availability. Although Tor Browser's traditional circuit display can only show a single path at a time, you have likely benefited from a multi-path circuit without even knowing it. What's more, this exciting update will mark the debut of the circuit display on Tor Browser for Android, which has been exclusive to desktop until now.
