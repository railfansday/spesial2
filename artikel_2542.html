Josh Hokit: Ciryl Gane's dirty tactics come from 'a place of fear' — 'coward-based instincts'

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Josh Hokit knew he was in for a war against Ciryl Gane at UFC 334.

Two weeks ago, Hokit competed in the biggest fight of his still-young MMA career. The first-time heavyweight title challenger brought the fight to Gane like few before, pushing an unrelenting pace for 25 minutes. Ultimately, Hokit came up short in the hard-fought battle, losing a unanimous decision that provided many lessons.

On Monday's edition of "The Ariel Helwani Show," Hokit reflected on the first loss of his career, acknowledging his heavy-pressure approach was always the game plan.

"It needed to be ugly," Hokit told Uncrowned. "He's a good athlete. He has a lot of experience, bouncing around, jabbing, teeping, fighting from the outside, where I don't have too much fighting experience. So I needed to cancel things out with just pressure and trusting my defense and my eyes. How else would I have fought him? From the outside? That would have been a f***ing long night for me, where he's just going to slowly pick me apart.

"What surprised me was his mental toughness for a 6-foot-4 athletic dude like that. He didn't quit on himself. A lot of those types of guys, they don't have to make it a dogfight, and that's also their downfall, I believe. He had beginning, middle, and end game. He was doing inside boxing stuff like you see [Alex] Pereira do. But you see Pereira's training, and he's constantly doing that. So in fights it's nothing out of the ordinary, but Ciryl Gane's doing slips and rips, great exits, standing in the pocket as well. That was impressive to look back on. Now, I know."

Hokit, 29, made history in defeat inside Madison Square Garden. Earning Fight of the Night honors, Hokit and Gane threw strikes at such a high frequency that they broke the UFC's single-fight heavyweight records for most combined significant strikes landed (383), as well as most combined total strikes landed (541) and thrown (796). Gane also broke the record for most significant strikes landed by a heavyweight, with 237.

After experiencing what Gane has to offer, Hokit is confident he can make the right adjustments in a rematch, especially since his approach was arguably too broad.

"My mentality and my game plan was literally just going to break him by making it ugly. So that's just a broad game plan," Hokit said.

"I'm convinced I still can break him, but I need to give him more reasons to break. We'll f***ing get him next time."

A topic of discussion around Gane in recent fights has been his continuous fouls, whether intentional or not. In his prior undisputed title bout in October 2025, Gane and former champion Tom Aspinall fought to an early no-contest after Gane's eye-pokes rendered Aspinall unable to continue. The Brit has been sidelined ever since.

Gane won interim gold in his subsequent bout, knocking out Alex Pereira at UFC Freedom 250 in June. Afterward, Pereira openly lambasted Gane for alleged illegal back-of-the-head strikes during the fight's most impactful sequences.

Those fights, in particular, further inflamed a narrative around Gane being a dirty fighter, and more of that showed up early in the Hokit bout with another Gane first-round eye-poke. Hokit says the foul didn't disable him long-term, but admitted a few punches were harder to see shortly afterward.

"I knew what I was getting myself into," Hokit said. "It's ironic that I was joking about it all week and the first 30 seconds, he does that. I was seeing triple. It was blurry, whatever. Maybe it played a factor in the fight, but I don't want to dwell on that.

"It is what it is. I need to blind myself in practice and beat him with no eyes. That was the mentality going in. My only regret is not poking him back. We need to cut off his fingers or something at this point, huh?"

The timing of the foul controversy comes at a unique time for Gane, as MMA's chief officials are seemingly getting more aggressive with taking actions against fouls, whether in the form of points deductions or mid-fight enforcements. Yet despite Gane's questionable history, referee Marc Goddard ignored the call, which surprised Hokit.

"I don't know how Marc Goddard isn't on top of that," Hokit said. "He gave [Gane] the benefit of the doubt. I think it's been a certain amount of fights where he's poked someone in the eye or fouled them in some sense. I said my eye was poked, and he's like, 'No, I didn't see it.'

"I'm like, I think he would give me the benefit of the doubt. But it is what it is, dude. I need to be more prepared. He pokes both of my eyes, I'm still fighting. We're still going to fight, and I'm still going to find a way. A little eye-poke is not going to take me out of the process. That's the mentality, at least."

What surprised Hokit more than Goddard's lack of action on the foul was Gane's continued infractions. At this point, Hokit views it as an approach Gane intentionally utilizes, maybe not so much out of trying to fight dirty, but fear, says Hokit.

Josh Hokit is the latest in a long line of fighters to accuse Ciryl Gane of dirty tactics.

"He just can't help himself. When the bullets are flying, you revert back to fear-based moves, coward-based instincts, where it's like you push away and all this other stuff," Hokit said.

"Maybe that's also his process where he's thinking of getting away from you so sporadically that he doesn't even care that he's going to foul to do it. I don't think he's doing it on purpose, but it's coming from maybe a place of fear. How could someone be so unaware he keeps doing it?

"With Gane it's, 'Everyone has a plan until you get poked in the eye,'" Hokit continued. "I could have done a Tom Aspinall and said I can't continue, ruin the whole event, because I don't think the fights were very good until our fight."

After suffering his first professional loss, Hokit (10-1) will go back to the drawing board and await the outcome of the UFC's upcoming heavyweight schedule. The fight that has most of the division's attention is the clash between the aforementioned Pereira and Sergei Pavlovich at UFC 335 on Dec. 12.

Aspinall also remains a piece in the heavyweight puzzle. UFC CEO Dana White stated after the Gane vs. Hokit bout that Aspinall is still next in line after relinquishing his title, and it just comes down to when he's ready to return.

That leaves Hokit in a tricky position, hoping for a Gane rematch after the fight's competitive nature. He believes the UFC has appreciated his efforts inside and outside of the Octagon enough to make that fight possible. If not, he wants Pereira regardless of the outcome at UFC 335.

"The whole Aspinall thing is interesting. Maybe they promised him his fight when he comes back, but who knows? [For me] it needs to be a [Gane] rematch or Pereira. Simple as that," Hokit said.

"No matter what, I want [Pereira]. He's not in the game forever, and I've got to back up what I've been saying to him. ... If he still feels some type of way, I'm willing to give him an opportunity to fight for his honor, fight for his respect. So we'll see.

"He has one of the biggest names in the sport," Hokit added. "He shook my hand after the [Gane] fight. He said 'respect' and all this other stuff, but it doesn't discredit all the stuff I've said to him. I'm still standing on it, so if he wants to do something about it, let's do it. It's a fight that everybody wants to see. Win or lose [against Pavlovich], I think it wouldn't matter. He has that type of name in the sport, and I want to boost my name off him."
