Bessent said U.S. AI chip policy toward China is unchanged after Trump-Xi talks

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Treasury secretary said Nvidia's H200 is the threshold chip allowed for sale to China, and Jensen Huang was not discussed

Maxim Shemetov / POOL / AFP via Getty Images

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC on Friday that Washington's stance permitting Nvidia $NVDA to sell its H200 processors in China has not shifted since the most recent round of discussions with Beijing.

"We've already agreed that the H200s are the threshold level of chips that could be sold to the Chinese, and, you know, that continues as planned," Bessent said on "Squawk Box." Asked about Nvidia's statement that it has stopped selling H200s to China, he said, "I wouldn't anticipate any change in the policy."

Bessent also said former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang did not come up in the talks. He answered "No" without elaborating when asked.

The interview came after President Donald Trump returned from an Asia trip that, according to CNBC, featured a meeting in Shenzhen, China, with Xi Jinping, China's president, and Vladimir Putin, Russia's president. Bessent said Trump and Xi had a "robust discussion" on trade policy and AI. He said the two leaders settled on keeping a person in the loop whenever AI is used to make military choices, and on barring AI from operating independently in significant armed operations.

Asked whether he and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick are at odds on the forcefulness of the push to restrict exports to China, Bessent said, "I think we're all committed to following President Trump's instructions." He added that "the proper chip restrictions" will be in place and "the remote access has been curtailed." Commerce has moved to shut off one avenue Chinese buyers use to obtain AI processors, according to CNBC.

Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO last week after acknowledging an affair with a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed. U.S. intelligence officials suspect the executive of spying for China. Megaspeed is suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China in breach of U.S. export controls. Nvidia's board named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO.

Bessent said in a CNBC appearance last week that Commerce "has the pen" on adding Megaspeed to the export blacklist. He said the Treasury, State and Defense departments and the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative supported the move. As of last week, Commerce had declined to list the company.

In that Nov. 13  interview with CNBC, Bessent also said Trump had made clear China should not receive Nvidia chips above H100s, and he called any leakage of chips "very frustrating."
