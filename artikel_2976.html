Elon Musk Just Killed the Cybertruck to Build Cybercabs. Here’s What It Means for Tesla Stock -- and Rivian’s.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Tesla's focus on artificial intelligence could open up an opportunity in the premium vehicle market.

After years of hype, the Cybertruck is going out with a whimper. Tesla (TSLA -2.69%) is officially shutting down production of the futuristic-looking truck, replacing it with the autonomous Cybercab in the middle of next year.

The Cybertruck never lived up to the hype, and now Elon Musk and Tesla are pivoting to new, potentially more promising vehicles. Tesla may benefit from better utilization of factory lines and growing its self-driving subscription services. However, there is one stock that may benefit even more as Tesla shifts its strategic priorities.

That stock is Rivian Automotive (RIVN -0.66%).

When it entered production, Tesla planned to build 125,000 Cybertrucks a year and has the factory capacity to do so. You may have noticed there are not many Cybertrucks on the road today.

Tesla decided to shut down the project entirely, with the company likely losing money on every Cybertruck sold.

Luckily for Tesla, it won't have a large impact on 2027 deliveries, as it wasn't pumping out many in the first place. Now, Tesla will try to repurpose these factory lines for the Cybercab, its futuristic self-driving car that it wants to operate as a fleet of robotaxis in cities around the globe.

If Tesla can ramp up production of the Cybercab, it will gain leverage over its idle Cybertruck factory. Plus, more Cybercabs in circulation mean more potential to sell its high-margin self-driving software and earn fees through robotaxi trips. One of the factors holding back Tesla stock is low profit margins, with its operating margin down to 3.5% over the last 12 months.

The Cybertruck was supposed to be a premium off-road vehicle designed for electric vehicle shoppers. This is the exact market Rivian Automotive is trying to serve.

It is the second-largest pure-play electric vehicle (EV) company from the United States, operating at the premium end of the market. However, with Tesla dominating the EV market, demand has remained low for many years now.

To fix this, Rivian is now producing a five-seat SUV called the R2, which is more affordable than its first trucks and SUVs. The R2 is selling for just under $60,000, which Rivian hopes will reinvigorate growth and lead to positive profitability.

Last quarter, Rivian's deliveries reached an all-time high of 19,000, with hopes they can keep scaling in the quarters ahead as customers buy the R2. With the Cybertruck out of the way, maybe that will also impact growth. This will ideally finally lead to profits and positive cash flow.

Right now, Rivian is unprofitable. It also burned $1 billion in free cash flow last quarter alone. Compared with about $5.5 billion in cash on the balance sheet, Rivian has only a few quarters at its current burn rate to reach positive free cash flow. It does have major partners like Volkswagen and Amazon, but it still eventually needs to be a business that generates cash.

If the R2 proves successful, Rivian stock could be a good buy. It has a market cap of $18 billion, compared with trailing revenue of $6.5 billion. Scaling the R2 to much larger deliveries could drive revenue into the tens of billions of dollars, making this trailing market cap seem rather cheap. Still, it is unclear exactly how profitable Rivian could be at scale, as the company has always been a money loser.

Rivian may see a nice growth spurt in the coming quarters, but the automotive business is quite difficult, making it a graveyard for investment theses. Don't add Rivian stock to your portfolio despite the R2's initial growth. Your portfolio will thank you for it.
