Go from a one-sentence request to a small, tested program without guessing what the business meant.

Part 1 of the "Zero to Full-Stack .NET" series. Section: Getting Started.

Level: Beginner · Prerequisites: A basic .NET developer environment installed and a willingness to ask questions before coding · Reading time: 10 min

The product owner stops by your desk: "Can you send a reminder to customers whose invoices are overdue?" She leaves. You open the editor and stare at an empty file. Where does the code even start? What is an invoice in this system? What does overdue mean? Email or SMS?

Many beginners start typing anyway. Two hours later there is a loop, a half-written email template, and a growing feeling that the thing being built is not the thing that was asked for. The problem is not your C#. The problem is that the request was never turned into something precise enough to code.

This article walks through that translation with a real example, ends with a console app you can run, and then covers the three skills that make the difference on day one: reading an error, using the debugger, and searching for the right thing.
