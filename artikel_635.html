Sequels, Subtitles and Spring Releases: How the Best Picture Race Is Rewriting the Academy’s Playbook

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

A preview of the best picture race with 'The Odyssey,' 'Project Hail Mary,' 'You Can See Everything' and more.

Somebody at every awards season party, and everybody on the internet, will tell you why a movie can’t grab a best picture nomination — or even win the prize. It opened too early. It’s a sequel. It has subtitles. It made no money. It’s a documentary.

And then the Academy goes and proves everyone wrong.

With the fall festivals in the rearview mirror and the holiday corridor still loading, it’s time to take a look at the best picture race. Christopher Nolan’s “The Odyssey” holds the top spot on Variety’s best picture chart and nearly every pundit’s list. But the margin between No. 1 and No. 20 feels thinner than it has in years. A24 has many films in the projected lineup. Warner Bros., the reigning champion after its “One Battle After Another” win, with a new boss at the helm of the company, Skydance CEO David Ellison, is keeping Denis Villeneuve’s franchise finale, “Dune: Part Three,” close to the vest until December.

And we’re still waiting for Robert Eggers’ “Werwulf,” with Aaron Taylor-Johnson. That film could shake up the race once voters finally get a look at it, and, for argument’s sake, so can “Avengers: Doomsday” from the Russo brothers.

That leaves 20 to 30 films chasing 10 slots, each making its strongest case for why it belongs. Standing in the way are the old rules, and this season is poised to test every one of them.

Nolan has already broken this rule himself. Universal Pictures’ “Oppenheimer” opened in July 2023 and won seven Oscars the following March. Three years later, “The Odyssey,” the adaptation of Homer’s Greek epic, has the footprint that can lead the nominations field, with an eye on the record 16 nominations “Sinners” set last season. As Odysseus, Matt Damon anchors the voyage home; co-stars Anne Hathaway and Samantha Morton are both tracking for supporting actress recognition, while John Leguizamo and Robert Pattinson are legitimate supporting actor threats.

The bigger question is whether five months of fresher challengers will wear it down.

That’s because only two have. “The Godfather Part II” (1974) followed the 1973 best picture winner, “The Godfather,” and “The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King” (2003) remains the only sequel to win after its earlier chapters fell short. The latter provides the playbook for Warner Bros.’ “Dune: Part Three,” which arrives after Villeneuve was left out of the directing lineup for both previous installments. Almost nobody has seen the finale, but Zendaya’s expanded role gives it an acting hook the earlier chapters lacked.

Further down the list, Netflix’s “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” tests the same rule, with David Fincher directing a Quentin Tarantino script based on his 2019 hit “Once Upon a Time … in Hollywood.”

SUBTITLES AND STREAMERS MUST CLEAR HURDLES

Bong Joon Ho’s “Parasite” remains the only non-English-language film to win best picture. Netflix is still waiting for its first, after coming painfully close with “Roma” (2018) and “Emilia Pérez” (2024). Enter the Cannes sensation “La Bola Negra,” the streamer’s Spanish-language drama from Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi. It has Penélope Cruz in the supporting actress conversation, singer-turned-actor Guitarricadelafuente emerging as one of the season’s breakout performers and an international feature campaign supported by a passionate base. It has the texture of a film that could finally get Netflix a win.

Neon won with “Parasite” and “Anora,” both Palme d’Or winners, and it has this year’s Palme winner, too: Cristian Mungiu’s drama “Fjord,” starring Sebastian Stan and Renate Reinsve. But its slot is not a sure thing in the best picture race with any number of international contenders waiting for a late surge, including Lee Chang-dong’s “Possible Love” from South Korea, Andrey Zvyagintsev’s “Minotaur” from Mubi and Ryusuke Hamaguchi’s “All of a Sudden,” also from Neon.

Then again, not all of these comedies are funny. Martin McDonagh is back for another round with “Wild Horse Nine,” following “The Banshees of Inisherin” (2022), which went home empty-handed despite nine nominations. John Malkovich and Sam Rockwell deliver the kind of performances that could get the actors’ branch buzzing.

A24 is doubling down on laughs. “The Invite” has director and star Olivia Wilde vying for at least two nominations, along with her ensemble. Jesse Eisenberg’s “The Debut” features best actress front-runner Julianne Moore and Paul Giamatti, who is tracking for his third Oscar nom and possible first win. The indie label also has “Club Kid,” from debut director Jordan Firstman, looking for awards season love after declaring itself a drama for the Golden Globes.

Meanwhile, Mike Leigh’s “Tender Loving Care,” from Bleecker Street, is entering the Golden Globes race as a comedy, but is more likely to leave audiences in tears than have them laughing.

DOCUMENTARIES AND CARTOONS ARE SPILLING OUT OF THEIR OWN CATEGORIES

No documentary has ever been nominated for best picture, and three animated films have, with none winning. A24’s “You Can See Everything,” from Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, looks more like a sure thing by the day after winning over audiences at industry screenings. It doesn’t hurt that it has become one of the biggest-grossing documentaries in decades.

Laika’s stop-motion epic “Wildwood” is the rare animated feature with a credible best picture argument. Voters have shown they’ll go there when the craft is undeniable.

“Everything Everywhere All at Once” opened in March and swept the Oscars the following year. Amazon MGM’s “Project Hail Mary” opened in March, too, and Phil Lord and Christopher Miller’s adaptation of Andy Weir’s novel is the crowd-pleaser of this race. Crowd-pleasers can survive the preferential ballot, especially with Ryan Gosling firmly in the best actor conversation.

Antoine Fuqua’s “Michael,” from Lionsgate, opened in April and became the highest-grossing biopic of all time. Jaafar Jackson hopes to crack the best actor race for playing his uncle, Michael Jackson, while Colman Domingo’s turn as patriarch Joe Jackson is in the supporting actor conversation.

Don’t tell the actors’ branch. It’s the Academy’s largest, and it has a way of carrying its favorites across the categories. Neon’s “Artificial” has the supporting actor front-runner in Andrew Garfield’s portrayal of OpenAI CEO Sam Altman. Focus Features acquired “Elsinore” over the summer and hopes Andrew Scott’s towering performance as Ian Charleson can carry it into the best picture race.

Pedro Pascal in Tony Gilroy’s “Behemoth!” and Inde Navarrette in the horror breakout “Obsession” are among the likeliest performers to pull their films into other categories. The same goes for newcomer Anthony Ippolito, so authentic as Sylvester Stallone in Peter Farrelly’s “I Play Rocky” — it’s the kind of underdog story voters love. John Turturro’s performance in “The Only Living Pickpocket in New York” from Sony Pictures Classics is also in the best actor conversation.

A few others are flying under the radar but still looking for a defining moment this season. Sumerian Pictures’ “Josephine” won the Grand Jury Prize at Sundance, while Apple’s “Being Heumann” finished as the second runner-up for the People’s Choice Award at TIFF.

The lesson of the past few seasons is that the race is never settled in November. It just looks that way.
