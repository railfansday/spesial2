Kid Rock’s Nashville Bar Sued After Bouncers Allegedly Beat Patron Who ‘Tapped’ Guitar

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A Michigan man is suing Kid Rock’s Big Ass Honky Tonk Rock N’ Roll Steakhouse in Nashville with claims he was beaten by bouncers at the venue last December.

Plaintiff Joshua Welch claims he was celebrating his recent college graduation with friends at the multilevel bar when he passed close to the stage and “in a moment of poor judgment, lightly tapped the performer’s guitar with the tip of his finger.” The contact was “a light nose-tap,” his lawsuit obtained by Rolling Stone alleges, but a burly security guard quickly confronted him and started to escort him out.

“As Mr. Welch and Bouncer #1 again passed the live-music area on the way to the elevator, Mr. Welch attempted to touch the guitar a second time. At that point, Bouncer #1 became disproportionately agitated. He began pushing Mr. Welch aggressively toward the elevator,” the lawsuit, which was filed in March but only surfaced on Tuesday in a TMZ report, reads.

Welch claims two other bouncers joined in, with one allegedly slamming him against an elevator wall and pressing his arm against Welch’s neck “with significant force.” Once on the ground floor, the bouncers allegedly refused to let Welch leave and instead repeatedly shoved him to the floor, injuring his hand and finger so badly that he needed surgery, the lawsuit claims.

“Each time Mr. Welch attempted to stand up, he was forcefully shoved back down to the ground,” the filing claims. “It was as if they were using a show of force for some sick personal gratification.”

The lawsuit includes claims for assault and battery, negligence, and false imprisonment. Kid Rock, born Robert Ritchie, is not an individual defendant, only his venue. But the lawsuit claims the alleged acts “were performed by agents/employees of Kid Rock, individually and/or collectively, for the benefit of the defendant and the advancement of their business interests.”

Lawyers on both sides did not immediately respond to requests for comment. In a formal answer filed with the court, the bar said its bouncers “were justified in the use of force, if any, to respond to the violence initiated by plaintiff.” It said it had a right to protect employees and patrons from Welch’s “aggressive actions.” A case management hearing is set for Jan. 11.
