Watch Jesse Welles Cover Black Sabbath for ‘Austin City Limits’

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

If protest songs require a ready audience, Jesse Welles certainly had a hungry one on his episode of Austin City Limits, set to air in February. The “War Isn’t Murder” singer made his ACL debut with an hour-long set featuring hits from his now-Grammy nominated album Masks Off. This included a high-octane performance of Black Sabbath’s iconic “War Pigs” as well as his record’s titular song for the Feb. 13 special, which is premiering exclusively on Rolling Stone.

The performance took place under a set of cascading powerlines, a nod to his track-heavy compilations Under the Powerlines, and included nods to the political nature of Welles’ most popular work — like the rough shape of the U.S. Capitol Building in the background. “In the fields, the bodies burning / As the war machine keeps turning,” Welles crooned to synchronized electric guitars. “Death and hatred to mankind / Poisoning their brainwashed minds / Oh, Lord, yeah!”

Welles also blasted out his anti-MAGA folk rock anthem “Masks Off,” just one in his stacked lineup of Americana-focused protest songs including “War Isn’t Murder” and “The Nerd Reich.” “Killing people from a distance in an instant / At the insistence of a president / With a blackmail gun to his head / For the fun that he had in the Dark Ages / Pages and pages and pages and pages / They thought they’d get away with it,” he sang before landing the song’s deceptively simple chorus. “All the masks are off / And they’re getting lazy / They think you’re so damn stupid / They think you’re so damn crazy.”

News of the special comes days after Welles was nominated five times at 2027 Grammys, for Best American Roots Song, Best Americana Album, Best American Roots Performance, Best Americana Performance, and Best Traditional Folk Album. It’s the second nomination year for Welles, who started as a viral sensation on TikTok before getting the stamp of approval from massive voices in the folk and Americana scene like Joan Baez, Dave Matthews, Nathaniel Rateliff, and Margo Price.

When he spoke to Rolling Stone in 2025, Welles wasn’t specific about political parties or even politicians, but said that his political music was a way for him to grapple with the overwhelming wave of news happening all around him. “There’s a lot of people protesting different things in their own way, which is a beautiful thing about America, and something we can’t forget — that we have the great gift to be able to sing these songs, and I don’t have to worry about my life,” he said at the time. “No one will come and kill me.”

Welles’ ACL special will be available to watch on PBS and ACL’s YouTube page, premiering Feb. 13.
