Sam Altman On ‘Artificial’ Movie About Him — Not Among “Ten Weirdest Things In My Life” Right Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

“If you had told me twenty years ago that someone was going to make a movie about me, that would have struck me as a really weird thing. Now it doesn’t feel like it’s in the top ten weirdest things in my life,” said Sam Altman, asked about the upcoming film Artificial.

“What is?” asked Andrew Ross Sorking during a Q&A with the OpenAI co-founder and CEO at the New York Times DealBook Summit Wednesday

“The fact that we are ushering in this technology that we have all of these very real concerns about, and that we find ourselves in a role of such responsibility. That is a crazy situation to find yourself in,” said Altman. Neon is releasing the Luca Guadagnino film about OpenAI and Altman, who is played by Andrew Garfield, in December. It’s not complimentary to the executive. Garfield, speaking at Artificial’s world premiere at the New York Film Festival earlier this fall, said the project was meant to question “who we are giving all of our autonomy, power, and data to.” Watch on Deadline

Altman appeared with the company’s president and co-founder Greg Brockman. With public fear of AI mounting as new models occasionally go rogue and distaste with data centers a flashpoint of the recent midterm elections, they both agreed that the past year has been a key transitional phase in the industry, and that the last few months have been especially difficult.

“We are we are very clearly in the new phase, and I think we will have to, as we’ve had to in other new phases, operate differently for this new phase. We’ve been talking a lot about what’s going to happen or what needs to happen,” Altman said.

“I think if the world and the companies developing AI treated the next four years like they’ve treated the last four years, that would be very bad. We have not been doing that. We have been some of the loudest voices” about the increasingly high stakes of artificial intelligence.

He believes “the world needs a clear plan from the companies” in the space.

The sector was stressed most recently by the ouster of its rock star, Nvidia founder and CEO Jensen Huang, who had a relationship with a tech executive alleged to be a Chinese spy. A bizarre series of back-to-back outages across sectors from Microsoft email to aviation to banking over the past week, although not necessarily AI related, has exacerbated fears of global dependence on a handful of big tech players. Meanwhile, investors have become jittery over whether massive capital spending by so-called AI hyper-scalers will result in profits, or a potentially devastating stock market bubble.

OpenAI rival Anthropic is set to go public this week, a major test of investor sentiment on the business. OpenAI itself had plans for an IPO. They have been delayed, although Altman said today he believes it will be ultimately be a publicly traded company.
