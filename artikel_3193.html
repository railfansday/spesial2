Banks and the NSA reportedly blame US outages on patches for AI-found flaws

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Rushed patches for AI-found flaws overwhelmed systems, sources told The Washington Sun. Senators and a new House bill now point at the speed of AI bug hunting.

United States Capitol, First Street Southeast, Washington, DC, USA

Wall Street executives and the NSA have told members of Congress that this week’s US tech outages came from rushed fixes for AI-found flaws. The flaws were found by AI models that banks and agencies now use on their own systems. Three people familiar with the private talks told The Washington Sun’s Jeff Stein. TNW has not independently verified the report.

The fixes temporarily overwhelmed the systems, according to the sources. Banks and agencies feel pressure to close gaps fast, the report said. They know attackers have similar AI tools.

“These models are identifying massive vulnerabilities, and that increases the urgency to patch right away,” a congressional official told the paper.

The official added that organisations were trying to push through a lot of patches all at once.

Since last Thursday, a Microsoft 365 outage hit email for millions, and the FAA halted flights nationwide. Chase customers had trouble reaching their money. The NSA’s classified network went down, and Apple suffered an iCloud and Apple Pay outage.

None of the companies or agencies involved has called any of it an attack. Most blamed system upgrades that went wrong, The Wall Street Journal’s Robert McMillan reported. Administration officials say the outages are not connected.

The patch load has grown fast. Last month, Microsoft’s monthly update fixed about 1,400 security flaws, a record, the Journal reported. A year earlier, it fixed about 170.

Senator Mark Warner, the top Democrat on the Senate intelligence committee, made the same case in a post on X.

“AI is making it possible to identify vulnerabilities across enormous numbers of systems at unprecedented speed,” Warner wrote.

Senators Maria Cantwell and Mike Rounds asked Jay Clayton for a national response to AI-enabled cyber threats. Clayton is the director of national intelligence and also chairs the White House’s Super Intelligence Task Force.

“The fundamental problem is that AI can discover software weaknesses and help attackers exploit them faster than many organizations can respond,” they wrote in a letter.

In the House, Representatives Morgan Luttrell and Ritchie Torres introduced the bipartisan ROOT CAUSE Act on Wednesday, Nextgov reported. It would set up a board inside CISA to investigate major incidents. That includes faulty updates, even with no sign of an attack.

Christiaan Beek of the security firm Rapid7 told Bloomberg he sees no public evidence that rushed security updates caused the outages.

“Complex systems fail in boring ways far more often than because of an adversary,” Beek said.

The FAA’s ground stop began as a contractor replaced old copper wiring, and officials have tied it to a single router. Microsoft blamed a technical defect in its own outage. The NSA has said routine maintenance caused its network problems.
