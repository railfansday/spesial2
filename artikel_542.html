Best Sunrise Alarm Clock (2026): Hatch, Lumie

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

With darker days ahead, waking up in the morning can be rough. If you never feel rested when your alarm goes off, a sunrise alarm clock could change your life. The gradually brightening light signals that it’s time to wake up instead of beeps and harsh, unnatural tones. I’m a chronic snooze-button kind of gal, and I’ve found a sunrise clock, or wake-up light, incredibly helpful for getting up when I need to and for supporting a healthier circadian rhythm. Plus, it’s much harder to ignore than a phone alarm that I can simply snooze.

If you want to face dark winter mornings by waking up more gently and naturally, using a sunrise alarm clock might just improve your mood. We recommend the newly released Hatch Sleep Clock if you want a sunrise alarm and sleep tracker in one machine, as well as a personal favorite, the Lumie Bodyclock Luxe 700FM.

Here are our favorite sunrise alarm clocks, each tested for up to a week.

Your body is built to wake naturally with the sunrise and feel sleepy as the sun sets. The light at both the beginning and end of the day is warm and golden, unlike the bright, slightly blue midday sunlight that keeps you wide awake. A sunrise alarm clock mimics this transition from morning to bright daytime light, helping signal your body to wake up (or fall asleep, if the clock also has a sunset option).

A sunrise alarm has a built-in light you can set to brighten at a certain time. It doesn’t happen instantly; it will slowly transition over a period of anywhere from 10 minutes to an hour, and better sunrise alarms let you choose your exact brightening window. I usually choose a half-hour window for the light to start brightening, and let the gradually brightening light wake me.

There are also sound effects. Sunrise alarms feature a mix of relaxing and classic sounds that usually start when the light begins to brighten, helping to rouse you. I’d prefer the light to go first, with music activating later, but that isn’t always an option with these lights.

Some sunrise alarms also offer a sunset routine, meaning you can turn them on at night and they'll slowly dim to darkness to help you feel sleepy. It’s another good visual cue for your brain and body that it’s time for bed, and I prefer sunrise alarms like Hatch and Lumie that let you choose when to activate a routine (for example, pressing a button starts the routine over the course of 30 minutes) rather than needing to be in bed by a set time to use the routine.

Ultimately, these lights can do a lot. They can double as a sound machine, help you wake up and fall asleep, and even serve as a regular bedside lamp if they’re bright enough. Not all sunrise alarms have all of these features, though, so you have to decide how much you want to spend and which features are most important to you.

You might see a range of features listed for a sunrise alarm, and more expensive models will include more of these than cheaper models. If you’re not sure which features you want, try this series of questions to figure out which features you need.

Do you struggle to fall asleep? Splurge on a sunrise alarm with a nighttime or wind-down routine. These help establish a routine that makes it easier to fall asleep.

Do you need one device that doubles as an alarm and a bedside lamp? Get a brighter sunrise clock with easy controls to turn it on as a bedside lamp. Not all sunrise clocks have these features, so check the details carefully (and reviews like mine!) and note that cheaper, smaller sunrise alarm clocks usually won’t brighten an entire bedroom.

Are you picky about your alarm sounds? Check how many sounds are offered. Just about every sunrise clock has some sound machine features and options, but cheaper models tend to offer only a couple of sounds and might not have the sound you’re looking for.

Do you want app control? Some options in this guide don’t include a companion app or Wi-Fi capabilities, especially some of my favorites. An app doesn’t necessarily make a sunrise clock better, but it can be convenient to use. If you prefer an app to set up your sunrise lamp, try Casper, Hatch, and Loftie.

Do Sunrise Alarms Work for Heavy Sleepers?

Yes! I'm a heavy sleeper in the morning, and these can still help me wake up. You might want to choose the most gradual increase in brightness on the sunrise lamp you choose, so the slow brightening of the room can better rouse you from that deep sleep.

What’s the Difference Between a Sunrise Clock and a SAD Lamp?

These two gadgets might look similar on the outside, but sunrise clocks and other wake-up-style lights are designed to wake you up, while a SAD (seasonal affective disorder) lamp is meant to simulate sunlight during the day. SAD lamps provide light therapy to help combat seasonal depression during the darker months, while sunrise alarms are just meant to make the morning wake-up less rough. Both can help regulate your circadian rhythm, but in different ways.

How Does WIRED Choose Sunrise Clocks to Test?

We research all models in advance to determine which are a fit for testing. We ensure they have wake-up features. (Some SAD lamps look similar to a sunrise alarm, for example, but if there’s no gradual sunrise to wake up to, they don’t qualify for testing with this guide.) We also test a variety of price points and additional features to see what’s really worth buying. We make an effort to select both more affordable options with fewer features and high-end options to compare and contrast in our testing rounds.

I tested each recommended sunrise clock on my bedside table in a room with blackout curtains to see how much it could brighten the room over the course of its sunrise routine. I slept with each model for anywhere between one and seven nights, depending on its performance. I also tried out the various sound options each machine had to offer to test the speakers and see how pleasant the sounds were for waking up or falling asleep. If there was a nighttime routine option, I tested that too.
