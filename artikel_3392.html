‘Rivals’ Stars Confirm Rupert and Taggie’s Happy Ending Won’t Happen the Way You Think

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Editor's Note: The following contains spoilers for Episode 10 of Rivals Season 2.

For fans of Jilly Cooper's Rutshire Chronicles books, the Hunt Ball in Rivals is likely one of the most anticipated moments of Season 2. Episode 10 not only sees the show finally revealing who the mole has been this entire time, but it brings everything to a head. Whether it's Maud (Victoria Smurfit) and Declan's (Aidan Turner) chaotic marriage or Daysee's (Lara Peake) PTSD coming to a head, the show has reached a tipping point. No where is this more true than with our biggest slow-burn couple: Rupert (Alex Hassell) and Taggie (Bella Maclean).

Rupert gets a rude awakening at the ball when he sees Taggie arriving on Bas' (Luca Pasqualino) arm, dressed to the nines, as his date for the evening. What follows is a funny but ultimately painful montage of Rupert watching as Taggie dances with every man at the ball but him. Following a caustic argument where he declares her a "tart," Rupert ends up chainsmoking at Bas' and watches as he and Taggie head into his flat. Then everything cuts to black just as Bas pushes the straps of Taggie's dress off of her shoulders.

Hassell, Maclean, and Pasqualino sat down with Collider to break down the complicated love triangle between Rupert, Taggie and Bas. Pasqualino discusses the bet Bas made over Taggie in Episode 9 while also sharing the note executive producer Alex Lamb gave him on Bas and Taggie's final scene in Episode 10. Maclean discusses how Taggie has been pushed to question her love for Rupert after multiple rejections. Hassell reveals how Taggie's love has changed his perspective in Season 2. The two actors also get into where the relationship has to go after Episode 10 and how things might not play out as fans would expect.

During the Hunt Ball, Taggie and Rupert get into this argument on the dance floor, and I like that Taggie pushes back when they're sparring and talking. I like this confident version of Taggie that we really kind of haven't seen so much before. Bella, do you think, even though this experience has been very painful for her, that she's gained a lot of self-confidence out of it?

BELLA MACLEAN: Definitely. I mean, we were just saying, Rupert's almost shot himself in the foot because he encouraged Taggie to stand up for herself, and by doing that, she stands up to him. She basically makes the decision, like, 'Is this worth it? Like, you really are starting to treat me... I've had your back for so much of this, but now, actually, is this worth it anymore? You're getting more and more closed off by me, and actually, in reality, you're hurting me. I don't know if this is right.' I think she really, really, really questions her love for Rupert in that moment; it's the first time you see it crack.

I think it's interesting that in that scene, I feel like it's a callback to all of those other moments in the previous episodes. Alex, do you think Rupert's aware that in trying not to hurt Taggie, he's hurting her more by pushing her away? How much pain is he willing to put himself through during all this?

MACLEAN: And Taggie? Sorry, I just think that's such a good question.

ALEX HASSELL: Yeah, no, that's really, really good. That's really good. I think he's quite blinkered in that. I don't think he realizes he's hurting her. I think he is so consumed with his own feelings for her, and it's so difficult for him to take what he sees as the right path, which is to stay away from her, that he's probably unable to see that she has feelings for him in that way. He kind of knows, but I think he's trying hard not to know. Yeah, it's painful stuff. But he really believes in her, and his belief in her and her goodness and his desire for her to be happy means that, in his mind, he should stay away from her.

Yeah, I mean, seeing him seeing him chain smoking in a car outside while he's watching the love of his life with Bas is probably a little bit too much self-flagellating even for me, personally, but I guess he's willing to go through it, right?

HASSELL: [Laughs] He's a perv. He's not the most emotionally developed of people. He's trying. He's trying to see his emotions clearly and be reflective, but he still has a lot of repressed emotions and is very angry and very blinkered, and it comes out in slightly odd ways.

In Episode 9, Luca, Bas makes a bet with the twins on how soon he can get Taggie into bed. How much of that was the impetus for him to push to sleep with Taggie on the night of the Hunt Ball?

PASQUALINO: I don't feel like they're mutually connected, really. I feel like when Bas makes that bet with the twins, I think he's acting out of bravado, really. Because I feel like he's had his nose put out joint a little bit when Taggie declines his offer to go to the Hunt Ball with him, so I feel like that was his way of trying to one-up himself to make it not look like he's just been rejected, I guess. When he does that, I don't really feel like he totally means it, and yeah, after the Hunt Ball, I think Taggie goes back to his place with him, but I don't really know if there was any intention before they got there that that was going to happen.

I was given a great note by Alex Lamb when we were shooting that. Do you remember? It felt a bit friendly at first, and then Alex Lamb, he came up, and he said, "The wolf is in the hen house," and I think that was just his way of saying, like, just ramp up the sort of, like... What's the word?

PASQUALINO: The predatory nature of it, and I think we got it, but I don't know if the bet and Taggie going back to his place after the Hunt Ball are totally connected, personally.

Alex, I think one of the most devastating moments of this season is seeing Rupert's last moments with Monica. I was sobbing while watching it, and I think it's interesting that when every single woman is talking to him, they're always saying, like, "You're gonna hurt somebody. You're gonna hurt somebody, and you only know how to break hearts." For you as an actor, how do you think Taggie changes Rupert and how he approaches love with her?

HASSELL: She seems sort of enduringly hopeful and believing about his innate goodness, and actually, we see a lot of that goodness in this part of the season. He is actually very, very loyal to people. He becomes a port in a storm for Sarah Stratton, which lots of people would find that a very complicated thing, considering what their relationship has cost them both. He's trying really, really hard, particularly with the women — lots of whom are exes in his relationships. [Laughs]

But I think he actually, oddly, is trying to see himself as having the potential to be as good as Taggie thinks he might be able to be. And I think being repeatedly told that he is not actually makes him continue to try and change and evaluate as much as he is able, as a person who is, as I've said, not deeply evolved in being able to see himself as easily as people who, in a modern world, might have been through lots of therapy and that kind of stuff. But he's not had the support system in his life in that way.

Looking forward, I haven't seen Episodes 11 and 12 yet, but Bella, what, for you, do you think would convince Taggie to take Rupert back? Because I do feel like she's done everything she can to be like, "I'm here, I want you," and he keeps saying, "No, no, no." What do you think is going to be the force that makes her be like, "Okay, yes, we can be together?" because I feel like he should do some groveling.

MACLEAN: Yeah, he should. Yeah, I mean, he really, really should. I'm trying to think about what you should do. Sorry, I'm just thinking about... I think Taggie actually has been so available this whole time, and actually it's when she really distances herself from him, and actually, you know that that time when we shot those scenes and I walked over to you and I was like, "I need you to win?"

MACLEAN: You know, I really felt like that was when she actually put the wall up with you and actually showed you, you know, "Fine. You've closed this off," and I think I wonder whether that— This is not what you're asking me, sorry, I'm actually genuinely thinking about it. Because, basically, there isn't really anything Taggie can do too much; it's Rupert [who is] pushing her away. For her, what I would say is her interest in Bas, and also her going, "Well, fuck it, fine. I'm not gonna stay available to you anymore," I think it's the catalyst for Rupert in being like, "Oh god, I've been pushing you away so much. I don't know if I actually want to do this." You know, maybe I wonder for you...

There are worse things in store than laxatives and escaping naked out of a bathroom window.

HASSELL: I was just thinking about that and thinking about some of the things that we know happens and the sort of twists and turns of it all. And one thing that I think is really superb about Jilly's writing and about the writers and the way that the show is written is that things don't happen in the way that you think they will from a narrative, romantic/thrilling thing. You think someone is going to have to, you know...

HASSELL: ...make up for this thing or this twist is going to happen, and life often is not like that. It's not as clean as that—

HASSELL: —characters are not as self-aware as that. And actually, often you are in love with someone, and you just try and work it out. And it's not the best romantic version of, you know, happily ever after. Life and relationships are, if anything, this show is talking about how complicated they are. No matter what your situation is, no matter whether you're married, whether you've got kids, whether you haven't, whether you're trying to do that, it's tough.
