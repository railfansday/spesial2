Netflix’s Next Big Political Thriller Brings Back An Iconic Spy Trend

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

While Netflix’s next major thriller series won’t arrive until December, the show has already managed to revive a TV trend that hasn’t been seen in action since the 2010s. Like every other industry, TV and streaming go through trend cycles wherein certain types of shows will be popular for a stretch of time, only to fade from public relevance after enough titles try to replicate their appeal.

Although The Big Bang Theory’s historic popularity briefly revived the traditional multi-camera sitcom formula, complete with laugh track and limited locations, viewers would be hard-pressed to find more than a handful of ongoing shows fitting this description in 2026. Similarly, the popularity of the Yellowstone franchise saw a string of expensive Western shows green lit at Netflix and elsewhere, but series like The Abandons and American Primeval ended as fast as they began.

As such, it shouldn’t come as much of a surprise that, despite the popularity of ABC’s addictive political thriller Scandal, the political thriller as a subgenre has taken a backseat to the more traditional spy thriller since 2018. Both the preponderance of real-life political scandals and corruption, often more absurd than its fictional counterparts, and the lack of winning spy stories on the big screen seemingly fueled the comeback of grittier spy stories on TV and streaming. However, Netflix’s Trinity now looks set to buck this trend.

Created by Line of Duty’s Jed Mercurio and directed by TV veteran Michael Cuesta, Trinity stars Richard Madden as Secretary of Defense Webb Preston and Gugu Mbatha-Raw as Commander Katherine Decker. A decorated naval commander, the conflicted Decker’s personal connection with the Defense Secretary leads her to fear that he is part of a conspiracy to take down the government from the inside.

As this summary implies, Trinity represents a clever hybrid of traditional spy thrillers like Prime Video’s Tom Clancy series Jack Ryan and the iconic 2010s political thriller vibe shared by House of Cards, Madam Secretary, Designated Survivor, Scandal, and arguably even the comedic satire Veep. Decker’s role as Trinity’s protagonist ensures the show has a direct link to the military, but Madden’s Preston instead occupies the glitzy, exclusive world of elite Washington power players.

Recent spy thrillers shifted their focus to field work rather than the glamorous world of politics. From CIA to Lioness, The Agency, Ponies, The Copenhagen Test, The Asset, Mr. & Mrs. Smith, Jack Ryan, Black Doves, Slow Horses, and The Night Agent, the genre has focused on shows about the hard work of espionage in an attempt to give spy stories a sense of comparative realism.

One factor that may have contributed to this existential shift is the failure of a string of high-profile spy movies, almost all of which went with a campier, more glamorized and romanticized take on spy craft. After the underperformance of movies like 2015’s The Man from U.N.C.L.E, Argyle, Black Bag, Operation Fortune: Ruse de Guerre, and The Ministry of Ungentlemanly Warfare, perhaps it is unsurprising that Mobland’s Guy Ritchie returned to gangsters and spy shows returned to a more grounded, less explosive image of espionage.

That said, Trinity proves that there is now an emergent move away from this shift. Netflix’s hit The Diplomat paved the way for the upcoming Netflix show much like Black Doves season 2 married off its heroine Helen off to the Prime Minister, pulling its characters into the glitzy world of political elites for a subtle genre shift from spy thriller to political thriller.

For almost a decade, the political thriller appeared to have fallen out of favor with audiences as viewers flocked to more realistic, less flashy depictions of spies on the small screen. However, Netflix’s Trinity may prove viewers are ready for a comeback from this iconic genre.
