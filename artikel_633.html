Inside the ‘Pillsbury Bake-Off Classic’: A Recipe for Blending Brands With TV (and a Full-Circle Career Moment for Host Drew Barrymore)

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The folks behind CBS‘ Pillsbury Bake-Off Classic are hoping to make this holiday special an annual Thanksgiving week tradition.

“I feel like this is going to become part of our cultural tradition,” says Scott Weintrob, founder of Studio Large Eyes and executive producer of the special. “Every Thanksgiving for the next 10 years, with the Pillsbury Bake-Off as the trigger to Thanksgiving week.”

The show is actually a collaboration between CBS and General Mills — which, incidentally, owns the “Bake-Off” trademark in the United States. (“Which is why they cannot call The Great British Bake Off the Bake Off in the U.S. and they have to call it The Great British Baking Show,” says Lisa Balzo, head of brand studio partnerships at General Mills.) In developing the special, Weintrob drew on his background in advertising, which has included award-winning work with Rihanna (he designed and directed Amazon’s Emmy-winning Savage x Fenty show) along with Lewis Hamilton x Ferris Bueller’s Day Off, reimagining the film’s classic valet scene with Lewis Hamilton, Edward Norton and Ferrari, which went viral. (He also recently directed and produced Mr. T: I Pity the Fool for Netflix and created Orlando Bloom: To the Edge for Peacock.)

Weintrob’s company, Studio Large Eyes, aims to create brand-inspired entertainment with the philosophy “entertainment first, marketing second.” He says his background crossing both advertising and TV gives him a unique perspective to understand both parties and what their objectives are. “They are quite different,” he says. “And they have different approaches. Being able to talk to networks and streamers and also being able to talk to global brands. I had this in-between knowledge because they are different in so many ways but they all are trying to do the same thing, so I was perfectly positioned to be the conduit.”

Balzo notes that GM, parent company of Pillsbury, had been interested in the idea of a “big grand spectacle” that would incorporate one of the company’s brands. She had already met Weintrob previously at an industry event, and the two began having conversations about some options. “I started talking [with Weintrob] about the history of the Bake-Off, and how it used to be this kind of big grand spectacle that was put on as a live event,” she says. “Over the years, we’ve done various different things with it, and it’s become a smaller digital activation. We’ve also collaborated with the Food Network. I mentioned to Scott that we have been talking about, for years, wanting to bring it back to its full glory. And he pitched this incredible live event.”

“What I originally said to them was, I love the format of the Westminster Dog Show as a baking show, a large event as a live, two-hour special focusing on ordinary people doing extraordinary things,” Weintrob recalls. They agreed on the idea and decided that Thanksgiving would be a key time for the project to air. “I felt we had the opportunity to go and talk to distributors about making this wonderful event. And that’s the key word: It’s an event.”

Balzo says the concept “felt so perfect” and “felt very serendipitous to me at the time. He had this project in his head that that was so perfectly aligned with Pillsbury and what we were looking to do with the Bake Off.”

Back to that trademark: Pillsbury held its first Bake-Off in 1949, when it was called Pillsbury’s Best Grand National Baking Contest. A total of 100 finalists went to New York to compete, and each received at least $100 and kept the G.E. stove they baked on. Eleanor Roosevelt presented the grand prizes, which ranged from $25,000 to $50,000.

Balzo says that baking competitions are immensely watchable because it offers inspiration for viewers at home. “The recipes they’re making are so relatable, and I think people are really going to feel that when they’re watching” the new show, she said, adding that many brands are trying to get into this space, and for GM, the Bake-Off special offers an “authentic connection” with Pillsbury. “It feels like bringing back something that people have already experienced and want more of, as opposed to trying to advertise through content like this.”

For 2026’s Bake-Off, Weintrob began assembling a production team and then setting up meetings with distributors. “When network TV is involved, the word ‘spectacle’ is a brilliant word as a reference point,” he shares. “It needs to be a spectacle to work on network, and CBS saw just how great the show could be. They were the perfect partner to work with.”

Balzo agrees. She cites Barrymore’s authenticity and relatability as two qualities that make her a great host. “Drew authentically remembers that experience [of the commercial], and the brand means something to her,” Balzo says. “She really cares about that connection and the authenticity to the brand in this partnership, and I think people will see that come through on screen when they watch the special, which is why we’re so excited to have her as our host. [In addition], we wanted this to be about more than just baking. We want to tell the stories of a diverse set of people and their backstories and what baking means to them and memories mean to them. And she’s the perfect host to pull those stories out of our contestants. It was incredible to watch her on set because it felt like she was having just this intimate conversation with [the bakers]. I think they felt that too.”

Weintrob and showrunner Brielle Cohen watched 250 submissions from home bakers to cull the list down to 24 competitors who all have “incredible” backstories to share. In addition, famed baker and author Amirah Kassem will join Barrymore and celebrity chef judges Christina Tosi and Dook Chase in the special. It was filmed live on Oct. 23, he shares, which meant a tight turnaround for the Nov. 23 airdate.

For her part, Balzo says this was the first time General Mills had done anything “on this scale,” so they really leaned on Weintrob and the production team to guide them through the process, though GM did play a hand in everything from creative direction to final casting to set direction to the judges and host. Execs also saw all the cuts and provided feedback. (Pillsbury Bake-Off Classic is produced by Studio Large Eyes, Pantheon Media Group and Juma Entertainment. Executive producers include Weintrob for Studio Large Eyes, Jennifer O’Connell, Ariel Brozell, Eden Sutley and Martha Chaput for Pantheon, Robert Horowitz for Juma Entertainment and Brielle Cohen is showrunner. Balzo, Kenzie Caron and Eleanor Harkness serve as executive producers for Pillsbury.)

But, he adds, it’s not just matching a brand with a network and assuming the magic will happen. He says there is a lot of listening on his end and working with the brand to understand what they need while at the same time creating something that’s right for a distributor and their audience. “You’ve got to create bespoke entertainment for a brand,” he summarizes. “You can’t just send them random ideas.”

There’s also the challenge of the promotional timeline for brands vs. distributors. Brands usually begin working on their promotional campaigns months out, while the distributors work on a shorter timeline, sometimes even just weeks. Weintrob says he worked with both companies to come up with an approach that benefited each of them.

Both Weintrob and Balzo hope this special becomes an annual tradition that families can enjoy for years. And, Weintrob suggests, the special could expand to include Valentine’s Day or Super Bowl editions, for example.

“As a brand and especially the Pillsbury brand, the whole family is so important,” Balzo adds. “So we wanted to make sure that we created something that could be one of those moments to bring families together around and create memories around.”

Pillsbury Bake-Off Classic airs Nov. 23 from 8-10 p.m. ET/PT on CBS and streams on Paramount+ (Premium).
