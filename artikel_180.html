Labour MPs sign up for leadership course to counter rightwing populism

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Course run by former No 10 advisers is funded by Gary Lubner, who has given more than £11m to Labour

More than 20 Labour MPs have signed up to a year-long leadership course helping them to counter rightwing populism and deal with hostility, run by two former No 10 advisers and ultimately funded by multimillionaire donor Gary Lubner.

The training courses worth about £9,600 are being led by Ruth Turner, who was Tony Blair’s director of government relations, and Peter Hyman, who has worked for Blair, Keir Starmer and Andy Burnham’s governments.

About 18 MPs including former ministers Dawn Butler, Rachel Blake and Fleur Anderson declared their attendance at a three-day residential training course from a company called August Ltd, which is owned by Turner. Several more have been trained by the programme without attending the awaydays.

The programme on the “craft of leadership” and the “unique demands” facing MPs is ultimately being funded by a grant from Lubner, who made his fortune running the car repair company Autoglass.

He has given more than £11m to Labour and the thinktank Labour Together, now known as ThinkLabour. Under the terms of the grant, Lubner does not have any influence over or input into the leadership course.

Three themes of the sessions are resilience, agency and teamwork, with MPs also learning about countering far-right populism, including how algorithms can radicalise people and allow their grievances to be exploited.

Turner said she and Hyman came up with the idea for better training for MPs on leadership after seeing how many are thrown into the deep end on entering the House of Commons.

She said from her time at No 10 it struck her “that we were getting very senior people to make consequential decisions and largely keeping them quite isolated and unsupported”. She said ministers now get training from the Institute for Government and other places but “backbenchers are a much more neglected bunch”.

Turner said the MPs had helped to “co-design” the programme based on what they need as they learn how to navigate an “age of upheaval”.

She said that one of the learning areas was about “individual resilience” and the “sense of threat that MPs have – not just the physical threat, but also just the hostility that they face”.

She added: “I’m not sure people perform at their best when they’re feeling constantly under threat.”

Turner said teamwork was a “really underbaked quality in a sector that can be hyper-individualised, and hyper-competitive” but it was important for MPs to work together and with people leading society and business leaders in their constituencies .

She said another one of the aims was for the current MPs on the leadership course “to help shift the culture a little bit to make [it] much more normal” to undergo training.

When entering the Commons, MPs do not get mandatory training on how to do their jobs or hold leadership roles, which span from attending to parliamentary business to dealing with the concerns of constituents.

They are not employed by the House of Commons, and operate as effectively self-employed people, making them responsible for their own training and development as well as that of their staff.
