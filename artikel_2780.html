Tim O’Neal Highlights PGA Diversity Push At Schwab Cup Finale

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Tim O'Neal stands alone as the only African-American golfer competing for his first title at the Charles Schwab Cup Championship this week in the Valley. Running from Nov. 12 through Nov. 15, the PGA Tour Champions season finale features 36 qualifiers, including the 54-year-old veteran. He missed earning his PGA Tour card by a single stroke in both 2001 and 2004 before finally securing his spot in 2008, a journey that spans nearly 30 years as a professional.

A rookie in 1997, O'Neal began his professional journey on the Korn Ferry Tour immediately after graduating from Jackson State University, a historically Black college or university. When Tiger Woods turned pro and subsequently became the first African-American to win the Masters, that specific year marked a turning point for the sport. O'Neal believes the landscape has shifted since then. "With Tiger getting on tour, winning majors and dominating for a long time, that definitely I would think has had an effect," O'Neal said, according to the Arizona Republic. The numbers support his view. According to the National Golf Foundation and the United States Golf Association, golf diversity reached an all-time high of 25% in 2024, representing seven million Black, Asian, or Latinx players. This is a massive jump from just 8% in 1990.

Despite the rise in participation, the financial barrier to reaching the professional level remains steep. O'Neal notes that the economics of the game have changed drastically over his three decades in the sport. "Twenty-something years ago, you could probably skimp here and skimp there to find a way to get through, but now it's impossible," O'Neal told The Arizona Republic. He emphasizes that backing is essential for success. "These financial groups, whatever they can do to help golfers get to the next level, you need it because there's very few golfers who made it that didn't have backing behind them," O'Neal said.

His performance this season shows he is still competitive. O'Neal has recorded five top-10 finishes through 27 events in 2026, demonstrating that experience still matters on the senior tour. Other Black pro golfers currently on the tour include Harold Varner II, Joseph Bramlett, and Cameron Champ. O'Neal hopes his path is a beacon for the next generation. "Hopefully, other golfers of color will realize and see if I can do it, they can do it too," O'Neal told The Arizona Republic earlier this week.

As O'Neal approaches his fourth PGA Tour Champions season, his approach to the game has evolved. The aggressive play of his younger days has given way to a more calculated strategy. "You get a little smarter, course management's a little bit better," O'Neal said. This shift in mindset has helped him navigate the complex courses of the Champions tour.

The growth of diversity in the sport is a key factor for the future health of the game, according to the data from the USGA and NGF. O'Neal's continued presence on the tour highlights that the pipeline from HBCUs and diverse communities is producing professionals who can compete at the highest level.

The Charles Schwab Cup Championship concludes on Sunday, Nov. 15, in the Valley.
