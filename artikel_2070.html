Stoke City’s Middlesbrough transfer envy has faded thanks to £2m deal

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Stoke City have had an incredible 2026/27 campaign so far, due in part to how successful their summer transfer window was that preceded it.

Indeed, Mark Robins' side didn't win a league game until their clash with Norwich City on Deadline Day, but since then, the only way has been up, and for the second straight season, the Potters are amongst the early pacesetters heading into December.

The likes of Ethan Galbraith, Justin Devenny and Jack McGlynn have stolen headlines in the midfield, while George Hirst proved the hero in their win over Birmingham City last weekend, but one of the best pieces of business in the entire window has to be the deal which brought Luke Graham to the bet365 Stadium from Dunee FC.

Stoke would have gone into the season worrying about who was going to replace Ashley Phillips at the heart of their backline, and those questions have been answered immediately.

Phillips' time at Tottenham Hotspur came to an end in the summer, as he signed for Middlesbrough for an initial £7 million fee, which could rise to £13 million through add-ons.

The centre-back only just turned 21 in June and is highly regarded as one of the brightest up-and-coming English defenders in the game. He became that while at Stoke over the past two seasons.

It's not often that a player spends consecutive seasons on loan at one club, but the Potters were brilliant in aiding Phillips' development. He made 83 appearances over both campaigns, including 40 in the Championship last year.

The 21-year-old ranked in the top 20% of centre-backs last season, per FotMob, in defensive contributions, blocked shots and clearances, while also becoming a lot more comfortable on the ball. The number of passes he made was also among the top 20%.

He also made his debut for the England Under-21 side last season, playing a role in their successful qualification campaign in the European Championships.

As far as progression goes, the summer of 2026 Phillips was much better than he was in the summer of 2024, before he made his Stoke debut, and Potters fans would have been frustrated to see those two years of development benefit another side.

Despite the departure of such a key defender over the past couple of seasons, Mark Robins' side only spent an initial £2 million on his replacement.

In came Luke Graham from Dundee FC, who had broken into the Scottish Premiership side just last season. He'd worked his way up the Scottish ladder in loan spells with fourth-tier Albion Rovers, League One outfit Montrose and then second-tier side Falkirk.

Graham was one of the best defenders in the SPL outside of the Old Firm giants and Hearts. No defender won more duels than the Scotsman, and in terms of defensive stats, FotMob had him in the top 6% in defensive contributions, blocks and clearances.

He was comfortable in possession, was able to play long when needed, and at 22 years old, had a massive ceiling that Stoke were now tasked with helping him reach.

Questions may have been asked surrounding whether Graham could make that step-up to Championship level immediately, and if only spending a small seven-figure fee on his services to replace Phillips was the right decision, but he's more than impressed so far.

Graham cost Stoke an initial £2 million, but the Potters are already having to send Dundee more money after he broke into the Scotland senior squad already under new Tartan Army boss Sébastien Pocognoli and started their Nations League games against North Macedonia and Switzerland.

It's been a deserved debut, too, as Graham has been one of the more underrated signings in the entire Championship, and a big reason why Stoke are doing as well as they are.

The Potters have one of the best defensive records in the division, and the 22-year-old has been a mainstay in that defence. He was benched in their 3-1 defeat against Southampton and their 4-1 loss against Wolves. Since then, he's played every minute, with Mark Robins' side conceding just 11 times in the 14 games he's played.

He's a top 10% defender for tackles and duels won, and sits in the top 13% for defensive contributions. Stoke have had to do a lot of defending slender leads in games, and while Ben Wilmot is playing incredibly, so is Graham alongside him.

It's good that Stoke fans can debate the pros and cons of both Phillips and Graham with no real heavy favour towards either.

They loved having the Boro man at their club, and he's realising his Premier League potential at the Riverside, but as it stands, Stoke and Graham could well be joining them there.

The one overarching positive here, though, is that the Potters don't have to worry about overseeing the development of Graham, just for him to return to a parent club and be sold for up to £13 million to a new team.

The Scotsman is theirs, and they'll be able to profit from his potential, or even see that come to fruition in the top flight in the upcoming seasons, if Mark Robins' side can maintain this promotion-winning form.

For an initial £2 million, he may go down as one of the best deals Stoke have ever done, if he can continue to reach new heights at the bet365 Stadium.
