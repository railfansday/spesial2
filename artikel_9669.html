I Self-Hosted n8n and Replaced Three Subscriptions

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Posted on Nov 16 • Originally published at clinerds.com

So I did what any self-hoster would do: deleted all three and ran one container. The replacement is n8n — a workflow automation tool you self-host, connect to ~400 services, and keep entirely on your own infrastructure. It's the closest thing to "Zapier, but mine" that I've found, and this post is the honest setup guide — including the license asterisk you should read before falling in love.

Full disclosure, because this blog cares about that stuff (see what happened when HashiCorp changed Vault's license): n8n uses a "fair-code" license (Sustainable Use License). Source-available, free to self-host and use, but with commercial restrictions and not OSI-approved. My read: for personal automation it's a non-issue; if you're building a product on top of it, read the license or reach for something like Windmill or Activepieces instead. Know what you're adopting — that goes for every tool on this blog.

The power move with n8n is the webhook trigger: an HTTP endpoint that starts a workflow. That's also your attack surface. Two rules:

The original three jobs, rebuilt as two workflows:

1. RSS → filtered digest. Trigger node polls a few feeds hourly, a filter drops everything that doesn't match keywords I'm tracking, and the survivors land in a Telegram message each morning. Replaces the RSS-watcher subscription — and the keyword filter is better because I wrote it.

2. Form → spreadsheet → draft social post. A webhook receives a form submission, n8n appends a row to a Google Sheet (the integrations are first-class — OAuth setup takes minutes), then drafts a social post I review before anything goes live. Human in the loop, automation doing the boring 90%.

Total running time per execution: seconds. Total cost: the VPS I was already paying for. There's also an AI node if you want to wire in a local model through the Ollama setup — summarize-then-post is where I'm experimenting next.

Your workflows and credentials live in Postgres now, which means your existing Postgres backup routine covers everything. Take the volume backup too (n8n-data holds the encryption key material — back it up, or your credential backups are unreadable). Test a restore once; an automation tool whose config you can't restore is a very elaborate way to lose your afternoons.

If you pay for one or two lightweight automation subscriptions and already run a server: yes, emphatically. An afternoon of setup traded for permanently lower bills and a stack that keeps your data home is exactly the trade this blog exists to make. Just go in with open eyes about the license, set the encryption key before the first credential, and put auth on every webhook.

And if you build the AI-summary workflow before I do — the RSS digest summarized by a local model every morning — tell me how it goes. That's the next post brewing.
