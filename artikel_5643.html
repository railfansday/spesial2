October jobs report strengthens case for December rate hike

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

October hiring rebounded to 165,000 jobs and revisions lifted August and September payrolls by a total of 84,000, while unemployment stayed near 4.1%. Economists say the data supports a December Fed rate hike, with the path after that tied to inflation and upcoming labor reports.  AI Summary

A December rate hike is coming into focus due to the resilience of the U.S. job market. In October, the U.S. economy added 165,000 total non-farm payroll jobs, according to data released Friday by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics.

October’s job gain is notably higher than the average monthly gain of 48,000 jobs for the past 12 months.

In addition, the jobs reports for August and September were revised upward, with an additional 53,000 jobs in August and 31,000 more in September.

“Employers turned more of their open positions into actual paychecks in October,” Sam Williamson, First American’s senior economist, said in a statement. “The rebound in hiring, upward revisions and low unemployment point to a healthier labor market, keeping a December rate hike firmly in play and potentially delaying mortgage-rate relief.”

The unemployment rate also remained fairly steady, at 4.1%, with 7.1 million unemployed people.

“While the headline unemployment rate decreased, some of this was due to individuals taking part-time work when they would have preferred a full-time position,” Mike Fratantoni, the Mortgage Bankers Association’s senior vice president and chief economist, said in a statement. “The U-6, which more broadly captures underemployment, increased to 7.9% from 7.6% the prior month. Individuals who have lost jobs are also finding a difficult time getting back in, as the median and average length of unemployment spells are increasing.”

Sectors that saw sizable increases in employment in October included professional and business services (+70,000 jobs), healthcare (+42,000 jobs), and transportation and warehousing (+25,000 jobs).

“The pickup reached beyond the industries that have carried much of the job growth in recent years,” Williamson said. “Professional and business services led the increase, followed by health care and transportation and warehousing. Manufacturing added 19,000 jobs, well above expectations and further evidence that the hiring rebound extended beyond services.”

The construction sector lost 3,000 jobs in October, with residential building construction losing 1,200 jobs and residential specialty trade contractors losing 15,200 jobs. Within the construction sector, notable job growth occurred in nonresidential building construction (+2,900 jobs) and nonresidential specialty trade contractors (+7,900 jobs).

Real estate, rental and leasing, on the other hand, added 5,600 jobs in October, with 4,300 jobs coming from the real estate sector alone.

Economists feel that October’s strong jobs report clears the way for the Federal Reserve to raise rates in December.

“After last month’s hot inflation print put the next increase in ‘when’ not ‘if’ territory, a soft jobs report was the one thing that could have complicated December,” Jake Krimmel, Realtor.com’s senior economist, said in a statement. “If Fed hawks were waiting to exhale, they can do so now.”

While a rate hike in December now seems nearly undeniable, economists are less certain about what may come after that.

“The outlook beyond December will depend on what happens with inflation and the next jobs report,” Williamson said. “For housing, stronger hiring cuts both ways. More jobs mean more income and more reasons to move, while a stronger economy could prolong the wait for mortgage-rate relief. Still, a healthier labor market could put a little more churn into a housing market stuck in a low-churn, slow-burn recovery.”
