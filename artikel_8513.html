Your Memberships Can Cut Hotel & Cruise Prices – Check Each Against Booking Direct

Before you book a hotel or cruise, check the member rates you already have through a warehouse club, AAA or AARP, your employer, your credit union, or military service. Price the same trip booked direct too, subtract any shop card or onboard credit the member deal adds, and book whichever costs less.

You won’t find these rates by accident. They sit behind a member login or a hotel’s special-rates menu, so a regular search shows you the public price.

Much of the saving comes as extras rather than a lower price, so count those extras when you compare. Costco Travel adds a digital Costco Shop Card, a gift card for Costco purchases, to every cruise it books. Members on Costco’s Executive tier also earn its 2% annual reward on qualifying Costco Travel purchases. BJ’s Travel adds a BJ’s gift card to most cruise bookings.

AAA and AARP cut the room rate itself, by at least 10% at participating Choice Hotels for members of either. Both also add onboard credit, money to spend on the ship, on select cruises. But AAA’s own fine print says its standard hotel discount is no lowest-price guarantee.

Many employer and credit union perks sites carry hotel deals too. Service members, veterans, and their families can look for military rates, which many hotel chains and cruise lines set aside. Government employees can ask hotels for a government rate. Read the terms before you book either for a vacation, since IHG, for one, lets each hotel decide whether those rates cover leisure stays.

Then check what the same dates, room or cabin, and cancellation terms cost when you book direct with the hotel or cruise line. Subtract any shop card or onboard credit from the member price, and book the lower total. Say a family’s Caribbean cruise costs $6,000 for the same cabin and cancellation terms on the cruise line’s site and on its club’s travel site, and the club adds a $400 shop card. Booking through the club leaves the family $400 ahead.

Pack the membership card or ID your rate requires. At IHG hotels, a guest who can’t show it pays the hotel’s undiscounted flexible rate instead.
