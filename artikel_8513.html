Personal Finance (Not Investing) • Re: Testamentary Trust - Making sure money goes to kids, not a new spouse

Tanggal: 2026-11-10


The most common plan is to give the child control over his/her trust at a specified age, often 35. In other words, upon reaching 35, the child may become a trustee, may remove and replace his/her co-trustee (provided the replacement isn't a close relative or subordinate employee), and may appoint (give or leave) the trust assets to anyone he/she wants (other than the child or his/her estate or creditors).

If the co-trustee won't make a requested distribution, and the child removes and replaces the co-trustee, presumably the replacement won't make a distribution if it's not a good idea.

If the child isn't yet 35, the trustees can usually decant the trust to remove the powers that the child will get at 35.

The trustee may make distributions for a child's expenses, except for those that the trustee is himself/herself obligated to provide.
Thanks for your expertise. Sounds like decanting the trust could be quite a legal battle. How common is it?
Decanting is very common. We spend a good deal of time decanting trusts where we can't fix them by exercising a power of appointment.

There's unlikely to be a legal battle since trustees usually decant because the beneficiaries want them to do so.
