Rockets 119, Pacers 104: Alperen Şengün exits, Reed Sheppard gets hot and rotation tweaks

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

INDIANAPOLIS - Wednesday night was supposed to be the first chance to see the Houston Rockets as close to full strength as they'd been in the 2026-27 season.

With Marcus Smart's suspension over, Houston entered its 119-104 win over the Indiana Pacers with the top 10 players in its rotation available - third-string center Clint Capela (ankle) marked the only injury-related absence.

That lasted less than 1 ½ quarters. Starting center Alperen Şengün - who entered the day leading Houston in points, rebounds and assists per game - left the game after getting hit in both eyes by a seemingly inadvertent swipe by Indiana forward Aaron Nesmith.

Rockets coach Ime Udoka did not have a timeline for Şengün's potential return. Udoka said he thought Şengün, who donned sunglasses in the Gainbridge Fieldhouse locker room postgame, had a scratch on his left cornea and swelling on the right eye.

"A little bit," Udoka said when asked if it was frustrating not to have his full rotation for an extended period, "but it's good to see guys step in. … Next person up, I think our guys have lived by that all season. But it's nice to have your whole group at some point as well."

Houston was facing an even more shorthanded Indiana team missing star point guard Tyrese Haliburton, among others. The Rockets trailed for about the first two minutes but that didn't last for long - they led by as much as 21 en route to their fifth win in six games.

Kevin Durant led Houston - which had six double-digit scorers, generated assists on 68.2% of baskets and shot a blistering 54.3% on 3-pointers - with 21 points. It took him just nine shots, seven makes, to get there.

"I was just patient, wasn't rushing. I felt like last game, I rushed shots just to get shots up," said Durant, who shot 8-for-19 against the Miami Heat on Monday. "So just being more patient with my looks and holding my follow-through on my threes and I think I was able to get going from the 3-point line."

Indiana Pacers forward Pascal Siakam (43) shoots over Houston Rockets guard Reed Sheppard (15) during the first half of an NBA basketball game in Indianapolis, Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026. (AP Photo/Doug McSchooler) (Doug McSchooler/AP Photo/Doug McSchooler)

Durant went 4-for-5 on triples, his efficiency from deep only outmatched by teammate Reed Sheppard, who scored a season-high 20 points on 7-for-10 shooting, making six of seven 3-pointers. The third-year guard entered the game shooting just 30.5% from deep, well below his career average. Some astute observers (me) predicted he was due for positive regression and he got it.

"Finally got some shots to go in," Sheppard said.

The guard said he never lost confidence amid the early slump, crediting that faith to his work in the gym along with teammates and coaches encouraging him to keep shooting.

Does a game like Wednesday's help the confidence?

Houston starting the game with a full rotation did give some insight into Udoka's rotation choices. Earlier in the season, he usually brought in Smart for Amen Thompson before inserting Sheppard for Fred VanVleet. Against Indiana, he brought Sheppard in first to spell Thompson and often played Sheppard alongside VanVleet - a look the veteran point guard said earlier in the season he wanted to see more often and one Houston got a chance to see in Smart's absence.

That won't always be the rotation pattern, with Udoka saying it was partially influenced by Indiana playing a smaller lineup.

"But we do like Reed and Fred out there together … two handlers, two shooters, a lot of spacing," he said.

Udoka said he liked what he saw from the VanVleet-Sheppard duo in the games Smart missed, noting Houston could put those two in a zone when the Rockets go with a double-big man lineup or play them with Şengün as the lone big. That experimentation is a silver lining amid Houston's absences in the early season, giving different lineups an opportunity to build chemistry that could help down the line.

"It's not frustrating," Durant said of the absences. "Can't control it. You can't get mad over stuff like that … We care more so about Alpi's health than anything. … Hopefully he gets right. Maybe we send him some goggles here soon, change up his look a little bit but for the most part I heard he was all right."

Wednesday's game was a surprisingly chippy affair considering the two teams don't share much history.

Indiana coach Rick Carlisle was ejected in the second quarter after receiving two technical fouls - the first for "disrespectfully addressing an official and for the use of profanity" and the second for clapping his hands emphatically and walking close to the official, per the pool report. Those were among the five technicals, four on the Pacers, handed out in a game that featured multiple hard falls and fouls that prompted jawing from all sides.

"Very strange game in a lot of ways … a lot of technicals, ejections, missed calls, giving the ball to the wrong team," Udoka said. "Officiated very weirdly."
