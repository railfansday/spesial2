Nvidia interim CEO Kress tells employees ‘let’s move forward’ at first all-hands meeting since Jensen Huang exit

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Interim Nvidia CEO Colette Kress told employees "let's move forward" on Wednesday during the company's first all-hands meeting since founder Jensen Huang resigned, according to an attendee who spoke to CNBC on the condition of anonymity.

Kress was joined by newly appointed board chair Harvey Jones and hyperscale and high-performance computing head Ian Buck. Several other senior members of Nvidia's executive team spoke, including long-time company veteran Jeff Fisher, senior vice president of GPU engineering Jonah Alben, executive vice president in charge of Operations Debora Shoquist and co-founder Chris Malachowsky, according to a person familiar with the matter.

Kress opened with the message about moving forward, and also brought up pay raises for employees, according to the attendee. Jones then pivoted to a discussion about the search for the next CEO, the attendee added. The meeting lasted about an hour and was in person as well as streamed on Microsoft Teams.

The general sentiment swirling in the room, according to the attendee, is that employees want Huang to come back. Employees have spoken up on X in recent days to call for the return of Huang as CEO, and one senior employee reportedly sent a letter to the board warning Nvidia would lose "irreplaceable talent" if it doesn't consider reinstating Huang.

But another person familiar with the matter disagreed, saying that employees welcomed the remarks from leadership as well as the visibility into company leadership.

"The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," Jones said during the earnings call on Tuesday.

Despite recent turmoil, Wednesday's meeting was not out of the ordinary. Nvidia typically holds all-hands the morning after quarterly earnings reports, according to a former employee who spoke to CNBC on the condition of anonymity. Kress recapped Tuesday's earnings report, calling it a "great quarter," according to one person familiar with the matter.

In past meetings, Huang took time to answer questions submitted by employees, according to the former employee.

On Tuesday, Huang said he would be "honored to answer these calls and return as CEO."

Nvidia shares were down 2% on Wednesday, a day after the company reported third-quarter earnings that topped Wall Street expectations and gave no signal that the company was going to slow its growth. Still, Nvidia faced several downgrades from Wall Street on Wednesday, including from Bernstein and Evercore.
