Martin Zubimendi is reportedly becoming increasingly open to leaving Arsenal, with Chelsea and Atletico Madrid monitoring the midfielder’s situation ahead of the January and summer transfer windows.

The Spain international was a major part of Mikel Arteta’s side last season, but increased competition has significantly reduced his role this term. Declan Rice remains central to Arsenal’s midfield, while Bruno Guimaraes’ arrival and Myles Lewis-Skelly’s development have made regular starts harder to find.

For a player entering his prime at 27, the prospect of spending another season on the fringes is understandably beginning to raise questions.

According to AS, Zubimendi is considering options he previously would not have entertained, with Atletico Madrid and Chelsea both alert to his situation.

The report claims Arsenal could demand around €70million, while Xabi Alonso’s admiration makes Chelsea an interesting destination.

Zubimendi has made 12 competitive appearances this season but accumulated only around 380 minutes, underlining how dramatically his role has changed.

However, Fabrizio Romano has played down suggestions that an exit is already being negotiated. CaughtOffside reports that there are currently no talks between Arsenal and Atletico, while Zubimendi is not considered desperate to leave.

Selling Zubimendi could generate a significant fee, but Arsenal should avoid making a decision purely because he is not currently first choice.

Arteta has always rated his intelligence highly. Shortly after his arrival, the Arsenal manager said: “When you have him every single day, how he captures the information, how he processes things on the pitch, it’s very impressive.”

That quality has not suddenly disappeared.

Arsenal are challenging across several competitions, and midfield depth becomes especially important during the winter months. Zubimendi’s recent hamstring scare with Spain is another reminder of how quickly squad circumstances can change.

Chelsea would undoubtedly benefit from his composure and positional intelligence, particularly under Alonso, while Atletico could offer the regular starting role he appears to want.

But unless Zubimendi actively pushes for a move, Arsenal should resist selling in January.

A summer conversation would make more sense. By then, both club and player will have a much clearer idea whether his reduced role is temporary or evidence that the relationship has reached a natural endpoint.
