Mistletoe Martini

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Recipe by Kate McKiernan, Chicago, Illinois

A mistletoe martini is holiday sparkle you can sip. It tastes like a frosty-cold vodka-cranberry with added floral notes, and the most festive garnish you can dream of.

The mistletoe martini is the perfect spirited sipper for your holiday parties. It’s flavored with cranberry—a classic December flavor—but adds elderflower liqueur and a little simple syrup for a French kiss of floral sweetness. A hard shake turns the concoction into a cocktail that feels way fancier than the effort it takes.

You don’t have to be a bartender or jolly old St. Nick to whip one up. The hardest part is the garnish, and even that is just a couple of sage leaves cozied up between two cranberries on a pick. Just don’t garnish it with actual mistletoe, no matter how hard you’re angling for a smooch, because mistletoe is toxic. Let it work its magic from the doorway, not your glass! Shake up a mistletoe martini for yourself after a long night of gift wrapping, or batch a pitcher and make it the most kissable drink in your lineup of Christmas cocktails.

Add the vodka, cranberry juice, elderflower liqueur and simple syrup.

Editor’s Tip: If you’re not sure how sweet your cranberry juice is, start with half the simple syrup and add more to taste.

Shake hard until the liquid is very cold and the shaker is frosty, about 10 to 15 seconds.

Strain the cocktail and pour it over fresh ice into a cocktail glass.

Editor’s Tip: Pop your glasses in the freezer for 10 to 15 minutes before guests arrive so they stay frosty longer.

Garnish the cocktail with a couple of sage leaves between two cranberries on a cocktail pick. Lay the pick across the rim of the glass or let it rest in the drink. Serve the cocktail right away while it’s cold.

Editor’s Tip: Before you skewer the sage, gently clap the leaves between your palms to release their aroma. It’s an old bartender trick that makes every sip smell like the holidays.

A shaken mistletoe martini is best enjoyed right away, since ice keeps melting and diluting the drink the longer it sits. The good news is that this drink can easily be made into a batched cocktail, by mixing the base ahead of time and keeping it in the fridge until the party starts. Just leave out the ice and the garnish until you’re ready to serve.

You can make mistletoe martinis ahead of time by mixing a big batch of the base in a pitcher. For eight drinks, stir together 2 cups of cranberry juice, 2 cups of vodka, 1 cup of elderflower liqueur and 1 cup of simple syrup, then chill it for at least a few hours. When guests arrive, you can shake individual portions with ice or you can dilute the base by stirring about 1 cup of cold water into the pitcher before pouring it over fresh ice. Skewer your garnishes a few hours ahead and keep them covered in the fridge so the sage stays fresh.

A batched mistletoe martini base lasts up to three days in a covered pitcher or jar in the refrigerator. Give it a good stir before serving, since the ingredients can settle.

The best cranberry juice for a mistletoe martini depends on how sweet you like your cocktails, and you’ll want to adjust the simple syrup to match. Unsweetened, pure cranberry juice is super tart, so use the full ounce of simple syrup if you go this route. A 100% juice blend gets its sweetness from other juices like apple or grape, so start with 1/2 ounce to 3/4 ounce of syrup. Cranberry juice cocktail—the classic bar ingredient—already has added sugar, so start with 1/4 ounce of simple syrup, or skip it entirely. Whichever bottle you grab, taste the cocktail as you go.

To make simple syrup, stir together equal parts sugar and water in a small saucepan, and heat them just until the sugar dissolves. Let it cool completely, then pour it into a jar and refrigerate it until you need it. Our homemade simple syrup recipe walks you through it, and once you have a jar in the fridge, you’ll find excuses to use it in everything from iced coffee to holiday punch.

You can substitute orange liqueur for the elderflower liqueur, which pushes the drink into cosmo territory and makes them a lot like these Santa Clausmopolitans.
