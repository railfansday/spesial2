9 Best Hotels in Rio de Janeiro for a Taste of the Carioca Lifestyle

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

“I live where you vacation” takes on a whole other meaning when, like me, you're based in Rio de Janeiro. I live smack in the middle of South America’s two hottest beaches—Copacabana and Ipanema—but it’s not the ocean that keeps me in Brazil's buzziest city. It’s the life that happens around it. It’s the sound of the first coconuts being cut open on the beach in the early morning, the sight of barefoot surfers passing beneath my window, and the honey-like melody of bossa nova flowing from a nearby beach kiosk. Not everyone can live here, of course. But luckily, the best hotels in Rio de Janeiro give you a good taste of what it feels like to be a local in Brazil’s most exciting metropolis.

Over the past six years, I’ve come to appreciate how each neighborhood lets you experience the city differently. Copacabana delivers the high-energy hustle and bustle you’d expect from a place that hosts the biggest Carnival celebration on Earth. In contrast, Leblon offers a quieter, more upscale seaside rhythm. And then there’s Santa Teresa, secluded on the forested hilltops, a tried-and-true bohemian village that’s managed to hold onto its personality despite the city’s rising tourism numbers. The hotels below span these special neighborhoods, with each property offering its own unique take on life in Rio, with Brazilian design, memorable views, and hospitality that’ll make you want to stick around just a little bit longer. Read on for the best hotels in Rio de Janeiro, according to our editors.

For glorious Brazilian beaches, Copacabana is ground zero. But the elegant Copacabana Palace is the hotel that created this place, making this strip of sand famous and helping to conjure the idea of the beach as central to Brazilian identity. When the Copacabana Palace opened over a hundred years ago, it occupied an unheralded neighborhood, amid modest houses and fishermen’s shacks. Until then, Rio de Janeiro had been centered on the old downtown areas of Centro and Castelo, and the 19th-century mansions of leafy Santa Teresa, but a new age of leisure arrived in the post-war years. Sunbathing was suddenly a thing, and Coco Chanel made having a tan fashionable. Designed by French architect Joseph Gire, the Copacabana Palace adopted the elegant Art Deco lines of the grand hotels of the French Riviera. More than a century on, the Copacabana Palace is still a Rio icon, bestriding the Avenida Atlântica on that incomparable bay. The style is classic opulence, with vast chandeliers, acres of marble, and Brazilian hardwoods; a sanctuary among the city’s endless partying. But this is Brazil. The Palace may be grand, but it is also fun. The dining options are fabulous, there is a rooftop tennis court, and, across the avenue on that famous beach, hotel staff attend to guests with umbrellas, loungers, cold towels, and sun lotion. —Stanley Stewart

The modern antidote to the Copacabana Palace’s grand hotel glamour, Philippe Starck’s Brazilian debut—which opened in 2007—has design cred in spades. The 89 all-white rooms have balconies (42 with ocean views), but you’ll want to spend time at the rooftop bar by the infinity pool to gaze at Ipanema, Leblon, the Two Brothers Mountain, and Corcovado; watch the sunset; and sip rum muddled with fresh strawberries, a local fave. Other amenities include a spa, beach service, a fitness center, and bike rentals. Proximity, too, is key: The Fasano is across the street from Ipanema Beach, and a seven-minute walk from General Osório metro and 12 minutes from Copacabana Beach.

The Fairmont Rio de Janeiro Copacabana is a lively beachfront hotel that pairs the comforts of a major luxury brand with the Brazilian energy people come searching for. The 375 rooms and suites are spacious and comfortable, and all come with balconies to enjoy the fabulous views. Speaking of which, the property's signature sixth-floor infinity pool melts into the Copacabana horizon, and everything happens around it: specialty coffee and gelato are served in the roomy lounge, contemporary Brazilian dining is highlighted at the outdoor Marine Restô, and cocktails are enjoyed on loungers and cozy couches around the poolside bar. The pool deck also hosts live music, late-night DJs, and Sunday feijoada (a traditional black bean stew) meals, making this a space where Rio’s visitors and locals collide. Elsewhere, the modern gym has everything you need, and I’d return for the spa alone—the two-hour Fire Ritual treatment is a particular highlight. With ongoing beach activities, weekend yoga, and Ipanema within walking distance, this is a hotel that matches Rio’s upbeat social life.

Think of Hotel Santa Teresa Rio MGallery by Sofitel as a hacienda-style counterpoint to the flashier options on the beach. Part of the mini boutique hotel boom happening in the quiet hilltop neighborhood of Santa Teresa, this former derelict mansion has 44 rooms, all with wood floors and sun-filtering shutters that nod to the tropical surroundings without hitting guests over the head with it. The service can be uneven—veering between over-attentive and indifferent—but the 80-foot pool, with its view over Santa Teresa’s stately villas and the glittering waters of Guanabara Bay, and lush gardens more than make up for it. The spa here is worth checking out, too.

Subtle and modern, in contrast to the go-go party scene of the Fasano, Emiliano was a welcome addition to Rio’s oceanfront Avenida Atlântica when it opened in 2016. A five-star SLH property, the 90 rooms and suites—spread across 12 floors—are large and filled with lovely touches like plants and plump white sofas that make it feel like your own sophisticated beachside apartment, while the bathrooms have huge showers and bathtubs. Plus, beach access is great: It's right across the street from the southern part of Copacabana Beach. Be sure to enjoy the rooftop infinity pool overlooking the ocean.

With 51 rooms, Janeiro has the intimacy of a boutique hotel with the five-star service of a much larger luxury brand. Sitting across the street from Leblon Beach, one of the city’s most beautiful stretches of ocean, the beachy property's interior design reflects the effortlessly chic aesthetic of co-owner Oskar Metsavaht, founder of Brazilian fashion label Osklen. Monochromatic tones, warm wood, and oversized windows keep Rio’s natural surroundings front and center in all rooms. Thoughtful touches like yoga mats, afternoon tea service, exceptionally plush robes, and complimentary Ipanema flip-flops make the stay feel more personal than most. The breakfast service feels like being secretly let into a bakery, with warm pão de queijo (Brazilian cheese bread) promptly delivered directly to your table, and a colorful assortment of bread spread out over the bar. Downstairs, Cedilha draws a cool crowd for New York–style bistro dinners, while the rooftop plunge pool frames the twin-peaked Two Brothers Mountain through a circular window. Beach service, bicycles, and nearby gym access make settling into Rio’s laid-back lifestyle easy.

Form meets function at Ipanema Inn, an independently owned urban hotel just one block from the world-famous Ipanema Beach. This thoughtfully designed property channels Brazilian modernism through midcentury furniture by designer Sergio Rodrigues, natural materials, and rooms that range from compact courtyard-facing doubles to a duplex suite with sweeping mountain vistas and space for the whole family. At ground level, Quitéria, the acclaimed in-house restaurant, spills out onto the mosaic sidewalk of Rua Maria Quitéria, where chef David Cruz serves a refined menu sourced from small Brazilian producers. Breakfast runs until 11:30 a.m., staff is among the warmest you'll encounter anywhere, and complimentary bikes connect you to a segregated coastal path stretching from Leme all the way to Leblon. With the metro two minutes away and three of Brazil's hottest designer boutiques within the same radius, this is Rio at its most stylish and convenient. —Patrick Gray

An effortless oceanside social hub with modernist Brazilian design notes, Hotel Arpoador is known for its cool, carefree style and on-site wellness offerings. I first visited Hotel Arpoador when it reopened in 2019 and noticed its unique setting: Arpoador is on one of the only stretches of coastline in Rio that is traffic-free. Returning several years later, I rediscovered a hotel that has become a modern classic on the Rio scene, and the freshness is a joy to behold. Do as I do and head down to spend your days surrounded by the spectacle of Rio’s famous beach culture. Hotel Arpoador offers a beach service with chairs, umbrellas, towels, and staff that can look after your belongings as you frolic in the frothy, wild Atlantic. Be sure to make time to enjoy the rooftop with its triangular pool and panoramic ocean views, as well as the Maré Spa wellness space with its excellent treatments and steam room. And don't forget to grab a drink at Arp Bar—with its in-the-know crowd that spills out onto the beachfront from dawn to dusk, it has become the beating heart of Rio’s social scene. —P.G.

Set on a hilltop in the historic Santa Teresa neighborhood, Chez Georges is a striking example of Brazilian Brutalism, offering sweeping 360-degree views of Sugarloaf Mountain and Guanabara Bay. Just a 20-minute drive from the vibrant Copacabana Beach, its secluded setting feels like an urban retreat, where time slows down and the morning begins with the gentle sound of birds. Whether lounging by the 50-foot pool, listening to soft Brazilian music from the record player, or watching the sunset, Chez Georges offers a tranquil, almost dreamlike escape. While Chez Georges may be modest in size, with only a handful of suites, the rooms themselves feel anything but small. Each suite is designed with ’70s-inspired minimalism. Inside, you'll find a standard wardrobe, desk, and king-size bed, but it’s the floor-to-ceiling windows that steal the show, opening onto a shared balcony with sweeping views that are perfect for watching the sunrise. —Kayla Brock

What is the best neighborhood in Rio de Janeiro to stay in?

For a first visit, I’d choose Ipanema. You have the beach, excellent restaurants, Brazilian boutiques, and metro stations all within easy reach—and it’s very much a lived-in neighborhood, teeming with locals and businesses around the clock.

What is the nicest hotel in Rio de Janeiro?

For classic luxury, Copacabana Palace gets my vote. The century-long history, grand beachfront setting, celebrated pool, and attentive service make staying here feel like you’re playing a part in Rio's story.
