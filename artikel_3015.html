UiPath vs. Palantir Technologies: Which AI Automation Platform Makes a Better Stock Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

UiPath and Palantir post very different growth profiles, but their valuations tell an even sharper story for investors weighing 2027 exposure.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 22, 2026 at 1:13PM EST

Artificial intelligence is reshaping the corporate world, forcing investors to choose which software giants will lead the next decade. Comparing UiPath (PATH -2.40%) and Palantir Technologies (PLTR -6.63%) reveals two distinct paths to growth.

UiPath focuses on robotic process automation to handle routine work, while Palantir Technologies provides advanced data analytics and decision-making platforms. They are often compared because both leverage artificial intelligence to enhance productivity for large organizations. Understanding their different growth stages and valuation levels is key for any investor looking toward 2027.

UiPath provides an automation platform designed to help organizations run more efficiently by orchestrating AI, robots, and people. The company operates among tech stocks focused on enterprise productivity. It serves roughly 10,747 customers across sectors like finance and healthcare.

The business relies heavily on its top-tier clients, including 357 customers with annual recurring revenue of $1 million or more. Strategic alliances with partners like Snowflake (SNOW -5.13%) are vital for expanding its market reach. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached approximately $1.7 billion. This represents a revenue growth of roughly 15% compared to the prior year. The company reported a net income of about $361.9 million for this same interval, resulting in a net margin of about 21%.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is roughly 2.5x. This figure indicates the company's ability to meet its short-term obligations using its current assets. The debt-to-equity ratio is 0.0x, comparing total debt to total shareholder equity. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow reached about $352.2 million. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented roughly 78.3% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Palantir Technologies builds software platforms for integrating and analyzing data for human-driven decision-making. It serves a diverse range of public institutions and commercial enterprises, including a significant engagement with the U.S. Army. The company manages strategic partnerships with global entities like SOMPO Holdings (NHOLF +0.00%) to support its implementation services.

Revenue concentration is a factor for the business, as its top three customers accounted for roughly 16% of revenue in 2025. This reliance on a small number of high-value contracts can lead to volatility if major projects are delayed. However, the company continues to expand its reach through joint ventures and global system integrators.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $7.3 billion. This reflects a revenue growth of approximately 87% over the preceding year. During this time, the company generated a net income of about $3.7 billion, yielding a net margin of around 50%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio stands at approximately 7.1x. This metric measures a firm's ability to cover its short-term debts with assets that can be converted to cash within a year. The company has a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.0x, which measures total debt relative to shareholders' equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was close to $2.1 billion. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 32% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation, since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

UiPath faces risks from its heavy reliance on a single automation platform and potential customer concentration. It competes against large cloud providers like Salesforce.com Inc. (CRM -1.47%) and new start-ups focused on artificial intelligence. The company also navigates complex international regulations and operational challenges from rapid global scaling.

UiPath appears to be the more value-oriented choice with a much lower sales multiple, whereas Palantir trades at a substantial premium reflecting its faster growth.

The Forward P/E is a valuation based on future earnings estimates. The P/S ratio measures market cap divided by sales over the past twelve months.

AI is becoming a dominant theme in UiPath's business, with 18 of its 20 largest deals in its most recently reported quarter involving AI. The business raised its annual recurring revenue to more than $1.9 billion in its second quarter of fiscal 2027, up 13% year over year. Revenue in the quarter rose 13% to $410 million. For the full-year fiscal 2027, Wall Street analysts project revenue to rise 11% to $1.79 billion. Net income is expected to drop 30% to $200 million, while free cash flow is set to rise about 16% to $425 million, according to consensus estimates.

UiPath says that in the age of AI, its platform is about orchestration of enterprisewide software and systems to enable companies to find the efficiencies that AI promises while providing guardrails around AI and also working to ensure exactness--the idea being that even if AI is accurate 99.9% of the time, businesses like banks and medical providers need 100% accuracy. UiPath management says many businesses are deploying AI agents, but few have the connective tissue to link these single-task agents to the larger enterprise and to the multiple other software platforms they have deployed. UiPath says its software provides all that in a vendor-agnostic manner. That is its path to long-term growth.

Palantir started out as a tech contractor to the U.S. military, and the federal government still accounts for half of its revenue. But the business has been successfully expanding beyond government work, with about a quarter of sales coming from international customers and a significant minority of revenue from the private sector. For the first time in it smost recently reported quarter, private sector revenue exceeded U.S. government revenue.

Palantir says customers are drawn to its sovereign AI--its term for systems that can help build themselves while protecting what makes each system the AI is refining, so it is not commoditized. Companies like Nvidia Corp. (NVDA -14.01%) and L3Harris Technologies (LHX -2.59%) are notable customers of Palantir's sovereign AI.

The boom in private-sector AI business should double Palantir's 2026 sales to $8.6 billion, with a $2.7 billion jump in net income to $4.4 billion. 2027 should see continued rapid growth, with consensus estimates projecting revenue of $13.1 billion and net income exceeding $6 billion.

Palantir has the fastest-growing business, but its shares come at an exceptional premium. Still, the expected drop in UiPath net income is a bit of a concern. Yet, UiPath shares are priced below the average S&P 500 stock (which trades at around 19x forward earnings), making UiPath shares the better risk relative to pricey Palantir.
