Yaxel Lendeborg's bold spirit being tested by his unwillingness to impose will

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Yaxel Lendeborg's bold spirit being tested by his unwillingness to impose will originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

In the first half of the final game of the Warriors' disastrous five-game road trip, Thursday night in Memphis, the curtain was raised on cinema the likes of which they never again care to watch.

Their offense was an oil spill, with turnovers coming in waves, giving the Grizzlies 21 of their 55 points. Their defense was shockingly lenient, allowing the Grizzlies wide-open 3-point shots with the kind of comfort typically associated with a robe, slippers and a pipe.

Yet the most concerning observation for the Warriors today and beyond was Yaxel Lendeborg's unwillingness to impose his will.

Lendeborg, a rookie but the most dynamic Warrior on the floor, played 17 of the quietest minutes of his promising career. He scored four points before intermission, fewer than four of his teammates. He made zero field goals, which is unsatisfying but not alarming. Most startling was that he attempted only three shots from the field.

The top scorer on Golden State's active roster, whose flame roared so loudly only 10 days ago, left his bravado in the locker room – or locked it inside his head. The force behind his best work was on recess in the first 24 minutes.

To be fair, there were many culprits behind a 126-98 loss that sent the Warriors home with their first 0-5 road trip in more than four years. Once Stephen Curry was injured in the road opener, feebleness cascaded over the likes of Draymond Green, Brandin Podziemski, De'Anthony Melton, Gui Santos, Al Horford and Georges Niang.

Golden State's starting lineup in Memphis – Charles Bassey, Santos, Lendeborg, Melton and Podziemski – combined for seven first-half field goals. Bassey, effectively the team's third-string center, was responsible for all but three of the buckets.

Lendeborg's performance in this game and, moreover, his output on the entire road trip, is most troubling because of what he represents. Selected No. 11 overall in the 2026 draft, he is Golden State's first lottery pick with apparent cornerstone potential since Klay Thompson in 2011. Yaxel is the most viable reason for a kernel of optimism about the franchise's post-Curry future.

Lendeborg was more intentional in the second half, shooting 3-of-8 from the field, including nailing his only triple, adding six assists and four rebounds. He finished with 11 points, eight rebounds and seven assists. After halftime, he looked as if he was trying to gather scattered bits of vitality.

If this game had come two weeks earlier, Lendeborg likely would have glared at Memphis rookie Cam Boozer, selected eight picks earlier in the draft and a staunch competitor in the Rookie of the Year race, and breathed fire. Armed himself for battle.

While it's unfair to expect and unreasonable to believe Lendeborg should be the driving force of the Curry-less Warriors, it's within his ability to sharpen his focus, which spent most of the game floating about FedExForum. He fell asleep so soundly on defense during one Memphis possession – granted, the same could be said of most of his teammates – that coach Steve Kerr called a timeout and addressed him coming off the floor.

Focus and effort top the list of things an athlete can control. Yaxel's focus clearly impacted his effort on this night and during this trip. He was tentative with his shot, despite having a green light. His most demonstrative moment was a physical hallelujah, arms raised, after his third-quarter triple.

He deserved that moment after a road trip in which he averaged 16 points (5.4 below his previous season average) on 33.3-percent shooting from the field, including 20 percent beyond the arc.  His average plus-minus was minus-28.2

Lendeborg's superpower is his determination to make himself matter. To let his teammates know he'll do whatever it takes to win while also showing opponents that he's not afraid to engage physically or even verbally if so moved.

That Yax and his prodigious promise made barely a cameo appearance Thursday, and the same could be said of most of a road trip he and the Warriors never again wish to experience.

Lendeborg's journey to the NBA and the Warriors suggests those five games are not the first time he has been slapped by failure. He always recovered in the past, and that spirit needs to reemerge.
