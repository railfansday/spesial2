Investing - Theory, News &amp; General • Re: How to create a Credit Default Swap (CDS) to hedge counterparty risk for a consumer

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

CDS can be complex and don’t always play out as expected.

- The book/movie Big Short shows how they can be illiquid and those marking to market may not act in good faith.

- Some GM bond holders around the same time bought CDS and were upset when GM got bailed out and CDS didn’t fully pay out.

- I know of a company, a well known yesteryear brand name that was on its way to certain bankruptcy. The stock was less than a dollar. The revenue had shrank to a fraction of its history. Yet there were something like 50 billion of CDS trading on it. At the time there were two primary debt holders. One wanted to drive it into bankruptcy to salvage as much as possible, the other wanted to invest in it as a going concern. It is rumored that the one wanting a going concern was writing CDS as they provided funding to keep the company alive until the CDS expiration date. The company did eventually go bankrupt but hung on for several more months
These concerns are overblown.

The Big Short and Long Term Capital Management had issues and regulatory reforms happened. Few issues here when you have a single name. Even less when you are talking about something centrally cleared - can’t really manipulate the prices here.

And can I get more details on your story? It doesn’t exactly hang together. Selling swaps doesn’t directly help the company. Yeah, buying (protection) cheap issuance will lower the yield spread of the corporate bonds. Selling protection doesn’t do that. There are better ways to help a company than this.
