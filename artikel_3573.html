Cause of Sports Broadcaster Dan Hicks' Sudden Death at 64 Revealed

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A cause of death has been revealed for legendary NBC Sports broadcaster Dan Hicks following his sudden death on Wednesday, November 11.

Hicks’ immediate cause of death was multifocal pneumonia, according to a death certificate obtained by The California Post. He was 64.

He was pronounced dead at 5:32 p.m. on Wednesday at a hospital in Greenwich, Connecticut.

The certificate stated that Hicks had also been dealing with a pulmonary edema (caused by fluid buildup in the lungs) that was preceded by an upper respiratory infection. He also had autoimmune hemolytic anemia, described by the Cleveland Clinic as “a rare condition where the immune system mistakenly produces antibodies that attack and destroy the body’s red blood cells faster than the bone marrow can replace them.”

The broadcaster’s longtime wife, ESPN anchor Hannah Storm, announced his death on social media last week.

“He was a devoted husband of over 30 years, loving father to our three daughters, and cherished friend to so many,” Storm shared via Instagram on Thursday, November 12. “His loss is immeasurable.”

Hicks and Storm shared daughters Hannah, 29, Ellery, 28, and Riley, 25.

“Dan was truly one of a kind and, quite simply, the person everyone wanted to be around,” Storm said. “When he walked into the room, he brought the energy, the laughter, and the people together. He was endlessly kind, thoughtful and funny, and he made everyone feel like they mattered. Our three daughters and I are heartbroken. We are grateful for the love and support surrounding our family during this incredibly difficult time.”

She concluded, “There are simply no words to express how much he will be missed.”

Hicks was a towering figure at NBC Sports over his decades-long career, becoming the voice of its golf coverage and Notre Dame football, as well as making some of the most iconic Olympics calls in history.

“In his 34 years at NBC Sports, he was an exceptional storyteller and caller of games and events who elevated some of the biggest moments in sports history,” the network said in a statement. “From Olympic swimming and skiing to Notre Dame football, numerous other high profile events and especially as the signature voice of our golf coverage for the last 26 years, Dan’s blend of knowledge, style, enthusiasm and humor informed and entertained fans for decades.”

NBC added, “Dan was a better person who welcomed teammates with a smile, a joke, or a word of encouragement, sometimes all three in the same sentence.”
