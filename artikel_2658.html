Ravens Report Card, Week 10: Poor run defense and costly turnovers doom the Ravens

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Baltimore Ravens dropped their fifth consecutive game on Monday night, losing to the previously winless Los Angeles Chargers by three points (23-20). Here are report card grades for the team's position groups from another disheartening defeat in Week 10.

In his debut with the Ravens, Jacoby Brissett wound up airing the ball out a ton with the team trailing early and the run game ineffective. Brissett threw 47 passes and completed 32 of them for 319 passing yards. He had one interception on an errant throw and a couple other nearly intercepted passes. Otherwise, Brissett did just about all you could ask of him with poor blocking up front and no support from the rushing attack. He was close to leading a potential go-ahead touchdown drive late in the fourth quarter, but a turnover robbed him of the chance to do so. It was a gutsy showing from the veteran signal-caller.

Derrick Henry scored both of the offense's two touchdowns but otherwise had an uneventful performance. Once again, Henry found almost no running room behind the offensive line. He was repeatedly hit at or behind the line of scrimmage. Henry had zero rushing yards in the first half and finished with ___ yards on ___ carries, which unsurprsingly are his lowest totals of the season and in recent memory. Justice Hill broke off one long run, but it was called back due to a penalty.

Ja'Kobi Lane had the eyes of Brissett all night, receiving a team-high 10 targets. He was only able to catch three passes, though, two of which gained 20+ yards. Lane had a costly drop on third down that would have given the Ravens a first down just one yard outside the end zone. Zay Flowers got going late in the game and finished with six catches for 74 yards, but his production was overshadowed by a back-breaking fumble late in the fourth quarter that effectively ended the game. Had Flowers not fumbled, the Ravens were nearing field goal range with a chance to tie the game at 23-23 or take the lead. Rashod Bateman caught three passes for 42 yards.

It was a turn-back-the-clock performance for Mark Andrews, who caught all eight of his targets for 82 receiving yards. Andrews had multiple first-down conversions and, most importantly, no drops or penalties. Rookie Josh Cuevas had a trio of receptions and gained 37 yards with some nice production in hurry-up offense mode just before halftime. Matt Hibner has not been involved offensively in recent weeks, but Cuevas continues to show some receiving promise.

It was more of the same for the Ravens' offensive line, which struggled with penalties, got no push in the running game and surrendered pressure. Ronnie Stanley was once again flagged multiple times and Vega Ioane struggled early before exiting with a pre-existing shoulder injury. The Ravens had a lot of trouble against the Chargers' interior defensive linemen, who were consistently in the backfield. To only rush for 16 total yards as a team on 15 carries — against any NFL defense — is nothing short of abysmal.

The Chargers had a middle-of-the-pack rushing attack entering this game, but the Ravens had difficulty slowing them down on the ground. Omarion Hampton gained 105 yards on 22 carries and Justin Herbert broke contain several times. Herbert posted 51 rushing yards on seven carries with a 22-yard score in the first quarter. The Ravens had Herbert dead to rights multiple times but did not finish plays with sacks consistently, which has been a recurring trend this season. Calais Campbell had two quarterback hits and Nnamdi Madubuike exited the game early with a hamstring injury. Depth veterans like Broderick Washington and John Jenkins did not make much of an impact.

Aside from a costly roughing the passer penalty in the second half, Mike Green was wreaking havoc. He had a team-high three quarterback hits along with two sacks and two tackles for loss. It was one of the better games of his young career. Trey Hendrickson's impact continues to leave much to be desired, as he had just one lone tackle with no sacks nor quarterback hits. Hendrickson has not had a sack since Week 4.

Roquan Smith had one big tackle for loss that forced a fourth down and nearly had a goal-line run stop but was overpowered by an extra Chargers' blocker. He appeared to get lost in coverage at least once or twice on a long completion. Trenton Simpson made some notable plays, finishing with two quarterback hits and one tackle for loss with eight total tackles. The Ravens' linebackers have to be knocked for the team's poor run defense as well.

The Ravens were struggling to defend wide receiver Tre Harris early in the game, who quickly caught five passes for 86 yards. He was a non-factor for most of the night after that, though, as was the Chargers' passing attack as a whole in the second half. No other wideout had more than one catch for the Chargers. Marlon Humphrey forced a fumble that was recovered by Los Angeles, but it was a good play against Hampton nonetheless.

Kyle Hamilton had a team-high 10 tackles and was playing clean-up crew against ball-carriers in open space many times. Hamilton was the primary defender on a few completed first-down passes. Malaki Starks appeared to have his snaps reduced. He came up with a big pass breakup on a short pass attempt that would have converted a first down. The Chargers hit on a couple long passes early but the Ravens took away big plays through the air after that.

In his Ravens debut, kicker Riley Patterson connected on both of his field goal tries and extra points. LaJohntay Wester finally had a long return, taking a punt back for 20 yards. The Ravens' return coverage has not been great at times this season. The Chargers had a 24-yard punt return tonight and averaged 15.7 yards per return in total.
