Heat 108, Knicks 105: “Really tough to win a poorly coached, corruptly reffed, half rotation injured game.”

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The Knicks (12-7) lost two Emirates NBA Cup games this season but defeated the Cavaliers on Wednesday. Their chances of reaching the next round and continuing their defense of the Cup weren't nonexistent, but they needed to beat the Heat (11-7) by more than six points and for the Pacers to drop the Sixers.

First things first: the surging Heat are a handful even when New York's roster is complete. The Knicks had to meet the challenge without OG Anunoby (calf), Andre Drummond (concussion), Miles McBride (groin), and Jordan Clarkson (foot). The remaining players weren't altogether healthy, either: Karl-Anthony Towns (19 PTS, 10 RBS, 34 MIN) has an abdominal contusion, and Josh Hart (6 PTS, 9 RBS, 8 AST) is playing through a sore knee and shoulder. Our depleted Knickerbockers fell behind in the first quarter, then went down by 23 in the second. They cut their deficit to five in the third frame and had the Heat on the ropes in crunch time. A few missed free throws, an uncalled flagrant foul, and odd coaching decisions let the rally—and their Cup dreams—slip through their fingers. Final score: 108-105.

Quoth DWLLTheKnicksfan: "Really tough to win a poorly coached, corruptly reffed, half rotation injured game. Respect that it was close."

After the Knicks fell behind 15-7, coach Mike Brown replaced Hart with Pacôme Dadiet (4 PTS, 1 STL, 21 MIN, +7). Pac-Man played arm-flapping defense that disrupted the flow and led to a Miami turnover, then dunked a Brunson pass on the run. We're starting to see the vision! The French fella stayed on the floor when the next round of swaps came, playing alongside Brunson, Hart, Jose Alvarado (7 PTS, 15 MIN), and James Wiseman (11 PTS). The latter lasted just two minutes and committed a foul that heralded the return of Towns. Not a great first look on a night when Drummond was out.

New York logged nine boards in the first quarter. Giannis Antetokounmpo (22 PTS, 23 RBS, 32 MIN) matched that total alone, scarfing up most of Miami's 14 boards. A physical game had Towns wincing in pain, and we were wincing at New York's crappy shooting (2-of-11 from deep). With the Floridians sinking half of their three-pointers, the Knicks trailed 31-21 at the break.

In the second quarter, Mohamed Diawara and the Tylers (Kolek, Nickel) took the floor. Miami scored 12 unanswered while the 'Bockers continued to shoot like the Blind Boys of Alabama. The rookie Nickel looked lost and the coach bewildered. Mike Brown called for time when the score reached 48-27.

Out of that timeout, Brown sent Wiseman and Dadiet back in. Wiseman's minutes were rough. On consecutive plays, Wiggins picked off his inbound pass for a layup, and then Ryan Conwell intercepted his NEXT inbound pass. Unsurprisingly, he suffered a quick hook. Mike was running out of levers to pull as his team fell behind by 23. With four rotation players out and his starters all posting a plus-minus of -13 or worse, the coach fielded 11 different players in the first half in an attempt to slow Erik Spoelstra's squad, which scored inside, outside, and wherever else it wanted.

Thanks to a spirited late-half nine-oh run, the home team entered halftime trailing 60-44. There was plenty of blame to go around for our heroes. Towns (still painin'!) and Brunson combined for 25 of their team's 44 points, but the other starters had five points each, and the Knicks' bench had been outscored by its Miami counterpart, 30-4.

Through the half, the Flamingos shot 45% from the field and 36% from deep (9-of-25), while New York managed 40% and a miserable 21% (4-of-19). The Heat held only slight advantages in rebounds (24-23) and points in the paint (22-18) but were considerably better at protecting the ball. Nine Knicks turnovers yielded 11 Miami points, while the Heat committed just four turnovers that yielded three New York points. Worse, Miami won the battle for second-chance points, 10-0.

Over the first two minutes of the second half, Towns, Bridges, and Shamet combined for nine points to cut the deficit to 61-53. Miami fought back, but New York looked rejuvenated and alert (a marked improvement from the tryptophan blues of the first half). By the midway point of the quarter, the Knicks had converted five straight shots, reducing their deficit to five. As we've seen in game after game this season, it takes a lot of energy to climb out of deep holes. As the Knicks faltered, back-to-back Bridges turnovers allowed Giannis and Bam Adebayo (25 PTS, 7 RBS) to hammer Miami's lead back to 11.

Our heroes got an unexpected lift from Wiseman. He leaped into the fray, scoring seven points on three buckets and a free throw late in the third. That was more like it! Add a Brunson floater and an Antetokounmpo shot-clock violation, and New York trailed 86-79 at the horn.

Bam was a bother to start the fourth, netting two buckets, blocking shots by Shamet and Kolek, and drawing two fouls from Towns. Towns headed to the bench with four fouls at the 8:27 mark. Meanwhile, Shamet, Bridges, and Kolek each contributed layups to keep the Knicks within striking distance. The Heat struggled from the perimeter, with OAKAAKUYOAKs Tim Hardaway Jr. and Bobby Portis blowing opportunities to extend the lead.

Down 91-85 with plenty of game remaining, New York scored seven unanswered points, fueled by the bench mob. Wednesday's hero, Alvarado, scored five consecutive points, including a step-back three off his own offensive rebound, while Dadiet contributed a steal and a reverse layup off Brunson's eighth assist to give the Knicks their first lead. Davion Mitchell finally broke Miami's scoring drought with a floater, putting the Heat ahead 93-92 with six minutes remaining.

At the two-minute mark, the good guys trailed by three points after two freebies by Shamet. From there, Giannis launched an ill-advised, deep airball late in the shot clock; Brunson and Pelle Larsson traded free throws; Hart made one of two at the stripe; and Adebayo made the differential four with 44 seconds left. Captain Clutch's floater made it 104-102. Giannis missed two free throws with 14 seconds on the clock, and Adebayo fouled Wiseman on the loose ball, giving our heroes a soupçon of hope. My bingo card did not have Wiseman shooting free throws with the game on the line. How about yours?

The young journeyman made one, missed one, and Giannis snagged the board and called a timeout. 104-103, 13 seconds to go. When play resumed, Dadiet fouled Mitchell, who made both attempts. On the Knicks' inbounds play, the ball swung to Brunson in the corner, where he was fouled by Conwell. Even though Conwell violated JB's landing space, Scott "The Enforcer" Foster deemed it a common foul. Cap made two of three at the line. Spoelstra called a timeout. 106-105, nine seconds.

Larsson got a breakaway dunk—on the exact same inbounds play the Heat had run on their previous possession. Mike Brown elected not to call a timeout, and Brunson missed a contested triple to end the game.

This is your recap, chaps. As for the Knicks, they'll sleep in their own beds for a few more days and host the Hawks on Sunday. Rest up, troops.
