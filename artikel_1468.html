Methane emissions remain flat even as biggest US oil region opens the tap

A flare burns off excess methane in the Permian Basin, where yearly methane emissions are more than twice as high as regulators’ estimates. Credit: Justin Hamel/Bloomberg/Getty

Oil and gas production in Texas’s Permian Basin has tripled since 2018. But according to satellite monitoring, the invisible methane emissions leaking from the basin’s fossil-fuel infrastructure didn’t change over the same time1. This suggests that the industry has made some progress towards controlling this potent greenhouse gas, which is responsible for an estimated 30% of human-caused global warming.
