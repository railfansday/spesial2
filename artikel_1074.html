How Andoni Iraola made Liverpool's fans - and players - fall in love with him: 'There's a connection building. He gets the club'

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Published: 21:00 EST, 27 November 2026 | Updated: 21:00 EST, 27 November 2026

The first sign that Andoni Iraola was different came this summer in Chicago during Liverpool's pre-season tour.

Sitting in a conference room up in the clouds overlooking the Windy City skyline and out to Lake Michigan, Iraola relaxed into a chair and held court for the best part of an hour. It was his first interview with the assembled Merseyside media corps but felt more laidback and personable than most of those chats tend to.

Most managers have little time for outsiders but Iraola, sipping a black coffee, was happy to stay put and talk about modern football, life in the Basque country and cycling. Only some of that conversation made the cut for the write-up but it all painted a clear picture of the new Liverpool boss.

Four months later, the Anfield faithful are slowly falling in love with Iraola.

It’ll take plenty more time and trophies to reach the adoration they had for Jurgen Klopp but, if Iraola can get halfway to that level, he will have done well.

So ahead of his first Merseyside derby, how has Iraola settled in at Liverpool – and why are the fans taking to him in return?

The Anfield faithful are slowly falling in love with their Spanish manager Andoni Iraola

Iraola takes training in Chicago on Liverpool's pre-season tour. The manager was relaxed during that time despite the pressures of a move to a huge club

It’s the little things that have helped Iraola forge a bond with the Kop.

He has been spotted on the parks of Merseyside taking in Sunday league football. He took his young daughter to a Women’s Super League game and attends her netball matches. And he has taken time to learn about the history of the club and the city, with incognito visits to tourist hotspots.

Iraola and his wife came to Liverpool on a city-break long before he became manager and loved the feel of it; it was a reminder of his hometown Usurbil, near San Sebastian in northern Spain.

He also enjoys cycling, a lifelong hobby, and his helmet helps him go unrecognised on rides around the city.

At the AXA Training Centre, there is a feeling of unity. Virgil van Dijk is a regular at academy matches, Dominik Szoboszlai dropped in on an age-group side last week and first-team players are tutoring youngsters and tracking the teams' scorelines.

None of the above might feel noteworthy in isolation but, to Liverpool fans, these little signs of a joined-up club mean the world. This is a unique fanbase which is often misunderstood: they want people who ‘get’ the club and the city. By immersing himself in them, Iraola is achieving those small wins.

‘There’s definitely a connection building between us and Iraola,’ says Dan Clubbe of fan channel Redmen TV. ‘First and foremost, matters on the pitch are starting to get exciting and the belief is growing as a result.

‘But beyond that, he’s very likeable and although it’s a cliche, he seems to “get it”. Small as they may seem, things like the WSL game, getting out to grassroots footy, integrating youngsters... that all resonates with us as fans.

‘It even goes beyond that. He seems to genuinely love it (here) and wants to understand better the opportunity he’s got, the place that he’s in. It’s still early days but it feels like he realises the significance and responsibility of his position.’

This time last year, the wheels were starting to fall off for Arne Slot. Liverpool felt like a fractured club

Tensions were rumbling around Mohamed Salah, who kicked off publicly and said the club had thrown him ‘under the bus’

This time last year, the wheels were starting to fall off for Arne Slot. Just six months after a Premier League trophy parade through the streets of the city, Liverpool lost 4-1 at home to PSV Eindhoven to record their ninth loss in 12 games, also including a 3-0 defeat by Nottingham Forest at Anfield.

Tensions were rumbling around Mohamed Salah, who in early December kicked off publicly and said the club had thrown him ‘under the bus’. He was dropped from a matchday squad and his relationship with Slot never really recovered. He is now at Turkish side Trabzonspor.

The best way to describe the feeling around the club last season was fractured. Sources point to how Slot kept the same outlook when things were going badly as he did when they were going well.

The place needed a fresh outlook. Iraola was the leading candidate to succeed Slot, though the hierarchy also assessed Pierre Sage, who has since been hired and fired by Crystal Palace, and Sebastian Hoeness of Stuttgart.

The Basque has brought back a feelgood factor to Anfield. Everything feels brighter, smiles are worn more often and even the online naysayers have had to find new things to complain about.

Captain Van Dijk has helped Iraola settle and took the squad to the museum at Anfield a fortnight ago to teach the new signings and academy graduates about the club. Iraola did not join them as he was too busy in the analysis room.

One of Iraola’s assistants, former Bournemouth defender Tommy Elphick, is a big Liverpool fan and that has helped, too. Elphick, alongside fellow assistant Shaun Cooper, has been influential in sorting out a set-piece record that was the worst in the league last season.

Liverpool captain Virgil van Dijk has helped Iraola settle into the club

Iraola with assistants Tommy Elphick (left) and Shaun Cooper, who have helped sort out the team's set-piece problems

Elphick and Cooper were with Iraola at Bournemouth and helped him settle into English football after his move from Spanish side Rayo Vallecano.

All of this is helping fans have a better connection with the players, and one small tweak has been to starting holding a team huddle right by the travelling supporters in away games. No team has more away points in the Premier League than Liverpool and Alexander Isak has scored in six straight away trips in all competitions.

It helps that the football is more exciting, too. Isak has scored 10 for the season already and looks a completely different player to the unfit striker that was bereft of confidence last term and managed only four goals.

Florian Wirtz has also been revived and this week publicly thanked Iraola – and his Germany boss Klopp – for helping him rediscover the levels which saw him earn a £116million move to Anfield.

The list goes on: Alexis Mac Allister looked like a man whose best days were behind him but now is back playing like the midfielder who won the World Cup in 2022, while Jeremy Jacquet looks a Rolls-Royce defender in the making. Milos Kerkez is slowly trending upwards.

And then there is Iraola’s faith in youth. Rio Ngumoha, 18, and Trey Nyoni, 19, have been trusted to perform by the new boss. Lewis Koumas and James McConnell have been getting the nod in squads ahead of senior pros Federico Chiesa and Wataru Endo.

Iraola, who is superstitious and tapes his fingers before games for this reason, knows the significance of this week: having thrashed Manchester United and won away in Europe against a decent Club Bruges side, a Merseyside derby win would complete a trio of fantastic results.

Florian Wirtz this week thanked Iraola for helping him rediscover his best level

Iraola has shown faith in youngsters including winger Rio Ngumoha - and he has been repaid handsomely

‘It’s a little bit like here – you know a lot of people on the other side,’ he said on Friday, comparing the clash with Everton to the derby between his boyhood club Athletic Club, of Bilbao, and Real Sociedad, of San Sebastian.

‘I have a lot of friends who are Real Sociedad and they have a lot of friends who are from Athletic.

‘There is always banter throughout the season and everyone wants to finish above them and win the games against them. It’s quite a natural rivalry. The relationship between them is normally quite good but everyone wants to win that game.'

Iraola went on to add that he is very happy in Liverpool, which he described as an ‘easy’ way of life. It will certainly feel that way if he completes the perfect double of beating Manchester United and Everton in the same week.
