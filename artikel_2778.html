Patrick Mahomes found his deep ball again in Chiefs’ latest win

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Kansas City Chiefs defeated the Atlanta Falcons 35-13 on Sunday to improve to 7-2 and tie the Denver Broncos for first place in the AFC West.

The Chiefs offense suffered multiple big blows from injuries before and during the game, but they made up for it with their passing attack.

Quarterback Patrick Mahomes had a massive day, throwing for 348 yards on 22 completions, a 135.4 quarterback rating, and a whopping five touchdowns, the most he has thrown in a game since his most recent MVP season in 2022.

Mahomes' monster day came from an onslaught of deep balls, multiple new weapons in the offense and well-timed protection sets from the offensive line.

A week after Chiefs fans held their breath when he exited the field with a visible elbow injury, Mahomes played his best game of the season and instilled a reinvented attack.

Josh Downs played only eight snaps on passing downs, but he made the most of them, and Mahomes made sure to find him.

On four catches in five targets, he put up 53 yards and a touchdown, showing the route-tree diversity the Chiefs coveted when they acquired him.

With a play-action look and an expanded pocket for Mahomes, Caliendo pulls, the middle of the field opens up for Downs, and Mahomes finds him for a 20+ yard gain.

This look is as easy a layup as there is for Mahomes, and it was a great way to get Downs involved in a play that helped the offense move the chains.

Later in the first half, Mahomes would find Downs for the pair's first touchdown connection.

The Chiefs sought a man-beating wideout before the deadline, and that is what they got in Downs.

Out of a tight bunch, the Chiefs go with a post-wheel concept, with Downs releasing all the way from the inside. The deep safety follows Xavier Worthy on the post; meanwhile, Downs already has a space advantage on the corner due to his delayed release behind Worthy and Cyrus Allen.

The corner squares up Downs, who slow plays it initially, but once he recognizes the safety go to the post, he plants and gets upfield in a hurry.

Mahomes maneuvers the pocket and delivers a perfect back shoulder throw.

The pocket isn't perfect, but with the front line walled off and the edge rushers running past him, he has time to step up and fire a bullet.

Downs' role will only grow in the coming weeks.

Downs wasn't the only Chiefs wideout to find the endzone on Sunday.

Mahomes connected with two of his newer wide receivers on big shots to the end zone, the first to Cyrus Allen.

This play is a simple concept being run to a high level, and Allen runs a fantastic route to find the endzone.

Only three Chiefs run routes on the play, but the stop-and-start move from Allen, Mahomes holds the safety to the middle with his eyes before firing it deep.

With seven in protection, Mahomes has plenty of time to drop back and fires a dart to Allen, who easily took it in for six.

Not every concept needs to be a complex equation; sometimes the best course is to let good players win snaps.

Later in the game, Mahomes found Armstrong deep on a third down.

On third down and one, the Chiefs run a roll-out look with receivers flooding one side of the field. Mahomes has time to throw shorter routes and convert the needed yard, but Armstrong has his man beat deep, and Mahomes knows it.

With a little pump fake of his own, Mahomes holds the defensive end in place and buys time to roll and throw the ball deep. With a big rip, Mahomes fires the pass, and Armstrong locates it in the air and comes back to it.

Mahomes lets it fly, and Armstrong is there to haul it in, likely securing himself more snaps and more targets moving forward.

Rashee Rice didn't find the end zone, but he led the Chiefs with 82 receiving yards on five receptions.

Early in the game, Mahomes and Rice connected on their usual underneath looks, with Mahomes doing a great job to extend the pocket.

These underneath throws have been the bread and butter for the Mahomes and Rice connection, but later in the game, Mahomes would finally give Rice a chance to show off his ball-tracking ability.

Rice and Allen line up out of a stacked look, and on the snap both run vertical routes, with Allen going up the middle and Rice staying outside. The deep safeties go after Allen, but with leverage inside, Rice continues upfield vertically.

In the pocket, Mahomes has to deal with pressure and can't even fully step into the pass, and it doesn't matter.

With a flick of the wrist, Mahomes throws a bomb, and Rice does a phenomenal job tracking the ball and hauling it in for a big catch.

Most of Mahomes and Rice's big plays have come underneath and with YAC attached, so it will be interesting to see if the deep ball connections continue.

Mahomes must have heard the ADOT (Average Depth of Target) crowd this week, and he made it his mission to attack the Falcons vertically. This performance also came as he missed his starting right tackle and starting running back; right guard Trey Smith left the game with a concussion, and center Creed Humphrey missed snaps after being gouged in the eye.

Mahomes is as good as ever, and while it is still to be seen if the deep ball can become a consistent threat in the Chiefs' offense, the building blocks are now in place, and the chemistry between Mahomes and his receivers is coming into its own.
