‘Rate It X’ Restored: A Documentary Traversing American Sexism in the 1980s Returns to Theaters

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Living under the pump of graceless digital advertising and toxic social media personalities, it seems like the world has less interest than ever in even bothering to hide the misogyny built into our social order. Nonetheless, there’s an arresting power to watching people witlessly admit to partaking in it. Released in 1986, Lucy Winer and Paula de Koenigsberg’s documentary “Rate It X” offers just that, playing out as a gauntlet of its time’s image-makers — from television producers to advertising executives to cake designers — casually describing how they use sexualized images to keep the material world turning.

Restored by IndieCollect in association with the UCLA Film & Television Archive, the “Rate It X” release has even been updated with a gut-churning, though perhaps unsurprising addendum for one of its interview subjects’ years after the film’s original release. “Rate It X” starts a weeklong run at IFC Center on Friday, featuring Q&As with the filmmakers over the weekend. It will expand to select cities afterward, before a digital and physical media release in mid-December. “We screened this new restoration of ‘Rate It X’ this summer in IndieCollect’s ‘Declaration of Independents!’ Film Festival at the IFC Center, and we got to see a younger generation respond to the film for the first time. One comment we heard over and over, and we quote, ‘This film is more relevant than ever,'” directors Paula De Koenigsberg and Lucy Winer wrote in a statement timed to the release. “What is wonderful about younger people today is that they are often able to see the problems in our society clearly and are ready to create change. Forty years after we first made this film, sexism is still alive and well in our culture.”

The duo continued: “Women have seen our rights attacked and minimized, particularly since far-right conservatives have taken hold of the government, and the overturning of Roe v Wade is just one example. It’s so important to us that the film is being re-released now because we feel there’s still so much work to be done! Not only to protect women’s rights but civil liberties for all. But now, just look at the way we Americans came out to vote in the midterm elections — many of us not only willing to challenge our own voting allegiances but that of our families and communities in order to fight corruption and support ethical reform. We are very hopeful.”
