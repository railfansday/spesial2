A 2-0 win over Haiti ends Pochettino’s USMNT tenure with confident hope

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Christian Pulisic and Julian Hall score in first half

US advance to Nations League finals 8-0 on aggregate

Mauricio Pochettino departs to take over Man United

The US men’s national team is through to the semi-final of the Concacaf Nations League, following Saturday’s 6-0 romp with a workmanlike 2-0 win over Haiti in the Cincinnati-hosted second leg. Christian Pulisic opened the scoring with an acrobatic overhead goal, while 18-year-old Julian Hall scored his fifth goal in six caps since debuting in September. Towering 19-year-old center back Neil Pierre was cap-tied to the US with an appearance, eliminating the potential for him to represent Haiti in future windows.

In keeping a clean sheet, the United States has now played over 400 minutes without conceding a goal, last allowing a goal against Chile on 29 September.

The real impact of the match, though, is on the sideline; the win marks the end of the USMNT’s Mauricio Pochettino era, with the manager trading his summer safari duds for an all-black outfit including a puffer vest to mark his final match in the role. The Argentinian will now follow his assistants across the Atlantic to try turning things around at Manchester United. That undertaking will be even more daunting than the project he inherited from Gregg Berhalter two years ago, due to the scale of the work to be done and the relative expectations around United and the United States.

Much has been written, here and elsewhere, about the short-lived and abruptly ended Pochettino tenure. With his time in the role having firmly drifted into the rear-view, what can we make from his two-plus years in charge?

Pochettino and his staff launched a deep and wide-ranging survey of the US player pool in their first year, giving 77 different players run-outs over 25 months. That work didn’t just pull together a credible 26-man roster for the World Cup, but gave the group a crucial lens into the considerable rising talent this program boasts. By the time this international window rolled around, it wasn’t a surprise to see Hall, Diego Kochen, Cavan Sullivan, and other young emerging talents in the squad. The culture, in this game and across both of Pochettino’s final two windows, felt invigorated.

Indeed, while Pulisic’s acrobatic opener stunned, Julian Hall’s cool finish under pressure to double the lead in the 43rd minute might be the most promising sign of the US’s direction. The New York Red Bulls striker has exceptional ability to find space in the final third and a natural finishing touch, and after Saturday’s appearance is now cap-tied to the US after interest from Poland.

The US are now a near-blemishless 6-0-0 since getting bounced by Belgium in the last-16. The rout of Haiti will have hardly surprised, but the previous window saw the US completely outplay Mexico and withstand a physical test from Canada, as well as multi-goal wins over Chile and Peru.

“The way we played, that is what we dream[ed] when we arrived,” Pochettino told reporters afterward. “When you talk about these games, it looks like it was easy. But Haiti is a World Cup team. The players make the job easier.”

Then again, Pochettino’s tenure hasn’t seen players pushed beyond their comfort zones terribly often. Tuesday’s win in Cincinnati was the 35th of Pochettino’s 37 matches in charge to be played within the United States. The other two came nearby in Mexico and Jamaica.

International coaches seldom book their team’s friendlies, of course, and the global Nations League era makes booking cross-confederation dates a tough task. Still, it’s one that the federation would be wise to incorporate between now and a 2030 World Cup played across three continents. Haiti, for as heroic as their World Cup appearance was, were simply not on the same level as the US tonight or in Miami on Saturday, where the same US lineup played through them with ease.

At this stage, the US is largely composed of two bands of talent. The relative old guard is the wave ushered in by Pulisic at the tail-end of 2018 qualifying, with Tyler Adams, Weston McKennie, Antonee Robinson, and Chris Richards alongside, all between ages 26 and 29. Sullivan heads up the next generation, a group teeming with potential who grew up aspiring to follow in Pulisic’s footsteps. A few others, like Sebastian Berhalter, Folarin Balogun, Gio Reyna, Malik Tillman, and Alex Freeman bridge the gap. While there are still untapped options to test – with Santiago Castañeda and Reed Baker-Whiting becoming the 31st and 32nd national team debutants of Pochettino’s tenure on Tuesday – the skeleton is there upon which to flesh out a dark horse challenger for a deep World Cup run.

“I am sure that this group of players will achieve big things in the future. I have no doubt,” Pochettino said. “Experienced players, with the mix of younger talent that is coming, it’s the perfect combination.”

Pochettino also made clear that this player pool isn’t one to relish playing as relative underdogs. This is a generation of aspiring entertainers, players who want to dictate the proceedings by carrying a majority of possession and fashioning dangerous chances whenever on offer. How to sustain that ethos in the testy arenas of World Cup qualifying is now somebody else’s problem.

Along the way, Pochettino had a knack for heightening the national team’s relevance. Even on Tuesday afternoon, American sports programs like Pardon the Interruption that treat discussing soccer like a taboo that would demand censorship threw the US’s second leg against Haiti into the rundown.

The US men’s national team felt more important, because Pochettino decided it was important enough to lead it. Time will tell whether his successor can demand a similar focus.
