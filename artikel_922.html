'This cannot continue'.- Ferrari to hold fresh Leclerc-Hamilton talks after collision

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Ferrari's drivers involved in costly on-track incident for second time in three races, with Leclerc given penalty in Vegas; Frederic Vasseur bemoans a "lack of respect"; watch every session of the Qatar GP live on Sky Sports F1 from Friday, with Sunday's race at 4pm

Ferrari team boss Frederic Vasseur says he will hold further discussions with drivers Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton after their latest tangle in the Las Vegas Grand Prix, admitting such incidents "cannot continue".

After costly clashes in Monza and Mexico City earlier this season, the Ferrari drivers came together when battling over sixth place with 12 laps to go on the Strip circuit in a race dominated by slip-streaming in which the leading runners were covered by a handful of seconds for most of the race.

Leclerc attempted to overtake Hamilton with a move down the inside of Turn Seven but made contact with the Briton, who was spun around. Stewards found Leclerc at fault and handed him a 10-second penalty, which dropped him to 10th place. Hamilton finished seventh.

In an unusually-strongly worded statement in Ferrari's post-race press release, Vasseur said: "For the second time in three races, something unacceptable has happened between our drivers, and this cannot continue.

"We sent a very clear message to both drivers in Brazil, and today during the race we told them to think about the team when racing each other.

"Of course, in this kind of race you cannot freeze the positions because, with the slipstream, if you slow down, the car behind will overtake you.

"But our drivers should have handled the situation differently, as other team-mates did both in front of and behind them.

"Charles knows he made a mistake and apologised, but that doesn't change the result."

Speaking to the written media after the race, Vasseur had said: "All the other teams, they were able to overtake each other 20 times during the race.

"[Kimi] Antonelli-[George] Russell, [Oscar] Piastri-[Lando] Norris, even Isack [Hadjar] overtook Max [Verstappen].

"Honestly, you can't imagine the frustration for me but, for more importantly, for all the guys at the [team]."

The situation is increasingly frustrating for Vasseur given it was only three weeks ago that he had to resolve the fallout from a first-lap battle between the drivers that likely cost the team a one-two result in Mexico.

Vasseur brought Hamilton and Leclerc together to speak four days later ahead of the Sao Paulo Grand Prix, a meeting he said had "cleared the situation" and acted as "a good reset on the mindset" ahead of that weekend. The Frenchman added that they even "left my room joking together".

Immediately after Sunday's race in Las Vegas, the Ferrari team principal held an initial conversation with Leclerc having asked the Monegasque driver to see him before anything else.

Asked afterwards what they had talked about, Vasseur said: "It was not a discussion, I spoke!

"He apologised, he understands, but the result is there. That you do a mistake, you say 'it's my mistake, I'm sorry' but you did a mistake."

Asked what would happen next, Vasseur said: "I want to digest tonight [after the race] and we'll have to sit down tomorrow with both of them and to take a decision."

He added: "I want, once again, to have a discussion tomorrow. Also Charles will digest the situation and I think it's not the right place to discuss with them two minutes after the race.

"I saw Charles a bit down and I told him what I wanted to say but I'd prefer to discuss with him tomorrow about it."

The Las Vegas incident has placed renewed focus on what Vasseur will do to try and bring his drivers definitively into line, with scrutiny growing on whether Ferrari will follow the lead of other teams, such as McLaren and Mercedes, and introduce more formal 'rules of engagement' for what is allowed when in team-mate combat.

Hamilton has called for clearer policies in recent months and said after Sunday's race that "hopefully in the background they eventually figure it out".

Vasseur, however, believes the chaotic nature of Sunday's race shows that clean driving between team-mates is all about respect rather than predefined agreements.

"Honestly, it's not a matter of rules," insisted Vasseur.

"What can you do differently except that you have to respect your team-mate? In this kind of race you have to overtake the car who is in front of you. You can't stay behind, it's completely unmanageable.

"It means you just have to do it with respect when it's your team-mate, to take less risk, perhaps to come side by side and to come back behind. But you can't put it on the paper. I think the race of today [Sunday] nobody would write a scenario like this. Nobody can imagine a race like this. It means they just have to act in a professional way.
