Steele stunned in overtime Lake Travis boots Knights from high school playoffs

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

SAN MARCOS - A silence fell across the stands of the home side at Rattler Stadium as the ball landed firmly in Austin Lake Travis senior receiver Westin Smith's hands.

Earlier in the night, those same bleachers jolted from stomping Steele fans cheering on their team that appeared bound for next week's regional finals.

But Lake Travis sent Steele home, sticking the Knights with a 39-38 loss in overtime in a Class 6A Division I area round playoff match Friday night on a successful two-point conversion.

The heartbreaking loss adds to Steele's list of games decided by thin margins against Lake Travis - almost all in favor of the Cavaliers.

"We knew it was going to be a four-quarter game," Steele coach David Saenz said. "Just wished it would've ended a bit differently."

Steele's offense was up first after Lake Travis won the overtime coin toss and opted to start on defense. Senior running back Marshall Kenner found the end zone for the fifth time on a 6-yard rush, and junior kicker Ralph Lara's extra point attempt was good. Lake Travis senior running back Teddy Lofgren responded on the Cavaliers' turn by taking the pitch and connecting with senior receiver Luca Antoun for a 13-yard touchdown pass to the front right pylon on a trick play.

Lake Travis Cavaliers linebacker Owen Aird (9) celebrates an interception with his teammates in the first quarter of the area-round playoff game between the Lake Travis Cavaliers and Cibolo Steele Knights at Rattler Stadium in San Marcos, Nov. 20, 2026. (Sara Diggins/Austin American-Statesman)

But instead of running off to the sidelines, Lake Travis junior quarterback Jason Carter remained on the field to go for the win. His pass went just over a leaping linebacker and into Smith's arms for a successful two-point conversion.

"I'm just proud of our seniors and the way they competed throughout the whole year and it went down to the wire there," Saenz said. "The last two-point conversion just didn't go our way."

Steele held a 14-point lead over Lake Travis at halftime, but the Cavaliers chipped away at the deficit, tying the game with 8:41 left in regulation off of a 37-yard field goal by Harrison Walters.

"Offensively, they were able to hit some third downs and convert and extend drives and get back in the game," Saenz said of Lake Travis, which went scoreless in the second quarter.

Lake Travis stole a possession in the third quarter with a successful onside kick, and Steele struggled to mount a drive in the second half to put the game away. The final three Steele drives ended in a field goal and two punts.

But Steele was rolling in the first half, despite a shaky start. Lake Travis scored first after capitalizing off of a Steele interception. Lofgren ran the ball into the end zone for a 3-yard rushing touchdown after linebacker Owen Aird picked off Steele quarterback DJ Green.

But the Steele offense immediately bounced back with a six-play scoring drive that ended in a 31-yard rushing score for Kenner. He continued his hot streak in the second quarter, scoring all four of Steele's touchdowns before halftime. Kenner finished his final high school football game with nearly 300 rushing yards.

"He's kind of the heartbeat of our program and our team," Saenz said of Kenner. "I know we're going to miss him, but he has bigger things to go on to, going to the college level next. He's just been a huge part of our program."

Steele finishes the season with a 9-2-1 record. Lake Travis faces Brennan next week in the regional semifinals.

"They came out and got hit in the mouth a little bit and they responded and kept playing," Saenz said of his team. "That's what I'm most proud of. Just the way they competed for the entire time."
