If Aaron Rodgers is done for the season, why are Steelers rolling with Mason Rudolph?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Standing just past the end zone of the Pittsburgh Steelers' training camp field on an August afternoon, Will Howard reflected on his offseason.

He'd dropped from 242 pounds to 230, he said. He wasn't battling the bone fracture on his hand that had slowed his rookie year. He was a year into learning his personnel, even if he was newer to head coach Mike McCarthy's offensive system. The Steelers' 2025 sixth-round pick admitted he was excited.

"I feel like it's been a long time since I've actually played real football," Howard told Yahoo Sports on Aug. 4. "And I think there's a lot of excitement around the Steelers and around, I feel like, me a little bit."

Howard appreciated the energy from fans flocking to training camp headquarters in Latrobe, Pennsylvania. But the Ohio State product was also realistic about buzz around the Steelers' younger quarterbacks.

"Makes me feel great, but at the same time, it's all anticipatory," Howard said. "I haven't really given them anything to truly cheer about. So I want to actually go out there and improve myself this year and give them something to really cheer about."

After Tuesday night's stunning benching of Aaron Rodgers, could that materialize?

The Steelers announced at 11:10 p.m. local time Tuesday that 31-year-old Mason Rudolph would be the starting quarterback for Friday's game against the Denver Broncos.

"Aaron Rodgers," they added, "will be inactive for the game on Friday."

The primary oddity lies in the Steelers' current decision, which feels sudden and strangely timed on a short week to prepare for a complex Broncos defense.

But there also remains the questions facing Pittsburgh's medium- to long-term future: How do the Steelers weigh their playoff contention this year against their quarterback outlook longer term?

It shouldn't surprise anyone that head coach Mike McCarthy would rather call plays for Rudolph, who has 38 games of NFL experience, including 19 starts, compared to Howard and Drew Allar at zero. Right now, the 6-4 Steelers sit seventh in the AFC with the final wild-card spot.

The 5-5 New England Patriots lost quarterback Drake Maye this season and, seemingly, their playoff promise with it. The 4-6 Houston Texans loom below New England — and also loom on the Steelers' schedule after the Broncos.

The Steelers need a quarterback who can keep receiver DK Metcalf in check, spark their run game and overcome protection deficits.

Perhaps Rudolph starts at least until that Sunday primetime game vs. Houston in Week 13, while playoff hopes live. And perhaps Pittsburgh's postseason outlook is rosier than its recent play in losses to the Cincinnati Bengals and Philadelphia Eagles, given the relatively shallow state of the AFC compared to the NFC.

Either way, general manager Omar Khan may need to ask himself: How long should I chase a roster on track for a first-round playoff exit, rather than build a roster that's set up to threaten for years to come?

McCarthy waxed poetic at training camp about how much his younger quarterbacks were learning in meetings from Rodgers' reflections on footwork, reads and plays in this offensive system.

The Steelers' closest taste so far this year was in the preseason with Howard, Allar (a 2026 third-round draft pick) and Rudolph.

Their surrounding casts varied. But across three preseason games, Allar completed 55.6% of pass attempts for 351 yards and two touchdowns and Howard completed 66.7% for 229 yards, two touchdowns and three interceptions. Allar also rushed for a touchdown.

In one preseason game, Rudolph went 10-for-11 passing for 93 yards, no touchdowns and no interceptions.

Rudolph was named Rodgers' backup when the 53-man roster came down.

But the question remained: Would the Steelers reach a point in their season when they want to evaluate the room more broadly before deciding how to approach the 2027 draft?

Rodgers had stated clearly that this is his last season. He was, at season's start, the Steelers' present but not their future.
