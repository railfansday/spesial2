4 Top Free Agents Come Off of the Board in Flurry of Signings

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

When Tarik Skubal signed his landmark $450 million deal with the Mets, it opened the floodgates of MLB free agency. Since then, four of the top free agents have signed in the last 24 hours. How do their signings affect the broader landscape of free agency, and what could happen in the final week before the impending lockout goes into effect?

Between Skubal, Freddy Peralta, Trevor Rogers and Kaima Taira, the Mets, Orioles, Cubs and Blue Jays have spent a total of $775 million on four starting pitchers. That's an average of roughly $194 million per starting pitcher. Sure, much of that is Skubal's bloated deal with New York, but even without him it's roughly $108 million per pitcher. If that's what teams are willing to pay for their starting rotation, the price tag on remaining options could increase. Clay Holmes and Michael King are the last remaining top-caliber pitchers on the market. Plenty of teams still need high-caliber starting pitching. They'll be forced to pay through the nose to get it.

However, several mid-tier options exist to bolster organizations' rosters. Matthew Boyd, Kevin Gausman, Foster Griffin, Shota Imanaga, Nick Martinez, Casey Mize, David Peterson and Nick Pivetta are all solid options left on the board. However, none of them are anywhere close to the caliber of pitcher that Peralta or Rogers are. Given the amount of money teams are paying for starting pitchers, it's difficult to tell how it could affect contracts for these players. Regardless, teams have plenty of options remaining. Whether those players will sign before the lockout begins next week remains to be seen.

With so few top options remaining, teams could pivot and upgrade their offense while signing mid-tier arms. The only position player to sign thus far has been Teruaki Sato. Sato signed a six-year, $120 million deal with the Cubs to bolster an outfield that had only 2026 NL MVP Pete Crow-Armstrong to anchor it. It's a large deal for an unproven commodity, but Sato could certainly prove himself to be a potent bat in the big leagues. Still, other offensive options remain on the board.

Randy Arozarena and Seiya Suzuki are the top options among a relatively weak outfield crop. Second base has plenty of viable options for teams to turn to, with Luis Arraez, Jazz Chisholm Jr. and Brandon Lowe all expected to garner plenty of attention. The remainder of the infield is fairly weak, with only J.P. Crawford, Ty France and Eugenio Suárez as top options for shortstop, first and third base. A number of teams have the financial flexibility to make these moves, including the Padres, White Sox and Yankees.
