Vikings vs. Packers score, live updates: Kirk Cousins returns to Minnesota in trade deadline stunner with Raiders

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

The Minnesota Vikings and Green Bay Packers will face off in their second of two meetings this season in Week 10, with kickoff set for 1 p.m. ET at Lambeau Field in Green Bay, Wisconsin.

The Vikings dominated the Packers with a 39-22 win in Week 1 and will look to keep that momentum rolling on the road to sweep their regular-season divisional series. And with each of the teams in the NFC North sitting with six or seven wins heading into Week 10, the outcome of this game will no doubt have plenty of playoff implications down the line.

The Vikings will look a little different this go-around, as veteran quarterback Kirk Cousins makes his first start with the team since 2023 following a trade from the Las Vegas Raiders on Tuesday. Cousins should slot naturally into Kevin O'Connell's offense, having spent the 2022 and 2023 seasons with him, completing 67.1% of passes with a 4.9% touchdown rate and 2.0 interception rate in that span.

The Packers enter this matchup with plenty of momentum, winning five of their last six games, with their only loss in that span coming against the Detroit Lions in Week 7. Even that game came down to the wire, though, as Detroit took a 20-17 victory thanks to a 22-yard field goal from Jake Bates as time expired.

Green Bay's offense is coming off its best performance of the year against the New England Patriots, as Jordan Love threw for 372 yards and three touchdowns, and the receiving duo of Christian Watson and Matthew Golden combined for a ridiculous 17-335-2 stat line.

The second quarter almost could not have begun worse for Green Bay.

The Packers started on the 13-yard line. Tight end Tucker Kraft was whistled for a false start, backing the offense up five yards.

Quarterback Jordan Love then threw an interception in the end zone and it was Harrison Smith who picked off his 41st pass of his illustrious career.

Green Bay is driving as the first quarter ends. When play resumes, the Packers will have it second and nine on the Minnesota 13-yard line, trailing 10-0.

Kirk Cousins' first TD in his return to Minnesota came at an unexpected time. Cousins and the Vikings were facing 3rd and 23 after a facemask penalty.

Under pressure, Cousins got the ball away to the left. Myles Price happened to be turning when he caught it, and the Green Bay defender ended up not tackling him, but pushing him into space. He ran all the way to the end zone for his first career TD.

After the extra point, the Vikings lead 10-0.

The Packers were rolling, including a little trick play with a direct snap to Tucker Kraft, who flipped it to quarterback Jordan Love, who then threw it for a 15-yard completion to Christian Watson.

After that, though, Green Bay had back-to-back runs that combined for three yards, and then Vikings defender Dallas Turner logged his eighth sack of the season to force a field goal attempt.

Packers kicker Trey Smack lined up for the 53-yarder, but pushed it wide right.

The Vikings got on the board first with a field goal by Will Reichard. Minnesota took over with great field position after the Andrew Van Ginkel fumble recovery, but the offense couldn't make anything of it.

The short drive was highlighted by Aaron Jones picking up eight yards. Kirk Cousins has still not been able to pick up a first down, but the Vikings lead thanks to Reichard's 34-yard field goal.

Minnesota got the ball back after the Packers ran just two plays. After a run for no gain, Green Bay quarterback Jordan Love completed a pass to Mark Redman for a gain of four.

Vikings LB Andrew Van Ginkel forced the fumble and Minnesota was able to recover and take over with great field position.

The Vikings were unable to capitalize on their first drive with Kirk Cousins back under center. Cousins began the drive with an incomplete pass to Justin Jefferson.

Running back Aaron Jones picked up three yards on first down, but then Cousins' third-down completion to Myles Price lost four yards with the Packers' defense all over it.

All eyes will be on the Vikings-Packers game as Kirk Cousins makes his return to Minnesota after the franchise acquired him ahead of the 2026 trade deadline.

Unfortunately for Cousins, his receiving options are pretty decimated by injuries.

The Vikings ruled out Jordan Addison Sunday morning, after labeling him as questionable heading into the weekend.

Minnesota will also be without WR Jauan Jennings and TE T.J. Hockenson. Cousins will have Justin Jefferson to lean on, but beyond him, it will be Myles Price, Tai Felton, Jeshaun Jones, and backup tight end Ben Yurosek.

Days after acquiring Kirk Cousins at the trade deadline, the Minnesota Vikings will officially have the veteran quarterback start this week.

Head coach Kevin O'Connell announced Friday that Cousins will be the team's starting QB for Sunday's Week 10 matchup against the Green Bay Packers. Cousins gets the start with quarterback Kyler Murray out with a hamstring injury.

"Cannot give him enough credit for coming in here, how he's going about the week," O'Connell said in a press conference. "It's been really impressive, what he's been able to do. Just the command and getting right back in the huddle with a lot of guys he's played with, but certainly some guys he hasn't. It's been something that everybody's been very, very impressed by."

While the trade for Kirk Cousins keeps the Vikings' Super Bowl odds alive with an inarguable top-two defense, the real story for us is saving Justin Jefferson.

The career splits are very, very encouraging. In four seasons with Kirk Cousins, Jefferson averaged 16.1 half-PPR points per game, which would be WR7 this year. Without Cousins throughout his career, JJ had averaged 12.5 fantasy points (WR16). This year? Oh boy, 11.4 (WR21). It's dangerous to assume an older version of Cousins will get Jefferson back to those mid-range WR1 highs with the Vikings running the ball more now than then, but fringe WR1/2 value is officially back on the menu. I would've treated him as the WR16-22 or so if it were Murray only.

Take Bowers' production as an example. Bowers' success rate on throws sits at 61%, while Jefferson is at 48%. This is partially because of the catchable target rate, which Bowers clears at 73% versus Jefferson's 69%. In terms of catchable targets per game, which is a better metric here because of how many turn-down throws Murray had in this offense, the gap widens. Bowers averaged 7.3 catchable targets per game with Cousins. Jefferson is at 5.1.
