Danaos (DAC) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Backlog climbs as dry bulk rebounds and a special dividend lands for shareholders.

By Motley Fool Transcribing – Nov 24, 2026 at 3:23PM EST

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Danaos (DAC -2.91%) reported significant growth in both net income and revenue for the third quarter, supported by a strengthening container market and a sharp recovery in the dry bulk sector. Management capitalized on scarce open tonnage to expand the contracted revenue backlog to $5 billion, extending visibility through 2030. The company maintained a conservative balance sheet while significantly increasing shareholder distributions through a higher quarterly dividend and a large special dividend. Despite ongoing geopolitical disruptions in the Middle East and evolving global trade policies, the company continues to execute its massive newbuilding program and strategic investments in related shipping sectors.

The Capesize market also strengthened with charter rates at multiyear highs, and our dry bulk segment contributed $22.4 million of adjusted EBITDA for the quarter against $9.1 million a year ago. We decided to defer the carve-out and sale of a minority stake in the segment in the Norwegian market for now so that any transaction is executed at the time and on terms that optimize value for our shareholders.

The Board declared a special dividend of $5 per share and raised the quarterly dividend to $1 per share, in line with our policy of rewarding shareholders. In October, we marked 20 years since our listing on the New York Stock Exchange, and Moody's upgraded our senior unsecured notes to Ba1 and changed the outlook of our Ba1 corporate family rating to positive, which confirms our solid performance and financial strength. Adjusted net income for the third quarter was $152.1 million or $8.32 per share compared with $124.1 million or $6.75 per share in the third quarter of 2025. That is an increase of $28 million or approximately 23% on a per share basis.

This improvement is the combined result of a $23.6 million increase in operating revenues a $4.6 million reduction in net finance expenses and a $5.3 million increase in dividend income by our Star Bulk holding, partially offset by a $5.2 million increase in total operating expenses and $0.3 million increase in loss of equity investments. Let me start with revenues. Container vessel revenue increased by $8.7 million to $247.8 million total. Our newbuilding deliveries contributed an incremental $8.2 million, reflecting one 6,000 TEU vessel delivery in the fourth quarter of 2025 that commenced a 7-year charter and three 8,250 TEU vessels that joined the fleet in the current quarter, each commencing a 5-year charter.

Lower off-hire added a further $4.3 million with container fleet utilization improving to 99.6% this quarter from 98.1% a year ago and higher charter rates contributed $0.3 million. Offsetting these was a $4.1 million reduction in noncash revenue recognition under U.S. GAAP. Dry bulk revenue increased by $14.9 million or 69% to $36.5 million in the current quarter from $21.6 million in the third quarter of 2025. The principal driver, of course, was the strength of the Capesize market, and our dry bulk time charter equivalent rate rose to $32,690 per day from $19,910 per day in the comparable quarter, while we also operated additional Cape vessel that joined our fleet during the second quarter of this year.

As a result, the dry bulk segment adjusted EBITDA increased to $22.4 million compared to $9.1 million in the third quarter of 2025, and the net income rose to $15.8 million from $3.4 million a year ago. Turning now to operating costs. Vessel operating expenses came in at $53.3 million, up only $1 million from the $52.3 million number posted in Q3 of 2025, notwithstanding the increase in the average number of vessels in our fleet. Our daily operating costs declined to $6,790 per day from $6,927 per day a year ago. Our operating costs remain among the most competitive in the industry.

G&A expenses increased by $1.5 million to $14.1 million from $12.6 million in the third quarter of 2025, and the increase relates mainly to higher management fees while corporate G&A was -- came in marginally lower this quarter. On the finance cost side, interest expense, excluding amortization of finance fees and debt discount, increased by $1.4 million to $9.2 million in the current quarter, up from $7.8 million in the third quarter of 2025. And there are 2 components to that. Average indebtedness increased by approximately $0.5 billion to $1.29 billion this quarter as we drew on our committed financing for our newbuilding deliveries. And that added $5 million in interest expense.

This effect was meaningfully mitigated by a reduction in our average cost of debt service of approximately 1.3%, reflecting lower SOFR rates and the lower coupon following the refinancing of our bond. Working in the other direction, capitalized interest on vessels under construction rose to $9.6 million from $6 million as our newbuilding program advanced, reducing interest expense by $3.6 million. At the same time, interest income increased by $6 million to $9.8 million this quarter from $3.8 million a year ago, obviously driven by higher average cash balances.

We also received $5.6 million in dividends from our Star Bulk shareholding this quarter compared to $3.8 million a year ago -- sorry, compared to $0.3 million in the third quarter of last year. Adjusted EBITDA increased by 14.2% or $25.7 million to $207.3 million compared to $181.6 million in the third quarter of 2025 for the reasons that have already been outlined on this call. We would encourage you to review our updated investor presentation and the subsequent events disclosures, both of which are available on our website. Turning to some of the highlights. Since the date of our last earnings release, we have added approximately $611 million to our contracted revenue backlog.

As a result, our backlog now stands at a record level of $5 billion with a 4.5-year average remaining charter duration, while contract coverage has already been outlined by John earlier. Within our investor presentation, you will find analytical disclosure on our charter contracts. On the fleet side, in September, we added two 9,000 TEU container ships to our order book for delivery in the first quarter of 2029. Our order book now comprises of 28 container ships with an aggregate capacity of approximately 178,000 TEU and 4 Newcastlemax dry bulk vessels.

On a fully delivered basis, our fleet will consist of 106 container ships of approximately 680,000 TEU and 15 dry bulk vessels, combination of Capes and Newcastlemaxes with a capacity of approximately 2.8 million deadweight. On the financing side, in August, we entered into an additional $200 million Japanese Operating Lease financing transactions with a tenor of 8 years for 2 of our newbuildings that were delivered to us in September of this year. Both transactions have now been consummated. In September, given our strong liquidity position and our committed newbuilding financing, we canceled our revolving credit facility, which was undrawn and was scheduled to mature in December of 2027.

Also in October, Moody's upgraded our senior unsecured notes to Ba1 and moved the outlook on our Ba1 corporate family rating to positive. As of September 30, net debt came in at $327.9 million, equivalent to 0.43x over last 12 months EBITDA. And out of our current fleet of 89 vessels, 78 are unencumbered and debt-free. As of the end of the third quarter, cash stood at $1.06 billion. And together with our Star Bulk holding, total liquidity stood at approximately $1.24 billion. In addition, as of today, we hold approximately $1 billion of committed undrawn facilities in support of our newbuilding program.

Finally, just to note that in October, we paid a quarterly dividend of $1 per share, together with an extraordinary special dividend of $5 per share for a total distribution of $109 million.
