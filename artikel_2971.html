4 Keys To The Lakers Surviving Against Kings In 106-104 Thriller

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Los Angeles Lakers had to earn every bit of their 106-104 victory over the Sacramento Kings.

In a game that featured multiple momentum swings and an 11-point Sacramento lead, the Lakers found just enough offense and defensive stops to escape with a narrow win.

They shot an efficient 50% from the field, but their 31% shooting from three-point range and 35-44 rebounding deficit made this far from a comfortable night.

Luka Doncic led the way with 25 points and eight assists, while Walker Kessler provided an important interior presence with 15 points, seven rebounds, and three blocks. Quentin Grimes added 13 points and seven assists, and the Lakers received valuable contributions from their bench, particularly Collin Sexton and Sandro Mamukelashvili.

Here are four keys that helped Los Angeles survive a tough test against Sacramento.

Luka Doncic finished with 25 points, eight assists, four rebounds, and two steals while shooting 9-18 from the field. He wasn't perfect, committing six turnovers and making just two of his nine attempts from beyond the arc, but his ability to create offense remained central to the Lakers' attack.

Sacramento had several players producing offensively, including De'Andre Hunter's 30 points on 12-16 shooting. The Lakers needed their own star to answer, and Doncic gave them that scoring presence while also setting up teammates.

His eight assists helped Los Angeles finish with 27 as a team. Against a Kings squad that had five players score at least 13 points, Doncic's ability to generate offense was essential to keeping the Lakers within reach.

Kessler delivered one of the Lakers' most important performances, recording 15 points, seven rebounds, two assists, one steal, and three blocks. He shot an efficient 7-11 from the field and gave Los Angeles a reliable interior scoring option.

The Lakers were outrebounded 44-35, including a significant 16-9 disadvantage on the offensive glass. That makes Kessler's work inside even more important. Sacramento's Domantas Sabonis collected 19 points and 16 rebounds, giving the Kings a major advantage on the boards.

However, Kessler's three blocks helped provide a defensive presence that doesn't always show up in the final score. Los Angeles also finished with five blocks as a team, compared to Sacramento's four.

The Lakers couldn't completely neutralize Sabonis, but Kessler's combination of efficient scoring and rim protection helped them withstand Sacramento's interior pressure.

This was not a game in which Doncic had to do absolutely everything. Los Angeles received meaningful contributions from several players, particularly off the bench.

Mamukelashvili scored nine points and grabbed nine rebounds in 20 minutes, giving the Lakers additional size and energy. Sexton added 10 points and four assists while shooting 4-7 from the field. Vanderbilt contributed four points and four rebounds in just 10 minutes, while LaRavia chipped in six points.

Thybulle also provided an efficient offensive performance, scoring 14 points on 5-8 shooting, including 4-5 from three-point range. Grimes had 13 points and seven assists, although his 2-7 shooting from the field was offset by an 8-9 performance at the free-throw line.

The Lakers' 67 points from their starting lineup were supplemented by 34 from their bench. That balance mattered in a two-point game, especially with Sacramento getting 30 points from Hunter and 19 apiece from Sabonis and Zach LaVine.

The biggest statistical difference came at the free-throw line. The Lakers attempted 22 free throws and made 17, while Sacramento went just 9-of-12. That eight-point difference in made free throws proved significant in a game decided by two points.

Los Angeles also forced Sacramento into 17 turnovers, compared to 15 of its own, and recorded eight steals. The Kings still managed 28 assists and scored 50 points in the paint, but the Lakers found ways to disrupt their offense and generate extra possessions.

That defensive pressure came at a cost: Sacramento scored 20 points off turnovers, while Los Angeles conceded 25. Still, the Lakers made enough defensive plays to keep the Kings from turning their rebounding advantage into a decisive lead.

In a matchup this close, every possession mattered. The Lakers' ability to get to the line and disrupt the opponent's offense ultimately helped them escape with the win.
