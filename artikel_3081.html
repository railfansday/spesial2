OraSure (OSUR) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Ebola demand lifted sales, but cash flow breakeven timing slipped.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

OraSure Technologies, Inc. (OSUR +2.62%) reported its results for the third quarter of 2026, noting a return to growth driven by expanded diagnostics and sample management portfolios. Management indicated that core business performance was supported by stabilization in international markets and demand for syndemic testing in the United States. The company is currently navigating the regulatory process for its molecular self-testing product while launching new diagnostic tools for Ebola and urine-based STI collection. Strategy for the upcoming year involves prioritizing operational efficiency and manufacturing consolidation to support a path toward positive operating cash flow.

Continuing now with more on each of our portfolios. In International Diagnostics, our Q3 performance reflected our approach to stabilize, diversify and localize our business.

We are building on our long-standing leadership in HIV testing while expanding our portfolio and strengthening partnerships that broaden our reach and enable us to address critical public health needs across Africa, Latin America and other international markets. Starting with Ebola. As discussed last quarter, in July, we received FDA emergency use authorization for our OraQuick Ebola 2.0 rapid antigen test. The test detects all 4 Ebola virus species known to cause human disease, including Bundibugyo, the species responsible for the current outbreak. Since the outbreak began, its scale and geographic reach have increased dramatically. As of November 3, WHO reports that there are now more than 10,600 confirmed cases and nearly 5,200 deaths.

Transmission has spread across multiple provinces in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and across international borders, including the first recorded cases in Kenya's history as well as the case in Nigeria. Against this backdrop, the need for rapid decentralized diagnostic capacity is clear. Rapid testing can play an important role alongside surveillance, contact tracing, isolation and other public health measures to identify cases quickly and help interrupt chains of transmission. Our test is particularly well suited for point-of-need deployment with its low infrastructure needs, speed and ease of use. Following our FDA EUA, we have quickly fulfilled initial commercial orders for Ebola 2.0. We believe, however, that the more meaningful catalysts for broader adoption are still ahead of us.

One important catalyst will be the results of the BRIDGE Access Platform study, which we previously discussed. Led by Africa CDC and WHO with Unitaid support, the BRIDGE Access Study is independently evaluating diagnostic products for the current Bundibugyo outbreak with the evidence intended to inform Africa CDC recommendations as well as national and regional decisions regarding registration and introduction of diagnostic tests. This is an important example of how the public health environment in Africa is evolving post-pandemic and following the international aid cuts of 2025. African countries are increasingly focused on building greater self-reliance and locally led health security infrastructure.

Africa CDC itself has made local manufacturing, reduced dependency and greater health sovereignty central elements of its health security agenda. That evolution means adoption increasingly occurs through African-led country-specific pathways for evaluation, registration, procurement and deployment. These include new processes that are taking time even during an urgent outbreak, but they also reinforce the importance of the investments we have been making to localize our presence and capabilities in the region. To best serve Africa, OraSure has launched local assembly in Uganda, where our partner is already expanding access to our HIV products and has the potential to add Ebola and other products in the future. Nigeria is our next planned market for launching our localized approach in Africa.

We believe we are well positioned as the Ebola response moves into its next phase. CDC currently assesses the most likely near-term scenario as a very large, sustained outbreak with high-intensity transmission in multiple areas, underscoring the immediate and continued need for a range of effective diagnostic tools. Our product can play an important role on the front lines and porous borders of this outbreak. Our manufacturing capability is ready, and we bring decades of experience and partnerships to readily serve the region's needs. Beyond this outbreak, we believe rapid point-of-need diagnostics can become an increasingly important component of outbreak preparedness and response and that OraSure is ready and able to play a meaningful role as these pathways mature.

Moving to sickle cell disease. We continue to support growing awareness and expansion of newborn screening across Africa, where Africa CDC estimates that 300,000 to 400,000 babies are born with sickle cell disease each year. This momentum is driving increased demand for Sickle SCAN and our successful integration of BioMedomics is enabling us to leverage our international commercial infrastructure to broaden access in markets with significant unmet need. We are also building partnerships with national and local health programs to expand point-of-need testing. Our recently announced partnership in Nairobi is one example, focused on scaling Sickle SCAN across community health centers and bringing testing closer to patients.

At the same time, our participation in the World Coalition on Sickle Cell Disease connects OTI with leaders across government, health care and patient advocacy to advance earlier diagnosis and strengthen testing pathways. We believe these relationships and joint efforts can help create the conditions for broader adoption of solutions like Sickle SCAN over time. In summary, the ongoing progress of HIV, early momentum of Sickle SCAN, the potential of Ebola plus the launch of our partners' near-shoring operations reflect the continued evolution of our international diagnostics strategy. We are successfully and increasingly pairing differentiated point-of-need diagnostics with localized capabilities and strong partnerships to expand access, diversify our business and unlock new growth priorities.

Over time, we believe this approach can continue to broaden our global reach, strengthen our competitive position and enhance the durability of our growth. Switching to U.S. Diagnostics. We delivered strong year-over-year growth driven by strength in HCV, syphilis and the continued underlying demand for HIV testing. Our syndemic testing strategy continues to be an important differentiator for us. With rapid testing solutions for HIV, HCV and syphilis, we give customers a broader set of tools to reach populations that face multiple interconnected infectious disease risks. We're very pleased with the progress of this strategy and the clinical and economic value it can deliver for our customers and the communities they serve.

Turning to a regulatory process update on our InteliQuick CT/NG test on the Sherlock platform, intended as a first-of-its-kind rapid molecular self-test. We continue to work towards our resubmission with the FDA following our announcement of withdrawal in August. We are currently working within the pre-sub process, the aim of which is to align on the best path to regulatory access. We have valuable experience and feedback from our prior submission and are leveraging that and this pre-sub process to evaluate a range of pathways that can bring this important innovation to market. We remain confident in the product's performance, the impact it can have on patients and our ability to navigate the regulatory landscape.

As announced last quarter, we received FDA clearance for our Colli-Pee Dx urine collection kit in June. We believe this technology can help expand access to STI testing through a more convenient and private approach to sample collection. We commercially launched the product in the third quarter and have been very encouraged by initial sales.

At the same time, we are excited by the pipeline of potential customers who see real opportunities for the technology, both the chemistry and the device, with many evaluating the workflow required to incorporate self-collected urine into their testing programs. This customer engagement reinforces our confidence in the opportunity for Colli-Pee Dx to support expanded access to STI testing over time. In Q3, we delivered strong growth in our SMS portfolio.
