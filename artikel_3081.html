Why Broadcom Stock Dove Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

One Wall Street analyst has concerns about the semiconductor and networking specialist.

Shares of Broadcom (AVGO -4.88%) turned lower on Friday, falling as much as 7.3%. As of 12:53 p.m. ET, the stock was still down 5.1%.

The catalyst that sent the semiconductor and networking specialist lower was a downgrade and bearish commentary by a Wall Street analyst.

Rothschild & Co Redburn Mike Harrison downgraded Broadcom to neutral (hold) while simultaneously reducing his price target to $398 from $595. That represents potential upside of just 9% compared to Thursday's closing price.

The analyst takes issue with Broadcom's customer concentration and increasing reliance on artificial intelligence (AI) start-ups OpenAI and Anthropic. He goes on to say that Broadcom's growth will ultimately depend on the two AI start-ups becoming self-reliant. Anthropic and OpenAI "need to establish sustained primacy over open models and become self-financing in short order ... clearing these hurdles is appreciable but is unlikely to occur quickly."

The analyst may have a point. While Broadcom's application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) -- also called XPUs -- are in high demand, the company is backstopping financing for some of its biggest customers. OpenAI is currently Broadcom's second-largest custom chip customer, and Anthropic is expected to surpass Alphabet as the largest purchaser of XPUs by 2027.

Yet the devil's in the details. Broadcom has provided up to $63 billion in financing guarantees to help Anthropic buy its chips, while backstopping another $50 billion for OpenAI.

CEO Hock Tan laid out a compelling argument for guaranteeing financing deals for OpenAI and Anthropic. "It's a great investment for us when you think about it: every gigawatt of compute they deploy, they could achieve $30 billion of ARR [annual recurring revenue], annual revenue per gigawatt. That's a hell of a business model."

There's no question that Broadcom is taking on a degree of risk by guaranteeing these loans for OpenAI and Anthropic -- giving rise to concerns about circular financing.

That said, at less than 18 times forward earnings, that risk is already baked into Broadcom's stock price.
