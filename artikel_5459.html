Yum! Brands posted higher revenue despite Taco Bell's cyclospora slump

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Taco Bell same-store sales fell 4% in the quarter, while KFC same-store sales rose 1% and Yum completed its Pizza Hut sales

Yum! Brands, the parent of Taco Bell and KFC, reported third-quarter revenue of $2.05 billion, up 4% from a year earlier, as same-store sales at Taco Bell fell 4% after a July cyclospora outbreak.

Adjusted earnings per share were $1.56, down 1% from $1.58 a year earlier. Net income reached $1.62 billion, equal to $5.92 per share, up from $397 million, equal to $1.41 per share, in the year-earlier period. The latest quarter also booked roughly $1.7 billion in pre-tax gains tied to the Pizza Hut sales.

Adjusted earnings per share topped the $1.49 analysts expected, and revenue matched the $2.05 billion estimate, according to CNBC.

Taco Bell division system sales fell 2%, and operating profit declined 5%. Operating margin fell to 33.1% from 36.6%. U.S. system sales dropped 3%, while international same-store sales grew 5%. KFC same-store sales rose 1%, though KFC system sales in the U.S. fell 6%.

Health authorities pointed to lettuce supplied by Taylor Farms as the source of the outbreak, according to The Wall Street Journal. Customers cut back on visits to Taco Bell, according to CNBC. Yum executives have described the July 18 weekend as the low point for sales, with results getting better afterward.

"Since the end of September, Taco Bell U.S. has been outpacing the QSR industry on same-store sales growth," CEO Chris Turner said in a statement. "Taco Bell will continue building on this momentum, with differentiated value and innovation that resonate across all consumer groups."

Yum stock gained over 3% ahead of the market open.

Yum finalized the sale of the Pizza Hut mainland China business to Yum China on Aug. 7, then handed the remainder of the brand to LongRange Capital on Sept. 1. Third-quarter results count Pizza Hut's contribution only through the respective closing dates. Excluding Pizza Hut, core operating profit grew 1% for the quarter.

Yum’s second-quarter results didn’t capture the outbreak. Taco Bell same-store sales rose 7% in the second quarter, a period that ended before the outbreak emerged in mid-July, though the company’s risk disclosures in that report already cited its impact on sales and the pace of recovery.

Yum announced in June that it would sell Pizza Hut for $2.7 billion in two transactions: $1.5 billion from LongRange Capital for the operations outside mainland China, and $1.2 billion from Yum China for the mainland business. With both sales closed, Yum operates Taco Bell, KFC and Habit Burger & Grill.
