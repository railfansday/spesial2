Aaron Rodgers' benching marks new era for the Steelers in so many ways

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The Pittsburgh Steelers have benched Aaron Rodgers in favor of backup quarterback Mason Rudolph.

Whether Rudolph is the QB1 answer, with his career 8-5-1 record as the Steelers' starter, is beside the point. What matters is that Pittsburgh made the change at all. Benching a quarterback of a winning team (6-4) is a sign a new era for a franchise that is no longer content to settle.

Rodgers, at 42 years old, has struggled this season despite the team's winning record with him as a starter. His 4.4% touchdown rate and 6.2 yards per attempt mark his lowest career marks as a starter. And his once seemingly effortless leadership has been outshone by his frustration.

Those frustrations, observed by those with a keen eye on the television, have left many to wonder — is Rodgers really the answer for the Steelers at quarterback at this point in his career?

And to be fair, Pittsburgh's fan base has been vocal in recent years about its discontent with the team's ability to sit with the familiar despite a lack of success. Sure, Mike Tomlin went 19 seasons without a losing season. And, for years, patience was the Steelers' default, even when the results didn't follow.

But the team's last playoff win came on Jan. 15, 2017 — the very game where Chris Boswell, a kicker, accounted for all of Pittsburgh's 18 points. The Steelers have dropped seven consecutive playoff games since that January day.

Regular-season wins are key, naturally, given that enough of them usually lead to a postseason bid. However, what comes beyond that is never guaranteed. And those playoff wins under Tomlin never followed.

Despite the lack of postseason success, the Steelers forged on, and Tomlin held his position as head coach for another nine seasons following his last playoff win. And for better or worse, the team never fell below .500 during Tomlin's tenure, nor had they ever drafted earlier than the 15th overall pick (without a trade).

To be clear, it wasn't a foregone conclusion the team would move on from Tomlin or that Tomlin would move on from Pittsburgh at the conclusion of the 2025 season. After all, the Steelers went 10-7 in the regular season before getting knocked out of the wild-card round by the Houston Texans. There was talent and promise there.

Despite that, once again, a less-than-desirable outcome happened. Steelers owner Art Rooney II still expressed the desire to keep Tomlin on board, saying it was Tomlin's decision to step down from the job despite being under contract for another two seasons.

It's not as though Tomlin didn't have a metaphorical scapegoat at times, either. With plenty of high-powered offensive weapons (and stars on defense, like former Defensive Player of the Year T.J. Watt and All-Pro defensive tackle Cameron Heyward), it was clear the talent was there. It just never resulted in playoff wins.

It's never just on the head coach. It also comes down to the assistants the head coach hires, even if those hires are ultimately the head coach's responsibility.

Former offensive coordinator Matt Canada? The offense hadn't ranked better than 23rd in yards or 21st in points in his first two seasons as offensive coordinator. And yet, despite plenty of backlash from fans thanks to those results, Canada had been extended for the 2023 season.

Then, there were the decisions on defense that came under fire during Tomlin's tenure, particularly regarding the role of former coordinator Teryl Austin.

Austin served as Pittsburgh's defensive coordinator from the 2022 through 2025 seasons, and while Pittsburgh thrived in terms of turnovers, the unit continued to give up yards, never ranking better than 12th in yards allowed during his tenure.

The team's most recent decisions with Tomlin, Canada and Austin have represented a level of patience with a coaching staff and regime that would be reasonably deemed infuriating to the average NFL fan. The benching of Rodgers? That changes the equation. Maybe this team is ready to make meaningful adjustments after all.

Rodgers' benching is not just a signal that this franchise is ready for a new(ish) quarterback; it is a signal that the Steelers are no longer content to be content.

The Steelers will not placate a former NFL MVP with the role of a starting quarterback for the sole reason that he is an MVP; rather, they will look for another solution, even if it is beyond the team's comfort zone. And that alone should give Steelers fans some hope in the direction of the team after several years of uncertainty.
