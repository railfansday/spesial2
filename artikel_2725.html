‘We need to stay on the same standard as the foreigners’: Aayan Khan on ILT20’s role in UAE cricket’s growth

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Dubai: For Aayan Khan, the value of the ILT20 is not just about sharing a dressing room with some of the biggest names in T20 cricket, but learning from them and applying those lessons to his own game.The 21-year-old UAE all-rounder enters Season 5 with Dubai Capitals, who secured his services for $95,000 at the auction. Having spent four years in the competition with the Gulf Giants, Aayan believes the exposure to international stars has helped him improve, not just in franchise cricket but also at the international level, where he's played 48 ODIs and 40 T20Is."I need to do well in this league. To do well, I need to learn so many things from these international players. They have been playing franchise leagues all around the world. If I can see what they do on the field and apply that in my game, it should help me," Aayan told TimesofIndia.com.His move to Dubai Capitals brings another opportunity to share a dressing room with established names such as Faf du Plessis, Marcus Stoinis, Phil Salt and Noor Ahmad. Having previously played alongside the likes of Andre Russell, Sunil Narine, Kieron Pollard and Nicholas Pooran, the UAE youngster says the initial nervousness of being around international stars has gradually given way to familiarity."I was a bit nervous at the start, but now I know these international players. They are so good to be around. They know me, and we know each other," he said.

For the UAE and Associate players, however, the value of the ILT20 extends beyond exposure. The competition's playing conditions mandate at least two UAE players, two Afghanistan players and one ICC Associate Member player in every playing XI, creating regular opportunities for regional talent to compete alongside international stars.Teams must also maintain a minimum of four UAE and four Afghanistan players in their squads, alongside representation from Ireland, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia and another Associate nation.Aayan sees the UAE-player requirement as an opportunity rather than added pressure, with consistent performances potentially opening doors to other franchise leagues."For us, two players to play in all the games is a big thing. If we do well, we get to play more franchise leagues," he said, adding that UAE players must strive to maintain the same standards as their international teammates.

"We think that we should stay on the same standard as the international players. We don't feel like, because we are from the UAE, that we are different. They make us feel that the international players and us are on the same page."Aayan's own journey has been rapid. He became the youngest player to feature in a men's T20 World Cup when he made his debut at 16 during the 2022 tournament in Australia. However, his career has also had setbacks, including a period away from the UAE national team, making his return a lesson in patience and resilience.He believes Associate cricket has grown considerably in recent years, with players increasingly making their mark on international and franchise platforms. Competitions such as the ILT20, he feels, can further accelerate that progress by giving Associate players more opportunities to perform at a higher level.

For now, his focus is on helping Dubai Capitals win. The auction price, he insists, does not bring additional pressure."It does not add much pressure. It is a happy moment for me. The team has shown interest in me, and I am happy to be in this new team. Hopefully, we do well this year," he said.Beyond the $95,000 signing, the larger opportunity is what Aayan hopes to take away from the season — more experience, a better understanding of the game and the confidence to compete with the best. For UAE cricket, the challenge is to turn such opportunities into sustained progress, with players increasingly able to translate franchise exposure into the international stage.
