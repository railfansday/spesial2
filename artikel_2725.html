Five Plays: North Carolina Second Quarter

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Lincoln Keinholz was very critical of his own play in this game, and this play is one that I would bet stuck with him. He simply reads this very poorly. They run a simple QB Power with Marquis Davis as a lead blocker. You can see Evan Wibberley and Eryx Daugherty turn their assignments out to the left of the play. Cason Henry works his way to the linebacker to create a seam as well.

The only thing I can gather from this is that Keinholz sees Johnnie Brown cross his face on a block that should be a wall-off to the right, and he panics. But that's being very generous. Davis gets in front of him and gives him a lead to follow behind. I just don't know what his plan was here. This was a likely touchdown that turned into a field goal.

There was a legit change in the defensive plan this week, and it was blitzing from the safety position. I clipped this a little longer to show Koen Entringer sliding over to the edge before this play starts. Notice that there is no adjustment to the motion. This comes after UNC shifted the two tight ends to the top of the formation as well. This was a clear call to get an extra rusher, no matter what UNC is showing. Really good to see.

As for the play, Entringer does a really good job of forcing Billy Edwards to climb the pocket. That's his number one assignment on this play. Make the quarterback move his feet and make him move up the pocket where other guys are to make a play. If you can get the sack, that is even better, but you have to get around the edge and get there with speed. Entringer does just that, and he reaches out to force the fumble. You can't do it much better than he does on this play.

This is just beautiful football. Every single player on this play does their job exactly how it is supposed to be done. It's the type of play I kill to see when I do these reviews because it's an example of what it should look like. When everyone executes, you end up with plays like this.

The motion on this play draws the eyes of the defense, which then gets doubled up with the run motion to the right of the quarterback. The goal here is to at least get the defense to freeze, which is exactly what happens. Then you have two things happening simultaneously. Lincoln Keinholz is looking to his right like he is going to throw to the motion man while Jaleel Skinner is getting depth up the field. Skinner sneaks behind the linebackers who are frozen, and Keinholz flips his hips to his left to find his wide-open tight end all alone in the corner.

I feel bad for Brody Foley, who no doubt had thousands of Cards fans expecting him to get his first touchdown here. This is at least the second time he's been left wide open while Keinholz picks a different option.

An added benefit of playing man coverage is that you force the quarterback to be very accurate. Billy Edwards was not overly accurate on Saturday, and I'm not sure it would have mattered with this type of coverage from Ben Perry. I truly hope we see them call the defense with this type of aggression to close out this season. It's well past time.

This is a really nice play from Greg Smith of UNC. Jaleel Skinner goes in motion into the line, and Smith just fires his gun to blitz here before he even knows what Skinner is going to do. It's a smart decision, because if you're Smith, you can judge by where Skinner ends up that he can't run a route. There's nowhere for him to go other than to block on this play. Good heads-up play by their best player.

I'm still not totally sure what the call is here for the Cards. Keinholz has a quick hitch to the top of the screen for a first down, but he doesn't throw the ball for whatever reason. He also has the running back on a quick flat route as well. This might be the target for this play. I'm wondering if it is a dummy look to the hitch, and Keinholz is supposed to come to the backside to dump it to the running back. It just gets blown up.

I'm not sure what this was supposed to be, but I have a hard time not thinking that this ball was supposed to come out quickly to the hitch. It's an easy first down. Not sure why he didn't let it rip, however.

Here is your weekly installment of Lincoln Keinholz doing something crazy. Notice that he waits for Tre Richardson to clear out the middle of the field before he lets this go. He also puts the ball high and outside to Lawayne McCoy to protect the ball and his receiver. This throw is about 27 yards on a frozen rope on third and fifteen.

One of my favorite things to see from a defensive lineman is sniffing out a screen pass. Josh Donald does a nice job of realizing he was allowed through the line, and when he does, he gets his hands on the running back and tracks him. It's hard to know what led Billy Edwards to not throw this ball, but Donald is going to make this a negative play in some way if this ball is thrown. Kudos as well to TJ Capers, who sees this develop and immediately fires to beat the blockers to the spot.

This was such a big play for the defense at a key moment. Entringer got the strip earlier in the quarter, and that turned into a touchdown. The defense followed that up with a three-and-out. This play was the next in line for a really great string of drives by the defense.

Stanquan Clark showed his true potential on consecutive plays. First, he shoots a gap by getting underneath the tackle who is crashing down to seal the backside of this play. Clark starts to lean towards the line at the snap, and he gets his shoulders low as he meets the block. That allows him to squeeze through the block before taking out the legs of the running back.

On the second play, he just explodes on the running back like he had a personal issue with the dude. These are the types of plays I expected to see more often from Clark this season. Not saying he has played poorly, but he can be this guy much more often than we've seen this season.
