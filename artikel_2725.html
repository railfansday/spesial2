Bruins Practice: Sturm Praises Top Four, Power Play Finding Rhythm

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

BRIGHTON – What's a day of practice like for the Boston Bruins after a 7-5 win on home ice?

Well, it was long, and it ended with some tired legs.

"It's a nice little battle practice," Hampus Lindholm said. "Little sweat it out, but got a few days here before a game, so it's nice to have a good practice here before your days off."

The Bruins are at home through Dec. 5, when they take on the Detroit Red Wings for their second meeting of the season. They do not play again until Wednesday, when they close out the season series against the Winnipeg Jets. The Bruins are set for another three days off. They've had that once already this season, but when they returned, they came out flat in a 5-2 loss to the Montreal Canadiens.

The team did not do video on Sunday, according to Marco Sturm, and when they took to the ice, none of the forward lines or defensive pairings were different.

Elias Lindholm, who scored four goals for the first time in his NHL career, led the opening stretch. He shook his head no at the end of practice when asked to go back in the center circle, so birthday boy Casey Mittelstadt took the reins.

While Lindholm was the story on Saturday night, the Bruins had 10 players (including Jeremy Swayman) record a point. Sean Kuraly, who has been skating as the fourth-line center, recorded his first assist of the season on Saturday night, a secondary helper on Tanner Jeannot's goal.

"Every time you notice if players are cheering for you, I think that just shows how much he means to this hockey team," Sturm said of Kuraly. "Especially in this room, guys are cheering for him, and anytime he's going to be on the scoresheet, it's even better. He's a great person. He was really good even when he was not playing. He would try to work on things where he thought maybe he was lacking, and he knows he just had to be patient enough, and yeah, it's nice to see he got rewarded too last night."

Hampus Lindholm and Charlie McAvoy have skated on a defensive pairing since McAvoy returned to the lineup after a six-game suspension. While the goals have not come much for the blue line since McAvoy returned, McAvoy finished with three assists on Saturday night, and they both finished with a plus-two rating.

This season, the two own a 57.87% share of shot attempts, and with them on the ice at 5-on-5, the Bruins have outscored opponents 14-9. The pairing has also limited high-danger chances, accounting for a 64.37% share according to Natural Stat Trick.

"We played together in the past. I feel like we feed off each other well," Lindholm said. "Like, I think we play our game, but it works out well together because we both like to play with the puck and skate, and close plays quick. I think we've been way better at that this time around playing together. Just being aggressive together defensively, but in a smart way, and making it hard on the other team and turning pucks over. I think we've done that as a D. The whole D corps has been way better, gaps this year, and just tightening up and being hard to play against, and taking some credit about being hard to play against because that's how we want people to feel when they play us."

Sturm has also kept his second pair largely intact since McAvoy returned. Will Borgen and Nikita Zadorov have operated as Sturm's shutdown pair. The underlying numbers don't favor them, as they have been outattempted 245-190, but Borgen (38) and Zadorov (31) rank first and second on the Bruins in blocked shots. With the pair on the ice, the Bruins have outshot opponents 107-102 and outscored them 10-5.

The top four give Marco Sturm plenty to work with, which he deemed a "luxury."

"We used Zadorov-Borgen a lot as a shutdown, but on the road, you don't have that opportunity sometimes, and that's why it's always nice to have those four guys, I would say, where you can use them anytime," Sturm said. "If Borgen and Zadorov are not on the ice, then McAvoy and Lindholm are on the ice. I think, as a forward, me thinking that's what I'm looking for, who's out there on D. And if one of them is always out there, I think it makes it hard for an opponent. So I think that's the luxury we have right now, to have those two pairs, especially, playing against the big line."

The Bruins' power play has seen an uptick in success over the past two games, especially the first unit. They scored in back-to-back games, with Elias Lindholm's first of four goals coming just four seconds into the Bruins' first power play opportunity.

"Yeah, they moved it better. They moved it better, quicker, faster, especially in Detroit. I think they just had that mindset, you know, that broken play Pav scored," Sturm said. "But just even slinging there and having that mindset, and instead of going on top again and kind of playing on the outside, I think they did a better job in Detroit, and they did a better job last night."

They went 4-for-31 between Oct. 27 and Nov. 17, which ranked them 31st in the league during the three-week span. Sturm backed his units, not changing them around amid the slump, and now they have clicked on two of their last five.
