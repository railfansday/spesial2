'We got heart.' Texas A&amp;M rallies from 12 down to beat TCU in overtime for first road win

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

FORT WORTH - Texas A&M had plenty of opportunities Thursday night to let its first road game of the season slip away.

The Aggies could have been overwhelmed by a TCU team that couldn't seem to miss from 3-point range in the first half. They could have folded after falling behind by double digits. They could have lost their composure when foul troubles and turnovers repeatedly stalled their comeback.

A&M erased a double-digit halftime deficit, forced overtime and eventually pulled away for a 97-92 victory over the Horned Frogs at Schollmaier Arena. Though the Aggies extended their unbeaten nonconference run with the win, the significance of the performance went beyond record.

This was a test of whether A&M (6-0) could respond when a game went every way but according to plan.

The Aggies committed 15 turnovers, shot just 56% from the free-throw line and seemed to be on the losing side of the whistle whenever they harnessed any bit of momentum. But at the half, they found the defensive adjustment they needed - and used the oft-potent attack of a "Bucky Ball" offense to make enough plays down the stretch and finish with a lead that, for 32 minutes, did not belong to them.

"Our guys have a tenacity to them," coach Bucky McMillan said. "We talked in the locker room before the game. The most important thing was to play off intensity and not emotion in the game."

In front of a crowd of more than 7,000 - with the few pockets of maroon making their voices heard - TCU (4-1) came out scorching from beyond the arc, sinking 13 of its first 16 3-point attempts. The Horned Frogs had 51 points by halftime, while A&M trailed by 12 despite shooting nearly 60% from the field in the opening half.

McMillan succinctly put the issue: his players were a step late.

"We were a second late moving when the ball moved," McMillan said.

That second was enough for TCU to easily find open shooters. Five of the eight players to take the court for the Horned Frogs made at least two from long-range.

But Texas A&M didn't need to solve everything at once; it needed to stay close enough for the game to shift.

The Aggies switched to leaning on zone defense in the second half, taking away the same perimeter looks that fueled the home team's early run. And, sure enough, TCU made only one of its 11 3-pointers after halftime.

A&M gradually chipped away at the deficit, eventually tying the game at 71 with under 8 minutes to play. Still, the game wouldn't let the Aggies have it easily.

A&M had an opportunity to take control after a defensive stop, only to turn the ball over. Another necessary offensive possession ended without a basket as free throws continued to keep TCU within reach.

Even after the Aggies took a one-point lead late in regulation, the Horned Frogs answered. TCU eventually went ahead 81-79 before the Aggies tied the game again and forced overtime.

There, A&M finally grasped what it had been chasing.

Jalen Shelley hit a pair of 3-pointers in overtime, while PJ Haggerty continued to shoulder the majority of the offensive burden. Haggerty finished with 29 points, 7 rebounds, 7 assists and 3 steals. Shelley had 19 points, 6 rebounds, 3 assists and 2 steals. Mackenzie Mgbako added 15 points, 7 rebounds, 4 assists and 1 steal. Jordan Pope delivered 12 points and 3 assists, and Zach Clemence - forced to step up in the absence of the injured Cade Phillips - recorded 14 points, 7 rebounds, 4 assists, 1 block and 1 steal prior to fouling out.

The final minutes still required A&M to withstand more pressure. This time, the Aggies answered every push.

For Haggerty, that resilience said something about he and his teammates.

"We got heart," Haggerty said. "We got dogs, and we're together. I mean, we all love each other off the court…When you love the person next to you, you always go hard."

A&M did not put the game away cleanly, but it continued to give itself chance after chance.

The Aggies' first road game became their first road win, and a much more revealing test of their resolve than the five victories that came before it.
