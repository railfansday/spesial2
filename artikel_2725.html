Klint Kubiak coached scared in Las Vegas Raiders’ Week 10 loss to Seattle Seahawks

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Fernando Mendoza's starting debut for the Las Vegas Raiders started out hot, with the Raiders taking a 10-7 lead into the locker room at halftime. However, a second-half collapse led to a 21-13 Seattle Seahawks victory, handing the Raiders their sixth loss in seven games. That leads to another somber mood for this week's winners and losers.

To Kubiak's credit, he had a good opening script that included a lot of easy throws for Mendoza. That led to scores (a touchdown and a field goal) on Vegas' first two possessions. However, the offense put points on the board just once on the eight proceeding possessions, including a run of five straight three-and-outs to begin the second half.

Of course, Brock Bowers getting injured early and leaving the game at halftime didn't help, but neither did Kubiak's play calling. He even left meat on the bone during the field goal drive in the second quarter, running the ball on third and five when Mendoza had completed six of seven passes for 41 yards and had an eight-yard scramble to get the offense into the red zone during the possession. The run, unsurprisingly, was stuffed for a one-yard gain.

That ended up setting a precedent for the rest of the game, as the offensive play caller got way too conservative after any negative play happened, allowing the lost yards to turn into drive killers. Sure, not having Bowers for the second half played a factor, but that's where a creative offensive mind should help bridge the gap by scheming other players open.

Ultimately, Kubiak coached scared by trying to protect Mendoza and ended up doing the quarterback a disservice. He admitted as much after the game, stating: "I've got to help him better in second half. What happened in second half to go 0-for-7 on third down is unacceptable," while also saying he should have called more bootlegs.

Stokes wasn't the secondary's biggest problem against Seattle and hasn't been the unit's biggest issue all season. However, he hasn't been the stabilizing presence he was expected to be either. The veteran is less than 200 yards away from giving up a career-high in receiving yards (417) this season and has already matched his career-high in touchdowns allowed with four, according to Pro Football Focus.

The biggest play that landed Stokes on the losers list this week was a 34-yard touchdown to Cooper Kupp — Kupp's only target of the game — on a scramble drill where the corner seemingly gave up on the play. That can't happen from someone who is supposed to be a leader in the defensive backfield, and he ended up giving up the second-most receiving yards (54) on the team for the entire game, per PFF.

While Stokes got singled out above, he didn't do a terrible job against Jaxon Smith-Njigba, drawing two targets and allowing just one catch for 11 yards. The problem was that the reigning Offensive Player of the Year was targeted 10 times and caught eight passes for 171 yards and a touchdown, and it's impossible to single out one player as the primary culprit.

JSN drew at least one target against seven different defenders and caught at least one pass against all of them en route to posting his season-high yardage total, according to PFF. The most notable catch came on a third-and-three scramble drill that left linebacker Quay Walker isolated against the receiver and led to a 71-yard touchdown, which is the play most people will harp on.

But the fact of the matter is that Smith-Njigba still had seven catches for 100 yards and five first downs outside of that reception. From defensive coordinator Rob Leonard to the players on the field, it was a total failure by the entire coverage unit.

Linderbaum faced plenty of scrutiny earlier in the season for not living up to his contract. However, the free-agent signing has been playing a lot better recently, and Sunday was arguably his best game with the Silver and Black. He posted season-highs in overall PFF grade (84.2) and as a run blocker (88.8). Since Week 7, Linderbaum ranks first among centers with an 81.7 grade.

Tre Tucker could be listed here too, because the Raiders needed a pass catcher to step up once Bowers went down, but no one did. Meanwhile, Bech drew more targets than anyone on the roster with seven and only managed to come away with two catches for 10 yards. PFF had him on the hook for one drop, and at least one more target could have gone down as a drop, too.

Also, contested catches and run blocking are supposed to be the second-year pro's calling card. He was 0-for-2 on contested targets and posted a team-low 33.5 run-blocking grade.

Speaking of run-blocking grades, Burford was one spot ahead of Bech with a 43.2 mark. He was also on the hook for three pressures — including a sack — in pass protection, according to PFF. What's frustrating is that the majority of the offensive line graded out well in the ground game, with Kolton Miller posting a 90.9, Linderbaum at 88.8 and Trey Zuhn III coming in at 71.7. But the unit was let down by one lineman and poor perimeter blocking.

Part of the poor perimeter blocking is that Mayer had 15 run-blocking snaps and posted the third-lowest grade (44.6) in that department. He also had a missed chip block in pass protection that led to Mendoza getting hit on a third-down throw, which was open for a first down if the pass wasn't impacted by the pressure. Also, Mayer is a prime candidate to step up without Bowers on the field. Instead, the second tight end had just two catches for 16 yards.
