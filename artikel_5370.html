The Skype co-founder who fears AI could end humanity might make billions in Anthropic's IPO

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Tallinn led Anthropic's $124 million first funding round in 2021, and the company is set to list on Nasdaq on Thursday

Jaan Tallinn, the Skype co-founder who has warned that artificial intelligence may wipe out humankind, stands to rank among the largest beneficiaries of Anthropic's IPO, according to CNBC.

Anthropic will make its Nasdaq $NDAQ debut on Thursday, trading as ANT. Tallinn led the company's first funding round, a $124 million Series A raised five years ago, well ahead of Claude's commercial launch, according to CNBC.

Tallinn's holdings remain undisclosed. With Anthropic expected to debut at about $1.4 trillion, his payout could climb into the tens of billions of dollars, though the final figure hinges on how much later fundraising shrank his stake and whether he cashed out any holdings, according to CNBC.

Tallinn estimated in a 2020 conversation with CNBC that humanity faces a one-in-six risk of not making it through the century, drawing on a book by Toby Ord, an Oxford professor. He helped found the Cambridge Centre for the Study of Existential Risk, along with the Future of Life Institute, the group behind the 2023 "Pause Giant AI Experiments" letter, according to CNBC.

Anthropic's earliest backers emerged from charitable donor networks anxious about the existential danger AI poses, according to CNBC. Tallinn and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei moved in the same circles within effective altruism, a philosophy that applies evidence and data to get the greatest effect from charitable giving. Tallinn viewed backing an AI company built around safety as among the most effective means of keeping the danger in check.

Anthropic has signaled to investors that it expects to price its IPO at or above the top of its $70 to $78 range. At the high end, the company would be valued at about $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis. Anthropic most recently secured private funding that valued it at $965 billion, according to CNBC.

Many people in AI safety circles object to the listing, according to CNBC. They fear that commercial success and a broader base of shareholders will push safety down the list of priorities.

Anthropic's co-founders will alone make up the membership of Founder, which will control 50.1% of voting power, according to CNBC. The company is also a public benefit corporation, with a stated purpose of the "responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity."

Tallinn declined to be interviewed by CNBC.
