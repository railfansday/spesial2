Honeywell International vs. Vertiv: Which Industrials Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Honeywell's broad automation scale faces off against Vertiv's surging AI data center demand, with valuation and risk trade-offs that could tip the decision.

As global data needs surge alongside industrial modernization, Honeywell International (HON +0.46%) and Vertiv Holdings Inc. (VRT +0.90%) represent two different ways to play the infrastructure boom. Which stock is the better long-term buy?

Honeywell International is an arm of the legacy conglomerate, focusing on automation, while Vertiv specializes in the cooling and power systems essential for data centers. Both are vital to the modern economy, but they offer vastly different growth profiles. Investors must weigh the stability of an established industrial giant against the high-speed expansion of a digital infrastructure specialist.

Honeywell International designs mission-critical solutions around building automation. It serves diverse clients including refineries, hospitals, and power grids.

In FY 2025, revenue reached about $37.4 billion, representing a revenue growth rate of roughly 7.8% over the previous year. The company reported net income of approximately $4.7 billion. This was a decrease from the prior year when net income was closer to $5.7 billion, showing a contraction in the net margin from 16.4% to 12.6%. However, these results are before the business split into two businesses, Honeywell International and Honeywell Aerospace (HONA -1.67%). On a pro forma basis excluding the separated businesses, Honeywell International produced $19.95 billion in sales and $1.37 billion in net income

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 2.2x. This ratio compares total debt to shareholder equity, indicating the company uses more than twice as much debt as equity to fund its assets. The current ratio, which measures the ability to pay short-term debts with current assets, is approximately 1.3x, while free cash flow reached nearly $5.4 billion in FY 2025.

Vertiv provides the cooling and power infrastructure necessary for many industrial stocks that power the internet. The company serves major data center providers like Microsoft Corp. (MSFT -2.27%) and Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN +0.05%). It also maintains critical partnerships with Nvidia (NVDA -0.44%) and Infineon Technologies (IFNNF -2.03%) to secure its supply chain for AI infrastructure.

In FY 2025, revenue reached around $10.2 billion, fueled by a high revenue growth rate of approximately 27.7%. Net income for the year was roughly $1.3 billion. This represents a significant jump from the previous year when net income was about $495.8 million, helping the net margin expand to 13%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was roughly 0.8x. This means the company carries less debt than the value of its equity. The current ratio is approximately 1.5x, and free cash flow, which is the cash left over after a company pays for capital expenditures, reached nearly $1.9 billion in FY 2025.

Honeywell International faces risks from ongoing litigation. The company also relies on a limited set of suppliers for certain components, which could disproportionately affect revenue if disruptions occur. Additionally, there are integration risks following the acquisitions of CAES and Civitanavi Systems, along with potential charges for businesses held for sale.

Vertiv depends heavily on the continued growth of data center and communication infrastructure, particularly for artificial intelligence applications. It faces stiff competition from global firms like Schneider Electric (SBGSF -0.83%), Eaton Corp. (ETN +0.07%), and Legrand (LGRVF -4.42%). Furthermore, the company manages a high volume of fixed-price contracts, which can lead to cost overruns if raw material prices rise unexpectedly.

Honeywell International is cheaper based on its lower P/S ratio and its Forward P/E relative to future earnings estimates.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

Honeywell International just reported its first quarter as a stand-alone entity, after Honeywell Technologies split into multiple businesses. The business saw organic sales growth of 7%, to just under $5 billion, with net income of $664 million, the latter of which beat analysts' estimates. For the fourth quarter, Wall Street expects Honeywell International to post $5.16 billion in sales and $691 million in net income.

Automation technologies are generally positive right now, especially in data centers and liquid natural gas facilities. Longer-term management sees climate change-related businesses, including carbon capture and sustainable aviation tech, as long-term growth drivers.

Vertiv, meanwhile, has been a specialist in computer cooling systems since World War Two, and it also offers complementary products for mission-critical technological infrastructure, such as uninterruptible power supplies. The AI-driven data center boom presents a long-term opportunity for Vertiv, especially for its liquid cooling systems. Liquid cooling is better for quickly removing heat from high-powered chipsets. Vertiv believes one-third of the total addressable market for data center cooling will eventually use liquid solutions (technically, some air-cooling and heat-dissipation systems will always be needed to work in tandem with liquid systems).

For 2026, Vertiv revenue should rise 44% to $14.7 billion, with net income shooting up about 90% to $2.5 billion as AI data center demand powers the business. Those figures are much stronger than anticipated just a few months ago. For 2027, analyst consensus calls for Vertiv revenue to jump about a third, with net income up more than 40% to $4.6 billion.

So which is the better buy for 2027? There is likely an adjustment period in the market for spun-off stocks, so it may take time for Honeywell International to find its level and tell its story more effectively to Wall Street. Vertiv is firing on all cylinders, showing exceptional growth for what had long been a steady, if unexceptional business. Vertiv shares come at a premium to Honeywell International's, but sometimes it makes sense to pay up for growth.
