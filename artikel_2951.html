monday.com's 2027 Outlook: Scaling Enterprise Segment to Drive Sustainable Revenue Growth

A monday.com (MNDY +2.60%) customer deploying over 87,000 seats is the kind of massive, enterprise-grade scale that would have been unthinkable for the company just a few years ago. Once a simple project-tracking tool for small teams, the company has evolved into a cloud-native "Work OS" where organizations build bespoke applications for everything from CRM to software development. As of November 19, 2026, the stock trades at $70.81, reflecting a 53% decline over the past year as the market grapples with a transition toward AI-integrated workflows and a more mature, slower-growth phase.

Like many software-as-a-service (SaaS) companies this year, monday.com was seen as exposed to structural shifts in how software is bought. The rise of artificial intelligence means fewer employees need to perform manual work in favor of using AI agents, making monday.com's seat-based pricing vulnerable to erosion. Fewer seats purchased equals lower revenue.

The company is taking action to mitigate this threat. It adopted hybrid pricing, where customers can continue to buy seats and also purchase usage-based credit packages for its AI offerings. The approach is working. In Q3, ARR for its AI tools hit $36 million, showing that customers are willing to pay based on usage.

Moreover, monday.com is successfully moving beyond small businesses to land larger, stickier enterprise contracts. Customers with over $500,000 in ARR was up 58% year over year in Q3. This trend bodes well for its long-term business growth.

While its trailing P/E ratio is not cheap, it's at its lowest level in the past year, suggesting now is a good time to consider buying shares compared to historical levels. Given monday.com's pivot to hybrid pricing, its solid product that is producing growing revenue, and its transition to profitability, the company looks poised for business expansion in the coming years.
