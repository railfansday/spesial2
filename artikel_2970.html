Nex Lex survives defensive showdown to shut down Heath

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

NEW LEXINGTON — A defensive battle ensued in Jim Rockwell Stadium on Friday night.

New Lexington and Heath combined for just 11 first-half points in the Division IV, Region 15 quarterfinal.

Both sides were looking for that game-changing play, and it was the Panthers that delivered.

Tyson Spicer's interception set up the go-ahead score, and Carson McCoy's 47-yard pick six helped second seed New Lexington seal a 24-17 win over the 10th seeded Bulldogs.

New Lex (10-1) advanced to host third seed West Holmes at 7 p.m. Friday, Nov. 13.

"They have a lot of great athletes and can make big plays. Our goal was to minimize their big plays, which we were able to do," New Lex coach Kevin Board said. "Our defense came up with the two biggest plays of the game. Our guys showed resiliency, and they answered the call in the second half."

Heath coach Tim Ward praised his defense for slowing down New Lex. Unfortunately, the second-half turnovers were the Bulldogs' undoing.

"They're a well-coached team. We knew they were going to throw multiple formations at us and use a lot of motion," Ward said. "Our guys lined up and played fast and physical, but they made a couple great plays on defense. The game was a battle for the ages, but unfortunately, only one of us could come out of here with the win."

The Bulldogs, who trailed 8-3 at halftime, took the opening possession of the third quarter and scored. A pair of third-down conversions set up a 16-yard touchdown run by Tristyn Jones, and the extra point put Heath up 10-8 with eight minutes left in the third.

New Lex tried to answer, but the Bulldogs forced a turnover on downs inside the Heath 20.

However, the game turned a few plays later. The New Lex defense tipped a pass from Heath quarterback Ethan Grise that landed in the arms of Spicer, who returned the ball to the Heath 18.

A few plays later, Colton Mathias bowled his way in from 4 yards out then Lane Ratliff added the two-point run for a 16-10 lead with five seconds left in the third.

McCoy then intercepted Grise on the next series and extended the New Lex lead to 14 points.

Grise helped the Bulldogs pull within 24-17 with a 2-yard touchdown run with 9:27 left in the game. However, Heath could not pull even, and New Lex ran out the clock.

Board gave credit to Heath's effort, while lamenting his team's mistakes. Those will be a focal point going into a semifinal contest with the Knights.

"Heath gave us the looks we expected, but they played fast, and their linebackers rallied to the ball," Board said. "We hurt ourselves with some penalties and put ourselves behind the chains. We'll have to clean that up, but our offensive line did open some holes against them in the second half. There's some good things to build on as well."

Despite the disappointing finish, Ward applauded his team, especially the seniors, for another memorable season.

"My son is one of these seniors, and I've coached them since biddy league. Their leadership and the heart they play with stands out about this group," he said. "This senior class is full of leaders. They're a special group who will be missed."

The game was scoreless after one quarter. Cam Montgomery's 32-yard field goal in the opening seconds of the second quarter put Heath up 3-0, but New Lex took an 8-3 lead into the half on Mathias' 11-yard scoring run and two-point scamper.
