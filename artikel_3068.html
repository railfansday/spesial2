Why Texas Pacific Land Stock Was Slumping While the Market Surged Today

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The company announced a roughly $3 billion acquisition.

By Eric Volkman – Nov 23, 2026 at 3:01PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Texas Pacific Land ›

On a hot Monday for the stock market, investors were rather cool on Texas Pacific Land (TPL -3.50%) stock. In late-session trading, the land management company's stock was down by nearly 3% after it announced the signing of a $3 billion deal to acquire a peer. The decline of the shares was counter to the general direction of the market; at that point, the benchmark S&P 500 index was up by nearly 5%.

That morning, Texas Pacific announced it has agreed to acquire EagleRock Land (EROK +7.72%) for roughly $3 billion in an all-stock transaction. Like Texas Pacific, EagleRock holds and manages acreage in and around the Permian Basin, a vast area in the Southwest that comprises the largest oil field in the U.S.

Upon closing the deal, the two companies will be merged into a new holding company called Texas Pacific Land Corporation. Shares of the current Texas Pacific will automatically be converted into equity of the new company.

Under the terms of the arrangement, EagleRock shareholders are to receive slightly over 0.08 shares of Texas Pacific Land Corporation for each EagleRock share they own. When the sale is completed, it's estimated that current Texas Pacific investors will own roughly 86% of Texas Pacific Land Corporation, and EagleRock stockholders the remainder.

Texas Pacific's board also authorized a boost to the company's existing common stock buyback program, to a total of $500 million. The company estimates that the annual run rate synergies of owning EagleRock will amount to $45 million. It also believes the merger will be immediately accretive to cash flow per share.

The deal has been unanimously approved by both Texas Pacific's and EagleRock's board of directors. It is subject to approval by the latter company's shareholders. Texas Pacific expects the transaction will close in the first half of 2027.

Texas Pacific's market cap is just under $18 billion, so it's understandable that investors are worried about dilution. But this is quite a complementary asset purchase by the company, and I think its synergies will make the move worthwhile before long.
