Tom Brady Admits He’s Made 'A Lot of Mistakes' as a Father

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Tom Brady got brutally honest about where he may have slipped up as a father.

During the Sunday, November 22, episode of the “High Performance” podcast, Brady, 49, acknowledged that he could be a “better” dad to his three kids. (The retired NFL quarterback shares son Jack, 19, with ex Bridget Moynahan, as well as son Benjamin, 16, and daughter Vivian, 13, with ex-wife Gisele Bündchen.)

“The important thing for me in my personal life is to go where my kids are at too,” he explained. “I think it’s been a really important lesson for me over the years to go to my kids and say, ‘What’s really important to you, what’s driving you, or what are you scared of, or how can I pay attention to the things that you value?’”

Brady broke down his approach to connecting with his kids, adding, “So part of that is just being really open and present and also trying to be really relatable to the younger [generation], which I was very fortunate to learn over the course of my playing career.”

When host Jake Humphrey recalled listening to Brady’s “Parenting Tips” podcast,the retired athlete replied, “Well, I’ve made a lot of mistakes.”

“So, a lot like [being a] quarterback. I threw a lot of interceptions. And [at] every one I’ve tried to learn from,” he noted. “And I’d hope I’m a better parent today than I was when I first started having kids. And hopefully I’m a better parent 10 years from now.”

Elsewhere in the podcast, Brody discussed how he has “doubled down” on not letting his kids “off the hook with the lessons they need to learn” in recent years. He explained that he is “trying to really be a mentor” for his children.

“My son said to me the other night, he’s like, ‘Dad, sometimes we sit down at dinner, and it’s just, like, a really easy conversation to start, and all of a sudden you get really serious, Dad,’” he recalled. “And I’m like, ‘You’re right, Benny. Sometimes I do, sometimes that coach comes out in me.’”

Brady has had to shift his approach after realizing how much his parenting mirrored coaching.

“You know, I’m taking these things that they’re giving to me and their lessons from their day,” he shared. “And then I’m trying to say, ‘OK, well, you’re 16 years old. I’m a little bit older than you. I was 16 [once]. I remember that this is what I would do, based on the 30-plus years of experience that I have.’”

Brady has been aiming to be “really present” and “non-judgmental” with his children. “Just trying to understand their perspective, but also trying to get them to understand my perspective a little bit too,” he concluded.
