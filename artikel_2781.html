NASCAR’s 800 Horsepower Move Changes The Game In 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

NASCAR is done playing it safe with power. The sport is dumping the 670-horsepower cap for intermediate tracks and cranking it up to 750hp starting at the Charlotte race in 2027. But the real shock comes at North Wilkesboro Speedway. Teams will run 800hp for the All-Star Race there, a massive jump that changes everything about how the cars handle. This move signals a clear shift toward more speed and less restriction for the 2027 season.

The new rule book details exactly how this power hike works. Intermediate tracks will see the bump to 750hp, matching what short tracks got last year. However, the All-Star Race at North Wilkesboro gets a special 800hp package. If the race goes well, NASCAR might apply that 800hp rule to all short tracks and road courses by the Bristol race later in the year.

Qualifying gets a shake-up too. Daytona and Talladega will use a specific setup just for qualifying, aiming for lap speeds near 200 mph. Superspeedway qualifying sessions will drop to just one round, making every single lap count even more. These changes show NASCAR wants to fix the boring parts of the race week and make the on-track action feel dangerous again.

Rules that held teams back are being tossed out the window. Splitter stuffers are gone from all short tracks and road courses. The minimum damper length rule has also been removed, giving crew chiefs more freedom to tune their cars. Even the double painted line rule got clearer language to stop arguments about where the apron ends and the track begins. These updates let teams build cars that actually fit the track rather than just fit a rule book.

Legacy Motor Club is already moving fast to match this new energy. The team just closed on an $85 million investment to expand to three full-time cars in 2027. They are building a new headquarters in Mooresville, NC, which will open in 2028. Seven-time champion Jimmie Johnson will stay as Chairman of the Board and return to drive the No. 48 car. Johnson told the team that "Our new partners believe in LEGACY, and they have a history of sports investment in the NFL and MLB. That makes this a special day for the CLUB."

NASCAR also fixed how points work for drivers who miss a race without permission. If a driver skips a race before The Chase, they start the playoff with only 2,000 points. This is a huge drop that makes missing a race a costly mistake. The rule applies to the Cup Series, while the O'Reilly Auto Parts Series resets to 2,020 points and the Truck Series resets to 2,030 points. The Cup Series rule change forces teams to keep their best drivers in the seat every single week.

Legacy Motor Club will field three cars next year with new drivers Josh Berry and Riley Herbst joining Erik Jones. Kyle Kneisly, a partner at Knighthead, said the group has "great confidence in Jimmie, the LEGACY leadership team, and the organization they are building."

The 2027 Daytona 500 will be Jimmie Johnson's final NASCAR Cup Series race.
