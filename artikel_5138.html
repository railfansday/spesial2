Bitcoin’s year-end liquidity hopes face a projected $150 billion Treasury cash rebuild

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Any year-end reserve relief depends on actual cash flows and funding costs, with a larger first-quarter Treasury cash rebuild already assumed.

The U.S. Treasury’s Nov. 2 borrowing estimates put a possible year-end liquidity benefit for Bitcoin on a short timetable. Its cash assumptions imply a fourth-quarter drawdown followed by a larger rebuild early in 2027, a path that could reverse the Treasury-account contribution to bank reserves.

The department expects to borrow $618 billion in privately held net marketable debt during October–December, $10 billion less than estimated in August. But that lower headline owes to a larger inherited cash buffer. Strip out the opening-cash surprise and the borrowing requirement is $24 billion higher.

Treasury entered the quarter with $984 billion in cash, compared with the $950 billion assumed in August. That extra $34 billion offsets the $24 billion increase in the cash-adjusted borrowing requirement, leaving the headline estimate $10 billion lower.

The end-December cash assumption remains $850 billion. Against the observed September endpoint, that implies a $134 billion decline across the whole fourth quarter. The projection covers the whole fourth quarter, including cash flows before Nov. 2.

The next quarter changes the direction. Treasury estimates $800 billion of borrowing for January–March 2027 and assumes an end-March cash balance of $1 trillion. Relative to the assumed December endpoint, that is a $150 billion rebuild.

If those assumptions are realized, the first-quarter refill would more than reverse the preceding quarter’s projected cash drawdown through the Treasury-account reserve channel. The figures describe quarter-end assumptions; they do not provide a daily liquidity timetable.

The Federal Reserve’s accounting explanation describes the channel. When Treasury pays from its account at the central bank, funds move into a depository institution’s account, increasing bank reserves, all else equal. Receipts into Treasury’s account work in the opposite direction.

A falling Treasury General Account, or TGA, can therefore support reserves. The eventual total also depends on Fed assets and other liabilities, including currency, reverse repos and other deposits.

The net cash movement already reflects financing, receipts and spending. Counting debt issuance as an additional reserve drain on top of that same net TGA change would count the financing component twice.

The latest H.4.1 release, published Oct. 29, showed reserves averaging about $2.89 trillion in the week ended Oct. 28, down $69 billion from the prior weekly average. Treasury’s account averaged $1 trillion, up $48 billion.

The Wednesday TGA balance was a separate observation: $1.02 trillion on Oct. 28.

Fed operations can also change the outcome. For Oct. 15–Nov. 13, the New York Fed planned approximately $15 billion in reinvestment purchases and no reserve management purchases. Reinvesting principal is distinct from expanding the portfolio. Future reserve management purchases remain conditional on reserve and money-market conditions.

The latest overnight observations available early Nov. 3 were for Oct. 30. The New York Fed’s reference-rate data put the secured overnight financing rate, or SOFR, at 3.95% and the effective federal funds rate at 3.88%.

SOFR was five basis points above the Fed’s 3.9% interest rate on reserve balances, which its Oct. 28 implementation note maintained effective Oct. 29. Effective federal funds remained inside the 3.75%–4% target range. The observation predates the Treasury release.

For Bitcoin’s liquidity thesis to strengthen, a realized cash decline would need to translate into easier reserve and funding conditions, then meet actual demand for the asset.

Treasury’s additional financing details are due Nov. 4 at 8:30 a.m. The issuance mix and settlement path will help define how the aggregate plan reaches markets. Until those details and subsequent cash flows arrive, the new forecast supports a possible temporary funding benefit, with a larger planned cash rebuild already on the horizon.
