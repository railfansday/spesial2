Top 10 best EFL League One players aged 22 or below

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

There are numerous exciting young stars in the third tier who have top-end Championship or even Premier League ceilings.

Now more than ever, youngsters are learning their craft in the lower leagues of the EFL before making a splash further forward, and with the January transfer window closing in, there may be some League One sides hoping that their young players will be left alone.

This list focuses only on players 22 or under who are currently contracted to their club, rather than on loan. These stars could well be the cornerstones of the future of these third-tier teams, but if not, their careers will surely be a success nonetheless.

This was difficult to do, but here are the 10 best players aged 22 or under in League One.

N.B. Reading fans - with Kyreece Lisbie turning 23 in a week from when this is being written, I've decided to opt for 10 players a bit further away from their birthdays!

The senior experience that Jake Richards has gotten so far in his young career has been exceptional. He's closing in on 100 career appearances, despite only just turning 18 back in August.

The 2024/25 League One Apprentice of the Year is yet another product of what has been a very successful EFL academy out of Exeter City, and at Luton Town, the midfielder is an important part of one of the strongest squads on paper in the entire division.

He's having to battle with the likes of Kasey Palmer and Jamie Donley for minutes, and is getting the majority of his minutes off the right because of that, but the fact that Jack Wilshere is finding ways to get him into the starting eleven just highlights how promising a star he is.

Richards has three goal involvements in eight starts this season, and his dribble success and the number of duels he's won are above average for his position in the league. He has plenty of room to grow, but also loads of time to achieve that growth, too.

Bradford City's jewel in their crown hasn't been as impactful this season as he was last year, but that's mainly due to the Bantams not packing much of a punch going forward.

That hasn't detracted too much from Bobby Pointon's promise, though, and the Bradford-born academy graduate is still held in high regard at Valley Parade.

The 22-year-old netted 10 and assisted three last season in his debut campaign at the level, and so far this year has scored a couple of goals, while also creating just under two chances per game, per FotMob.

Bradford will miss him for the next couple of months after he suffered an MCL injury. Pointon will be hoping that the Bantams sort out their form in front of goal in the meantime, as he could be a major player in a Michael Duff style when it's full-flowing.

Charlie Webster has been one of the more underrated EFL youngsters for a couple of years now, mainly because he plays for Burton Albion and isn't often in conversation right at the top of the division.

However, the ex-Chelsea academy graduate has run the show in the middle for the Brewers ever since he arrived in the summer of 2024, and more importantly, this season, he's remained fit. Last season, he missed half the year, and in his debut term, he missed 14 league games.

Now, he's in the midst of one of his longest runs in a side in his career, and has the numbers to show for it. Among his fellow attacking midfielders in the third tier, he sits in the top 3% for successful passes, line-breaking passes and touches, highlighting how in control of the Burton attack he is, and is in the top 11% for chances created.

Webster is someone to definitely keep an eye on come January, as his contract is set to run out at the end of this season. If Burton wants to cash in, he could be on his way to the Championship sooner rather than later.

In these kinds of lists, I always try to limit picks to one per team, but in Barnsley's case, and in another case which you'll find out soon, it's pretty much impossible.

Daniel Stendel's side is the youngest in the EFL, so it's no surprise that there are some real gems in amongst what has been a poor Tykes side so far. One of whom, Arley Kay, wasn't even a professional player until this past June.

However, in the early stages of this season, he's putting up some of the best defensive stats in the entire division. He's third in total tackles and second in total interceptions as of writing, per Opta, while no left-back has more defensive contributions, per FotMob.

At just 18, his ability to adapt to a professional level, and at League One level, has been very impressive, and has often gone under the radar due to Barnsley's current less-than-ideal defensive record.

None of that's been down to him, though, who has had to plug gaps all across the backline at times this season. Kay is one for the future, and if Barnsley go down, he won't be playing in League Two with them.

Like Kay, Karoy Anderson hasn't exactly been at the forefront of the conversation surrounding young players, as Blackpool have been very leaky at the back.

However, the Jamaican international has been one of the strongest defensive midfielders in the league this season, and if not for him, the Tangerines would be in an even more perilous position than they already are.

Per FotMob, Anderson sits in the top 5% of central midfielders in duels won, defensive contributions, tackles, clearances and blocked shots. He's quite literally everywhere in a defensive sense.

The Charlton Athletic academy graduate may not have been completely ready for the Championship last year, but it's quickly becoming the case that letting him go permanently this past summer is proving to be a mistake.

Joe Whitworth had some big shoes to fill in the Wigan Athletic goal this past summer, as academy graduate Sam Tickle made the step-up to the Championship with Bristol City, and he's doing an incredible job so far.

The former Crystal Palace man reunited with Gary Caldwell, who had loaned him to Exeter twice while he was there, and the 22-year-old is arguably playing his best football at the Brick Community Stadium.

He makes close to 3.5 saves per 90 at a 71.4% rate, and the 6.01 goals he's prevented is higher than anyone else in the division. Whitworth saves Wigan points, as seen at Blackpool when he stopped Niall Ennis one-on-one.

Whitworth's kicking is also well above league average, which bodes well for a Latics side keen to build through him under Caldwell.

There are arguments to be made that the 22-year-old is the best goalkeeper in the third tier right now, but that's for another list. For now, he sits in the top five under 22.

Russell Martin's switch to a back three in the last month has really gotten the best out of Leicester City's wide men. Will Alves has been revitalised as a right-wing-back, and is arguably one of the best in his position in the league at the moment.

Leicester's academy was always going to be profiled at this level, as the Foxes' slide down the EFL meant their prospects were actually as good as what was required for the first team (and we'll see another example of that in a bit), but Alves has taken this chance with both hands.

The 21-year-old was exciting in a loan spell with Cardiff City back in 2025 in the Championship, and never really had the chance to be a top winger in the third tier with Huddersfield Town last season due to injury.

Now, he's showing his second-tier quality, especially when it comes to taking people on. The fact that he's completed 24 dribbles, the most in his position, at an 80% rate is quite absurd, and he's among the best chance creators because of that.

Only Stockport County centre-back Eoghan O'Connell has carried the ball further than he has, and no player has created more chances off the back of dribbles than the Leicester man, either.

His open-play expected assists tally is third in League One. He's quickly becoming one of the more important players in Russell Martin's side, and well worthy of a place near the top of this list.

Reyes Cleary may be having somewhat of a down year by his own lofty standards, given he registered 20 goal contributions in 30 starts for Barnsley in his debut campaign last season, but there are few more dangerous out wide in a one-on-one situation than the 22-year-old.

He's starting to find his groove as a chance creator once more, too, with identical assists in recent league games against Wigan and Sheffield Wednesday, driving to the byline and crossing in for a box-crashing midfielder.

Cleary needs to do that more, as he's practically unstoppable from that position, but he can still be dangerous cutting in and shooting on his right, too. If anything, that makes him even harder to handle.

Barnsley fans are almost resigned to the possibility of the winger leaving in January, especially if they're still residing in the relegation zone by then, as his value won't be as high if he ends up being a League Two player in the summer.

Perhaps then, we need to appreciate the 22-year-old while he's still in the third tier.

While Cleary often gets the plaudits from critics given how exciting he was last season and how dangerous he can be out wide, it's Patrick Kelly who has arguably been Barnsley's best performer so far this season.

The Northern Irish international has been brilliant with the ball going forward this season. Only Derensili Sanches Fernandes and Oliver Norwood have created more open play chances this season, and only Alves and Sanches Fernandes have done so after carrying the ball.

Kelly has also thrived in this high-intensity playstyle that Daniel Stendel has deployed at Oakwell, too, ranking high amongst attacking midfielders in the number of duels he wins, recoveries and defensive contributions, per FotMob.

He's a perfect attacking midfielder, and one that Barnsley will be desperate to keep hold of past the January window.

Topping the pile, though, is Leicester's Louis Page, who arguably has the highest ceiling out of any League One midfielder in the division's history. The recently turned 18-year-old had links with Manchester United and Arsenal over the summer, which says a lot.

Page has been perfect for Russell Martin and how he wants to play. The youngster is so calm on the ball, sitting top 12 in open play passes per 90 and sixth in final third passes per 90. He can control things from deep and, recently, can impact them further forward, too.
