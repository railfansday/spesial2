Cowboys roundtable discussion: Christian Parker, Christian McCaffrey, and Tennessee Titans

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Every week, we gather to discuss the latest news about the Dallas Cowboys and seek our writer's perspective on each headline. Welcome back to the roundtable. This week we have Jorge Guajardo, Dyllan Rivers, Tom Ryle and Sean Martin.

Mike – The biggest responsibility falls squarely on Christian Parker here. Players have to execute, maintain their assignments and make tackles, but when your defense surrenders a franchise-record 584 yards and 46 points at home, that's a complete failure of coaching.

Kyle Shanahan repeatedly exposed Dallas with misdirection, poor defensive positioning and mismatches that Parker simply had no answers for. Parker is being outcoached every week, and the decision to strip him of play-calling duties tells you everything about how badly things have gone.

Jorge – Both. Pairing a rookie DC with this roster is a bad combination, and the results show every week. From a player standpoint, watching the film there are some guys not playing through the whistle, with some jogging while players appeared to be still in the mix.

As for Parker, he's in way over his head, just like his head coach. Now, I think something needs to be said for good coaching. I think good coaching gets you to hustle and finish every play. Good coaching arms you with the knowledge to take the right angles to stop a certain play; good coaching puts you in positions to succeed, but none of that is apparent on this defense. Yes, the players also have responsibility. So this is a result of a match made in hell with Parker and a poor defensive roster.

Tom – Really, that's the only answer that makes sense. It's a perfect storm of ineptitude and it is threatening to sink Dallas. There are some talented players on the defensive side of the ball, but a combination of misuse and not everyone shouldering their share of the load is nullifying it. And let's not forget the role the head coach plays in the motivation arena. I don't know what is going on in the locker room, but I can't help but worry that Schottenheimer is on the verge of losing it – if he hasn't already.

Sean – All the respect in the world to Von Miller, but this was on coaching. It's being kind to only use the last three weeks as a sample size, but just in this stretch where the Cowboys are 1-2 against two losing teams and a 49ers team they let break records on them, the explosive plays allowed are outrageous.

Look elsewhere around the NFL, and games are being decided by razor thin margins even between teams you might expect to have a little more of a gap. Explosive plays are earned through a clear, visible effort from coaches to put players in those positions. Christian Parker's defense is the antithesis to this. Whenever an offense needs a huge play, even for a touchdown, they can get it.

Dyllan – All of the above. Christian Parker can't control Elijah Garcia and James Houston chasing down a phantom running back 40 yards the wrong direction instead of containing the Deebo Samuel reverse. But Parker can decide to not call a zero blitz in the most crucial moment of the game, leaving Von Miller one-on-one with Christian McCaffrey. The perfect blend of mediocrity and misery has found its way to the Cowboys defense.

Mike – For me, this comes down to poor coaching, dreadful defensive discipline and Christian Parker repeatedly putting his players in positions to fail. Having a 37-year-old Von Miller isolated against McCaffrey in coverage is baffling and the best example of my statement of Parker putting players in positions to fail, he has to take responsibility for allowing such a ridiculous mismatch. The Cowboys continually lose their assignments, take poor tackling angles and fail to communicate, but these are recurring problems that should be getting corrected on the practice field. When the same mistakes happen every week, it's no longer just poor execution, it's poor coaching.

Jorge – In a game where the Cowboys gave up three plays over 65 yards that all ended in TDs, it's hard to say that this is where the game was lost. However, the offense had clawed back to within one score; it was a 2nd-and-9, and a stop gives you a chance. You would think that in a game where your defense has already given up two disastrously long plays, you wouldn't ask your 37-year-old, pass-rushing specialist linebacker to cover a back like CMC in his prime, right? Wrong.

That's exactly what Christian Parker did. A lot of people have said it was just a great counter from Shanahan to route CMC right at Miller. My counter is they're right. Shanahan is a great coach with an incredible feel for the game, its moments and his roster. Christian Parker is not. How are you not prepared for that alternative? There never seems to be any contingencies with this team, and it's defense. They simply run what they run, fail because their plans never survive contact with the enemy, and they have nothing prepared for the possibility that their plans may not work. Parker left Miller out to dry.

Tom – It is beyond embarrassing how the Cowboys just let one or more players absolutely go off on them every week. The topic of Schottenheimer's job security is a popular one, but the seat is far hotter under Parker. A well run team would likely write him off as a failed one-year experiment and start planning for the next DC. That would hardly be helpful for the players to have to go into the third new system in three years, but what is the point in prolonging the bleeding?

Unfortunately, that part about a well run team really doesn't apply for Dallas. Worse, there is no likelihood that is going to change.

Sean – Maybe we should have just known better to not have hope during another frantic fourth quarter comeback by the Cowboys, but for all the ones in the past that felt pointless and hollow, this one against the 7-1 49ers actually felt real. The offense earned back a legitimate chance to retake the lead down by six, the comeback had legs. Then in the blink of an eye, McCaffrey is literally running so free that a Dallas defender couldn't even dream of touching him. There was a 0% chance to make a tackle on that play, zero.

It was sickening, inexcusable, and to answer your question, yes the most baffling decision of the game.

Dyllan – I don't believe having Von Miller man up against Christian McCaffrey is something the 31 other defensive coordinators in the NFL would ever dream of doing, especially in clutch time. As far as the other explosives, it's coming from a blend of losing the schematic chess match, poor tackling, and players simply making bad decisions. The fundamentals are missing for the second year in a row. The explosives will continue for as long as the most basic elements of defense are lost.

Mike – Absolutely. Anything less than a convincing victory against Tennessee would be deeply concerning, and losing this game should effectively end any serious conversation about Dallas making the playoffs. Brian Schottenheimer needs to have this team prepared, aggressive and ready to take control early. More importantly, the defense must finally show it can handle an opponent without surrendering ridiculous explosive plays.

Jorge – The Cowboys will almost certainly make this a game, as they did against Indianapolis. The defense can only play solid for an entire half, so expect this to go down to the wire. Tennessee is not in the business of winning football games this year; I mean, neither are the Cowboys, although there is an illusion of it, but that's a different story. That said, if the Cowboys lose to Tennessee on Sunday, the season is over, and the staff will pay with their jobs come season's end. What a terrible season.

Tom – To your last question, yes, it would be the effective end of things. I just don't see this team as having the resiliency to come back from that level of embarrassment, and while I agree that they will probably win, it is not a given.

So we may see the flickering hopes for making something out of this year resting on Parker/Mike Pettine figuring things out. That is not good. The play-calling change change might shake things up and get a different fire lit under the players.

Sean – I don't even want to slap a must win label on this one, because that also carries the connotation of a team building towards a goal by getting such wins, and the Cowboys don't have that right now. They could win the next two and the only realistic take to have about it is that the team's best case scenario is a repeat of last year, playing well through a stretch in November and that's it.

Even if they get these two wins, they would need one more to match last year's win total and two to pass. The most likely two that Dallas can win are against their own division to end the season, and that's just the worst sequel of all time at this point when it comes to not taking the bait over beating bad division opponents.

Beating the Titans would make Thanksgiving a little more enjoyable to not feel like absolutely all hope is lost by the holiday, but I think that's about it.

Dyllan – Simply put, losing any more games against bad teams effectively ends the season. The "easy wins" need to be easy while Dallas also needs to steal potentially two out of three games against Jacksonville, Seattle, and the Rams. The Giants are helping the Cowboys stay alive, so Dallas needs to capitalize on the gift.
