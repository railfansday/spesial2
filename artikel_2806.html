Why is Luke Butkus exempt from public criticism?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Matt LaFleur has no problem criticizing the offensive line. He has no problems saying there are issues with how the offensive line is coached. He, like everyone else, knows the offensive line is a problem.

So why is his offensive line coach exempt from criticism? Bring up the name Luke Butkus and suddenly the offensive line picture is rosy.

"He was the same guy when we were rolling," LaFleur said of Butkus after the Packers' latest offensive line collapse. "I think it's easy to pinpoint and point the finger, and everybody wants to pinpoint blame, but the blame can go to everybody. So it's all of us in this together. Certainly it starts with myself, and then it goes to everybody else, and it's coaches and players collectively."

According to LaFleur, then, it's not Butkus's fault, but it also is his fault, and it's the players' fault, and it's the other coaches' fault, and possibly the general manager's fault. It might also be your fault, which is something I hadn't considered before. I've been spending Packers' gamedays just watching their offensive line get shredded and never once have I tried to do anything about it. I'm sorry. It starts with me.

But why can't Butkus be criticized? Why is it impossible that he's part of the problem?

I understand that LaFleur isn't going to stand at the lectern and blast one of his coaches any more than he's going to single out an individual player for post-loss criticism delivered through the media, but it's insulting to our collective football intelligence that he won't even entertain the question.

That's not hyperbole, lest we forget. After an embarrassing Thursday Night Football loss to the Atlanta Falcons earlier this year, reporters brought Luke Butkus up as a possible reason for the poor offensive line performance. LaFleur said "I'm not even going to respond to that."

Why? LaFleur is more than willing to criticize the coaching obliquely.

After the Packers' Week 1 collapse LaFleur said he and the Packers' coaching staff have "got to find a way to get it done" regardless of who's on the field.

A few days after the Falcons loss, LaFleur directly criticized the offensive line's fundamentals.

"II think that's where the majority of our focus will lie is in terms of our pass sets and how we're passing off twists, how we punch," he said. "All that stuff, it's got to improve in a hurry."

That's a particularly telling bit of criticism, because Butkus himself has identified improving fundamentals as one of the core aspects of his job. In 2025, Butkus was asked specifically what Jordan Morgan needed to improve to earn a full-time starting job, and Butkus responded that it was all about fundamentals — which he and the other coaches would work to help him improve.

"So you'd better be good at technique, and that's what we have to do constantly, be fanatics about working your technique and making sure if you're letting defenders in your chest, you better be better with your hands," Butkus said. "And that's our job as coaches, help him with that."

Previous linemen have praised Butkus and offensive coordinator Adam Stenavich for helping them improve their fundamentals. The most pointed praise came from former Packers guard Jon Runyan.

"Coach Steno, Coach Butkus, they make it so simple and it's so easy to understand so that we go out there, we take everything they tell us from the film room onto the field and it's just really easy to follow," he said in 2021. "They just do a really good job of holding all of us to a really high standard and making sure that we know our stuff and they feel comfortable. They're just doing their job at such a high level, it makes it a lot easier for us."

Why, then, are there repeated questions and concerns from the head coach about the offensive line's fundamentals?

It seems telling that the Runyan quote comes from 2021, when Adam Stenavich was a full-time offensive line coach and Butkus was merely his assistant. Both have been promoted since then; in 2022, Stenavich made the jump to offensive coordinator, while Butkus got the promotion to offensive line coach. Since then, it doesn't seem like hyperbole to say the offensive line has been in a steady state of decline.

Personnel plays a role in that; the Packers' line is certainly less talented now than it was in the early Matt LaFleur era. But a coach's job is to work with what he's got, and many of the Packers' early season struggles centered on Donovan Jennings and Jacob Monk, two players who had been in the Packers' system for years. Both had several seasons of development under their belt, tutored all the while by Luke Butkus. If they weren't ready to go when finally called upon, who's to blame other than their position coach?

The near-automatic response to LaFleur's reticence to move on from struggling coaches is loyalty, but I'm not sure where that comes from in this particular instance. LaFleur and Stenavich go way back; Stenavich first worked with LaFleur as a practice squad member of the Houston Texans while LaFleur was a low-level assistant on Gary Kubiak's staff. But there's no such connection with Luke Butkus. He arrived courtesy of Nathaniel Hackett, with whom he'd worked for the Jacksonville Jaguars. His relationship with LaFleur began in 2019; it's not like there's some deep-seated relationship there.

Maybe it's just LaFleur's general hesitance toward firing anyone rearing its head. Maybe there's more going on behind the scenes than we're seeing publicly. Maybe the second this season ends, Butkus will be out of a job.

But for now, it seems like the man that should be getting rightly criticized — and in some cases, actually is facing some not-so-veiled criticism — is viewed as untouchable. In a season on the brink of total collapse, that's hard to understand.
