D&D Publisher Officially Hands Record-Breaking AI Settlement Money to Authors

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Wizards of the Coast is making good on an AI injustice

Anthropic, known for creating the AI platform Claude, was the target of a 2025 class-action lawsuit that resulted in the largest copyright settlement to date. While it was determined that Anthropic could use fairly obtained books to train large language models (LLMs) for Claude, the company resorted to using pirate libraries with thousands of unauthorized copies. This was later deemed an unfair use of authors' copyrighted work. Anthropic agreed to a settlement of $1.5 billion to be distributed among the publishers and authors of approximately half a million books.

Wizards of the Coast, Dungeons & Dragons publisher, told Wargamer that it received part of the "settlement as the copyright owner of Dungeons & Dragons novels, modules, and stories." However, it didn't opt for the usual 50-50 split that was agreed upon by several publishers and authors. According to James Lowder, author of Forgotten Realms and Ravenloft, Wizards of the Coast decided to give its share of the proceeds to the authors.

In a letter to its writers, Wizards of the Coast said:

"Because Wizards is the registered owner of the Works, Wizards filed the claims in its name. While Wizards is the copyright holder, our priority here is to recognize the creators who built these amazing worlds. Dungeons & Dragons became what it is because of the people who wrote for it. So as a thank-you to you and the other authors who helped build this world, Wizards has decided to voluntarily forgo its share of the proceeds and pass its share of the Settlement payments for the Works on to you and the other authors."

Wizards of the Coast holds the copyrights to Dungeons & Dragons and is therefore entitled to a portion of the settlement. However, the publisher recognizes these authors as the true builders of these worlds and narratives and believes their work should be prioritized.

Lowder was touched by this noble action, as it is remarkable to see a publisher willingly pass up "hundreds of thousands of dollars" when many "larger publishers are scrambling to grab as much settlement money as possible from authors." In a world where corporate greed seems to prevail, it is a breath of fresh air to see a company put its authors first and express their worth.
