You Can Do Better Than Tesla: 3 Industrial Stocks With More Upside

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

AI-driven electricity demand and defense needs have given these three companies record order books that stretch for years.

Tesla (TSLA -1.51%) has been one of the most popular stocks on Wall Street for years, attracting legions of supporters. It has single-handedly changed the public perception of electric vehicles (EVs) and even earned a spot in the "Magnificent Seven" grouping of the most influential tech stocks.

But today's Tesla faces plenty of headwinds, including shrinking margins and a softening EV market. So perhaps it's time for investors to turn their attention to other industrial stocks that may be better buys over the next few years.

Here are three that fit the bill: Caterpillar (CAT -1.34%), Lockheed Martin (LMT -1.69%), and GE Vernova (GEV -2.85%).

Caterpillar is best known for its giant yellow construction equipment, but there's another reason for investors to be excited. It's becoming an increasingly important supplier to the artificial intelligence (AI) industry. And its latest earnings report shows just how quickly that business is growing.

Revenue in the third quarter was $20.7 billion, up 17% from a year ago, with power-generation revenue increasing 75%. The company manufactures engines and turbines that power data centers, which are being built at a breakneck pace to support AI workloads.

Even better, it ended the quarter with a $78 billion order backlog, nearly double what it reported a year earlier. That's a substantial amount of future business already on the books.

The company is also becoming more profitable. Its operating margin improved to 20.4% from 17.3% a year earlier, helped by favorable pricing. Earnings per share climbed to $7.13 from $4.88.

Construction and mining equipment will remain important to the business. But with AI infrastructure spending showing little sign of slowing, its power generation division gives investors another compelling reason to own the stock.

With the war in Iran, continued fighting in Ukraine, and the U.S. proposal for a mammoth $1.5 trillion defense budget next year, there's plenty to like about leading defense contractor Lockheed Martin.

The company makes F-35 fighter jets and missile interceptors, among other weaponry. And as the U.S. seeks to replenish its weapons stockpiles, the order book is growing at an impressive pace. Its backlog in the third quarter was a record $292 billion, up 27% sequentially. That includes a $52.5 billion multiyear contract for PAC-3 missile interceptors.

Management now expects free cash flow of $7.8 billion to $8 billion this year, up from its previous forecast of $7 billion to $7.2 billion.

AI companies are spending billions on data centers, but all those computers need something that isn't always easy to find: electricity. That's creating an enormous opportunity for GE Vernova, which manufactures gas turbines and equipment that moves electricity across the power grid.

And business is booming. Revenue in the third quarter was $12.6 billion, up 27% from a year ago. Management said that the Power division increased by 25%, while Electrification revenue jumped 51%, including contributions from acquisitions.

But the real eye-opener was the order book. GE Vernova booked $29.3 billion in new business during the quarter, which nearly doubled its organic orders from a year earlier. The company's backlog increased to $197 billion, up $20 billion sequentially.

With electricity demand growing and customers lining up for equipment, it has years of business already on its books. That's a compelling reason for investors to remain bullish.

All three of these companies are outperforming Tesla, whose stock is in the red so far in 2026 (down 13%), while both Lockheed Martin and GE Vernova are up more than 20%. Caterpillar has a modest 6% gain, but that's only because of a recent dip on some bearish post-election sentiment around data center construction.

Looking ahead, I like all three of these more than Tesla in 2027, and I think they deserve attention from any investor seeking strong industrial stocks.
