Related Threat Context also provides ready-made signals that help analysts decide where to focus their attention. Instead of requiring teams to interpret raw historical data themselves, these signals immediately show whether an indicator stands out or is commonly encountered in related activity.

These signals are not verdicts. A Never Seen Before indicator is not automatically malicious, while a Commonly Seen indicator is not automatically harmless. Instead, they give security teams an immediate way to allocate attention and combine historical context with the object’s behavior, verdict, and other available evidence.

1. What is Related Threat Context in ANY.RUN?

Related Threat Context shows whether IOCs found during an Interactive Sandbox analysis have appeared in previous team investigations, industry-related analyses, or ANY.RUN Threat Intelligence, giving SOCs and MSSPs additional context.

Related Threat Context is available to ANY.RUN Enterprise clients. Internal SOCs can access industry, team, and ANY.RUN TI context, while MSSPs can use team and ANY.RUN TI context.

3. What types of context does it provide?

Related Threat Context provides three types of context: Industry Context, Team Context, and ANY.RUN Threat Intelligence. Together, they show whether an IOC has appeared in relevant industry activity, previous team analyses, or broader TI data.

4. Does Related Threat Context determine whether an IOC is malicious?

No. Related Threat Context provides additional evidence for investigation and prioritization, but analysts still need to consider the IOC’s behavior, verdict, and other available evidence.

An industry match shows that an IOC has appeared in analyses associated with the organization’s industry. It is an additional signal, not proof that the threat is targeting that industry or a specific victim.

6. What do “Never Seen Before” and “Commonly Seen” mean?

Never Seen Before means the IOC has not previously appeared in the available related context. Commonly Seen means it has appeared frequently. Neither signal is a verdict on its own.

7. Can Related Threat Context help reduce time-to-triage?

Yes. By surfacing relevant context directly in the Sandbox, it helps analysts make faster decisions about whether to close, investigate, enrich in TI, or escalate a case.

8. Can Related Threat Context uncover additional infrastructure?

Yes. SOC and MSSP analysts can follow connections from a Sandbox IOC into Threat Intelligence Lookup to investigate related domains, samples, infrastructure, and activity that may not have been visible in the original analysis.
