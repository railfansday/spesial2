Rams captain unveils how defense plays chess with quarterbacks: ‘Eyes won’t lie’

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The Los Angeles Rams have assembled one of the best defenses in all of football. But according to team captain Quentin Lake, the team's success this season hasn't been the return of Myles Garrett or Aaron Donald, or the addition of All-Pro cornerback Trent McDuffie.

It's the weekly chess match the Rams are winning on the field, and yes, getting some of the major pieces back on the board has allowed them to start dictating their opponent's moves.

Sunday's 34-6 demolition of the Arizona Cardinals offered another glimpse of what this defense can become as they continue to get healthy. The Cardinals failed to score a touchdown against the Rams in both of their meetings this season, settling for eight field goals combined.

Now, with defensive tackle Kobie Turner expected to return, the Rams could be even more dangerous when they host the Green Bay Packers on Thanksgiving Eve in a primetime showdown with enormous NFC playoff implications.

Quentin Lake of the Los Angeles Rams looks on from the sideline before an NFL football game against the Washington Commanders on Sunday, Nov. 8, 2026. Getty Images

And Lake believes there's still another gear they can reach.

"I know we're excited to really show what it looks like to put a whole game together," Lake told The California Post in an exclusive interview.

Lake offered a fascinating breakdown of how Garrett and Donald change the way opposing offenses operate, particularly when quarterbacks are forced to make decisions before they want to.

"Especially at safety, the eyes won't lie," Lake said. "Quarterbacks won't necessarily have enough time to look you off because they're like, 'OK, I have to make sure this pass is going to be accurate. I have to make sure this pass is going to get there in time,' dealing with those two guys up front."

In other words, quarterbacks become so preoccupied with surviving the pass rush that disguising their intentions becomes an afterthought.

Los Angeles Rams defensive tackle Aaron Donald (99) greets defensive end Myles Garrett (95) during an NFL game in Landover, Md., Sunday, Nov. 8, 2026. AP Photo/Stephanie Scarbrough

That gives Lake and the Rams' secondary an opportunity to anticipate throws, jump routes and create turnovers.

Lake explained that opposing offensive coordinators often build their game plans around avoiding Garrett and Donald altogether, using screens, quick passes, misdirection and even waiting until one of them leaves the field before attempting a deep shot.

"Myles might not be there every single snap," Lake said. "So he probably has something on his play sheet where it's like, 'Hey, these are the calls that we want when 95's out of the game.'"

To his point, Garrett was only on the field against the Cardinals for 17 total snaps—mostly on obvious passing situations. Donald only played 23 snaps. Lake played in every defensive snap for the Rams, and as the captain, he has to recognize what their opponents are trying to do when L.A.'s biggest stars are on the sidelines catching their breath.

Los Angeles Rams defensive end Aaron Donald (99) embraces safety Quentin Lake (37) during practice at the Rams Practice Facility in Woodland Hills, California, on Aug. 30, 2026. IMAGN IMAGES via Reuters Connect

According to Lake, the secret to winning this weekly NFL game of chess begins with stopping the run, creating negative plays on early downs and forcing quarterbacks into those obvious passing situations where the Rams can unleash Garrett and Donald together.

"We have to be able to stop the run so that you earn the right to rush the passer," Lake said.

Once that happens, the entire defensive line, including Byron Young, Braden Fiske, and potentially Turner can pin their ears back and hunt the quarterback.

That will be crucial against the Packers, especially when the Rams' offense is without Puka Nacua, Davante Adams and starting center Coleman Shelton. Not to mention, the availability of Steve Avila and Terrance Ferguson is still uncertain.

That puts an even greater burden on the defense to keep Los Angeles in the playoff race by beating the Packers, and Lake isn't running from that extra responsibility.

"Anytime we can, we want to be able to carry the load," Lake said of the Rams' defense. "Good teams can win in any way. Whether that's the defense carrying them, whether that's the offense carrying them, or whether that's being able to do special stuff in the kicking game."

The Rams have already demonstrated they can suffocate an opponent. Against Green Bay, they'll need to prove they can do it when the stakes are highest.

Because in a game that could help determine the NFC's final wild-card spot, the Rams don't just need to win the chess match, they need to make sure they put Jordan Love in checkmate.
