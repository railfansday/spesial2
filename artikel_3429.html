The 'Avengers: Doomsday' Clock Has Somehow Become the Internet's Most Fascinating Nerd Hangout

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Robert Brian Taylor is a journalist, critic, and occasional screenwriter based out of Pittsburgh. Throughout his career, his writing has appeared within an eclectic combination of newspapers, magazines, books, and websites. He currently works as a Training and Features Editor for Collider, where he regularly contributes entertainment reviews, interviews, and features. In the final run-up to Avengers: Doomsday, there's going to be a lot of talk about whether Marvel's still got it. Box office projections will be closely tracked. Kevin Feige, the cast, and the crew will be deployed on a press tour to convince everyone that the Multiverse Saga has been worth it and that, over the course of the next two Avengers movies, it will be paid off in an epic and satisfying way.

But there's one strange corner of the Internet where the power of the MCU has never diminished. A place where thousands of folks congregate every day to chat about all things Marvel, and where Doomsday is the biggest and most important movie ever to be released. I'm talking, of course, about the Avengers: Doomsday Clock livestream and accompanying chat on Marvel Entertainment's YouTube channel.

The Doomsday Clock, which, most of the time, is just an image of Robert Downey Jr.'s Doctor Doom with a clock overtop of it counting down the months, days, hours, minutes, and seconds until Avengers: Doomsday releases, launched back on January 13, 2026. It went live at roughly the same time Marvel released the first four teasers for the new film, revealing, among other things, that Chris Evans' Steve Rogers would be returning to the Avengers lineup in some capacity later this year.

The clock seemed like the spiritual sequel to Marvel's "chairs" video, a five and a half hour livestream in which the studio slowly revealed a huge majority of the Doomsday cast, one director's chair at a time. (To this point, "Doomsday chairs video day" is my single favorite day working at Collider. Our company Slack was bombarded with a steady stream of messages as we did nothing but watch the stream on the corner of our monitors while working, and then laugh about the idiocy of what we — and apparently 275 million other people — were wasting our time on.)

At first glance, the Doomsday Clock seems less eventful than even the chairs stream. It's just a clock counting down! However, it will occasionally toss in a curveball for anyone who happens to be watching at the right time. The cities included in the Doomsday global tour were first announced on the clock stream, and it will sometimes change colors or drop new images or sound clips to tease the arrival of new Doomsday content. On Monday, when a Doomsday-related post-credits scene dropped on the VisionQuest finale, the Doomsday Clock took on a magical red hue to indicate the official return of Elizabeth Olsen's Wanda Maximoff, the Scarlet Witch.

I had seen that little tidbit pointed out on Bluesky later that evening, and like a good nerd, I clicked over to the clock because I wanted to see what all the fuss was about. I was astounded to see that, 90 minutes after the finale (and after the red hue had already faded), more than 11,000 people were still watching the clock. And the accompanying chat was buzzing with posts flying back at rapid speeds: "WANDAAAAAAAA"; "SCARLET WITCH, AWAKEN"; "MOTHER IS BACK!!!!!!"

I laughed and clicked away. Three days later, on the following Thursday afternoon, I clicked back to the clock to see if that number had come down. I am here to report, friends, that it had not. Instead, the clock's livestreaming audience had ballooned to more than 40,000 people, many of whom had apparently congregated because there were unsubstantiated rumors a new trailer would be debuting that afternoon. That did not come to pass, but the chat was once again an orgy of mindless MCU commentary: "Team doom or team thor????"; "EVERYONE LETS SPAM HEARTS FOR WANDA"; "Doom is stronger than Thanos!"

Even funnier is when the chat takes on a more meta flavor and comments on itself: "45K people??!!! But why"; "WAHT ARE YOULLL DOING HERE MANN"; "WTF 42K WATCHING?" Proper spelling and grammar isn't exactly a strong point on the Doomsday Clock chat, but that's hardly an issue when some hint of new Doomsday content could be unveiled at any minute.

This past Saturday, I checked back in with the Doomsday Clock, which has a total views count of 1.2 million, one last time before writing this article, and 41,000 people were tuned in live. Messages like "Spiderman brand new brand new day is releasing on 4th dec with extra 7 min. Doom is coming again!" were posted alongside off-topic missives to free Palestine, bad Internet jokes ("my dad will return in Doomsday"), and the usual questions asking when the next trailer will be released. (It seems like, every single day, folks are in the Doomsday Clock stream under the mistaken impression that a new trailer is about to release.)

This all leads me to a series of questions I don't have answers to: If the MCU has really lost its mojo, why are tens of thousands of bored YouTube watchers swinging by the channel at any given moment to watch a clock tick down? Are they all even human? Are some of them bots? (That's a question that gets asked in the Doomsday Clock chat a lot, but it's tough to suss out what Disney or the fanbase itself would gain by throwing a bot army at the countdown clock to post endless drivel in the chat, other than just raw YouTube numbers, I guess.)

Maybe the Doosmday chat is an appealing throwback to an earlier, simpler internet, one where chatrooms were king, and you could log on anonymously and talk about your passions with random strangers on the internet in real time. (To the youngsters out there: trust me, this was an incredible thing in the late '90s.) Or maybe it's all just good marketing, and Marvel knows if they throw up a live Doomsday Clock to run for a whole year, the MCU's most insanely committed fans are going to be here for it.

I remain fascinated by the Doomsday Clock, both because it's kind of silly and because it shows that there is still some indescribable magic left in the MCU. Name another franchise that would have fans lining up in droves to watch chairs being slowly unveiled or a clock slowly counting down. The truth of the matter is that either the residual goodwill left over from the Infinity Saga is still strong enough to power this kind of devotion ... OR the anticipation — at least among the hardcore — for Doosmday and future MCU movies is stronger than some of us realize.

That doesn't mean Avengers: Doomsday is guaranteed to gross $2 billion at the box office. You need all the normies to also go to the theater to hit those kinds of numbers, and the normies aren't firing up YouTube to see what's going on at the Doomsday Clock a few times a week. But it could mean the core fan base — think of them as the MCU's very own Iron Man-esque arc reactor — is still humming along like it did in the 2010s. They're out there. They're excited. And they will come and watch a stupid clock count down on YouTube, hoping the latest new footage is only moments away.
