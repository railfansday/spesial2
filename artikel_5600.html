4 holiday gift categories where timing decides whether you save or miss out

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Fragrances, handbags, travel gear and tech toys each follow a different holiday buying clock. See when to buy and when to hold out.

Every holiday shopper eventually has to guess about timing. Buy a gift early and you may pay more than you needed to, but wait for the markdown and the gift you wanted may be gone. Limited-edition items raise the stakes on that guess, because a product that sells out doesn't return later at a lower price. It is simply unavailable. The usual shopping advice offers one rule for everything: always shop early, or always wait for clearance. Neither rule works across a whole gift list. Each kind of gift has its own best time to buy, and the useful skill is knowing which pattern a particular category follows.

No holiday price calendar is a guarantee, and even the people who put one together say so. Guides that track when gifts tend to be cheapest warn that prices and stock change from year to year, which means last year's pattern describes what usually happens rather than what will happen this time. Toy experts take the same cautious line. WCPO reports that they tell gift buyers to get their shopping done ahead of time and to check what more than one store charges. That is advice to keep options open, not to trust a single date. A shopper who knows the usual pattern still has to watch what stores are doing this year. The question then changes from when the sale starts to whether a gift is one where waiting saves money or one where waiting means going without.

Google $GOOGL's The Keyword blog set out to show the best time to buy each holiday gift category, from big suitcases to a bottle of perfume to a motorized toy on a child's wish list. This slideshow sets those timing patterns against conditions in stores this season, including how readily brands discount and how quickly a popular item that sells out can be restocked. Taken together, the patterns and this year's conditions show which gifts reward a shopper who waits and which ones cost a late shopper the gift itself. For each gift, a shopper comes away with a clear sense of when to buy right away and when to hold out.

A bottle of prestige perfume is a poor gift to put off in the hope of a late markdown. Google $GOOGL advises shoppers to buy a fragrance early in the holiday season. Early timing counts most for the labels people already want, and shoppers have been buying more Le Labo, Tom Ford $F and Jo Malone London, according to Reuters. The early buyer can still pick from a full range of bottles. The late buyer competes with everyone else who wanted the same scent.

Holding off until late in the season to catch a bigger promotion is unlikely to pay off on Estee Lauder $EL's brands. The company is focusing on desirability and wants to be "less reliant on promotion overall everywhere around the world," CEO Stephane de La Faverie told Reuters. For fiscal 2027, the company expects its organic sales to rise by between four and 5.5 per cent. A company that expects to grow while it leans less on deals has little reason to mark down its bottles for latecomers. On Estee Lauder's brands, a shopper gives up little by buying early in the season, and a shopper who waits for a deal may find none.

Fragrance sales are holding up while the wider luxury trade struggles. Reuters reports that Kering, LVMH and Hermes are contending with war in the Middle East and stricter tax enforcement in China. Sky Canaves, an analyst at eMarketer, says shoppers who want a taste of luxury are moving away from costly goods and toward cheaper categories such as beauty. A slowdown in luxury can therefore send more shoppers to the beauty counter. For a gift giver, that steady demand is the reason to buy a perfume early. A weak luxury market is no promise that perfume will go on sale.

In fragrance, holding out for a bargain is the expensive choice.

Handbags are a gift worth putting off until December, after the early holiday rush. Shoppers get two good chances to buy that month: the middle of December offers big discounts along with a good selection, and late December offers the lowest prices on the bags that remain, according to Google $GOOGL. A shopper who holds off into December can find a markdown at either point. The trade-off is in the range of styles. Prices are lower at the end of the month, but there are fewer bags to choose from. The cheapest handbags of the season are the leftovers, the styles that other shoppers passed over.

Waiting costs a handbag shopper choice rather than money. Someone who has settled on one particular style takes a real risk by holding out past mid-December, since that bag may be gone by the final week. For that shopper, the middle of the month is the right time to buy. It is the last stretch when strong markdowns and a decent range of styles are both available. A shopper who simply wants a nice bag at the best price should do the opposite and wait for the final clearance. That gift doesn't depend on any one style still being in stock. The person shopping for it can take whatever is left at the lowest price of the season and still come away with a good handbag.

With a handbag, the right day to buy depends less on the calendar than on how specific the wish list is.

Travel gear looks like one errand, but it is two purchases with different best days to buy. A gift for someone who is always on the move often means a suitcase plus the smaller things that go inside it, such as packing cubes. Travel accessories are cheapest in November, while retailers offer their deepest markdowns on large suitcases in late-December clearance, according to Google $GOOGL. That puts the best day for the add-ons and the best day for the bag a month apart, and no single shopping trip gets the lowest price on both. A shopper who buys everything together in November pays the best price for the accessories and more than necessary for the suitcase. A shopper who waits until late December gets the bag at its lowest price but misses the November deals on the smaller pieces. In this category, the early shopper and the patient one each come out ahead on half of the purchase.

The practical plan is to split the purchase, buying the small pieces in November and leaving the suitcase for later. Waiting on the suitcase has a cost, because the lowest prices are available only at the very end of the year. A giver who holds out has little time to buy the bag and wrap it before the holiday. That makes patience on the suitcase a fair bet mainly for a giver willing to hand over a promise, such as a card announcing the bag, and then buy it during the clearance. A giver who wants a wrapped suitcase on the day itself may decide that paying more earlier is worth it.

With travel gear, the question is not when to shop but which half of the gift you are shopping for.

Electronic toys reward the shopper who waits until the very end of the season, after most people have finished buying. Google $GOOGL advises parents with an electronic toy on a child's wish list to consider holding off until the last stretch of holiday shopping for unexpected markdowns. Those markdowns come late and have no set date. A shopper can count on the chance of a discount but not on the day it arrives. The whole plan depends on one condition: the toy has to still be in the store when the price is cut.

Waiting is a safe bet on toys already in stores and a riskier one on anything that has to be reordered. "That's not going to affect the products already on our shelves, but it could impact restocks as we're starting to see container rates spike again," James Zahn, senior editor of The Toy Insider, told WCPO. A toy bought from current stock is not affected. A popular toy that sells out is different, because it has to wait for a new shipment. The late discount and the restock risk fall in the same weeks. The final stretch, when the best markdowns are most likely, is also when a sold-out toy depends on a restock that may come too late for the holiday.

If a child has asked for one particular motorized toy, buy it while it is in stock. Missing a markdown costs some money, but a sellout followed by a delayed restock means there is no gift to give. If any of several gadgets would make the child happy, waiting still makes sense, because when one toy sells out, the shopper can pick another from the same shelf.

With a tech toy, patience can pay off or backfire in the same season, and the shopper should decide by how badly the child wants that exact toy.
