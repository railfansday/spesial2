The Used EV Market Is Now Hotter than the Used Gas Car Market in America

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Pre-owned vehicle shoppers are increasingly turning to electric vehicles as gas prices skyrocket.

The national average gas price as of this story’s October 28 publication is $4.24 per gallon. And many budget-minded car shoppers are taking these soaring gas prices to heart, as recent iSeeCars data shows used electric vehicle sales are hotter than ever.

The new study analyzed more than 1.5 million 1-to-5-year-old used vehicles, and found that used electric vehicles are selling faster than used gas cars despite rising prices for battery-powered models. On average, used EVs sell 13 days faster than their gas counterparts, according to iSeeCars; pre-owned electric cars averaged 41.9 days as of September 2026, a 10-percent reduction in selling time compared to a year back For reference, the average selling time for a used gas car is 54.8 days—a 19.9 percent increase from last September.

“Used EVs have moved from selling slightly slower than gasoline vehicles to selling nearly two weeks faster in just one year,” said iSeeCars executive analyst Karl Brauer. “That reversal, combined with substantially higher average prices, is one of the clearest signs of how dramatically the used car market has changed across drivetrain types.”

This decrease in EV selling time isn’t because of savings at the dealership. In fact, average used electric vehicle prices are up $2896 year-over-year, with an average transaction price of $32,533 in September 2026—a 9.8 percent increase since 2025. Similarly, hybrid vehicles saw a $1625 increase to an average transaction price of $34,476 as of September 2026. By comparison, average pricing for purely gas-powered vehicles showed little movement, increasing just $90 since 2025. The average price for a used gas vehicle is currently $31,954. That means used EV prices now average $579 more than gas vehicles, despite costing $2227 less than gasoline models last September.

“These pricing shifts reflect significantly different year-over-year trends across drivetrains,” said Brauer. “Ongoing higher fuel prices are likely driving the shift, though the EV segment has also recovered from the demand turbulence caused by the loss of federal incentives a year ago.”

Overall, luxury vehicles tend to spend the least time on the market, according to iSeeCars, while hybrid vehicles sit for extended periods. Interestingly, some of the quickest-moving models are recently discontinued ones; the Lexus IS 500 and Audi A4 Allroad typically spend less than 30 days on the market, while the Mercedes-Benz S-Class hybrid spends an average of nearly 130 days on the lot. Three electric vehicles—the Audi Q4 Sportback e-tron, Toyota bZ4X, and Lexus RZ 300—were included in the top 10 fastest-selling cars list, while the Honda Prologue, Polestar 2, and BMW iX are also selling rapidly. Meanwhile, the Cadillac Escalade iQ and GMC Sierra EV made the 10 slowest-selling cars list. Other slow-selling EVs include the Dodge Charger and Volvo EX90.

“While hybrids and EVs are selling faster than gasoline vehicles on average, certain electrified models remain on the market much longer than the typical used car,” said Brauer. “Essentially, high-value, well-executed used EVs are benefiting from ongoing high gas prices, but even high fuel prices can’t save an unpopular electric car.”
