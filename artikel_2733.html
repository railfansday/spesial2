Treydan Stukes, Jermod McCoy, Las Vegas Raiders’ young defensive backs face Cleveland Browns’ young receiving corps in Week 12 key matchups

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The Las Vegas Raiders' playoff chances have been freefalling recently, to the point where the Week 12 Sunday Night Football contest against the Cleveland Browns is a "must-win" to keep the Raiders' slim postseason hopes alive. That adds some extra importance to the key matchups below, and Las Vegas will be leaning heavily on several young players.

While Treydan Stukes has primarily lined up at free safety this season, the Raiders' coaching staff has been tapping into his versatility recently. According to Pro Football Focus, the second-round pick has recorded double-digit snaps in the box in seven of the last eight games and has 37 snaps as a slot corner during that time frame. Part of the reason for that is he's been tasked with covering tight ends.

Per Sumer Sports, Stukes' first target against a tight end came in Week 5, and he's been thrown at 13 times while guarding the position. That's yielded just nine catches for 46 yards (5.1 ypc) and a touchdown, on top of the defensive back recording an interception and three pass breakups. He especially turned heads against the San Francisco 49ers, giving up just two catches for nine yards to George Kittle, per PFF.

Meanwhile, tight end Harold Fannin Jr. leads the Browns in every major receiving category with 44 catches for 544 yards and four touchdowns. Those numbers are also fifth, sixth and tied for fourth at the position across the NFL, and Fannin ranks 12th with 1.63 yards per route run, according to PFF.

The second-year pro has been especially good after the catch. Among tight ends, he trails only Travis Kelce (375) with 320 YAC and is fourth with 7.3 YAC per reception. A big reason is that Fannin leads his position with 11 missed tackles forced. The good news for the Raiders is that Stukes has only missed three tackles at a 10.3 percent rate in coverage this season.

That sets up an interesting battle between two young, up-and-coming players in the league.

Vegas has played musical chairs at one of its two outside cornerback spots, giving starts to and rotating second-year pro Darien Porter Jr. and rookies Hezekiah Masses and Jermod McCoy week-by-week and throughout the game.

However, that trend stopped last week, when McCoy made his first NFL start and played 71 of the team's 73 defensive snaps. The plays he missed were short-yardage situations where the Raiders didn't have a corner on the field.

The Tennessee product certainly didn't have a perfect showing in Denver. He was targeted five times and yielded two catches for 55 yards, per PFF, with one of the incompletions coming via a drop where he was beaten deep. However, a lot of McCoy's bad reps were symptomatic of a player who just hasn't gotten much playing time, and he showed enough promise to, presumptively, hold down the starting spot for at least one more week. Most notably, he had an excellent rep against Broncos top receiver Jaylen Waddle to record a PBU.

The Raiders' problem is that the defense is also having issues at the other cornerback spot. Veteran Eric Stokes was supposed to be the team's shutdown corner but has already given up over 200 more yards in 2026 (460) than he did in all of 2025 (261), per PFF. That's in addition to an eight percent increase in completion percentage (56.0% to 64.2%) and three more touchdowns (one to four).

Porter and Masses have each had their moments this year, but neither has been consistent. The former is allowing a 56.5 completion percentage (13 catches on 23 targets) for 216 yards, no touchdowns and three forced incompletions but no interceptions. The latter's figures sit at 54.8 percent (17 for 31), 274 yards, five touchdowns and three forced incompletions, in addition to three picks.

Meanwhile, the Browns have a couple of impressive rookie receivers in KC Concepcion and Denzel Boston. The former has racked up 40 catches for 458 yards and a touchdown, while the latter has 33 grabs for 523 yards and three touchdowns. And the two wideouts complement each other well.

Concepcion is more of a chess piece who can be moved around, spending 63.4 percent of his pass snaps in the slot and 36.3 percent out wide, per PFF. A good amount of his production comes after the catch, where 245 or 53.5 percent of his yards have come, ranking ninth among receivers with 6.1 YAC per reception.

Boston, on the other hand, is a typical X-receiver type with 89.9 percent of his pass snaps coming on the outside. He's more of a downfield and contested catch threat, owning a 15.3-yard average depth of target and 10 contested catches (tied for 10th among wideouts) compared to Concepcion's 8.2 ADOT and two contested grabs.

So, two different flavors of receivers that are almost equally as effective, with Concepcion averaging 1.47 yards per route run and Boston right behind him at 1.41 yprr. That presents a difficult challenge for the Silver and Black's secondary, regardless of who's getting snaps at cornerback.

The Raiders' rushing attack had been pretty dormant for about two months, going seven weeks without registering 100 yards as a team until this past Sunday. The good news is the offense is coming off its best game on the ground, recording a season-high 143 rushing yards and 6.0 ypc.

Running backs Ashton Jeanty (11 carries, 79 yards and 7.2 ypc) and Mike Washington Jr. (nine carries, 42 yards and 4.7 ypc) finally had enough breathing room to be effective in the ground game. That's a testament to Vegas' offensive line, which has been searching all season for its best five and may have found it with rookie Trey Zuhn III getting the nod at right guard during the last four games.

But this week brings a new test for Las Vegas' ground game because Cleveland's linebackers, Quincy Williams and Carson Schwesinger, have been pretty good against the run this season.

Per PFF, Williams ranks tied for second at the position with 24 defensive stops against the run and is tied for eighth with a 9.7 percent run-stop rate. That's helped him post an 85.6 run defense grade, fourth-best among linebackers. Schwesinger doesn't have nearly as many impact tackles against the run with just eight run stops, but a 70.2 mark that ranks 30th is nothing to scoff at.

On top of this, the Raiders have leaned heavily on Jeanty in the passing game. He leads the team in receptions (51), is third in receiving yards (507) and is tied for second in receiving touchdowns (four). Additionally, those figures are second, first and tied for first among running backs across the NFL. In other words, the 2025 first-round pick will also test the Browns' linebackers as a receiver.

Williams' numbers in coverage are also impressive this season, giving up just 204 yards while recording three pass breakups and 13 stops. The latter two figures are tied for sixth and tied for 10th among linebackers. Meanwhile, Schewesinger has yielded a rather high 347 receiving yards with no forced incompletions, but is 11th in coverage snaps per reception allowed (12.2) and tied for seventh with 14 stops.

That adds extra importance to the running back-linebacker matchups this week, especially when it comes to Jeanty.
