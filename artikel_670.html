‘Hanuman Ansh,’ ‘Pyre’ and Shah Rukh Khan Classics Lead International Film Festival of India’s Inflatable Cinema Lineup as NFDC, ESG Renew PictureTime Partnership (EXCLUSIVE)

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Vishal Chaturvedi’s hit “Hanuman Ansh” and Vinod Kapri’s “Pyre” will headline a pop-up inflatable cinema at the International Film Festival of India (IFFI) in Goa, where the National Film Development Corporation (NFDC) and the Entertainment Society of Goa (ESG) have teamed with PictureTime Digiplex again.

The mobile auditorium is going up on the Kala Academy campus and will serve as a festival hub, screening films every day from Nov. 21 to Nov. 28. PictureTime is known for deploying portable inflatable theaters in underserved areas across India. The program is split into three strands: Contemporary, Children’s Films and Nostalgia. Alongside “Hanuman Ansh” and “Pyre,” the contemporary selection includes Deepti Pillay Sivan’s Malayalam-language “Achappa’s Album” and Sachin Mohite’s Marathi-language “Kapuskoonda.”

“Our association with NFDC and IFFI has grown into a meaningful, long-standing partnership, and it is always special for us to return to a festival that brings together the best of Indian and international cinema,” said Sushil Chaudhary, founder and CEO of PictureTime Digiplex.

Shah Rukh Khan romances dominate the Nostalgia section. Yash Chopra’s “Darr: A Violent Love Story,” “Dil To Pagal Hai,” “Jab Tak Hai Jaan” and “Veer Zaara” will play along with Aditya Chopra’s “Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge.” Kunal Kohli’s “Fanaa” and two Nasir Hussain films, “Yaadon Ki Baarat” and “Hum Kisise Kum Naheen,” round out the strand.

On the children’s side, Kaushal Oza’s 2026 film “Little Thomas,” with Gulshan Devaiah and Rasika Dugal in the cast and Ranjan Singh among its co-producers, will anchor the morning screenings for schoolchildren. Other family titles include Sai Paranjpye’s “Bhago Bhoot” (2000), Rajeev Sharma’s “Bandu Boxer” (2006), Rajan Khosa’s “Gattu” (2011), Batul Mukhtiar’s “Kaphal-Wild Berries” (2013) and Pankuj Parashar’s “Banarasi Jasoos” (2015).

Chaudhary added: “What makes this edition especially exciting is the range of audiences and cinematic experiences we are looking to bring together. … Equally important for us is extending the festival experience beyond its core venues and delegates, and bringing these films to local communities across Goa so that they too can participate in and feel a part of IFFI.”

The Goa run caps a busy year for the company. PictureTime also supported the exhibition infrastructure at the Dharamshala International Film Festival and took “Pyre” to audiences in the Indian hinterland.
