BVNK Opens OUSD Minting and Payments Across Four Blockchains

TL;DR: BVNK says eligible business customers can now mint, redeem, hold and transfer Open USD (OUSD) on Ethereum, Solana, Base and Tempo. The November 6 rollout is a practical payments integration, but the company has not provided transaction volumes proving how extensively the new service is being used.

A stablecoin’s biggest hurdle may not be issuing tokens but making them useful inside ordinary financial operations. Payments infrastructure company BVNK has taken a step in that direction by making Open USD accessible through its existing platform.

In a November 6 announcement, BVNK said eligible businesses can mint and redeem OUSD against dollar balances, hold it and transfer it on Ethereum, Solana, Base and Tempo. The product is aimed at payment providers, treasury teams and companies that need to move between bank money and blockchain settlement without assembling separate integrations for every network.

BVNK outlines three routes. A direct integration allows a company to use OUSD in its own treasury and operations. An embedded option lets developers offer wallets, pay-ins or payouts within customer-facing applications. Wholesale access is aimed at exchanges, trading desks and market makers that need larger-scale minting and redemption.

These categories are more important than a promotional claim of being multichain. A business needs clarity about the currency held, when redemption is available, which network is supported and whether the underlying legal and compliance arrangements suit the countries involved. A token arriving quickly on a blockchain is only one step in an end-to-end payment.

BVNK says the launch offers 1:1 conversion against dollar balances and positions OUSD as part of an open-infrastructure model. The firm identifies a consortium of financial and technology companies behind the wider initiative, but membership of a consortium should not be mistaken for a guarantee of liquidity, issuer credit quality or operational support from every named institution.

A company with customers on different chains can otherwise face bridge complexity, additional custody arrangements and fragmented liquidity. Supporting several networks in one interface potentially reduces that operational burden, especially for businesses already using BVNK’s services.

However, crosschain availability does not mean every route is interchangeable or that every address and wallet supports every version of the token. Businesses still have to prevent wrong-network transfers and reconcile balances across chains. The value of the integration will be visible in reliability and error handling, not just a list of supported networks.

For context, NewsBTC has covered how Solana’s settlement tools are being built for institutions. OUSD addresses a related but separate problem: supplying a payment asset and an operating layer through which companies can use it.

BVNK says OUSD is available to eligible business customers outside the European Economic Area. That geographic qualification matters because stablecoin access and licensing rules differ between jurisdictions. Users should check eligibility before assuming they can open a new account or send tokens through a particular network.

The company has not published figures establishing how much OUSD has been minted or transferred specifically through this new integration. For now, the news is a live product capability, not a demonstrated leap in adoption. The next useful evidence would be sustained payment volume, repeat business usage and disclosures about redemption performance during busy markets.
