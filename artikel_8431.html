The Foothills Trail – Day 2

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

My alarm went off at 6 a.m. After hitting the snooze button a few times, I was up and packing by 6:30. With my pack loaded up, I was back on trail, passing a large group of overnighters and a couple of fellow FHT thru-hikers. The first half of the day was beautiful, skirting the river and allowing plenty of opportunities to refill my water. I found a spot to relax on some rocks that stretched out over the river. Listening to the current rush downstream like white noise, I started to drift off into what felt like a meditative state. I could have easily fallen asleep right there, but my itinerary was set and I had miles to hike. A quick break from the weight of my pack and a few handfuls of SunChips reenergized me for the remainder of the day, or so I thought.

The second half of my day was rough, as that was where most of the climbing happened. Did my husband fail to mention how many stairs were on this trail? Yes. Did he laugh when I texted him in all caps, “STAIRS!?!” Yeah, he told me he thought it was only right that I find out the stair situation the same way he did, a rite of passage to this trail if you will.

Reaching the top of one climb, I saw a large group of hikers taking a break overlooking Whiteside Mountain. An older gentleman and I made eye contact and exchanged smiles before he quickly turned to the group and yelled, “Hiker!” At that moment, I smirked, playing out a scene in my head: “Step aside for hiker royalty! We’re not worthy! We’re not worthy! Party on, hiker!” Channeling my inner Garth and Wayne from Wayne’s World, I grinned at the group as I passed. They were completely unaware of the daydream keeping me entertained on the trail. Hiking completely solo so far, my inner dialogue was like a hamster on a wheel. I pushed on, eager to get to camp, knowing it was going to be an even colder night.

Stepping out to Fish Hatchery Road Access, I peered up the beautifully paved flat road and then back toward the trail, where a small sign read, “Table Rock St Park 55.9.” It felt like I hadn’t made a dent in the miles. A quick glance at my FarOut app showed three more miles to camp. A gloomy sky sat above me all day, the kind of heavy gray overcast that makes you feel cold just looking at it. My fingers were starting to go numb from the windchill. Wearing only shorts and my long-sleeve sun shirt, I thought about throwing on my rain pants for an extra layer. My husband’s voice echoed in my head, “It’s only cold if you think about it. Three miles, walk fast.” I pushed through and reached camp an hour later.

Walking down into the campsite, I found a roomy spot just off the trail, complete with fire rings scattered throughout and water access tucked behind some bushes. A few widow makers loomed overhead, so I found a spot of least resistance to quickly set up my tent. Everything was damp, as it was every day, and the air was cold, so I decided to build a fire. Most of the wood was damp, but I was eager for warmth since the high for the day was 55°F with a projected low of 33°F.

While dinner rehydrated, I stacked firewood next to the ring and set out to get a small fire going, which happened eventually. Dinner followed, some Mountain House meal with vegetables, possibly a stir-fry? My appetite had been nonexistent since the day before. Still, I forced myself to eat just to give my body what it needed. Sitting by the fire for a while, I hoped to see other hikers, though having just missed the peak FHT season, I wasn’t expecting anyone. Finally, I hung my bear bag, brushed my teeth, and snuggled into my tent for the night. You could hear the occasional car pass by from Route 170. I watched a couple of shows on my phone and closed my eyes, ready to start another day tomorrow.

My initial plan had been to take a week off in December for this trail, but I was worried I’d be miserable on my first solo hike and wanted to make it as good an experience as possible to focus on my gear. Instead, I got the opposite of what I planned for, which summed up exactly what thru-hiking was: unplanned events, constant changes of plans, and unexpected weather. Almost immediately after I finished my hike, daytime temps jumped back into the 60s and nighttime temps into the 40s, the exact weather I had originally hoped for.
