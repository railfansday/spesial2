This 10/10 Action Thriller Is Still Jason Statham’s Greatest Movie 8 Years Later

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

There are action stars, and then there is Jason Statham. There is no question that everyone has seen at least one of this performer's flicks, whether they know it or not. The English actor got his start as a Guy Ritchie favorite in movies such as Lock, Stock, and Two Smoking Barrels as well as Snatch. It was his entry into the classic Fast and the Furious franchise, however, that was not his most memorable role.

His part as the villain turned antihero contributed to him being an action star, but it still doesn’t compare to the action thriller to compare all action thrillers to. While it may not spring to mind with the greats such as The Transporter, Crank, or even the more recent Beekeeper, his magnum opus does what these other films only dream of.

At a certain point, Jason Statham has got to be in on the joke. The action star has been in too many different vocation-adjacent roles. It has become admirable how many times his characters can take another job where gunfights are necessary. The 2018 movie, The Meg, doesn’t quite fit into this category but also seems to be in on what Statham is selling. The Meg, of course, stands for megalodon, a prehistoric shark that is the focal point of the movie.

This is the type of narrative that is meant to be seen in theaters, but even if it isn’t, it is still a wild ride. The outrageous concept of a submarine crew encountering an extinct shark is a movie begging to be watched. This isn’t a self-serious journey into the waves. The Meg understands what it is about and doesn’t apologize for it.

Statham stars as Jonas Taylor, a renowned diver who has become known for crying megalodon. No one quite believes that his last crew was chomped to pieces by a giant shark, ushering him into a state of drunken retirement in Thailand. Of course, as these things go, no one can let sleeping sharks lie. Years later, another crew takes a journey into the treacherous waters and discovers that, yes, there is indeed a megalodon beneath the waters.

Even the characters in The Meg acknowledge the predicament they’re in. No one in their right mind would believe that Jonas saw a prehistoric shark, but they are all proven wrong when another crew gets endangered by its chomping jaws. They call in the only man who has claimed to see one, Jonas, himself. The Meg and its sequel are a fun ride, made purposefully for its underwater antics. It is a return to movies like Deep Blue Sea and even Jaws, where the most terrifying thing that exists is the rule of nature.

Statham chews the scenery as he is wont to do, and no one does it better. The ensemble made up of Cliff Curtis and Rainn Wilson brings together the humor that this sort of movie couldn't do without. The Meg doesn’t hold back on the visuals either. If people pay to see a big shark, they're going to see a big shark. It seems that all other attempts by Statham in later years were just a pale imitation of one of his best action thrillers. The Meg is quite literally all killer, no filler, and a visual treat for the senses.
