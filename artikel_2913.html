Warriors rediscover two keys to Steph Curry-less success in win vs. Celtics

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Warriors rediscover two keys to Steph Curry-less success in win vs. Celtics originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SAN FRANCISCO – Steve Kerr preached defense for three days, Draymond Green brought defense when it mattered most, and defense was the soap that allowed the Warriors to wash off the stink of a five-game losing streak that invited little pity from the basketball world.

Green's promising first quarter and lockdown second half Sunday night were at the foundation of Golden State's pulsating, crowd-pleasing 88-87 victory over the Boston Celtics at Chase Center.

It was an impressive end to a losing streak, and much of the credit goes to Kerr. And to Green, who gained additional inspiration from outside agitator Eddie Johnson.

Johnson, an NBA player-turned-analyst, spent much of the weekend on social media jabbing Green's light statistics and mocked his reliance on Stephen Curry. Which led to exchanges with Draymond, who on Sunday ended a six-month Twitter/X hiatus to remind Eddie of his lack of star credentials.

So, naturally, Green couldn't resist addressing the issue during his postgame news conference.

"If you notice, it's never really the greats debating, and it's never the winners debating," Green said. "It's always the losers and the guys who just didn't quite do it. So, let them debate."

Johnson, a prolific scorer for six different teams during a 17-year career that ended in 1999, now is an analyst for the Phoenix Suns and a host on SiriusXM NBA Radio. None of that held any weight for Green. Not after their exchanges.

"You got people flexing their resumes, and if I flex my resume back, I'm a bully," Green said. "And so in this world we live in, this this soft world that we live in, it's like, 'Oh, he's picking on someone.'  People are going to say what they want to say. If I have time, I respond. I usually respond once, and then just kind of let them talk to their microphone or their laptop. Or, in Eddie's case, whoever he has typing for him, because we know he don't know how to use those things."

Green even took a few shots at "Edification with Eddie Johnson," his latest venture in which he offers pointers to those interested in the finer points of basketball.

"No disrespect to y'all," Green said, addressing the assembled media. "Y'all been doing this for a long time. You ain't new to it, true to it. But then so many idiots start getting into media and microphones, and a microphone is 100 bucks. Microphones used to cost way more money. It used to be so much, (but) so everybody can afford one now.

"And because they can afford one, they just start spewing things because they think hate will drive you to edification."

Draymond's "edification" on Sunday began in the first quarter, as he drained a 3-pointer 15 seconds into the game and buried two more within the first six minutes for nine points. That won't impress Johnson, but it set a tone that granted a request from Kerr.

Kerr informed Green during the recent road trip that he would be moving back to his more natural power forward position, Yaxel Lendeborg sliding to small forward and Charles Bassey taking over as the starting center for the foreseeable future.

"We needed more size on the front line," Kerr said. "Bassey's a great roll threat for Yax, and that was going to put Draymond in the corner. So that meant he's just got to let it fly, and he did. He took that first one and knocked it down, knocked down another one, and played a key role on the offensive side.

"But of course, it's his defense that makes him who he is."

Which is why Kerr resists the idea of moving Green to the bench. He's the team's best defender, the orchestrator on that end, and he validated that in the second half.

After Boston's six-time All-Star Jayson Tatum spent the first half lighting up a variety of defenders, including Green — scoring 23 points on 8-of-13 shooting from the field, including 4 of 6 from deep — Draymond flipped the script after halftime.

Tatum scored four points, all free throws, and was 0 of 7 from the field. His last miss, a potential game-winner with two seconds remaining, was forced by Green.

"I always tell you there's no way to stop a superstar in his league," Green said. "You try to make him take tough shots, and you live with the results. Second half, I think the pressure was better on him. He wasn't as comfortable as he was in the first half. So, overall, we did a pretty good job."

The question the Warriors must answer in the coming days and weeks is whether they fully understand that defense is the only way for their fractured squad to get a sniff of success. It surely is, with or without Stephen Curry.
