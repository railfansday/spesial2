What midseason 49ers trends matter?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Through ten weeks, the San Francisco 49ers are 8-1, with their two most difficult matchups of the season in Mexico City against Minnesota and at home against the Seahawks upcoming. With eight games remaining, this feels like the halfway point of the season for the 49ers and a large enough sample size to analyze certain trends and tendencies.

Which trends will continue, and which have a chance to change?

This trend matters because of Brian Flores and the Vikings defense. The 49ers offense won't be able to use play action, since turning your back to the coverages is as difficult as it gets when the coverage changes. Expect a ton of straight dropbacks from under center to give Brock Purdy a chance to decipher the coverages.

Against the blitz, Purdy has punished opposing defenses to the tune of +0.33 EPA/dropback on 86 blitzed dropbacks. Flores will turn the heat up, but the simulated pressures will be something to watch as the defensive coordinator plays on your expectations and could just send four and drop four more, or any other variation you can think of.

This number matters as teams are looking for any way to slow down this 49ers offense.

The 49ers defense in expected passing situations

Look, Sunday was rough on defense, in particular the secondary. The Cowboys did what they wanted all Sunday. Surprisingly, the 49ers are eighth in the NFL in expected passing situations, allowing a -0.13 EPA/dropback. Expected passing situations are defined as 70% based on down, distance, score, time left, etc.

The 49ers under Raheem Morris are a Cover 3 defense with a 48% C3 rate in expected passing situations. The second-highest defensive coverage is Cover 1 Man at 16.6%. The 49ers will have to pressure Kirk Cousins on Sunday night. Speaking of…

At first glance, it felt like the 49ers' pass rush was nonexistent. After a rewatch, I don't hold that same opinion. The Cowboys dropped back trying to keep up, and the 49ers generated 26 pressures, two sacks (one negated by a penalty), 12 QB hits, and 12 hurries.

Mykel Williams had an effective game, as did Keion White and Nick Bosa. White finished with a 30.8% pass-rush win rate in true pass sets, and Bosa was second at 29.4%. Pressuring Cousins will lead to turnover chances for the 49ers' defense, but the pass rush is on the cusp of a huge game.
