UK Championship match schedule, seeds & BBC TV coverage

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

The 50th UK Championship starts at York Barbican on Saturday with England's Mark Selby seeking to defend his title.

Selby won his third UK title last year by beating 2024 champion Judd Trump 10-8 and, as the reigning champion, is the number one seed this year.

World champion Wu Yize and world number one Zhao Xintong, both from China, are the second and third seeds respectively.

Kyren Wilson has won the past three ranking events this season and arguably starts as favourite, although he is seeded eighth.

Ronnie O'Sullivan, who has lifted the trophy a record eight times, has had a challenging campaign and is seeded 13th as he seeks a first ranking title since January 2024.

The top 16 seeds will be drawn against one of 16 qualifiers, with the 128-player qualifying tournament taking place in Leicester from 21-26 November.

The championship is the first Triple Crown event of the season, with the other two - the Masters and the World Championship - both taking place in 2027.

The BBC has extensive daily coverage on BBC TV, while you can watch every shot of the championship on BBC iPlayer, the BBC Sport website and the BBC Sport app.

The match schedule, seeds, coverage times and prize money are listed below.

Ronnie O'Sullivan was 17 years and 358 days old when he won the UK Championship for the first time [BBC Sport]

Ronnie O'Sullivan, John Higgins and Mark Williams - the sport's celebrated 'Class of 92 - are all in action at the event.

O'Sullivan won the first of his record eight UK titles by beating Stephen Hendry as a 17-year-old in 1993.

The Rocket, who will be 51 on 5 December, was the most recent of the three legends to claim the title thanks to a 10-7 victory over Ding Junhui in 2023.

The Englishman has not played many tournaments this season and has fallen down the world rankings, but can never be written off.

Higgins won the title in 1998, 2000 and 2010 - with all three of the Scot's wins coming against fellow 51-year-old Williams.

Welshman Williams won his two titles in 1999 and 2002.

The trio have won a World Snooker Tour title every year since 1993 - a remarkable run of 33 years - but have yet to lift a trophy in 2026.

The UK Championship, Shoot Out and Scottish Open are the only events left this year for them to continue their winning sequence.

Williams starts his campaign on Saturday afternoon, O'Sullivan plays on Sunday afternoon and Higgins has to wait until Tuesday evening.

The top 16 in the world rankings following the International Championship are guaranteed places in the last 32 in York.

Apart from O'Sullivan, there are eight other former winners in the top 16 - Selby, Higgins, Neil Robertson, Williams, Trump, Shaun Murphy, Zhao and Mark Allen.

The draw for the first round will be made on Thursday at 14:30 GMT during the final round of qualifying.

Three-time UK champion Ding Junhui will be one of the players hoping to qualify for York.

The 16 qualifiers will drawn to face one of the seeded players.

The prize fund has gone up nearly £300,000 from 2025, with the winner earning £312,500 this year.

O'Sullivan leads the way with eight titles, having won his first Triple Crown event in 1993.

O'Sullivan also lifted the trophy in 1997, 2001, 2007, 2014, 2017, 2018 and 2023.

Fellow Englishman Steve Davis has won the title six times - one more than Scottish rival Hendry.

Wilson, world champion in 2024 and Masters winner in 2026, would become only the 12th player to achieve a career Triple Crown if we wins the final on 6 December.

2017: Ronnie O'Sullivan 10-5 Shaun Murphy

There have been 23 maximum 147 breaks made across 49 previous editions of the UK Championship and its qualifying tournament.

Willie Thorne compiled the first one against Tommy Murphy in 1987.

Hendry and O'Sullivan are the only players to have made two, with Hendry achieving his 147s in 1995 and 1999.

O'Sullivan displayed incredible composure to make a 147 in the final frame of his semi-final against Selby in 2007. He made his other in 2014.

The 2012 championship featured three 147s with Andy Hicks, Jack Lisowski and Higgins all achieving maximum.

Australian Robertson holds the distinction of being the only player to achieve a 147 in a UK Championship final, doing so in the sixth frame of his 2015 triumph against Liang Wenbo.

Liam Pullen and Chang Bingyu, the 16th seed this year, made maximums in qualifying last year.
