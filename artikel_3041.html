Where Will AbbVie Stock Be in 5 Years? Its 2 Immunology Blockbusters Are Still Growing 20%-Plus per Year

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Combined annual sales of Skyrizi and Rinvoq have surpassed Humira’s peak, and AbbVie’s pipeline is just getting started.

By Prosper Junior Bakiny – Nov 27, 2026 at 1:17PM EST

Over the past five years, AbbVie (ABBV -0.88%) has more than tripled the S&P 500's total return, despite losing patent exclusivity for its biggest growth driver, Humira, an immunology medicine. The company's ability to overcome the loss of Humira, the best-selling drug in the industry's history, speaks volumes about its underlying business. But where might AbbVie be headed over the next five years? Let's see whether the drugmaker can continue beating the market.

AbbVie's core growth drivers in recent years have been Skyrizi and Rinvoq, two immunology drugs that have earned numerous indications overlapping with those of Humira. In fact, these two drugs have demonstrated superior efficacy compared to Humira across several indications. For instance, in a Phase 3 study in certain patients with plaque psoriasis, AbbVie pitted Skyrizi against Humira, and the former performed better.

Rinvoq also beat Humira in studies in some patients with rheumatoid arthritis. It's no wonder, then, that these two medicines already combine to generate more than Humira's $21.2 billion peak annual sales. Through Sept. 30, Skyrizi's revenue was $15.8 billion, up 25.6% year over year. Rinvoq's sales came in at $7.4 billion, 23.9% higher than the year-ago period. What's more, both have secured several expansions this year and should win more over the next five years.

And neither will lose patent exclusivity until at least 2033. Note that they have made it a habit to exceed expectations. AbbVie expects them to combine for more than $31 billion in sales this year. It had initially set a $31 billion combined target for 2027 (and even that 2027 guidance was $4 billion above a previous one).

True, the competitive landscape will evolve over the next few years. That could be a problem for AbbVie. But even with that caveat, we can expect Skyrizi and Rinvoq to continue performing well over the medium term.

Five years is enough time for AbbVie to launch several new products that will help it improve its financial results and overcome challenges, including government-led drug price negotiations in the U.S., which could reduce sales for some of its products. The pharmaceutical leader boasts several promising candidates investors should keep an eye on. One of the most promising is zumilokibart, an investigational medicine for eczema. Here's why this candidate looks particularly attractive.

According to some estimates, the eczema drug market was valued at $21 billion last year and could rise to $46 billion by 2034. It's a highly competitive industry, but zumilokibart has shown promising efficacy data while also benefiting from a very convenient twice-a-year maintenance dosing schedule, something no currently approved medicine in the U.S. offers.

If zumilokibart performs well in Phase 3 studies and earns approval within the next five years, it could help strengthen AbbVie's portfolio and extend its lead in immunology. Another important candidate AbbVie is working on is ABBV-295, an investigational weight-loss medicine, perhaps one of the fastest-growing therapeutic areas in the industry.

AbbVie licensed ABBV-295 from a Denmark-based company. The weight loss drug market might be worth $150 billion by 2035, up from $15 billion in 2024. AbbVie's ABBV-295 is being investigated as a long-acting treatment and, if approved, may be administered once per month. Investors should also keep track of ABBV-295's progress over the next few years, and these two candidates are only the tip of the iceberg. AbbVie is developing many more brand-new drugs and could have an even stronger portfolio by 2031.

In the meantime, the company expects no major loss of patent exclusivity through the end of the decade.

The market is well aware of AbbVie's excellent prospects. That's why the drugmaker's shares have crushed broader equities this year. But is the stock too expensive? Not in my view. AbbVie is currently trading at 18.5x forward earnings, versus an average of 19.2x for healthcare stocks. One might argue that AbbVie is worth a premium over its peers, especially those in the pharmaceutical industry. Many drugmakers will face major patent cliffs by 2030, and few post sales and earnings growth as consistently as AbbVie's.

Further, AbbVie has an outstanding dividend program, having increased its payouts for 55 consecutive years, making it a Dividend King (a company with 50 or more consecutive dividend increases). Given all that, I'd value AbbVie slightly above 20x forward earnings. So, at current levels, the stock looks like a steal.
