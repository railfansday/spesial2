23 Ways to Save Money on Vacation – Plan, Book, and Spend for Less

Save the most on a vacation by deciding when to go, how to fly, and where to sleep before you book anything, since those choices set the trip’s biggest costs. Then trim the remaining costs with smaller moves at the airport and once you arrive.

U.S. scheduled passenger airlines collected $7.4 billion in baggage fees in 2025, the Bureau of Transportation Statistics reports, and a bag fee is one charge you can often skip.

The moves below are grouped by when you’d make them: while planning the trip, while booking it, and once you’re on your way. The money they save can go back into the trip itself, so spending less doesn’t have to mean seeing less.

Paying for the trip from savings means no credit card balance after you’re home, and every other move on this list shrinks the amount you need to save.

Price your next vacation, divide the cost by the months until you leave, and set up an automatic transfer for that amount into a high-yield savings account used only for the trip. Send windfalls like a tax refund or a work bonus there too, to reach the full cost sooner.

Where you go sets the price of the flight and much of the rest of the trip, so it pays to keep your options open if more than one place would make you happy.

Pick your vacation spot first, and you pay whatever it costs to fly there. Instead, search a fare map from your home airport with the destination left blank. Set price alerts on a short list of trips you’d happily take, and book when one drops to a price you’re willing to pay.

Flights and rooms at popular places cost the most when everyone wants to go, so travelers who know where they’re headed but can be flexible about dates stand to save.

Move your trip into the destination’s shoulder season, the weeks just before or after its peak, and the same flights often cost less, with thinner crowds and weather that’s usually still good. The discount varies by place and year, so compare prices for several weeks around your dates before you book.

Read more: Travel in the Shoulder Season - Price the Same Trip Week by Week

On a short trip to a big city, the room’s location, how you get around, and which sights you pay for decide much of what you spend.

On a city weekend, book a room near a train line rather than downtown, ride transit instead of paying to park, and plan your days around sights that are free for visitors. Buy an attractions pass only if the places you’ll see would cost more at the gate.

Read more: A Cheaper City Weekend - Stay Near a Train Line and Do the Pass Math

Even with the place and the week settled, the fare for the same route can change with the day you fly and the airport you leave from.

The cheapest flight usually goes to the traveler who can shift dates or airports. Search a calendar of fares instead of fixed dates, include every airport near you, and set a price alert so you can book when the fare dips. Compare fares only after adding the bags and seat you’ll pay for.

Read more: Find the Cheapest Flight - Search Flexible Dates & Nearby Airports, Then Track the Price

Credit card points you’ve already collected can pay for the flight instead of cash, which leaves more of the trip’s budget for everything else.

Flexible credit card points can usually move into an airline’s frequent flyer program at one point per mile. There, the same flight often costs far fewer points than in your card’s travel portal, plus taxes and fees in cash. Find the seat on the airline’s site first, then transfer only what the booking needs, since transfers are final.

Read more: Transfer Card Points to Airline Partners - Same Flight, Often Far Fewer Points

Bag fees come on top of the fare and repeat on every flight, so frequent flyers and families have the most to gain here.

Carry on whenever your fare includes a carry-on bag, since a checked bag now costs $45 or more each way on Delta, American, and Southwest. If you check bags on most trips, get an airline credit card or elite status that waives the first bag for you and the companions on your reservation.

Read more: Avoid Checked Bag Fees - Carry On, or Get Them Waived for Your Whole Party

Solo travelers and couples who would rather spend on the destination than on hotel extras are the ones a hostel suits best.

Many hostels rent private rooms, not just dorm bunks, for well under a hotel room’s price, though you may share a bathroom. Book one that recent guests rate highly for security and location, and cook in the shared kitchen to cut your food bill too.

Read more: Book a Hostel's Private Room for About 40% Less Than a Hotel - Bunks Optional

If the trip is about hiking or scenery, a campsite puts you inside the park itself, with no hotel bill at all.

A campsite in a state or national park costs a fraction of a hotel room, but popular campgrounds can fill the day reservations open. Book on Recreation.gov or your state’s park reservation site that morning, buy an annual pass if you’ll visit several parks, and borrow gear for your first trip.

Read more: Park Campsites Cost a Fraction of a Hotel Room - Book the Day Reservations Open

House-sitting takes more groundwork than booking a room, so it suits travelers with flexible dates who like animals and plan to travel this way more than once.

House-sitting gets you a free place to stay in exchange for caring for someone’s home and pets while they’re away. Join a house-sitting site and earn your first reviews on short sits near home, since owners choose sitters by their reviews. Then apply for longer sits whose dates and pets fit you.

Read more: House-Sit for Free Lodging - Earn Reviews Near Home Before You Sit Abroad

Restaurant meals for a family get expensive over a long stay, so the room you pick can decide how much of the food budget goes to eating out.

For a family trip of three nights or more, compare a hotel suite, extended-stay room, or rental with a kitchen to a standard room. Add up the kitchen’s extra cost for the whole stay, any cleaning fee included, and book the one with the kitchen if cooking breakfast and a few dinners would save your family more than that.

Read more: Book a Room With a Kitchen - Cooking Breakfast Can Cover the Higher Rate

When cooking isn’t an option, a free hotel breakfast can still take one meal a day off the family’s food bill.

Midprice hotel chains such as Hampton by Hilton, Holiday Inn Express, and Drury include a hot breakfast in the room rate. When you compare hotels, add what your family would spend on breakfast out to the cheaper room’s rate, and book the breakfast hotel when its rate is lower than that total.

Read more: 12 Hotel Chains With Free Breakfast - and When They Beat a Cheaper Room

Travelers who’ve already picked a chain hotel can still get more for their money, and this check takes only a few minutes before you book.

Before you book a chain hotel through a booking app, check the chain’s own site, signed in to its free loyalty program. Members often pay no more there than on the app, and booking through the app forfeits the chain’s points and credit toward elite status. Compare the same room, dates, and cancellation terms at the final price.

Read more: The Hotel's Member Rate Often Beats the Booking App - Check It Before You Book

The discount comes from something you already belong to, so checking costs nothing, and it applies to cruises as well as hotels.

Before you book a hotel or cruise, check the member rates you already have through a warehouse club, AAA or AARP, your employer, your credit union, or military service. Price the same trip booked direct too, subtract any shop card or onboard credit the member deal adds, and book whichever costs less.

Read more: Your Memberships Can Cut Hotel & Cruise Prices - Check Each Against Booking Direct

This move can still save money after you’ve booked, so it suits travelers who reserve a hotel early to be sure of a room.

A refundable hotel rate usually costs more than a prepaid one, but it lets you rebook the same room for less if the price drops before your stay. Book one early, set a price alert for your dates, and when the rate falls, reserve the cheaper room first and cancel the original second.

Read more: A Refundable Hotel Booking Can Only Get Cheaper - Rebook When Prices Drop

Prepaying for flights and rooms saves money until something cancels the trip, and buying a travel insurance policy you don’t need would undo part of those savings.

Many credit cards repay prepaid, nonrefundable costs when illness, severe weather, or another covered reason cancels or cuts short a trip you paid for with the card, and some cover meals and lodging during a long delay. Check your card’s guide to benefits before you buy travel insurance, and book with a card that carries the coverage.

Read more: Your Credit Card Can Cover a Canceled Trip - Check It Before Buying Travel Insurance

Airport restaurants and shops charge more than the same food costs at home, and the gap grows on a long travel day or with a family to feed.

Pack sandwiches and snacks from your kitchen and carry an empty water bottle to fill past security, since TSA lets solid food and empty bottles through the checkpoint. Spreads, yogurt, and drinks fall under the liquids rule, so each needs a container of 3.4 ounces or less.

Unlike most moves here, this one pays you instead of cutting a cost, which suits travelers with nothing urgent waiting at their destination.

If you can arrive a few hours late, volunteer your seat when an airline oversells your flight, and treat its first offer as an opening bid. Before you accept, get a confirmed seat on a later flight and ask what the travel credit is worth, when it expires, and whether you can take cash instead.

Read more: Volunteer Your Seat on an Oversold Flight - Then Negotiate Before You Accept

Rental companies offer extra coverage at pickup, charged by the day, so its cost grows with every day of a road trip.

Check whether your credit card covers collision damage and theft on rental cars before your next trip. Many cards do, but only if you pay for the whole rental with that card and decline the counter’s damage waiver. Read your card’s benefits guide first to confirm the coverage, then skip the waiver’s daily charge.

Read more: Decline the Rental Counter's Damage Waiver If Your Credit Card Covers the Car

International travelers can sort this out at home, before the trip, and a fee-free card used to pay in the local currency avoids both a card fee and a currency markup.

Before you travel abroad, check your credit card for a foreign transaction fee, usually 1% to 3% of every purchase processed outside the U.S. If it has one, pack a card that charges none. The fee buys you nothing, and it adds up over a whole trip.

Read more: Pack a Card With No Foreign Transaction Fee - Others Charge Up to 3%

Abroad, the offer to pay in dollars appears at checkout after checkout, so it helps to know your answer before you’re first asked.

When a card terminal, ATM, or foreign website offers to charge you in U.S. dollars, decline and pay in the local currency. Saying yes lets the merchant or ATM operator set the exchange rate, and its markup routinely adds several percent to what your own card would charge.

Read more: Choose the Local Currency Abroad - The Dollar Option Comes With a Markup

Some places abroad take only cash, such as small shops, markets, and taxis, so most international travelers need some local money on hand.

Before a trip abroad, get a debit card that charges no foreign transaction or ATM fees. When you arrive, use it to take out local cash at a bank’s ATM, and pick the local currency over dollars when the screen asks. That withdrawal costs less than changing money at a counter or taking a credit card cash advance.

Read more: Get Cash Abroad at a Bank's ATM - On a Debit Card Without Foreign Fees

Maps, translation, and ride-hailing apps all use data, so most travelers abroad need some, and how you buy it sets the price.

Before you take your phone abroad, check whether your plan already includes data where you’re going. If it doesn’t, download a travel eSIM from an app, since a week of its data can cost less than two days of Verizon’s or AT&T’s $12 day pass. Turn off data roaming on your U.S. line before you land.

Read more: Phone Data Abroad - A Travel eSIM Costs a Fraction of a Carrier's Day Pass

Used together, these moves cut the cost of a vacation at every stage, so the savings add up across the whole trip. Set your trip budget and pick your travel dates first, before you search for a single flight or room.
