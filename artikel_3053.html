Here's How the S&P 500 Has Moved the Day After Each of Nvidia's Last 12 Reports

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The chipmaker is around 6% of the index. How much have its reports actually moved it?

Nvidia (NVDA -4.12%) is set to report results after the bell on Tuesday, Nov. 17, for the three months ending Oct. 25 (its fiscal 2027 third quarter). That makes Wednesday, Nov. 18, the first session when the S&P 500 (^GSPC -1.92%) can react to the numbers.

It's also the graphics processing unit maker's first report since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on Nov. 11. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim CEO, and Nvidia said its results would still come out on schedule.

Index investors have a stake in this one, too. Nvidia makes up around 6% of the S&P 500's value, one of the largest weights among tech companies in the index. And as of this writing, the index stands near 7,560. That puts it around 3% under the record it set when it closed at 7,818.93 on Oct. 6.

I went back over Nvidia's last 12 reports to check what the index did the next day. It rose seven times and dropped five times. On average, the move came to almost nothing.

I got every report date from Nvidia's own earnings releases, starting with Nov. 21, 2023, and paired it with the S&P 500's daily closing levels from Yahoo Finance, which don't include dividends. As Nvidia reports after the bell, each move goes from the report day's close to the next session's close.

The largest gain was on Feb. 22, 2024, after Nvidia's February 2024 report, when the index rose around 2.1%. That was its best day in over a year. The two worst days were almost tied, with losses of around 1.6% on both Feb. 27, 2025, and Nov. 20, 2025.

The other nine moves were small. None topped 0.8% either way, and one of the five down days hardly counts. On Aug. 29, 2024, the index ended at 5,591.96, less than a quarter point below its close the day before.

All told, the 12 days averaged a gain of around 0.02%. And the median gain was around 0.2%.

To check whether those days stood out, I compared them with every session over the same span, from the Nov. 21, 2023, close through Nov. 12, 2026. The index climbed on around 56% of those days, so seven of 12 (58%) is roughly the same rate.

The size of the moves is where the report days differ a bit. Up or down, the S&P 500 moved an average of around 0.65% a day in those three years. On the 12 days after Nvidia's reports, it moved about 0.76%. And three of the 12 saw a swing of more than 1.5%, occurring in only about 9% of sessions. This arguably fits with Nvidia's size, because a 10% move in Nvidia's shares alone could shift the index by roughly 0.6% before accounting for other chip and AI stocks that often trade alongside it.

Of course, 12 days is a small sample, and three big days could easily be chance. The three also split two to one in favor of losses.

For an investor in an S&P 500 index fund like the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -1.89%), the longer view is likely the more helpful one. The index closed Nov. 21, 2023, the day of the first report in this set, at around 4,538. By Nov. 12, 2026, it had risen around 67% to 7,592.

Together, the 12 days after Nvidia's reports added about 0.2% of that. So nearly all of the gain came on the other 734 sessions, which didn't follow an Nvidia report.

The November reports don't show a pattern, either. The index climbed around 0.4% after the November 2023 report and about 0.5% after the November 2024 report, then lost around 1.6% after the November 2025 report.

Of course, a big day on Nov. 18 wouldn't look unusual this month. In the 10 sessions through Nov. 12, the S&P 500 swung more than 1.5% four times, and Nov. 5's drop of more than 4% was bigger than any of the 12 days in this set.

But nothing in Nvidia's report history shows which way the index is likely to go next. The Vanguard ETF trades around $693 as I write, about 3.5% under its 52-week high of $718.47. I'd feel comfortable buying shares of the fund in the week of the report, just as I would in any other week.
