Reddit's 2027 Outlook: Scaling Advertising Business Drives Sustained Growth

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

This under-the-radar communication stock boasts a Superscore of 86 from our Hidden Gems Primary database, part of The Motley Fool's Moneyball Database system. Here's why.

By Robert Izquierdo – Updated Nov 26, 2026 at 11:51AM EST

Picture a digital town square where millions of people gather daily to argue about everything from stock picks to the best way to boil an egg. That square is Reddit (RDDT +1.29%), an internet content platform that has successfully converted years of chaotic, human-generated chatter into a highly profitable advertising business. As of November 19, 2026, the stock trades at $91.04, reflecting a wild ride since its public debut, with the stock falling 52% over the last year despite significant operational progress.

Our proprietary Hidden Gems scoring system assigns Reddit an overall Superscore of 86 out of 100, placing it in the Strong category. It represents the unification of all our scores into a single score for public companies, with five rating bands: Exceptional (90-100), Strong (75-89), Above Average (60-74), Average (40-59), and Cautious (0-39).

An 86 Superscore places Reddit in the Top ~3% of every company we score, ahead of roughly 97 out of every 100 companies we track. Reddit runs a highly capital-efficient business, earning significant profits on a small base of hard physical assets. This means that each dollar of revenue growth effectively translates into outsized returns, a trait that often leads the market to apply a higher valuation multiple, though this efficiency does not fully eliminate the risks posed by shifting search traffic and platform competition.

I'm a big fan of Reddit, both the community-based platform and the company, which is why I bought the stock. Its valuable discussion forums in the era of synthetic content generated by artificial intelligence can easily enable Reddit to achieve years of ongoing business expansion.

The one key headwind is how AI is transforming the discoverability of Reddit's site. The company has seen a meaningful decline in consumer traffic from search engines to the point where its Q3 DAUqs of 130.3 million could drop in Q4. Reddit is addressing this challenge by targeting logged-in users. These DAUqs contribute more than 80% of revenue, so management is focused on growing this segment.

One way Reddit is protecting its content from AI bot-scrapping is by requiring visitors to log in. This not only protects community data, but also improves the targeting for ads, which should improve the revenue Reddit generates.

The company is also making improvements to its recommendation engine, used to personalize content and improve customer retention. As a result, management reported that new users who returned to Reddit are up 50% year over year in Q3, indicating its efforts are working.

The transition away from a dependence on search engines will take time, and in the interim, its shares may experience higher volatility. But the success it's delivering is why I believe Reddit's business will continue to strengthen over the next several years, and its stock price with it.
