Here's What Airline Maintenance Technicians Really Earn In 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Brandon's passion for aviation started at a young age. He became involved in the flight simulator and VATSIM communities, which sparked his interest in aviation and eventually led him to earn his private pilot license. Outside of work, he continues to build flight hours, explore new airports, and stay involved in the aviation community.

Airline maintenance technicians are among the best-paid workers in the skilled trades. At several of the largest US airlines, base pay at the top of the scale now passes $60 an hour, before premiums for licenses, shift work, and seniority are added. That puts experienced airline technicians well ahead of the national average for their occupation, which covers repair shops, manufacturers, and smaller operators as well as airlines.

Pay varies widely depending on the employer. Union contracts set rates at most major carriers, Delta sets its own as a non-union airline, and technicians at regional airlines earn considerably less. United’s technicians are still working under an older scale after a new agreement failed to win approval this year. Meanwhile, a long-running shortage of licensed technicians continues to shape what airlines are willing to pay.

Bureau of Labor Statistics data puts the median annual wage for aircraft mechanics and service technicians at $79,870 in May 2025, with a mean annual wage of $84,740 and a median hourly wage of $38.40. The range around that midpoint is wide. The lowest-paid 10% earn less than $48,780 a year, while the top 10% earn more than $128,890. That spread reflects the variety of employers covered, from small general aviation repair shops to the largest airlines.

Where a technician works makes a large difference. Air transportation had a May 2025 median of $96,170 for aircraft mechanics and service technicians, about $16,000 above the national figure for the occupation as a whole. Manufacturing pays less. BLS lists 23,590 aircraft mechanics in aerospace product and parts manufacturing, with a mean hourly wage of $35.84, or $74,550 a year, the industry with the largest single concentration of these jobs. Airlines pay more partly because their technicians work on large transport-category aircraft under union contracts with defined pay scales.

Aircraft technicians also earn well above most other mechanics. BLS puts the median annual wage for automotive service technicians and mechanics at $50,620 in May 2025, which leaves aircraft mechanics about 58% ahead. The difference reflects the training and certification the job requires. Most airline technicians hold an FAA mechanic certificate with airframe and powerplant ratings, which requires an approved training program or documented experience, along with written, oral, and practical exams. National medians also include technicians early in their careers and those outside airlines, so they understate what experienced staff at major carriers earn.

Union contracts set pay at most major US airlines, and they publish it as hourly scales that rise with years of service. At American, technicians represented by the TWU-IAM Association start at $35.59 an hour in their first year and reach a top rate of $60.72 after eight to nine years. That scale is listed as effective January 1, 2026, although the AMT Index, which compiles rates from contract documents, notes that the date has not been confirmed as a payroll start.

Southwest and Alaska, both represented by the Aircraft Mechanics Fraternal Association, sit at almost the same level. Southwest technicians earn $35.77 an hour for their first six months and reach $60.42 after six years, under rates effective August 16, 2026. That is the fastest progression of the three carriers. Alaska’s scale starts at $36.61 an hour at Step 1 and reaches $60.47 at Step 8, with those rates taking effect on October 17, 2026.

The three top rates fall within about 30 cents an hour of one another. At $60.72 an hour, a technician working a standard 2,080-hour year at the top of American’s scale would earn roughly $126,300 in base pay alone. These figures cover base wages only. The AMT Index describes them as scale rates before license, shift, lead, longevity, and retirement add-ons, so most technicians at the top of these scales take home more than the base rate suggests. Years to top pay matters as well, since a shorter progression means a technician reaches the highest rate sooner and earns more over a career.

Why do airlines pay A350 and Boeing 777 Captains the same, even though no US carrier operates both of the aircraft types?

United has the lowest top rate of the four largest US airlines. Its technicians, represented by the Teamsters, start at $29.27 an hour and reach $54.74 after eight years. That scale dates from a contract that became amendable in December 2024. United’s Teamsters-represented technicians began successor-contract talks in August 2024, ahead of the existing agreement’s December 5, 2024, amendable date.

Negotiations produced an agreement in principle in July 2026. The Teamsters said it would deliver industry-leading wage increases, accelerate progression to top pay from eight years to five, and provide a $54 million signing bonus upon ratification, which the union said worked out to $5,000 per member. Pay charts the union published in August said the tentative agreement increases rates by $4.98 or more and reduces basic wage scale progression. Members voted down the agreement in September, so United’s technicians remain on the existing scale while talks continue.

Delta takes a different approach. Its technicians are not represented by a union, so the airline sets pay through company policy and does not publish a full scale. A March 2026 Delta listing for an aircraft maintenance technician in Minneapolis advertised a starting pay of $43.47 per hour, well above the starting rates at American, Alaska, Southwest, and United. Delta also gave employees a raise this year. The airline said it would implement a 4% pay increase for more than 80,000 employees effective June 1, the fifth consecutive year of raises, on top of regular step increases for many frontline teams.

Base scales capture only part of what an airline technician earns. Contracts add premiums for holding an airframe and powerplant certificate, for working line maintenance, and for seniority. The Teamsters’ pay charts for United show how much those additions are worth, defining an all-in rate as basic rates plus a $1.20 VEBA contribution and premiums for A&P, Line, and Longevity. The union estimated that A&P technicians would see pay increases of between $59,342 and $131,747 over the four-year term of the agreement members later rejected. Night shift differentials and overtime come on top of all-in rates.

Pay falls sharply outside the largest carriers. Allegiant technicians start at $36.50 an hour and reach $50.52 after nine years of longevity, and Hawaiian’s scale runs from $30.02 to $47.83 after about eight years. Hawaiian’s rates are also in transition. AMFA won the combined Alaska-Hawaiian representation election on February 25, 2026, and the IAM agreement stays in place until a joint contract is ratified.

Regional airlines pay the least. Horizon technicians reach $39.31 after six years, and PSA’s top rate is $39.00 at nine and 10 years. At Envoy, the agreement’s top step pays $38.51 since November 2025, rising to $39.67 from November 2026. Those top regional rates sit only a few dollars above what a first-year technician earns at American, Alaska, or Southwest.

Airlines are competing for technicians in a tight labor market. Boeing’s 2026 Pilot and Technician Outlook projects that the industry will need about 728,000 new maintenance technicians worldwide over the next 20 years, more than the 674,000 new pilots it expects airlines to require. Boeing describes maintenance technician hiring as one of the most competitive segments of the aviation labor market. Retirements are a major factor. Aviation Week reported that Boeing estimates retirements account for 40% of maintenance technician attrition.

The US training pipeline has grown, but not enough to close the gap. The 2026 Pipeline Report from the Aviation Technician Education Council and Oliver Wyman counted 11,103 new FAA airframe and powerplant mechanic certificates issued in 2025, a record. Even so, Oliver Wyman forecasts demand for 25,000 more North American aviation technical personnel than the industry will have, including 9,100 certificated mechanics. Many newly certificated mechanics also take jobs outside airlines, in general aviation, manufacturing or other industries.

Consultancies outside the industry see the same pressure. McKinsey estimated in March 2026 that without intervention, the technician deficit could reach 60,000 in 2029, and it warned that shortages can drive up aviation costs, delay aircraft turnaround times, and hamper aircraft reliability. Labor costs and maintenance capacity are linked for airlines because a carrier that cannot staff its hangars must send work to outside repair stations or leave aircraft out of service longer. Recent contracts at American, Alaska, and Southwest have pushed top base rates past $60 an hour, while United’s technicians are holding out for terms they consider competitive with those carriers.
