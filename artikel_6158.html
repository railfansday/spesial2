Delta Air Lines Cancels 18 Boeing 757 Routes: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Delta Air Lines is withdrawing its Boeing 757s from 18 routes this winter, as the Atlanta-based carrier continues transitioning toward newer, more efficient aircraft. A comparison of its winter 2025/26 and 2026/27 schedules reveals the changes, which include several transcontinental and Hawaii services. However, most affected routes will continue operating with other aircraft types.

The Airbus A321neo is taking over many of these services, while Delta is increasingly concentrating its remaining 757 operations around its largest hubs. Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL) dominates the airline's 757 network, with Florida accounting for seven of its ten busiest routes. The changes illustrate the evolving role of an aircraft that first joined Delta's fleet in 1984.

The biggest change is on the route from Atlanta to Richmond International Airport (RIC), which had 260 scheduled 757 departures last winter. Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA) to Seattle-Tacoma International Airport (SEA) follows with 154, while Seattle–Kona and Seattle–Lihue each recorded 140. Together, these four routes accounted for nearly two-thirds of the 1,121 departures affected by the changes.

The Hawaii changes are particularly significant, with the A321neo replacing the 757 on three routes. Delta describes the 194-seat A321neo as its most fuel-efficient aircraft, and its growing importance is reflected in an order for 34 additional examples announced in February. Kristen Bojko, Delta's Vice President of Fleet, explained:

"The A321neo has proven to be an exceptional aircraft for Delta, delivering industry-leading efficiency while offering the premium experience our customers value."

Importantly, these changes do not represent 18 outright route cancellations. Most services continue using replacement aircraft, although Minneapolis–Sint Maarten is absent from the current schedule. Delta is simultaneously expanding elsewhere, including its Hawaii network, where additional frequencies and new services are planned for winter 2026/27.

For passengers hoping to experience the iconic aircraft, Atlanta remains the best option. The hub accounts for 11,575 scheduled 757 departures in Delta's winter schedule, compared with 2,526 at Detroit and 2,055 at Minneapolis. Together, these three hubs dominate Delta's remaining 757 network, although the aircraft also operates selected transcontinental services.

Florida is particularly important, accounting for seven of the top remaining routes. Delta has long used the 757 as a workhorse to Florida, where its higher seating capacity remains especially useful on busy leisure services. However, even here, the 757 is starting to fade away, with Orlando International Airport (MCO) being a prime example. One of Delta's busiest routes, with up to 17 daily flights, used to operate exclusively with 757s. Today, up to half of the flights are with A321 aircraft.

Replacing its 757s is far easier said than done for Delta.

Delta has been gradually retiring the older aircraft in its 757 fleet. Its regulatory filings show that from a peak of nearly 200 aircraft immediately after the Northwest Airlines merger, the airline has just 70 in operation today, with six retirements so far this year. Meanwhile, the incoming Airbus A321neo fleet has been moving in the opposite direction.

Delta has been unusually explicit about what comes next. At its 2023 Investor Day, the airline placed the 757-200 directly alongside the A321neo as its next-generation replacement, estimating around 10% lower seat costs, 13 more premium seats, and an additional $4-5 million in annual contribution per replacement aircraft.

Delta currently has 110 A321neos operating and has 79 on firm order after it exercised options for 34 aircraft earlier this year, and also retains further options for 36 additional A321neos. But the A321neo will not shoulder the entire 757 replacement burden. Delta also has commitments for 100 Boeing 737 MAX 10s plus 30 options, giving it another high-capacity, high-premium narrowbody for domestic flying once the type enters service.
