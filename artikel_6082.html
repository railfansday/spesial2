Only 22% Full: Breeze Airways' 11 Emptiest Routes Revealed

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Utah-based Breeze Airways has carved out a niche for nonstop flights in secondary markets across the US. The low-cost carrier offers a unique point-to-point market on underserved domestic routes and operates a fleet of modern Airbus A220-300s.

Reviewing data from the Bureau of Transportation Statistics (BTS) suggests that a number of its routes are flying below the 'sweet spot' of 80% load factor. This is a target most airlines set to ensure that all operational costs are covered and that the route turns a profit. Overall, the average Breeze load factor sits at 77.9%.

Despite Breeze's rapid expansion across the US since the airline's launch in 2021, the airline's primary focus has been connecting secondary cities with nonstop flights. Reviewing the data from BTS, it suggests the airline's poorest performing routes flew with an average of less than 46% during the period of January to August 2026.

These low numbers are the polar opposite to the airline's fullest routes, which average more than 91% load factor, with the most popular route for the airline being that of Atlantic City (ACY) to Orlando (MCO), achieving a 93.8% average load factor.

As a comparison, the poorest performing route was between Orange County (SNA) and Montrose (MTJ), which averaged load factors of just 22.4%, which forced the carrier to suspend the service in April of this year.

With Orange County and Montrose having the poorest load factors, which led to the route being suspended, a number of other routes in the Breeze network are still performing with low patronage, questioning whether the airline may reconsider these routes. Simple Flying reached out to Breeze for comment on the below routes' load factors; however, an airline spokesperson could not immediately be reached.

For Breeze, its second-worst-performing route is between Charleston (CHS) in South Carolina and Richmond (RIC) in Virginia. The route continues to operate, despite a load factor averaging 38.5%. This is followed by Louisville (SDF) and Pittsburgh (PIT), which began in April and have only been able to achieve a 42.8% load factor. This route is expected to end in January.

Airlines will typically aim for an 80% passenger load factor, to cover high fixed costs and historically thin profit margins. Aiming for 80% helps cover the operating costs and turns the flight profitable. This can fluctuate between airlines, but this is the industry benchmark.

Underused seasonal links have crumbled under poor loads while the airline pivots its fleet toward bigger Myrtle Beach plans.

After Atlantic City to Orlando remains the airline's most popular route by load factor, the carrier's transcontinental services continue to pull high patronage. The airline's second most popular route is that of Norfolk (ORF) to San Diego (SAN), which during the period of January to August of this year recorded a 93% load factor.

Los Angeles (LAX) to Richmond is another popular link, which during the same period achieved 92.3% load factor.
