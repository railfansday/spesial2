West Ham should appoint London-born boss if Nuno Santo is sacked

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Nuno Espirito Santo's future at West Ham United is in serious doubt following consecutive home defeats to Southampton and West Bromwich Albion, and if he is fired there's one candidate who stands out from the rest.

The mood of the West Ham United faithful was very audible indeed at the end of their team's 3-1 home defeat to West Bromwich Albion on Saturday afternoon.

Boos echoed around an already half-empty London Stadium upon the final whistle at the end of a second home defeat in eight days, which dropped the Irons down to eighth place in the Championship table, leaving them four points from the division's automatic promotion places.

This result piled even greater pressure on the West Ham head coach Nuno Espirito Santo. Given that Santo was unable to keep the club in the Premier League at the end of the 2025-26 season, there had been some surprise that he remained in his position over the summer, but amid reports of a crisis meeting at which his future is to be discussed, talk is already turning to who might replace him and set their season back on track.

And while there will be suggestions for a replacement from all over Europe and possibly beyond, London-born Scott Parker, who played for West Ham and has already proven himself more than once in the Championship, stands out from the other potential candidates for the position.

When Nuno Espirito Santo was hired as the head coach at West Ham United on the 27th September 2025, the task ahead for him was clear. West Ham had just sacked Graham Potter and had lost four of their first five games of the season, a run which left them 19th in the Premier League. Santo's job was to keep the club in the Premier League.

But Santo did not keep West Ham in the Premier League. Results did pick up in the new year, and they went on a run which saw them lose just three times in 13 games between the middle of January and the end of April. But just when they needed that little nudge to get over the line, their form fell away again.

Three successive defeats to Brentford, Arsenal and Newcastle left them needing a win from their final home match of the season against Leeds United while hoping that Spurs lost at home to Everton. On this occasion, the West Ham players did rise to the occasion, winning 3-0, but a 1-0 win for Spurs against Everton rendered these efforts irrelevant. After 14 consecutive years of Premier League football, West Ham would be returning to the Championship.

Santo wasn't sacked and replaced over the summer, and some West Ham supporters might have questioned the wisdom of that non-decision when his team picked up just two points from their first three games, including a 2-1 home defeat to Charlton Athletic, but form did pick up, and by the time of their 6-0 win against Wrexham on the 11th September, they were top of the table.

But since then, they've only won two out of six, and two consecutive home defeats in eight days have turned the pressure back up under the embattled head coach. With the defeat to West Bromwich Albion coming on top of a 3-2 home loss to Southampton a week earlier, conceding six goals over two consecutive home matches is not something that West Ham fans would have been expecting to see this season. Coming on top of the relegation at the end of last season, the calls for Santo's departure have become impossible to ignore.

A slow start to West Ham's return to the Championship has turned the heat up under Nuno Espirito Santo, and Parker could be waiting in the wings.

As a big club in reduced circumstances, West Ham United require careful handling this season, but ahead of anything else they need a quick return to the Premier League. And this is where Scott Parker comes in. Parker was born in London and started his career with Charlton Athletic, but he also spent four years at West Ham from 2007 to 2011, scoring twelve goals and registering ten assists in 141 appearances for the club before signing for Tottenham Hotspur for £6 million.

Parker, then, has West Ham credentials, but he also has something else in his locker which should be of interest to the club's directors. Since entering the managerial game in 2019 with Fulham he has developed something of a reputation as a Championship specialist. Three times over the last seven years he's finished his season by leading a club into the Premier League, and that is the precise skill set that the club need at the moment.

​​​​​​In 2021, he returned Fulham to the Premier League through the play-offs, after they beat Brentford 2-1 in the Wembley final. Two years later at Bournemouth, he returned the Cherries to the top-flight as runners-up behind his former club, who'd been relegated back at the first attempt. And in 2025 he took Burnley back into the Premier League as runners-up behind Leeds United on goal difference, despite having run up 100 points with a team who conceded just 16 goals in 46 Championship games.

The issue with Parker has always been what happens once his teams get back to the Premier League. He was sacked by the end of the following season at all three of the clubs that he took up, and there's been little indication that his Championship credentials can be expanded to the peculiarities of the Premier League.

But Parker ticks a lot of boxes. For one thing, since he hasn't been in work since leaving Burnley shortly before the end of last season, he's available, meaning that hiring him would less expensive than having to pay compensation to another club. He's also a former West Ham player who knows the club.

And for the time being, the only thing that really matters in the first place is getting back into the top-flight. When West Ham were relegated last time, they did bounce back immediately, though it took the play-offs to get them there. Premier League parachute payments don't last forever, and every year that they don't get back up will be costing the club serious money.

London-born and with a history both with West Ham and of getting teams out of the Championship in an upward direction, Scott Parker could be an ideal solution for the club, should they decide that Nuno Espirito Santo has used up all of his chances at The London Stadium.
