GTA 6 Not Only Lets You Steal Cars, It’s The Most Grand Theft Auto Game To Date

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

It’s bizarre seeing the blatant lies that people spin about GTA 6 on Twitter to try and cash in on a few internet points. But the biggest misconception to come out of the rage-bait farm is that you can’t steal cars, presumably because of the key cloning and neighborhood limitations in the early game that have been wholly misunderstood. You can steal cars. And you can go back to those neighborhoods later and do whatever you want. The game is literally called Grand Theft Auto — it’s not just a blatant lie to get a few likes, either, it also undersells just how far Rockstar has taken car theft this time.

I’d never really considered this before, but for a series called Grand Theft Auto, it’s surreal how simplistic car theft has always been. Sure, there are variations. Sometimes you pull someone out, sometimes you smash a window and an alarm goes off, and sometimes you need to get a paint job to lose the cops, but it all boils down to one prompt — press Y or triangle. A one-size fits all system that doesn’t have a whole lot of depth. So, when I tried to rob my first flashy car in GTA 6 and couldn’t even start the engine because I lacked a key cloner, I was amazed.

Some have taken this as a personal slight, because friction in video games is, for some reason, the greatest sin a developer can commit in the 2020s. Everything must be handed to the player on a silver platter the second the disc enters the drive — or, I guess, the second you input the code. But these immediate limitations are a perfect way to add layers to car theft, introducing a seamless progression system folded into the narrative that reflects where Jason and Lucia are in their criminal lives that also keeps you invested in honing your skills.

You don’t just need a key cloner to jack expensive cars, but a dealer who can remove the advanced trackers that alert the cops. It stops the narrative from feeling disjointed from the free roam. How jarring would it be for Jason and Lucia to have access to a Pegassi while they’re forced to rob pill dealers and pawn the goods to someone out of a trailer for peanuts? Immediately throwing cars in front of us that we can’t steal, or at least keep, is every bit an incentive to keep climbing the criminal ladder for the player as enjoying the high life with Mattia is for Jason and Lucia.

The car fences are a crucial part of finally putting the Grand Theft Auto into, well, Grand Theft Auto. You start with the well-meaning Moses who doesn’t want to push his luck, handling one car at a time and never anything with a tracker. The lower the stakes, the less likely he is to have cops pull up at his garage door with a warrant. That means less money for each sale. But he also teaches Lucia and Jason the basics with a way to break into cars without needing to smash the window or set off the alarm, a huge leap from Chapter 1 that plants the first domino on that invisible progression we steadily move through.

Hell, it all kicks off from the very beginning with Jason clumsily breaking into a car and meeting Lucia.

The arrogant and obnoxious Randy pops up later in Chapter 3 with the key cloner, teaching Jason and Lucia how the advanced trackers work — drive fast, and it won’t alert the cops, which means risky driving through Vice City just to reach his garage in time. He hands you a list of flashy cars to steal, meaning that not only are you swiping cars off the street for your own personal collection, but to make some serious bank. Something that Moses gets the ball rolling with. Eventually, though, you can outright disable the advanced trackers, and bring any car to the Spray and Pray shops for easy money.

It’s a staggered system that means the more ambitious Jason and Lucia get, and the riskier jobs they take, the bigger the reward. You go from robbing diners for petty change to dealing in Grotti and Invetero Coquettes, quickly hitting the ceiling of your BuckMe account. And with more expensive jobs comes casing more expensive joints, tying car theft directly into burglaries as you stake out the high-end mansions on Starfish Island or cruise Vice Beach with the WAiNK app in hand, scanning cars on the road to see if there are any high-riders worth jacking.

I’ve lost countless hours in GTA 6 doing grand theft auto when I should be doing the story. In past games, stealing cars was just a means to an end. You needed a car to get somewhere, so you tapped Y and got in — bar a few missions where you’d swipe cars for someone. GTA 5 upped the ante with Franklin and the dealership, sure, but GTA 6 is where things finally feel like Grand Theft Auto. Completing Randy’s wishlist, cloning keys, disabling trackers, breaking into garages; that steady climb is unbelievably rewarding and makes that first Granforma Zorrusso you register feel like lightning in a bottle, rather than just another car on the road.

"The stance, the proportions, the headlights, the body lines, and the little design details" - Rockstar even nails the Floridian vibes of its cars.

It’s baffling that ‘you can’t steal cars in GTA 6’ has become a genuine talking point that anyone is taking seriously, when really, the one conversation we should be having right now is just how impressive the whole system really is. After I write this and log off work, I’m headed right back to Vice City to swipe some more cars from a bunch of rich snobs to fund my shopaholic lifestyle. It’s gonna be weird going back to older GTA games where the only incentive to steal a car is to get from A to B using Y.

The most anticipated video game of all time is a triumph on every level that exceeds years of unbelievable expectations. You're not ready for Leonida.
