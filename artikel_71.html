Woman whose baby died after free birth didn’t know she was pregnant with twins, NSW inquest hears

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Inquest continues into deaths of four babies born in free births or home births in northern rivers

Get our breaking news email, free app or daily news podcast

A northern rivers mother whose baby died after a free birth did not know she was having twins until she was in labour after having no pregnancy scans, the New South Wales coroner’s court has heard.

The inquest will examine the deaths of four babies born in free births or home births who died in the northern rivers region between 2022 and 2024. It will consider whether the care was reasonable in the circumstances and whether care by an unregistered support worker was reasonable and appropriate before, during and after the birth. Free births are when a person gives birth without a registered health professional present.

Both of the twins died – one was stillborn and most likely died in the womb before he was born, and one died within three hours of birth, the inquest heard. Only Baby D, who was born alive, is included in the inquest – coroner’s courts cannot examine the death of a stillborn baby. It is unclear why Baby D died, but the court heard it was possible the baby died from blood loss after the umbilical cord was cut but not clamped.

The inquest heard on Tuesday that the mother – who cannot be named for legal reasons – had decided to do a “sovereign birth”, where a woman gives birth at home on her own.

The mother had three to four sessions with Sita Tara Kali, a self-described birth keeper, before the birth, according to a statement the mother gave to police that was read to the inquest. She was paid a fee of $4,100.

Kali, who is expected to give evidence at a later date, was involved in the care of two of the four babies included in the inquest. She is not facing criminal charges.

The mother said in the police statement that her sessions with Kali before the birth were “mostly bodywork”. She said: “I didn’t get anything much out of the system in terms of birth preparation, a few books. Nothing of the quality she offered me.” The court heard that Kali had performed “body mapping”, but did not identify that there were two babies.

She said she felt “vibrant” and “healthy” during the pregnancy and the birth was “amazing”.

The mother only realised she was having another baby after giving birth to the first baby.

She said Kali advised them to ring an ambulance immediately, according to the police statement.

Sgt David Greenway told the inquest on Tuesday that when he arrived at the family’s house in 2024, he found one of the newborn twins had died. The other baby was on its way to hospital, he said.

The second twin, referred to as Baby D, did not survive.

Greenway said the parents were in shock and had not known they were expecting twins.

Greenway told the inquest that Kali was present at the house and said to him: “I’m the birth keeper, so I support women that want to free birth.

“I am not medically trained. And there is a big need because a lot of women don’t want to go to the hospital, and they can’t afford midwives. I’m not doing anything illegal. I know my rights,” Greenway recalled her saying.

“When people are choosing to be in their sovereignty, it is up to them what they choose to do,” Kali said, according to Greenway.

Dr Alison Teate, the president of the Australian College of Midwives, told the inquest on Tuesday if there are two twins and one placenta, they are at a higher risk category than if there are two placentas.

“The level of surveillance goes up significantly. It would be appointments every fortnight, from 20 weeks,” she said.

The inquest heard the neonatal expert Prof Nicholas Evans believed both twins would probably have survived if the pregnancy had been managed conventionally.

In the police statement, the mother said: “Our decision to not systemize our birth was very prominent. We decided to not have scans and try to birth as holistically and spiritually as possible. Which would mean that we would have a home birth out of the system with a doula who is incredibly experienced.”
