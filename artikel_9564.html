Meeting HIPAA and GDPR compliance requirements while running AI inference requires control over every prompt, document, embedding, and log. Both frameworks also place restrictions over who can read that data and how long you can store it.

Self-hosting open-source models on infrastructure you control is the most direct way to manage such requirements, but it’s not the only option.

Agents make the whole situation even more complicated. One user query can turn into a dozen model calls, and every call represents another point where sensitive data can end up.

TL;DR Neither HIPAA nor GDPR requires self-hosting. OpenAI and Anthropic both offer HIPAA-related contracts for eligible features. Self-hosting may be a better option when feature scope, data retention, data location, version pinning, or volume make a hosted contract impractical.

Let’s see where an AI agent can expose sensitive data. Is the prompt the only thing that needs protection? Spoiler: its just the tip of the iceberg.

User input, often containing health and other personal details

Retrieved context (Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG)

Often more sensitive than the prompt itself.

Vec2Text demonstrated exact recovery of 92% of 32-token inputs, including names from clinical notes.

Full request bodies when debug logging is on

Agent tools and MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers

Each external tool is a new data processor.

Models can repeat parts of what they were trained on.

Both laws require contracts, appropriate safeguarding, and minimal data. However, they phrase it differently.

Protected Health Information (PHI) held by covered entities and their vendors

Any personal data; health data is a "special category" under Article 9

Business Associate Agreement (BAA), per 45 CFR 164.502(e) and 164.504(e)

Data Processing Agreement (DPA), per Article 28

Technical safeguards in 164.312: access control, audit controls, integrity, authentication, transmission security

Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) under Article 35

Transfers outside the EU need a legal basis (Chapter V)

No, you don’t need to rely on self-hosting. There are several hosted options that offer protections for sensitive data, but they also come with their own set of limitations.
