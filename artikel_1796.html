England Have Improved Since Berlin – Now Comes the Hard Part

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

When Thomas Tuchel looks across the touchline in Madrid on Sunday, he will not simply see the reigning world champions. He will see the team that has become England’s reference point over the past few years.

Spain have beaten England in the Euro 2024 final. They have extended an extraordinary unbeaten run. They remain the side that, more often than not, finds the right solution when the margins are finest and the pressure is greatest. If England are serious about becoming tournament winners under Tuchel, they must eventually prove they can do more than compete with Spain. They must show they can beat them. That is the challenge awaiting them in the Nations League decider.

The encouraging news for England is that there are genuine signs of progress. The uncomfortable reality is that progress and victory are not always the same thing.

One of the striking features of England’s recent encounters with Spain is that they no longer feel overawed by them.

The Euro 2024 final exposed deficiencies in England’s ability to manage elite opposition over the course of ninety minutes. Spain controlled key phases of the contest and ultimately deserved their victory. When the teams met again at Wembley earlier in this Nations League campaign, however, the contest felt markedly different.

England lost 3-2, but they also produced periods of football that genuinely unsettled Spain. There were phases when Tuchel’s side pressed aggressively, attacked with conviction and looked capable of taking the game away from their opponents. According to Tuchel, Harry Kane’s missed penalty at 2-1 represented a pivotal moment because England had established significant momentum and were in a position to extend their advantage. Had that opportunity been converted, the complexion of the match might have changed entirely.

That does not mean England were unfortunate to lose. Spain still created better chances overall and once again demonstrated their ability to remain calm when the game became chaotic. It does suggest, however, that England are moving closer to the level required. The difference between the two sides no longer appears structural or insurmountable. Instead, it increasingly comes down to small moments, concentration and execution.

Perhaps the biggest change under Tuchel has been psychological.

There is a greater willingness to take risks. England’s attacking players appear to have more freedom, and individuals such as Jude Bellingham, Anthony Gordon and Morgan Rogers have benefited from a more proactive approach. England are creating more situations in which their athleticism, pace and attacking quality can influence elite opposition.

The concern is that the same approach has often left them vulnerable.

Tuchel’s side continue to make costly defensive mistakes. Against Spain at Wembley, several of the goals they conceded stemmed from their own errors. Croatia’s recent equaliser also arrived following a defensive lapse. Even before that, England’s exit against Argentina at the World Cup highlighted a recurring issue when facing the very best teams. They frequently produce enough quality to stay in the contest, but too often undermine themselves through moments of poor decision-making.

That explains why Tuchel continues to search for solutions at the back. Injuries have played a role, but the constant changes to England’s defensive partnerships have prevented the sort of continuity that Spain enjoy. While Harry Kane and Jude Bellingham remain fixed points around which the team is built, the defence still feels like an area of ongoing experimentation.

Against lesser opponents, that may be manageable. Against Spain, every weakness tends to be exposed.

What makes Spain such a difficult obstacle is that they possess qualities England are still trying to develop.

Almost every major international side can produce moments of brilliance. Spain combine those moments with control. They understand when to accelerate games and when to slow them down. They cope with adversity without losing their structure. Even significant injuries, including the absence of Rodri for this match, rarely seem to alter their collective identity.

England’s growing confidence means they can now trade punches with Spain. The challenge is sustaining that level over an entire match.

The European champions demonstrated this in Berlin and again at Wembley. There were periods when England looked stronger, more dynamic and more threatening. Yet Spain retained an ability to navigate difficult passages and eventually reassert themselves. That quality is often the difference between excellent international teams and serial winners.

England have made considerable progress under Tuchel, but they have not yet acquired that same aura of inevitability.

The stakes extend beyond finishing top of the group.

For England, this fixture offers an opportunity to measure themselves against the benchmark of international football. Tuchel has spoken repeatedly about wanting to push the limits of the team and test whether they are genuinely evolving. Spain provide the perfect examination because they have consistently answered questions that England have not.

A victory would not erase the pain of previous defeats, nor would it suddenly make England favourites for every tournament they enter. It would, however, represent tangible evidence that this team is beginning to overcome a hurdle that has repeatedly blocked its path.

For several years, England have been close. They reached a European Championship final. They reached a World Cup semi-final. They remain one of the most talented squads in international football. What they have lacked is a signature result against a side of Spain’s calibre when the pressure is highest.

Sunday offers another chance to secure one.

Whether England take it will tell us a great deal about how much progress has really been made under Thomas Tuchel. Spain remain the team to beat. The question England must answer now is whether they have finally become good enough to do exactly that.
