Can Real stem REMAX’s agent attrition? Executives outline their plans

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Real REMAX Group reported pro forma Q3 2026 revenue of $770 million, up 20% year over year, and a net loss of $67.2 million. Management highlighted $40 million in annualized cost savings and more than 150 eliminated roles as integration continues.  AI Summary

The Real Brokerage’s acquisition of REMAX may have closed less than a week prior to the end of the third quarter of 2026, but the merger still took center stage during the combined Real REMAX Group’s Q3 2026 earnings call with investors and analysts Thursday morning.

“It’s been an exciting two months, and I’m encouraged that we’re already seeing what this combined organization is capable of,” Tamir Poleg, the CEO of Real REMAX Group, said on Thursday’s calls. “I think that the biggest challenge is bringing together two very different organizations and making sure we move with the speed and urgency that this opportunity requires.”

Poleg, a HousingWire Vanguard award winner, noted that while Real and REMAX have complementary strengths, they also have different operating models, cultures and ways of doing things.

“Our job isn’t simply to put the two companies together,” he said. “It’s to take the best of both, simplify how we operate and build a much stronger value proposition for agents and franchise owners.”

Since closing the acquisition a little over two months ago, Real REMAX Group, a RealTrend Verified brokerage, executives said they had found more than $40 million in annualized gross cost savings and that they expect to realize more than $30 million in adjusted EBITDA synergies in full-year 2027, increasing to more than $40 million in full-year 2028. This came as the company reduced its combined headcount by more than 14%, eliminating over 150 positions across the combined organization. Company executives said the initial phase of headcount restructuring is largely complete, but they are still looking for ways to simplify operations and improve efficiencies. One way they are looking to do this is by consolidating technology systems, vendor relationships and other duplicative costs.

In addition to this, executives also noted that they have been working to wind down “underperforming businesses” like Motto Mortgage and wemlo, as previously reported by HousingWire, instead focusing resources on “areas with greater long-term potential.” At the end of August, Motto’s franchise network had 151 offices, many of which Real executives said were unprofitable.

“In just two months, approximately 69% of the network or 104 offices have already closed and 92% of the network is now moving towards termination. We understand these are difficult decisions, but given the legacy REMAX mortgage businesses generated a combined $5 million adjusted EBITDA loss in 2025, these actions are necessary to build a more focused, efficient and profitable organization,” Jenna Rozenblat, the company’s president and Chief Integration Officer, said during the call.

Beyond winding down these businesses, executives said they are also focusing on returning REMAX to sustainable growth, especially as the franchise brand’s U.S. and Canada agent count continues to decline, dropping 3% annually to 72,118 agents. REMAX’s global agent count increased 0.4% year-over-year to 148,088 agents in total. In contrast, during the same quarter, Real’s agent count rose 21% annually to 36,610 agents. However, the company noted that as of early November, Real’s agent count was down to roughly 36,000 as more than 500 agents proactively offboarded by the company due to unpaid dues or expired real estate licenses.

“While the North American agent count trends remain challenging, I’ve spent considerable time with REMAX broker-owners over the past two months, and one thing has become abundantly clear: the strength of this brand and the passion of the people behind it are extraordinary,” Rozenblat, a HousingWire Woman of Influence and Vanguard award winner, said. “We don’t expect an immediate reversal in agent attrition in a franchise business, but our priority is to improve franchise owner economics and help them deliver a more competitive value proposition to agents and the clients they serve.”

According to Poleg, a central part of these efforts is the recent appointment of former ERA president Alex Vidal as president of REMAX.

“Within just a few weeks, Alex managed to generate a lot of excitement and energy around the REMAX network,” Poleg said. “He’s traveling the country. He’s talking to a lot of broker-owners, a lot of agents, and I think that there’s just a renewed energy around the brand and within the network, and that’s great because energy is contagious, and if you want to grow, you need that type of energy.”

The company did not address the breach of contract lawsuit filed by Compass International Holdings, ERA’s parent company, against Vidal claiming that he violated his non-compete agreement.

In addition to Vidal’s appointment, executives also noted that they are actively working to provide REMAX agents, franchisees and broker-owners with access to Real’s proprietary technology, including the reZEN technology platform and the recently launched Leo Pro.

When it comes to overall profitability, the Real REMAX Group appears to have a long way to go, based on its Q3 2026 earnings report. During the quarter, on a pro forma basis, assuming Real’s acquisition of REMAX closed on January 1, 2025, revenue was up 20% annually to $770.0 million. However, net loss rose to $67.223 million, up from a net loss of $280,000 a year ago.

This came as One Real Title’s revenue rose to $1.616 million, up over $300,000 from a year prior. Poleg said the title attach rate has remained steady at roughly 3%, but that within it title joint ventures it can be as high as 40% or even 70%.

Things were not as strong at One Real Mortgage in Q3, as revenue dropped to $1.508 million compared to $1.757 million a year ago. Executives attributed this to the rising mortgage rate environment during the quarter. According to Poleg, the attach rate for One Real Mortgage still remains below 1%, but he sees great potential for growth on the horizon. Some of this comes from an uptick in the number of agents expressing interest in becoming licensed as loan officers. In addition to this, Poleg noted that over the past few weeks, the company has begun experimenting with sending leads from REMAX.com to One Real Mortgage.

“We’re actually seeing encouraging data, but it is still a little too early to actually draw any conclusion from,” he said. “It’s kind of an ongoing effort.”

Executives added that they see opportunities to create value that do not require a housing recovery, including organic market share gains for Real and cost savings and operational improvements at REMAX, as well as margin expansion across the firm’s ancillary services offerings, including mortgage, title and Real Wallet.

Poleg also addresses Real’s decision to become a founding member of the national residential real estate data-sharing initiative, Open Real Estate.

“We joined Open Real Estate because we believe the industry would benefit from a more open, transparent and competitive approach to how real estate data is distributed. As one of the largest real estate platforms in the world, we think that it’s important that Real Remax Group has a seat at the table in shaping how that ecosystem evolves,” Poleg said.

He said it was too early to speculate about what products the initiative may offer or any potential revenue opportunities, but he added that strategically, Real REMAX Group sees “the potential to create a more efficient marketplace that ultimately benefits agents, brokers, broker-owners and consumers.”

“That’s why we’re excited to be involved,” Poleg said.
