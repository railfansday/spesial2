Meeting AI in 2026: Notetakers Are Out, Live Help Is In

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Meeting notes used to be the product. In 2026 they're a checkbox.

I'm the developer of Roofle, a free, open-source meeting copilot for Mac, so I have a stake here. But I spent time reading what the big platforms actually ship, and the shift is easy to see.

1. Bots. A visible bot joins your call, records it, and emails a summary. It documents what happened.

2. Bot-free capture. The tool listens through your computer's audio instead. Same output, less awkward.

3. Live help. The AI works while the conversation is happening: answering questions, suggesting what to ask, tracking what's still open.

Generation 3 is where everyone is moving.

Most live help is tied to one platform. If the other side picked a different one, or you're a guest, it may not be there.

The outside-guest rule matters. Claude Meetings and Teams Copilot are both cautious about external participants, for good legal reasons. But clients, candidates and customers are external by definition, so the calls that matter most are the ones these tools sit out by default.

Notes are the floor. Every platform above includes some form of them. The new question is what the tool does while you're still talking.

Roofle captures your mic and your Mac's speaker output, transcribes with Whisper on your device, and suggests follow-up questions during the call. No bot joins. You pick your role at setup, so a recruiter and a salesperson get different questions.

It has real limits. It only runs on a Mac today, it's early, and capturing other people's audio means you have to tell them. Local models also depend on your hardware.

Code: https://github.com/roofle-ai/roofle

What would you want a meeting tool to do during a call that none of these do? And would you trust one that only works on its own platform?
