CZ says Binance used its balance sheet without borrowing, selling assets, or raising funds.

Binance has reportedly covered 95% of the losses linked to its $3.3 billion hack, with withdrawals among the top 10 assets restored. The remaining 5% involves less liquid tokens, making the final sourcing and conversion process slower.

CZ said Binance has already allocated 100% of the required funds. In other words, the outstanding issue isn’t an alleged funding shortfall. It’s converting the remaining allocation into smaller, less liquid assets.

That distinction matters. Getting the money allocated and completing the asset conversion aren’t necessarily the same thing.

Another detail deserves attention: Binance says it didn’t borrow money, sell assets, or raise fresh capital to cover the losses.

Instead, the exchange used its own balance sheet. That’s a significant part of the response, particularly when comparisons with other crypto industry incidents start flying around.

Still, covering losses doesn’t automatically erase the security concerns that follow a major hack. Investors may appreciate the financial commitment, but trust takes longer to rebuild.

Binance has allocated 100% of the funds, it's just 5% remaining to convert to some of the small coins taking time. https://t.co/9Edm6M23vw

The final 5% is reportedly tied to tokens with thinner liquidity. Sourcing those assets takes time, even with the full funding allocation already in place.

For Binance, the immediate task is straightforward: finish converting the remaining assets and complete the recovery process. The Binance hack recovery effort has reached the reported 95% mark, but the final stretch still matters. Until that last portion is settled, the exchange has unfinished business.
