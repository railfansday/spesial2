Sandro Tonali alarm bells ringing at Tottenham, De Zerbi should be seriously concerned

Sandro Tonali arrived at Tottenham Hotspur shouldering the heavy weight of expectation due to his £100million price tag.

In his two full seasons with Newcastle United, the midfielder notched 18 goal contributions in 98 appearances, dominating the middle of the park.

He worked in tandem with Bruno Guimaraes and Joelinton, but he doesn't appear to have found himself in a golden trio at Tottenham just yet.

Tonali may have started all 10 of Spurs' Premier League matches so far, but it could be argued that's because Roberto De Zerbi has nobody else.

The Lilywhites spent a lot of money to drag him away from St James' Park, so dropping him from the lineup simply isn't a choice.

But with more and more minutes on the pitch, it's becoming increasingly obvious that Tonali just isn't the same player he was last season.

The 26-year-old continues to be a consistent name for the Italian national team, facing both France and Belgium over the international break.

Italy claimed a surprise 3-1 win over France, in which he played 90 minutes, before an incredibly difficult clash against Belgium on Sunday, 15 November.

The Red Devils claimed a 4-1 win, with goals from Maxim De Cuyper, Nathan Ngoy, Jeremy Doku and Diego Moreira.

Tonali, in a 47-touch display, may have created two chances and won 67 per cent of his ground duels, it was far from his best display [FotMob].

He picked up a yellow card up against Doku, struggling to contain him for most of the game, until being substituted in the 62nd minute.

Unsurprisingly, his stats did a lot of the talking about Belgium, and the Italian media were equally as critical of his performance.

La Repubblica said that Tonali was "forced to chase the ball backwards," which clearly isn't one of his strengths, but there's lots of that at Spurs.

De Zerbi will be hoping desperately that he finds his feet in North London soon, but the early signs are not as positive as many would have hoped.

Spurs fans have every right to be concerned, given the price the club paid, but there's plenty of time for the player to turn things around.
