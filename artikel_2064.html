Where Will Lankshear ranks in Middlesbrough's top 7 best strikers in 21st century list

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Middlesbrough fans will be hoping that the 2026/27 season is one which ends in promotion under Kim Hellberg, having spent the best part of a decade outside the Premier League.

To football supporters of a certain era, it would also feel right to see the Teesside outfit competing amongst English football's elite, having spent the majority of the 1990s and 2000s in the top-flight, whilst boasting some top talents during that time.

Such pedigree is what makes potentially signing for Boro such an attractive proposition right now, with Steve Gibson remaining as ambitious as ever, which was demonstrated this summer by the high-profile signing of Will Lankshear.

In his first months at the Riverside Stadium, the highly-rated centre-forward has certainly been the talk of the town, but where does he rank in comparison to the club's best strikers this century? FLW have debated such questions...

Kickstarting this list of Middlesbrough goalscoring supremos is Chuba Akpom, who spent three years at the Riverside.

The former Arsenal academy graduate's first two seasons on Teesside were certainly not ones to write home about, but his exploits in the 2022/23 season will not be forgotten anytime soon.

After netting a brace against Sheffield United under Chris Wilder in August 2022, Akpom ended the campaign as the Championship's top scorer and player of the season after netting 28 goals in 38 appearances, including a run of 14 goals in as many games between February and April 2023.

Such form then paved the way for his £12.2m switch to Ajax, where he also enjoyed relative goalscoring success.

In sixth is Alvaro Negredo, who still left a lasting impression on Middlesbrough fans regardless of their side's relegation during his only season at the club.

The Spanish forward won the Premier League with Manchester City just three seasons before he was loaned in from Valencia, and his top-level experience was made clear for all to see at the Riverside.

Including a debut strike against Stoke City, Negredo scored nine times and provided four assists in just 36 Premier League appearances, before winding down his professional career in Turkey, Saudi Arabia and his native Spain.

Lankshear is up next, with the England youth international already earning rave reviews on Teesside.

There were question marks over Tottenham Hotspur's decision to accept an initial £10m deal, which could rise to £20m depending on add-ons, and the 21-year-old is only adding to those on a weekly basis.

The forward, unsurprisingly, leads the way as the Championship's top scorer this season, which is largely down to a remarkable run of 13 goals in as many games prior to a minor injury, which derailed his chances of becoming the first second-tier player in 14 years to have received a call-up to the senior Three Lions squad.

Spurs sold Will Lankshear to Boro in the summer, but, they have clauses written into the contract to protect their position in the future.

Alen Boksic was 30 years old by the time he arrived in the North East from Lazio for £2.5m, but it certainly proved to be a bargain deal.

The 40-time Croatian international had already scored 34 goals in Serie A for the Rome outfit and Juventus, and in his first campaign at the Riverside, netted 12 times and was named Middlesbrough's Player of the Season.

It begs the question as to how many goals he could have gone on to score in the Premier League for the Reds, as, in February 2003, Boksic made the decision to retire after injury issues, with his top-flight record reading 22 goals in just 68 appearances.

In third place is Yakubu, with the Nigerian proving to be an inspired £7.5m buy from Portsmouth in 2005.

The 2005/06 campaign is one remembered with great fondness by Middlesbrough supporters, as Steve McClaren's side reached the UEFA Cup final, despite eventually losing to Sevilla.

The Nigerian striker scored 19 times in all competitions throughout said campaign, including a crucial winner against Roma in March 2006, before hitting double-figures the following season despite the club's second successive mid-table finish.

Ultimately, 35 goals and 11 assists in 104 appearances allowed Boro to make a profit, as Yakubu signed for Everton in an £11.25m deal.

Jimmy Floyd-Hasselbaink's quality at the top level was also clear to see by the time he arrived at Middlesbrough in the summer of 2004, with the club competing in the UEFA Cup that particular campaign also, having defeated Bolton Wanderers in the EFL Cup final the season previous.

The Dutchman had previously scored over 150 times for the likes of Atlético Madrid, Leeds United and Chelsea, and in his first season at the Riverside, hit 16 goals and provided nine assists across 57 games in all competitions, including the vital strike against Manchester City which, again, guaranteed European qualification.

Hasselbaink still had a strong influence in his second and final season for the Reds too, scoring 17 times in all competitions, including four in the UEFA Cup, with the final defeat to Sevilla being the last of his 89 appearances.

Mark Viduka had already certified himself as a lethal finisher with the likes of Dinamo Zagreb, Celtic and Leeds United, with Middlesbrough fans also getting three seasons' worth of joy out of the Australian international after his Elland Road exit.

After a disappointing first campaign at the Riverside, Viduka then hit double figures in both of his remaining two seasons, with seven of his 16 strikes across 2005/06 helping McClaren's side to Eindhoven - three of which came in the dramatic quarter-final and semi-final comebacks over FC Basel and Steaua Bucharest.

In 2006/07, Viduka netted 14 Premier League goals, including a run of five goals in the final four games of the campaign, as well as five goals in seven FA Cup appearances, before he eventually moved to North East rivals, Newcastle United, upon the expiration of his contract.
