Marco Palestra can transform Chelsea's right flank, Italy stats show his potential

Marco Palestra has not had the immediate impact at Chelsea that many would have hoped.

Xabi Alonso has been slowly introducing him into his plans, with just two starts in the Premier League this season and five overall appearances.

Interestingly, those two starts have arrived in the last two league matches, with the Italian hoping to make his case to be the long-term option.

His latest display away from Chelsea with Italy has offered a reminder of just what he can bring.

Despite limited game time against Belgium in a 4-1 defeat, his attacking data was quite promising.

And if that can be translated back at Stamford Bridge, it can transform the London side.

For a Chelsea side still searching for a consistent long-term option at right-back, Palestra has shown signs he can be that person.

The summer signing may still need time to adapt to the demands of English football, but his willingness to get forward already gives Chelsea what they have been lacking.

Despite Italy's 4-1 defeat, and Palestra not exactly having the best of performances, the 21-year-old did show those promising glimpses.

The Blues' defender was only given 28 minutes off the bench, yet, as a defender, he had the joint-highest touches in the opposition box with four for Italy, according to FotMob.

Only striker and starter Francesco Pio Esposito had matched the Italian substitute.

Additionally, Palestra also created the highest expected assists for Italy, with 0.33.

His performance, despite the stats, did not exactly impress his national media, though, with Sky Sports Italy sharing that he failed to make the desired impact, labelling him "inconsistent."

While that may be fair over his entire 28-minute appearance, those attacking stats do make for good reading.

And one Alonso will certainly be looking into, with hope he can push the young defender in the right direction.

Palestra will not get any better for Chelsea unless he is given backing to go on a run of starts.

This season, he has only played 180 minutes, which is far from enough to be played into form.

His two starts before the international break may be signs that Alonso is finally willing to place trust in him.

And as his 28 minutes against Belgium showed, he can get into dangerous attacking areas and create for his side.
