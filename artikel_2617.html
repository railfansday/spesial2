Yaxel Lendeborg, Warriors fail to overcome abysmal start in loss to Blazers

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The Golden State Warriors two-game winning streak came to an unequivocal end on Friday night in a 116-94 loss to the Portland Trail Blazers. After facing a shorthanded and schedule tired Celtics and the Kings in the team's last two contest, the depleted Dubs were steamrolled by a Blazers team that has been playing like one of the best team's in the league early. Despite eventually bouncing back, it was too little too late.

When rookie Yaxel Lendeborg fails to be the dominant offensive force he's looked like at times early in the season, the Warriors offense is essentially entirely reliant on three-point shot. When Golden State is struggling from deep, the team's limited on-ball creation, athleticism, and size makes them look out of place in the NBA. Since no shot sets up an opposing offense better than a missed three, the failing offense makes their job even harder defensively. That's how things started in Portland.

Lendeborg was unusually hesitant to attack offensively while outside shots weren't falling. The Blazers pounced, building a lead beyond 20 points in the first quarter. Before the Warriors outside shooting came alive in the second quarter, the Blazers had amassed an absurd 47-11 lead.

The Warriors offense is at least passable when they can find a rhythm from deep. De'Anthony Melton's scorching hot shooting streak continued, bringing the Dubs to life with three consecutive made threes. While it was nothing to celebrate, the Warriors stopped the bleeding and were able to cut the deficit down to 23 by halftime.

In the third quarter, Lendeborg came to life. After an incredibly tentative first half, he began initiating more offense and forcing the ball downhill. He drew a few fouls and occasionally finished through contact. He scored 12 points in the quarter, including a forceful and-one dunk through firm contact from Jrue Holiday. Not long after, Lendeborg made his first three of the night to cut the deficit to 13.

Once they weren't forced to consistently play in transition, the Warriors defense finally began giving the Blazers problems. Portland was missing their two primary point guards (Damian Lillard and Ja Morant), which forced the bulk of offensive creation into the hands of Deni Avdjia and Scoot Henderson. While both players had plenty of success, they are inconsistent shooters. The Warriors comeback efforts largely relied on those struggles.

Avdjia finished with a game-high 26 points, 7 rebounds, 7 assists, and 2 steals, but was 0-for-6 from three and committed 5 turnovers. Henderson scored 18 points, but was 6-for-15 from the field. In fact, the Blazers were just 12-for-49 (24.5%) from three as a team.

Early in the fourth quarter, Brandin Podziemski missed a corner three that would have cut the deficit to seven. The shot went in-and-out and the Blazers answered with a 6-0 run. The Warriors would pull within 10 a few times in the final quarter, but were never able to cut the deficit to single digits. You pay a price when you fall behind 36 points in the first half.

Lendeborg led the Warriors with 18 points, 7 rebounds, 2 assists, 2 blocks, 1 steal, and 1 turnover on 5-for-14 shooting (1-for-4 from three). Melton had 17 points and was 5-for-10 from three, but committed 5 turnvoers and struggled inside the arc. Podziemski and Charles Bassey were the only other Warriors in double-figures, scoring 16 and 12 points respectively.

With the loss, the Warriors were officially eliminated from the NBA Cup, which will at least give them a lighter schedule in the coming weeks. Although the team would surely prefer the pay checks that would have come with advancing. For now, Golden State will return home and continue the usual regular-season schedule on Sunday. They will host the Los Angeles Clippers.
