GTA VI ties Metacritic’s all-time record with a 99 as it launches

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

GTA VI’s 147 PS5 critic reviews on Metacritic are all positive, and Take-Two’s shares jumped. Rockstar also explained why the game runs at 30fps on every console.

Cover art for Grand Theft Auto VI: The Album, featuring the game’s leads, Jason and Lucia

Grand Theft Auto VI has a score of 99 out of 100 on Metacritic, as the game begins its worldwide launch. All 147 critic reviews for the PS5 version listed on the site on Wednesday were positive. That puts Rockstar Games’ open-world crime game level with The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. The 1998 game has long topped the site.

The game also scored 98 on OpenCritic, making it the top-scoring game on both sites, Bloomberg’s Jason Schreier reported. IGN and GameSpot gave it a perfect 10. Shares in Rockstar’s parent, Take-Two Interactive, rose as much as 8.4% in New York.

“Grand Theft Auto VI is, in short, a masterpiece,” Schreier wrote in Bloomberg’s review.

GTA VI comes out on 19 November for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, at $80. It follows GTA V, which has sold more than 230 million copies since 2013. Take-Two said last week that pre-orders beat expectations.

The game follows Jason and Lucia, a couple of criminals on the run in Vice City, Rockstar’s version of Miami. Lucia is the series’ first female lead. Players can nurture the relationship between them, or let it cool.

“It’s a risk. It’s a real change for Grand Theft Auto. You can still play it the old way, you can reject all of this stuff,” Rob Nelson, co-studio head of Rockstar North, told The Verge.

The game took more than eight years and a studio of over 4,700 people, Bloomberg reported. Rockstar will not confirm independent estimates that it cost more than $1bn, according to The Guardian. Nelson told the paper the studio could have made a quicker sequel. Take-Two’s chief executive said in May the game was made without generative AI.

“You have to take big swings. It can’t be just more of the same,” Nelson said.

Four writers at IGN and its sister sites finished the story in 42 to 115 hours. The fastest rushed through; the slowest took every detour.

The game targets 30 frames per second on every console, with no performance mode. Rockstar’s technology leads explained the choice in a statement to Wccftech.

“It became clear early on that given the scale of the team’s ambition, we’d be targeting 30fps on this generation of hardware,” Rockstar said.

The PS5 and Xbox Series X upscale the game from 1440p to 4K, Rockstar said. The PS5 Pro uses Sony’s PSSR2 for the same jump. The Xbox Series S renders at 720p and uses AMD’s FSR 4 to reach 1080p.

PS5 owners also need a new system update, version 14.23, before they can play. Sony released it on 17 November and called it mandatory, PCMag reported. Boxed copies hold a download code rather than a disc. Sony plans to stop making discs in 2028.

The downloads, of up to 212GB, are already testing networks. Australia’s NBN hit a record 33.19Tbps on 12 November, when boxed copies went on sale there, TechRadar reported. NBN Co said a Fortnite patch released that day also added to the load.

The lowest score on Metacritic’s PS5 page on Wednesday was an 80, from ScreenHub.

“There are large sections where it is an absolutely epic game, and large sections that are boring and too limited,” ScreenHub’s review said.

The Guardian’s Keza MacDonald praised the game but found its controls complex.

“This can be a frustrating game at times: unpredictable, laborious, unwieldy and, occasionally, unfair,” MacDonald wrote.

Bloomberg’s Schreier wrote that the story does not reach the emotional highs of Red Dead Redemption 2.

The launch also lands as Rockstar staff vote on whether to unionise, The Verge reported. Rockstar is also defending a tribunal claim from 34 former employees. They say Rockstar fired them over union organising, The Guardian reported.
