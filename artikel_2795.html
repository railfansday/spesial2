WIAA releases update on I-638's implementation ahead of Dec. 3 start date

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Washington's voter-approved statewide ballot measure that bans transgender girls from girls school sports, which passed with 56% of the vote earlier this month, will go into effect in two weeks.

For the Washington Interscholastic Activities Association (WIAA), the governing body of school sports in the state, that means adjusting rules around processes around athletic eligibility for girls school sports.

In the first update on the process to align WIAA policy with Initiative 638, the organization said that students would need to obtain a "biological sex attestation addendum" form to be considered eligible for school sports, even if they already have a valid physical exam form. A new sports physical will not be required.

WIAA released updated health and wellness forms, which WIAA director of communications Sean Bessette said in an email are form examples and "not mandated forms." Schools can choose to use their own separate forms, Bessette said. Older versions of the state's example forms available on the WIAA website as recently as Oct. 30, including those for physicals and confirming medical eligibility, did not address biological sex; the updated WIAA example forms now include a biological sex selection.

The "Pre-Participation Biological Sex Attestation" form model, which now appears on the WIAA website, is intended to be filled out by a licensed health care provider and attest that any student looking to play female sports has a "biological sex" of "female."

WIAA's handbook will be updated by Dec. 3, according to the statement, the day I-638 is set to go into effect. That change will rollback longstanding state policy - first enacted nearly twenty years ago as the first policy of its kind in the nation - allowing transgender students to play on sports teams that align with their identity. Washington voters passed the initiative earlier this month 56% to 44%, and a coalition of nonprofit organization and student-athlete parents filed a lawsuit on Nov. 9 against the state to stop the measure from being implemented.

WIAA wrote in the update that "determination of biological sex falls outside the WIAA's purview and is a matter best addressed by medical professionals." Schools will remain responsible, according to the statement, for determining eligibility.

The Washington State Medical Association, the state's largest physician professional association, released a statement on Nov. 17 representing seven professional medical associations in the state and supporting the pause of I-638 while "significant medical, ethical, and practical concerns" with how the measure is implemented are addressed.

The sports immediately impacted by this change include: Girls Basketball, Girls Wrestling, Girls Flag Football, Girls Bowling, Girls Gymnastics, and Competitive Cheer. Dance/Drill is a coed sport and will not be impacted, and Sideline Cheer is not a competition sport and will also not be affected.

"The WIAA must follow state law. That commitment remains unchanged," the organization wrote in a statement. "To clear up any confusion or misunderstanding, the WIAA does not hold a political position on any measure and will not challenge Initiative 638's implementation. At no point has the Association indicated otherwise."

The WIAA wrote on Nov. 18 that a number of girls' sports would be immediately impacted by the change, including Girls Basketball, Girls Wrestling, Girls Flag Football, Girls Bowling, Girls Gymnastics, and Competitive Cheer.

There are also other sports that would be affected, including:

Spring: Girls Golf, Girls Softball (Fastpitch), Girls Tennis, and Girls Track & Field

Fall: Girls Cross Country, Girls Golf, Girls Soccer, Girls Softball (Slowpitch), Girls Swim & Dive, Girls Tennis, and Girls Volleyball

I-638 adds language to determine who can participate in girls sports. All students looking to participate in girls sports will now be required to provide "a health examination and consent form or other stated signed by the student's personal health care provider that verifies the student's biological sex."

The initiative states that this examination would be "part of the required routine sports physical examination" for student athletes in Washington. The health examination and consent form would require verification of the student's biological sex relying on one or more of the following, according to the measure's language: "the student's reproductive anatomy, genetic makeup, or normal endogenously produced testosterone levels."

Sports physicals are valid for 24 months, according to the 2026-2027 WIAA Handbook, and can be performed by a medical doctor, doctor of osteopathy, advanced registered nurse practitioner, physician's assistant, or a naturopathic physician.
