Rob Jones got several decisions wrong as Tottenham Hotspur beat Ipswich Town 2-0 on Saturday.

Luca Williams-Barnett and Dominic Solanke got the goals in a game that was ultimately free of major controversy.

Yet the referee did not always make the right call, and will have moments to look back on when he reviews his performance.

Tottenham will not care, however, with Roberto De Zerbi's back to winning ways and back in the top half of the Premier League.

The Lilywhites have now won four of their last five league games, and look like a side that have finally undergone a much-needed transition.

Here, Football Insider has picked out three of Jones' key blunders on Saturday.

Midway through the first half, Ipswich broke away, with the ball eventually making its way to Leif Davis on the left.

The 26-year-old felt the presence of Pedro Porro behind him and attempted to win a free-kick, throwing himself to the ground upon the touch of the defender's hand.

Jones did not buy it, and instead awarded a free-kick the other way when Davis handled the ball, but he should have reached for his pocket.

By definition, the defender had dived in the hopes of winning a free-kick, and should have been rightly punished for it.

Ipswich looked to be gaining momentum in the game as the first half drew on, with Tottenham pinned back.

The Lilywhites had just cleared away from an Ipswich set piece, when it was picked up again and about to be fired back into the penalty area.

But Jones noticed Gallagher down and blew his whistle to stop play, much to the frustration of the visitors.

The Tottenham midfielder had not been down for a head injury and could have been ignored until Ipswich's attack was completed, with their momentum stopped by the whistle.

Ipswich did not produce many good chances but one came when Zian Flemming's header was comfortably saved by Antonin Kinsky.

Tottenham may have felt extremely hard done by if they had conceded, with the chance having ultimately come from Julio Enciso's dive.

The Paraguay international had sold the dummy to Mateus Fernandes, who drew his foot back as his opponent threw himself to the ground.

Jones bought the dive and awarded a free-kick, and was ultimately lucky that nothing came of it.
