West Ham watched £3.3m Cardiff City star – fresh January move makes sense

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

West Ham United and Cardiff City may be new to the division, but both have ambitions of securing promotion and returning to the top flight. Could Yousef Salech be a key man for Cardiff or join the Hammers instead?

Cardiff City have made an encouraging start under the excellent Brian Barry-Murphy, while West Ham United's stature, financial muscle, and expectations mean anything other than a serious promotion challenge would represent disappointment.

However, Scott Parker still has plenty of work to do in shaping his squad and implementing his ideas. The January transfer window could present an opportunity to address one particular weakness, with Yousef Salech an increasingly attractive option after previously attracting West Ham's attention.

West Ham have scored plenty of goals this season, so finding the back of the net has not necessarily been their biggest problem. However, neither Nuno Espirito Santo nor Scott Parker has managed to identify a reliable focal point to spearhead the attack.

Joel Piroe, Taty Castellanos, and Pablo have all flattered to deceive in that role. Each possesses some useful attributes, but none has consistently demonstrated the ability to lead the line and provide the presence required to bring others into play.

The 25 top scorers in the Championship since the second tier was rebranded back in 2004/05, including Billy Sharp and Aleksandar Mitrovic.

That is where Cardiff could come into the picture. West Ham have previously been credited with interest in Cardiff striker Salech, and they should seriously consider firming that up in January.

His development has been particularly encouraging, having kicked on well this season. The £3.3 million-rated Cardiff forward could give Parker the focal point his new-look side is missing. The 24-year-old would almost certainly cost much more than that currently, though.

Salech offers considerably more than just a physical presence through the middle, with his ability to occupy defenders complemented by an increasingly impressive goalscoring record.

He has nine goals in all competitions this season, including six in the Championship after his strike against Norwich City over the weekend, but the Dane's underlying numbers are arguably even more encouraging from the perspective of any potential suitors.

Yousef Salech's Cardiff City seasons (all comps)

Despite playing for a newly-promoted side, he boasts the second-highest xG in the division at 8.6, according to FotMob, behind only Middlesbrough's Will Lankshear. Cardiff teammate Jack Moylan is third with 7.7, highlighting the excellent positions Barry-Murphy is getting his attacking players into.

Given he has six goals from that, Salech has been profligate with some of those opportunities, but his consistent ability to find time and space inside the box is testament to both his movement and the coaching he is receiving with Cardiff.

There is an argument that West Ham have more pressing concerns than another striker, given how unbalanced their summer recruitment has left the squad. Naturally, after relegation, there was a decent overhaul of the squad.

They have plenty of quality, but only one attack-minded right-back in Kyle Walker-Peters, no reliable left-back thus far, and no defensive midfielder has established themselves as the ideal partner for Arne Engels at the base of midfield for a team chasing automatic promotion from the Championship.

Playing central defenders at full-back is hardly conducive to a side that should be dominating opponents with two overlapping players providing width. Their goalkeepers have also underwhelmed, despite Mads Hermansen previously demonstrating his quality at this level with Leicester City.

Addressing those weaknesses arguably takes priority, but that should not prevent Parker from pursuing Salech if the opportunity arises. A genuine focal point could make the difference in turning West Ham's attacking quality into a promotion-winning side. Cardiff's asking price may be beyond them, though.
