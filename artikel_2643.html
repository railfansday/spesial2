Aaron Rodgers blasted by former teammate for ‘victim mentality’ after Mike McCarthy takedown

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

A former teammate of Aaron Rodgers took aim at the quarterback over his scorched-earth tirade toward Steelers coach Mike McCarthy on Wednesday, which preceded his release from the organization.

Rodgers went on "The Pat McAfee Show" and tore into McCarthy, alleging that the coach slept through meetings and called the team the "Packers" instead of the "Steelers" as he was breaking them down at halftime during the loss to the Eagles, among other accusations.

But T.J. Lang, who played with Rodgers on the Packers from 2009 to 2016 and is now an analyst on the Lions Radio broadcast, criticized his former quarterback on 97.1's "The Valenti Show with Rico."

Aaron Rodgers looks on during the third quarter of the Steelers' loss to the Bengals on Nov. 15, 2026, in Cincinnati. Getty Images

"I think it's despicable," Lang said. "I think that Aaron Rodgers just became everything that he has bitched about and moaned about over the last 10 years with former coaches, former teammates, airing grievances out, giving opinions out in the media.

"He has blacklisted, I don't know how many, former teammates from just simple comments telling the truth because he just— he was sick of it. Honestly, I don't even know if I want to watch the whole thing. It's just unnecessary. It's a victim mentality. It's a poor me, woe is me. Oh, I thought this, and it wasn't that.' And I'm sick, man.

Packers guard T.J. Lang on the sidelines during a game against the Cardinals. Getty Images

"I've been in a group chat with a whole lot of former teammates that feel the exact same way that I do."

Lang admitted that he does not have the greatest relationship with Rodgers, while still maintaining one with McCarthy, who he said that he talked with on Monday.

"I haven't talked to Aaron Rodgers in probably five years," Lang said. "I can't remember the last time he returned a text. I don't have a relationship with him. And I think it's just absolutely despicable what he just did to Big Mike."

Pittsburgh head coach Mike McCarthy looks on during the first quarter of the Steelers' loss to the Eagles on Nov. 22, 2026, in Philadelphia. Getty Images

McCarthy spoke with reporters Wednesday evening and did acknowledge the miscue referring to the Steelers as the Packers in the locker room, and called it "embarrassing."

Various reports indicated that the Steelers (6-4) don't have any plans to make a change at head coach and that McCarthy has the organization's support. Rodgers reportedly gave McCarthy an ultimatum to give up playcalling duties or cut him as frustrations over the offense's success came to a head.

Mason Rudolph will start at quarterback for the Steelers when they face the Broncos on Black Friday.
