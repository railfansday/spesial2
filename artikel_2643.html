3 takeaways from Utah’s blowout win over West Virginia to complete its perfect regular season

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

For the first time since 2008, the Utah Utes are undefeated at the regular season's end following a 52-28 drubbing of the West Virginia Mountaineers on Black Friday at Rice-Eccles Stadium in Salt Lake City.

It was never very close as the Utes seized control early behind the star play of quarterback Devon Dampier, who totaled 422 yards and five touchdowns before being retired early in the third quarter on Senior Night.

Dampier left a strong impression for Heisman Trophy voters with 393 passing yards in what will likely be his final performance at Rice-Eccles Stadium this season.

To no one's surprise, the Mountaineer defense was no match for Utah's innovative and potent offense, and without starting quarterback Michael Hawkins Jr., the West Virginia offense struggled to get anything going until the second half with backup Scotty Fox Jr.

After struggling through a quad injury against BYU, Wayshawn Parker played at the start of the games against Arizona and TCU only to exit almost immediately from both contests.

However, in what may have been his last opportunity to play in front of a home crowd in Salt Lake City, Parker scored a touchdown on the Utes' first drive of the night Friday, his first score since the team's road trip to Cincinnati.

Recording just a single rushing yard on five carries, his performance was not anything sensational, but getting Parker back and easing him into game situations could not have come at a better time for Utah as the Utes ready themselves for the Big 12 championship game next week.

While Daniel Bray has filled in quite nicely in Parker's place, Parker adds another element of flexibility and surprise to Utah's offensive attack in addition to his flat-out ability to run the ball.

Parker's presence, and comfortability with his heel, will be a major factor for this Ute squad's postseason journey.

Dampier is expected to receive an invite to New York as a finalist for the Heisman Trophy.

To start the season, Dampier was not on anyone's radars, at least nationally, for the Heisman, but after consistently leading his team to victory after victory, heads began to turn.

Averaging 244 passing yards per game this season with 29 passing and 11 rushing touchdowns, Dampier has continued to not only impress in the box score, but also in the highlights.

Time after time Dampier has broken tackles to escape the pocket and make something happen out of nothing, turned on the legs and has even lined up to receive passes from his receivers.

Now, with his team 12-0 and headed to the Big 12 championship in Arlington against the Texas Tech Red Raiders, Dampier made another statement for his Heisman campaign Friday.

In just the first half, Dampier had 251 passing yards and three passing touchdowns against a porous Mountaineer defense. When he ended his night before the midway mark of the third quarter, he had 393 yards passing and five touchdowns with a big thanks to a 75-yard touchdown pass on the first play of the second half to Braden Pegan.

While Ohio State's Jeremiah Smith is still widely considered to be the favorite to win the Heisman, some speculate whether a poor team record for the Buckeyes (7-4 ahead of a rivalry matchup with Kyle Wittingham and Michigan on Saturday) could impact his odds, despite averaging 134 receiving yards with 17 touchdowns on the season.

If Dampier does have a chance, nights like the one he just had sure help his cause.

The last two coaches to lead Utah to undefeated regular seasons are Urban Meyer (2004) and Wittingham (2008). They went on to win the Fiesta Bowl and the Sugar Bowl, respectively.

However, for Scalley, finishing the season undefeated like his predecessors would mean winning the national championship, something Meyer and Wittingham could not do at Utah, although Meyer did go on to win three national championships after his time at Utah (two with Florida and one with Ohio State).

If Scalley can lead his team to be the second undefeated champion in the 12-team playoff era (Indiana did it last season in the second year of the expanded playoff), it would be the single greatest season in Utah football history and put the first-time head coach on quite the trajectory.
