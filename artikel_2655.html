Wayshawn Parker’s injury, late-game play calling, third-quarter woes and more from Morgan Scalley’s press conference

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

In each of Utah's last two games, running back Wayshawn Parker has played in one series before heading to the sidelines for the rest of the game.

Utah's star running back has been dealing with a heel bruise that he suffered early in the BYU game. In the two games since, Parker has attempted to play, but the pain has been too great for him to continue.

Parker practiced ahead of Utah's win at TCU, warmed up and led the team in his customary "wake up" chant ahead of the contest in Fort Worth. But after one carry, Parker limped off the field and eventually watched the rest of the game in street clothes.

"This week we had a little bit more, not a little bit, we had much more practice time with him — so the ability to cut and do some of the things that he would've been doing on game day. It happened that in this warmup, he just felt a different type of pain," Scalley said.

Parker did not worsen the existing injury, Scalley said.

"It ends up being not any more significant of injury. He didn't worsen the issue, so that's good. But we just did not, at that point, want to do anything that would keep him from further football this season," Scalley said.

Parker has been a huge piece of Utah's offense, rushing for 765 yards and nine touchdowns on 125 carries. He's added 269 receiving yards and four touchdowns and has been a big plus in pass blocking, something that has been glaring in his absence in the last two games.

Parker's status for this week's game will become more clear as Friday approaches, but it wouldn't be a big surprise if Utah rests him. The Utes are 22.5-point favorites over West Virginia, which has one of the worst defenses in all of college football.

Daniel Bray had the best game of his career against Arizona, rushing for 197 yards and a score, but had 57 yards on 14 carries against TCU. That's partly due to the strength of TCU's defense, which was as good as any that Utah has faced this year.

The Horned Frogs' front was able to get into the backfield, especially in the first half, sacking quarterback Devon Dampier four times and limiting Utah's rushing attack.

Bray was outmatched much of the game in pass protection, allowing three sacks, according to Pro Football Focus.

Utah's offensive line was also unable to get the push they've been used to this season, especially in the first half. On a few short-yardage situations, the Utes were unable to convert.

Could Utah bring in running backs like Mateaki Helu or Steve Chavez-Soto for key passing downs or short-yardage situations against West Virginia?

"Fifth in the country on third down, so I think we're doing pretty good. It's the fourth-down stuff. Short yardage is a combination of your offensive line and your back trusting gaps that are going to be opening up for you. It can be a power thing as well, but Daniel Bray has proven that he can get those yards just as much as those guys have," Scalley said.

Ultimately, Scalley is going to trust running backs coach Mark Atuaia in those moments.

Speaking of trusting his coordinators, Scalley has put a lot of belief in offensive coordinator Kevin McGiven, who has called a couple of gutsy plays in Utah's last two wins.

McGiven has dialed up two deep shots in key situations, and quarterback Devon Dampier and his receivers have executed to perfection. Against Arizona, Creed Whittemore caught a 70-yard touchdown pass from Dampier, and against TCU, it was Larry Simmons with an 85-yard touchdown reception.

In the TCU game, Utah was up by seven points and was facing a third-and-4 from its own 15-yard line. The safe play could have been a run or a short pass. Instead, McGiven took a risk that paid off.

"I've never doubted Kevin," Scalley said.

As a defensive coordinator, Scalley would be expecting a run in that spot and would play aggressive defense as a result.

"They're thinking about, OK, hold onto the football, thinking aggressive (defense). They're going to run the ball, keep the clock going. So in my opinion, do the opposite, go deep," Scalley said.

Players can also feed off the confidence a play call like the one last Saturday demonstrates in the offense.

"That's another thing too that players can probably sense is by the play calling, are you becoming safer?" Scalley said. "Are you tightening up versus do you trust them to go make a play?"

McGiven certainly trusts his players to make plays in crunch time, and calls plays accordingly.

Utah has shown time and time again that it has the clutch gene, though Scalley would prefer if the Utes would need to make fewer comebacks, especially on the road.

In Big 12 play, the Utes are being outscored 82-41 in the third quarter, and the Utes haven't scored more than seven points in any third quarter in conference play.

"Third quarter is something that really has been an Achilles heel for us, and really it's been on the road, when we have the lead — that's the crazy thing," Scalley said.

The common denominator in those third-quarter performances on the road has been a turnover.

"The biggest thing is turnover on downs or turnover in general, and all of a sudden you get the crowd back in it. Now we're able to respond eventually, but I'd like not to have that happen," Scalley said.

"So we've got to find out what the — maybe it's my just crappy halftime speeches, maybe that's it. I need to just shut up is what I need to do."

Ute quarterback Devon Dampier is eligible to return next season thanks to the NCAA's five-in-five rule, which grants players five years to play five seasons. The rule retroactively applies to current players, and Dampier has only played four seasons, so he could decide to return to college football if he so chooses.

Asked if he has had conversations with Dampier about a return, here's what Scalley had to say.

"Those conversations we've started to have. Some of it is with the individual, some of it is with their agents. I think everyone's focused right now on finishing the season the right way," Scalley said.

"... I mean we'd be dumb not to be having those conversations right now, but I'm definitely not open to telling you about those conversations."
