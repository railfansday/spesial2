All Roads Lead Back to East Lansing For Demond Williams Jr.

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

For the first time in his college career, Washington Huskies quarterback Demond Williams Jr. is set to hit the field against Michigan State, where his father made a name for himself as an impact defensive back and kick returner two decades ago.

Since UW's move to the Big Ten in 2024, when Williams Jr. was a freshman, the dynamic dual-threat signal-caller has faced every single conference opponent Demond Williams Sr. did from 2005-06 except MSU, the one school his father couldn't play against.

With the unique family history serving as the backdrop for the Huskies' second-to-last road game of the season, there is sure to be some special moments from the third-year QB, something first-year Spartans head coach Pat Fitzgerald is well aware of heading into the Week 11 matchup.

"When I watch him, he's just spectacular. Spectacular," Fitzgerald said Monday of the younger Williams. "He can make all the throws. He's an incredible athlete. The two-point play at the end of the game against Penn State, I mean his ability to spin move, to spin out of it, falling away to his left and the throw he made, there's not very many people that can do what he did on that play, let alone his style of play. In our game, he presents huge problems for our defense in his holistic game. It's everything that he does. We got our hands full; there's no doubt about that."

In five of nine games this season, MSU has allowed a rushing touchdown to an opposing quarterback, including at home to Illinois and Northwestern in consecutive losses last month, something Williams Jr. hasn't done since UW's 27-24 loss to Minnesota to open conference play and what could prove to be the difference Saturday as the team looks to secure its third consecutive bowl berth under Jedd Fisch.

If there were a game that could bring the best out of the third-year UW quarterback, MSU does provide the best opportunity for success with the Spartans in the bottom half of the Big Ten in sacks, interceptions, and scoring defense coming into the game.

However, given the Huskies' offensive struggles this season and Williams Jr. himself is well off the mark in every major category from last season, there isn't a guarantee that the Spartans can't play spoiler as they did, for instance, back in 2005 when his father made a crucial pass breakup late against then-No. 10 Notre Dame that ultimately secured a 44-41 overtime victory over the Fighting Irish.

Much like the elder Williams, who etched his name in the MSU record books as the first player in school history to return an opening kickoff for a touchdown the same year as the Notre Dame upset, it only takes one moment for the Huskies' signal-caller to change the game, as he showed in Lincoln two weeks ago.
