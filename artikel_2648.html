Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Before evaluating the performance of his players or coaches like Matt Patricia, Ohio State's Ryan Day should first evaluate his own.

This includes misuse of time outs, the decision to go for a fourth-and-1 when playing a ranked team and always put the points on the board when facing such teams.

Remember the final game of 2025, the Big Ten Championship against Indiana, and the first game of 2026 against Texas when Day decided to go for a fourth-and-1 and didn't even learn from last year's loss, in both cases a good field goal would have changed the dynamics.

More: How did Ohio State's Ryan Day explain loss to Nebraska?

This season Day's anger on the sidelines hasn't been seen in that compacity since Woody Hayes. Anger doesn't get you results it makes your team go through a downhill spiral.

Day's a good coach and a bad season shouldn't define him; Day has to adjust his thinking like he expects players and other coaches to adjust their thinking after or during an obvious loss. Day needs to keep Patricia and look long and hard in the mirror regarding his own misgivings.

We have the same morning routine as most other Bexley families: breakfast, backpacks, a quick hug at the school door.

None of us should have to wonder whether that goodbye might be our last. And none of our children should have to learn what to do if a stranger enters their school with a weapon of war, intent on causing maximum harm. That they must is our failure.

On Dec. 2, the Supreme Court will hear arguments on whether state and local bans on assault-style rifles and large-capacity magazines violate the Second Amendment. These cases didn't start here, but the ruling will reach every community.

AR-style rifles are the weapons of choice for mass shooters.

All 10 of the deadliest mass shootings since 2016 involved an assault weapon and a high-capacity magazine. According to Everytown Research, mass shootings involving an assault weapon kill more than twice as many people and wound 23 times as many as those that don't.

The Constitution doesn't require us to accept weapons of war in our schools. Communities should have the power to draw that line. I hope the Supreme Court agrees with the lower courts, upholds these bans and protects our children.

It occurred to me that the "brilliant" creators of AI programing are ignoring the potential catastrophic legal consequences of their creations' deeds.

Let's say an AI application plots and carries out murders, even genocide. Or it decides to accumulate massive wealth by stealing from others.

In warranty law terms, they are creating an inherently dangerous product, AND they have made enough public statements to prove that they are completely aware of the dangers.

Imagine ... murder charges against AI creators? Civil liability for the money AI steals?

Could it be that voters want leaders to adhere to the Constitution and do their job as elected leaders for their constituents, and not one person? The U.S. House met only 77 days this past year before the midterm election. Could voters think the gerrymandering and changing voting rules right before elections so one party would have an advantage is not real representation?

Could it be because constituents are appalled that an elected leader has been allowed not to follow established rules because elected officials gave their power to one person? The vanity of this person destroyed a part of our historical White House, put his name on a historical monument and plans to build an enormous, pompous monument to honor himself.

Could it be voters don't want presidents and their families to become billionaires from foreign dealings and keep millions of dollars in gifts while in office? Could it be because the leaders are allowing billionaires to buy and destroy our national forests and parts of the ocean that we own and want to preserve?

We, the People, want elected officials, not one person, to determine if constituents are sent to war.
