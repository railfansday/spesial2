How Andy MacWilliams' famous call captured Xavier basketball history

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The words became part of Xavier basketball history.

"The UC Bearcats are No. 1 in the country, No. 2 in their own city!"

Andy MacWilliams delivered the line moments after Lenny Brown's buzzer-beater stunned top-ranked Cincinnati in the 1996 Crosstown Shootout.

It was the perfect punctuation to one of Xavier's biggest wins, captured the rivalry's intensity and gave Musketeer fans a phrase they would repeat for years.

The idea didn't originate in the broadcast booth. It started the night before, in a conversation between MacWilliams and his son, Bryan.

Bryan, then a freshman at Xavier and a former ball boy for the Musketeers, discussed the possibility of an upset with his father.

"We talked the night before the Shootout," MacWilliams said. "He said, 'Dad, in case the upset does happen, you have to have a monumental signature line ready.' We batted around a bunch of thoughts. He came up with 'No. 1 in the country, No. 2 in their own city.' I just happened to remember it when the circumstances dictated it."

Those circumstances arrived in the final seconds at Shoemaker Center. Brown's shot went through the net as time expired, giving Xavier a 71-69 victory over its crosstown rival. What followed was an emotional stretch on the radio broadcast.

"My main thought was that I interrupted Joe Sunderman, my longtime broadcast partner, about five times," MacWilliams said. "I was on an epic roll for about 30-40 seconds after the game. The words just came out."

MacWilliams apologized to Sunderman profusely, but the emotion of the moment had taken over. Although the game was televised and the call was made on the radio, its impact endured well beyond that November night.

Xavier fans began using the line on their answering machines. Television producers have repeatedly asked MacWilliams to revisit it, and the call has periodically been replayed during Crosstown Shootout pregame coverage.

MacWilliams and Byron Larkin preview Xavier's season every year with the Musketeer Club at Hofbrauhaus in Newport. Before they start breaking down Xavier's roster and schedule, MacWilliams' call is played and met with applause.

Dan Hoard, who served as Fox 19's color commentator for the game, considers it "one of the greatest calls in sports broadcasting history."

"It's the type of moment as an announcer that you hope to nail, and he crushed it," Hoard said. "He delivered it perfectly. It has just a little bit of 'in your face' way of using his voice in that call. I gotta hand it to him. Announcer-to-announcer, that's about as good as it gets."

Jeff Battle was an assistant coach for Xavier in 1996. He's now on Ed Cooley's staff at Georgetown and makes sure to find MacWilliams each year when the Hoyas visit Cintas Center.

"I still hear that call in my sleep," Battle said. "I'll never forget it. It's one of the all-time great calls."

MacWilliams, a Xavier Hall-of-Famer who did play-by-play for the Musketeers from 1983-1998, has other memorable moments on the microphone. He recalls Xavier beating "The Beast from the East" in Georgetown at the Hoosier Dome to advance to the Sweet 16, but the Crosstown Shootout line is hard to beat.

"Frankly, I've gotten a lot of mileage out of it," MacWilliams said. "It's been good for the ego, because people associate that call with me and the circumstances. It epitomizes the rivalry."
