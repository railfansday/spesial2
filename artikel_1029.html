Jones: Erasmus overstepped the mark with officials comments

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Rassie Erasmus was handed a six-game ban for his extraordinary criticisms about officials; the South Africa coach claimed there were 17 illegal tackles during his side's defeat to France; Jones says he might tune in to watch Erasmus' live watch along of Ireland vs South Africa

Eddie Jones believes Rassie Erasmus "overstepped the mark" with his extraordinary criticisms of match officials that saw the South Africa coach receive a six-game ban.

Erasmus hit out at the officiating during his side's 21-19 defeat to France in a press conference on Monday, suggesting officials missed 17 illegal hip-drop tackles before questioning whether "manipulation of the game" was behind why these incidents were not penalised.

Writing in his column for The Daily Mail, Jones expressed sympathy for the Springboks coach, who is barred from attending his side's Nations Championship game against Ireland in Dublin on Saturday, but said his "match-fixing" claims were "inappropriate".

"As coaches, we're only human. We can all get emotional and tip over the edge," Jones wrote.

"There's a lot of pressure in Test rugby, especially if you're coaching one of the big nations.

"But Rassie has definitely overstepped the mark this time. To use cricket parlance, he's bowled a few no-balls in his career. And this is the latest."

While he won't be at the Arriva Stadium come Saturday, Jones believes Erasmus will still have a large influence on South Africa's preparations.

On Wednesday, Erasmus took to X to reveal he'd be hosting a watch along of the match, and invited social media users to join and listen to him watch the game from his hotel room.

Jones believes Erasmus will "still be busy" despite the ban, and added he may even tune in for some of the South Africa coaches' watch along on Saturday after Japan's game against Scotland.

"I wouldn't put it past him for the Springboks to smuggle him into the ground, a la Jose Mourinho's washing basket trick with Chelsea all those years ago!" he joked.

As part of his sanctions, Erasmus will also miss the Nations Championship finals weekend on November 27, with the coach prevented from entering the stadium or having direct contact with his players or management team. He will be free to return in time for the 2027 Rugby World Cup.

During the press conference, Erasmus also said he would send footage to World Rugby of the illegal tackles during his side's game against France, claiming South Africa should have had three yellow cards.

An independent panel, instructed to investigate the matter by World Rugby, said Erasmus' comments "constituted an unacceptable attack on the credibility and competence of the match officials".

South Africa Rugby requested a full hearing in response to Erasmus' sanction and condemned online abuse World Rugby officials have received over the past week.

Jones, meanwhile, was banned for six weeks in May for criticising a match official during an U23s tour of Australia.

The Japan coach later apologised for his "inappropriate remarks", adding "I deeply regret my words and actions."

"Rassie was obviously frustrated and I can understand why," Jones said. "He's a very strong character. A deep thinker.

"Rugby needs that, but it needs respect too."

The hip-drop tackle has been a contentious issue during the Nations Championship Northern Series following it's implementation on November 1, with Ireland's Sam Prendergast being cited and later cleared for an illegal tackle on Fiji captain Peceli Yato last week.

Jones added: "Rassie had a point when he questioned some of the decisions made in his team's defeat by France, and I sent Rassie a text to that effect - he didn't reply.

"But there are appropriate and inappropriate ways to make a point and Rassie's was the latter, especially with his 'match-fixing' claim."

Erasmus has been banned twice in the past for his criticisms of referees, once for two months following the first Test of the British and Irish Lions tour to South Africa in 2021, and then again in 2022, after he published a series of X posts questioning the officiating during his side's defeat to France.
