SHENZHEN LEADS THE WAY FORWARD

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

SHENZHEN, China, Nov. 17, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- A news report from China Daily:

Shenzhen, Guangdong province, continues its growth momentum into this year, with GDP hitting 3.03 trillion yuan ($454.4 billion) in the first three quarters, an increase of 6 percent year-on-year.

Government data show the city's value-added output from industrial enterprises above designated size â those with annual revenue of 20 million yuan or more â expanded by 9.5 percent, total foreign trade surged 38.8 percent, and investment in high-tech industries jumped 46.6 percent.

Shenzhen has evolved from a fishing and farming village into an economic powerhouse with its annual GDP exceeding 3 trillion yuan in just more than four decades. Its remarkable growth story is embodied in a long list of pioneering achievements.

In the past 46 years, the city has notched more than 1,000 "national firsts". It issued China's first stock in 1983, giving rise to the Chinese mainland's first joint-stock enterprise in the reform and opening-up era.

In 1987, it hosted the nation's first auction for State-owned land-use rights, a milestone that directly informed a constitutional amendment. Then in 1990, the Shenzhen Stock Exchange launched China's first centralized securities trading system. Each of these steps blazed new trails for reform nationwide.

Behind those trailblazing experiments lies the persistence and resilience of the Shenzhen people. The special economic zone's spirit of exploration, leadership and diligence has been woven into the genes of the city.

This spirit transformed Shenzhen from a national reform testing ground into a pacesetter for high-quality development.

Institutional innovation runs even deeper. Shenzhen pioneered China's "10 systems", including an ownership framework where public ownership plays the dominant role alongside diverse forms of ownership, and a market-driven pricing system.

Many local breakthroughs were refined into replicable policy models for nationwide adoption.

Over the past three years, 87 sets of "Shenzhen experiences" have been promoted nationwide by the National Development and Reform Commission, the country's top economic policymaking body.

Moreover, with no established precedents to follow, Shenzhen forged ahead to build China's socialist market economy system. This gave rise to its distinctive "six-90s" innovation profile: over 90 percent of innovative companies are homegrown; more than 90 percent of the city's research and development institutions, staff and funding sit within companies; and over 90 percent of service invention patents and invention patents for major science and technology projects originate from corporate entities.

Entrepreneurial dynamism is tangible evidence of this ecosystem. By the end of 2025, Shenzhen was home to more than 4.65 million market entities, giving it China's highest business-startup density â roughly one in every four local residents runs a business.

Shenzhen is famous for change: visitors often encounter a dramatically transformed urban landscape on each return trip. What began as a fishing village has morphed into a modern metropolis with skyscrapers, sprawling financial districts, as well as high-tech and free trade zones.

Multinational corporations once set up factories and production lines in Shenzhen because of cost-effective labor and land. Today, higher-value innovation functions are becoming increasingly concentrated in the city.

In June, Merck, the 358-year-old German science and technology company, completed its Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area innovation and collaboration center in the city's Nanshan district. Earlier in April, chip designer AMD opened an ecosystem innovation center in Shenzhen to complement its R&D hubs in China.

Stretching from chip design to end-product manufacturing, and spanning new energy and biomedicine sectors, Shenzhen and other cities in the Greater Bay Area host one of the world's most complete manufacturing supply chains.

An original concept can secure local suppliers in Shenzhen, generate physical prototypes and move into mass production at a remarkable speed.

Tan Poh Hwee, president of the Singapore-based Asia Academy of Digital Economics, noted that early-era Shenzhen acted as an interface connecting the Chinese mainland with global markets across divergent economic systems.

As time went on, he said, the city linked research, design, manufacturing, capital and consumer markets, turning laboratory technologies into products that could be made on a large scale.

Taylor Ogan, founder and CEO of Snow Bull Capital, underscored Shenzhen's unique innovative edge, saying that successful startups operate close to comprehensive supply chains. Virtually every component they need can be sourced within an hour's drive.

Urban governance in Shenzhen has also drawn global attention. Guo Renzhong, an academician of the Chinese Academy of Engineering and dean of Shenzhen University's Research Institute for Smart Cities, said Shenzhen was among China's earliest cities to explore digital governance, launching the "Smart Shenzhen" initiative early on.

From Nov 18 to 19, Shenzhen will host the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting, marking the first time the city will take center stage for Asia-Pacific economic cooperation.

It will share "China solutions" covering the digital economy and artificial intelligence-driven regional growth, advancing China's shift from "exporting finished products" toward "exporting technologies and standards".

The city will also share experience in building a greener Asia-Pacific region, drawing on its track record in new energy deployment and low-carbon urban governance.

Shenzhen is globally celebrated for innovation, advanced manufacturing, entrepreneurship and international trade. Yet its most profound lesson lies in transformation, said Amjad Aziz Malik, chief editor of Pakistan's Daily Kasoti Peshawar.

Shenzhen has shown the world that development is never accidental, he said. It requires long-term planning, solid infrastructure, quality education, technological progress and â above all â sustained resolve to pursue reform and continuous improvement.

As a pilot demonstration area for socialism with Chinese characteristics, Shenzhen's explorations serve as a vivid illustration of its pioneering practice on the path of Chinese modernization. More than just a microcosm of China's reform and opening-up, Shenzhen offers a living sample for Asia-Pacific economies seeking to understand China's high-quality development trajectory.
