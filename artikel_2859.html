The Aaron Rodgers ultimatum that sent the Steelers into chaos

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Aaron Rodgers ended up on the wrong side of his own ultimatum.

The now-former Steelers quarterback was waived by the team on Wednesday, following a wild 18-hour period that saw him get benched in a late-night announcement and then go nuclear on coach Mike McCarthy in a nationally televised tirade.

It all started with a Tuesday meeting between the coach and QB, who won a Super Bowl together in 2011 with the Packers.

"I didn't want this to be the way that it ended," Rodgers said in an explosive appearance on the "Pat McAfee Show" on Wednesday. "But I was very clear with the ultimatum and it wasn't a veiled ultimatum. It wasn't posturing. It was me as a leader of the football team inspired by my teammates who talk about this all the time. Literally all the time in council meetings, on group chats, in the locker room, just some of the things that are just like wow, how is this the reality we're dealing with? I was, in my professional opinion based on 22 years of experience … the only person who could talk to him and be honest with him to tell him that what's best for the team is for him to not call the plays and if he wasn't up for that, they should probably release me."

According to Netflix NFL insider Tom Pelissero, McCarthy was open to Rodgers' demand, as the veteran has been calling plays himself in recent weeks, but other coaches and "some in the locker room" convinced him it was not a move they wanted.

McCarthy confirmed in a press conference later in the day that Rodgers attempted to put the screws to him.

Mike McCarthy and Aaron Rodgers speak on the sidelines as the Steelers face the Bengals. Getty Images

"We got to a point, we talked some things — you know communication and things like that — and there was an ultimatum made clearly outside of his job description and so it was my decision to bench him," McCarthy told reporters.

McCarthy made the call to move backup Mason Rudolph into the starter's role, kicking off a chaotic day in which Rodgers accused his coach of falling asleep in meetings and divulging a halftime gaffe in which he said, "1-2-3 Packers!" among other things.

Pittsburgh players reportedly watched Rodgers burn it all down on McAfee's live ESPN broadcast on their phones in the locker room in real time.

Hours later, the team granted the soon-to-be 43-year-old quarterback his ultimatum and sent him packing.
