Berlinale Alum Sreemoyee Singh’s WAVES Film Bazaar Selection ‘Fireflowers,’ About a Mother’s Lost Singing Voice, Boards Dutch Co-Producer (EXCLUSIVE)

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Indian filmmaker Sreemoyee Singh’s personal documentary “Fireflowers,” about a mother who lost her singing voice after her husband died, has added Dutch outfit New Amsterdam Film Company, through Sander Verdonk, as co-producer.

The project is also among the selections at this year’s WAVES Film Bazaar, the market component of the International Film Festival of India in Goa.

The co-production was finalized ahead of the market. “Fireflowers” is Singh’s follow-up to “And, Towards Happy Alleys,” her debut documentary, which she shot in Iran and which world premiered in the Berlinale’s Panorama strand in 2023. The new film turns to Singh’s own family. Her parents used to sing together, and within a year of her father’s death, her mother could no longer sing. Singh began training in vocal music around the same time. The film moves between her father’s 1980s Calcutta and the present day, as mother and daughter work through grief that was never fully dealt with, in a city that is itself losing much of its old character. Animation is by Bob and Bobby.

“This is a story I have been born into, lived and grown with. Finally I feel ready to share it. I filmed my debut documentary in Iran, but here I turn the lens inward – into my home, and tell the story of my parents and the city I have grown up in,” says Singh.

“In ‘Fireflowers,’ Sreemoyee Singh brings together her mother’s lost voice, her father’s absence and a disappearing Calcutta [Kolkata] in one intimate film. After her acclaimed Berlinale debut, I’m thrilled to support her as co-producer on this deeply personal and universal story,” says Verdonk.

Triparna Banerjee and Singh produce for Happy Alley Films, with Dibya Chatterjee and Indraprasad Ghatak of HandyKraft Pictures also co-producing. The project was earlier pitched at DocEdge Kolkata and has received support from South Korea’s DMZ Docs.

“‘Fireflowers’ is a mother and a daughter reflecting on grief, memory and the fragility of voice, held together by music and the city of Calcutta [Kolkata]. Producing it is my quiet resistance to the expectation that documentaries from India or South Asia must be boxed into social issues,” says Banerjee.

Chatterjee calls the film a daughter’s look back at her father and her city, made with a tenderness that is sacred. “Sreemoyee turns her own past into something the whole world can feel, a journey of grief, memory and hope. I truly hope her mother sings again. It’s my privilege to walk beside her on this, as a producer.”

Ghatak, for his part, says there is something extraordinarily brave about reaching into the deepest corners of one’s heart and laying it bare for the world to feel and perhaps even mourn alongside. “I have long wanted to support cinema that carries this kind of emotional truth, and ‘Fireflowers’ is exactly that,” he says.
