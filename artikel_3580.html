‘Newlyweds’ Gives Tim Daly and Téa Leoni a Honeymoon With a Twist

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Back in the 1990s, Tim Daly and Tea Leoni were NBC sitcom mainstays on Wings and The Naked Truth, respectively. Now, they're back on NBC as a couple (on-screen and in real life) in the new sitcom Newlyweds, which premiered on the network last month. Tonight, the newlyweds are headed on their honeymoon, and Collider is proud to present an exclusive sneak peek of it.

Newlyweds centers around Jeanie (Leoni), a middle school science teacher, and James (Daly), a philosophy professor; they're both divorced with grown children, and they've been set up on a blind date by their mutual friend Dan (Al Madrigal). As soon as they meet, sparks fly. In fact, they hit it off so well that they get married in Las Vegas in the pilot episode, much to the consternation of James' ex-wife, Claudia (Jamie Lee Curtis), and the confusion of Jeanie's children, Poppy (Emma Meisel) and Lolo (Ignacio Diaz-Silverio).

Now, in tonight's episode, "We'll Never Have Paris," the duo go on their honeymoon. However, the plans for that are as whirlwind as their wedding, because they're not actually going anywhere. Nevertheless, James is determined to take his new wife to Paris, or a reasonable approximation of it, so he has his assistant Arvid (Bill O'Neill) lead a beret-clad Jeanie to a "café" (their kitchen), then to an adjoining room, where James is waiting with a scale model of the Eiffel Tower—and Arvid dressed as a mime. Will Jeanie and James' wedded bliss last, or is the honeymoon over? You'll have to tune into NBC tonight at 8:30 p.m. ET to find out; it will also stream tomorrow on Peacock.

Four love stories, four completely different ideas of what it means to be somebody's person. This is the one that matches how you actually fall, and what it would ask of you.

You do not fall for strangers. You fall for the person you have already been talking to for years, which is the slowest and by some distance the most durable way anybody has found to do this.

You want a partner, not an audience. Somebody who wants their own life as badly as you want yours, and who would never ask you to be smaller to make the relationship easier.

The intensity was never the problem. The secrecy is. You are drawn to a love that lives entirely in private, and to the particular ache of wanting somebody you are not supposed to want.

You do not do this cautiously. When it happens for you it is immediate and total and it rearranges everything, and you would not accept a smaller version of it.

Jamie Lee Curtis, who also recurs as Claudia, co-created the series with Gail Lerner (Will and Grace), with the latter serving as showrunner; Lerner departed the series last month and was replaced as showrunner by sitcom veteran Bruce Helford (The Conners). It is executive produced by Curtis, Eric Tannenbaum, Kim Tannenbaum, and Scott Schwartz. The series' pilot was directed by Pamela Fryman (How I Met Your Mother).

"We'll Never Have Paris," the third episode of Newlyweds, will premiere on NBC tonight, November 6, at 8:30 p.m., and will stream tomorrow on Peacock. New episodes of Newlyweds air weekly on Fridays at 8:30 pm ET/PT on NBC. Stay tuned to Collider for future updates.
