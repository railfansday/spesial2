Mike McCarthy admits he hasn't spent enough time with the defense

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

After registering an unlikely 27-5 win over the previously 8-2 Broncos, Steelers coach Mike McCarthy wasn't angry, resentful, or defiant.

He was the same guy he always is, and he admitted that (as former Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers said this week), McCarthy hasn't been sufficiently invested in the side of the ball that isn't his personal expertise.

"I have not spent enough time with the defense," McCarthy told reporters. "You know, and that's something that, as far as job responsibility, job description, you know, these are things that'll be looked at in the offseason. But, you know, it's a point in our year where we're making adjustments and definitely conscientious of it. But at the end of the day, we have to all do our jobs and make sure we're giving the players the plan and all the other things that they need that we can perform and play with that type of urgency, physicality that we played today."

McCarthy prefaced his comments with a lesson he learned as a boy, growing up in Pittsburgh.

"My father used to tell me all the time as a kid, 'You tell the truth, you only have to tell your story once,'" McCarthy said.

The truth is that this week has entailed two sharply different versions of reality, where both things can indeed be the truth. One, McCarthy (like all of us) has flaws. Two, Rodgers should not have spoken about them publicly.

Rodgers clumsily defended his decision to break the code of a football locker room by texting this to Al Michaels: "The code or sanctity exists to protect the integrity of the locker room, not to provide immunity from accountability for its leadership."

That's fine, but how does disregarding the "sanctity" of the locker room (a word Rodgers undoubtedly selected in response to Tom Brady using it on Thursday) create "accountability"? McCarthy is accountable to team owner Art Rooney II. Not to the media or the fans.

And to what end? He was either doing it to help the Steelers save their season, or he was lashing out.

We'll let you decide which explanation is more likely.
