Five Plays: Pitt Second Quarter

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

This is what they've been missing from the interior defensive line. Demecco Kennedy splits a double team with quickness here, and it forces the running back well off his path for a big loss. Kennedy had a play like this in the first quarter, and just like that play, none of this is going to show up on a stat sheet. The other players will get credit for a tackle for loss, and everyone will focus on them, but Kennedy makes this play. It's a thankless job, but these are the plays that coaches praise on Sunday during film.

At the time, I was really frustrated because it looked like the entire offense watched a fumble bounce around on the ground for the second time this year. After watching it again, everyone reacted once they noticed the ball was out. The linemen weren't facing towards the ball, and neither was Isaac Brown. But if you watch it a few times, you can see that everyone ran for the ball once they realized what was happening.

The effort to get into this play might be worth questioning, but it wasn't a boneheaded mistake like earlier this year. I would also like to see Lincoln Kienholz chase this down as opposed to looking for a call that it was a pass. This isn't really what you want to see from the leader of your offense.

For the second straight week, we saw a new pressure package from the Cards. Last week, we saw Koen Entringer and TJ Capers get in for sacks off the edge. Against Pitt, they looped Clev Lubin into the middle of the line, while Kaleb Perry blitzes right into the A gap to the center's left shoulder. Lubin gets a clear shot at the quarterback and forces a bad throw right into the middle of Louisville's zone coverage. Ben Perry gets the easiest interception of his life to spark the entire team and gets things going.

I talk a lot about coverage matching pressure on Mike's show on Fridays, and this is what that looks like. The quarterback has to wait just a beat to find an open receiver because the coverage is taking away options for him. Lubin needs a little extra time because of the path he has to take to the quarterback. Everyone does their part and you get a pick.

The second edition of Lincoln Kienholz doing wild things is a little less impressive than the first edition, but it is still impressive.

Here we get a rare instance of Kienholz showing poor mechanics. He goes through his progressions, but when he gets to Justyn Reed on the backside, he feels the pressure to his left as he's going to throw the ball. He ends up opening his left hip way too far as he rushes the throw, and the ball sails on him. He's delivering this throw with all arm, and you have to be a rare talent to make this kind of throw. I'm not sure if he can make this throw if he doesn't get it out when he does, but the alternative of pulling it down and getting what he can is a much better outcome.

There was a play in a game earlier in the day on Saturday where the defense blitzed a safety up the middle, with the two middle linebackers blitzing ahead of him and pulling the interior offensive line to the side like a parting of the sea. The safety timed it perfectly, and he got in for a sack to seal a win. It was beautiful football.

This blitz by Tayon Holloway gave me the same feeling. Lubin and AJ Green are lined up to his side on the defensive line, and they split at the snap to give a wide-open lane for Holloway to run through. The key to these types of pressures is everyone being unselfish. Lubin and Green are both guys looking to make big plays for the team, and if we're being honest, for themselves. They are veteran players looking to improve their draft stock. They both make the unselfish play to make this play work, and Holloway gets the highlight.
