Personal Investments • Re: Transitioning from Vanguard PAS to DIY

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

1. Recently VCOBX was traded for VBTLX in our taxable account for tax loss harvesting to offset an earlier gain due to rebalancing. PAS has suggested moving back into VCOBX following the wait period. Should VCOBX be used for the bond holdings or VBTLX?
I agree VCOBX or VBTLX are two reasonable basic bond funds to own. "Basic" means they give you very low cost exposure to a diversified set of bonds with an average intermediate-term duration. That's what many Bogleheads use to build a solid DIY portfolio.

There are other basic bond funds that are Bogleheads style and get the job done. For example, all Treasury fund like VSIGX or an all TIPS fund like SCHP, VTP or VTIP. An investor might choose something other than VBTLX because of his or her own personal preferences. For example, you want to avoid "manager risk" (VCOBX is actively managed). Or you want TIPS because they can help when the market ends up underestimating CPI inflation. Or you prefer a slightly shorter duration. Those tickers are some of the more commonly used bond funds besides VBTLX and you'll see them mentioned on Bogleheads.
If VBTLX, does it make sense to also trade VCOBX in our IRA account so we have one bond fund?
Given that you're doing tax loss harvesting you need to be mindful of a wash sale.

If I understand correctly, PAS sold some VCOBX at a loss and you do own VCOBX in His IRA and Her IRA. If you bought new shares of VCOBX (termed "replacement shares") in those IRA accounts within 30 days before or after the VCOBX loss, that's a wash sale. Reinvesting dividends is actually buying new shares, so it seems likely you do have a wash sale. With a wash sale, you can't claim the full tax loss -- you reduce the loss by the amount of the replacement shares. But you do add the disallowed loss to the basis of the replacement shares. Unfortunately, with shares in an IRA those disallowed losses are gone forever.

If Vanguard PAS was managing both your taxable and IRA accounts, presumably they would've been smart enough to avoid the wash sale. I have no experience with PAS, though, so I don't know if their TLH feature takes any IRA holdings into consideration.

To avoid all that confusing mess, it's recommended you not own the same fund in both taxable and tax-advantaged accounts. For example, use Vanguard Intermediate-Term Bond Index fund in the IRA but never own it in taxable. Use VCOBX and VBTLX as TLH partners in taxable.
