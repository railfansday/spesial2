You taught me something here! I love early reggae, but thought it had nothing to do with ska. Why? Because probably sometime in the '90s a kid was talking up ska music, and it sounded like rap to me. Hated it. So ever since then I was under the impression that ska was synonymous with rap, or hip-hop — whatever... I asked Google what was up, and it said this:
You aren't imagining things—the confusion happens because Jamaican music has a vocal style called "toasting" that directly invented rap, and modern ska bands often blend rap into their songs.
Here is why what you've heard sounds like rap, and how it connects back to Desmond Dekker:
• "Toasting" (The Bridge to Rap): In late 1960s Jamaica, DJs at sound system dances began rhythmically chanting and rhyming over instrumental ska and reggae records, a style called toasting (pioneered by artists like U-Roy and King Stitt). When Jamaican DJ Kool Herc moved to New York in the 1970s, he brought that exact technique to the Bronx, which directly created hip hop and rap.
• 1990s "Third-Wave" Ska-Punk: If someone played you "ska" from the 1990s or 2000s (bands like Sublime, Goldfinger, or No Doubt), those bands mixed ska's upbeat guitar rhythm with punk rock and hip hop, frequently featuring fast, spoken-word, rap-style vocals.
• Desmond Dekker's Style (Singing, Not Toasting): Desmond Dekker came from the vocal group and soul-singing tradition of 1960s Jamaica—inspired by American R&B crooners like Sam Cooke and Jackie Wilson—which is why his music is all about melody, harmonies, and soulful singing rather than rhythmic talking.
Learn something new every day!
