Personal Consumer Issues • Re: suggestions, moving away from run flats [tires]

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Here's exactly some of what I have and use:

BREAKER BAR

[Harbor Freight] PITTSBURGH 1/2 in. Drive, 25 in. Breaker Bar - $21.99 full price but frequently on sale, coupons, or Inside Track Club discounts
https://www.harborfreight.com/12-in-dri ... 62729.html

This is the breaker bar that lives in my vehicle's emergency kit. Doesn't need to be high-end, name brand, and expensive, but when you need it, you need it. It's decently long enough and the comfort grip is nice. It says it has a Lifetime Warranty, which can be done in-store I believe.

---------------------------------

WHEEL CHOCKS

HAUL-MASTER Solid Rubber Wheel Chock - $7.50 but frequently on sale
https://www.harborfreight.com/solid-rub ... 96479.html

Get at least 2. Chock the wheel diagonal to the flat tire. Safety very important. Honestly I keep these at home as I have some cheap plastic oranges ones in the vehicle.

---------------------------------

SOCKETS

[Home Depot] Husky 1/2 in. Drive 21 mm 6-Point Deep Impact Socket - $14
https://www.homedepot.com/p/Husky-1-2-i ... /205534363

For my home workshop, I have nicer quality sockets from Tekton but decided for my car kit that Home Depot is a good happy medium for me. In-store warranty I believe in case they are defective. Not too expensive but not as cheap as even more generic brands. Heck, the Pittsburgh/Pittsburgh Pro brand at Harbor Freight is cheaper than Home Depot's Husky if you wanna save even more $$$.

These can also come in "lug nut" kits if you wanna buy common sizes all at once. I have the Tekton one but didn't wanna carry it in my car. There's also sockets with color-coded protective coatings on the outside to protect shiny lug nuts, but I've read mixed reviews on durability. So I'm fine with just the uncoated black ones, and I go gentle.

Note: Find the right size for your vehicle(s). Common sizes are 17mm, 19mm, 21mm, 22mm, 13/16" and maybe some others. I bought all the sizes in case I am able/willing to be a good Samaritan on the road, but my RAV4 for example uses 21mm, and many larger CUV/SUVs and trucks use 22mm. Sedans often use 17mm or 19mm.

---------------------------------

FLASHLIGHTS AND LANTERNS AND BLINKY THINGS

Visibility if it's night time so you can see what you're doing + so others can see your vehicle and not kill you. I'm too much of a flashlight nerd and I'd make a massive list. I carry an O-Light Baton 3 Pro max on my person as my Every Day Carry, but I also have multiple backups on me. Magnetic and lovely.

Home Depot has a lot of Husky magnetic lights on sale for $10-25 right now. You can potentially have one lighting up the tire and a second one lighting up your car from behind.

There's various flashing red "flares" and flashing red magnetic things you can attach to your vehicle that flash red. I've had my battery go dead on the side of the road from hazard lights, so I love the redundancy. Right now I have a pair of Olight Sphere magnetic light thingies.

Reflective Warning triangles -- I bought the "Bell" brand from Walmart or Amazon. They collapse down and have a weighted base if it's windy. THESE REALLY WORK. I put 2 behind my vehicle spaced maybe 10 or so feet apart. 3rd one goes in front of the vehicle. I've noticeably seen many, many people change lanes to the left lane when they see my car with 5 million flashing white and red lights, warning triangles, etc. I don't care how obnoxious I look. As long as you don't blind people of course.

Cheap orange reflective vest from Walmart -- I don't care about the fancy quality ones, cheap Walmart one is fine.

---------------------------------

INFLATOR

CRAFTSMAN Inflator/Vacuum/Blower 3-IN-1,Tire Inflator Portable Air Compressor with LED Light,Quiet Car Vacuum or Blower, 160 PSI for Car RV Truck,12V DC Corded and 20V DC Cordless,Tool Only
https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DGGHDDHQ - Amazon, Lowe's, Ace Hardware

DEWALT 20V MAX Air Inflator, Tool Only (DCC020IB)
https://www.amazon.com/DEWALT-DCC020IB- ... B07H39S9JQ - Amazon, Home Depot, etc.

I also carry a Slime brand dual-motor 12V inflator I've had for years. It requires clamping it to your 12V battery, but I think it's more robust than even the DeWalt. It's more of a hassle hooking it up to the vehicle battery, but these are robust. If you have the budget, VIAIR seems to be a common higher-end inflator brand that's popular in the off-roading and RV communities. Several hundred $$$ though.

I own and like both the DeWalt and Craftsman (same parent company, Stanley Black & Decker). I'm already in the DeWalt 20V line so got the DeWalt. It's a beast and pretty nice. It's loud enough I use hearing protection earmuffs when I use it. It's also too expensive to "always" be in my vehicle kit, so I keep the DeWalt at home. The Milwaukee M18 inflators get excellent reviews too.

So enter the Craftsman. Very similar to the DeWalt, doesn't feel "as" beefy or as loud, but I've used it on multiple 1000 mile road trips and it hasn't let me down. It's cheaper so I feel more comfortable keeping it full time in the vehicle kit in case of loss or theft or damage. I wait for sales. I've used the vacuum feature a couple times for crumbs/debris when on road trips. Not a gimmick but it's not meant to be amazing.

Note: The accessory needles (like for sports balls and pool toys, air mattresses, etc.) are clipped into both of these models, and I've had them fall off onto the floor of the car or the ground. Might wanna tape them before you lose them.

I got the DeWalt and Craftsman since they allow for removable batteries so that as batteries get old and fail, I can use the inflator for many years. There are LOTS of cheaper inflators on the market that are cuter, smaller, all-in-one units that have sealed batteries. Some of these work great, but I personally don't think they're environmentally friendly since when the battery fails the whole thing is e-waste. Personal preference. See Project Farm YouTube channel for various recommendations.

Ridgid 18V Cordless Portable Inflator Kit with 2.0 Ah Battery and Charger - $99 Black Friday
https://www.homedepot.com/p/RIDGID-18V- ... /314026557

I don't own the Ridgid but considered it for years. It might be a great option.

Recommended cheap accessory:

[credit to Shop Tool Reviews YouTube channel]
2-Pack Locking Tire Air Chuck, Brass Tire Inflator Hose Adapter w/Clip Adapter & Release Button for Lock On, No Leakage Air Compressor Pump Clip On Tire Chuck Nozzle Adapter Connect Accessories Tool - $9
https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CNSQ94BQ

These are decent. Instead of the hassle of screwing on and off the inflator, you can screw on this brass chuck and it bites down on your tire stem really fast. It's not perfect and sometimes it leaks until you get it "just right" but overall it's worth the convenience.

TIRE PRESSURE GAUGES

I got one at Walmart for $10-15 that has the analog dial and button to release pressure. Been fine for 15+ years.

But I wanted something easier to see as my eyes age and follow Project Farm on YouTube.

Project Farm YouTube video: Finally! An Accurate Tire Pressure Gauge!
He's pretty detailed in his tests and also tests them at cold temperatures.

Ha, I bought 3 from this video and love all 3:

JACO Superior Products ElitePro Digital Tire Pressure Gauge - Professional Accuracy - 200 PSI - $30
https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07JL8YB36
Digital, backlit screen, very easy to see, comes with 2 AAA heavy duty batteries (less likely to leak than alkalines)

Longacre 50417 0-60 PSI Analog Tire Pressure Gauge, 2 Inch - $36
https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00DL6T2YK

Large dial, slight glow in the dark. I think mine's made in USA but I can't verify it still is from the Amazon listing.

Milton S-920 Pencil Tire Pressure Gauge With Tread Depth Gauge - $13
https://www.amazon.com/dp/B002YKB4BS

Pencil style, accurate, made in USA. I never use this kind but it's like my 3rd backup in my vehicle.

I think I keep the Longacre in my car and the JACO Digital at home. Do what you want haha. I definitely like these two the most, and they seem to be accurate per the YouTube video. If I'm lazy, I use the cheap Walmart one since it's in the center console. The others are in my tool bag in the trunk.

PORTABLE JUMP STARTER

NOCO Boost GBX45 1250A UltraSafe Jump Starter, 12V Lithium Battery Booster $125 (I got it for $95)
https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0924V8SPC

Highly rated per Amazon reviews and Project Farm. I keep this in my vehicle (+ bought the Noco carrying case separately) and charge it about once a year. Get whatever size works for your vehicle -- this one is plenty big enough for my compact crossover. Knock on wood, I haven't had a battery failure yet so I haven't used this in the real world.

For 10-15+ years, I used ot carry a Clore Jump 'n' Carry JNC660 lead acid jump starter from Amazon in my car. Big, beefy, and heavy. Battery is dead now but it's been reliable for 10+ years. I think Lithium-ion jump starters are better for most people since they're much smaller and lighter and can be emergency power banks to charge a phone to call 911, AAA, insurance, or loved ones.

---------------------------------

MODERN CPU CONTROLLED, MULTI-STAGE BATTERY CHARGERS

Trickle chargers are obsolete, since those just blindly trickle charge forever. Modern ones have different modes for bulk charge, float charge, etc. and built-in desulfate and repair modes. Can also handle Lithium based batteries.

NOCO Genius 5 - 5 Amps - $70
https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07W8KJH44

NOCO Genius 1 - 1 Amp - $30
https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07W46BX31

Also very reputable and been around longer: Battery Tender brand (Amazon, Costco, etc.)

I wouldn't personally go faster than 5 Amp charging even though there's 10, 20, 50, 100 amps...slower is safer and healthier usually. I have the 1 Amps for vehicle storage, since you can leave these hooked up 24/7.
