Arizona to Petaluma: 15 minimum wage changes set for 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-09

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Arizona, California, Michigan, and nine more states set new pay floors in 2027, alongside Seattle, Saint Paul, and Petaluma. See the rates.

A cashier in one town and a cashier a few miles away can start 2027 with different paychecks for the same shift. The legal minimum an employer owes depends on where the work happens, and in a dozen states that answer shifts in 2027. Workers who earn at or near the bottom of the pay scale will feel the change in their hourly earnings. Business owners will feel the same change in labor budgets, bookkeeping, and the signs on the break-room wall. Anyone who hires, manages staff, or takes a job paying close to the legal floor has a stake in knowing which numbers apply and when they take hold.

Pay floors in the U.S. stack in layers, and the layers do not always match. A state sets one minimum, and a city inside its borders can demand more. Local ordinances can draw their own lines on who counts as covered, how large a business must grow before a given rate applies, and which notices an employer must display. A change to the base figure can pull other figures along with it. Tipped workers, younger employees, trainees, and salaried staff near an exemption cutoff can each fall under a separate number, and those numbers may move on independent schedules. Employers inherit paperwork with every adjustment, including refreshed workplace posters, revised written wage notices, and new pay-stub details in some states. A business that skips any of those steps can fall out of compliance despite a correct hourly amount in its payroll software. The January calendar flip opens a task that stretches past the first payroll run.

Experian gathered the state and local minimum wage changes scheduled for 2027 in a guide aimed at businesses. The roundup describes no research method. It compiles the scheduled rates and requirements across 13 states and 11 local jurisdictions, along with a size-based schedule for Saint Paul, Minnesota, county posting notes for Maryland, and an employer checklist. This list draws on 12 of those states and leaves out Alaska, whose increase takes effect July 1, 2027. Three city entries round out the selection: Seattle, Saint Paul, and Petaluma, California. Each item pairs the 2027 rate with any extra duties, categories, or thresholds the guide attaches to it. Readers can weigh what the new figure asks of a paycheck and of the business that signs it.

Arizona's statewide minimum wage climbs from $15.15 to $15.65 an hour in 2027. The 50-cent gain works out to about 3.3% on top of the 2026 figure.

A full-time Arizona worker at the state minimum would collect $1,040 more in gross pay over the year, assuming 40 hours a week across 52 weeks. Part-time staff gain a smaller amount, scaled to the hours they log.

Employers also owe a task outside payroll in 2027. They must post the 2027 Fair Wages and Healthy Families Act notice. A business that reprograms its pay system but leaves the old poster on the breakroom wall has finished part of the job. A stale notice would show workers figures that no longer apply.

Flagstaff shows how far a city ordinance can push pay past the state level. Its minimum reaches $18.95 on Jan. 1, or $3.30 above the 2027 statewide figure. The gap equals about 21% of the state rate. Over a full-time year of 2,080 hours, the difference adds up to $6,864 in gross pay. An employer with workers in Flagstaff owes the city amount for the hours its staff put in there, and the state number sits beneath it as a base.

Flagstaff's ordinance treats tipped and non-tipped workers alike. The city holds that anyone who earns tips on a regular basis is entitled to the same hourly minimum as coworkers who earn none. A Flagstaff server and a Flagstaff cashier draw an identical base, and gratuities land on top of it without replacing any of it.

An Arizona business running locations both inside and outside Flagstaff would need two pay scales for the same job in 2027.

California's statewide minimum wage climbs from $16.90 to $17.40 an hour in 2027, a 50-cent gain that marks the lowest legal pay in the state. Treat the figure as a floor. Higher local ordinances and industry-specific requirements can lift the real obligation past it, and across a string of California cities they do.

West Hollywood opens the widest gap of the California cities covered here, moving non-hotel employees from $20.25 to $20.94 on Jan. 1. Its new rate runs $3.54 above the state's. Hotel workers in West Hollywood follow a separate $20.87 rate covering July 1, 2026, through June 30, 2027. Palo Alto and Santa Clara each land at $19.25. Burlingame advances from $17.86 to $18.47, and San Diego goes from $17.75 to $18.35. Every one of these cities demands at least 95 cents an hour beyond the statewide base.

Hayward's smaller employers owe no more than the state requires, despite the city's own ordinance. Starting Jan. 1, businesses there with 26 or more employees pay $18.39, and those with 25 or fewer pay $17.40, an amount identical to California's own rate. The size cutoff splits Hayward's workforce into two pay tiers 99 cents apart, and only the larger tier earns a local premium.

The salary threshold for many exempt California employees reaches $72,384 a year in 2027. A full-time worker paid $17.40 for 2,080 hours earns $36,192 a year. The cutoff equals twice that sum. Pay level is one test among several. An employee must also meet the duties standard and the other exemption requirements to qualify.

California employers need to refresh pay-stub and wage-rate information to match the 2027 amounts. A business running sites in West Hollywood and San Diego plus a small Hayward office would list three different hourly minimums on those records: $20.94, $18.35, and $17.40. The state figure gives such a company a starting line, and the binding number shifts from one address to the next.

Maine's standard minimum wage steps up from $15.10 to $15.70 an hour in 2027, and its direct service and tipped wage moves from $7.55 to $7.85. The full rate gains 60 cents and the lower rate adds 30 cents, a bump of about 4% on each scale. In both years, the direct service and tipped wage sits at half the standard minimum, so the two scales advance in lockstep.

The 60-cent raise adds $1,248 to a full-time year at Maine's standard minimum, based on 2,080 hours of work. A worker paid the direct service and tipped wage collects $624 more from the employer across the same hours. The dollar boost for that group comes to half the standard one, although the percentage climb matches.

The distance between Maine's two hourly scales widens from $7.55 in 2026 to $7.85 in 2027. The wider spread means each hour at the lower wage sits 30 cents further from the standard minimum than in 2026. Maine sets the monthly tip-credit threshold at $199 among its 2027 requirements.

Maine's salary cutoff for certain overtime exemptions reaches $905.79 a week, or $47,101.08 a year, which pulls salaried roles into the 2027 update. Spread across a 40-hour week, the weekly figure works out to about $22.64 an hour, or 1.44 times the new hourly minimum. A full-time employee earning $15.70 for 2,080 hours would gross $32,656 a year, $14,445.08 below the exemption line. An employer paying a salaried worker less than $905.79 a week cannot classify the position as exempt under those provisions.

Maine employers must refresh the state notice to reflect the new figures. A poster listing $15.10 and $7.55 would display pay levels the law no longer permits. With hourly, tipped, and salaried numbers all shifting in 2027, a Maine payroll review that stops at the hourly column would miss at least two required changes.

Minnesota's 90-day training wage for workers under 20 rises from $9.31 to $9.68 in 2027, while the standard statewide minimum climbs from $11.41 to $11.87. The youth tier gains 37 cents, and the standard rate picks up 46 cents. The distance between them stretches from $2.10 to $2.19 an hour. Neither increase reaches half a dollar.

Two conditions confine the lower tier. A worker must be younger than 20, and the reduced pay lasts no longer than a 90-day training stretch. Staff 20 or older fall outside the training wage. A trainee under 20 costs an employer $2.19 less per hour than a coworker on the standard rate, and the discount expires with the 90-day period.

Minneapolis sets its own city rate, which moves from $16.37 to $17.02 on Jan. 1. The city applies that figure to work done in Minneapolis regardless of employer size or employee age. A 19-year-old trainee there earns at least $17.02, or $7.34 more per hour than the state training wage requires. A Minneapolis employer gains nothing on wages by classifying a teenager as a trainee. Inside city limits, the youth discount carries no weight.

Minnesota attaches a paperwork duty to the change. State law requires employers to hand workers an updated written wage notice before any rate change takes effect. Compliance work starts ahead of the first affected payroll. An employer with trainees under 20 faces two adjustments at once, the $9.68 tier and the $11.87 standard, and each worker's notice must show the figure that applies to that person.

The spread between Minneapolis and the state standard grows from $4.96 to $5.15 in 2027. The city adds 65 cents, against 46 cents at the state level. Minimum-wage workers outside cities with their own ordinances fall under the state schedule, and they slip another 19 cents behind their Minneapolis counterparts.

Ohio's hourly minimum climbs from $11 to $11.40 in 2027 for employers that gross more than $419,000 a year. The revenue cutoff decides whether a business owes the state figure at all. No other state entry in this roundup ties its base pay to a company's sales.

The 40-cent raise lifts the base 3.6%. Tipped workers see their cash wage rise from $5.50 to $5.70, a 20-cent gain at the same 3.6% pace. Both the old and new tipped figures equal half the standard rate. Ohio's two pay scales move in step, and the distance between them grows by 20 cents an hour.

Businesses below the $419,000 line in annual gross receipts fall under the federal minimum in most cases. The same holds, as a rule, for workers ages 14 and 15. Two Ohio workers doing identical jobs can earn different minimums depending on their employer's revenue or their own age. The $11.40 headline overstates the floor for part of Ohio's workforce.

Ohio's $11.40 rate sits lowest among the states in this list for 2027, 47 cents behind Minnesota's $11.87 and $2.35 behind Virginia's $13.75. Ohio and Minnesota stand alone on that list below $12. Michigan's new $15 base gives it a $3.60 edge over Ohio. Ohio's 40-cent step trails Virginia's 98-cent increase, and the gap between the two states widens in 2027. Ohio's $5.70 tipped cash wage falls 60 cents below Michigan's $6.30 and $2.15 below Maine's $7.85 direct service and tipped wage. Colorado's $12.69 tipped rate more than doubles Ohio's figure and exceeds Ohio's full standard rate by $1.29.

Ohio's cutoff turns a company's annual receipts into a payroll variable. An employer whose gross receipts cross $419,000 in either direction shifts between the state and federal floors. A firm near that line must watch revenue alongside timesheets to know which hourly minimum applies.

Colorado's statewide minimum wage increases from $15.16 to $15.71 an hour on Jan. 1, 2027, adding 55 cents to the least an employer may pay for an hour of work. The raise works out to about 3.6% over the 2026 level. A full-time worker logging 40 hours a week for 52 weeks would collect $1,144 more before taxes over the year. Colorado tracks inflation for its statewide minimum with the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers.

Tipped employees in Colorado have a separate minimum of $12.69 an hour in 2027. The tipped floor stood at $12.14 an hour in 2026, according to the Colorado Department of Labor and Employment. Tipped workers gain 55 cents, the same raise the standard rate gets, so the distance between the two scales holds at $3.02 in both years. A tipped worker on the new rate starts each shift with about 81% of the base hourly pay before any gratuities arrive, up from about 80% in 2026. Employers with both tipped and non-tipped staff must track two different minimums from the first payroll of 2027.

Boulder outpaces the state, lifting its city minimum from $16.82 to $18.17 on Jan. 1. The $1.35 jump is more than double the statewide gain and leaves Boulder's rate $2.46 above Colorado's. Across a 2,080-hour work year, Boulder's edge over the state minimum comes to $5,116.80 in gross pay. Other Colorado localities set their own minimums by local ordinance as well. Denver measures inflation for its local minimum with a different gauge, the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers.

A Boulder worker at the city minimum earns $18.17 an hour, while a tipped worker on the state schedule earns $12.69, a $5.48 spread inside a single state. Job type and work location together decide which figure applies, and an employer with sites in more than one city may owe several rates at once. The gap between Denver's index and the state's means the two minimums can drift apart in future years even as both follow inflation.

Connecticut workers at the bottom of the pay scale gain 54 cents an hour on Jan. 1, 2027, when the state minimum moves from $16.94 to $17.48. The step amounts to a 3.2% raise over the 2026 level, a modest percentage that compounds into real money across a working year. Payroll systems need the higher amount in place for hours worked from the first day of the year.

A full-time employee logging 40 hours a week collects $21.60 more in gross weekly pay at the new rate. Over 52 weeks, the gap widens to about $1,123 before taxes. Annual gross earnings at the 2027 minimum reach $36,358, compared with $35,235 under the 2026 rate. Part-time staff pocket a smaller slice of the gain, in step with the hours they work. An employer staffing several full-time minimum-wage positions faces the same annual difference for each worker, which turns the change into a budget line as well as a paycheck gain.

Connecticut's adjustment lands in the same cluster of near-half-dollar increases that Arizona, California, Colorado, and New Jersey schedule for 2027. Virginia, Rhode Island, and Michigan plan steeper jumps, each close to or above a full dollar. Against those two groups, Connecticut's 2027 step sits on the measured end, short of the sharper leaps elsewhere. Connecticut has used the Employment Cost Index to escalate its minimum wage. An index-linked approach gives employers a basis for anticipating future changes in the hourly minimum.

At $17.48, Connecticut outpaces California's statewide base by 8 cents and Rhode Island's by 48 cents. Rhode Island's bigger raise leaves it below Connecticut's level, proof that the size of a single year's increase says little about where a state's pay floor ends up. An employer weighing statewide minimums across the guide would face higher entry-level labor costs in Connecticut than anywhere except Washington state.

Michigan lifts its minimum wage from $13.73 to $15 on Jan. 1, 2027, a gain of $1.27 an hour and the biggest dollar jump among the states on this list that show a 2026 rate. Rhode Island and Virginia come closest, adding $1 and 98 cents. Michigan's raise equals 9.2% of its old figure, the steepest percentage climb in the same group.

Michigan's new floor falls short of Arizona, Maine, and Colorado, each of which lands above $15.60 in 2027. California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Washington state each reach $17 or more. The $15 figure matches Alaska's 2027 rate, which arrives July 1. Michigan widens its margin over Virginia, Minnesota, and Ohio, the three states here with 2027 minimums under $14. Michigan's move narrows the distance to the higher-paying states without catching them.

Tipped workers in Michigan see their guaranteed cash wage rise from $5.49 to $6.30 on the same date, an 81-cent bump. The increase equals 14.8% of the old figure, outpacing the standard minimum's growth. The tipped amount reaches 42% of the full minimum, up from 40% in 2026.

Michigan's tipped cash wage sits between the tipped figures in two other states on this list. Ohio sets its tipped wage at $5.70 in 2027, and Maine sets its direct service and tipped wage at $7.85. Each of those amounts equals half of its state's standard minimum, a larger share than Michigan's 42%. Colorado's $12.69 tipped rate equals 81% of its $15.71 base. Michigan leaves a larger slice of a tipped worker's guaranteed pay to customers' tips than any of those three states.

Michigan employers absorb both changes on one date. The base and tipped figures each take effect Jan. 1, 2027, which leaves payroll teams no phased rollout to stagger the update.

The dollar gap between Michigan's two pay floors widens as the ratio improves. Tipped workers sat $8.24 below the standard minimum in 2026 and will sit $8.70 below it in 2027. Michigan's tipped workforce gains ground in proportional terms yet slips another 46 cents behind in raw dollars.

Most New Jersey workers will earn at least $16.48 an hour in 2027, up from $15.92 in 2026. The 56-cent gain sets the baseline for anyone whose job sits outside the state's special pay categories.

Seasonal and small employers, agricultural workers, and tipped workers each follow a separate 2027 scale. A farmhand, a restaurant server, or a clerk at a small or seasonal shop cannot assume the general number marks their own minimum. Each of those groups answers to its own figure.

Two of the carve-outs hinge on the business, and two hinge on the job. A seasonal or small company qualifies through its size or its calendar, while a field laborer or server qualifies through the work they do. Payroll staff have to examine both the employer and the individual position before settling on a figure. A tipped server at a seasonal restaurant matches two descriptions at once.

Employers must sort staff into the correct group before they set 2027 pay. New Jersey businesses should confirm which category applies to each person on the books. A company that files a tipped employee or a seasonal hire under the general track risks miscalculating that person's earnings.

People in the special groups have reason to check their own classification too. The headline figure tells a server or a farm crew member little about what an employer owes for a given shift.

New Jersey's system turns one statewide change into four tracks. A single number for most workers leaves farm, tipped, seasonal, and small-employer staff without a ready answer about their own minimum.

Rhode Island's minimum wage climbs a full dollar on Jan. 1, 2027, moving from $16 to $17 an hour.

The $1 step amounts to a 6.25% raise for anyone paid the state minimum. Among the 11 states in this group with both a 2026 and a 2027 figure on record, only Michigan delivers a bigger dollar gain. Virginia trails Rhode Island at 98 cents. In percentage terms, Virginia's 7.67% and Michigan's 9.25% both outpace the Rhode Island increase, since each starts from a lower base. The remaining eight states advance by 40 to 60 cents. Rhode Island's lowest earners collect close to double the typical bump in those eight.

The split between the dollar and percentage standings shows how much a starting point shapes a pay hike. A Virginia worker gains more in proportional terms, yet the Rhode Island worker pockets more cash for every hour worked.

A full-time employee logging 40 hours a week at $17 earns $40 more in gross pay each week than at $16. Across 52 weeks of such shifts, the added income reaches $2,080.

Three of the 12 states here set a higher floor than Rhode Island's $17: Washington, Connecticut, and California. Base-rate workers in Rhode Island out-earn their counterparts in the other eight. The distance to Connecticut shrinks from 94 cents in 2026 to 48 cents in 2027, a cut of about half in one year. California's lead narrows from 90 cents to 40 cents. Washington's $17.73, the steepest statewide requirement in the group, still sits 73 cents above Rhode Island. An employer staffing locations in both Rhode Island and Connecticut now budgets around a much slimmer difference in base pay.

Rhode Island's edge over New Jersey's pay for most workers widens from eight cents in 2026 to 52 cents in 2027. Rhode Island opens 2027 closer to the top of this group than to its midpoint, which falls between Colorado's $15.71 and Maine's $15.70. The gaps above Rhode Island are closing as the gaps below open up. The state is moving away from the middle of the pack and toward the costliest tier for entry-level labor.

Virginia's minimum wage climbs from $12.77 to $13.75 on Jan. 1, 2027. The 98-cent gain amounts to a 7.7% raise for Virginians earning the legal minimum.

Virginia posts the second-steepest percentage jump among the five states that pair a 2026 figure with a stated Jan. 1, 2027 start date. Only Michigan outpaces it on that measure, and Colorado, Connecticut, and Rhode Island all trail. In raw dollars, Virginia's increase falls short of the hikes in Michigan and Rhode Island. The two measures diverge because Virginia starts from the smallest 2026 base in the group, so a modest dollar amount translates into a larger share of the old wage.

Virginia's $13.75 remains the lowest 2027 figure in the January group, a set that includes Washington state, despite the boost. The sharp climb trims Virginia's deficit without erasing it.

A full-time employee on a 40-hour week collects $39.20 more in gross pay each week at the new rate. Over 52 weeks, the extra money totals $2,038.40 and lifts yearly gross earnings at the 2027 minimum to $28,600. The annual figures assume steady 40-hour weeks, and workers on part-time or irregular schedules capture a smaller slice.

Virginia's raise narrows the hourly gap with two of its January peers. Colorado's edge over Virginia drops from $2.39 to $1.96 an hour, and Connecticut's shrinks from $4.17 to $3.73. Michigan moves the other way and stretches its margin from 96 cents to $1.25. Rhode Island's lead inches from $3.23 to $3.25. The split tracks the size of each state's hike. Virginia gains ground on states adding less than a dollar and loses ground to the two that add a dollar or more.

Outside the January group, Virginia's new rate clears the 2027 statewide minimums in Ohio and Minnesota. The comparison leaves Virginia between two tiers, above the two states with the thinnest 2027 floors on this list and below every peer that shares its Jan. 1 start.

Washington state requires employers to pay at least $17.73 an hour starting Jan. 1, 2027, the steepest statewide base among the 12 states in this selection. The figure edges past the 2027 rates in Connecticut, California, and Rhode Island, the only other states here that clear $17. Connecticut comes closest, trailing by 25 cents an hour. California trails by 33 cents and Rhode Island by 73 cents. A full-time worker logging 40 hours at the Washington rate grosses $709.20 a week before taxes. Over a full year of 40-hour weeks, that pay comes to $36,878.40.

Teens ages 14 and 15 fall under a separate youth wage of $15.07 an hour. The gap between the two scales comes to $2.66, which leaves younger workers at 85% of the standard rate. At 40 hours, the difference adds up to $106.40 a week. Across a year of full-time work, the gap grows to $5,532.80. The youth bracket spans two ages, a brief stretch on the lower scale.

Washington's discounted teen wage outstrips the full statewide minimum in four other states on this list: Michigan, Minnesota, Ohio, and Virginia. A 14-year-old in Washington state starts above the legal floor an adult faces in any of those four. Michigan comes closest, falling 7 cents short of the Washington teen rate. Ohio's base trails the Washington youth wage by $3.67 an hour, the widest gap among the four.

Seattle, SeaTac, Tukwila, Renton, Bellingham, Everett, Burien, and unincorporated King County each set a minimum wage above the state's. Inside those jurisdictions, the statewide figure becomes the bottom rung of a taller ladder. An employer with locations in several of those places may owe more than one hourly rate inside a single state. The distance between Washington's statewide figure and Seattle's city rate reaches $4.41 an hour. Payroll teams in that position need to track where each shift happens.

Seattle's city minimum wage climbs from $21.30 to $22.14 on Jan. 1 for every employer. The 84-cent step works out to a 3.9% raise. No other state or local rate scheduled for 2027 in this roundup reaches $22.14. West Hollywood, California, comes closest with $20.94 for non-hotel employees, still $1.20 short of Seattle.

Seattle's single rate contrasts with cities that tier their minimums by size. Hayward, California, splits its January pay scale at 25 employees. Firms with 26 or more owe $18.39, and smaller ones owe $17.40. Seattle draws no such line. A five-person shop pays the same hourly minimum as the city's largest employer.

Seattle's ordinance follows the work itself. Any hours an employee logs inside city limits earn the city rate, whatever the size of the business behind the paycheck. Seattle's floor runs $4.41 an hour above Washington state's 2027 minimum. Across a 40-hour week, the gap comes to $176.40 in gross pay. Seattle belongs to a cluster of Washington jurisdictions with local minimums above the state's, a list that also includes SeaTac, Tukwila, Renton, Bellingham, Everett, Burien, and unincorporated King County. A worker who splits shifts between Seattle and a town without its own ordinance would face two different minimums in the same week.

Small Seattle employers have gone without two pay offsets since 2025. Neither tips nor medical-benefit payments count toward the city's minimum-compensation requirement at those businesses. Gratuities a server collects at a small cafe land on top of the $22.14 base. An owner's medical-benefit payments cannot trim the required hourly pay either. At those firms, base pay alone must absorb the January step-up.

Seattle will issue a revised workplace poster for 2027. Employers will need to replace the current notice once the new version appears. Seattle's smallest payrolls absorb the highest hourly floor in the 2027 lineup at full strength, with no tip or benefit credit to cushion it.

Saint Paul's city minimum wage opens 2027 at $17.02 an hour for employers with 101 or more workers, and smaller businesses pay less under a schedule keyed to headcount. The city sorts employers into four size categories. Macro and large businesses share a single rate on the schedule, and both groups owe the top figure from the first day of the year.

Small employers, those with six to 100 workers, owe $16.37 an hour on Jan. 1, 2027. Micro employers, with five or fewer people on staff, owe $14.25. The spread between the biggest and smallest employers reaches $2.77 an hour in January. The middle tier trails the top by 65 cents an hour, a difference that separates paychecks at a 50-person shop and a 150-person company.

Small employers catch up on July 1, 2027, when their minimum rises to $17.02 and matches the top tier. Micro employers move to $15 the same day, a 75-cent gain. Macro and large employers hold at $17.02 through the midyear change. Three pay levels shrink to two, and the micro category keeps the only lower rate, now $2.02 an hour below everyone else.

Saint Paul's headcount rule decides which tier applies, and it extends past full-time payroll. Employers must count every worker, including part-time, temporary, jointly employed, and remote staff. A business with five full-time employees and one part-timer counts six workers, which puts it in the small tier. Crossing that line in January lifts the business's required minimum by $2.12 an hour.

The city has refreshed its Wage Theft, Minimum Wage, and Earned Sick and Safe Time materials. Employers face two effective dates in a single year, and each one calls for a review of the pay schedule and the employee postings. A Saint Paul owner who checks the numbers in January and never again will shortchange small-tier workers by July, when their required pay hits the top-tier level.

Petaluma's city minimum wage climbs from $18.31 to $18.93 an hour on Jan. 1, a gain of 62 cents for every hour on the clock. The 3.4% raise rests on a city law that stacks above California's statewide minimum.

California's 2027 base of $17.40 trails Petaluma's new rate by $1.53 an hour. The state's own move from $16.90 amounts to an increase of under 3%, a slower pace than the city's. As a result, Petaluma's lead over the state grows from $1.41 in 2026.

Petaluma's 2027 figure lands in the middle of the California city schedules taking effect on Jan. 1. San Diego reaches $18.35, Hayward sets $18.39 for employers with 26 or more workers, and Burlingame moves to $18.47. Palo Alto and Santa Clara both arrive at $19.25, and West Hollywood demands $20.94 for non-hotel employees.

Petaluma's 62-cent step mirrors what its California peers add. San Diego tacks on 60 cents, Burlingame 61 cents, and Palo Alto 55 cents. West Hollywood's non-hotel figure rises 69 cents. Measured against each city's 2026 rate, San Diego, Burlingame, and West Hollywood all advance about 3.4%, in line with Petaluma. Palo Alto stands apart at under 3%. The matching percentages put these four cities on parallel tracks, though their 2026 starting points sat $2.50 apart, from San Diego's $17.75 to West Hollywood's $20.25.

California's local ordinances differ on who qualifies for the higher pay. Santa Clara extends its minimum to employees who work at least two hours a week in the city, and Palo Alto applies a similar two-hour test in most cases. Hayward sorts businesses by headcount and charges employers with 25 or fewer workers $17.40, the same amount the state requires. Petaluma's own rules may diverge on those points as well. A business with staff in Petaluma and in areas that follow only the state minimum will run two pay scales in 2027.
