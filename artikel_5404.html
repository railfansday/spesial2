Canada's Carney will raise Chinese EV manufacturing with Xi at APEC

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The prime minister said Chinese investment depends on several factors; no joint ventures under January's trade pact have been announced

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said electric vehicle manufacturing will be on the agenda for his Nov. 17 meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping, according to Bloomberg. Carney cautioned that vehicles from Chinese brands are unlikely to be built on Canadian soil anytime soon.

"The extent to which there's Chinese investment depends on a variety of factors," Carney said, according to Bloomberg. "It will be one of the discussions with President Xi." He added, without elaborating, that the question of Chinese EVs bears on Canada's continuing negotiations with Washington.

Carney spoke on Thursday at the European Political Community summit in Dublin, which kicked off a journey that will also take him to the Philippines, Cambodia and China. He plans to meet Xi after arriving in Shenzhen for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation leaders' summit, according to Bloomberg.

Carney also said European automakers could become partners for Canada's auto industry, a sector geared toward shipping vehicles to the U.S. that is having trouble adjusting to the tariffs President Donald Trump has imposed. "I think you will see more European partnerships coming out of this relationship," he said, according to Bloomberg.

In January, Carney unveiled an agreement in Beijing under which China lowered its duties on farm goods from Canada, according to Bloomberg. Canada in turn opened a quota of up to 49,000 Chinese-made EVs for a 12-month period, with a levy of roughly 6% applied to those vehicles. Vehicles above the quota face an additional 100% surtax.

The deal also aimed to attract fresh joint ventures backed by Chinese firms to Canada's car-making sector within three years, and it drew objections from members of the Trump administration, including Trump.

Industry Minister Mélanie Joly has said she is seeking joint ventures that would expand domestic parts production, employment and technological capacity in Canada. None has been announced, according to Bloomberg.

Stellantis $STLA sat down with Joly's office to explore producing EVs built on Chinese technology at an idle facility outside Toronto. The government refused to accept a setup where most components would be made in China and merely put together in Canada, and negotiations then stalled, according to Bloomberg. Stellantis now intends to offload the facility to Roshel, a Canadian maker of armored vehicles.

Carney said the Roshel sale is a "partial" solution and that he wants Stellantis to honor the commitments it made in its contracts with the plant's unionized workers. The plant has turned out no vehicles for roughly three years. Joly is set to meet Stellantis officials and the union in Toronto on Thursday.

Chinese automakers are already preparing to enter the Canadian market. Geely Auto announced plans to begin selling vehicles in Canada in 2027. BYD is planning 20 Canadian dealerships, starting in Toronto, and Chery Automobile is setting up distribution.
