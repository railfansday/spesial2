Everyone on the internet loves to overreact to things, and that's especially true when we're talking about the NFL. Personally, I overreact to everything. With that in mind, Zach Pereles decided to take a look at several things that happened during Week 8, so decide for yourself if we're all overreacting.

Statement: Vikings should bench Kyler Murray.Overreaction or reality: Reality. I gave Murray a lot of grace early this year. He was on a new team and in a new system. He suffered a concussion moments into his Vikings debut. It was about as inauspicious a start as possible, and even as he struggled after he returned, I was willing to give him some time to figure it out. That time is over. Murray was dreadful again Sunday. Murray hasn't been close to good enough for a team with a championship-level defense, and Minnesota simply can't keep the status quo much longer.

Statement: Colts should trade Jonathan Taylor and fire Shane Steichen.Overreaction or reality: Reality. Not trading Taylor would be a massive mistake. Indianapolis is facing a multi-year rebuild. They have no competent quarterback and likely no way to find one in the draft due to the Sauce Gardner trade. Taylor is reportedly commanding significant offers. The Vikings would be a great fit, as their running game continues to stink. Indianapolis must move their star running back, and it must move on from Steichen, too. If not, it's a damning mismanagement by Carlie Irsay-Gordon at an absolutely critical junction early in her ownership.

Statement: Buccaneers will win the NFC South. Overreaction or reality: Reality. Baker Mayfield did not complete a single pass in the Buccaneers' 16-0 win over the Falcons on Sunday, a game played in an absolute downpour.  Mayfield only attempted three passes. The week before, he only attempted 11 (completing seven) in a 43-13 domination of the Panthers. Tampa Bay ran for 258 yards in that game. The Buccaneers cannot win this way forever, clearly. But they don't have to be that far off.  It's not like the bar is that high in the NFC South, and the Buccaneers' defense is the best single unit in the division. If Mayfield can avoid making multiple back-breaking mistakes per game, the Buccaneers will join the 1992 Chargers as the only teams to make the postseason after starting 0-4.

The NFL gave us one of the worst Sunday night games ever in Week 8, but the league is more than making up for that fact by giving us an NFC showdown in Seattle tonight.

This is a huge game for both teams. If the Seahawks win, they'll move up from eighth to seventh in the playoff standings and the Bears will drop from sixth to eighth, putting them on the outside of the playoffs heading into Week 9. If the Bears win, they'll move up to fifth in the NFC while the Seahawks will stay put at eighth. This will also give the winning team a key head-to-head tiebreaker if these two teams end up battling for a wild card spot with the same record at the end of the season.

In terms of injuries, the Bears will be without Kyle Monangai, who will miss his fourth straight game. On the Seahawks' end, starting left tackle Charles Cross has already been ruled out. Rashid Shaheed is also out of the game, and the Seahawks might not have starting linebacker Uchenna Nwosu, who has been listed as doubtful.
