Kirk Herbstreit Distances Himself From Viral Video Mocking ESPN, CFP

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Kirk Herbstreit has no relationship with a comedy group that went viral Monday with a video mocking the College Football Playoff and ESPN's show presenting its rankings.

The College GameDay host and longtime ESPN analyst briefly appears near the end of the "SEC Shorts" video, delivering an ad read for Renasant Bank, an SEC Shorts sponsor.

Captioned "Embattled CFB Committee releases new commercial," the video racked up more than 2 million views and thousands of replies in less than nine hours. It stars SEC Shorts co-creator Robert Clay, who presents "Old Men in a Room" as a solution to identifying the 12 college football teams most worthy of competing in the College Football Playoff.

"I have a relationship with Renasant and they use me on all of SEC Shorts' weekly clips," Herbstreit told FOS in an email Monday. "But I'm not involved in their content and hope folks don't think I'm involved with their production." He added that he does not have a partnership with SEC Shorts.

Midway through the roughly two-and-a-half-minute sketch, Clay describes how "Talking Head Lady on TV" will repeat the Old Men's "nonsensical rankings verbatim with increased confidence and enthusiastic hand gestures." While his voiceover plays, fellow SEC Shorts cast member Chelsi Davis stands before a mocked-up ESPN studio set.

"While Tennessee did beat Alabama and LSU, the Committee felt that Alabama was the better team based on how they would have performed had they beaten Tennessee using their new metric this year," Davis says—a clear send-up of ESPN senior writer Heather Dinich, who is an analyst on the network's rankings show and has been ripped in some corners in recent days for her reporting on the CFP selection committee's new "strength of conference" metric.

Dinich declined to comment, though she appeared to reference the video Monday, writing that those "hanging onto every word I say" should preorder her forthcoming book.

Herbstreit later appears in the final seconds of the video, saying "and this episode is brought to you by Renasant Bank."

The College GameDay host has a longstanding partnership with the Mississippi-based bank, which operates across much of the South and is the presenting sponsor of all SEC Shorts videos.

An ESPN spokesperson declined to comment.
