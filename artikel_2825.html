Predicting how the NFC North will finish with 4 great teams

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

It's a really bad year to be a good team in the NFC North. While the majority of the NFL's divisions have settled into a football bell curve of the good, fine, and bad — the North is stacked with four of the league's best teams fighting over three playoff spots down the stretch. Someone is going to win, cementing themselves as a true contender in the NFC, two teams will likely secure wild-card spots, becoming the most dangerous non-winners in the playoffs, and one team is going to finish with a 10+ win record and have nothing to show for it.

We're hurtling towards another season where one fanbase decries the NFL's playoff format. The only question that remains is: Who? The Bears, Lions, Packers, and Vikings all have incredible characteristics in their own right, but diving into trends, remaining schedule, and future potential, there's a likely way we're going to see this play out.

The Bears are so much fun to watch. Boasting a Top 10 offense and defense in scoring/scoring allowed, the complete revitalization of this football team by Ben Johnson is almost complete in just his second year at the helm.

Caleb Williams took another step forward in his development this season, though it hasn't always been perfect. A hamstring injury forced him to miss three games, and his overall performance has been excellent on paper, but there's a key element to his game that's still missing: Red Zone efficiency. Moving the football between the 20s hasn't been a problem for Williams this season, but it's clear that Johnson still doesn't fully trust his young QB in short-field situations, favoring a shift to the run game over asking him to make tight throws.

This has resulted in him being on pace for an injury-adjusted 23 passing touchdowns this season, which puts him around 16th in the NFL in passing TDs, much closer to the Bo Nix/Geno Smith caliber of QB than the league's elite. Bears fans shouldn't take this as a knock on Williams, but rather an exciting development for the future. We are still yet to see Caleb be fully unlocked, and he's already on pace to lead this team to the playoffs. I have no doubt the Bears coaching staff can round out his game and make him even better.

When it comes to the remaining schedule, the Bears boast the toughest remaining slate in the NFC North. They have enough ability to win the majority of their remaining games, but not quite enough to take the division outright. The x-factor down the stretch will be Jeffrey Simmons. One of the best acquisitions around the trade deadline, Chicago got a massive boost to their defense with the interior pass rusher, who has been fantastic since his arrival. If Simmons is capable of disrupting key drives then there's a real chance the Bears could flip another loss to a win down the stretch.

"Undeniable" is the best adjective for the Lions offense this season. Working off the inverse of most offensive principles, Detroit uses Jared Goff as their chain-mover and red zone scoring threat, while Jahmyr Gibbs' rushing ability delivers the explosive plays. This has been incredibly difficult for opponents to deal with, as the Lions are poised to be the only team in the NFL to finish with a QB over 4,500 yards passing and a running back with over 1,500 yards rushing,

If you can sense the "but" coming, you'd be right. Detroit's offense has been incredible, but the defense is another story entirely. The injury bug has bitten this team big time, with its two most important players all out for the remainder of the season in LB Jack Campbell, and now DE Aidan Hutchinson, who hurt himself in Week 10.

Before these injuries, a banged-up secondary was struggling, meaning things are only going to get worse for Detroit defensively from here. This team ranks 15th in the NFL in points allowed and 28th against the pass. These are scary metrics for a team poised to hit the playoffs and face some of the league's best quarterbacks. The saving grace here is that the remaining schedule looks great for them, with only the Vikings and Bears looking like real challenges at this point.

This is a team that will get a high wild card seed, likely win a playoff game, but major questions remain about whether their defense has enough to stop the best teams in the league.

It's been a weird year for the Green Bay Packers. Full of ups and downs, the Packers have shown they can rise to beat some of the NFL's best teams, but also lose inexplicably to the likes of the Falcons, and be pushed to overtime by the Jets. This leaves us with a team that is very good, but just feels a step behind everyone else in the NFC North right now.

Jordan Love and the Packers' passing game are fascinatingly weird. This is a unit that never makes things easy on themselves, opting to generate as many explosive deep passes through the air as possible, in a very feast-or-famine playstyle. At times this has been incredible, typified by their blowout win over the Bears in Week 5 — but also looked sophomoric and unsophisticated against the Vikings in Week 10.

When we get down to brass tacks, there are just too many points of failure for Green Bay this season. The running game has been atrocious, the defense has been middling, and above all else it's tough to discern what the identity of this football team is. They're good at a lot of things, but not great at anything in particular. That's ultimately what will put them on the outside looking in when the playoffs roll around, especially in light of a difficult schedule down the stretch.

It's impossible to heap any more praise on the Minnesota defense. Watch this team play one game and you immediately understand how special this unit is. Defensive coordinator Brian Flores has steadily improved his players from the moment he arrived, with 2026 being the crescendo of his brilliance — orchestrating a group of guys who aren't household names, and transforming them into a group of monsters.

There have been some standout performances in Dallas Turner and Andrew Van Ginkel, but overall this isn't a team you'd characterize as being full of elite talent on defense. The only thing holding them back was an absolutely putrid offense, which was led by two horrible quarterbacks in Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz.

That all changed at the NFL trade deadline when the Vikings re-acquired Kirk Cousins for their playoff push. The impact was immediately noticeable, with Cousins being simply good enough to allow the defense to swallow the Packers, and ultimately deliver the win.

Minnesota is in an incredible spot right now where they not only have momentum behind them, but the easiest remaining schedule in the NFC North. The only game they're not favored in to close out the season is against the 49ers, which could quite easily be a win, depending on how the defense is able to stop Brock Purdy's quick passing game.

It's beginning to feel like the Vikings' division to lose, with the stars aligning for them. As for the playoffs? We'll need to wait and see. As brilliant as this team could be, there's also a weight of baggage and history pressuring them to perform. It's unclear if this team is ready to rise to the moment.
