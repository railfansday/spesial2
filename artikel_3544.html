Details Emerge About the Sudden Death of ESPN Legend Tom Jackson at 75

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

New details have emerged about the death of ESPN broadcaster and former NFL player Tom Jackson after the sudden loss, which friends have described as a “shock.”

Jackson was pronounced dead at 4:15 p.m. on Friday, November 20, at Christ Hospital in Cincinnati, the Hamilton County Coroner’s Office confirmed to Us on Monday, November 23. He was 75.

An autopsy was performed on Monday, and results are still pending. Jackson’s cause of death remains undetermined.

Jackson’s former ESPN colleague Chris Berman said he had spoken “at length” with Jackson on November 17, three days before his death.

“All of us are stunned,” Berman said in a statement released by ESPN on Sunday, November 22. “I’m stunned, we’re stunned and you’re stunned. … I was fortunate to work side by side with Tom for 30 years, primarily on NFL Primetime and Sunday NFL Countdown. It was a labor of love for our whole team, both in front of the camera and behind it. We always hoped our viewers enjoyed the shows as much as we did bringing it to them every week.”

Berman added, “Tom was so much more than an accomplished NFL analyst. There was nobody kinder, more thoughtful, more loyal or more engaging than him. And when it came to laughter, Tom’s was frequent and unmistakable. He is easily summed up by this classic old song: ‘He’s got sunshine on a cloudy day.’”

Jackson’s former Denver Broncos teammate Bob Swenson called his death “unexpected.”

“I talked to him every week, sometimes every day,” Swenson, 73, told the Denver Gazette’s Chris Tomasson on Sunday. “I talked to him, and he was going back and forth about getting a back operation, and then he said he was going to do it. Then I texted him, but I wasn’t getting a response the last couple of days.”

Swenson added, “He just said he was going to do the back surgery and he had some things going on with his leg. I was just waiting to hear back. I texted him, ‘Are you OK?’ I think I texted him on Thursday and Friday and no response. … I was just stunned.”

Before his broadcasting career, Jackson was a part of the Broncos’ iconic “Orange Crush” defense, which helped lead Denver to Super Bowl appearances 1978 and 1987. Jackson played all 14 of his NFL seasons with the Broncos and was named a First-Team All-Pro in 1977 and a three-time Pro Bowler in three consecutive seasons from 1977 to 1979.

Jackson is survived by his wife, Jennifer, and his two daughters, Taylor and Morgan.
