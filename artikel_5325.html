Iraq is challenging OPEC+'s assessment of its oil production capacity

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Baghdad disputes a consultants' study that put its maximum at under 5 million barrels a day ahead of Sunday's OPEC+ meeting

Iraq has turned down OPEC+'s commissioned review of how much oil it can produce, according to Bloomberg. The dispute could spark a confrontation at the group's online meeting on Sunday, Nov 29., where OPEC and its allies, under Saudi leadership, plan to rely on the findings to set production targets.

Baghdad is contesting a study by outside consultants, who put its maximum capacity at less than 5 million barrels a day, according to Bloomberg. Oil Minister Basim Mohammed Khudair has written to OPEC to insist that Iraq can reach 5.2 million barrels a day.

Under the review, a country given a bigger capacity figure has a firmer basis for seeking a larger output allowance, according to Bloomberg. Iraq's oil ministry and OPEC's secretariat in Vienna had not responded to requests for comment from Bloomberg as of its publication.

This year, Iraq warned it could exit OPEC unless the assessment credited it with a sufficiently large number, according to Bloomberg. Iraq later withdrew the warning. The warning came weeks after the United Arab Emirates' exit from the group, and the UAE has since pushed its production to record levels.

Iraq has bristled at OPEC+ quotas for 10 years, wanting to boost its earnings and restore an economy hurt by war and sanctions, according to Bloomberg.

The two sides have also differed on how much Iraq is producing. Iraq told OPEC its October output came to nearly 4.9 million barrels daily, the highest of the wartime period and far over its quota, according to Bloomberg. Figures that OPEC draws from outside sources put the number at 4.491 million barrels a day.

OPEC and its allies have been examining the volumes each member can technically produce as groundwork for 2027 output targets.

Separately, the International Energy Agency put Iraq's sustainable capacity at 4.87 million barrels a day in its monthly oil market report this month. The agency estimated Iraq's October output at 4.6 million barrels a day, the highest since September 2019.
