Rams ride Blake Corum to Thanksgiving Eve win over Packers; Jordan Love throws 2 INTs with Christian Watson out

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

On his 26th birthday and in the NFL's first Thanksgiving Eve game, Los Angeles Rams running back Blake Corum starred with 184 rushing yards during a 26-13 win over the Green Bay Packers at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, California.

Corum became the first Rams player to carry the ball at least 30 times in a game during the Sean McVay era. Meanwhile, the Packers' offense floundered after losing wide receiver Christian Watson to a concussion late in the first half.

Green Bay quarterback Jordan Love threw a pair of interceptions and could have had a couple more. His team mustered a mere 55 total yards of offense in the second half.

McVay, previously 0-5 against Packers head coach Matt LaFleur, finally beat his former assistant. The Rams (7-4) have won five straight games. Green Bay (6-5) remains last in a crowded NFC North.

Packers edge rusher Micah Parsons, playing in his first game in nearly a year after tearing an ACL and meniscus in his left knee against the Denver Broncos in Week 15 last season, recorded a long-awaited sack.

Watson left the game twice: first with a thigh injury after making a 6-yard catch on the opening play from scrimmage and then for the concussion caused by a massive hit near the sideline late in the second quarter for which Rams safety Kamren Kinchens was ejected.

Forging forward without wide receivers Puka Nacua — who is reportedly out for the season after undergoing MCL surgery — and Davante Adams — who was still sidelined by a hamstring injury — the Rams managed to stake themselves to a 13-3 advantage, scoring on their first three possessions.

Stafford, who finished with 140 yards on 16-of-23 passing, made do with his lesser-known receivers. A 37-yard defensive pass interference penalty catapulted L.A.'s first drive. That ended with a 43-yard Brandon McManus field goal. McManus trotted back out to kick a 41-yarder following a Rams takeaway later in the opening frame. L.A. linebacker Nate Landman, forcing his eighth regular-season fumble in the past two seasons, punched the ball free from the grasp of Packers tight end Tucker Kraft.

A 32-yard connection between Love and Watson put the wheels in motion for Green Bay. Rookie kicker Trey Smack, who came in with just 15 makes on 21 field-goal attempts, got the Packers on the board with a 54-yard kick.

That's when Stafford answered with the kind of efficiency that made him the league MVP last season. He went 5 of 6, at one point keeping the series alive with a clutch dump-off to running back Kyren Williams despite duress on third-and-5. It wasn't long before Williams was surging through the middle for a 7-yard touchdown run to make it a two-score game.

A pair of highlight-reel plays thrust Green Bay right back into the thick of things. LaFleur turned to trickery initially, with wideout Savion Williams taking a handoff and throwing the ball back to Love, who went on to fling a pass downfield to Watson. His toe-tapping 21-yard reception was followed by a 39-yard catch and run from another receiver, Matthew Golden.

Watson was the one who found pay dirt, reeling in a 6-yard touchdown pass from Love midway through the second quarter. That score cut the Packers' deficit to 13-10.

Watson's night came to a premature end on Green Bay's next drive. LaFleur rolled the dice on fourth-and-1 from the L.A. 30-yard line, and it originally looked like that decision was going to be rewarded. Love located Watson for what would have been a chains-moving pickup, except the pass was broken up by a brutal blow from Kinchens near the sideline that leveled Watson.

Kinchens was ejected because, per the Netflix broadcast, he left his feet and made forcible contact to the head/neck area with his helmet.

Offsetting penalties — Golden was flagged for offensive pass interference — brought up another fourth-and-1. That time, Smack was sent out for a 48-yard field-goal attempt.

With that boot, it was 13-13. It remained that way heading into the break.

Wednesday was a game of attrition. Not only did the Packers lose Watson, but the Rams watched a pair of tight ends — Davis Allen (ankle) and Tyler Higbee (chest) — go out, along with left guard Bill Murray (ankle).

Even so, L.A.'s offense kept churning. It relied heavily on Corum. The ascending third-year pro from Michigan carried the rock nine times on a 14-play, eight-plus-minute drive that began the third quarter. Corum scored from a yard out to restore the Rams' lead.

While dominating possession, the Rams eventually established a cushion — that was after Stafford and Love traded interceptions. The latter took place in L.A. territory, as miscommunication between Love and Golden cost the Packers dearly.

A 35-yard Corum run set the stage for the third of McManus' four field goals.

Although Love converted a fourth-and-8 with a strike to wideout Bo Melton down the stretch, he tossed his second pick the next snap. Rams cornerback Emmanuel Forbes Jr. dropped deep into coverage and climbed the ladder to force the Packers' third turnover.

L.A.'s roster may be depleted, but McVay's group is overcoming that cascade of injuries with toughness. The Rams have now rushed for at least 200 yards in back-to-back games.

"Our identity going into this game was the most physical team's gonna win," Corum said during his on-field postgame interview. "And I think we showed that today. It's something to build on."
