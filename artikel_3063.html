After Plummeting 62% This Year, Could Buying Intel Stock Today Set You Up for Life?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Intel now sells at a considerable discount from its high, but setting small investors up for life could be a challenge.

By Will Healy – Nov 21, 2026 at 10:30AM EST

Few stocks in the semiconductor sector have experienced a more dramatic fall this year than Intel (INTC -7.97%). Since reaching a peak closing price of nearly $141 per share in June, the stock has lost 62% of its value.

This has attracted bargain hunters who perceived Intel as a stock to own, and some may wonder whether Intel can set investors for life. Although Intel could beat the market over time, investors should not assume that a modest investment in the stock will bring financial independence. Here's why.

The prospect for Intel's return to prominence has dramatically improved under Lip-Bu Tan. The former Cadence Design Systems CEO has begun leveraging Intel's existing competitive advantages to compete in today's semiconductor industry.

In short, Tan is forging ahead where the previous Intel CEO, Pat Gelsinger, fell short. Intel has finally succeeded with its longtime plan to master advanced chip production with its 18A manufacturing process node.

This has allowed it to produce chips smaller than 2nm in the U.S., making it more competitive with the industry's dominant manufacturer, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSMC). That move could signal a revival in U.S. chip manufacturing.

Moreover, Fortune Business Insights forecasts that the artificial intelligence (AI) chip market will grow at a 30% compound annual rate through 2034, positioning Intel in a lucrative growth market.

Additionally, during Gelsinger's tenure, Intel opened its production capacity to third-party manufacturers. The foundry continues to flourish, attracting business from companies like Microsoft and Amazon's AWS. It has also made a deal with Tesla, with its upcoming 14A process node slated for production beginning in the second half of 2027.

Although these improvements could make Intel a market-beating stock, the law of large numbers hampers Intel's ability to make small investors wealthy.

When investors read stories about $5,000 investments turning into millions, that is usually a case of a small- or mid-cap stock eventually becoming one of America's largest companies.

Unfortunately for investors who want Intel to set them up for life, Intel remained in large-cap territory even at the height of its more recent troubles.

Also, despite its recent decline, its market capitalization is just over $290 billion. That places it among the 50 largest companies trading on U.S. markets today. Doubling that four times would take Intel's market value close to that of today's market cap leader, Apple, at $5.2 trillion. Still, while doubling four times is an impressive return, it would take a $5,000 investment to about $80,000, not enough to set one up for life.

Furthermore, despite optimism about its future, analysts project 17% revenue growth this year, then 12% in 2027. That is lower than the aforementioned CAGR, suggesting it may take some time for Intel's AI-driven growth to reach its full potential.

Achieving that potential will likely take time, as competing in these markets requires considerable capital expenditures (capex). Over the trailing 12 months alone, Intel's capex was $16 billion.

Although free cash flow remains positive for now, such spending likely contributed to Intel's Q3 warnings about negative free cash flow in future quarters.

Amid the considerable demand for AI chips, Intel could overcome these challenges. Moreover, even though its P/E ratio reflects profitability that is too low to serve as a meaningful valuation measure, the forward P/E ratio of 37 suggests Intel stock now trades at a reasonable price.

Nonetheless, even if Intel ultimately brings investors closer to being set for life, its current financial condition indicates the process will take longer.

Given Intel's massive size, the company's stock is unlikely to set a small investor up for life. Still, it offers the possibility of making considerable progress toward that goal -- if one has patience.

Intel's technical breakthroughs appear to have given TSMC a viable competitor and a path to again capitalize on the cutting edge of semiconductor technology.

Unfortunately for investors, Intel's market cap indicates it has grown too large to set up a small investor for life.

Investors can now buy Intel at an increasingly attractive valuation, and amid its growth opportunities, Intel's stock price could be much higher after one year. However, investors who buy this stock should not expect an investment in Intel alone to make them financially independent.
