CME vs. Interactive Brokers Group: Which Financial Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

CME commands a 62% net margin but trades at a higher sales multiple, while Interactive Brokers posts stronger free cash flow despite lower profitability.

Market volatility often drives trading activity, creating a compelling environment for exchange and brokerage firms. Choosing between CME Group (CME +1.02%) and Interactive Brokers Group (IBKR -2.69%) requires analyzing their distinct roles in the global markets.

CME operates as a primary marketplace for derivatives, while Interactive Brokers serves as a high-tech gateway for traders. Both companies benefit from increased volume, yet they offer different risk and reward profiles for investors seeking exposure to the financial sector.

CME Group operates the world's leading derivatives marketplace, offering futures and options contracts for a global audience. The company provides clearing and trading services to a wide range of customers, including institutional investors, professional traders, and central banks. In late 2025, CME launched a joint venture with Flutter Entertainment (FLUT +6.14%) brand FanDuel to offer a prediction markets application to retail users. It maintains critical commercial relationships through licensing agreements with S&P Global (SPGI +1.85%) and Nasdaq (NDAQ +2.25%). Both CME and Interactive Brokers offer unique entry points into the financial stocks category for different types of investors.

In fiscal 2025, revenue reached nearly $6.5 billion, reflecting growth of roughly 6.4% compared to the previous year. The company reported net income of close to $4 billion for the same period. This resulted in a net margin of approximately 62%, which measures the portion of revenue remaining after all expenses and taxes are paid. The steady growth in revenue and high profitability highlight CME's established position in the derivatives market.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was roughly 0.1. This ratio measures total debt relative to shareholder equity, indicating a low level of borrowing. The current ratio, which compares short-term assets to short-term liabilities, was approximately 1.0. Free cash flow for the fiscal year was nearly $4.2 billion, calculated as cash from operations minus capital expenditures. These figures suggest CME has a solid financial foundation with significant cash generation.

Interactive Brokers Group serves a diverse global client base, including hedge funds, registered investment advisors, and individual investors. The company provides automated trade execution across more than 175 markets, allowing users to trade stocks, bonds, and options from a single platform. It also partners with third-party providers to facilitate the trading of Bitcoin (BTC -0.75%) for its customers. Interactive Brokers operates specialized services like white branding for introducing brokers. This global reach allows the company to capture trading activity across multiple asset classes and geographic regions.

In fiscal 2025, revenue reached nearly $10.2 billion, representing growth of approximately 9.8% over the prior year. Net income for the period was close to $984 million. The resulting net margin was roughly 9.6%, indicating the percentage of sales converted into actual profit. While its net margin is lower than that of an exchange operator, Interactive Brokers has shown consistent growth in both revenue and profit as its account base expands.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the long-term debt-to-equity ratio was nearly 0, showing almost no debt relative to equity. The current ratio stands at approximately 1.1, suggesting the company has sufficient short-term assets to meet its immediate obligations. Free cash flow for fiscal 2025 was nearly $15.7 billion. This figure represents the cash generated from operations minus capital expenditures, providing a measure of cash available for expansion or shareholder returns.

CME Group faces intense competition from other global exchange operators such as Intercontinental Exchange (ICE +2.03%) and Cboe Global Markets (CBOE +4.08%). The business remains heavily regulated, and new oversight could lead to higher operating costs or capital requirements. Cybersecurity threats are a material concern, particularly because the company relies on third-party cloud services for its infrastructure. Revenue is also highly sensitive to market volatility and trading volumes, which can fluctuate based on macroeconomic shifts beyond the company's control.

Interactive Brokers Group deals with significant competition from online equity brokers and zero-commission platforms that could pressure its pricing. The company carries substantial credit risk through its margin lending and clearing activities, where a systemic market event might cause customer defaults. Its reliance on proprietary technology means any system failures or security breaches could cause major reputational damage. Additionally, Interactive Brokers' digital asset offerings depend on third-party providers, exposing the company to risks involving their security or regulatory standing.

Interactive Brokers Group appears more expensive based on future earnings estimates, while CME Group carries a higher premium relative to its sales over the past 12 months.

CME Group's eye-popping net margin would be the envy of most companies. However, revenue growth for fiscal 2025 was lackluster. Profitability is important, but investors typically want to see rising sales, too. Interactive Brokers' revenue growth wasn't especially impressive, either, and the company also reported a dramatically lower net margin.

As for their valuations, the two stocks are mixed depending on the metric used, so I think it's a wash.
