Ex-Everton employee shares Thierno Barry claim amid contract concerns

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Everton will not be thinking about a new contract for Thierno Barry despite the growing interest in his services.

That is according to former senior Everton scout, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The 24-year-old is enjoying a superb season and has already outscored his first campaign, netting 10 goals in 13 games across all competitions so far.

Barry joined Everton from Villarreal last summer for around £27million, putting pen to paper on a contract until 2029.

It was a difficult first season of transition, but the France youth international now looks to have found his feet and his confidence.

Barry is reportedly already attracting interest from elsewhere, with some concerns emerging over the length of his contract.

Everton will soon be entering the decision zone on Barry, who will have a choice to make himself.

Should he continue to thrive this season, then he may have admirers willing to try to tempt the Toffees into a sale next summer.

And if the striker decides to leave, then Everton will be powerless to accept any other outcome with their only other option to allow him to run his contract down into its final year.

It could be a dangerous situation to be in for the Toffees, who could solve it by extending Barry's contract as his breakthrough is still in process.

Not everybody is convinced that there is any issue, however. "The interest is because he's starting to score goals for Everton Football Club," King told Football Insider when asked about a potential new deal.

"Again, he cost decent money to bring to the football club. He's only been there a year, he signed in 2025. You're looking at £27m with the deal going up to somewhere between £32-£35m.

"He'll be on good money anyway. He's on good money now and will be until 2029."

Before Barry can expect offers from the clubs he may well aspire to play for, he will need to prove that his form this season is no purple patch.

The Frenchman is showing plenty of good signs. He is getting on the end of chances at an alarming rate, and will be scoring goals at will if he can start converting more of them.

Yet his all-round game still needs work, and David Moyes will hope to be the man to guide him through that.

It is easy to forget that, at the age of 24, Barry is still nowhere near his prime as a striker.
