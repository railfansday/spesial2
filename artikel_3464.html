Gabriel Iglesias Breaks Silence after Onstage Health Scare: "Really Bad'

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Gabriel Iglesias has broken his silence after his onstage health scare, admitting his decision to not “take breaks”from standup comedy has caught up to him.

“It was a scare of scares,” Iglesias, 50, revealed on the Tuesday, November 24, episode of The Jennifer Hudson Show. “I’ve been running with the check engine light on for a minute.”

The comedian noted, “I was kinda going through, finishing up a cold and I powered through some shows. A show that I did in Raleigh, North Carolina, I was feeling really out of it on stage.”

He recalled feeling worse than he ever had while performing, saying he’d “never” had to “stop the show and just say, ‘I don’t feel well. I need help.’”

“They had to walk me off stage. I was about to pass out,” Iglesias shared. “It was really, really bad. They had oxygen and the whole thing. It was a mess backstage.”

The Mr. Iglesias alum said he was “lucky” to have people “tending to me and that I got through it,” confessing it was “scary.”

Iglesias made headlines over the weekend after a viral video circulated online, showing the standup favorite hunched over a stool on stage and calling for opener Martin Moreno to help him away during his Sunday, November 22, performance at the Lenovo Center. Iglesias received oxygen backstage and didn’t return.

“I’m 50, yeah, whatever, I qualify for discounts and stuff like that, but I didn’t think I would be in a situation — you never think it’s going to be you,” Iglesias told host Jennifer Hudson on Tuesday.

He admitted, “I didn’t listen to friends and family,” telling the audience, “I’m a workhouse. I don’t take vacations. I don’t take breaks. I’ve never taken a vacation. I work, work, work. That’s me. It caught up.”

Iglesias also addressed people’s assumption that his health scare was a result of his weight.

“I’m not the same size as I was when I first started. I was performing at 430 pounds,” he said. “That was my heaviest. Right now I’m 280.”

Iglesias noted that he did see a doctor after the show, who joked, “Well, you’re alive,” which everyone agreed was a “good thing.”

Moving forward, Iglesias said he wants to take better care of himself, but he is still grappling with his intense work ethic.

“I’m still like, ‘No, I’ve got commitments.’ It’s hard to turn that off,” he confessed, admitting that “I do gotta slow down a little bit. I do want to be around.”
