Andoni Iraola Has Quietly Filled £37.5m Hole in Liverpool’s Defence

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Liverpool came into the new season with uncertainty in almost every area. A new manager, a new system and several new players, with many stars from yesteryear moving on. Slowly but surely, Andoni Iraola has started to work things out.

The Reds have endured some turbulent times already under the former Bournemouth boss. Defeats at home to Manchester City and Arsenal prompted scrutiny from their own supporters at Anfield, while a chaotic loss away at Fenerbahce was a low point.

However, their fortunes have stabilised the more Iraola has been able to work with his players, and they currently sit inside the Premier League's top four — an impressive feat after ten matches, in a league with so much parity, where most struggle for consistency.

Iraola still needs to elicit more from his attack, which remains very much a work in progress. Alexander Isak has shown real signs of life, scoring eight goals already this term, but Florian Wirtz and Bradley Barcola remain unproven in the Premier League. As a cohesive unit, they still look dysfunctional, and this has led to many a drab performance.

Surprisingly, however, the Reds appeared to have settled well defensively under their new head coach, who deserves a lot of praise for sorting this side of Liverpool's game.

There was concern around the defensive side of Liverpool's game heading into this season, largely because of the departure of Ibrahima Konate, who left Merseyside for the bright lights of the Bernabeu and Real Madrid in the summer, for free.

While many felt Konate's form dropped off towards the end of his time in the north-west, few would deny how significant the Frenchman's partnership with Virgil van Dijk was for the Reds over the last few years. The duo formed the cornerstone of Liverpool's title-winning team in the 2024/25 season, with their imperiousness keeping the opposition out more often than they got in.

Naturally, when one of these pieces was taken away and replaced by a 21-year-old defender with just over 50 Ligue 1 appearances to his name, there was scepticism about how well Liverpool's defence would cope.

Konate, valued at £37.5 million (via TransferMarkt), was a sure thing. Liverpool knew what they'd get from him on a weekly basis. Jeremy Jacquet was an unknown quantity, as much as he'd been viewed as an exciting young talent in France.

Three months into the season, and nobody is talking about how Konate has been missed at Anfield. Instead, Jacquet has already established himself as one of the Premier League's best defenders and has broken into the France squad to play alongside the Real Madrid star.

Some would even argue that Jacquet has outshone Van Dijk in Liverpool's defence. Regardless, as a pairing, they've led the Premier League's fourth-least-fallible defence, conceding just ten goals so far. The underlying numbers back the Reds up as one of the best defensive teams in the league, conceding the fifth-fewest non-penalty expected goals.

Jacquet has looked physically robust, athletic and incredibly effective at reading the game to snuff out opposition danger. On the ball, he's been secure, albeit slightly reserved regarding the aggression of his passing.

With a new manager, new goals and squad changes, expect plenty of transfer business from Liverpool this summer.

It's remarkable how seamlessly the Frenchman has slotted in and replaced Konate, and it's arguably been overlooked.

Liverpool essentially lost one of their best players and replaced him with ease with a starlet defender from Rennes. This outstanding piece of recruitment deserves more praise, while Iraola deserves more credit for creating an environment, tactically and psychologically, in which Jacquet can thrive.
