Nvidia poured $58.1 billion into AI companies’ stock in 9 months—and an analyst warns it’s now “double exposed” if the boom slows

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Nvidia spent $58.1 billion buying other companies’ stock in the nine months ending Oct. 25—nearly 12 times the $4.95 billion it spent in the same period last year, according to the quarterly results it posted late Tuesday. This almost matches the $61.2 billion Nvidia spent on stock buybacks, up from $36.3 billion last year.

It was Nvidia’s first earnings report without Jensen Huang as the CEO. The company reported $115.8 billion in revenue for the three months ending Oct. 25, more than double the $57 billion the year prior and beating analysts’ expectations of $109 billion.

The near-even split between outside stakes and buybacks shows Nvidia’s growing dual role as both chip supplier and investor in the AI industry, with its equity investments “focused on frontier labs, infrastructure financiers, and other private companies,” according to its Q3 2026 CFO commentary (Nvidia has invested in OpenAI and Anthropic). This raises the question of circular financing—whether Nvidia is funding the demand that drives its chip sales. And if AI spending falters, Nvidia could suffer from both lower chip sales and falling valuations for the companies it has invested in, said James Kisner, the managing director of technology at equity research firm Water Tower Research.

“It’s magnified and you feel more pain because you’re kind of double exposed here,” Kisner told Fortune.

Kisner said that Nvidia’s billions in free cash flow means it can afford to pour $58.1 billion into other companies’ stock, but that can be seen as fueling its own demand. Equity securities only generated $1.8 billion in net gains–which included a $1 billion loss from non-marketable equity securities—for Nvidia in Q3.

“If you were really cynical, you could say, ‘Oh, like these guys are just generating their own demand,’’’ Kisner said.

Still, Kisner said Nvidia has incentives to keep investing given how much its growth has benefitted shareholders. It became the first company to cross the $5 trillion market valuation mark last year, though it has since dropped to $3.7 trillion as of Nov. 18, per Yahoo Finance data.

“There are all kinds of incentives for them to fuel the fire if they have the cash, so there’s a self-serving aspect,” he said. “The markets really reward them for pouring all this money.”

He said the strategy reflects Huang’s approach of “spreading money around and kind of keeping the ecosystem going,” and he sees interim CEO Colette Kress continuing it with “a little more conservatism” at the margins.

Kress told analysts on the Nov. 17 earnings call that giving returns to shareholders is a “super top priority,” with Nvidia having $227.3 billion left under its stock buyback authorization program that Kress said it intends to use “by the end of fiscal 2028.” But she said another priority is supporting Nvidia’s suppliers who “are working feverishly to build more” and might need “assistance in terms of capital.”

“We want to make sure that we’re there for them so they can move as quickly as possible,” Kress said. “I think that is a very great use of our capital to help support that part of it.”

Nvidia is also willing to help finance select customers’ data centers, but Kress told analysts it’s “a lot of work to understand if those are the right types of investments” for Nvidia to make.

“It is a process that we will look very deeply in terms of the company, the strength of what they have planned, as well as everything that they need in terms of that data center to do so,” Kress said.

But Nvidia’s financial backing of the AI industry doesn’t stop at that. Kress said on the call that Nvidia’s future commitments to neoclouds, the small firms that buy Nvidia chips and then rent out computing capacity, totaled $32 billion in Q3. Separately, she said Nvidia’s total credit guarantees reached $118 billion, including $105 billion in credit support for the PORTS-Pike data center project in Ohio, which will host NVIDIA infrastructure under a 20-year lease with OpenAI.

Kress also said some of Nvidia’s guarantees only take effect once customers get financing for their data centers, and may no longer apply if construction runs into trouble.

“If all of that begins and unfortunately they are delayed or they have a challenge in terms of building that, our guarantee doesn’t exist anymore,” she told analysts on the call. “Our guarantee for most of these are really about when they are ready to go.”
