House bill takes on judicial foreclosure requirements for FHA loans in non-judicial states

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Reps. Young Kim and Nikema Williams introduced the Housing Process Parity Act, a bipartisan bill to clarify FHA partial claim lien treatment in nonjudicial foreclosures and move foreclosed homes back to market faster.   AI Summary

Representatives Young Kim (R-California) and Nikema Williams (D-Georgia) introduced this week the bipartisan Housing Process Parity Act, a bill that would streamline how certain foreclosed Federal Housing Administration (FHA) properties return to the market and direct a federal study of zero-down-payment FHA loans.

Under current law, differing federal requirements around partial claim liens can force servicers to pursue judicial foreclosures on some FHA loans even in nonjudicial foreclosure states, lengthening timelines and increasing legal costs before a vacant home can be resold.The bill, H.R. 11082, seeks to clarify how partial claim liens are treated following nonjudicial foreclosures for FHA-insured mortgages, bringing FHA authorities closer to those Congress granted the Department of Veterans Affairs’ (V.A.) home loan program last year.

“I’ve been proud to champion bipartisan legislation to alleviate the housing crisis and put the American Dream back within reach,” Rep. Kim said in a statement. “My Housing Process Parity Act will build on these efforts by getting homes back on the market faster and exploring new pathways to homeownership for everyday Americans.”Williams said the limited stock of affordable homes is undermining families’ ability to build wealth. “The bipartisan Housing Process Parity Act cuts unnecessary barriers to get homes back on the market faster and expand housing supply,” she said. “Every family, no matter their ZIP code or bank account, deserves a fair shot at building wealth for the next generation.”

The Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA) endorsed the bill, saying it “advances FHA program improvements, provides parity across government-insured mortgage programs, and supports broader efforts to expand access to sustainable homeownership,” according to a statement from MBA President and CEO Bob Broeksmit, CMB.

“Clarifying partial claim lien treatment following non-judicial foreclosures would provide greater certainty for servicers and lower costs for borrowers,” Broeksmit said. “While foreclosure is never the desired outcome, a non-judicial process reduces legal costs, shortens timelines, and limits the financial impact on homeowners.”

Beyond foreclosure process changes, the bill would require the Government Accountability Office (GAO) to study the feasibility of a new FHA-insured mortgage product with no down payment.

The study would examine potential benefits to low- and moderate-income and first-time buyers, as well as risks to taxpayers and the FHA Mutual Mortgage Insurance Fund, and identify safeguards needed to keep such loans sustainable.

An Urban Institute analysis – authored by Alexei Alexandrov, Laurie Goodman, Ted Tozer and Sam Valverde – from earlier this year showed that moving from a loan-to-value (LTV) ratio range of 96% to 99% to a range of 100% to 104% LTVs raises the default probability by just 12 basis points — a difference the authors describe as statistically insignificant.“Allowing zero-down payment mortgages would level the playing field for potential homeowners without family wealth,” the researchers wrote in the report. “Moreover, FHA zero-down payment loans would also replace the inconsistent patchwork of down payment assistance programs.”
