6 keys to an Eagles win over the Steelers in Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Week 10 produced the worst kind of week a Philadelphia Eagles fan can endure, one where their team isn't scheduled to play a football game. Then again, some may see a bright side. Nick Sirianni's squad needed time to regroup, and despite seven straight losses, they aren't mathematically eliminated from playoff contention.

So after some expected assessment, everyone will be watching to see if this team emerges from the break in better shape than they remember. Sunday's matchup with the Pittsburgh Steelers will offer the first indication of whether anything has actually changed.

Philadelphia has struggled to establish an offensive identity, consistently stay ahead of the chains, and avoid the mistakes that have made life unnecessarily difficult. The defense has also struggled with communication breakdowns and inconsistency. That leaves the Eagles with plenty to clean up against a Steelers team that won't necessarily give them opportunities to recover from their own mistakes.

A victory may require a near-perfect performance, but time will tell. Philadelphia obviously needs to execute better in several key areas. Here are a handful of keys to an Eagles win in Week 11.

The Eagles have spent too much of this season playing behind the chains, and the consequences have been obvious. Philadelphia leads the league in three-and-outs, putting unnecessary pressure on its defense while making it increasingly difficult for the offense to establish any rhythm.

The numbers paint an ugly picture. The Eagles have surrendered 41 more first downs than they've produced and have allowed more rushing yards than they've gained. Their inability to consistently establish the run has also limited their ability to dictate defensive personnel and create favorable situations in the passing game.

Philadelphia must find ways to make the offense less predictable. Getting creative with formations, motion and play sequencing could help generate easier opportunities while forcing defenses to account for more than the traditional approach.

The Eagles also need to take advantage of the opportunities available on early downs. Avoiding obvious passing situations would give Joe Milton more manageable third downs and let the offense keep the playbook open rather than constantly playing catch-up.

The Eagles will have to approach their run defense differently without Jordan Davis. His departure leaves a significant gap along the defensive line, and Pittsburgh will likely look to test Philadelphia's ability to handle the ground game.

Vic Fangio and Nick Sirianni discussed the importance of collectively addressing Davis' absence throughout the week, so it's clearly something the coaching staff has emphasized. Rather than expecting one player to replicate Davis' impact, Philadelphia will need contributions from multiple players along the defensive front and at the second level.

The Steelers have Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle available to help control tempo, making it especially important for the Eagles to maintain gap discipline and avoid allowing Pittsburgh to consistently create favorable down-and-distance situations.

Communication breakdowns have plagued the Eagles at various points this season, and the defense can't afford to continue making preventable mistakes against Pittsburgh. The second and third levels need tighter pre- and post-snap communication to avoid blown assignments and drive-extending penalties. Sean Mannion has discussed the offense's need to clean up its communication, but Vic Fangio's unit should be having those same conversations.

Philadelphia also needs to remain physical without crossing the line into costly penalties. The secondary must avoid giving Pittsburgh free yardage, while the linebackers need to limit production over the middle and prevent receivers from turning short completions into substantial gains after the catch.

The Steelers don't necessarily need explosive plays to move the ball. If Philadelphia repeatedly gives them favorable situations through missed assignments or penalties, Pittsburgh could sustain drives without having to do anything particularly impressive.

Aaron Rodgers has leaned on his tight ends in scoring situations, particularly Darnell Washington. He has scored three touchdowns. Pat Freiermuth leads Pittsburgh with five touchdown receptions.

Philadelphia needs to make Pittsburgh work for its touchdowns rather than allowing Rodgers to find favorable matchups near the goal line. Washington and Pat Freiermuth present different challenges because of their size, ability to create separation and usefulness in tight spaces.

The Eagles need to make those windows difficult to find and force Rodgers to look elsewhere when the field shrinks. That doesn't mean Philadelphia can simply focus on the tight ends and ignore everyone else, but if Pittsburgh reaches the red zone, eliminating its preferred targets could force the Steelers into more difficult decisions and give the Eagles a chance to make a stand.

Zack Baun is out with a back injury, and the Eagles expect Jihaad Campbell to start alongside Jeremiah Trotter Jr. Campbell missed the past two games. Pittsburgh can test the pairing by moving Freiermuth or Washington before the snap, forcing Philadelphia to decide who takes the tight end if he releases and who fills the gap if he stays in to block.

The Eagles' pass rush has shown signs of improvement, and Sunday's matchup offers an opportunity to build on that momentum. Aaron Rodgers hasn't necessarily been looking to get the ball out immediately, and Philadelphia could benefit if he, as usual, looks to hold on to it longer than he would prefer.

The Eagles recorded six sacks in Week 9, and replicating that performance may be difficult. Still, getting consistent pressure and forcing Rodgers into hurried decisions would go a long way toward controlling the game.

Pittsburgh will also lean on Warren and Dowdle to establish the run and dictate tempo. Philadelphia needs to avoid letting the Steelers get comfortable offensively, particularly on early downs, where a successful running game could make the pass rush less effective.

Pittsburgh placed starting guard Mason McCormick on injured reserve this week. That gives Philadelphia a reason to test the interior early. If the Eagles can make Rodgers move off his spot with four rushers, Fangio can keep an extra defender in the middle of the field, where Pittsburgh's tight ends can give Rodgers a quick answer.

This has become such a familiar talking point that it almost feels redundant, but the Eagles have struggled enough in this area that it remains a legitimate concern. Philadelphia isn't good enough to place unnecessary roadblocks in front of itself and expect to overcome them. Penalties, turnovers, and wasted possessions have made an already inconsistent offense even more difficult to watch.

The defense has occasionally compounded those problems with its own mistakes. The Steelers have been relatively disciplined, committing 51 penalties this season, which isn't many when compared to other franchises. Philadelphia can't afford to be the team that disrupts its own rhythm through flags and preventable errors.

Winning the time-of-possession battle, protecting the football and limiting penalties aren't glamorous keys to victory, but they could be especially important Sunday. The Eagles need to make Pittsburgh earn everything rather than giving the Steelers opportunities to capitalize on Philadelphia's mistakes.
