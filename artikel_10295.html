The 4 secret Amazon clearance sites you should check before buying retail

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

As we power toward the holidays, the shopping pressure is officially on. Your natural instinct may be to type what you need into the Amazon search bar, click the first Prime-eligible result, and pay whatever price the algorithm spits out. That’s a huge mistake.

Amazon buries its best discounts on purpose. The retail giant operates three distinct clearance portals where you can score open-box electronics, overstocked gifts, and refurbished tech for up to 70% off retail. Before you pay full price for holiday gifts this year, here’s where you actually need to look.

Amazon Resale

Formerly known as Amazon Warehouse, Amazon Resale is where Amazon sends customer returns. The trick to using Resale is understanding its strict grading system.

If you buy an item rated as Used—Like New, it means the product itself is in perfect, untouched condition, but the original shrink wrap is broken or the cardboard box has a minor dent.

You get a massive discount simply because the packaging isn’t pretty enough to sit on a retail shelf. If you’re buying tech gear or kitchen appliances where the box just goes straight into the recycling bin anyway, it’s the easiest way to save hundreds. Just stick strictly to the Like New or Very Good tiers.

Amazon Outlet

Unlike Resale, everything in Amazon Outlet is brand new. This is where Amazon dumps seasonal overstock and excess inventory that’s taking up too much physical space in its fulfillment centers.

Finding it is the hard part. It isn’t advertised on the home page. You have to type amazon.com/outlet directly into your browser or dig deep through the Deals and Savings menu on the mobile app.

Once you’re in, you’ll find everything from holiday decorations to winter apparel marked down by 50% to 70%. It’s the absolute best spot to hunt for stocking stuffers and white elephant gifts without breaking the bank.

Woot

Amazon bought Woot back in 2010, but it still operates as a standalone site at woot.com. It’s a dedicated daily deal platform that runs flash sales on electronics, tools, and apparel until the inventory sells out.

If you’re hunting for a refurbished television, a discounted sound bar, or cheap smart home gear, Woot should be your first stop every morning. The checkout system is fully integrated, meaning your Prime membership still gets you free shipping. Just be prepared to act fast, because the best tech deals usually vanish before your morning coffee gets cold.

Amazon Haul

If you’ve ever been tempted to buy cheap stocking stuffers off Temu or Shein but didn’t want to hand your credit card to an overseas app, you’ll be interested to know that Amazon built a direct competitor right inside its own ecosystem.

It’s called Amazon Haul, and the gimmick is simple: Every item comes from outside the U.S., which often means cheaper, direct-from-manufacturer pricing.

Free shipping kicks in once you hit $25; less than that, and there’s a $4 shipping charge per order. While you lose next-day delivery—items can take up to a couple of weeks to arrive—and the return window drops to just 15 days, every purchase is still backed by Amazon. That makes it a safer way to buy ultra-cheap phone cases, kitchen gadgets, and holiday party supplies without risking a scam.
