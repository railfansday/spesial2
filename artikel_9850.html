Clockify in 2026 covering its free plan, paid tiers and monitoring

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Posted on Nov 21 • Originally published at timeuno.io

Clockify is a good timer and timesheet for a small group. It lists a free plan and adds billing, shift and monitoring tools on paid plans, as of November 2026. It fits less well if you want tracking that runs without a timer. This is based on the vendor's public pages, read on 10 November 2026, not a long-term test. I work on TimeUno, a competing tool, so I also say where TimeUno is the wrong pick.

Clockify has a free plan and four paid plans billed per seat. Use the Clockify pricing page for current prices and for the free plan's user, report range and API limits.

Check the pricing FAQ for how seats are counted.

You get a timer, timesheet entry, a calendar view and reports. That covers recording hours. The limits appear when you manage people or bill clients. Kiosk clock-in and breaks start on Basic. Invoicing, approval, time off and timesheet locking start on Standard, so a freelancer who wants to invoice from tracked time pays for that tier.

Clockify has an auto tracker on every plan, free included. It records apps, window titles and websites on your desktop, and its docs say the data is saved locally, visible only to you, with desktop apps for Mac, Windows and Linux. Turning that record into time entries is manual, so it helps you remember what you did but does not replace the timer.

Activity monitoring is a Pro feature. The feature page describes an Agent app installed on company devices that records visited sites and apps, keyboard and mouse activity, and optional screenshots on an admin-set interval, with a blur option. The page says it works silently in the background.

If you track your own time, none of that applies, since you choose what to install. If you join someone else's workspace, ask your admin whether it is switched on.

Pro adds optional GPS tracking of client site visits while on the clock, plus scheduling, expenses and labor cost and profit reports. Pro also lets you pick a data region: EU (Germany), UK, USA or Australia.

TimeUno records apps, websites and AI tool sessions such as Claude Code, Cursor and Codex CLI on macOS and Windows, with no timer. It saves no screenshots, though it reads on-screen text by default, which you can switch off. Its MCP server lets you ask Claude or ChatGPT about your own time. In a team, admins see worked time, productivity score and AI usage, and see per-person apps and websites only after a notice and a 7-day delay.
