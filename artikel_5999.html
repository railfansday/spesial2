Delta Flight Attendants May Get Their First Union Vote Since 2010 — But Saying Yes Could Mean Smaller Paychecks

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Delta Air Lines flight attendants could be on the cusp of unionizing, but creating a union may be a card best kept in reserve – rather than one they should actually play. It can be a useful stalking horse, where the airline pays up generously to keep its crew from unionizing. But once they actually pull the trigger, there’s little reason – or ability – to continue paying above industry wages. AFA-CWA union head Sara Nelson says she’s close to the card count her union wants before asking for a vote at Delta Air Lines. Brian Sumers came away from their November 12 interview at the Skift Aviation Forum thinking she’ll get an election and win it. A vote in 2027 is a real possibility. If I were Delta crew I’d vote no. The union would get about 30,000 flight attendants to represent. Crews who already get better than what other airline unions bargain for could wind up taking home less. Nelson told Sumers she’s close to the union’s private target, which is above 50%. The National Mediation Board needs valid cards from at least half the eligible flight attendants, and a card more than a year old won’t count. Delta organizers put the minimum at roughly 15,000 signatures. Once there’s an election, a majority of valid votes cast decides it. Nelson also said she was months away in 2024. Later that November organizers said they were weeks from having the cards. Delta flight attendants have rejected representation three times, but the vote after the Northwest merger was close. In 2010, 9,544 voted against representation and 9,216 voted for it. Sumers says he hears more confidence from Nelson this time. She can pick when to file, and a loss would normally mean waiting a year to try again while cards expire. If Nelson files with a comfortable cushion above the threshold, that would give her the edge in an election. Delta’s Pay Advantage Is Profit Sharing American Airlines and United Airlines now pay slightly higher hourly rates at the top of the flight pay scale. It’s $86.32 per flight hour at Delta, $87.04 at American and $87.47 at United. At 80 flight hours a month, United’s $1.15 advantage is about $1,100 a year in base pay.For newer hires, Delta starts a little higher than both. But Delta flight attendants actually make quite a bit more. Delta paid out $1.3 billion in February for 2025, or 8.9% of eligible earnings. United recorded $704 million for profit sharing and associated payroll taxes for the same year. Delta calculates that a flight attendant working 80 flight hours a month makes $5,100 more than United for a new hire and $6,200 at the top of the scale, including United’s retro pay attributable to 2026. United got boarding pay in its new deal. It kept a its inferior profit sharing formula. Delta puts 10% of the first $2.5 billion of eligible pretax profit into the pool and 20% above that. At United, the 20% rate kicks in only on earnings above the previous year’s. American negotiated the Delta thresholds and still has much smaller checks because American Airlines doesn’t make money. . The Delta campaign’s website still lists $50 a month in dues and says they’d start after flight attendants ratify a first contract. The union’s board raised the standard rate to $65.36 in June so Delta’s cabin crew are being sold a lower cost than they’d actually face. That’s $784 a year, or about $23.5 million in gross annual dues from 30,000 flight attendants. Union Flight Attendants Go A Long Time Between Raises Delta gave its flight attendants a 4% raise in June, its fifth annual increase in a row (there was a pause for the depths of the pandemic, when Delta didn’t furlough anyone while unionized American and United furloughed tens of thousands). United’s old wage table last went up in 2020. The contract became amendable in August 2021, and the next general raise took effect May 31, 2026 with the new contract. Consumer prices went up about 29% between September 2020 and May 2026. A senior United flight attendant was still on $67.11 per flight hour just before the new deal. It took more than $86 by then just to buy what $67.11 did in 2020. Airline labor contracts become amendable and the old terms generally stay in force while the parties bargain. The National Mediation Board can release the parties from mediation, normally starting a 30 day cooling off period before the union can strike or the airline can impose new terms. In practice that hasn’t happened for flight attendants since 1993. Nelson said at Skift that she wants more frequent bargaining to deliver smaller, more regular raises. Why It Took So Long To Pay For Boarding Delta started paying for scheduled boarding time in 2022 at half the flight hourly rate. I wrote then that the organizing drive probably helped get it done. American added boarding pay in 2024 and United now uses the same half-rate approach as Delta. At Hawaiian Airlines, the same union asked for boarding pay and came away without it in its 2025 extension. AFA-CWA hadn’t gotten it at Spirit, either. There’s a reason boarding pay is a bigger deal to junior crew. Fly three short flights and you board three airplanes. Fly one long trip with the same flight hours and you board once. If the airline’s putting a limited amount of money on the table, the senior flight attendant who can bid the long trips generally does better with a higher flight rate. A junior flight attendant gets more out of a separate boarding premium. Both have to live with what the bargaining committee prioritizes. Boarding pay didn’t happen until non-union Delta did it because unions are often about dividing a fixed pie of spoils between members, rather than getting more from the airline. Senior crew preferred higher hourly rates, redistributing income up to them from more junior crew. Once Delta did it, though, that changed the dynamic and flight attendant unions had to re-prioritize their bargaining. What Delta And Its Crews Have To Lose I generally find Delta’s crew a little friendlier and more interested in getting the service right. Southwest Airlines has some of the friendliest flight attendants in the business and they’re unionized. Southwest has always hired for the right attitude. At Delta, part of the advantage comes from being able to change how the cabin runs and reward the people doing the work. The union’s first contract policy calls for writing existing work rules into a contract. If new rules make it harder to run the operation or deliver service that passengers pay extra for, Delta has less money and profit sharing falls. Bear in mind also that while the union says they protect workers from management, sometimes it’s the union that a flight attendant needs protection from. I covered United firing two union representatives after the airline concluded they’d solicited false complaints against a flight attendant who raised a safety concern. The union sued over United’s investigation of the reps and a federal judge dismissed its suit. At Southwest, four flight attendants faced internal charges for allegedly trying to replace their union. Another flight attendant won $1 million after the union got the airline to fire her. Alaska Airlines flight attendants, meanwhile, have been suing saying that the union pushed htem out over their Christianity and views on LGBT rights. If Delta flight attendants vote down a first contract, they’re still stuck with the union. If union leaders don’t follow the wishes of flight attendants, they’re still stuck with the union (and they don’t actually have direct say in how the parent union is governed). To go back to representing themselves, they’d normally have to wait two years from certification, get valid cards from at least half the workgroup and then win another election. Union organizers at Delta pitch that they’d take current pay, benefits and work rules and negotiate up from there. That’s not how any of this works. The current union status and bundle they receive today is all bundled together. A union deal would be ex nihilo and more like other airlines. There are tradeoffs between their pay and work rules. Someone working at Delta might prefer a different arrangement (though they should remember why they chose Delta over others in the first place). But it’s a different arrangement. At some airlines I’d take that deal. At Delta, flight attendants already have it better than at their peers. They have better pay and a product they’re proud of. Sara Nelson wants them to give her union their money – and possibly wind up taking home less. Since it’s virtually an irreversible decision, that’s not a button I’d push. It seems like a decision to make only when things are dire, not when they’re going well relative to peers. Union organizers at Delta pitch that they’d take current pay, benefits and work rules and negotiate up from there. That’s not how any of this works. The current union status and bundle they receive today is all bundled together. A union deal would be ex nihilo and more like other airlines. There are tradeoffs between their pay and work rules. Someone working at Delta might prefer a different arrangement (though they should remember why they chose Delta over others in the first place). But it’s a different arrangement.
