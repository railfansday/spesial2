Judge who repeatedly used racist slur removed by court

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

A judge in upstate New York was removed from the bench Tuesday by the New York Court of Appeals, the state’s highest court, for repeatedly using a racial slur while recounting a personal story and making other racially insensitive comments. (Image from Shutterstock)

A judge in upstate New York was removed from the bench Tuesday by the New York Court of Appeals, the state’s highest court, for repeatedly using a racial slur while recounting a personal story and making other racially insensitive comments.

Judge Walter W. Jones of Canandaigua, New York, used the N-word at least three times during a conversation with a court staff member and a public defender outside the courtroom in May 2024. Jones, who is white, was telling a story about a Black man who had worked in his grandfather’s cotton fields in Texas, according to the court.

Jones said in a court hearing he used the slur just one time because he was quoting his father, whom he described as “racist.”

In affirming the New York State Commission on Judicial Conduct, the appeals court noted that Jones also made racially insensitive remarks during the arraignment of a Black defendant, saying she had “played the race card” when she accused him of setting her bail too high because she was Black.

The racial slur that Jones used “was unquestionably offensive and was followed just days later” by his “racially biased, on-the-record comments regarding a Black defendant who had appeared before him,” the appeals court wrote in its ruling.

Jones’ “inability to recognize the impact that his statements could have on the public’s confidence in the judiciary’s impartiality and integrity demonstrates his lack of understanding of the need for judges ‘to both be and appear to be impartial,’” the appeals court said.

Jones was first elected as a town justice in Canandaigua, located in the Finger Lakes region of western New York, in 1999, according to a story by the New York Times.
