Julio Iglesias Being Investigated by Spanish Court Over Sexual Abuse Claims

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Spanish prosecutors had previously declined to pursue a probe over alleged 2021 incidents at the singer's homes in the Dominican Republic and the Bahamas.

A Spanish court has launched a criminal investigation into singer Julio Iglesias over accusations that he sexually and physically abused two female employees at his Caribbean residences.

The country’s National High Court said Wednesday (Nov. 18) that the claims lodged by the two women, over alleged incidents in 2021 at the singer’s residences in the Dominican Republic and the Bahamas, were enough to justify “initiation of the criminal investigation.” The court cited preliminary evidence, including a photograph that showed alleged injuries sustained by the two women.

“This photograph shows a bruise on one of her arms, consistent with her having been gripped with force,” the court said in an announcement of the investigation. “Also visible in the same photograph, on one of her thighs, are two marks that could correspond to a human bite; the complaint states that these resulted from the complainant being forced to participate in sexual acts.”

Iglesias and his attorney did not immediately return requests for comment on Wednesday.

Iglesias, 83, has been one of the world’s best-selling Latin artists for decades, with more than 300 million records sold and 20 songs entering Billboard‘s Hot Latin Songs chart, including three No. 1 hits: “Lo Mejor De Tu Vida,” “Torero” and “Que No Se Rompa la Noche.” The Grammy-winning artist has also put three songs on the Billboard Hot 100, most notably his “To All The Girls I’ve Loved Before” alongside Willie Nelson, which reached No. 5 in 1984.

The two women, identified by the pseudonyms Laura and Rebeca in court filings, made their accusations public in January, when they claimed they had been subjected to a sustained environment of control and intimidation while working in the singer’s homes for 10 months in 2021.

Iglesias vehemently denied the accusations as “completely false,” and Spanish prosecutors quickly rejected the case, saying the nation’s courts “lacked jurisdiction” over the alleged crimes because they occurred in other countries. But the rights group Women’s Link Worldwide filed a new complaint on their behalf in September, arguing that the National High Court (Audiencia Nacional) had jurisdiction and that Iglesias’ Spanish nationality gave the court grounds to hear the case.

In an order on Wednesday, Judge Antonio ​Piña partly agreed with that request. He said the upcoming probe would cover their claims of crimes against sexual freedom as well as their allegations of assault and degrading treatment. But he rejected their other claims, which covered alleged human trafficking and violations of the rights of workers.

Judge Piña ordered both women to testify in court in February.
