Sunrise On The Reaping Soundtrack Guide: Every Song & When They Play

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Warning: Contains spoilers for The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping!Despite its dystopian sci-fi setting, The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping includes multiple songs, with its full soundtrack even bigger than the movie lets on. The Hunger Games movies have always incorporated music, with Katniss Everdeen singing throughout the first four films and Lucy Gray Baird's songs heavily featured in The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes.

While Sunrise on the Reaping shifts the story to a 16-year-old Haymitch Abernathy (Joseph Zada), the District 12 tribute is hardly someone to break into song. Nor are there any major needle drops. The movie instead utilizes the Covey and known District 12 songs to sprinkle in some music as Haymitch's life and the 50th Hunger Games unfold.

In typical Hunger Games fashion, the songs speak directly to the story and larger themes. Multiple are performed by Haymitch's Covey girlfriend, Lenore Dove Baird (Whitney Peak). And while there's only five songs heard from the start to finish of Sunrise on the Reaping, the movie's complete soundtrack includes a lot more Hunger Games-inspired music.

"The Raven" by Whitney Peak: Sunrise on the Reaping gets the music going from the very beginning. As establishing shots of District 12 are shown to get audiences accustomed to how it looks 24 years before Katniss' story began and some prep for the 50th Hunger Games reaping, the movie introduces the vocal performance of Peak's Lenore Dove. The movie brings "The Raven" back shortly thereafter, as Lenore is singing it in the meadow when Haymitch comes to see her on the morning of the reaping.

"The Goose and the Common" by Whitney Peak: Lenore is responsible for the next song in Sunrise on the Reaping, which occurs while the 2nd Quarter Quell is underway. Haymitch is poisoned by water and only survives by eating a charcoal tablet, leaving him to lay on the ground and recover. As he looks to the sky, birds fly overhead and he hears Lenore singing "The Goose and the Common."

"The Old Therebefore" by Scot Greenan: Sunrise on the Reaping's soundtrack doesn't resume until after the 50th Hunger Games are over. After Haymitch returns to District 12, the locals gather for a funeral to honor the fallen tributes: Maysilee Donner, Wyatt Callow, and Louella McCoy. The funeral concludes with Scot Greenan's Burdock Everdeen singing "The Old Therebefore," which was originally performed by Rachel Zegler's Lucy Gray Baird in The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes.

"Deep in the Meadow (Lullaby)" by Jennifer Lawrence: One of the last songs in Sunrise on the Reaping comes as part of the epilogue, which jumps beyond the events of Mockingjay and shows Haymitch celebrating the sun rising on his birthday without a reaping. After a short exchange with Katniss and Peeta Mellark, featuring Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson back in their iconic roles, Woody Harrelson's version of Haymitch is shown in the meadow outside District 12. Lawrence sings a new rendition of "Deep in the Meadow" as Haymitch remembers his time with Lenore.

"Draw You Out" by Noah Kahan: Once Sunrise on the Reaping's credits begin, the movie shifts to another song, "Draw You Out" by Noah Kahan. The song was written exclusively for the Hunger Games movie and plays over the early credits that highlight the film's main cast members.

Those five songs are not the only ones on Sunrise on the Reaping's soundtrack, though. Lionsgate and MCA recruited a massive collection of artists to curate an entire album of songs that are almost exclusively written, performed, and produced specifically for the film.
