Steelers report card Week 10: Ugly grades for Aaron Rodgers, DK Metcalf in Bengals loss

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Pittsburgh Steelers were hammered by the Cincinnati Bengals, 38-18, in their "Sunday Night Football" matchup that decided control of the AFC North.

The Bengals now take a one-game lead heading into the second half of the season, with both teams facing fairly soft schedules.

First-year coach Mike McCarthy's squad will now have to play catch-up against their division rival that looked invincible at times, outgaining the Steelers by a 484-221 margin en route to the blowout victory.

Here's USA TODAY Sports' report card on the Steelers' performance.

Aaron Rodgers did not complete a pass until about 20 minutes into the game. By that point, Bengals counterpart Joe Burrow had already thrown for close to 250 yards and two touchdowns as his team took a 17-0 first-half lead. Rodgers' happy feet were noticeable. Some of that was due to significant pressure at times, but he never really seemed in sync, even when he had time to throw. And when he did, he was frequently inaccurate. He finished the night 15-of-29 passing for 143 yards. It was one of his worst performances as a Steeler before he padded the stats somewhat late.

Jaylen Warren did not make many people miss. You can blame some of that on poor run blocking in front of him. Warren has put himself among the NFL's leaders in scrimmage yards with a ton of yards after contact, however. When he's not getting those yards, it noticeably impacts this offense. His offensive line has to be better, but we can't let him completely off the hook here either.

The drops never seem to end for DK Metcalf. He had multiple in this one and registered zero catches in the first half, when the Steelers still had a chance to make it a game. The former Pro Bowler is supposed to be a safety blanket for Rodgers in games like this one. The guy Rodgers knows he can find with some level of consistency, even when other things aren't working offensively. He's definitively not that right now, in the second year of his five-year, $150 million contract, which was signed after he was acquired in a trade with the Seattle Seahawks in the 2025 offseason. The Steelers need Michael Pittman Jr. back as soon as possible, and Metcalf's garbage-time touchdown does little to change that reality.

Pat Freiermuth has had some of the best days of his career against the Bengals defense, but like Metcalf, he was almost invisible until the late fourth-quarter touchdown that didn't matter. He received just one first-half target and no catches. Darnell Washington wasn't much better. Granted, the Steelers didn't have a ton of offensive snaps to get these guys touches. They didn't do much to solve that problem, either.

Rodgers was sacked three times and was frequently under duress. Warren went almost nowhere as a ball carrier with seven carries for 15 yards. That's very little for this group to hang their collective hats on. As we've covered, Rodgers and Warren also were not very good individually in this one, but we can't let these guys off the proverbial hook here either.

Burrow had all day to throw as he dissected this defense. Some of that is on the linebackers, who we'll get to in a minute. Still, with the return of 2025 first-round pick Derrick Harmon from injury, you would have expected these guys to be far more disruptive than they were. They were better in the running game – the Bengals finished with just 78 rushing yards, which is manageable on paper. That total is probably deceptive, though. Chase Brown was able to run well situationally against this defense, and the pass was working so well that he didn't need to do much more.

The Bengals have mastered the art of neutralizing T.J. Watt, Alex Highsmith and Nick Herbig by getting the ball out quickly. That was especially true in this one against DC Patrick Graham's soft zone scheme, which encourages quarterbacks to nibble underneath until they make big mistakes. Burrow doesn't make many. And the Steelers' highly paid trio did little to cause any. All three were basically invisible, and that's unacceptable in a game of this magnitude.

You'd think these guys were gashed deep all night, given Burrow's gaudy 409-yard stat line, but that wasn't really the case. Ja'Marr Chase broke free for a couple of big ones that bolstered his stats, including one in garbage time in the fourth quarter. For the most part, it was otherwise just down-to-down brilliance from the Bengals quarterback. He found seemingly every soft spot in the zone, and the Steelers' defensive backs obliged by missing a lot of tackles and conceding extra yardage. In some ways, these guys did what they were supposed to by keeping the play in front of them. In doing so, however, they proved the limitations of this defensive scheme against the quality quarterbacks the Steelers will face in the playoffs, assuming they make it that far.

McCarthy's offensive play calls did little to get Rodgers into any kind of comfort zone. The execution was also dreadful, which has to land on the head coach coming off 13 days of bye-week rest. As we mentioned above, it's alarming that Graham's defense doesn't seem to have another gear when the zone is getting picked apart by a quarterback capable of executing against it. He needs to find some ways to mix things up if this becomes a first-round playoff matchup later in the year.
