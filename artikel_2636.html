FPL: Bruno Fernandes and 5 Man United Players to Target Under Pochettino

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Mauricio Pochettino has arrived at Manchester United, and suddenly the Fantasy Premier League conversation around the Red Devils has changed.

After a miserable opening to the season under Michael Carrick, United find themselves in the Premier League relegation zone after 10 matches. Carrick was dismissed during the international break, with Pochettino taking over as the club looks for a major turnaround.

For FPL managers, though, the question is slightly different: which Manchester United players are actually worth buying under the new manager? The answer is not as simple as loading up on United assets immediately.

Pochettino's first game comes at Anfield against Liverpool on November 22, giving him only a handful of training sessions with his new squad. After that, however, the fixture list becomes considerably more attractive, with Brentford, Newcastle United, Coventry City and Crystal Palace among United's next four Premier League opponents.

That makes this an interesting moment for FPL managers to get ahead of the curve.

Here are the Manchester United players I would be watching most closely:

If you're looking for the safest attacking investment at Manchester United, it remains Bruno Fernandes. The price is obviously the biggest obstacle. At £11.6m, Fernandes is no longer a player you can casually add to your squad. But his role under Pochettino could make the investment worthwhile.

The Portuguese midfielder has already shown why he remains one of United's most important Fantasy assets. Earlier this season, he produced 27 FPL points across the opening three Gameweeks, including three goals and an assist. He has also continued to be heavily involved in United's chance creation.

The biggest attraction is his likely position. Pochettino has traditionally favored a system that can put a creative No. 10 at the heart of the attack. Fernandes should be a natural fit for that role, particularly because he also carries penalty responsibility.

That combination of position, set pieces and creative involvement gives him one of the highest ceilings in the United squad. The concern is that £11.6m is a lot to spend on a player whose team has struggled badly.

Still, if Pochettino can get United's attack functioning again, Fernandes is probably the first player to benefit.

Mbeumo could be the most interesting United attacker under Pochettino. The former Brentford star was already one of the better United options before the managerial change, and his profile looks particularly well suited to Pochettino's attacking structure.

Mbeumo began the season strongly, scoring twice and assisting once in the opening three Gameweeks while collecting 21 FPL points. More importantly, his underlying numbers were encouraging: he recorded four big chances and nine shots in the box during that opening spell. That goal threat is exactly what makes him attractive.

Pochettino's teams have generally used wide attackers aggressively, while his full-backs provide much of the width. That can allow the wingers to move into narrower, more dangerous positions closer to the striker and attacking midfielder. For Mbeumo, that could mean more touches in the box rather than simply being asked to hold the width.

At £7.9m, he is also considerably cheaper than Fernandes and offers a more manageable route into the United attack.

Cunha is another player who could benefit significantly from the managerial change. The Brazilian's versatility makes him particularly interesting. He can operate as a forward, play behind the striker or drift into wider areas, which gives Pochettino plenty of tactical options. That flexibility could be valuable because United currently have a problem at centre-forward.

Benjamin Šeško remains sidelined with a significant lower-leg injury and is not expected back until 2027. That leaves United without one of their main No. 9 options for the foreseeable future. Cunha could therefore become an important part of the solution.

A Pochettino system that gets Cunha closer to goal would be particularly attractive from an FPL perspective. Rather than relying purely on assists, the Brazilian has the ability to create chances for himself and attack the box.

The problem is that we don't yet know exactly where Pochettino will deploy him. That uncertainty makes Cunha slightly less appealing than Fernandes or Mbeumo right now, but his upside is substantial.

If you're looking for a cheaper route into the new Pochettino attack, Amad deserves attention. The Ivorian is exactly the type of young, technically gifted player Pochettino could be interested in developing. Pochettino has a long history of giving young players significant responsibility. His previous clubs saw him work with and develop players such as Dele Alli and Cole Palmer, among others. That makes Amad particularly intriguing at £5.7m.

The issue, as always with a player at this price point, is minutes. There are several attacking options competing for places at United, and Pochettino has not even taken his first training session with the squad yet. Amad therefore feels more like a player to monitor than an immediate transfer.

If he becomes a regular starter, however, the price could quickly become very attractive.

Mainoo is another name worth keeping on the radar. The 21-year-old midfielder has reportedly been one of United's better performers this season, and Pochettino's preference for energetic, technically capable young players could work in his favor.

At £5.5m, Mainoo isn't being bought as a premium attacking midfielder. His appeal would instead come from regular starts, defensive contribution points and the possibility of becoming more involved in United's possession game. Pochettino's high-intensity style could also suit Mainoo's athletic profile.

The Fantasy problem is obvious: his attacking output is unlikely to match the likes of Fernandes, Mbeumo or Cunha. But if Pochettino makes him a regular starter in a more advanced role, his value could rise quickly.

Dorgu is perhaps the most interesting wild-card option. The Danish international is classified as a midfielder in FPL, despite operating primarily from a defensive or wing-back position. That classification limits his appeal because he does not receive clean-sheet points as a defender. However, his attacking potential makes him worth monitoring.

Dorgu scored and assisted after coming off the bench against Aston Villa before the international break, showing the attacking threat he can offer.

His problem is fitness. Dorgu is currently listed with a hamstring issue and could return around the Liverpool match, meaning his availability needs to be monitored before making any move. If Pochettino decides to use him aggressively as a wing-back or wide midfielder, Dorgu could become a fascinating differential.

This is where you need to be patient as an FPL manager. United have conceded 19 goals in their first 10 Premier League matches and have yet to keep a clean sheet. Only Ipswich Town and Crystal Palace have conceded more.

Pochettino should improve the organization eventually, but expecting an immediate defensive transformation would be risky. His first Premier League match is away to Liverpool, which is hardly the fixture you want to use as evidence that United's defense has suddenly become trustworthy.

There are also several injury concerns, with players including Luke Shaw and Harry Maguire among those potentially returning around the Liverpool match. For that reason, I would avoid rushing into United defenders.

If the defense improves over the next few Gameweeks, then the conversation changes.

But the Fixture Run Is the Real Attraction. The biggest reason to consider Manchester United players isn't actually Pochettino himself. It's the fixture run.

United's first game under the new manager is Liverpool away, followed by Sporting in the Champions League. But the Premier League schedule then becomes much more appealing:

The fixture ticker ranks United third for difficulty over the four Gameweeks after the Liverpool match, according to Fantasy Football Scout's latest analysis.

That is where the opportunity lies. An FPL manager does not necessarily need to buy a United player before Liverpool away. Waiting until after that match could actually be the smarter strategy, particularly if Pochettino's first lineup gives us clues about his preferred system and attacking roles.

In reality, Pochettino may not have time to transform Manchester United overnight. But for FPL managers, we may not need a transformation. We just need to identify the players who benefit first.
