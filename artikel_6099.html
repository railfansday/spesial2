Two American Airlines 737s Collide On Taxiway At Reagan National, One Loses Winglet

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Two American Airlines Boeing 737-800s collided while lining up on a taxiway at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA) on Friday evening, with images of the aftermath showing one jet had its winglet torn off. Moments after the collision, a pilot told air traffic control (ATC) that the aircraft had moved "slightly over the hold short line" and had "swapped paint" with the American jet next to it.

The mishap occurred at around 5:10 PM local time near Runway 19 and involved American Flight 638 to Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport (DFW) and American Flight 2152 to Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD). It is the second time in less than 20 months that two American-branded aircraft have collided while waiting to depart from DCA, following a similar wingtip collision in April 2025.

Flight tracking data shows that both 737s had taxied out and were lining up waiting to enter Runway 19, with Flight 2152 lined up behind Flight 638. When the controller instructed Flight 638 to hold short of the runway, one of its pilots reported that they had collided with a fellow American 737, before stating that they were unsure whether the two aircraft were still attached.

A pilot onboard Flight 2152 then confirmed, "Oh yeah, we got clipped," before stating that the two 737s had separated. Debris was then reported, with a pilot stating that a "whole winglet" had been torn off and was on the ground. Airport operations subsequently recovered the winglet, and both aircraft taxied back to their gates for inspection. An inbound Envoy Air aircraft was also ordered by ATC to go-around immediately after the incident.

In a statement to Simple Flying, American said that "the wingtips of both Boeing 737-800s made contact," and that both planes had been taken out of service for maintenance inspections. The airline added that passengers on both flights would depart later on Friday evening. Tracking data shows that Flight 2152 left at around 11:00 PM, while Flight 638 departed at 11:20 PM, representing a delay of around six hours for both flights. An American spokesperson said,

"While taxiing for departure at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA), an American Airlines aircraft made contact with another American aircraft."

Hold short lines consist of four yellow lines painted across a taxiway before it meets a runway, and are there to prevent potentially dangerous runway incursions. Two solid lines are on the taxiway side, while two dashed lines are on the runway side. Pilots must stop before the solid lines unless ATC has cleared them to enter the runway.

The taxiway leading to DCA's Runway 19 is wide enough to accommodate two aircraft lined up side-by-side, an arrangement attempted by both jets on Friday. The 737-800 has a wingspan of 117 feet 5 inches (35.8 meters) including winglets, and pilots are expected to maintain situational awareness to ensure sufficient clearance from other aircraft.

The Federal Aviation Administration has confirmed it is investigating the incident, while the extent of the damage will determine whether the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) also becomes involved. Under the NTSB's definitions, an incident without serious injuries is only classified as an accident if an aircraft sustains "substantial damage." Notably, this category specifically excludes damage to wingtips.

Winglets can really cut fuel consumption and costs, but engineers have to carefully weigh down sides.

Friday's incident is the latest in a series of ground collisions at major US airports in recent weeks. On November 14, a UPS Airlines Boeing 767 freighter and a Copa Airlines Boeing 737-800 collided at Miami International Airport (MIA) after the cargo jet taxied past the point where it had been instructed to give way.

On October 6, a Delta Air Lines Airbus A350 clipped the horizontal stabilizer of an Air Canada Boeing 777 being towed at Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), leaving its winglet lodged in the 777's tail. Friday's collision also has similarities to an incident at DCA in April 2025, when two American Eagle regional jets clipped wings while waiting to depart, one of which had several members of Congress on board.
