China’s factories are growing profits again, but the AI boom is picking the winners

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

China's industrial profits in October grew at their fastest pace since May, supported by an AI-driven electronics boom and higher energy prices, while consumer-facing industries squeezed by weak demand continued to be a drag.

Profits at sizeable industrial firms surged 20.1% in October from a year earlier, according to data released Friday by the National Bureau of Statistics. For the first 10 months this year, the earnings grew 16% year on year, having risen 15.5% through September.

That extends a notable rebound in 2026 after three years of declines followed by a tepid 0.6% growth last year, with profits on track to log their strongest annual expansion since 2021.

Despite the sharp headline figure, "the recovery has yet to broaden into a more durable improvement in corporate profitability," said Tianchen Xu, senior economist at Economist Intelligence Unit, as the rebound was largely led by upstream sectors amid higher energy prices.

China's economy has been running at two speeds, with AI-related manufacturing and exports powering growth while household consumption has been lagging. Retail sales grew a smaller-than-expected 0.4% in October, and fixed-asset investment steepened declines to 8.1% in the first 10 months.

Profit gains were concentrated in a set of sectors tied to the global AI buildout and higher commodity prices, while consumer-facing manufacturers struggled to pass on higher input costs, squeezing margins.

Earnings in the electronics sector doubled in the first 10 months from the same period last year, as demand for AI computing power lifted memory chips and related manufacturing, said Yu Weining, chief statistician of the NBS.

Profits in high-tech manufacturing jumped 50.9%, outpacing the industry as a whole, Yu added. Makers of spacecraft and launch vehicles reported a 63.2% rise.

Commodity producers also saw sharp growth in profits as demand tied to renewable energy and AI pushed up copper and aluminum prices, Yu said. Petroleum processors swung to a combined profit from a loss a year earlier.

Showcasing an increasingly bifurcated economy, industries tied to consumers and construction recorded steepening losses. Automakers saw profits drop 26% in the first 10 months, wider than the previous month, as costs outpaced a modest revenue increase.

Producers of construction materials such as cement and glass, and furniture makers continued to suffer the fallout from a prolonged real estate slump, with profits falling 46.7% and 52.4%, respectively.

Accounts receivable, or money owed to businesses by their customers, and finished-goods inventories also grew faster than revenue, official data showed, underscoring the strain on companies across sectors.

Overall activity in October picked up "substantially" compared to the past couple of months, expanding 2.4% from a year earlier, according to Capital Economics' China Activity Proxy, a gauge that excludes official data in favor of low-profile indicators, such as freight and port traffic, that are "less politically-sensitive and so less likely to be manipulated."

The growth in October was led by strong external demand and exports, even as manufacturing activity for domestic goods languished, said Leah Fahy, senior China economist at Capital Economics.

Local governments stepped up fiscal spending for the first time since February last month, Fahy said, but that "had little impact" on arresting the property slump and reviving domestic demand.

"Local governments [are] clearly struggling to make full use of their allocated funds, [and] there is a limit to how much fiscal policy will be able to support the economy," Fahy added.

The AI-driven growth in external demand has reduced the need for Chinese policymakers to ramp up stimulus to support domestic demand and the overall economy. China's GDP growth of 4.6% in the first three quarters has put Beijing's annual growth target of 4.5-5% within reach.

As global AI-related investment starts to slow, exports would also become a "waning prop to growth," Fahy said, with overall economic expansion to decline to just 2.8% next year in her estimates, from 3.9% in 2025.

Adding to fresh headwinds on trade, the U.S. proposed new tariffs that include a 20% headline rate on Chinese goods and punitive rates on several Asian economies that analysts say have been targeted for transshipments to China. The levies would rise to 40% if the U.S.-China truce does not get extended beyond January.

"Chinese firms now export intermediate goods to the rest of Asia and reach the U.S. indirectly," said Alicia Garcia Herrero, chief economist for APAC at Natixis, adding that economies with more Chinese inputs may face higher tariff risks.
