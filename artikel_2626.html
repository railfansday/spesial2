NFL Thanksgiving Bets: TD Props For Amon-Ra St. Brown, Josh Allen, More

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

The traditional NFL Thanksgiving feast serves up three appetizing selections this year.

Not only are we treated to a pair of battles between fierce and long-time division adversaries to open up the day, but the day wraps up with the latest chapter in the Patrick Mahomes-Josh Allen rivalry.

As is customary on the holiday slate, there are a slew of superstars taking the field in each game, with the likes of Jahmyr Gibbs, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Caleb Williams, Saquon Barkley, Dak Prescott and CeeDee Lamb just some of the prominent names.

With the stage set, let's dive into three TD scorer props worth considering for today's Thanksgiving pigskin extravaganza:

Amon-Ra St. Brown: To Score 2+ Touchdowns (+440 on FanDuel Sportsbook)

Javonte Williams: 2nd Half Anytime TD Scorer (+165 on FanDuel Sportsbook)

Josh Allen: Anytime TD Scorer (-109 on BetRivers Sportsbook)

With so many talented players on Thursday's docket, it's actually a bit challenging to find appealing prices on standard touchdown bets for some of the superstars. St. Brown is one good example, but we're navigating the odds conundrum by getting a bit bold.

St. Brown lit up the Bears for three touchdown catches the last time he saw them at Ford Field back in Week 2 of last season. This season, the standout receiver has already produced a trio of two-touchdown tallies, with two of those coming on the fast track of his home turf.

St. Brown is averaging just under two red-zone targets per game, and he's facing a Bears defense that's allowed an NFL-high 12.8 yards per completion on the road. Chicago has surrendered a relatively modest eight touchdown catches in 10 games to wide receivers, but the fact the Bears are also conceding a robust 15.1 yards per catch to the position ups the chances of St. Brown getting loose for a catch-and-run TD.

St. Brown already has nine receptions of 20-plus yards this season, and he has 37 career touchdown receptions at home. With the Lions carrying an implied team total of 27 points for Thursday's game, a multi-TD game is within St. Brown's range of outcomes.

Williams has been a workhorse for the Cowboys over the last several weeks, averaging 20.6 carries per game over the last five contests. The veteran back has four total touchdowns (three rushing, one receiving) as well in that span, and he has a solid outlook for at least one score again Thursday.

The Cowboys carry an implied team total of 28.5 points for their holiday divisional clash against the Eagles, and Williams comes in averaging just over a touchdown per game (12 in 11 contests this season). He also has 51 red-zone touches this season, averaging nearly 5.0 per game.

Williams is facing an Eagles defense that's allowed 121.8 rushing yards per road game at 4.2 yards per carry, and that's surrendered six rush TDs to running backs. Then, seven of Williams' 10 rushing touchdowns have notably come in the second halves of games, which gives us a chance to capture a plus-money bet for him by backing the idea of him scoring a second-half TD.

The game line also supports the notion of Williams having a busy second half, as Dallas is up to an 8.5-point home favorite. If oddsmakers are reasonably accurate in their estimation, game script should tilt very favorably to the run over the final two quarter as the Cowboys look to ice the game against the Joe Milton-led Eagles.

Allen has been his usual prolific self when it comes to touchdowns this season, scoring 25 (14 passing, 11 rushing) over 10 games. The Bills have been the AFC's most lethal offense, and Buffalo is averaging 31.3 points per game and a co-NFL-high 4.1 red-zone scoring attempts per contest as well.

The superstar signal-caller has four games apiece with multiple passing and rushing scores, and Thursday's showdown against Mahomes and company seems like a conducive environment for Allen to take matters into his own hands, considering how likely it is the game turns into a wire-to-wire affair.

Allen has 21 red-zone rushing touches so far, and he's facing a Chiefs defense Thursday that's allowed 3.0 red-zone scoring attempts per game. Additionally, even the weather concerns surrounding Thursday's game – which includes the potential for wind gusts up to 30 mph – could work in Allen's favor in terms of a possible rushing TD.

Inclement weather could encourage head coach Joe Brady to keep the ball on the ground near the goal line to cut down the chances of errant passes. What's more, Allen has seven career rush TDs against Kansas City over 10 total games, including a two-TD day last November in a 28-21 win for the Bills.
