Why is Championship cheat code Parker finding life tough at West Ham?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

West Ham on course for lowest finish since 1957 following six-game winless streak; Scott Parker unable to arrest slide of side accused of lacking leaders and character following Premier League relegation last season

Football Journalist @Ronnabe

The hope was that relegation would at least give West Ham the opportunity to reset, enjoy some winning football and, ideally, bounce straight back stronger. The reality, however, is proving rather different.

There was a sense that, had Nuno Espirito Santo been given more time, he might have kept West Ham in the Premier League. After all, 25 points from the final 17 games of the season suggested he was beginning to steady the ship and that, with a little longer, he could have turned things around.

Yet another 17 games later, Nuno is out of a job, while Scott Parker - a man who metaphorically bleeds claret and blue and boasts a formidable record in the Championship - has so far failed to make the anticipated impact as his replacement.

The Hammers have not finished a season outside the top half of the second tier for 71 years. However, an unbalanced squad and a dressing room increasingly feeling the pressure have fuelled fears that this remarkable record could finally come to an end this season - for the first time since the days of the Suez Crisis.

There is no question a squad boasting England winger Jarrod Bowen, Czech Republic stalwart Tomas Soucek and a number of other seasoned top-flight and established international players should be doing far better than 13th in the table and 11 points off the top.

But the logic behind West Ham's plight goes far beyond technical ability. They are among the slowest starters in the Championship and, despite losing only five games, have conceded first in 10 separate matches - second only to basement side Derby.

The Hammers need to play their way into games, both conceding a league-high and scoring a league-low proportion of their goals before half-time.

In a young dressing room, featuring nine players aged 21 or under, there have been justified questions over the lack of leaders beyond Bowen and, more damningly, a perceived lack of character - something Parker has himself felt the need to address from the touchline, having so far failed to arrest the slide that cost Nuno his job.

He recently told TalkSport: "You're coming out of the Premier League, the lights and the big stadiums and the clubs you play against.

"Then all of a sudden you have the big change of playing the Championship with certain habits and things you need to be. It's key in understanding that, and if there's a player here playing in this division at this moment in time, it's because it is what it is.

"We need to show the best versions of ourselves if we're going to get out of this division."

If anyone knows what it takes to do exactly that it is Parker, having achieved three promotions with three different clubs across as many seasons in the second tier.

In all of those three previous roles, he enjoyed the holy grail of a pre-season with his squad. But there is no such time to learn and tweak at the London Stadium, where the former England midfielder was thrust into his first competitive game at Cardiff only 12 hours after taking the job.

That would be challenging enough, but with the sheer number of issues Parker has to address, he has faced more spinning plates than he can handle at once in his first weeks in the role.

The Hammers are comfortably the Championship's highest scorers with their issues largely at the other end of the pitch, and frustration which has begun to spill over into the stands.

Goalkeeper Mads Hermansen has been dropped after drawing the ire of the home support, with homegrown replacement Finlay Herrick an obvious crowd favourite but struggling to adapt to the physical demands of the Championship. He was also largely at fault for Preston's opener from a corner last weekend.

Max Kilman's nightmare spell in east London has continued in the absence of Axel Disasi, who was unwilling to drop down to the Championship and turn his loan spell permanent in the summer after playing a major part in the Hammers' upturn last season.

There have been calls for Nottingham Forest loanee Morato to take his place, but nine of his 10 starts this season have come at left-back. The Hammers are also monitoring the situation of Man Utd defender Ayden Heaven, with the 20-year-old open to leaving Old Trafford in January.

But even in attack, things have begun to dry up. West Ham drew a blank for the first time all season in Tuesday night's 1-0 reverse at Portsmouth, after a toothless draw with Preston in their previous game.

It is far from conclusive proof, but the Hammers appear to miss January signing Pablo, who has been conspicuous by his absence from every matchday squad since Parker's arrival. West Ham picked up 13 points from the six games he started this season, despite the 22-year-old scoring just once.

There are also growing questions over the reasons behind his mysterious exile, adding to a wider sense of unease at the London Stadium which Parker must find a solution to sooner rather than later.

It is still barely two years since West Ham were playing in the Europa League quarter-finals and finishing ninth in the Premier League. Right now, however, that feels a lifetime ago.
