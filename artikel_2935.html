Five takeaways from Florida State's 87-63 win over Green Bay

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Florida State men's basketball extended its winning streak to three games Friday afternoon, defeating Green Bay 87-63 at the Donald L. Tucker Center to kick off Homecoming weekend.

The Seminoles (5-1) overcame another sluggish start before pulling away in the second half, outscoring the Phoenix 46-30 after halftime to secure their fifth victory of the season. Shon Abaev and Sebastian Rancik led Florida State with 24 points apiece, while Anthony Robinson II returned to the starting lineup after missing Tuesday's victory over Ball State.

Here are five takeaways from Florida State's latest home victory.

Florida State's offense continues to revolve around Rancik, but Friday offered another glimpse of just how explosive the Seminoles can be when Abaev is playing at his best.

The two forwards combined for 48 of Florida State's 87 points, accounting for 55.2% of the team's scoring. Abaev delivered a season-high 24 points, adding four rebounds and three assists while shooting 7-of-11 from the field, including 3-of-6 from beyond the arc.

Rancik was equally impressive, finishing with 24 points, five rebounds and three assists while connecting on seven of his nine field-goal attempts.

Their contrasting offensive styles give Loucks an intriguing combination. Rancik has established himself as a reliable scoring option, while Abaev's ability to attack defenders and create difficult shots provides another dimension.

Florida State welcomed back starting point guard Anthony Robinson II after the Missouri transfer missed Tuesday's victory over Ball State with foot and ankle discomfort.

Robinson immediately reminded the Seminoles why his presence is so valuable, contributing 13 points, seven rebounds and three assists in approximately 30 minutes. Beyond his scoring, Robinson provided another experienced ball-handler capable of organizing the offense and creating opportunities for teammates.

Perhaps the most encouraging development Friday had little to do with Florida State's offensive performance.

After allowing Ball State to make 15 three-pointers Tuesday, the Seminoles held Green Bay to just six makes on 22 attempts from beyond the arc. The Phoenix shot 27.3% from three-point range, a significant improvement for Florida State's perimeter defense after surrendering 42.9% against the Cardinals. Florida State also recorded nine steals and five blocked shots while forcing 14 turnovers.

The defensive effort became particularly noticeable after halftime, when the Seminoles limited Green Bay to 30 points and gradually extended an eight-point halftime advantage into a comfortable victory.

Florida State's three-point shooting has been inconsistent throughout the opening weeks of the season, but the Seminoles have repeatedly found success by attacking the basket and drawing fouls.

That trend continued against Green Bay. Florida State converted 27 of 34 free-throw attempts (79.4%), while the Phoenix managed just five makes on 11 attempts.

Despite shooting just 32% from beyond the arc, the Seminoles generated enough offense through their aggressiveness to overcome stretches of inconsistent perimeter shooting. For Loucks, maintaining that willingness to attack the basket could be essential when Florida State encounters more disciplined ACC defenses.

Freshman center Marcis Ponder entered Friday's matchup following one of the best performances of his young career, recording 11 points and a career-high 16 rebounds against Ball State.

However, the 7-footer struggled to make an impact against Green Bay, finishing scoreless with three rebounds in just 14 minutes. It marked a significant departure from his previous outings, particularly after recording three double-doubles through Florida State's first five games.

Despite Ponder's limited production, the Seminoles still controlled the rebounding battle 41-27, showing they are not dependednt on him to win on the glass.

Friday's victory wasn't flawless, particularly considering Green Bay briefly held a five-point lead during the first half. However, Florida State ultimately built a lead as large as 26 points and delivered a more convincing second-half performance than it did against Ball State.

The Seminoles return to action Tuesday, Nov. 24, when they host UNC Asheville at 9 p.m. ET inside the Tucker Center.
