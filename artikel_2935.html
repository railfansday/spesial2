The First AI Stock I Plan to Buy in 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Investors should still be considering the world's most popular AI stock.

By Ryan Vanzo – Nov 28, 2026 at 12:00AM EST

Volatility continues for artificial intelligence (AI) stocks. Long-term investors should view today's market conditions as an opportunity.

The AI opportunity isn't going anywhere. Experts still forecast sizable demand growth for years, if not decades, to come.

"As we prepare to turn the page on 2026, the data center build-out continues at full throttle," research from Boston Consulting Group concludes. "Yet even spending at this rate is not enough to meet the growing need for DC supply, brought about by the rising adoption of agentic AI," the firm warns. Boston Consulting Group "still expects the high rate of spending to continue through 2031."

Right now is the perfect time for patient investors to shop the AI stock bargain bin. And there's one popular AI stock every investor should strongly consider as a core portfolio position.

If AI spending remains elevated for years to come, Nvidia (NVDA +0.80%) becomes a promising core position for most growth-oriented investors.

But isn't Nvidia facing a lot of uncertainty right now? Absolutely. And that's exactly why shares look attractive.

On paper, everything is going right for Nvidia. As mentioned, the scale of AI spending forecasts remains strong, even if the exact pace is currently in question. The GPU maker holds a sizable market share in AI data centers, and its industry-leading margins indicate that the market views its products as superior.

Meanwhile, pricing and revenue potential of future chip architectures appear strong. CEO Jensen Huang recently revealed that Nvidia's next-gen Vera Rubin chips will generate $40 billion in revenue per gigawatt, up from just $25 billion per gigawatt for Nvidia's Grace Blackwell chips. Morgan Stanley estimates that a Vera Rubin VR200 rack will be priced at roughly $7.8 million, up from roughly $4 million for a GB300 rack.

To be clear, Nvidia faces meaningful near-term uncertainty. Will Huang remain as CEO for the long term? How impactful will regulatory actions and export restrictions be to Nvidia's bottom line? Can AI firms train competing chip architectures to be just as productive, or even end up producing their own chips in-house?

A negative answer to any one of these questions could significantly lower Nvidia's valuation. But at 17 times earnings, most of the uncertainty is arguably already priced in. Nvidia -- the world's biggest and highest-quality chip producer that operates in a multi-year, perhaps even a multi-decade growth window -- now trades at less than 10 times forward earnings. A share buyback program with $227 billion remaining could compound investor returns if shares are indeed severely undervalued.

I expect Nvidia shares to be highly volatile in the coming weeks and months, given ongoing uncertainty. There's no guarantee that today's valuation won't get even cheaper. But if it does, Nvidia will quickly move from an attractive investment candidate to my top stock pick for 2027.
