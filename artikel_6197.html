War Insurance Dries Up for Gulf Events as U.S.-Iran Conflict Continues

Gulf events can still insure against cancellation but not against war. That is pushing organizers to postpone events or to take on the risk themselves. It’s a growing issue, and Saudi Arabia’s calendar has been paying the price.

Event organizers in the Gulf can still insure against cancellation for many reasons, such as bad weather, a declared period of national mourning, or an IT failure. What they increasingly can’t buy is protection if an event is canceled because of war or political violence, industry executives told Skift.

Standard event-cancellation policies exclude war and political violence, so organizers routinely have to buy that coverage as a separate layer. Since the U.S.-Iran war began in late February, it is something that has become very hard to find for Gulf events.

Only one insurer, possibly two on very limited terms, is currently willing to consider war exposure for events in the region, according to Panteha Peters, head of sports, entertainment, media and special risks at insurance broker Marsh Middle East and Africa. It does so case by case, and generally no earlier than about one week before the event.

War and political violence cover for events in the Gulf has largely disappeared, and organizers are absorbing the risk themselves, industry executives told Skift. Standard event-cancellation policies exclude war, so that protection is normally bought as a separate layer — and since the U.S.-Iran war began in late February, only one insurer, possibly two on limited terms, will consider war exposure for Gulf events, according to Marsh Middle East and Africa's Panteha Peters, typically case by case and no earlier than about a week before the event. Marsh said in May that political violence premiums in the Gulf have risen at least 20-fold since the war began, while capacity to write that cover shrank from about $1 billion to roughly $200 million. Saudi Arabia is the sharpest pressure point: Morningstar DBRS's Marcos Alvarez said the Houthi missile and drone campaign on Riyadh, Abha, and Dammam in October turned war from a theoretical exclusion into a live, priced peril, and a long list of events has slipped, including the Future Investment Initiative conference, the EU-Gulf summit, Intersec Saudi Arabia 2026, the Saudi Retail Forum, the 2027 Dakar Rally, the Saudi International golf tournament, the Red Sea Film Festival, and Riyadh Fashion Week. Pricing for standard cancellation cover has generally held stable this year, Gallagher's Yasmin Dacuycuy said, so the squeeze is concentrated in the war layer. Events that proceed are doing so with lower limits, more exclusions, and in some cases no cancellation insurance at all.
