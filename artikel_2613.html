NBC's Mike Tomlin gives blunt assessment of Steelers' loss to Bengals

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Mike Tomlin did not dial back his criticism at the end of his first game covering his former team as a broadcaster.

The Pittsburgh Steelers coach for 19 seasons from 2007 to 2025 was on the crew for NBC's "Sunday Night Football" presentation of the crucial Week 10 matchup with the Cincinnati Bengals. And he was blunt in his assessment of what led to a 38-18 blowout defeat.

"You know, it was breakdowns in so many areas," Tomlin said during the network's postgame show. "I just think the first thing you've got to acknowledge is they just got smashed. They got smashed on defense. They got smashed on offense.

"They didn't get enough done in either of the phases for the storyline to take hold. And so there's something to be learned from it, but only if and when you acknowledge what happened. And I think that's the first thing. They got smashed tonight."

In fact, Bengals quarterback Joe Burrow dominated Steelers coordinator Patrick Graham's defense. He completed 35-of-44 passes for 409 yards and four touchdowns.

As a team, the Bengals outgained the Steelers, 484-222, as Pittsburgh quarterback Aaron Rodgers struggled to get the ball to playmakers, including DK Metcalf, Jaylen Warren and Pat Freiermuth.

Almost all of the offense's production came in the second half once the game was well out of reach. Cincinnati was so comfortably ahead that they could take Burrow out of the game for a good chunk of the fourth quarter.

Tomlin faulted both units for the success Burrow had in his comments with NBC colleague Maria Taylor.

"Yeah, we didn't get enough four-man rush, but they didn't get in enough one-dimensional passing circumstances," he said. "The Steelers didn't possess the ball enough. They didn't convert enough third downs, particularly early on. And so, you know, when you get a solid lead, then you're not beholden to situations."

Taylor quickly teased him for letting the objectivity slip a little bit in his answer about what worked for Burrow and Co.

"Did I hear you say 'we' one time?" she asked quizzically.

"Maybe," Tomlin said. "I've got a little Jason Kelce in me."

He recovered quickly to wrap up his thoughts on the damaging night that it was for his Steelers.

The team now falls one full game behind the Bengals in the division standings. Both teams have manageable schedules the rest of the way, which could make it difficult for Pittsburgh to make up ground.

They'll look to bounce back with a road trip to Philadelphia, where they haven't won since 1965, next Sunday afternoon.

"To come into a game like this and expect the type of battle we expected tonight, there's certainly some disappointment, just from a fan's perspective."
