Celtic have opened the door to Rangers being 'No1 in Scotland again' - ex-manager

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Celtic are not getting enough elite performances from their star players - and that has opened the door to Rangers.

That is according to ex-manager and experienced former Tottenham, Aston Villa and Everton scout, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The Hoops have struggled for consistency this season so far, winning just five of their last 12 games across all competitions.

Martin O'Neill had not expected to remain in charge of Celtic when he returned last season, and he has now faced mounting pressure after taking the job permanently once more.

But the problems at Celtic Park are considered to come from the top, with largest individual shareholder Dermot Desmond among several targets of discontent among the fanbase.

The Scottish champions currently sit top of the Premiership, with Rangers to have the chance to go within a point when they play their game in hand.

Kasper Hogh is one example of a player struggling to adapt to life since making the switch to Celtic.

The striker was a force with Bodo/Glimt in Norway, but has failed to score a single goal since netting a hat-trick on just his second appearance for the club in August.

"Just have a look at what's happened to Celtic as a team. They've been rather inconsistent," King told Football Insider when asked about Hogh.

"They've performed badly in Europe. There seems to be problems every week within the unit, and it's all about confidence.

"The centre forward's got to be scoring goals, but he can only score goals if the service is right. And now he's getting into a situation where Celtic haven't exactly been setting the Scottish league alight.

"They've been losing games which you wouldn't expect them to lose, which has allowed Rangers to start looking as though they could be the number one team in Scotland again.

"There are things not right within that football club as we speak, Celtic."

O'Neill's return last season ultimately saved Celtic from an abysmal season.

Brendan Rodgers had resigned in October and his eventual replacement, Wilfried Nancy, lasted just over a month before he was sacked.

O'Neill returned again and led his team to the Scottish Premiership and cup double - which many thought would be his last feat in charge of the Hoops.

But in extending his contract, Celtic have kicked the can down the road for next summer, when they will inevitably have to make a decision.

Then, without O'Neill to hold them back, the true extent of the problems at Parkhead may well emerge.
