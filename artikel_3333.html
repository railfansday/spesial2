All 50 Episodes Of Netflix's Psychological Thriller Series Masterpiece Are Perfect From Start To Finish

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Netflix's roster of thriller series includes a psychological masterpiece that released 50 episodes and managed to be perfect from start to finish. Netflix has found a lot of success with thriller shows of the most varied subgenres. For instance, Netflix's 2026 TV show catalog has included thrillers with supernatural twists, such as The Body and Something Very Bad Is Going to Happen.

The streaming service has also been the home of returning spy thrillers, including The Night Agent and Black Doves, as well as crime thrillers like His & Hers and I Will Find You. That said, one of the streamer's most popular subgenres continues to be that of psychological thriller series. There is just something special about getting to deconstruct the psyche of its characters, be them detectives, killers, or more.

That is why David Fincher's Mindhunter became such a beloved show on Netflix. Even though it did not pack the same kind of action of other detective series, Mindhunter used dialogue and characters with complex moral codes, sets of rules, and more to keep the viewers on the edge of their seat. This is what another show managed to do for a long five seasons.

Penn Badgley played the serial killer Joe Goldberg on Netflix's You, which originally premiered on Lifetime in 2018 before it became a Netflix Original series and ended its run with season 5's release on April 24, 2025. You is a high-stakes psychological thriller series, and even though its plot could have gotten old, the show delivered a wild 50-episode ride that was perfect from start to finish.

You's premise was simple, as Joe Goldberg was a serial killer who made a different woman the object of his obsession pretty much every season, murdering whoever got on the way to "true love" or risked exposing his dark secrets. However, You always remained a fresh addition to the genre in a few ways, chief among them being the fact that the audience was brought inside Joe's mind, with Badgley narrating the character's every move and thought. As such, You is one of the most entertaining and immersive psychological thriller series to date, with Joe seeing himself as the hero of his story.

This led to many viewers hoping Joe would avoid getting caught, escape near-death scenarios, and somehow find a way to change and quit his dark impulses throughout You's five seasons. That said, You was a TV show that kept getting more unhinged, and with every new season, fans got to see a different side of Joe, slowly understanding the character more. It was especially interesting to see how You kept reinventing itself over the years, from NYC to California suburbs to London and more, the Netflix psychological thriller series always felt fresh, with brand-new characters, conflicts, locations, and more.

You holds a stellar 89% critics' score on Rotten Tomatoes for its five seasons.

Joe himself was an ever-changing beast in the hit series. When You started, Badgley's character was working in a bookstore in New York City and had no one. Throughout the show, Joe would become a father, get married and try to adapt to a quiet suburban life, change his appearance and become a professor in London, and even have the full funding of a billionaire to cover up his murder spree.

You season 5 ended the show on a solid note, and while the final batch of 10 episodes did not revolve around one of Joe's strongest love interests, it was still a thrilling final season for the Netflix psychological thriller series that culminated in a fitting ending for its main character. Over the years, fans debated whether Joe would manage to escape punishment when all was said and done. Even though the serial killer was charismatic, that just would not have felt right, given how many lives Joe ruined.

Then comes the question of whether Joe should die. Such a fate would have both been disappointing for those fans who wanted the character to be free in the first place, but also to those that wanted Joe to pay for all the blood on his hands, as a quick death would really have been an act of mercy for the character. You's ending was perfect, with Joe sentenced to life in prison, as he is someone who fans watched yearn for connection for five seasons, so Joe being pretty much alone behind bars for the rest of his life was poetic justice in the You series finale.
