Personal Consumer Issues • Re: ventless dryer

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Thought I'd chime in here as I've used / owned many of these dryers over the years here in the UK. Some comments:

I would not use the term "ventless". I understand this to mean that there is not vent to the outdoors, which is a sensible use of the word. But I think of these units as venting into the room, rather than externally. Indeed with a strong enough unit, you can feel the air moving out the back, which for me is a vent.

Instead, the term "condensing" is how these are marketed here, and that fits better. Of course the idea is that the unit pulls (condenses) the water out of the air before expelling the air out of the ... vent (into the room).

Across the several units I have used, the effectiveness of pulling the water out of the air is much better than pulling the heat used by the dryer out of the air. In other words, I do not notice things like dampness / fogged windows when the dryer runs. It does however heat up the room. On a cold day this can be nice, but in certain climates / situations maybe less so.

We have had two versions of the space-saving combo washer-condenser dryers. Both were terrible. The big issue was that the capacities of the "washer mode" and "dryer mode" were different. I.e. you were supposed to take half the load out after the washing was complete, let the first half dry, then swap 'em. What a pain. We never did this, and got super-long drying cycles to provide damp wrinkly clothes. Totally sub-optimal. Maybe someone has solved this problem now, but I was very put off by the whole experience.

What has worked much better for us is the separate condenser-dryer / washer unit, where the capacities are matched. I have never separated cottons and synthetics. Far too lazy for that. I always use "low heat" mode, which supposedly helps clothes last longer. Loads aren't quick, I don't time them. They're monitored by a sensor (I use the "max dry" setting). Our dryer has a "extended wrinkle out" setting, that I also use. Basically this just turns the clothes for awhile even after they're dry. I just put it on in the evening and put them away the next morning. Clothes are always perfectly dry, and not at all wrinkly. Dump the condenser tank out, and it's all good.

The model we use is a Bosch Serie 4. Looks like similar ones are available in the US. It's our 2nd Bosch. The first lasted ca. 10 years before developing an electrical fault. I couldn't fix it myself, and calling someone out to fix it wasn't worth it, so time to get a new one. Our current one is several years old, and going strong. I will probably replace it with whatever the current high end Bosch model is when this one breaks. (Will avoid any "smart" model that connect to the internet though! )

Hope someone finds that helpful.
