If the Fed Hikes Rates This Year, I'm Loading Up on This 1 ETF

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Vanguard Energy ETF could be an even bigger sector exchange-traded fund star if the Fed raises interest rates again.

By Todd Shriber – Nov 21, 2026 at 1:08PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Vanguard Energy ETF ›

With just three weeks left before the Federal Reserve's December meeting, it's basically a foregone conclusion that the central bank will raise interest rates for a second time this year.

Speaking of interest rates, investors preparing for 2027 should note that Goldman Sachs recently forecast one more rate hike by the Fed next March, before the central bank begins to cut rates later in the year. We don’t know what the future holds, but the point is, it's nearly a sure thing that at least one more rate increase is on the way and more can't be ruled out.

Federal Reserve headquarters in Washington, D.C. Image source: Getty Images.

So now is an excellent time for equity investors of all stripes to understand that Fed actions affect various sectors in different ways. For example, the capital-intensive, debt-heavy utility sector is often vulnerable to higher borrowing costs when rates rise. In contrast, higher rates are seen as boons to financial services stocks due to net interest margin expansion.

Energy stocks have also shown strength as rates rise, suggesting that exchange-traded funds (ETFs) such as the Vanguard Energy ETF (VDE -0.73%) are worth considering ahead of the next Fed meeting and beyond.

Home to the largest domestic energy companies, the $11.9 billion Vanguard Energy ETF is one of this year's stars among basic sector ETFs, as highlighted by a year-to-date gain of 43% as I write this.

There's no denying that energy ETFs, including this Vanguard fund, are getting big boosts from oil prices driven up by current wars, but energy stocks also have a track record of performing well when interest rates rise. The Fed — whose main role is to promote “maximum employment” and “stable prices” — has already raised rates once this year, and 10- and 30-year Treasury yields recently touched their highest levels since 2002.

The bottom line is that energy stocks and ETFs such as this Vanguard fund are doing their jobs in a challenging interest rate environment, providing investors with effective portfolio hedges while stocks and bonds falter.

So it's pretty safe to say that we're in for at least one more rate hike, if not two, in the short term, but that doesn't make this Vanguard ETF a risk-free bet heading into 2027. Oil prices are notoriously volatile, and if new Democratic majorities in both houses of Congress succeed in compelling President Donald Trump to end the war in Iran, oil prices would likely tumble, potentially taking energy ETFs along for the ride.

Additionally, this ETF is top-heavy. It allocates nearly 40% of its weight to ExxonMobil (XOM +0.67%) and Chevron (CVX +0.91%). Those stocks have been sturdy this year, but if one sneezes, it's possible, if not probable, the other does the same, and this Vanguard ETF catches a cold.

Of course, there are no guarantees that any of those scenarios come to pass. I'm just laying out possible risks for investors evaluating this ETF.

There are plenty of bright spots to ponder, too. For example, Exxon and Chevron have strong balance sheets and are two of the energy patch's most dependable dividend growers. And in broader terms, the global energy sector is sporting the best earnings momentum of any sector over the past several months. On that basis, energy is even beating technology, and by a healthy margin at that. If that strength continues into 2027, the Vanguard Energy ETF won't need rising rates to thrive, but investors will certainly accept that assistance. And if the Fed raises rates this year, I plan to buy shares.
