So much pace and potential, not enough positive impact... surely Scotland must now redefine the role of Ben Gannon-Doak?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 12:41 EST, 14 November 2026 | Updated: 12:43 EST, 14 November 2026

In an age where much of the football on show in the English Premier League is so robotic as to be almost unwatchable, flashes of individual brilliance are increasingly becoming things to be cherished.

Across his time at Liverpool and Bournemouth, Ben Gannon-Doak has sporadically distinguished himself as someone who can light up a game by doing something out of the blue and out of the ordinary.

As many a defender will testify, that electric burst of pace across a short distance makes him unplayable at times. He has an X-factor which sets him apart from the crowd.

What a shame that injuries have restricted his number of appearances to this point. Since moving south from Celtic in 2022, including a loan spell with Middlesbrough, Gannon-Doak has played fewer than 50 times.

At the very time in his career when he could have done with smoothing off the rough edges, he’s spent far too long rehabilitating from one setback or another.

In the build-up to Friday’s match with North Macedonia, Sebastien Pocognoli spoke about how encouraged he was that the player was finally seeing more game time on the south coast, partly as a result of the team’s maiden voyage in Europe.

Ben Gannon-Doak started in a right wing-back role and had an energetic yet frustrating night

The hope of all concerned was that the recent run of matches which the winger had so evidently needed would translate into a more accomplished display in a Scotland jersey. Alas, this proved to be wishful thinking.

Even against very ordinary opposition, Gannon-Doak’s display proved to be another frustrating watch for the Tartan Army.

Time and time again, he brought them to the edge of their seats by facing up a white jersey and seemingly disappearing in a puff of smoke towards the line. Rarely, however, did the end product materialise.

For a winger with such blinding speed to continually struggle with the all-important final ball is a difficult thing to repeatedly witness.

We shall never know, of course, if the player’s crossing ability would be better by this stage if he hadn’t endured such wretched luck with injuries. But you suspect it’s now just about as good as it’s ever going to be.

The trouble for Pocognoli is that the quality of the delivery from wing-backs into the penalty box is arguably the most important facet of his preferred 3-4-3 system.

When it breaks down, as it did so often against the group outsiders, the whole thing threatens to grind to a halt.

Gannon-Doak's crossing remains an area of concern for Scotland given his wide position

On his record-equalling 102nd cap, Andy Robertson was also guilty of being wasteful on the other flank. Given we know what the skipper has delivered down the years, though, we can write that off as merely a bad night at the office.

The difference with Gannon-Doak is that we’ve rarely seen him tear it up on the flank in a Scotland jersey. So much pace and potential. Not enough positive outcomes when it comes to delivering crosses. In 21 matches, he has three assists and one goal. Those aren’t great numbers.

Just how long do you persevere with a player who doesn’t look to have the requisite skill set for such a specialised and vital position?

Aaron Hickey clearly doesn’t have the pace of the man who he replaced late on, yet he’s hardly a slouch. He is, though, more switched on defensively. And he does know how to cross a football.

It was telling that Gannon-Doak’s most positive contribution of the night against the group outsiders came when he left his station to move infield and take Oli McBurnie’s pass in his stride with the score standing at one-apiece.

His surge towards the goal initially looked like it had been hampered by a heavy touch only for VAR to correctly identify that he’d been impeded by the goalkeeper. He earned his corn in that moment.

John McGinn’s conversion from 12 yards, his second goal of the night, spared Pocognoli’s blushes after a display which didn’t exactly inspire.

That sequence of play certainly raised the possibility that Gannon-Doak could be utilised to better effect in another role.

Gannon-Doak's forward burst led to the award of a penalty and hinted at an alternative role

If played more centrally, his crossing – undoubtedly his weak spot – would not be such an issue. But the acceleration and driving runs would still be there to cause chaos in and around the penalty box. That will always be his strong suit.

To get the best out of him, maybe the manager needs to redefine him.

Any redeployment would, obviously, create another issue. If Pocognoli remains wedded to the current system, it seems likely that Billy Gilmour and Lewis Ferguson will occupy the two holding berths.

Ahead of them, and behind the main striker, McGinn and Scott McTominay are virtually undroppable. It might be the case then, that Gannon-Doak is eventually utilised as an impact sub. Yet, it would not be a plan without its merits.

He’s got a part to play in this set-up. Probably just not where he’s currently being positioned.

You’d be surprised, frankly, if he started in St Gallen on Monday. Switzerland are a far superior side to North Macedonia, already assured of promotion to League A by topping the section and looking to end this campaign with a flourish against a Scotland side they beat comfortably at Hampden.

They’ll ask far more questions of Pocognoli’s side. It feels like a task more suited to Hickey.

Scotland, collectively, will need to buck up their ideas if they are to stand any chance of emerging with a positive outcome or, at the least, some credit.

Scotland fans are desperate to see Gannon-Doak succeed but a little change might be needed

There was a flatness to Friday’s performance, especially in a first half in which the much-vaunted high-press was conspicuous by its absence.

The passing was too safe and predictable. The tempo was low and the play was unimaginative. By half-time, the visitors had fashioned the better chance when Enis Bardhi smashed the ball into the side net.

Scotland were markedly better after the turnaround. After Billy Gilmour’s scarcely believable miss, they were deserving of the opener which came when McGinn burst into the box and lashed home after fine work from Che Adams.

Stemming straight from a corner, North Macedonia’s equaliser – which ended their eight-game goal drought – asked a serious question of Pocognoli’s players.

To their credit, they stepped up with Gannon-Doak’s foray into the box causing the moment of indecision on which the game swung.

The manner of a first Hampden win for Pocognoli won’t live long in the memory. The fervent hope is that it proves to be the start of something.
