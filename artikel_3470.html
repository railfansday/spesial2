ESPN's Tom Jackson Had Health Dilemma Before His ‘Unexpected’ Death at 75

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Legendary ESPN broadcaster and former NFL star Tom Jackson was considering surgery before his sudden death on Friday, November 20.

Jackson’s death was “unexpected,” according to his former Denver Broncos teammate Bob Swenson.

“I talked to him every week, sometimes every day,” Swenson, 73, told the Denver Gazette’s Chris Tomasson on Sunday, November 22. “I talked to him, and he was going back and forth about getting a back operation, and then he said he was going to do it. Then I texted him, but I wasn’t getting a response the last couple of days.”

Swenson added, “He just said he was going to do the back surgery and he had some things going on with his leg. I was just waiting to hear back. I texted him, ‘Are you OK?’ I think I texted him on Thursday and Friday and no response. … I was just stunned.”

An official cause of death has not been released. Jackson was 75.

A member of the Broncos’ formidable “Orange Crush” defense, Jackson helped lead Denver to Super Bowl appearances 1978 and 1987. Jackson played all 14 of his NFL seasons with the Broncos before retiring at the conclusion of the 1987 season. He was named a First-Team All-Pro in 1977 and a three-time Pro Bowler in three consecutive seasons from 1977 to 1979.

For a younger generation, Jackson became well-known for his broadcasting career after joining ESPN in 1987. Jackson and broadcast partner Chris Berman became an iconic pair on the network’s Sunday NFL Countdown and NFL Primetime.

An emotional Berman, 71, announced Jackson’s death live on-air on Sunday, saying he had spoken with Jackson “at length” on Tuesday, November 17.

“Tom was poignant. He was active. This is a shock,” Berman said.

In a statement released by ESPN on Sunday, Berman called Jackson “one of the best human beings ever to walk the planet.”

“All of us are stunned,” Berman said. “I’m stunned, we’re stunned and you’re stunned. …  I was fortunate to work side by side with Tom for 30 years, primarily on NFL Primetime and Sunday NFL Countdown. It was a labor of love for our whole team, both in front of the camera and behind it. We always hoped our viewers enjoyed the shows as much as we did bringing it to them every week.”

Berman added, “Tom was so much more than an accomplished NFL analyst. There was nobody kinder, more thoughtful, more loyal or more engaging than him. And when it came to laughter, Tom’s was frequent and unmistakable. He is easily summed up by this classic old song: ‘He’s got sunshine on a cloudy day.’”

Jackson is survived by his wife, Jennifer, and his two daughters, Taylor and Morgan.
