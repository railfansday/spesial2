Black November arrives at Celtic as prosecution's case against Martin O'Neill grows

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

An audio clip appeared online this week of former Republic of Ireland winger James McClean winding back the clock to life as an international footballer under Martin O’Neill.

Promoting his new book, McClean offered the opinion that the current manager of Celtic was never terribly big on tactics.

The game, he believed, came down to eleven versus eleven and good players were expected to take the initiative and solve any problems on the pitch.

Sending the likes of Henrik Larsson, Chris Sutton, Alan Thompson and Bobo Balde in to the trenches, the strategy seemed to work like a dream. Steve Walford and John Robertson ran the training and the set piece drills at Barrowfield and results spoke for themselves.

The issue with the Ireland O’Neill managed 10 years ago was a squad full of young English Championship players who expected to be coached on everything.

Their world, said McClean, revolved around tactics, tactics, tactics. Some struggled to get their heads around O’Neill’s old school ways or cope with the administration of a few truths.

A damning indictment on the modern footballer, Celtic fans seized on McClean’s pearls of wisdom, piecing them together with recent clues gleaned from the Parkhead dressing room.

Defender Cameron Carter-Vickers suggested that the tactics were all a ‘bit too random’. Callum McGregor offered the view that players with the wrong profile were being shoe horned into the unfamiliar positions.

Carter-Vickers could make a more constructive contribution if he could stay fit for five minutes and stop gifting goals to Falkirk in the opening minutes of games. The captain might also explain why Scott Tanser was allowed to gift through the midfield for Falkirk’s second yesterday or how he managed to miss a real chance to get his team back into the game at 2-0.

Putting that stuff to one side, supporters are compiling a case for the prosecution of Martin O’Neill. They no longer care what he did for Celtic 20 years ago or even last season.

Where their frustration is usually aimed in the direction of the board of directors more and more is now being channelled towards their 74-year-old manager.

Many accuse him of being a Dermot Desmond appointee who picks the wrong teams and leans on coaches who failed to cut the mustard at clubs like Wigan and Huddersfield.

While older diehards are uncomfortable with criticising the man who gave them some of their greatest days, the young crew are unencumbered by sentiment or history.

They would rather that O’Neill had sailed off into retirement after steering the team to a league and cup double in the summer. And there must be times when the man himself wonders if they have a point.

Had Scott Bain played for Celtic the way he did against his old club yesterday the Falkirk keeper would still be earning the big bucks at Parkhead.

After the man’s best performance in living memory it feels harsh to rain on his parade, but none of Bainarumma’s stops fell into the world class variety. As a team Celtic have become easy to play against and the attacking blueprint seems to consist of getting the ball into wide areas, throwing in a cross and hoping for the best.

When Camilo Duran is on form there is always a chance that might work. Yesterday the Colombian missed some glaring opportunities - thwarted by Bain - and, while O’Neill can’t legislate for the individual errors of strikers or experienced defenders like Carter-Vickers, he knows fine how football works.

After four wins in 10 games many now think he is a man out of his time and the trust and faith of the fans who once adored him is draining. Few buy the notion he pushed after the game that the Falkirk defeat was just one of those days. A pattern is emerging.

Between now and the end of December Celtic play 12 games. While games against Besiktas, Kilmarnock and St Mirren offer some home comfort, away trips to Dundee, Hearts, Toreense, Omonia Nicosia, Dundee United and Rangers must hold all the appeal of a one-day cruise through the Strait of Hormuz.

Come the January window the plan is to sign a physical, mobile central midfielder. They might even shore up a porous defence.

Regardless of how much they spend the most recent window - and plenty others before -  make it hard to harbour for fans to harbour much faith that they will spend the money well.

The £11million record signing, Kasper Hogh can’t buy a goal. Egyptian winger Haissem Hassan remains a talented enigma wrapped in a conundrum. They ended the January window scrambling around to bring in Joel van der Berg, Jordan Lotomba, Oliver Sorensen, Shim Mheuka and Landon Emanelo and they barely see a minute of first team action

Uniquely amongst modern football clubs Celtic see no need to hire a qualified Sporting Director capable of getting a handle on recruitment. A year since Head of Football Operations Paul Tisdale left the club they have yet to replace him. So much for aspiring to be world class in everything they do.

Before Falkirk O’Neill spoke of the need to give summer signings time to adapt to a new club.

While the demands and expectations at Celtic are on a different level, Falkirk made 17 new signings of their own. And, after a ropey start, John McGlynn has now secured his first ever victory over his old employer and won four league games in a row. Some new signings clearly settle in a little faster than others.

McGlynn’s restoration of Falkirk is a testament to the power of a good coach. Taking the  job in May 2022 the Bairns were a basket case. A League One club in the third tier of Scottish football they were losing money hand over fist.

There is nothing flashy or showy about the way he does things. Mention his name for the Celtic job and the very idea would be ridiculed because he is, essentially, a modest, working class man from Wallyford.

Yet those who say he’s not Celtic class should probably ask themselves a question now. What is Celtic class these days? What do the club aspire to be? Is being one point clear of Rangers the height of their ambition or do they want more? If so how do they plan to get there?

As a plc Celtic are duty bound to convene an Annual General Meeting before the end of December. This year all of these questions - over recruitment, ambition, the lack of communication and the strategy - deserve to be asked and​ answered

Martin O’Neill is one of the issues facing Celtic now. He is by no means the only one.
