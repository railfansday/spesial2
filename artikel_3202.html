The Best Magnesium Supplements and What to Look For, According to an Expert

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Magnesium is one of the most popular supplements on the market thanks to wide-ranging potential benefits like better sleep, stress reduction, and improved digestion. Much of this popularity has been driven by social media, including the viral #SleepyGirlMocktail trend. Plus, a slew of A-listers from Taylor Swift to Scarlett Johansson to Lenny Kravitz have also talked about taking magnesium to relax.

So what is magnesium? Like most supplements, it’s a mineral used by our body’s cells to function. It helps turn food into energy, strengthen bones, and support normal heart rhythm. Another key function of magnesium is sleep regulation, since the mineral helps calm muscles and the nervous system and regulate sleep-related hormones. That means taking magnesium may help consumers get better natural sleep instead of being sedated.

There are several types of magnesium supplements, and each binds magnesium to a different molecule, affecting absorption and how the supplement is used. “Magnesium citrate, chloride, lactate, and aspartate generally have better bioavailability than magnesium oxide because they are more soluble and more readily absorbed,” Dr. Martina Ambardjieva, MD, Ph.D., urologist, and medical expert at Invigor Medical, tells Rolling Stone. “Magnesium glycinate also tends to be well tolerated.”

Studies show that magnesium citrate may support digestion and bowel regularity, chloride muscle and nerve function, lactate energy production, aspartate muscle function and energy, and glycinate relaxation, sleep, and stress management. According to Ambardjieva, the recommended total daily magnesium intake is approximately 310 to 320 milligrams daily for women and 400 to 420 milligrams for men, including food sources.

In order to choose the right magnesium supplement for your health and lifestyle, Ambardjieva recommends checking the amount of elemental magnesium (the actual weight of the magnesium in the supplement) and avoiding unnecessarily high doses. The best magnesium supplements also have third-party testing, such as USP or NSF verification.

There isn’t a single magnesium dose that’s right for everyone, and more isn’t necessarily better. According to Ambardjieva, the recommended daily intake for adults is about 310 to 320 milligrams for women and 400 to 420 milligrams for men, including magnesium from both food and supplements. How much you may need from a supplement depends on your diet and individual health needs. When comparing products, look for the amount of elemental magnesium on the label rather than simply the total amount of the magnesium compound.

Taking too much supplemental magnesium can cause diarrhea, nausea, and abdominal cramping, while extremely high magnesium levels can lead to more serious problems, including effects on the heart and kidneys. People with a documented deficiency or those advised to supplement by a healthcare professional are the clearest candidates. If you’re unsure whether you need magnesium or which form and dose are appropriate, talk with a healthcare professional.

Magnesium supplements are generally well tolerated when taken appropriately, but higher amounts can cause digestive side effects, including nausea, diarrhea, or abdominal discomfort. The risk of side effects can also vary depending on the form of magnesium.

Because magnesium supplements can also interact with some medications, people taking prescription drugs or managing a medical condition should check with a healthcare professional before supplementing.
