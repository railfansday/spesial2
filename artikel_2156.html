'Nonsense!' - Ex-QPR & Southampton star Charlie Austin launches rant on social media

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Former Southampton and QPR striker Charlie Austin has criticised coaches preying on gullible parents of young children trying to make it in the world of football.

The former forward has posted a message on social media in which he complained about coaches advertising the importance of ball mastery by the age of 10 or 12.

The 37-year-old’s playing career saw him reach the Premier League with the likes of QPR, the Saints and West Brom.

He also competed at a Championship level with the likes of Burnley, before moving to Australia in 2022 with Brisbane Roar.

Austin has since returned to British football, and he continues to compete at a non-league level.

However, he has also become a prominent voice in expressing his football opinions online, and this includes on his personal social media pages.

Austin has attacked the coaches that advertise their business by claiming that kids have to master certain skills with the ball by certain ages, suggesting that those claims are utter nonsense.

He believes that they are preying on gullible parents who won’t know any better, and that this is taking advantage of kids trying to fulfill their dream of making it as a professional footballer.

“I keep seeing coaches, etc. on Instagram talking about ‘oh, you’ve got to master these ball mastery skills by the age of 10. Oh you’ve got to do this by the age of five. Oh you’ve got to do this by the age of 12’. No you don’t,” said Austin, via Instagram.

“Just go about your business, and I certainly get you’re advertising your business, which is great, which is absolutely fine.

“But to come up with nonsense, spout nonsense to gullible parents — and there is plenty of gullible parents that want their kid to live the dream.

“And I get you want to charge £35 an hour, or £20 an hour to make a few quid because you don’t want to work, you don’t want to do the occasional nine to five job, or whatever, totally understand that.

“It’s all about growing your business, but seriously stop talking rubbish, stop hassling parents with nonsense.”

Austin originally started his career in the Reading academy system, but was released at the age of 15.

The striker took an unconventional career path from there, working his way through a number of non-league sides before earning an opportunity with Swindon Town at the age of 20.

He made 54 league appearances for the Robins after being given a place in the first team squad, which led to him signing for Burnley in 2011.

But it was at QPR where he first received a taste of Premier League football, helping the Hoops earn promotion from the Championship in 2013/14.

Austin scored an impressive 18 times in 2014/15, but it wasn’t enough to keep QPR in the top flight, with the club succumbing to relegation with a 20th place finish.

But the striker earned another shot at top flight football with a move to Southampton in 2015/16.

Injuries hampered his time at St. Mary’s, but he still contributed 16 goals and three assists in the division for the Hampshire outfit across four campaigns.

Austin went on to help West Brom earn Premier League promotion in 2019/20, but he played just five more times in the top flight in the remainder of his career.

Austin is clearly thinking about his own path to playing in the Premier League when going on this rant about kids needing to master certain skills by certain ages.

His opportunity to make it at the top level looked to be over as a teenager, but he was able to continue working on his own skills to eventually make it when he was in his 20s.

So it’s not too surprising that he is taking an issue with this kind of framing when it comes to underage player development, as he is an example that there is no perfect path to becoming a professional player.

Perhaps Austin should try his own hand at coaching in the future, as his experience could be quite a valuable asset for young kids to learn from.
