‘I Played With Wayne Rooney at Man Utd  - I Broke Down When the Judge Issued My Sentence’

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

A former Manchester United ace, who played alongside Wayne Rooney and Paul Scholes to play a role in the 2010/11 Premier League title-winning season, has revealed he broke down when a judge read out his second sentence.

The player in question, a midfielder known for his physicality and a right foot that regularly attempted and scored ambitious long range efforts - inspired by Sir Bobby Charlton's advice to 'keep shooting' - came up through United's Carrington academy.

He played 60 times for the club, before also making a name for himself at the likes of Everton, Sunderland, and Wolves. He only retired in 2022 after also earning 27 caps for his national team, but the autumnal years of his career saw him stand before court twice, in 2015 and again three years later in 2018.

During his first conviction, in 2015, retired Ireland midfielder Darron Gibson was sentenced to a 12-month community order (including 200 hours of unpaid work) and a 20-month driving ban after admitting drink-driving, careless driving, and failing to stop following a collision with three cyclists in Greater Manchester.

But in a recent interview with RTE Listen, he revealed it was his second conviction only three years later that scared him more. Not knowing whether he was going to be sent to prison for driving under the influence, he revealed (watch the full segment below):

"I was sat in a little side room before we go in [to court], and I'd hired one of the best barristers in England. Solicitor's there, and I've said to him, 'Listen, before we go in, what's the chances of me coming out of here?' And he said, 'It's 50/50.' So I've taken my ring off, watch off, chains, wallet, phone. Passed it all over. And, in the middle of the courtroom, I was sitting on my own on a little chair, I felt I was back at school.

"The judge started talking, obviously there's press everywhere, and the first thing he said was, 'we're dropping this from custodial to non-custodial'. Half the courtroom emptied, all the press left. I just remember breaking down as soon as he said that. And luckily, I didn't go to jail, because I really thought I was."

Instead of going behind bars, Gibson, now 39, was sentenced on May 21, 2018, at South Tyneside Magistrates' Court to a two-year community order (with 250 hours of unpaid work and 30 days of rehabilitation activity), a 40-month driving ban, £800 compensation, costs, and an £85 victim surcharge.

District Judge Roger Elsey opted against a custodial sentence after hearing about Gibson's psychological issues and ongoing treatment, as Gibson has spoken openly about anxiety, intrusive thoughts, severe insomnia, and a nine-year dependency on sleeping tablets.

Later on in the same podcast, Gibson opened up about his nine-year addiction to sleeping tablets, revealing how his dependency nearly cost him his life, in an emotional interview alongside his wife, Danielle.

Danielle recalled the moment she feared her husband was dead, admitting: "I just thought: 'At least he's not struggling anymore.'" He was taking as many as 14 sleeping tablets every night by 2021, alongside several more during the day. His addiction began after suffering a cruciate ligament injury while on international duty in 2013, with anxiety and intrusive thoughts leaving him unable to sleep.

"Looking back, who takes five sleeping tablets at 10 in the morning and doesn't go to sleep? It's ridiculous. But it's where I was," he admitted. Despite previous warning signs, including drink-driving convictions and a serious car crash in 2018, Gibson's addiction continued until he suffered a terrifying seizure at home in November 2021.

Reflecting on just how close he came to losing his life, Gibson told the podcast: "Ultimately, if that hadn't have happened, I wouldn't be here now." Danielle agreed that he might not have survived, prompting an emotional response from her husband.

He mentioned two names but gave the nod to one Man United legend.

Now recovered, the couple hope sharing their experience will help others struggling with addiction and highlight the lack of support available within professional football.

Danielle also criticised the football industry's handling of players' mental health, saying: "He's not the only one that was struggling. There were so many lads, whether it be sleeping tablets or mental health or depression or anxiety or booze." She added that players should have had better access to support, insisting the industry had a "duty of care".
