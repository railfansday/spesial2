Amazon.com vs. Home Depot: Which Consumer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Amazon's cloud and ad businesses face off against Home Depot's push into the professional contractor market, and the valuation gap may shape the choice.

By Seena Hassouna – Updated Nov 22, 2026 at 1:04PM EST

How do you choose between a technology titan and a physical retail leader? Both Amazon.com (AMZN -4.10%) and Home Depot (HD +0.70%) offer investors unique exposure to consumer spending patterns and logistics expertise.

Amazon.com relies on its massive scale and digital ecosystem to drive growth across retail and cloud computing. Home Depot focuses on the home improvement market, serving both casual DIYers and professional trade specialists. This comparison helps you evaluate which strategy offers the best potential for the coming years.

Amazon.com operates a vast ecosystem that includes online and physical stores, third-party seller services, and advertising. The company serves a diverse customer base ranging from individual shoppers to large enterprises seeking cloud computing through Amazon Web Services. Its business model relies heavily on a network of third-party sellers and suppliers, many of which are based in China, to maintain its broad inventory.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $797.5 billion. This performance represents revenue growth of approximately 15.4% compared to the previous twelve-month period. Net income reached about $134.8 billion, but roughly $69 billion of that came from non-operating items, mostly gains on its private AI investments.

Based on its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio is approximately 1.1x. This metric measures a company's ability to meet its short-term liabilities using assets that can be converted to cash within a year. The debt-to-equity ratio is about 0.4x, indicating the relationship between total debt and shareholders' equity. In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, free cash flow was negative roughly $47 billion, as equipment purchases outpaced operating cash flow.

Home Depot is the leading name among retail stocks focused on home improvement. The company serves do-it-yourself consumers and professional contractors, such as general contractors and trade specialists. Following its acquisition of SRS, the company has expanded its specialized distribution capabilities to better serve the complex needs of professional building product customers.

In the 12 months ended Nov. 1, 2026, revenue reached approximately $171.2 billion. This represents revenue growth of nearly 3% as the company navigates shifts in consumer spending on home projects. Net income during the same period was roughly $14.3 billion, demonstrating steady profitability despite a slower growth environment than the technology sector.

As of its February 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is approximately 1.1x. The debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 5.1x, indicating the level of debt used to finance assets relative to shareholders' equity. In the fiscal year ended Feb. 1, 2026, free cash flow was close to $12.6 billion, providing the company with substantial cash to fund operations and shareholder returns.

Amazon.com faces intense global competition across retail and cloud computing from well-funded rivals. The company must navigate evolving international regulations and geopolitical tensions, particularly regarding its operations in China and India. Operational risks include the complexity of its fulfillment network and data centers, where failures can cause significant disruptions. Additionally, the company faces legal risks from ongoing antitrust and consumer protection investigations that could impact its business practices.

Home Depot is highly sensitive to fluctuations in the housing market and interest rates, which directly influence demand for home improvement. The company faces operational risks related to the integration of large-scale strategic acquisitions like SRS and GMS. It is also subject to reputational risks from class action litigation, including legal challenges regarding AI-powered surveillance in store parking lots. Intense competition from both traditional and digital retailers requires the company to manage its aging store base while advancing its digital strategy.

Amazon.com looks more attractive based on future earnings estimates, while Home Depot offers a lower entry point based on trailing sales.

The Forward P/E ratio compares a stock's current price to its expected earnings per share over the next year. Meanwhile, the P/S ratio measures the market capitalization of the company against its sales over the past twelve months.

The differentiator is how far each business can stretch. Home Depot sells to people building and fixing homes, and no matter how well it executes, that stays the ceiling. Even its SRS and GMS deals deepen the same building-products footprint. Amazon already earns from retail, cloud, advertising, and subscriptions, and it keeps adding bets, including a satellite network and a pending Globalstar deal. AWS grew 39% last quarter, which shows how much room the compute side still has.The ride will be rougher. Amazon spends more cash than it generates, funds the gap with debt, and says capex will climb again in 2027. Investors will sit through stretches when the numbers look worse before they improve, and the stock can swing hard along the way. The thesis breaks only if demand for compute dries up, and that outcome would hit far more than one stock. I would buy Amazon for 2027 as a long-term holding, sized so a few bumpy years do not force a sale. Investors who want steady income and a smoother path should look at Home Depot instead.
