CRH: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The $8.5 billion Arcosa deal positions CRH to profit from infrastructure spending while cost cuts shield margins from near-term headwinds.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 24, 2026 at 11:39PM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 29, 2026, CRH (CRH +1.51%) updated its full-year adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (Adjusted EBITDA) guidance to between $7.8 billion and $8.0 billion. Higher energy costs and weak housing construction squeezed short-term margins, but management's multiyear expansion strategy stays intact. The building materials giant is cutting operational costs and expanding its footprint in high-growth infrastructure markets. Three takeaways from the call highlight where the company is heading.

Management launched Velocity, a transformation initiative designed to streamline management layers and lower production costs across operations. The company expects the program to deliver $400 million in annual run rate savings by the end of 2028 while expanding operating margins.

CRH completed its $8.5 billion acquisition of Arcosa to deepen its exposure to North American energy transmission, data center construction, and aggregate materials. Management expects the deal to generate $175 million in annual cost savings by its third year.

With a strong balance sheet supporting its capital strategy, CRH authorized a $6 billion share repurchase program running through 2030. The company has retired nearly 25% of its stock in recent years through disciplined repurchases.

Federal infrastructure spending continues to boost heavy construction, and management isn't hiding its optimism about multiyear demand.

CRH leverages nationwide terminal scale and pricing discipline to weather soft residential building while expanding into high-growth data center infrastructure.
