AirJoule Technologies Corporation (AIRJ -5.12%) completed the acquisition of BitSink during the third quarter, incorporating modular data center infrastructure and liquid cooling capabilities into its business operations. Management focused the AirJoule product roadmap on two specific platforms, Prime for atmospheric water generation and Core DH for energy-efficient dehumidification. The company established a preliminary revenue outlook for the newly acquired BitSink segment and confirmed the delivery of its first Prime system to a European data center operator. Financial results for the period included a noncash impairment charge related to the joint venture's research and development assets, while the company reaffirmed its 2026 cash spending guidance.

BitSink builds NEXUS, a modular data center platform designed to be a single answer for AI infrastructure at any scale. Our initial NEXUS blocks are designed to support NVIDIA GB300 and Cerebras CS-3 systems, with the flexibility to accommodate different AI computing architectures. NEXUS is delivered as standardized factory-assembled blocks in a range of base sizes, each with liquid cooling, power distribution and battery backup built in. Our 3-megawatt block, for example, supports 2.5 megawatts of critical IT load.

For customers, the value of NEXUS is straightforward: choose the block size that fits the site and the hardware, then add identical blocks as demand grows. Every block is the same proven design at a different scale, so there's no reengineering from one deployment to the next. This reduces the need for customized construction and integration at the site, which can help customers bring computing capacity online sooner and align infrastructure investment at a pace that reflects the actual demand. Our factory-based production model is an important part of how we deliver the value. We build and test every block in our factory before shipping it to a prepared customer site.

This allows us to standardize quality control, repeat proven designs and complete more of the integration work before the equipment reaches the customer. The result is superior product reliability and faster speed to compute. In recent weeks, we moved into our new 65,000 square foot factory in Chesnee, South Carolina, where we're now installing production equipment. We expect to begin initial production this month with full production capability in place by the end of the year. Traditional stick-built data centers can take more than a year to construct. NEXUS offers a fundamentally different approach using standardized pre-engineered blocks that can be manufactured and deployed in a fraction of that time.

While initial customer configuration requires design and approval, once reference design are established, additional blocks can be produced in a matter of weeks. This repeatable modular approach helps customers accelerate capacity expansion, reduce on-site construction complexity and scale infrastructure as demand grows. In addition to deployment speed, the NEXUS design is engineered for continuous operation. Redundant power paths are designed to keep every rack at full power if any one path goes down, and major cooling components are designed to be serviced without taking the block offline. Together, this architecture is intended to combine rapid modular deployment with the reliability and maintainability customers expect in critical data center infrastructure.

We have secured $15 million in signed purchase orders, and we expect to deliver our first NEXUS blocks in the coming months.
