AerCap: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Deep order book visibility through 2028 may lock in lease revenue as supply constraints keep rates elevated.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 28, 2026, AerCap (AER -1.02%) announced raised full-year earnings guidance driven by asset sales and aircraft lease demand. The aircraft leasing company benefits from tight global supply, allowing it to sell planes above book value while expanding its order book.

AerCap sells older planes into a tight secondary market to clear room for modern models. Management realized 2.6 times book equity, the net worth of assets on paper, on sales this quarter.

Airlines face higher fuel bills, yet demand for fuel-efficient narrowbody aircraft keeps AerCap's order pipeline locked up years in advance.

The company maintains leverage of two times net debt to equity, a ratio measuring borrowed funds against owner equity, running below its target of 2.7 times. That provides excess cash coverage to repurchase shares or acquire new assets.

Aircraft supply constraints keep global lease rates elevated, and AerCap is capitalizing on the supply imbalance.

AerCap relies on its scale and direct relationships with original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), companies that build aircraft engines and airframes, to secure early delivery slots. Management is watching airline fuel pressures and interest rates, while maintaining capital allocation discipline.
