‘GTA 6’ Sales Have Already Surpassed Christopher Nolan’s ‘The Odyssey’

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The Rockstar Games epic has generated $1.84 billion in sales — and it just launched today.

For those wondering why Rockstar Games hasn’t licensed Grand Theft Auto for a movie or TV show, this is probably why.

Grand Theft Auto VI has already generated an incredible $1.84 billion in sales, according to market intelligence firm Sensor Tower.

That’s more than Christopher Nolan‘s blockbuster The Odyssey made during its entire global run ($1.80 billion).

What’s more amazing, is GTA 6‘s eye-popping sales figure may already be outdated. While Sensor Tower updated its tracker Thursday, the underlying charts appear to only show pre-sales through Tuesday — meaning that $1.84 billion estimate doesn’t yet account for yesterday (when all those glowing reviews were published) or today (when the game was actually released). Therefore, current GTA 6 sales is very likely over $2 billion (in case you’re curious, the revenue split is 75 percent from PS5 sales and 25 percent for Xbox).

And GTA games tend to have very, very long tails. GTA 5 was released 13 years ago and has generated an estimated $5.4 billion in lifetime revenue (plus an additional $5.4 billion in revenue during that period from GTA Online), according to The Wall Street Journal. Even over the last four years, GTA 5 has averaged $400 million in sales … for, again, a game released in 2013. The top-grossing movie in 2013 was Disney’s Frozen, which made $1.28 billion … now imagine if last year Frozen was still averaging $400 million a year in ticket sales.

So needless to say, GTA 6 is just getting started.

Going back to that question up top, Rockstar Games is likely disinclined to risk its brand value or reputation through a movie or TV project given the revenue for the company would probably end up being a pretty small fraction of what they already make from the game itself.

As Strauss Zelnick, the CEO of Rockstar’s parent company Take-Two Interactive, told The David Rubenstein Show earlier this month: “We’re very careful about line extensions because this is a creative company and our goal is to make the best entertainment on earth, entertainment we’re always proud of. We would make a movie or television show based on our intellectual property if our creative teams were passionate about it, if they thought they would somehow engage and delight our consumers. It’s a high bar to do that. And of course, there’s always a risk of failure. So we don’t rule it out, but we’re very, very selective.”
