All 5 Episodes Of Spielberg's Apple TV Sci-Fi Series Show The Wrong Way To Reboot A Franchise

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

All five episodes of Steven Spielberg's Apple TV sci-fi series are a testament to how not to reboot a franchise after it's lain dormant for 35 years. Spielberg is best known for his movies, the filmmaker being a powerhouse of cinema whose decades of work have defined movies in both the 20th and 21st centuries. From the heartfelt, sci-fi story of E.T. the Extra-Terrrestrial to the tense yet thematically potent tale of Jurassic Park, Spielberg has made his mark on movies, especially in the sci-fi genre.

But he's also had a hand in plenty of other projects, too, particularly when it comes to serving as an executive producer for sci-fi found on TV. The underwater sci-fi show SeaQuest DSV is one such example, taking place in an aquatic, sea-focused future. Some more contemporary works of his include the alien invasion-focused Falling Skies, alongside the TV adaptation for Stephen King's Under the Dome. His sci-fi TV series have become integral parts of media history. One such series that he helped create and reboot is no exception.

That show is Amazing Stories, a fantasy sci-fi anthology series that was originally released from 1985 to 1987. Across the series' 45-episode run, each new installment would feature a story combining elements of fantasy or horror, crafting a tale much in the same vein as The Twilight Zone in terms of presentation and content. Spielberg created and executive produced the show, while directing some episodes. although the original series ended in the '80s, the show had a short-lived, five-episode run on Apple TV in 2020 — which didn't go according to plan.

Apple TV's reboot of Amazing Stories saw Spielberg back as an executive producer for the show, which lasted five episodes of roughly an hour each, as opposed to the half-hour runtimes of the original series' episodes. Each episode focused on a different sci-fi adventure, not necessarily in the vein of horror. This includes an episode starring the late Robert Forster in his final onscreen role, a posthumous release after his death in 2019. However, despite the admirable attempt, the show didn't stick the same landing as its predecessor.

According to reviews for Amazing Stories, the 2020 rendition of the show scored a 41% on Rotten Tomatoes from 34 critics. Audiences didn't fare much better, dropping it a 52% based on over 100 ratings. According to critics, the show doesn't feel like it evolved for modern times, offering a strong attempt at bringing the series to a modern audience that doesn't stick the landing. In particular, there was criticism toward the show's lack of originality, alongside messy writing that causes some stories to feel nonsensical.

What makes the Amazing Stories reboot all the more disappointing is that Jordan Peele's attempt at remaking The Twilight Zone served as a much better reinvention of episodic sci-fi anthology series. While the reboot wasn't perfect, it took risks in modernizing the series that eventually aided in it being a more memorable experience. This included both remixes of classic episodes from the original, alongside fully original terrors that would only work in the present day. Nostalgia wasn't the only factor, with the series also utilizing originality.

The overarching attempt The Twilight Zone made with its continuation far outshone that of Amazing Stories, which goes to show how the Apple TV series unfortunately fumbled what could have been a memorable modern comeback. While some episodes stand on their own, such as Forster's, it's ultimately nothing like the classic that Spielberg created back in the '80s. It proves that, despite an attempt, the show just couldn't make its way to the modern age.
