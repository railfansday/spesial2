Prediction: This Will Be Micron’s Stock Price by the End of 2028

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Is Micron's low valuation an attractive entry point?

By Adam Levy – Nov 21, 2026 at 9:15AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Micron Technology ›

Few companies have seen their earnings soar amid the artificial intelligence boom like Micron Technology (MU -13.89%). The memory chipmaker has benefited from a spike in demand for its chips, even as it and its competitors take years to ramp up new supply capacity. The result is eye-popping profit margins and a stock price that's gone parabolic.

The stock trades for about $690 per share as of this writing, an impressive 201% gain over the past year and a 598% gain over the past 18 months. Even so, the stock is about 43% off its all-time high reached over the summer.

But as the saying goes, "Past results are not indicative of future returns." Future potential investors need to consider what they can expect from Micron and what that means for the stock price. Here's my prediction for Micron's stock price by the end of 2028.

Micron's earnings growth is directly tied to its ability to charge extremely high prices for memory, specifically high bandwidth memory (HBM), which gets packaged with GPUs and AI accelerators for AI training and inference.

As a result, Micron's gross margin has climbed to an astonishing 87%. But that pricing isn't indicative of a competitive moat, as many high-gross-margin businesses exhibit. Note that rival chipmaker SK Hynix (SKHY -13.11%) produced a gross margin of 86% in its most recent quarter, practically on par with Micron.

The margin strength stems from the supply/demand imbalance. Micron said supply constraints will be even tighter in 2027 and 2028 than they were in 2026 in its fourth-quarter earnings presentation. That suggests prices could keep climbing through 2028.

SK Hynix suggested that demand could outpace supply through 2030. But just because demand remains higher than supply doesn't mean prices will keep climbing. Demand has to grow faster than supply.

So, investors expecting prices to continue climbing through 2030 may be mistaken. Micron, SK Hynix, and Samsung are set to open and ramp up new production facilities in late 2028.

So, it's likely pricing peaks in 2028 ahead of the massive ramp-up in production capacity. Earnings could peak in late 2028 or early 2029 as a result.

Importantly, as supply constraints ease, prices go down, gross margins decline, and operating costs increase. Once supply reaches equilibrium or exceeds demand, profits can sink quickly. And given the massive amounts memory chipmakers are spending on new capacity, the next downcycle could lead to a big drop in earnings.

The potential for that drop will affect how much investors are willing to pay for the stock's earnings near the cycle peak. A bigger drop-off in earnings will result in a lower multiple on the stock. Micron and its competitors are working to reduce the cyclicality of their businesses by signing long-term agreements with customers, but that may only delay an earnings decline and make the drop-off worse once those contracts expire.

As such, investors are unlikely to pay a very high multiple near Micron's peak earnings. Historically, Micron has commanded a multiple of 3-8 times earnings at its peak. With the potential for a large downcycle, shares are likely to trade in the 3-5-times range, with earnings expected to peak in 2028 or 2029.

It's important to note that the stock price usually peaks before earnings peak. Investors are almost always forward-looking. Any hint that pricing is nearing its peak and earnings are set for a near-term downturn will push the stock lower. We saw that happen last month when Sandisk reported disappointing price improvements for its flash memory NAND chips, and the stock dropped after earnings.

With analysts currently expecting $211 per share in earnings for fiscal 2028 and $218 per share for fiscal 2029, that puts my prediction for Micron's stock price at the end of 2028 somewhere between $610 and $1,040. With the stock trading for $690 right now, it doesn't look like a great time to buy unless you think the earnings cycle will last much longer and the drop-off in earnings will be far less severe once earnings peak.
