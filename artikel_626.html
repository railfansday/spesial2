Lisa Kudrow, Michael Schur and More Top TV Scribes Share Storytelling Secrets at Variety’s Night in the Writers’ Room: Photos

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Some of the biggest writers in television shared an insight into their processes during Variety’s annual Night in the Writers’ Room on November 11. For the “Awards Season Edition,” scribes behind top comedies, drama and limited series detailed their very different experiences of creating hit shows — whether new series like “The Greatest” and “Dig” or those wrapping up like “The Comeback.”

Creator, showrunner, executive producer, director, writer “P-Valley”

“I care about the story being the best it can be. I care about your perspective. I want to hear eerything that everyone has to say, and so my room is very democratic — literally everyone has equal say.”

Showrunner, executive producer, writer “Ride or Die”

“We want the comedy to be a relief to the action, to the mystery. If you bring that into the danger and into the thriller element of the show, it becomes silly. We’re always super conscious of, how would a person behave in this? What is the grounded version of this? To the extent that the show works at all the different genres, humor is the hardest part.”

Co-creator, executive producer, writer “Adults”

“From a production standpoint, we’re, of course, very daunted [by 16 episodes]. But from a storytelling perspective, it’s so exciting.”

Co-creator, executive prodcuer, writer “Adults”

“What our show tries to do is foster a sense of community… Community is so important to our age group, to Gen Z, to Gen Alpha, they long for it. I have to take a moment to credit everyone on this stage and the work that they did for imparting the sense that the people who are important are the people in your lives, your friends, your family. It is why we make this show and why this AI thing doesn’t really come close to being able to threaten that.”

Co-creator, co-showrunner, executive producer, writer “American Hostage”

“In running a room, I always feel like, around that table, we’re on a boat together. We rise and fall together. And I do feel that best idea wins and also having an openness to people and their experiences.”

Co-creator, co-showrunner, executive producer, writer “American Hostage”

“You’re putting [the writers’ room] together like a band. It’s like somebody plays guitar, somebody writes for this character. Somebody writes for that character. Somebody brings the humor. Somebody has expertise in spycraft, and you’re thinking about all those things when you’re interviewing writers.”

Co-creator, executive producer, director, writer “The Comeback”

“The only reason we got to keep going after it went away and came back was because the tone was original and people responded to it. So if you can whether the big bump of, what is it, the tone of something — an original thing — will be the thing that sustains… The tone is the people in the room that are removing stuff and adding stuff.”

Co-creator, executive producer, writer “The Comeback”

“I don’t worry about entertainment, because I feel like it makes room for new terrible things and it makes room for new phenomenal things that you didn’t know you’d be able to watch or see. So that’s my optimistic view… they’re gonna try every way to make things less expensive and with fewer people and see if they can sustain their programming, but I dont think they’ll be able to.”

Co-creator, co-showrunner, executive producer, writer “Dig”

“There’s very few people left who have a muscle memory of how to actually execute [22-episode seasons]. And I don’t want it to go away. I think we need it back.”

Creator, showrunner, executive producer, writer “Cape Fear”

“When you find those people whom you have a shorthand with, they’re just worth their weight in gold. They become your closest collaborators.”

Showrunner, executive producer, writer “Blake Runner 2099”

“I think we’re living in a time right now where the idea of human craftsmanship is being questioned, and to actually see people collaborate together in a room, and it’s so alive, I just feel so grateful to have had that gift. And I think when I watch the show, that’s the thing: I see everybody’s little voice on screen. It’s the magic.”

Creator, showrunner, executive producer, writer “Crystal Lake”

“I’ve been in rooms with showrunners who really go deep and really want to dissect things and work on things for a long time. I don’t. I’m very instinctive — if I feel something is the right direction, I just go in that direction.”

Creator, executive producer, director, writer “The Greatest”

“It’s a very sobering experience when you realize you’re number one. The amount of pressure that you feel from deadlines or budgets or just being in that seat and having everybody saying, ‘Where are we going?’ There is some pressure, and it will change you and I think it’s really important to acknowledge that.”

Co-creator, executive producer, writer “Lanterns”

“We’d be doing a really bad job if we weren’t taking the things [the writers are] bringing and incorporating them and growing.”

Creator, executive producer, writer “The Drop: A Snowfall Saga”

“We so disagreed about where the moral lines were for different characters in the room. I’d made the comment like, ‘Everyone thinks they’re writing a different show.’ … It was actually a compliment. It’s a trip watching the audience, literally having the same arguments that we were having in the writers’ room. I’m really proud of that… All these people who were in that room obviously must have had a real impact on it for the audience to be responding the exact same way.”

Creator, executive producer, writer “Furious”

“When I was doing the ‘New Girl’ pilot, Jake Kasdan who directed it, said, ‘As soon as we cast these chatacters, these characters belong to them, and you have to start writing to their strengths and give up the thing that’s in your head.’ It was one of the best pieces of advice.”
