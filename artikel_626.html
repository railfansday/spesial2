‘A Girl Unknown,’ ‘Crawling Into Myself,’ ‘Concrete Land’ Take Main Prizes at Doha Film Festival

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Zou Jing’s “A Girl Unknown,” Asmahan Bkerat’s “Concrete Land” and Hamad Al-Fayhani’s “Crawling Into Myself” won the top prizes at the sophomore edition of the revamped Doha Film Festival, which took place between Nov. 19-27 in the Qatari capital.

The Doha Film Festival has transformed the Doha Film Institute’s existing Ajyal Film Festival, dedicated to youth and family-friendly cinema, into a more ambitious international event for a broader audience. Prizes totaled over $300,000 across four competition strands: international feature film competition, short film competition, the Ajyal film competition (judged by the festival’s youth jury), and the Made in Qatar competition, dedicated to projects made in Qatar regardless of the director’s provenance. “A Girl Unknown,” which won Best Narrative Feature in International Competition, trails a young Chinese girl passed between three families between the ages of 6 and 18; “Crawling Into Myself,” the winner of the Made in Qatar competition, tells the story of a man grappling with a rare illness that erases his facial features. “Concrete Land,” the Best Documentary in International Competition, is a multi-generational portrait of a mixed-race family fighting displacement and hostility in Jordan.

Fatma Hassan Alremaihi, festival director and CEO of the Doha Film Institute, said: “Tonight, as we celebrate the winners of the 2026 Doha Film Festival, we celebrate far more than cinematic achievement. We recognize the power of stories to transcend borders, challenge perspectives and bring the experiences of people and communities into the global conversation.”

The festival director added that the Doha Film Institute “believes that important voices deserve to be seen, heard and recognized.”

“The Doha Film Festival exists to provide that platform—bringing together filmmakers and audiences from across the world, championing bold and independent storytelling, and creating space for stories that might otherwise go untold,” she added. “The films we honor tonight remind us that cinema is not simply a reflection of the world we live in; it is a force that can shape how we understand it, connect with one another and imagine a more empathetic future.”

This year’s jury at the Qatari festival was presided over by Japanese director Naomi Kawase, who chose the winners alongside French Tunisian actor Adam Bessa, Tunisian producer Dora Bouchoucha, Italian documentary filmmaker Gianfranco Rosi and award-winning Palestinian filmmaker and artist Kamal Aljafari.

The short film competition jury was presided over by French Moroccan producer Karim Aitouna, who was joined by Malaysian filmmaker Amanda Nell Eu, and Swiss-Rwandan actress and filmmaker Kayije Kagame. The Made in Qatar jury was led by Lebanese actress and filmmaker Yumna Marwan along with Qatari filmmaker Al Johara Al Thani and Palestinian Belgian filmmaker and producer Naël Khleifi.

The ceremony, which was hosted by Sudanese filmmaker and presenter Amjad Al-Nour, was attended by names such as Elia Suleiman, Brigitte Lacombe and Ahmed Al Baker. Other notable guests included H.E. Sheikha Al-Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, chairperson of DFI; H.E. Sheikh Dr. Abdulla bin Ali Al Thani, managing director and vice chairman of the board of directors of Qatar Leadership Centre; Hamad Omar Al-Mannai, chief executive officer of Media City Qatar; and Eng. Abdulaziz Ali Al Mawlawi, chief executive officer of Visit Qatar.

Earlier in the week, the festival delivered a successful industry sidebar, the Doha Industry Days. The three-day event was attended by some 450 prominent executives from 38 countries. Speakers included “Squid Game” creator Hwang Dong-hyuk, “Avengers” director Joe Russo, Omar Sy and Noomi Rapace. The buzzy Industry Days saw the announcement of several projects, including a Neon-backed biopic titled “Caesar” about Syrian military officer Farid Al-Madhhan and two other biopics, one on Egyptian music star Mohamed Mounir and the other on Lebanese pop star Fadel Shaker.
