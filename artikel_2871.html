NASCAR CEO Steve O’Donnell Gives State of The Sport Address

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

NASCAR CEO Steve O'Donnell delivered the annual State of The Sport address in the media center at Homestead-Miami Speedway on Friday. O'Donnell has been encouraged by the growth of the sport this year and is optimistic about the future of NASCAR as the 2026 season comes to a close this weekend.

O'Donnell took time to look back at the 2026 season and drop hints of ideas that are in the works for the 2026 season.

During Friday's press conference, O'Donnell revealed encouraging metrics regarding fan engagement in 2026. The CEO said attendance for NASCAR is up five percent and kids 12-and-under are up 12 percent. O'Donnell added that digital/social fan engagement for ages 18-34 is the highest it has been since 2013.

The theme of the 2026 NASCAR season is "encouraging," O'Donnell said, but that there is still more work to do. O'Donnell said the TV audience is "consistently strong" despite what the numbers might show.

"He says everyone has seen the numbers, but believes it's still good in the broader landscape and if it were a new sport, people would consider it a success story," Jeff Gluck wrote via X.

The 2026 season was marred by the sudden death of two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion Kyle Busch on May 21. Busch, 41, was the first active full-time driver to die since Dale Earnhardt Sr. in 2001.

O'Donnell spoke about Busch during the State of The Sport address, believing that his legacy will live on.

"We all miss Kyle. It's not the same without him in terms of celebrating a championship … but he's gonna be with us for years and years to come," O'Donnell said.

When looking to grow the sport, O'Donnell said it can be broken down into a blueprint of five different pillars: competition, audience growth and engagement, schedule and media strategy, international, and at-track fan development.

O'Donnell said the "facts" say that short-track racing is at the highest level possible in terms of positivity from the fans. However, the CEO said they do not intend on slowing their efforts in improving short-track racing.

"Does that mean you know everything's great and we we've stopped doing anything? No, I think you've seen us make some significant changes. We'll continue to do that," O'Donnell said.

O'Donnell said there will not be a Daytona winter test in preparation for Daytona Speedweeks in February, adding that the sport's sanctioning body feels "comfortable" about the rules package for the Daytona 500.

O'Donnell said there is not a Gen 8 car on the horizon at this time. What will happen, however, is that they will seek to make changes to the current NextGen car when needed.

"I'm confident in our guys and the industry and the OEMs to tweak what we have where necessary, but also be pretty confident in our drivers going out there and having the ability to go win races and put on some pretty darn good competition," O'Donnell said.
