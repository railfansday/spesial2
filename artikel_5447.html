Roche is planning to call its experimental breast cancer pill Wilserdly

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Roche has registered the name in multiple jurisdictions as the FDA reviews two applications, with a first decision due Nov. 30

Roche plans to market its experimental breast cancer pill giredestrant under the brand name Wilserdly, according to Bloomberg. The Swiss drugmaker has already lodged the name in several territories while it waits to learn whether the drug will be cleared.

Roche chose the name because it contains "SERD," the acronym for selective estrogen receptor degraders, according to Bloomberg, which cited unnamed people familiar with the matter. SERDs belong to a category of medicines that break down a receptor which breast tumors can rely on to fuel their growth.

Genentech, Roche's U.S. subsidiary, sought a trademark on Oct. 27 for the name, rendered in block capitals alongside a floral logo, according to Bloomberg. Roche has filed for the identical name with other trademark bodies around the globe, the European Union Intellectual Property Office among them. Roche declined to comment.

Regulators must approve a drug's name, according to Bloomberg. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has awarded priority status to two giredestrant filings. A decision on the first, for early-stage breast cancer, is due Nov. 30. A decision on the second, for use with everolimus in advanced disease, is expected in December.

Roche is getting ready to bring giredestrant to the American market the moment it gets the green light, according to Bloomberg. The launch would include a patient-support program for women starting treatment.

Oral SERDs have already won approval for more limited groups of advanced breast cancer patients, among them Menarini Group's Orserdu and Eli Lilly $LLY's Inluriyo, according to Bloomberg. Roche is aiming to reach the market first for the broader population of early-stage patients, before AstraZeneca $AZN's Etcamah.

Roche intends to apply for European approval in 2027, after additional evidence such as overall survival results becomes available, according to Bloomberg. Pricing negotiations might hold up the launch there, or leave the drug unavailable in certain European markets if the sides fail to strike deals.
