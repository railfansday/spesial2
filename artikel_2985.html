MP Materials' Earnings Report Had Multiple Bright Spots, and There's 1 Thing I'm Watching

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

There was a lot to like in MP's recent Q3 report, but the stock could remain under pressure in the near term.

MP Materials (MP -6.89%) published its third-quarter results after the market closed on Nov. 5, delivering sales and earnings that topped average analyst estimates. The company posted non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings per share of $0.04 on revenue of $147.6 million. Adjusted earnings per share beat the average analyst estimate by $0.05, and sales topped the average target by $28.7 million.

The company showed strong momentum for both the production and sale of neodymium-praseodymium (NdPr) -- the fused rare-earth oxide that is at the heart of its growth story and used in magnets that are crucial for some defense and robotics technologies.

MP registered 1,018 metric tons of NdPr production in the third quarter, marking a 41% year-over-year increase. Meanwhile, the business sold 1,020 metric tons of NdPr in the quarter -- good for annual growth of 94%. MP ended Q3 with a cash and equivalents position of roughly $1.34 billion and continues to expect capital expenditures for this year to be between $500 million and $600 million.

The company said that NdPr performance in the fourth quarter will likely remain largely consistent with performance in Q3 -- with guidance for roughly 1,000 metric tons sold and pricing holding at roughly $100 per kilogram. Next year, MP anticipates that its cost structure will improve as NdPr production scales and progress is made on efficiency initiatives. The company also said it had roughly $28 million in deferred revenue from magnetic precursor products, which it expects to realize over the next several quarters.

MP expects sales of magnets to scale gradually over the next couple of quarters and see a significant uptick in the second half of next year. The acceleration in the back half of the year will be partially driven by the initiation of magnet production for Apple. The company booked $8 million in prepayments from Apple in the third quarter and said that it expects an additional $120 million in prepayments over the next one to two years.

On the same day that MP released its Q3 results, Bloomberg published a report saying  that the investments made by the federal government in MP Materials and other domestic rare-earth minerals companies under President Donald Trump's administration could face heightened scrutiny following the Democratic Party's big wins in the midterm elections.

In July 2025, the U.S. government made an investment in MP that gave it a roughly 15% equity stake. The Department of Defense also provided the company with a $150 million loan and signed a 10-year production offtake agreement that established pricing floors.

While many figures in government, as well as in the defense and tech industries, have come to view improving the U.S.'s ability to source rare-earth minerals and magnets domestically as an important priority, the Trump administration's direct investments in companies have drawn political criticism. With Democrats regaining control of Congress, there's greater risk that the investments could be unwound or face scrutiny that creates headwinds for MP Materials stock.

I'll be watching how things shake out after the newly elected politicians are sworn in in January.
