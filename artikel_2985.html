Hope Bancorp: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Lower deposit costs and a variable-rate loan portfolio could position the bank to defend margins through interest-rate cycles.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 19, 2026 at 2:30PM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 28, 2026, Hope Bancorp (HOPE -1.70%) announced a 7% quarter-over-quarter revenue increase to $157 million following its acquisition of SMBC MANUBANK, a commercial banking unit. Expanding into complementary commercial lending niches and optimizing funding costs directly impacts long-term earnings power for regional bank shareholders.

Management offers three key insights into where the business is heading.

Integrating new commercial banking operations broadens regional market coverage and deepens corporate client relationships. Reaching distinct customer segments creates organic cross-selling opportunities without adding heavy overhead costs.

Replacing expensive time deposits (certificates of deposit with set expiration dates) with non-maturity deposits (flexible checking and savings accounts) reduces overall funding expense. Lower borrowing costs help expand net interest margin (the difference between interest earned on assets and interest paid on deposits).

Concentrating new loan originations in variable-rate structures protects interest income when market benchmark rates shift. Having nearly three-quarters of the loan book in variable or hybrid structures reduces exposure to fixed-rate repricing lags.

Revenue momentum and operating leverage are expanding into year-end, and leadership isn't hiding its satisfaction with the trajectory.

An expanded Southern California footprint, lower deposit costs, and a broader commercial client network support long-term earnings resilience. Leadership is prioritizing integration discipline and loan pricing oversight.
