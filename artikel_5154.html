Three months after selling $23 billion of new shares at $95 apiece, Intel now faces a lawsuit from investors who say they were kept in the dark. A Michigan pension fund, the City of Southfield Fire and Police Retirement System, filed a proposed class action on Friday in federal court in San Jose. The complaint claims Intel waited too long to tell shareholders how a weakening PC market and piles of unsold inventory would hurt its revenue. It argues those problems were bigger than any lift Intel was getting from AI data centre demand. The suit also alleges Intel had a reason to stay quiet, since it was raising money midway through a troubled quarter to fund its contract manufacturing push under CEO Lip-Bu Tan. Tan and finance chief David Zinsner are both named as defendants. The case covers investors who bought between July 23 and October 29, 2026, and seeks unspecified damages. Intel’s stock lost 28.6% on October 30, erasing about $140 billion in value, and closed Friday at $59.66. Intel has not yet commented.
