The United Kingdom has moved beyond simply asking how crypto should be regulated in 2026. The main rules have now been written into law, firms are preparing for authorization, tax authorities are building reporting systems, and regulators are defining how stablecoins, exchanges, custody, staking and market activity will be supervised.

The changes are being led by four key institutions: the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), HM Treasury (HMT), the Bank of England (BoE) and HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC).

Here’s a closer look at the UK’s crypto regulations in 2026, the latest changes and what they mean for businesses and investors.

The UK’s Cryptoasset Reporting Framework (CARF) took effect on January 1, 2026. Under the framework, crypto exchanges, brokers, and other service providers must collect customer identity and transaction data. The first reports cover 2026 activity and are due by May 31, 2027.

The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), through consultation paper CP26/4, proposed applying Consumer Duty, customer protection, safeguarding, training and reporting requirements to crypto firms. The consultation closed on March 12, and the FCA published the final rules on June 30.

On February 4, Parliament formally enacted the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026. Treasury commissioners Stephen Morgan and Christian Wakeford signed the legislation, which brings crypto trading, custody, stablecoins and staking under FCA regulation.

However, the regime is scheduled to take effect on October 25, 2027

HM Treasury published a draft proposal to move certain activities involving UK-issued stablecoins from general crypto trading rules to a separate payments framework. At the same time, the FCA opened a consultation to clarify which crypto activities fall under its supervision. The consultation closed on June 3, 2026.

The FCA published PS26/9, covering cryptoasset admissions, public offers and market abuse. The rules introduce disclosure requirements, due diligence standards and controls against insider dealing and market manipulation on regulated crypto trading platforms.

HMRC published draft legislation to change how crypto loans and liquidity pools are taxed. The proposal aims to prevent users from facing Capital Gains Tax (CGT) on certain DeFi activities when they have not actually received cash or sold their assets. The government later confirmed the change in its October 28 Autumn Budget.

HM Treasury laid the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations before Parliament. The amendments cover stablecoin payment arrangements, temporary settlement services, proprietary trading and technical services involving decentralised protocols.

The FCA published PS26/18, explaining which crypto activities require regulatory approval under the new framework. Existing crypto businesses can apply for permission to operate under the new regime until February 28, 2027, to qualify for transitional protections.

Chancellor John Healey presented the UK Budget 2026, confirming plans to change the tax treatment of eligible stablecoins, cryptoasset loans and liquidity pools. The government included these measures in its plans for Finance Bill 2026–27.

HMRC updated its CARF reporting guidance, explaining how crypto businesses must submit customer and transaction information. It removed the planned January 2027 reporting start date because its online service was not ready. The first reporting deadline remains May 31, 2027.

Treasury commissioners Christian Wakeford and Shaun Davies signed the Cryptoassets (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026. The changes exempt certain market-making, proprietary trading, and technical services from specific authorisation requirements. Most provisions took effect the following day.

The UK does not have one single crypto regulator. Instead, responsibilities are divided between four key regulators.Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)

The FCA is the main day-to-day regulator for crypto firms. Its responsibilities include anti-money laundering registration, financial promotion rules, market abuse requirements, and consumer protection.

The Bank of England focuses on financial stability and has a larger role in stablecoins. It will jointly supervise systemic stablecoins with the FCA and runs the Digital Securities Sandbox.

HM Treasury is responsible for designing the UK’s broader legislative framework. It determines which crypto activities fall within the statutory regulatory perimeter and can designate a stablecoin or payment system as systemic under the Banking Act 2009.

HMRC handles crypto taxes, including Capital Gains Tax, Income Tax and Corporation Tax. It also oversees the UK’s Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF).

Crypto mining is legal in the UK and does not require an FCA licence. However, miners must follow HMRC tax rules and meet their electricity supply and business requirements.

HMRC taxes mining rewards based on whether the activity is a hobby or a business. Hobby miners generally pay Income Tax on their rewards, while commercial miners pay tax on trading profits and may deduct eligible business expenses.

Miners who later sell, swap or spend their coins may also pay Capital Gains Tax on any increase in value. For the 2026/27 tax year, the annual CGT allowance is £3,000, with rates of 18% and 24%.

Crypto remains a significant retail asset in the UK despite tighter regulation. Around 8% of UK adults owned crypto in 2026. That is down from 12% in 2024 but double the 4% recorded in 2021.

Overall crypto awareness remains high at 91%.

The UK government holds around 61,245 Bitcoin (BTC), making it the third-largest government Bitcoin holder after the US and China. These holdings are worth an estimated $3.5 billion to $3.8 billion.

The UK did not buy these Bitcoins for a national crypto reserve.

More than 61,000 BTC were seized by London’s Metropolitan Police during an investigation into Zhimin Qian, who ran a large investment fraud in China between 2014 and 2017 before fleeing to the UK.

The most important date ahead is October 25, 2027, when the full crypto regulatory regime is scheduled to take effect.

Before that deadline, existing firms have to navigate the FCA authorization process, with the key application deadline for transitional protections set for February 28, 2027.

HMRC will also begin receiving the first CARF reports covering 2026 activity by May 31, 2027.

That makes 2027 a turning point for the UK crypto industry.
