How UTSA basketball aims to stay the course through coaching upheaval

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

UTSA guard Isaiah Watts can't imagine that any team has faced more adversity through the first two weeks of the 2026-27 season than the Roadrunners.

A team that entered the year with just one returning player after a 4-25 season was dealt a major blow off the court just days before the opening tipoff, as coach Austin Claunch was arrested on suspicion of DWI on Oct. 27 in Corpus Christi.

He was suspended the next day, and on Friday UTSA announced that the program and Claunch had parted ways, elevating assistant Nick Bowman to interim head coach for the rest of the season.

But through all the turbulence, the Roadrunners found some bright spots, claiming two of their first three games after winning just three games against Division I opponents all last year.

Though UTSA (2-2) hit another bump in the road with an 87-67 loss to Texas State (5-0) on Sunday in the Convocation Center, the Roadrunners resolved to continue holding steady, putting the early season upheaval in the past.

"We are the team that has faced the most adversity in the country, without a question," Watts said. "But I think we've done a great job of just coming together and like coach said, just enjoying the process, enjoying the change. We're bought in."

Entering the year, Claunch talked about wanting UTSA to reestablish its identity in his third season, wanting to push a faster pace while becoming more disruptive on defense.

Watts said nothing has changed about the team's mindset and approach because of the shift in leadership. Bownman acknowledged that his philosophy will continue to emphasize 3-pointers and layups, but he said the more important piece of the identity was playing as a unit.

"It's not my team, it's their team. That's the first thing," Bowman said. "It's their team, and it's a team that really, really enjoys playing together, and they enjoy being UTSA Roadrunners. ... We're not worried about any changes that have occurred. Those changes have been happening for three weeks."

The Roadrunners were chasing just their second 3-1 start in the past 14 seasons. UTSA opened 4-1 in 2024, but that included wins against non-Division I programs Trinity and St. Mary's.

Balanced scoring has been one of the early keys, as five players average at least 8.8 points, with KJ Tenner topping the group at 14.0 per game. Tenner's 18 points in an 86-59 win at Seattle on Wednesday marked the highest output for any UTSA player this season.

"We've really come together as a group," guard Shey Eberwein said. "No matter what the adversity is, the missions is still the same. The goals are still the same. We've got to show up every day and continue getting better."

UTSA entered the night forcing an average of 17.7 turnovers per game but struggled to have the same success against the methodical Texas State offense, forcing 14 turnovers.

The undersized, free-flowing Roadrunners have struggled in the paint early this season, and the Bobcats took advantage by building a 44-27 rebounding advantage. Texas State forward DJ Hall finished with game highs of 19 points, 18 rebounds and four assists.

"We're going to have to find a group that can rebound," Bowman said. "We're playing a lot of guys right now. I think that's good for us, but at some point, we can only play the guys that are going to be tough in the paint and rebound."

The Bobcats broke open a tight game in the second half when the Roadrunners went five minutes without a field goal, allowing Texas State to make a 13-2 run that pushed the margin to 65-47 midway through the final period.

UTSA never threatened the rest of the way, as Texas State, which continued its best start since the 1980-81 season, led by as many as 23 points.

Bowman pointed to Texas State's physicality, as the Bobcats won the rebounding battle, grabbed more loose balls and bullied their way to the free-throw line, going 23 of 29 for the game. He also pointed to some missed layups and UTSA shying away from the 3-pointer, attempting just nine in the second half compared to 17 in the first. But more than anything, he said he was focused on the Roadrunners' culture, eager to see how the group would respond to its latest test.

"I want it to be a team that plays with joy. I want it to be a team that plays with pride for UTSA," Bowman said. "I don't know if we've had that in the previous two years. I don't know if the guys have been fully committed to wanting to be good here. I think a lot of times now in college basketball, they want to be good so they can be good somewhere else."

This season marks Bowman's first opportunity as a head coach. He arrived at UTSA in 2024 after a year as the lead assistant and recruiting coordinator with New Orleans following five seasons on Claunch's staff at Nicholls.

He also spent three seasons as the director of recruiting at Clemson, a year as the director of basketball operations at his alma mater, Eastern Kentucky, and a year as an operations assistant at Tennessee following a season as a graduate assistant at EKU.

But through the whirlwind of a last-minute transition, Bowman hasn't had much time to consider what the moment means for his career, pushing focus back on a home matchup against Florida A&M at 7 p.m. Wednesday.

"I'm not trying to do anything but win Wednesday night," Bowman said. "There are certain people that I will really look forward to connecting with here in the coming weeks in terms of maybe a vision throughout the season, but I don't have time for anything else right now other than to think about beating Florida A&M on Wednesday night."
