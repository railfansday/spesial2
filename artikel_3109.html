Is Merck's Stock Still a Good Buy at $150?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The healthcare stock has surged nearly 40% this year.

It's been a tremendous year for drugmaker Merck (MRK -2.23%), whose shares have soared by nearly 40% thus far in 2026, performing far better than the broader market, as the S&P 500 has risen by just under 9%.

Merck hit a new all-time high of nearly $157 earlier this year. While it's come down from those levels, it's not far from its peak. Is this top healthcare stock still a good buy at around $150, or has it gotten too expensive?

The big concern around Merck in recent years has been that, as strong as its top cancer drug Keytruda has been, forward-looking investors worry about where the company's future growth will come from as key patents on the drug expire in 2028.

Merck, however, has launched a new version of the drug, which is a subcutaneous (under-the-skin) injection, called Keytruda Qlex. The company hopes that this will help it continue to generate revenue from Keytruda even after the loss of exclusivity for its original intravenous formulation.

Revenue for Keytruda in the third quarter (which went up until the end of September) was stronger than analysts expected, coming in at $8.6 billion, which included $830 million from Qlex. It has given investors hope that the company's Keytruda sales might not nosedive as sharply as previously feared as generics enter the market in the near future.

There is, nonetheless, still some risk with the stock. For investors, it may come down to how much of a discount is justifiable.

Keytruda is a massive part of Merck's business, accounting for close to half of its revenue in Q3. The outlook for the drug will have a drastic impact on the overview for the overall business and how its stock might perform.

Once generics are available in the market, there will inevitably be some drop in revenue; it's a matter of how significant it will be. That's the big question right now, and why some sort of discount is warranted.

Currently, Merck's stock trades at a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of 16, which is based on analyst expectations of future earnings. The S&P 500 averages a forward P/E multiple of less than 19. There's a bit of a discount for Merck's stock, but it isn't a big one.

While the business is doing well and the stock has been surging this year, I'd hold off on buying it because, at this level, it may have more downside risk than potential upside.
