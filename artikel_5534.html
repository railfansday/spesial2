Winter solstice daylight in 15 U.S. cities, from no sunrise to a short day

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

Winter solstice daylight shrinks with latitude, and these 15 U.S. cities show how, from Utqiaġvik, Alaska, where the sun never rises that day, to northern cities in the lower 48

On the same December date, one U.S. city goes without a sunrise. Cities farther south still see the sun cross the sky for several hours. The main reason for the gap is how far north each place sits.

The December solstice falls on or about Dec. 21 in the Northern Hemisphere. It brings the shortest period of daylight of the year. Solstice day length at any given place depends mainly on its latitude. The farther north a city lies, the less winter solstice daylight it gets.

Alaska holds the shortest solstice days in the U.S. Utqiaġvik, the city formerly known as Barrow, sits at roughly 71°N, north of the Arctic Circle. The sun sets there in mid-November and does not rise again until late January. That stretch of polar night means the solstice passes with no sunrise at all.

South of the Arctic Circle, the sun still rises on the solstice. Fairbanks, at about 64.8°N, gets about three hours and 42 minutes of daylight on the solstice. Anchorage, at about 61.2°N, gets about five hours and 27 minutes. Juneau, at about 58.3°N in southeast Alaska, gets about six hours and 21 minutes. Each step south adds light.

The same pattern holds in the lower 48, though the extremes are milder. Seattle, at about 47.6°N, is the northernmost of the large lower-48 cities in this selection. Its solstice day lasts less than nine hours. Fargo, North Dakota, at about 46.9°N, and Minneapolis, at about 45°N, also see under nine hours of light on the shortest day of the year.

The 15 cities in this list were chosen to trace that pattern. They are not a ranking. The selection starts above the Arctic Circle and moves south through Alaska. It then crosses the northern U.S., from the Pacific Northwest through the northern Plains and the Great Lakes to New England. It is not a complete list of the places with the least December sunlight.

Utqiaġvik, Alaska, gets no sunrise at all on the winter solstice. The sun stays below the horizon for the whole solstice date, which falls on or about Dec. 21.

The town, formerly known as Barrow, sits at roughly 71°N. That places it north of the Arctic Circle and farther north than any other city in this selection. It is the only place covered here where solstice daylight drops to zero.

The condition is called polar night. It is a stretch of the year when the sun never climbs above the horizon at a given location. Places north of the Arctic Circle go through it around the December solstice. Utqiaġvik's position puts it inside that zone.

The cause is the tilt of Earth's axis. At the December solstice, the Northern Hemisphere leans farthest away from the sun. At Utqiaġvik's latitude, the sun's daily path stays entirely below the horizon.

The dark period starts weeks before the solstice. The sun sets in Utqiaġvik in mid-November. It does not rise again until late January. The solstice lands inside that window rather than at either end of it.

Polar night is not the same as round-the-clock darkness. The sun sits only slightly below the horizon near midday. That lets the sky brighten into twilight for part of the day. The faint light arrives without the sun itself ever appearing.

The same tilt works in reverse in summer. Around the June solstice, the Northern Hemisphere leans toward the sun. Places north of the Arctic Circle then get a stretch when the sun does not set. Utqiaġvik's winter darkness is the mirror image of that summer period.

Fairbanks, the next city south in this selection, lies just under the Arctic Circle. The sun still rises there on the solstice. That makes the Arctic Circle the dividing line between a day with no sunrise and a day that is only short.
