Is Nvidia a Buy at $125? Here's My Honest Take

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

The top AI stock is down nearly 50%. Is it a buy?

By Jeremy Bowman – Nov 22, 2026 at 9:30AM EST

Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) was the world's most valuable company a month ago. Now, its valuation has been cut in half.

The stock plunged 23% last week, its worst week since the height of the financial crisis in 2008, as the fallout from Jensen Huang's resignation continues to play out.

In the midst of Nvidia's plunge, the AI chip leader reported another blowout earnings report, posting 103% revenue growth in the third quarter with strong margins, and it raised its guidance for next year.

The report was a reminder that even as the company is in turmoil over its leadership and multiple investigations over its chip exports to China, the business is as strong as ever, and some investors see the sell-off as a buying opportunity.

Nvidia is now cheaper than it's ever been, trading at a forward P/E of just 8, and it just doubled its revenue. Typically, a stock growing that fast is valued at such a low multiple because investors don't expect those profits to last.

In Nvidia's case, that could be for two reasons. First, the semiconductor sector is cyclical, and Nvidia has gone through several market cycles before. The AI infrastructure build-out will eventually peak, which could lead to Nvidia's sales peaking in the AI boom. The four major hyperscalers are planning to spend $700 billion on capital expenditures this year, and cash flow has turned negative for some of them, meaning that level of spending isn't sustainable.

The other explanation for Nvidia's low P/E ratio is that investors are skeptical of the company's long-term growth after Huang's resignation and as the company faces investigations and potential consequences like fines, tighter export restrictions, and increased oversight after Huang had an affair with an alleged Chinese intelligence agent who was helping to funnel Nvidia chips to China.

No one knows the outcome of these investigations, and Nvidia's next permanent CEO remains a question mark as well. The bad news isn't necessarily over for Nvidia. On Friday, Reuters reported that the SEC is investigating the company over accounting issues, which contributed to a 14% drop in the stock.

Nvidia faces an extraordinary number of risks right now. The only CEO the company has had in its 33 years is gone, and he exercised an unusual degree of control over the company and its relationships with customers and suppliers, including spinning a complex web of financing to help backstop the AI sector. Additionally, Congress and several government agencies are investigating the company over chip export violations.

On the other hand, Nvidia shares are incredibly cheap, down by nearly 50% from their peak last month, and if the company can overcome the challenges it currently faces, it looks like a surefire winner. Just getting back to where it was in October would be a double, and the company continues to dominate the market for AI chips, as its recent earnings report shows.

My honest take, though, is that there are easier opportunities to take advantage of the AI sell-off from stocks that don't have the risks that Nvidia does. TSMC, for example, is down 35% from its peak last month, and the world's largest chip manufacturer has one of the widest economic moats in business. Alphabet and Meta Platforms aren't as directly exposed to AI as Nvidia, but both trade at P/Es below 20, and they're down by roughly a third from their peaks this year.

If you recognize the risk and volatility facing Nvidia and still want to buy it, I think the best way to do so is with a variation of the dollar-cost averaging method.

I would choose a target amount I was willing to invest, capped at 5% of my portfolio,and invest a quarter now at $125, and then invest another quarter each time the stock falls 10%, if that happens. So, I'd invest another quarter of the allotment at $112, the next quarter at $101, and the last at $91. This method allows you to take advantage of further pullbacks, which we could see given the uncertainty facing the company.

If you exercised all of those purchases, you'd have a cost basis of $107 on Nvidia, and if the stock doesn't fall that far, you'd still benefit.

Over the long term, I think Nvidia is more likely than not to recover, but that could take years, and we’re likely to see considerable volatility over the coming months. If you're interested in the stock, saving some cash to take advantage of deeper sell-offs makes sense.
