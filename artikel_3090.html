BioCardia, Inc. (BCDA -10.38%) reported a focus on regulatory milestones for its lead therapeutic and delivery platforms in Japan and the United States. Management indicated that all remaining questions from Japan's Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency have been addressed, allowing the company to proceed toward a Shonin submission for its CardiAMP cell therapy. In the United States, the company is pursuing a de novo approval pathway for the Helix delivery system, supported by clinical data from approximately 500 patients across 12 trials. Financial results for the quarter showed a stable operational cash burn, with research and development investments directed toward advancing these regulatory filings and expanding clinical trial site enrollment.

I would like to focus a little time on each of these regulatory initiatives to detail where we are at, the risks and our anticipated successful outcomes. Our efforts to seek approval in Japan for our CardiAMP cell therapy for the treatment of ischemic heart failure followed Japan's leadership supporting these therapies as they have recognized the potential for meaningful impact to treat heart failure.

They have recently approved and reimbursed a cell therapy in Japan for patients with heart failure. Our CardiAMP cell therapy may have advantages over what has been approved in that it is autologous and requires no immunosuppression. Delivering a patient's own cells resonates with Japanese culture that has historically avoided the transplantation of organs. Further, our CardiAMP cell therapy procedure is performed using minimally invasive catheter delivery without need for opening the patient's chest surgically to deliver the cells. We expect to be able to discharge the patients soon after the procedure on the same day. The patient need is great, and we look forward to real-world assessment of the CardiAMP cell therapy for these patients in Japan.

In our last quarter, we shared that we had remaining questions to address before the submission of CardiAMP cell therapy for regulatory approval in Japan. We have answered all of these questions to Japan's Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency satisfaction, and we are proceeding to Shonin submission with our designated marketing authorization holder. Our designated marketing authorization holder will prepare and submit the Shonin application and act as the local regulatory representative to enable BioCardia sales of CardiAMP cell therapy in Japan. Our work with them is also supported by additional preparatory consultancies that we have engaged. The timing of this is fluid, but it looks like most tasks will be completed in the coming months.

A primary risk of this time line is if our product documentation does not meet a requisite standard and we need to repeat testing of some sort. No deficiencies have yet been identified by BioCardia. However, as we work with our designated marketing authorization holder, we may identify areas where they may request additional work. We have been getting ready for some time preparing for this. Our team recently completed a recertification audit to the international standard that sets requirements for a quality management system specifically tailored for our industry. Our team has also taken steps to revalidate our product sterilization process with an eye towards Japanese regulations.

The expected initial clinical indication in Japan will be for approximately 20,000 patients with approval expected 12 months after we complete our Shonin submission. During this period, we would expect to be training physicians and finalizing the details of the post-marketing study and its logistics so that we are ready to begin at all centers as soon as approval by the Ministry of Health, Labor and Welfare and subsequent reimbursement has been established. Although our initial approval is only expected to be for 20,000 patients in Japan, we note that there are approximately 300,000 patients in Japan with ischemic heart failure today.

If the FDA does not support the de novo approval pathway, there will be additional discussions ahead.

We firmly believe that there is every reason for the Helix to be approved and intend to work with the agency to make this happen. In addition to the role of the investigational Helix platform for our own therapeutic development activities, Helix has potential to become the leading development platform for biologic therapies to treat heart disease as it provides efficient delivery and control for these investigational agents to the heart. We have enhancements to Helix that will impact our investigational cardiac therapies as well as those of partners.
