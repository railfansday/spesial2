traditional bamboo scaffolding and tea-waste panels shape impromptu projects’ pagoda in macau

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

referencing traditional pagoda forms, the temporary installation integrates a slender bamboo frame and translucent fabric skin along macau's shoreline.

Joao O edited by christina vergopoulou November 1, 2026 3 min

Sited on the shoreline of Sai Van Lake in Macau, Bamboo Pagoda is a 13-meter-tall temporary pavilion designed by local practice Impromptu Projects, led by Rita Machado and João Ó. The installation draws directly from Macau’s vernacular bamboo scaffolding, a building technique listed on the city’s Inventory of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2017, reinterpreting construction-grade craft into a contemporary waterfront pavilion for contemplation.

The design by Impromptu Projects studio references classical pagoda geometries and Taoist concepts of equilibrium, structured over an octagonal footprint that tapers as it ascends. Slender structural bamboo culms form the exposed load-bearing skeleton, bound using traditional scaffolding lashing methods. A semi-translucent skin of bamboo fabric wraps the framework, filtering natural light and diffusing the vertical profile. Grounding the structure, the floor surface is laid with composite panels manufactured by Macau-based firm Zence Object Technology (CHAZENCE), composed of recycled tea waste and discarded wood chips. Integrated solar panels power the project’s architectural spotlights off the municipal grid, combining intangible cultural heritage with circular material systems.
