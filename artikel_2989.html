Walmart Reports Earnings on Nov. 19 2026. Here's What Investors Should Be Watching.

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Besides the standard revenue, profitability and comparable-sales figures, two line items in particular are crucial.

This coming Thursday will be an important one for all retail stock investors, as one of the sector's giants is slated to report its latest set of earnings. This is the 800-pound gorilla of American retail, Walmart (WMT +0.25%), which will unveil its fiscal third-quarter 2027 figures before market open.

Although the company's most recently reported quarters haven't necessarily captured investors' hearts, it has met or beaten bottom-line analyst estimates in each of the last four frames. Here's a look at its chances of repeating the feat in the third quarter and what investors should keep an eye on when the massive retailer reports.

Analysts tracking Walmart aren't expecting a blowout third quarter from the retailer. They do, however, believe it will post some growth, with anticipated 5% year-over-year revenue growth to almost $187.3 billion, driven by a 2.9% increase in comparable sales, excluding fuel. On the bottom line, those pundits are modeling a 3% improvement in net income not under generally accepted accounting principles (non-GAAP, or adjusted) to $0.64 per share, which matches the high end of management's guidance.

Over the years, the nature of the retail business has changed. It's now commonplace for Americans to get the goods they need by buying them online. The always tech-forward Walmart flagged this trend years ago and has devoted considerable resources to building its online presence to the point where it rivals that of quick-fulfillment specialist Amazon (AMZN -3.34%).

Online matters for Walmart, and how it continues to develop will significantly impact the company's results. In the second quarter, e-commerce sales contributed 510 basis points to the comparable-store growth figure. Since that number was 2.6%, it meant that traditional brick-and-mortar sales actually declined during the period. So it's not a stretch to say that online is the motor of growth.

Thankfully, e-commerce continues to grow robustly. In that quarter, it posted a 23% year-over-year improvement in sales globally. More consumers are clearly eager to get on the train and ride it regularly, as the company's take from membership fees, anchored by its Walmart+ service (which offers free and expedited delivery, among other perks) and Sam's Club network of warehouse stores, rose by 17%.

All investors should keep an eye on that global e-commerce growth figure. It's ranged from the aforementioned 23% to 27% across the past four quarters. Given that, any number outside of that fairly tight range should be cause for celebration... or, alternatively, concern.

If I were a Walmart shareholder, I'd also watch that global membership fee growth rate, and the company's take from advertising. Why? Because both are high-margin businesses, and even a sector giant like Walmart doesn't boast impressive margins from its pure retail activities.

Many folks might not be aware that the company even has an advertising unit. This isn't surprising, as it isn't typical for retailers. Despite contributing only a small percentage of the company's overall revenue, advertising is growing fast and boasts significantly higher profitability. The company's still-considerable network of brick-and-mortar stores and its increasingly strong online presence provide excellent venues for brands to advertise their wares, both online and offline.

Walmart isn't shy about convincing outside companies to pony up for ads, and they're more than willing to say yes. Growth in the business has been red-hot, with year-over-year revenue improvements of 37% and 38% in the first two quarters of the current fiscal year. For rather arcane accounting reasons, the company only discloses the exact dollar figure in annual, not quarterly, reports. Nevertheless, the full-year 2026 numbers can give us a good idea: ad revenue totaled almost $6.4 billion, comprising less than 1% of overall revenue. To say this business has potential is an understatement, and its development should be near the top of every investor's must-watch list.

I think investors will be expecting notable top- and bottom-line beats for Walmart on Thursday, and it'll help greatly if online sales growth is notably higher than in the trailing frames. That'll convince retail investors and other interested parties that Walmart, which traditionally does well thanks to rising bargain consciousness in strained economic times like these, is an outperformer.

One thing I like very much about this company is that it continues to pursue a smart, technology-embracing business strategy that has more than one path for growth. That makes it a solid, long-term investment. So unless it misses by a worrying margin on any of those analyst estimates, I'd be a buyer of the stock.
