Xerox: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Management is redesigning hardware to slash service costs, a structural shift that could reshape profitability if execution sticks.

By The Motley Fool Team – Nov 23, 2026 at 1:16AM EST

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 29, 2026, Xerox (XRX -0.65%) announced that it is lowering full-year profit expectations and suspending dividends to conserve cash. External cost inflation in memory and oil is squeezing margins faster than management can cut internal expenses.

Yet hardware sales ticked up for the first time in over three years, showing underlying momentum in workplace printing. Three key statements from management clarify where the business is heading.

Management lowered its full-year adjusted operating income guidance to between $450 million and $475 million. The board also halted cash payouts to shareholders, saving about $28 million annually to support debt reduction and protect total liquidity, which stood at $726 million.

Component costs expanded rapidly during the quarter, with memory rising from 2% of machine costs early in the year to 10%. Higher freight and materials prices added to those pressures, forcing management to plan portfolio price increases starting in early 2027.

Service maintenance generates over $3 billion in annual revenue, but sending technicians to fix broken machines cuts into profits. Management is designing printers with fewer internal parts and embedded artificial intelligence sensors so technical issues get resolved remotely.

Xerox faces higher input expenses, but cost reduction and debt reduction continue as planned. Integration savings from the Lexmark acquisition should yield $100 million in 2027. Management is expanding global capability centers, which are offshore internal service hubs employing 15% of staff, to increase operational efficiency.

The company is shifting balance sheet debt and enforcing stricter pricing to protect margins against entry-level hardware shifts.
