Not Starlink. Not Starship. This SpaceX Business Could Be Worth $80 Billion on Its Own.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Space Exploration Technologies' fastest-growing business doesn't fly, orbit, or beam internet, and it might be worth $80 billion on its own.

By Micah Zimmerman – Nov 24, 2026 at 12:30AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

The best tenant I could ever imagine having is the one that paid on the first of every month without a reminder. Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +4.35%) has one that pays $1.25 billion a month.

That tenant is Anthropic (ANT -1.43%), which signed cloud services agreements in May to use about 325,000 Nvidia GPUs across SpaceX's Colossus and Colossus II data centers, at $1.25 billion per month through May 2029. A few weeks later, Alphabet's Google signed up for roughly 110,000 GPUs at $920 million a month from October 2026 through June 2029.

That first tenant also just got a lot richer. Anthropic closed its IPO on Nov. 20, with about $91.6 billion of the proceeds going to the company. A tenant with that much cash is exactly the kind a landlord hopes for.

Rockets get the headlines. Rent is quietly becoming one of the most valuable things SpaceX sells.

SpaceX's Colossus clusters train its own Grok models, and in the second quarter of 2026, the company began renting capacity to outside customers. By the third quarter, SI solutions and infrastructure revenue had jumped to $7 billion, and even excluding the newly acquired Cursor, it grew 192% sequentially, "driven primarily by the activation of new cloud services agreements," CFO Bret Johnsen said.

"The industry continues to demand ever more massive amounts of compute," president Gwynne Shotwell told investors, while Johnsen added that "at current pricing, new compute deployments are generating a payback of less than a year." A landlord who earns back the cost of the building in under a year is running a very good business.

Elon Musk was blunter, saying, "we could rent out our compute several times over if we said yes to everyone," and, after noting SpaceX ended September with 1.7 gigawatts of capacity, "I would be surprised if we brought on less than 13 gigawatts of compute by the end of next year." More buildings, in other words, and a waiting list of tenants.

Here's how I get to $80 billion. The closest public comparison is CoreWeave, a pure-play AI cloud that expects to exit 2026 at an annualized revenue run rate of $20 billion to $21 billion. Its shares have fallen 55% in three months, leaving it with a market cap of about $22 billion, plus about $47 billion of debt against $4 billion of cash.

The two disclosed SpaceX contracts total $2.17 billion a month. That's a conservative enterprise value because it ignores every other cloud customer and uses a multiple from one of the most hated stocks in cloud computing right now.

It also explains why the business gets overlooked. SpaceX's market cap is about $1.74 trillion, and the stock has dropped about 20% in the past month.

The risks are real, and they live in the fine print. SpaceX's cloud contracts can typically be terminated by either party on 90 days' notice, and Musk likes it that way, saying "our approach is to rent at the -- effectively the spot market rate as opposed to sign these long-term deals." That works beautifully while compute is scarce, but rent could reset quickly if the AI spending boom cools.

Concentration matters, too. A single SI customer accounted for 34.5% of SpaceX's total third-quarter revenue. If that customer walked, the rent roll would look very different.

The build-out isn't cheap, either. SpaceX has a commitment with an Apollo-managed entity for GPU finance leases expected to total about $40 billion. That's a lot of money tied to chips that could look dated quickly once newer, faster ones arrive, and a landlord still owes the mortgage even when a tenant leaves.

Still, I keep coming back to that first-of-the-month check. While investors argue over Starship's next flight, SpaceX is already collecting rent, and in a market this nervous about AI, cash coming in every month is worth more than another promise.
