Keith Wyness suggests how far PIF are from agreeing £1.5bn Newcastle sale

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Newcastle United's PIF ownership could potentially sell the club for £1.5billion once they establish their new training ground and planned new stadium.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the club is already worth as much as £900million.

Recent reports have suggested that an announcement over plans for a new stadium on Tyneside could be just around the corner, which would boost the valuation even further.

However, Wyness does not expect a sale to truly be on PIF's radar for another three to five years at least.

Newcastle are hoping to have their training ground ready for the 2029-30 campaign, and a change of stadium could closely follow.

PIF's future at the club has been up in the air of late, but Wyness still believes they will at least see those projects through before pulling away.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that PIF simply wouldn't get the necessary rewards by selling the Magpies soon.

He outlined that they have already pumped around £600m into the club, so would have limited gain from a sale in the £900m ballpark.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained that a sale right now would have little benefit.

"Well, that's a difficult one because they paid £300m for the club and I think they've put in about £600m of their money so far. So you're already looking at £900m. Now that's just to get your money back," he said.

"You'd be looking at somewhere between £800m and £1bn would be the valuation I'd be looking at. However, if they were to put the training ground and stadium in place, then you could be looking at £1.2bn to £1.5bn in my opinion, depending on how the revenue and the size of the stadium pans out.

"That's the sort of time that they might consider a sale because they get some profit out of the whole thing. So I don't see it being any time soon; could be three to five years maybe.

"I don't really see a sale happening anytime soon because at present you wouldn't get the valuation they would need to make some money out of the whole thing."

Former Manchester City financial advisor Stefan Borson told Football Insider last weekend that Newcastle could potentially lose £75m a year on interest rates alone while building a new stadium.

The football finance expert noted that would be before the stage taking out a £1.5bn loan to help fund the project.

Borson pointed out that Newcastle will do well to secure an interest rate that is less than double the 2.5 per cent that Tottenham paid while building their new ground.

He pointed out that loan rates have climbed "even higher" since then, creating a difficult situation for Newcastle moving forward.
