Questions & Antlers: Early season impressions, featuring Nets Daily’s Anthony Puccio

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Welcome to our second instalment of Questions & Antlers for the 2026-27 season. Previously, Jack and I chinwagged in response to your questions about all things offseason: coaching, player projections, statistics, coverage, contracts, and priorities. This time, just short of the season's quarter-way mark, we welcome you to an open mic on early season impressions—and boy have the Bucks provided scope for that! Joining us, thanks to a terrific suggestion from Terrapin Revisited, is a man who knows a thing or two about the position the Bucks find themselves in, Nets Daily's Editor-in-Chief Anthony Puccio.

This season was always going to be a challenging one, but our preseason predictions—Part I and Part II—are already looking off. Yes, things have been T.O.U.G.H. With just one win in their first 14 appearances, the Bucks are making the wrong kind of history, and you'd be hard pressed to name three players who've exceeded expectations thus far. On top of the losing, the Gary Trent Jr. contract probe still lingers and the Bucks have finally tasted the injury bug, with Ousmane Dieng set to undergo surgery on a fractured finger.

Still, it's not all bricked Js and lost balls off feet. Tyler Herro has been (mostly) very good. So too has Jaime Jaquez Jr. The rookies, meanwhile, are already ahead of their curves. There have also been flashes from Kasparas Jakučionis and Kel'el Ware, even if they've been less frequent than what we'd hoped for. And it's clear that Taylor Jenkins is fine with experimentation until something sticks.

A side familiar with the Bucks' cellar-dwelling woes is the Brooklyn Nets. Following their loss to the Bucks in the epic, seven-game Eastern Conference Semifinals in 2021, the Nets were swept in the first round in both 2022 and 2023. Since then, they have failed to make the postseason, finishing 11th, 12th, and 13th in the Eastern Conference standings. Currently, the Nets sit 3-10 on the season and two spots ahead of the Bucks in 13th position, with their wins coming against the Indiana Pacers, Charlotte Hornets, and New Orleans Pelicans.

Individually, Michael Porter Jr. is leading the way in points (21.5 PPG), rebounds (7.5 RPG), and steals (1.3 SPG), while offseason acquisition Julius Randle is leading the team in assists (5.0 APG), and Day'Ron Sharpe is pacing the team in blocks (1.3 BPG). Like the Bucks, though, these Nets are building toward the future, with nine players 22 years of age or younger, headlined by 2026 sixth-overall pick Mikel Brown Jr. and 2025 eighth-overall pick Egor Demin.

This is where Puccio comes in. As Nets Daily's Editor-in-Chief, he knows our struggle better than we do (you can tell because his rundown of the Nets' season to this point—"I figured they wouldn't be good, but I hoped they'd at least be fun. Not sure they're either."—hits far too close to home). Of course, Puccio's expertise isn't limited to quips—or even the Nets. A digital media professional, he's worked at Reddit, Meta, Boardroom, Front Office Sports, and The Associated Press, so you can trust he knows his stuff.

And with the introductions out of the way, I hand it over to you, Brew Hoop fam. Step up to the mic with your questions for Puccio (and the BH staff)—and don't forget to reconvene for the "antlers," coming soon.
