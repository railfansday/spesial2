Milwaukee Brewers: Pat Murphy named NL Manager of the Year finalist for 3rd consecutive season

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Following the 2023 MLB season, Craig Counsell decided he could not win with the Milwaukee Brewers and ditched his hometown franchise for their arch-rivals, the Chicago Cubs. In the aftermath, there was plenty of anger felt by Brewers fans and, if they're being honest, the organization itself.

Arguably the smartest move the front office made after Counsell's defection was to hire a manager that already had a relationship with the players, someone they already respected immensely, and someone they felt could do what Counsell never could: win the World Series.

Milwaukee Brewers manager Pat Murphy holds up a Milwaukee Journal Sentinel World Champs newspaper while celebrating after winning Game 5 and the World Series against the Tampa Bay Rays 4-1 at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, Fla. on Oct 28, 2026.

What more is there to say about Murphy that has not already been said? When he took over as Milwaukee's manager, everyone expected the team to take a nosedive in the standings. However, he led them to a 93-69 record and won the NL Central. Due to the immense surprise many had at the Brewers' season performance, he became the first skipper in franchise history to win Manager of the Year.

Once again, the following year, many experts picked Milwaukee to take a dive in the division. Instead, Murphy led them to a then-franchise best 97-65 record, another NL Central title, and an appearance in the NLCS. He became the first skipper in National League history to win Manager of the Year two years in a row.

And again this year, many did not think the Brewers would win even 85 games. Murphy led them to a Major League and franchise-best 103-59 record, won the NL Central for the third time, and brought home the organization's first ever World Series trophy.

For all of that, Murphy has been named a finalist for Manager of the Year once again:

He faces stiff competition this year, especially from Don Mattingly in Philadelphia who took over the struggling Phillies mid-season and turned them around into a playoff team. Indeed, that is an impressive achievement.

What it will come down to, though, is whether or not the writers have voter fatigue after voting Murphy the NL Manager of the Year in each of the past two seasons. Up until last year, no manager had won it two years in a row. Both Murphy and AL winner Stephen Vogt won it for the second straight time last year.

Will Murphy three-peat? Time will tell, but he certainly seems to be popular among baseball writers, which could help.
