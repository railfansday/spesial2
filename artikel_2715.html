College football draft targets for the Cowboys: Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

The Dallas Cowboys are still chasing their playoff dream this season, although it is going to be tough to get there. While that is going on, there is no reason not to take a quick peek ahead to the 2027 draft. Here are some players that might be of interest.

Indiana returns from its bye needing a win to remain in the playoff race, while USC arrives with one of the most talented passing attacks in the Big Ten. The Trojans have two receivers capable of stretching the field and a quarterback whose draft stock continues to rise. That puts Indiana cornerback Jamari Sharpe under the spotlight. On the other side, USC defensive end Kameryn Crawford must create pressure against a Hoosiers line that has protected its quarterback well for much of the season.

Sharpe is a clean outside cornerback who has become one of the leaders of Indiana's defense. He enters the game with 25 tackles, seven passes defended and an interception this season. He's consistent and has the talent to keep up on a defense that asks its secondary to understand route combinations and keep the ball in front of it.

His best work comes in zone coverage. Sharpe reads the quarterback and recognizes routes developing around him. He also understands when to pass one receiver to a teammate before closing on another. His length helps him challenge the throwing window, while his closing speed allows him to break on the ball without giving every short completion room to become a larger gain.

We live in a world where cornerback is a top draft priority. DaRon Bland has yet to regain the form that made him a valuable starter. Shavon Revel is still trying to build his career. The Cowboys cannot enter another season relying on everything to break correctly for a thin group, and that includes the addition of Joey Porter Jr.

Sharpe would give Dallas size, experience and ball awareness on the outside. His comfort in zone coverage would help him contribute early while the coaching staff develops his man technique. He also plays with the physical mindset required to support the run and attack the football, which creates value beyond simply staying close to a receiver.

Crawford brings the frame teams search for at defensive end. The 6-foot-5, 255-pound junior has long arms and an explosive first step. He can rush from a two-point stance or place his hand on the ground. He has recorded 29 tackles, two sacks and seven quarterback hits this season after producing 5.5 sacks and 10.5 tackles for loss in 2025.

His burst creates the initial problem. Crawford can gain ground quickly and threaten the tackle's outside shoulder. Once the tackle opens the corner, Crawford has the length to keep himself clean and enough flexibility to flatten toward the quarterback. He can also convert speed into power when he builds momentum, driving through a blocker who sets too softly.

Dallas still needs young pass rushers who can grow into larger roles. The defense has produced better pressure in recent weeks, but its long-term edge rotation remains uncertain. Malachi Lawrence has offered useful snaps and Donovan Ezeiruaku is still trying to break through; neither removes the need to keep adding talent at a premium position.

Texas heads to Death Valley with major questions along its offensive line. Starting left tackle Trevor Goosby is out for the season, increasing the importance of right tackle Melvin Siani. LSU counters with Princewill Umanmielen, who ranks among the most disruptive edge defenders in the country. LSU can move Umanmielen around the formation, but his opportunities against Siani should provide some of the most useful draft tape for his evaluation.

Siani is a very tall and well built senior who has started all nine games for Texas, including seven at right tackle. He previously started at left tackle for Wake Forest and played on both sides at Temple, giving him more positional experience than most tackles in this class.

His movement ability stands out for a player of his size. Siani gains depth quickly in his pass set and mirrors changes of direction. He has enough range to reach defenders in space. In the running game, he is at his best when Texas allows him to move.

Terence Steele's contract and uneven play make his future at Dallas very sketchy next year, while Tyler Guyton gives fans and coaches optimism that the left tackle position could be resolved. The Cowboys need a player capable of competing on the right side and covering both positions when injuries strike.

Siani's experience at left and right tackle gives him a clear route onto the roster. He could begin as the swing tackle and provide competition for Steele's position to help coaches evaluate whether it's time to move on. His ability to move in the running game would also suit the offense.

LSU: Princewill Umanmielen, defensive end

Umanmielen has become one of the breakout defenders of the 2026 season. He's recorded 35 tackles, 12 tackles for loss and 8.5 sacks, adding 13 quarterback hits. He has produced at least half a sack in six games and entered November among the national leaders in both sacks and tackles for loss.

His athleticism drives the pass rush. Umanmielen accelerates quickly from a wide alignment and has the change of direction to finish when the quarterback steps away from the original pressure. He also uses his hands well enough to avoid being carried beyond the pocket.

Umanmielen fits the Cowboys' need for a pass rusher who can contribute quickly without requiring a first-round selection. His burst would give Parker a threat from wide alignments, and his experience standing up or playing with a hand down would allow Dallas to move him through different fronts.

He would not need to become a full-time run defender immediately. The Cowboys could use him in the rotation and let him attack on third down while he adds strength. That role would give the defense more speed off the edge and reduce its dependence on manufactured pressure.
