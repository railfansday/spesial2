Oil prices climb after U.S. strikes Iran's Qeshm Island in Strait of Hormuz

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Brent crude is up more than 1.7% after a Monday selloff of more than 7%, as U.S.-Iran talks and strike risks continue

The U.S. struck Iran's Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, according to The Wall Street Journal, and oil prices climbed on Tuesday.

Brent crude futures traded near $106.60 a barrel, up more than 1.7%, regaining some of the ground lost in Monday's drop of over 7%. West Texas Intermediate futures gained.

Three U.S. officials described the action as limited and defensive, meant to protect U.S. assets in the region, according to CNN. One official said the strike came after Iranian fire directed at an American aircraft. Washington has conducted a string of limited strikes over recent weeks, and this one is the newest, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Tehran retains its right to self-defense and called the incident part of an "ongoing war," according to CNN.

President Donald Trump wrote on social media Monday that an agreement "could happen," according to CNN. Vice President JD Vance told reporters the two sides had "certainly made some progress," according to CNN. Tehran says the Strait of Hormuz could be open again within two days if Washington satisfies seven demands, while the U.S. rejects Iran's claimed authority over the waterway.

U.S. Gulf allies are bracing for the chance of a wider American offensive on Iran, which might provoke reprisals aimed at commercial and civilian facilities throughout the Middle East, according to The Wall Street Journal. Trump has been inclined to order a more sweeping strike following Tuesday's midterms, though he has yet to reach a firm conclusion.

Two vessels were hit Monday by projectiles of unknown origin near the coasts of Saudi Arabia and Oman, according to U.K. Maritime Trade Operations. Both crews were said to be unharmed.

Saudi Aramco CEO Amin H. Nasser said on the company's third-quarter earnings call Tuesday that oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz had not yet returned to levels seen before the conflict, according to The Wall Street Journal. Nasser said ships had to wait a long time before they could transit safely.

Oil prices had fallen on Monday as negotiations to end the conflict eased worries about Middle East supply. Brent had closed out October at a six-month peak. The U.S. and Iran signed a memorandum of understanding on June 17, but the agreement collapsed.
