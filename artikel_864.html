Australian Sandpapergate probe to include 2017/18 Ashes series

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Cricket Australia said their forum on the ball-tampering scandal will include the 'ball-management practices in the Australian men's team during the 2017-2018 season'; this includes their4-0 Ashes series victory over England

The scope of Cricket Australia's forum into Sandpapergate has been extended to include the Ashes series from the 2017-2018 season.

The forum will delve into "ball-management practices in the Australian men's team during the 2017-2018 season".

That means the Ashes series which Australia won 4-0 will be included.

The forum is for players, coaches and staff involved in the 2018 tour to South Africa, as well as executives and directors of the sport's national administration, and will run for six weeks.

Senior lawyer Fiona McLeod was appointed to head the forum, and will publish a report and recommendations based on submissions, with Cricket Australia then having 30 days to respond.

Players and staff have been told their participation and submissions will be treated as confidential, unless otherwise stated by the participant, and no player will be compelled to give evidence or subject to retrospective sanctions.

The scandal resurfaced following the publication of retired opener Usman Khawaja's autobiography in November.

It has been dividing Australian cricket for weeks - reopening the saga that rocked the foundations of the game over eight years ago.

Khawaja said the whole squad knew of the ball-tampering plot and should have collectively been held to account, and that the three players who received harsh bans- Steve Smith, David Warner and Cameron Bancroft- were scapegoats.

Bancroft was banned for nine months. Smith and Warner, then captain and vice-captain, were banned for 12 months.

Warner and Bancroft supported his claims after maintaining a silence on the issue since their bans were imposed.

Current captain Pat Cummins rejected Khawaja's allegation that the whole team knew sandpaper would be used to alter the condition of the ball during the game, describing the claim as "utter nonsense".

Khawaja and Cummins have indicated they will make submissions to the forum.

"All participants in Australia's 2018 tour of South Africa, including players and support staff, will be invited to take part in the Independent Forum, as will CA management and directors at that time," Cricket Australia said.

"While all involved will be encouraged to participate, there will be no compulsion to do so. All materials supplied will be treated as confidential unless otherwise stated by the participant and no sanctions will be retrospectively applied.

"Participants can choose to attend the Independent Forum in person, online and/or provide information via written accounts or through relevant documents and materials."

Cricket Australia chairman David Boon said: "This is an opportunity to provide their accounts in a safe environment and to provide the CA board with relevant information including about the events of that tour and resultant investigation.

"We are acutely aware of the enormous toll the renewed focus on this tour eight years ago has had on those involved and their welfare remains our main priority."
