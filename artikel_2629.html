Analysis: No. 10 BYU grounds weary but surging Baylor, picks up some style points along the way

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Head coach Kalani Sitake's search for a complete game from his BYU football team will have to wait another day, but Saturday's dominating performance against once-surging Baylor should be enough to keep the Cougars solidly in the top 10 of the College Football Playoff rankings.

That's one way of looking at No. 10 BYU's workmanlike 28-7 win over the pesky Bears, who had to fly into Provo Saturday morning due to the FAA's nationwide ground stop on Friday.

Another bit of analysis from the defense-led win over Baylor is that if the Cougars (5-2, 8-2) hope to win their next two regular-season games and stay alive for a CFP at-large berth or an invitation to the Big 12 championship game, they almost certainly will need running back LJ Martin on the field as much as possible.

With Martin starting the game Saturday, BYU marched for three touchdowns on its first three possessions, taking a 21-0 lead early in the second quarter and looking like the team it was when the season began.

But on the reigning Big 12 Offensive Player of the Year's sixth carry, which gave him 49 yards for the game, he stayed down for a few moments as the crowd of 63,008 gasped and then held its collective breath.

Martin walked off the field on his own power but never returned. Later, RB2 Sione Moa went out with an apparent knee injury and also didn't return after rushing seven times for 37 yards.

Sitake said Martin probably could have gone back into the game but was held out for precautionary reasons. The coach did not have an immediate update on Moa's situation.

Injuries to its top running backs — Preston Rex returned after missing his two last games and picked up 28 yards on nine carries — have become a major storyline for BYU this season, and the issue that might keep the Cougars from realizing their potential in 2026.

Thanks to Baylor jumping offside on a BYU field goal attempt two plays after Martin left the game, the Cougars finished the drive with a 1-yard touchdown plunge by Bear Bachmeier, the quarterback's third TD run of the game.

After that, however, BYU's offense fell silent until midway through the fourth quarter, a troubling trend that has happened often when Martin is not in games.

There is simply no replacement for Martin, who sustained a high ankle sprain in the first quarter of the 17-10 win over TCU on Oct. 3. The Cougars will only go as far as Bachmeier and Martin can carry them — especially if freshman receiver Legend Glasker is bottled up as much as he was Saturday.

"I think he could have gone," Sitake said of Martin. "There was a play where he just got stepped on and kind of irritated (the old injury), so I think we just have to be smart and just (be careful). I mean, he's got some hardware down there in his right ankle."

Suffice it to say, BYU got the most out of its 312 total yards of offense. Twenty-eight points are the most the Bears have given up in a loss this season. Sitake said the Cougars didn't necessarily plan to play so conservatively on offense, but that's what the game dictated.

With BYU's defense playing lights out — much better than last week in the 28-20 loss to Utah, for instance — why risk it?

Rarely did Bachmeier look downfield Saturday after throwing four interceptions against the Utes, and the strategy paid off. The Cougars played turnover-free, while ending their own streak of going five games without an interception.

Safety Tommy Prassas got the pick that ended the streak, and at a good time because Baylor's offense was starting to come out of hibernation after a dismal first half for quarterback DJ Lagway and his many weapons.

The Bears had scored 41 points against UCF (remember that crew, BYU fans?) and 42 against Iowa State before learning that the Cougar defense is a whole different animal at home.

"It feels like we played one of our better games of the season," said BYU defensive lineman Bodie Schoonover, who had two sacks.

Viliami Po'uha had BYU's third sack as the Cougars did a much better job containing the former five-star prospect Lagway than they did Utah QB Devon Dampier last week.

Baylor had only 87 yards of offense through three quarters before going on an eight-play, 73-yard touchdown drive to open the fourth quarter. The drive came after BYU's Matthias Dunn missed a 45-yard field goal to have his consecutive made field goals streak snapped at nine.

Cannon DeVries gave the offense a spark with a 44-yard punt return into Bears territory, and three plays later Bachmeier found Jojo Phillips for a 23-yard TD reception with a well-thrown ball.

It was the fifth TD catch of Phillips' career, and was set up because BYU's offensive line handled six rushing Bears to give Bachmeier time to find Phillips.

It was fitting that offensive guard Sonny Makasini spoke at the postgame presser; the Cougars' offensive line punished a Baylor D line that was supposed to be one of the best in the country.

"That was a hard-fought game," said Sitake, who opened his postgame news conference by thanking Baylor and its administration for playing the game after its flight to Provo was delayed until Saturday morning.

"I am glad that we started fast on everything because it looked like they were just never going to quit. They were trying to come back at the end and making something out of it. I am glad our defense was able to hold tight and make plays, especially when the offense kind of sputtered a little bit there."

Asked why Bachmeier — who was 14 of 24 for 104 yards — didn't throw the ball downfield more, Sitake replied: "You are asking the same questions I ask."

For the second straight game, penalties were also an issue for the Cougars, particularly in the third quarter.

DeVries opened the second half with an 81-yard kickoff return, but Miles Hall was flagged for holding and BYU had to start at its own 12-yard-line.

That possession ended with a punt after Glasker had taken a screen pass 62 yards, a play that was also nullified by a holding penalty on fellow receiver Reggie Frischknecht.

"We have to be more disciplined," Sitake said. "I will take a look at the film, but the refs aren't trying to screw us in our big plays. They're calling it how they see it, and those are unfortunate mistakes. … We even had penalties cost us some momentum, and cost us some stops on defense, too."

The Cougars' defensive performance — the Bears finished with a season-low 238 yards — is even more remarkable considering that it was without two of its top players, linebacker Isaiah Glasker and cornerback Evan Johnson.

"I am liking the effort that I've seen from the guys, the physical part that they're playing with," Sitake said. "It is starting to really show. I think the interceptions, the big plays, will start to come as we just keep doing our 1/11th."

The third quarter notwithstanding, it was probably the most complete game BYU has played this season, right up there with the Notre Dame game and the second half against Arizona.

"We were protecting the lead a little bit, maybe too much," Sitake said. "I felt like we were in control. Maybe the (18-play) drive in the first half gave us a little bit more confidence to grind the clock out a little bit.

"It might have been boring to some people, but I think we've got to preserve our health and get guys ready for the next week. Eventually we are going to need to open up and do things differently in all three phases to get where we need to be."

Speaking of complete games, the first 20 minutes were a thing to behold for the home team.

Bachmeier capped all three of BYU's scoring drives with short touchdown runs and also picked up a couple first downs on short yardage situations to finish with 51 rushing yards in the first half on 10 carries.

The Cougars racked up 150 rushing yards, 220 overall, in the first half, averaging 5.2 yards per play.

On the other side, Baylor was stifled at every turn and picked up just 54 yards in the first half, 2.3 yards per play.

Lagway was a frosty 5 of 11 for 23 yards in the first half, and probably should have been picked off at least once by BYU cornerback Tre Alexander, but the junior dropped what would have been the Cougars' first interception since the TCU game.

Leading 14-0, BYU caught a big break when the Bears jumped offside on a short field goal attempt. On fourth and 1, Bachmeier ran to the 1, then carried it into the end zone on the next play to give the Cougars the 21-0 halftime advantage.

The drive took 9:00 off the clock, went for 96 yards and took 18 plays — all season-highs. It was prolonged by a 19-yard pass from receiver Kyler Kasper to tight end Walker Lyons, with Kasper taking a backward pass from Bachmeier.

It was BYU's largest halftime lead against a power conference opponent since the 2008 win over UCLA when the Cougars led 42-0 at the break.

Bachmeier became the first Big 12 QB with at least three rushing touchdowns in a first half since Texas Tech's Patrick Mahomes in 2016 vs. Kansas State.

That's pretty good company to be in. So is the top 10 of the CFP rankings, and BYU did more than enough to ensure it stays there for at least another week.
