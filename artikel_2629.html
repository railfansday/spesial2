'Losing my leg seemed a viable option' - Tweed

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

In the blink of an eye, Darryl Tweed's life was turned upside down.

During a Supersport qualifying session at this year's North West 200, the 36-year-old from Ballymoney crashed at Dhu Varren exit of the Whins section of the circuit and was rushed to the Royal Victoria Hospital in Belfast.

Injuries to his shoulder and hip gradually improved over time, but the damage to his shattered leg has proven another matter.

Initially, it was hoped Tweed would be back on his feet within 12 weeks, but setback followed setback.

There was a real danger he could have lost his leg altogether and having missed his son's birthday as well as his own, impatience began to take hold.

"I've suffered with it now for six months, not being on my feet at all and I was getting quite down about it and was quite upset about the whole thing because it went from a very active life to nothing," Tweed told the BBC Bikes podcast.

"I was saying to the doctor, had I taken this [leg] off when it happened six months ago, I'd probably be walking by now.

"I was getting into that sort of mindset where it was like, 'I should just get this took off and forgot about it', but you don't realise the complications that would have for real life at home.

"I'm glad the doctors were a bit more sensible about the thing than I was. I was just wanting fixed."

While concerns for his leg persist and there remains much road to travel, Tweed is now somewhat mobile with the help of walking aids and a leg cage.

He now feels he is in "a better place", but the past six months brought added grief with the loss of his grandfather, his "buffer" whom he credits with introducing him to road racing.

"He went to all the races in Europe with me," Tweed reflected.

"He died while I was injured, so it was sort of like the universe telling me I need to just slow down and that you need to be stood still for this moment basically."

Tweed's continued period of recovery has given him plenty of time to take stock of what lies ahead and he has come to the conclusion that his days as a road racer are likely at an end, certainly "not to the level I did".

With a young family, Tweed feels "it's time for a different outlook" as to what the future holds and dependent on his recovery, may explore the possibility of a pilot's licence.

Still, leaving road racing is now easy given it has played such a central role in his life and became his identity.

"My garage is racing, my whole house, it's pictures and trophies, and it's just racing, it's just everywhere in my life.

"To now sort of look at it and go, 'that's not going to be part of my life anymore', it's, it's very difficult.

"It makes you sad. You're sort of losing a part of yourself, but you're starting a new chapter."

A former Manx Grand Prix winner, Tweed is now loking to the future, and while his achievements on the bike can never be taken away, now is not the time to reflect.

"That's probably for down the line," he says, as emotions begind to engulf.

"It still sort of nearly hurts to look at that sort of stuff so far. It's difficult when something's been such a part of your life."
