Andy Reid loved the execution of Cyrus Allen’s first career touchdown

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

There were a lot of firsts during the Kansas City Chiefs' 35-13 win over the Atlanta Falcons in Week 10. Running back Emmett Johnson's first start was very memorable and wide receiver Andrew Armstrong scored his first-career touchdown on a long bomb from quarterback Patrick Mahomes. Wide receiver Josh Downs played his first game in a Chiefs uniform.

The game also featured rookie wideout Cyrus Allen's first career touchdown. It came on a 39-yard throw where Allen came wide open down the left sideline.

It was a long time coming for Chiefs fans who bought into the Cyrus Allen excitement during training camp. When head coach Andy Reid was asked about the play on Monday, he was fired up about how it went down.

"You look at this touchdown, and I love his execution of that, the way he handled it," Reid said during the video call.

Reid went into detail on how Allen came open.

"They were pressing up on him a little bit; it was a double-move play," Reid shared. "Sometimes, when you get that press, you just want to take off and run but he actually sold it — aggressively sold it."

Allen has been showing off route-running chops since he was a standout at last year's Senior Bowl. The Chiefs recognized the talent and selected him in the fifth round, and now, those routes are blending with the chemistry he built with Mahomes over the summer.

"[He] was able to stay within the red line on the sideline there, the thing we have on the practice field," Reid explained. "He was able to stay there and give the quarterback an opportunity to have some space to make the throw. Patrick threw the perfect ball to him, but the execution was great by the kid."

The highlight of Allen's career is more impressive than anything third-year wideout Xavier Worthy has done in recent memory. When asked about Worthy, Reid pointed to the Falcons' coverage working to cloud Worthy; he mentioned that "it allowed other guys to be available."

But none of the three passes thrown towards Worthy were completed. Still, Reid says it just wasn't his day — but pointed to his Week 6 output of 73 yards on seven catches against the Los Angeles Chargers as proof he can be productive.

"He just needs to keep doing his thing and working, and he'll be fine," Reid said about Worthy. "Some days, it will be his, and some days, it will go to other guys. That's just how this thing works. We're spreading it around."

"It's tough on defenses," Reid continued. "So everybody is kind of getting a little piece of the pie. You saw when he was only the guy against the Chargers and his production there was great, so it's not that he's not capable of that."

As Downs continues to work more in the offense, it will be worth monitoring how the receiver rotation changes. Worthy has led the receiver room in snaps most of the time, but players like Allen — and even Armstrong — are doing more with less time on the field.
