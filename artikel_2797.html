Fletcher’s 3 TD runs lift No. 5 Miami past Virginia Tech 28-20, ‘Canes clinch spot in ACC title game

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

MIAMI GARDENS, Fla. (AP) — Mark Fletcher Jr. has never played in an Atlantic Coast Conference championship game.

Fletcher rushed for three touchdowns, Jake Weinberg kicked the longest field goal in Miami history and the fifth-ranked Hurricanes erased a 14-point deficit to beat Virginia Tech 28-20 on Saturday night and clinch a spot in the ACC title game.

"It's a blessing," Fletcher said. "Truly, just a blessing."

Fletcher had the second three-touchdown game of his career for Miami (10-1, 8-0 ACC, No. 7 CFP), while Cooper Barkate and Vandrevius Jacobs combined on 14 catches for 132 yards for the Hurricanes — who will face Wake Forest for the ACC title in Charlotte, North Carolina, on Dec. 5.

"We coached poorly, we played poorly and got ourselves in a hole," Miami coach Mario Cristobal said. "Had to dig out against a really talented team. This thing's a rivalry game. Bottom line is we didn't play to our standard, but we found a way to win."

Weinberg connected on a school-record, 59-yard field goal to end the first half. The previous record was 57, done by three kickers: Danny Miller in 1981, Michael Badgley in 2015 and Jose Borregales in 2020.

Ethan Grunkemeyer completed 22 of 35 passes for 168 yards for Virginia Tech (6-5, 3-5), which lost to Miami for the sixth consecutive time. The Hokies led 20-14 lead entering the fourth quarter before Fletcher scored on the first play of the final stanza — capping a 17-play, 90-yard drive — to give Miami its first lead of the night.

"We did things tonight that gave us a chance to go win," Virginia Tech coach James Franklin said. "We did not turn the ball over. We created two turnovers. ... Some really good things to talk about in the first half, but we did not put a complete game together."

Fletcher scored from 11 yards out with 2:08 left to push the lead to eight, and Miami took the ball over on downs with 1:27 remaining.

"When it gets really, really tough, nobody's better than him in the country," Cristobal said of Fletcher.

Miami, which got 241 passing yards from Darian Mensah and played again without injured receiver Malachi Toney, was in big trouble from the outset.

Virginia Tech won the opening toss and elected to receive. Someone in the coin-flip huddle yelled "noooo" after a Hokies' captain told referee Garrett Dickerson that they wanted the football, though it wasn't clear if the visitors actually wanted to defer to the second half.

Either way, it worked out — at least, for a while.

The Hokies went 75 yards in 15 plays on the game's first drive, Grunkemeyer connecting with Jeffrey Overton Jr. for a five-yard touchdown pass to open the scoring.

Mensah's first interception of the night was a ball that skipped off Miami running back Javian Mallory's hands and was picked off by the Hokies' Joshua Clarke. An unsportsmanlike conduct penalty on Mallory advanced the ball to the Miami 15, and Messiah Mickens went in from there one play later to give the Hokies a two-touchdown lead.

"We've got no panic button," Fletcher said. "Hats off to us for that."

Weinberg made it 14-3 with a 49-yard field goal, and Fletcher's first TD of the night — combined with a 2-point conversion — made it 14-11. With that, the comeback was on, but the Hurricanes had to sweat everything out until the final moments.

"There's not a lot of panic in our offense," Mensah said. "It's in our DNA to respond."

Virginia Tech: The Hokies wasted a 14-point lead for the second time this season, after also doing so against Pitt in what became a 35-33 loss on Oct. 2. Virginia Tech also fell to 1-10 in its last 11 November games and teams coached by James Franklin .

Miami: The Hurricanes have now won 12 consecutive ACC games, the longest such run in school history. The last time Miami had an 11-game ACC winning streak, it was snapped — by Virginia Tech.

Mensah threw two interceptions — after throwing two, total, in the season's first 10 games. The first Saturday was off Mallory's hands, the other was on a play in which Miami insisted that Virginia Tech jumped offsides — and the Hokies also seemed to think the Hurricanes were getting a free play. "There are two asterisks next to those picks. They weren't entirely his fault," Barkate said.

Virginia Tech: Hosts Virginia next Saturday.

Miami: Hosts Boston College next Saturday.
