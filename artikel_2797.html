The Toronto Maple Leafs (8-13-3) have not won since Halloween and are already eight points behind the Florida Panthers for the second Wild Card slot in the Eastern Conference. Although there is a long way to go, reaching the Stanley Cup Playoffs is not a plausible goal at this point. They need to focus on developing their young talent, namely the No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 NHL Draft. Alas, head coach Jim Hiller is not quite ready to give Gavin McKenna free rein.

The 18-year-old left wing is enduring plenty of growing pains in his first season in The Show, but the best way to push through them is by working hard and racking up ice time. He was once again left on the bench during sudden death. Toronto fell 4-3 in a shootout versus the Los Angeles Kings Thursday night, extending its losing streak to a stomach-churning eight games.

It marked the fourth time this season the Leafs have gone into overtime, and yet, McKenna has not played a single OT shift. Hiller explained himself after another brutal ending in Scotiabank Arena.

"He's watched," the first-year Toronto HC said, per sdpn's Jesse Blake. "We've had him up. And then called him off. He'll get out there eventually. But it's obviously a high leverage time. And you need somebody who can make a play. And again I just want to make sure he's ready."

There is a fine line between protecting the future face of the franchise and stunting his growth by being too cautious. The Maple Leafs are trying to give themselves the best chance to win, and an inexperienced forward who carries a -9 plus-minus may not stand out as an ideal candidate in crunch time. However, this is not just any guy. Toronto chose Gavin McKenna to reshape this team's long-term identity.

That label, even if it is not glued to him just yet, comes with considerable responsibilities. Eventually, the Leafs will have to extend more trust to the former Penn State star. But when will that be?

Plummeting Toronto will have a day to regroup before welcoming in the Columbus Blue Jackets (8-12-0).
