Packers urged to avoid Jonathan Taylor trade as cheaper running back option emerges

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

The Green Bay Packers are expected to explore running back options before the trade deadline as their ground game ranks last in the league. Uncertainty surrounding Josh Jacobs' future, following additional allegations of past domestic abuse, has added to the urgency. Jonathan Taylor is among the potential targets, but despite his standing as a top available player, he may not be the right fit for Green Bay.

ON SI's Jacob Westendorf shared that view in his Thursday, November 5 analysis, advising Green Bay against pursuing a trade for Taylor.

"Simply put, it would be a misuse of resources. The Colts are reportedly looking for either a first-round pick or something similar to the package of second- through fifth-round picks that the Carolina Panthers got when they traded Christian McCaffery to the San Francisco 49ers," the NFL analyst wrote.

"As good as Taylor is, can he save the Packers' putrid run game on his own? The answer is that it is unlikely," Westendorf added.

"While it should be noted that a team can only acquire what is on the market, if the Packers are willing to move significant draft capital, they should be looking for other spots on the roster."

Westendorf suggested that if Green Bay makes a move for a running back before the deadline, it should consider Tennessee Titans back Tony Pollard, who would likely come at a lower cost. This remains an option regardless of whether the Packers also address their offensive line.

Pollard's time in Tennessee could be nearing its end, with the struggling Titans prioritizing a roster overhaul rather than bringing him back.

The running back may instead look to the open market for what could be his final major contract opportunity, entering the next offseason at 30. His performance and ability to stay on the field over the remaining weeks of 2026 will likely play a major role in determining his value.

Acquiring Pollard could require Green Bay to surrender a fourth or fifth-round selection. A deal for Taylor would demand a much larger investment, potentially including a second-round pick on top of a comparable asset. Taylor also has two years left on his $44 million extension, adding a significant financial commitment to the trade cost.
