Eaton vs. Corning: Which Industrials Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Eaton's 10% revenue growth and Corning's 19% jump tell very different stories, but their valuations and customer risks may tilt the choice.

Investors looking for long-term growth often find themselves choosing between companies that power the modern world and those that build its literal framework. You might consider adding Eaton Corp. (ETN +0.07%) or Corning Inc. (GLW +2.19%) to your portfolio today.

Eaton focuses on managing electrical, hydraulic, and mechanical power for various industries, while Corning provides the specialized glass and fiber optics that enable communication and consumer electronics. Both companies serve as critical infrastructure providers for the global economy.

Eaton operates as an intelligent power management company, serving diverse markets including electrical, aerospace, and vehicle systems. In its latest annual report, filed for 2025, the company highlighted its focus on electrification and digitalization through segments like Electrical Americas and Aerospace. Note that customer concentration adds a layer of risk, as nearly 22% of Electrical sector sales came from six large customers and roughly 37% of Vehicle segment sales were generated by four large original equipment manufacturers.

For many industrial stocks, revenue growth serves as a primary indicator of market demand. In FY 2025, revenue reached roughly $27.5 billion, which represented a growth of approximately 10.3% compared to the previous year. This growth helped the company achieve a net income of around $4.1 billion, showcasing a net margin of close to 14.9%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company maintained a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.6x, which measures total debt relative to shareholder equity. Its current ratio stood at roughly 1.3x, assessing its ability to cover short-term liabilities with short-term assets. During FY 2025, it generated approximately $3.6 billion in free cash flow, representing the cash remaining after paying for operations and capital expenditures.

Corning is a leader in materials science, producing specialized glass and ceramic technologies for mobile electronics, optical communications, and automotive markets. As noted in its latest annual report for FY 2025, the company collaborates with major technology firms like Apple Inc. (AAPL -0.25%) for advanced cover materials. However, its Display segment saw nearly 59% of sales from just three customers, while the Automotive segment derived approximately 61% of its sales from three customers.

In FY 2025, the company reported revenue of about $16.4 billion, marking a significant increase of roughly 13% year-over-year. This performance resulted in a net income of approximately $1.6 billion, compared to $506 million in the prior year. Its net margin for the period reached nearly 10.2%, reflecting the impact of its diverse product portfolio.

The balance sheet dated December 2025 shows a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.9x, representing total debt relative to shareholder equity. A current ratio of nearly 1.6x suggests a healthy ability to meet short-term financial obligations. Free cash flow for FY 2025 reached approximately $1.4 billion, which is the cash available after the company accounts for its capital expenditures.

Eaton faces operational risks associated with its strategy of making frequent acquisitions, such as the recent purchase of Fibrebond. The company must also navigate a global supply chain that is vulnerable to geopolitical instability and inflationary pressures on raw materials. Furthermore, the planned spin-off of its Mobility business may divert management attention and present execution challenges.

Corning relies on a highly concentrated customer base, where losing a single major relationship could significantly impact its total sales. The company is also exposed to supply chain risks because it often relies on single-sourced raw materials and specialized equipment. Additionally, Corning faces competition from global rivals such as Nippon Electric Glass, Sumitomo (SSUMF -5.36%), and Fujikura.

Eaton currently trades at a lower Forward P/E and P/S ratio than Corning, suggesting a more conservative entry point based on future earnings estimates and sales over the past twelve months. The Forward P/E measures the stock price against future earnings estimates, while the P/S ratio compares market value to sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

Recently struck deals with Meta Platforms (META -1.20%), Nvidia Corp. (NVDA -0.44%), and Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN +0.05%) with its AWS division has Corning management believing it can execute on its so-called Springboard program of jumping annual sales to $20 billion this year, $30 billion in 2028, and $40 billion in 2030. Each of the deals is in Corning's core area of expertise: fiber optics and connectivity. These are for AI data centers and new manufacturing plants being planned in the U.S. Much of the new capacity Corning needs to fulfill these deals will come from its own U.S. expansion, reducing its tariff and general geopolitical risk. In its fiscal 2026 third quarter, sales grew 19%, and net income rose about 60% to $660 million.

One of the keys to Corning's growth is sharing its development plans with major customers well in advance, allowing it and its partners to tweak plans and development as needed to better meet future needs. The addition of new AI customers also helps reduce its risk of overreliance on Apple, which makes the iPhone glass, among other products.

Turning to Eaton, it's a blue chip industrial company that is benefiting from megatrends, including energy transition, electrification, digitalization, and infrastructure spending. The company has been experiencing strong orders and record backlogs, which has led investors to believe top-line sales and profits should steadily increase. In particular, data centers have been a high-growth area for Eaton, with orders up more than threefold in 2026 and an order backlog extending 12 years. Management also anticipates strong growth in the aerospace and utility end markets.

In its third quarter, reported in early November, Eaton saw sales rise 28% to $8.9 billion, with net income up about 30% to $1.34 billion. Eaton is expected to finish 2026 with sales up more than 20% over 2025 and improved net income. For 2027, Wall Street analysts expect sales and net income to rise about 12%.

So which is the better buy for 2027? Corning's continual improvement of its fiber-optic and connectivity products has led to impressive growth, with much more on the way. Eaton, too, is executing well on its position in the electrification trend. Still, while Corning comes at a premium, its plans to more than double sales by decade's end seem to offer great promise for long-term investors.
