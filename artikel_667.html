‘Project 11,’ Shot by 11 Directors in 11 Global Cities, Returns on Oh Short Flips (EXCLUSIVE)

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

“Project 11,” a 2011 survival thriller made by 11 directors working remotely from 11 cities, will be re-released Nov. 26 as the first title under Oh Short Flips‘ new Classic Shorts vertical.

The short pits 11 gamers in 11 cities against one another. Each has 11 minutes to live, and only the final survivor wins. Made years before online phenomena such as the Blue Whale Challenge caused alarm worldwide, the film dealt with fears around deadly real-world gaming dares. Mumbai-based filmmaker Vikas Chandra drove the project, spending months finding 11 directors willing to take part. Their combined crews numbered around 100 people, spread across New York, Los Angeles, Toronto, London, Luxembourg, Tel Aviv, Dar es Salaam, Balochistan, Mumbai, Beijing and Tokyo.

The directors could not meet in person. All pre-production and storyboarding happened online. To make actors on different continents look as though they shared a frame, the filmmakers matched eyelines, faked over-the-shoulder setups and found lookalike locations in cities such as London and New York, then tied the footage together through lighting, lens choices and choreography.

The film went from script to finished cut in 149 days. A first version running 25 minutes was trimmed to 11 minutes and 11 seconds.

Alongside Chandra (Mumbai), the directors are Alessio Giorgetti (New York), Ali Ahmed Brohi (Balochistan), Dharamveer Singh Chauhan (Luxembourg), Ikematsu Hiro (Tokyo), Joseph Cohen (Tel Aviv), Nkumi Mtingwa (Dar es Salaam), Rafal Rakoczy (London), Ryan Singh (Toronto), Sydney Walsh (Los Angeles) and Yi Luo (Beijing).

“Long before the AI times, we made ‘Project 11’ with pure HumInt (Human Intelligence) – one part madness, one part ingenuity and a lot of passion, just like the write-ups called it back then,” Chandra said. “Eleven filmmakers from eleven cities who had mostly never met, connecting over late-night Skype calls, putting serious faith in strangers, and somehow scrambling crew, locations and equipment on a shoestring to pull off one crazy lab experiment in filmmaking.”

Classic Shorts will bring back well-regarded short films from earlier years that are now hard to come by, aimed at today’s online audiences.

Producer Ranjan Singh, founder of Oh Short Flips, said six months of releasing shorts on the company’s channel had shown that well-made stories always find an audience. “And we know there are so many classic shorts that are good but not available anywhere for the audience. When we did some inquiries, we realized so many filmmakers would like to release their earlier shorts for the audience to watch. Thus, we’ve decided to release these Classic Shorts also on Oh Short Flips.”

Singh added that the team made the film on a basic understanding of collaboration. “Even back then when social media wasn’t that popular we found these directors who wanted to tell a story together. Vikas led from the front and ensured the entire process was smooth and coherent. I am extremely proud that we’re starting our Classic Shorts section with ‘Project 11.'”

Chandra said that, looking back, the film was probably a little ahead of its moment. “It feels good that the film is getting another life now, on a channel dedicated to shorts.”

The news was unveiled on the sidelines of the WAVES Film Bazaar, the market arm of the International Film Festival of India in Goa.
