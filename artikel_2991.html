Anthropic vs. SpaceX: How 2026's Two Biggest IPOs Stack Up.

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

One company is the clear "winner," but its investors may not be...

By John Bromels – Nov 19, 2026 at 2:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

For months, one question has dominated coverage of the Anthropic (ANT +15.56%) IPO. No, not "How can I buy shares?" or "When will it start trading?"

The big question has been, "Will its debut be bigger than SpaceX's (SPCX -4.82%)?"

Well, now that shares have begun trading, we can give a definitive answer. And that answer is, "It depends on what you mean by 'bigger.'"

Here's how the Anthropic and SpaceX IPOs stacked up, which company came out on top by each metric, and -- most importantly -- which stock's shareholders should be happiest (for now).

Before this year, Saudi Aramco held the record for the largest IPO in stock market history (measured by the amount of money raised through the sale of IPO shares, not by the company’s total market capitalization at its IPO price). It raised $25.6 billion in its Dec. 2019 IPO.

Of course, Saudi Aramco isn't traded on U.S. exchanges, so the biggest U.S. IPO was still Alibaba Group’s (BABA -0.57%) in Sept. 2014, which raised $21.8 billion.

SpaceX shattered both records in June, offering 555.6 million shares at an IPO price of $135 each, for a total IPO of $75 billion. Until Anthropic priced its shares on Nov. 18, it wasn't clear whether the company would take the crown from SpaceX or fall just short.

Anthropic ended up the winner by pricing its IPO shares at $83/share. That's higher than the $70-$78/share range it set at the start of its roadshow, but much lower than its debut trading price (more on that later). At that price, it raised $81.3 billion, a full $6.3 billion ahead of SpaceX, and is -- for now, at least -- the biggest IPO in history.

Anthropic's IPO shares were priced at $83/share, but that was only the price for investors who were lucky enough to get in on the IPO. Everyone else needed to wait for shares to begin trading on the open market, which they did at 11:51 a.m., at $111.50/share. That's an impressive 34.3% premium to the IPO price.

Although SpaceX's per-share IPO price was higher than Anthropic's at $135/share, its shares debuted on the NASDAQ at $150/share, just an 11.1% premium to the IPO price. That means Anthropic IPO investors did better... at least as of the moment their shares started trading.

With about 13.2 billion Class A and Class N shares outstanding, Anthropic's $111.50 debut price values those shares alone at $1.5 trillion, but the company's fully diluted market cap at that price -- including unexercised stock options, restricted stock units, and convertible notes -- is roughly $2 trillion.

SpaceX's debut market cap was about the same, at roughly $1.95 trillion, but the inherent difficulty of calculating full dilution means the two companies are roughly tied by this metric.

Thus far, Anthropic has dominated SpaceX by most metrics, but here's where it may notch its first loss.

Of course, the trading day isn't over yet, but Anthropic's shares stumbled out of the gate. They rose only slightly in the first few moments of trading, to $114.99/share, and then quickly dropped below $100/share.

As of 1:30 p.m., less than two hours after their debut, Anthropic's shares are trading at about $95/share. That's still about 14% above their IPO price... but it's 15% below their market debut price.

SpaceX, meanwhile, saw its shares rise significantly above their debut price during their first two hours of trading in June. They reached an intraday high of $176.52/share (a 17.7% gain from their debut) at 1:10 p.m. They eventually closed at $160.95/share, but they never dropped below their debut price during the day (and in fact continued to move higher over the next few days).

Of course, the trading day isn't over yet, so Anthropic shares could still stage a late rally and pull out a win. But at least for now, on the metric that matters most to retail investors without IPO access, SpaceX's IPO is the winner.
