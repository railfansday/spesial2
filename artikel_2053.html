Rico Lewis has the chance to save his Man City career against Fulham this weekend as Enzo Maresca deals with several injury blows.

Man City are back in action after the international break on home turf as they welcome Fulham to the Etihad Stadium on Saturday.

Enzo Maresca's side are in desperate need of a victory, following three straight league defeats that have seen them drop to third place.

Maresca confirmed on Friday that both Matheus Nunes and Abdukodir Khusanov are likely to be sidelined for the visit of the Cottagers.

Lewis, therefore, should be handed a rare Premier League start at right-back amid doubts over his future.

The 21-year-old needs a big performance in front of their home fans to prove that he deserves more game time going forward.

Lewis' future at the club was up in the air over the summer as Nottingham Forest registered their interest in his services.

City made the decision to keep hold of him for this season, with a contract that runs until 2030, but he has continued to struggle for minutes under Maresca.

He has made just two top-flight appearances so far in 2026-27, with four further outings in European and cup games.

Nunes and Khusanov have each featured at right-back ahead of the England youth international, but neither could be available on Saturday.

Speaking to the press on Friday, Maresca said: "From the Forest game, we have still Matheus and Khusa that are still doubts for tomorrow because they are still with some problems. We will see in the next hours."

Lewis is primed to make his first league start since 23 August as their only other recognised right-back option.

Sources revealed to Football Insider last month that Premier League clubs are keeping tabs on Lewis and he could push to leave City in January if his situation does not improve.

The Fulham clash may present him with the opportunity he so desperately craves, with a strong performance likely to push Lewis further into Maresca's thoughts going forward.

Lewis recently admitted in an interview that he would prefer to break through in midfield at Man City, but simply wants to play as many games as he can in the next 12 months after a tough period.

His Premier League stats from last season point to someone who is ready to be a real key player for his side from full-back.

He ranked among the best in the position in the top flight for expected assists (0.31), chances created (1.57), line-breaking passes (14.3) and accurate long balls (2.01) per 90 minutes.

He is clearly a strong creative threat, but was also among the top performers for interceptions (1.57), possession won in the final third (1.12) and distance covered (11.9km) per 90 minutes.
