West Ham United have made the cut-throat decision to sack Nuno Espirito Santo just 13 games in the new Championship season.

Have the Hammers made the right decision? Well, only time will tell, with Scott Parker set to step into the role at the London Stadium.

Nuno leaves the team sitting eighth in the Championship table, six points off first-place Middlesbrough, and four points off automatic promotion.

On paper, that doesn't seem like the worst position to be in at all. But if you've looked at the performances at all, you'll know why there was so much anger.

West Ham may have scored the most goals in the division so far, but they're also leaking chances at the back, conceding six goals in two games against Southampton and West Brom.

The last two defeats have been rather damning. Sacking Nuno is undoubtedly the right decision, but clearly, not everyone agrees.

In his reaction to the news, Simon Jordan spoke about the Hammers' current situation, all while admitting that he hasn't watched much of them this season.

And that's where the problem lies - a pundit commenting on a situation that he has absolutely no clue about.

"They're eighth in the league, six points off top spot, which is not a catastrophe. It's not ideal for many sides," he said on talkSPORT.

"But how did Ipswich go last season? Were they setting the world alight when they first got down to the Championship? I don't think they were.

"I haven't watched West Ham enough this season to know if they're playing good football or not. But being six points off top spot, 13 games into a new season, doesn't seem like a crisis."

West Ham cannot afford to mess up this season, and Nuno's team have already done that multiple times in the early stages.

It might not yet be a so-called "crisis" but it was heading in that direction under the recently sacked manager.

Parker was just one of the options under consideration by the Irons, with Rob Edwards, Sean Dyche, Thomas Frank and Craig Bellamy all linked.

Speaking to Football Insider, former scout for Everton, Aston Villa and Tottenham Hotspur, Bryan King labelled it a "very sensible" move.

But is that what the Hammers need? I suppose it is. They need someone who can ensure automatic promotion, not someone glossy and sparkly.

West Ham can even decide to sack him for someone more high-profile after promotion, but that's quite some way away right now.
