How Liverpool finally unlocked Alexander Isak to solve big problem

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

As Liverpool prepare to make their shortest journey of the season, Alexander Isak has come a long way in a few months. The fact he is leaving Anfield, even if it is only about two-and-a-half miles to Hill Dickinson Stadium, for Sunday's Merseyside derby against Everton may bode well for an increasingly impressive traveller – the misfit who has become a mainstay.

Since his unhappy return to Newcastle in the season opener, when Isak was barracked by the supporters who used to adore him, the Swedish striker has played in six away games and scored in each; indeed, of his 10 goals under Andoni Iraola, only two have come at Anfield.

There may be some significance in the identity of the last Liverpool player to score in six straight away matches: Mohamed Salah, and if Isak is not a direct replacement for a right winger in one respect, he has been charged with taking on the Egyptian's mantle as Liverpool's scorer in chief.

Alexander Isak has been tasked with replacing Mohamed Salah at Liverpool (PA)

Even in Isak's decidedly difficult debut year at Anfield, Liverpool may note two-thirds of his meagre tally of three league goals came away. Since Iraola took the reins, however, Isak has become his king of the road. The head coach admitted he is unsure why.

"I don't have any clear reason, honestly," he said. "I think the way we play is the same whether away or at home. We know we have to go for the games either way. So I think it is just a coincidence. I don't think there is a big difference in performance away or at home."

There may, however, be a difference in the context, when opponents open up and allow a side with Liverpool's counter-attacking pace – wingers as speedy as Bradley Barcola, Victor Munoz and Rio Ngumoha, and a striker as quick as Isak – to break.

Isak and Bradley Barcola are part of Liverpool's lightning-fast counter-attacking unit (Getty)

"Normally you will find more spaces when you play away because teams are normally more brave and they usually press you more," said Iraola. "Then you will find more spaces." He has always caveated his comments about Liverpool's excellent away form this season with reminders that he prefers to play at home.

Does Isak? Much as St James' Park loved him, explaining the sense of betrayal when he went on strike to force his exit, he was almost as potent away. In his first season at Newcastle, 2022-23, he scored five times at home, five away. In the following year, there were 15 goals on Tyneside, 10 elsewhere. In his final campaign, he scored 14 times at home, 12 away and a Carabao Cup final goal against Liverpool at neutral Wembley.

In 2024, he reached double figures for the campaign on Boxing Day. The following year, he got there on 21 December. Isak has reached 10 in November now. Viewed that way, he is on course to beat his personal best of 27 goals in a campaign. Not that Iraola has set any targets.

Isak could be on course for his greatest goalscoring season (PA)

And he's enjoying a purple patch, on the road especially (PA)

"I know how No 9s are, they are very focused on scoring goals," he said. "But not from my side. It is not going to be the main thing. I will value him and rate him not because of his goals probably. I will focus in other areas which I think need improving and I think are very good for the team."

That can be the Iraola way: to look at the collective. Florian Wirtz has found goalscoring form of late, too, but his manager insisted: "For me, it is not just about Florian or Alex, it is about if we play well, someone will score a goal, someone will give the assist, someone will make the numbers."

At the moment, two players are and it's the two signed for big numbers – the £125m Isak and the £116m Wirtz. It took time, amid the centre-forward's injury-hit first year, but a connection is being forged. The German has found the Swede with a pass 29 times in the Premier League this season, and a further 10 in Wednesday's Champions League win against Club Brugge.

Both Isak and Florian Wirtz are big-money signings who are finally clicking at Liverpool (Getty)

That said, the assists for Isak have tended to come from others: four from Cody Gakpo, two from Barcola, even one from goalkeeper Alisson against Brugge. If that has a rarity value, he nevertheless has a fine supply line, while Iraola has channelled the striker's speed. Isak has made the most runs in behind opposition defences this season; one led to Leny Yoro's red card for Manchester United last week, and Dominik Szoboszlai's opening goal from the resulting free-kick.

Perhaps it is typical that much of Iraola's emphasis has been on regaining possession. Isak leads the line, and also the press.

"He has definitely improved a lot on the ball and without the ball," said his manager. "He is understanding better the press and the better he understands the press the less he has to run also. Also, on the ball we are using him much better in possession, he is linking up play very well. Technically he is a very nice player."

That technique has been apparent in the quality of some of Isak's goals. But the most important element – if not for Iraola – may be the quantity, with 10 so far, and eight on the road.
