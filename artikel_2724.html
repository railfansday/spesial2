Vintage Ansonia defense, Taylor's big day lead Chargers past Naugatuck for share of NVL title

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

NAUGATUCK - Just in case anyone had forgotten how dominant the Ansonia football program can be, the Chargers issued a reminder Thursday at Naugatuck's Craig Peters Field.

Playing with tremendous physicality and aggression on defense and leaning heavily on a supremely talented running back offensively, the Chargers earned a share of the Naugatuck Valley League championship by beating Naugatuck 32-0.

Tymir Taylor rushed for 261 yards on 33 carries, including touchdown runs of 1, 2, 19 and 55 yards to give Ansonia a ton of momentum heading into Tuesday's state playoffs.

"It means a lot, beating an undefeated team that beat a team (Holy Cross) that beat us," Taylor said. "It definitely means a lot to come here and put those points on the board like we did today. We're looking really good right now, just getting ready for our first opponent."

Ansonia improved to 9-1 and will host a Class SS quarterfinal Tuesday, Dec. 1. Naugatuck also finished 9-1 and will host a Class L quarterfinal. Their opponents will be determined when the CIAC releases the official seeds and pairings Friday.

Ansonia coach Shane Kingsley said he felt like a great performance was coming from his team from the moment the Chargers stepped onto the field on Thursday morning.

"I felt it from the kids in warmups," Kingsley said. "They had that Ansonia look about them. We told our offensive line they were going to have to control the line of scrimmage. We played Ansonia football today. We ran the ball, ran to the football aggressively on defense and tackled, and the results were what we wanted."

The result of such focused play was that the outcome of the game never seemed in doubt from the time Taylor scored his first touchdown. The Ansonia defense was unyielding, and the Chargers' offensive line gave Taylor all that he needed to make big plays.

"I think Tymir Taylor has become a complete back this year," Kingsley said. "Last year he might've gotten caught on a few of those runs, but not this year. He's breaking tackles at the line of scrimmage, and when he gets daylight, he's gone. He's worked really hard, and I'm happy he's seeing the results."

The Ansonia defense complemented him perfectly, shutting down the very talented Naugy duo of running back/QB Qwentarvous Lee and running back Brandon Andoh. Lee finished with 56 yards rushing and Andoh with 50.

"From the moment we came in for the pregame walkthru, we were locked in and ready to go," defensive lineman Zaire Moore said. "It was definitely the aggression and the mindset, and the things they'd been talking about over the internet. It was crazy. We just had to show them who rules."

Trailing 16-0 with 2:12 to play in the first half, Naugatuck appeared like it was going to close that gap as the Greyhounds drove from their own 32-yard line to the Ansonia 1-yard line, but time ran out in the half before they could get the ball into the end zone.

An interference call against Ansonia put the ball at the 5-yard line. A Naugatuck handoff to Brandon Andoh was stopped at the 1. The Greyhounds rushed to the line of scrimmage and got the snap off just before time expired, but Lee was stopped for no gain and time ran out.

"That gave us the momentum going into the second half," Moore said. "It was like an energy ball in the locker room. Everybody was hyped. It helped us come right out and score again."

In just six plays, Ansonia drove 80 yards, with Taylor ripping off a 50-yard run and later scoring on a 19-yard touchdown run to make it 24-0 on Ethan Blackwell's third two-point conversion run of the game.

Ansonia's defense, led by linemen Moore, Ishmael Smith and Josh Parks with plenty of help from linebackers Antonio Pinto and Kevin Godfrey, was dominant for most of the game.

Each team had a huge run negated on its third play from scrimmage. On Naugatuck's third play, Qwentarvous Lee broke off a 65-yard run for an apparent touchdown, but the play was called back for a holding penalty. The Greyhounds ended up punting on the possession.

On Ansonia's ensuing possession, Taylor had a 45-yard run called back on the Chargers' first possession, leading to a punt.

Naugatuck also went three-and-out on its second possession, and the field position tilted in Ansonia's favor.

The Chargers took possession at their own 49-yard line and nine plays later - eight of them runs by Taylor - Taylor scored on a 2-yard touchdown run. Ansonia went for a 2-point conversion, with Ethan Blackwell scoring for an 8-0 lead.

They extended that lead to 16-0 in much more efficient fashion on a one-play drive with 2:12 left in the second quarter as Ansonia scored on a 55-yard run by Taylor.

Taylor's final touchdown of the game came with 2:47 to play to cap off another 80-yard drive.

With 4:49 to play in the first quarter, Naugatuck star offensive lineman Isaiah Portalatin was injured badly enough that the game was stopped. A cart came out first, followed by a stretcher and ambulance personnel. He raised his left arm as he was rolled off the field on the stretcher with an apparent knee injury, letting the crowd know he was fine.

Portalatin has been a crucial part of the Greyhounds' potent running game all season. He has also played a very big role on the Naugatuck defensive line this year.

Naugatuck also lost two other key players, two-way standout Josiah Loyd and tight end Josh Thom, to injuries during the game.

Tymir Taylor, Ansonia: The junior running back scored on touchdown runs of 1, 2, 55 and 19 yards and finished with 261 yards on 33 carries.

"His attitude is what really changes the game for this team. Every play, I know what I'm going to get out of him. Early on, things weren't hitting the way we wanted, but I know every play he's going to run hard, do his job and be a great teammate. Today, his talent took over in the second half, and I'm just proud of him."- Ansonia coach Shane Kingsley on junior running back Tymir Taylor.
