Curt Cignetti's criticism isn't the problem. College football's gatekeepers are | Opinion

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Indiana coach Curt Cignetti forgot the cardinal rule of college football.

Cignetti drew immediate rebuke for his shots at the College Football Playoff rankings, and the veiled criticism of the Southeastern Conference. Never mind that Cignetti's rants and tweets are no different than what coaches have been doing since their sports began. Or that he wouldn't be doing his job if he wasn't defending his team.

But the SEC is college football's sacred cow, and Cignetti best not forget it.

"He seems like grandpa who has a phone in his hand for the first time, and he's just playing with all the emojis," Paul Finebaum said on ESPN's "Get Up!" on Tuesday, Nov. 17. "He doesn't really make any sense. … This team should really have four losses right now, and they played a couple of high school teams in the preseason.

"So coach Cignetti, you're a great coach, you have a national championship, quit complaining and being grandpa. Quit being 'get off my lawn' right now because you have a chance, you may be the Big Ten's only chance to get to the national championship game."

Aside from the irony of the 71-year-old Finebaum trying to shade Cignetti, who is six years younger, as being old and out of touch, the well-known SEC apologist's outrage is off the mark.

Yes, the Hoosiers played some bunnies in the preseason with games against North Texas, Howard and Western Kentucky. You know who else did? Pretty much every other big-time team in college football!

Georgia, the top seed in last week's CFP rankings, played Western Kentucky, as well as Tennessee State, and its in-state rivalry game against Georgia Tech isn't going to be a barn burner, either.

Alabama, which is at No. 3 and ahead of unbeaten UCLA in the CFP rankings despite having two losses, opened with East Carolina. It also played Florida State, which has been so bad recently it didn't even bother waiting until the end of the season to fire Mike Norvell, and has Chattanooga, a mediocre FCS team, still to come.

As for Finebaum and Heather Dinich's other criticism, about the games Indiana trailed and the losses they *should* have, you can woulda, coulda, shoulda anyone's schedule. Texas was trailing LSU by 11 points in the third quarter Saturday, Nov. 14, but you can be darn sure no one's going to give the Longhorns side eye for coming back and winning that game.

The point is to win games, which is what Indiana has done.

"It's all gonna play out on the field," Cignetti said. "You know, it's good for entertainment. Sure, this will get a lot of nice hits and play, but our guys understand that it'll play out on the field."

And therein lies the biggest problem with the umbrage at Cignetti.

The SEC and its backers long ago anointed the conference as the kings of college football, with everyone else merely a poor imitation. "It just means more," is even the SEC's slogan, implying college football isn't as important elsewhere.

Yet it's the Big Ten that's won the past three CFP titles, with Cignetti's Hoosiers capping their unbeaten season last year with the national championship. Heck, the SEC hasn't even had a team in the CFP championship game since Georgia won it in 2023!

This is not to dismiss the SEC's outsized accomplishments. They've won half the titles since the CFP began in 2014, and trail only the Big Ten with 27 national titles overall. They routinely have the most players taken in the NFL Draft.

Nor is it meant to diminish the SEC teams this year. Alabama will be a tough out no matter where it is in the rankings, and no one should be surprised if Georgia is the last team standing.

But not everyone lives and dies by the SEC. In fact, there's an entire country outside SEC territory that cares just as much about college football and has some pretty good teams of its own. Cignetti's included.

Cignetti was simply advocating for his team. That it caused such an uproar only shows he has a point.
