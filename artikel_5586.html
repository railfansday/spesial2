Does your brokerage need a mortgage servicing arm to keep past clients engaged?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Mortgage servicing became a key ancillary service in 2026, but many regional brokerages cannot own a servicer. To maintain mindshare, firms used practical homeowner value adds such as automated valuation tools, seasonal maintenance reminders, home transition handbooks, and education first content.  AI Summary

In 2026, mortgage servicing became one of the real estate brokerage industry’s must have ancillary services.

For Peter Voss, the broker-owner of 3% Listing Co.,  Rocket Companies’ acquisitions of Redfin and Mr.Cooper in mid-2025 was a real wake up call as to what true vertical integration looks like.

“That was like the blinders coming off for everyone,” Voss said. “It was like ‘Holy cow! That is real vertical integration.’ It was just so different from what everyone else had been thinking about.”

This move by Rocket was followed in 2026 by eXp Realty’s mortgage joint venture with Newrez, Revenos Mortgage, which includes a servicing component, and HomeServices of America adding in-house mortgage servicing through its wholly owned lender, Prosperity Home Mortgage, LLC.

At the time of the launch, Chris Kelly, the CEO of the RealTrends Verified HomeServices of America, told HousingWire that the company decided to venture into servicing because they were “increasingly seeing that loans end up with a servicer and those servicers are moving further upstream.”

“It used to be that a broker started a mortgage company, but now mortgage companies are starting real estate companies and the whole point of it is to use that servicing to try and siphon off that business and acquire home buyer and seller clients before they even start browsing for homes on a search portal,” Kelly told HousingWire in late summer.

But for smaller, regional brokerages owning a mortgage servicer is not an option. However, this doesn’t mean that they have given up in their efforts to stay top of mind with past clients.

Instead of venturing into mortgage servicing, Voss said 3% Listing Co. has employed a variety of strategies to ensure his company is the one past clients turn to when it is time to move, from a home value tool that allows homeowners to see the estimated value of their property without having to talk to an agent to reminders about seasonal home maintenance.

“We just sent out an email ahead of the winter season reminding people to unhook their outdoor hoses,” Voss said. “It’s really small things like that, trying to be real and making sure what we are doing is beneficial to past clients.”

Voss said his firm is also exploring a partnership with an HVAC filter company that sends homeowners new filters every three months when it’s time to swap out filters.

“We’re looking at the possibility of setting it up as a closing gift for clients where every three months they get a filter in the mail that is branded to us,” he said. “We want to think outside of the box because just because everyone else is doing things one way doesn’t mean we have to.”

Home Handoff is a proptech company focused on helping agents and their clients manage their home transition, provides home sellers with a structured space to provide information regarding property utilities, renovations and service providers that can then be shared with buyers or prospective buyers. The firm is all about finding different ways for agents to keep their name in front of past clients. The company is working with agents, teams and brokerages, to put their own branding on the “home handbook” the company provides clients with. In addition to information regarding the property, agents can also provide links to vetted service professionals such as contractors, landscapers and pet sitters.

“The whole platform is built to keep the agent as the hero. Every time the seller or buyer logs into the platform they see it is provided by their agent or their brokerage,” Cody Bartkoski, the CEO of Home Handoff, said.

In New York City, Brown Harris Stevens (BHS), a RealTrends Verified brokerage, is also employing different means of maintaining relationships with clients. The firm, which does not own any ancillary services operations, instead operates an advertising arm that works with trusted mortgage and title professionals to create content from podcasts, to blog posts to short video series.

According to Matt Leone, BHS’s chief marketing officer, the primary goal of this marketing and advertising program is to educate viewers and listeners and that they are not creating content just for the sake of “generating clicks.” He added that they don’t necessarily view these efforts as a lead generation tool, but as a way to get their “name out there as a thought leader.”

“You don’t want it to be a traditional ad message,” Leone said. “You want it to be through your thought leadership, your insights and being seen as an expert that you earn these opportunities.”

Leone added that consumers encounter so many different advertisements each day that many just tune out that type of content.

“We want to educate them, both consumers and agents, with the individuals in those spaces that are the most knowledgeable,” Leone said. “We work with our agents to find the providers that are the best at their craft and we see if they’d be interested in collaborating with us.”

While regional firms like 3% Listing Co. and BHS may not have mortgage servicers or even a stable of ancillary services within their company, they are still finding meaningful ways to engage with and keep their names in front of consumers.

“It isn’t just about handing out branded T-shirts to people, but about how you connect with them and give the T-shirt value so that people are going to reuse it,” Voss said. “I think that is where a lot of brokerages are struggling. They’re trying to find that additional income stream and where that additional lead source is, but without a whole lot of added cost.”
