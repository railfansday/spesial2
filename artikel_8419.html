Investing - Theory, News &amp; General • Re: Ten year projection of muted equity returns

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Why on earth would I do anything based on these forecasts.

In another thread started by user babystep they wrote:

They didn’t miss by a smidge. They missed by a ton. Making investment decisions based in these types of forecasts would be very costly.

I think Warren Buffet said “Market forecasts tell you more about the forecasters than they do about the future”
Out of curiosity why do you say they “missed by a ton”?
A median forecast means that they thought there was a 50% chance the outcome would be more than 6.5%, or for example 50% of their simulations had a value above 6.5%.

that’s a pretty broad range that covers everything from 6.5% to 100% return, so a realized value of 14.7% seems like it was likely within the range of their 50th-90th percentile I imagine
So what are we saying here? That we’d need to see annualized returns north of 23% before we’re willing to agree Vanguard missed bigly? At what point does a forecast become so broadly qualified that it becomes practically unfalsifiable?

I get the statistical argument. I just don’t see why that makes the forecast useful. If the whole point was to warn investors that returns would be muted, and we subsequently got 14.7% annualized, I think it’s fair to say the forecast was a pretty poor guide to what actually happened.

And that was my bigger point. These forecasts aren’t worth reading or thinking about. Whether they are “big wrong” or “technically correct”, they are not useful.
