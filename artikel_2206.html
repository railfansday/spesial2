Hibs manager on why his 'ice king' persona cracked and how his side can beat Rangers

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Marink Reedijk tonight admitted that seeing Hibernian fans singing Sunshine on Leith inside Hampden following their Premier Sports Cup semi-final win over Hearts had caused his “ice king” public persona to crack.

And Reedijk, whose team came from behind this afternoon and won thanks to Martin Boyle and Jamie McGrath goals, predicted the Easter Road club can break Rangers’ hearts in the final if they perform as well again.

The Dutchman, who took over from David Gray back in September, has endured a difficult start to his tenure in Scotland and was still searching his first win going into the last four encounter.

But the bottom-placed side in the William Hill Premiership deserved to record their first win ever over their Edinburgh rivals in Mount Florida and their manager was pleased their supporters were able to celebrate at long last.

Asked about their rendition of Sunshine on Leith following the final whistle, he said, “I'm not always showing my emotions. Maybe it looks like I'm an ice king. But it was really touching. For me, it is really important that this happens.

“It comes close to my heart because this is what football is about - guys who inspire people on the pitch with something that people off the pitch and in the stands love to see. That is what football is about.

“In the first half, I really enjoyed how we played football, how we dominated the game on the ball, how we made sure that we created chances, how we gave Hearts a big problem in defence.

“In the second half, I really enjoyed the togetherness, the work rate, the energy, the desire. That's so important because in a match you will dominate maybe some part of the game and then the rest is survival.

“Especially in these matches where it's sometimes electric, this can happen. This is where we made a big step today. I'm so happy for the staff, the players, because they worked so hard for this.

“Also for the fans who kept on supporting us, not only during the match, but those matches where we didn't have the result like we did today. That's really important to me. That's what togetherness means to me.”

Rangers, who beat Aberdeen 1-0 in the first Premier Sports Cup semi-final on Saturday night, will be strong favourites to win the final when Hibs return to Hampden on Sunday, December 13.

Asked why he believes his side can lift their first trophy since the Scottish Cup in 2016 and their first League Cup since 2007, Reedijk said, “The performance of today and the performance we showed the week before. If we have more progression, we progress even better and better and better.

“We can make every opponent's lives a little bit difficult or very difficult. No matter which team it is, it's always another match to play. Finals are special matches, but finals are not only decided on these special moments.

“It's also about teams with a plan and teams with an idea. I think it will have ups and downs. But I have a really good belief that we also can do something in the final if we build on what we are doing now and keep on going.”

Reedijk confessed that he was happy to win his first Edinburgh derby match and end Hibs’ four game winless run against Hearts at Hampden.

“That's fantastic,” he said. “Maybe it's an extra icing on the cake. That's fantastic to see that this is a sort of history we make. Hopefully we are having more history to make.

“I love these kinds of games. I believe that I have to be away from the emotions as much as possible because at the end of the day I have to make rational decisions, do the logical things.

“If I go into the emotion too much, which is tempting to be fair because it's a lovely atmosphere, then I lose sight of what is my job. This is something I always take into account. Maybe I don't seem emotional, but deep inside there is emotion.”
