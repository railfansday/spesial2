ChatGPT and Claude both sell a seat near $20 in 2026, and they gate completely different things

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Two pricing pages, opened within a minute of each other on 22 November 2026, both pointing at a seat around twenty dollars. OpenAI served me won; Anthropic served me dollars. Once you remove the Korean VAT and convert, ChatGPT Plus is $19.63 a month at 1,343.33 KRW/USD and Claude Pro is $20. That is where the agreement ends.

What follows is the two ladders as printed, then the four places where the $20 seat is not the same purchase: the model gate, the context budget, the annual toggle, and the free tier.

Read the two middle rows together and the shapes are different. Claude jumps $20 → $100 → $200 in steps of 5× and 10× usage. ChatGPT jumps $20 → $107.60 with a different promise: the frontier reasoning model, an always-on agent, image generation without a cap, and a monthly API credit allowance. One ladder sells more of the same thing; the other sells a class of thing you cannot buy below it.

On Claude's side, Pro is the first plan that includes Claude Code — the coding agent — plus the ability to hand off and schedule tasks. The feature table marks both "No" on Free and "Yes" on Pro. It also unlocks Projects and the Microsoft 365 and Chrome integrations.

On ChatGPT's side, Plus is the first plan that includes GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol, the frontier models. The comparison table is explicit: Free and Go are "No" on both; Plus is "Limited"; Pro is "Expanded". Plus also brings projects, scheduled tasks and custom GPTs — though Go already gets scheduled tasks, which is a quiet difference from last year's ladder.

So the same twenty dollars buys a coding agent on one side and access to the frontier chat models on the other. If your work is "an agent that writes and runs code", Claude Pro is the only one of the two $20 seats that includes it. If your work is "the best general model for writing and analysis", ChatGPT Plus is the only one of the two $20 seats that includes the top model at all.

The always-on agent is a third tier on both sides and costs differently: ChatGPT's "Dot" starts at Pro, ₩159,000 (~$107.60), and Claude's scheduling arrives at Pro, $20.

Anthropic's pricing page, read the same day, prints no context figure at any tier. It prints usage as a multiple ("more usage than Free", "5× Pro usage", "10× Pro usage") and leaves the token budget to its documentation. That is not a criticism of the product, but it is a real difference in what you can plan from the pricing page: with ChatGPT you can calculate whether a 200-page document fits on the plan you are looking at. With Claude you cannot, from this page.

Claude Pro is $20 billed monthly or $17 a month with $200 billed up front — a 16.7% discount for committing a year, stated on the card.

ChatGPT's own FAQ says: "We offer monthly plans for Go, Plus and Business and annual plans for Business and Enterprise." There is no annual individual plan to toggle. So the annual lever is worth $40 a year on the Claude side and does not exist on the ChatGPT side at this tier.

At ten seats the lever gets bigger. Claude Team standard seats are $25 monthly or $20 annually, so ten seats run $250 a month billed monthly or $200 a month on the annual commitment — $600 a year apart. For ChatGPT's Business tier I did not read a printed per-seat price on the page that served me, so I am not quoting one; that figure has to come from the Business tab or a sales conversation.

Claude's Free plan is a volume limit: "limited usage every 5 hours and every week", with chat, search, file creation, code running, memory and Artifacts included. You get the tools and a small tank.

ChatGPT's Free plan is a model gate: unlimited text chats with GPT-6 — but "No" on GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6 Sol, with GPT-6 Luna available. You get a large tank of the smaller model.

That difference matters more than the price. Someone testing whether an assistant can handle their week should look at how much they can use it; someone testing whether the frontier model is worth paying for gets nothing from ChatGPT's free tier, because the frontier model is not on it.
