Ex-PGMOL chief makes U-turn on Man United red card as new footage analysed

Manchester United may have reason to complaint after Leny Yoro was sent off just minutes into their defeat against Liverpool.

That is according to former PGMOL chief and ex-FIFA referee Keith Hackett, who exclusively told Football Insider that initially he thought the decision was "clear" and could not be argued with.

However, after reviewing new angles and footage emerging from Anfield, the refereeing expert changed his mind, insisting the decision was more subjective than it seemed.

The resulting free kick was slammed home by Dominik Szoboszlai to put his side ahead early in the game, before Florian Wirtz put the hosts two in front three minutes later.

Man United were then put to the sword in the second half as Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz both found the net to ensure all three points came in a 4-0 win for Liverpool.

Pochettino has a lot of work to do to turn around his side's dismal start to the season, with Man United still trapped inside the Premier League's relegation zone.

Referee Sam Barrott was forced into an early decision at Anfield as he quickly brandished the red card, sending Yoro down the tunnell, and the call was confirmed by VAR.

Some, likely including Pochettino, have since pointed to the fact that Isak's touch had sent the ball well in front of him, and he was unlikely to get a shot off from there.

That has led to questions about the law, which does suggest that VAR must assess whether the attacking player was likely to keep hold of the ball.

However, the final decision was that Yoro had denied Isak a clear goalscoring opportunity and the defender was shown a straight red card.

Barrott has been praised for his quick decision-making in a high-pressure scenario, especially so early in the game, as he immediately reached to his pocket to brandish the card.

However, VAR does have the benefit of seeing immediate replays, and questions have since been asked about whether Barrott should have been sent to the monitor.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Hackett explained: "That was a clear foul by Yoro, just outside his penalty area, which clearly denies a goal-scoring opportunity.

"Referee Barrott rightly, without hesitation, produced the red card out of his left-hand pocket. Clear denial of a goal-scoring opportunity.

"Very good piece of officiating, the referee was in a great position and without hesitation made the correct call. Manchester United can have no complaints."

However, he later made a U-turn, as he explained: "The criteria for DOGSO is as follows: The following must be considered; the distance between the offence and the goal, the general direction of play, and likelihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball.

"When you review the replays, which VAR does, what was the likihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball? That is where there is an element of doubt. A subjective call."

Those doubts have been made clear by Man United fans in the wake of the decision, and by Pochettino on the sidelines, but the decision seemed straightforward in the moment.
