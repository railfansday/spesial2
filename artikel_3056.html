2 Utility Stocks With Reliable Dividends to Hold for the Next 5 Years

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

These two stocks combine dependable growth with long histories of dividend increases.

By James Halley – Nov 22, 2026 at 11:00AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Consolidated Edison ›

If you're looking for dependable dividend growth from stable utilities, Consolidated Edison (ED +0.23%) and Southern Company (SO -0.17%) fit the bill.

ConEd, as it's more commonly known, has raised its dividend for 52 consecutive years, making it a Dividend King, one of the few companies that have raised their dividends for 50 or more consecutive years. ConEd has the longest uninterrupted dividend-growth streak among S&P 500 electric and gas utilities.

Southern Company has increased its dividend for 25 consecutive years and kept it the same or increased it for 79 consecutive years.

Here are three reasons to buy each stock and hold it for five or more years.

In the third quarter, ConEd reported nine-month earnings per share (EPS) of $5.44, up 12.3% year over year. The company, which serves more than 9 million people and 350,000 businesses in New York City and Westchester County, is seeing growth because of electrification needs in the New York area. The company provides electricity and natural gas and is the only steam utility in the United States.

Southern Company is also showing double-digit EPS growth. Through the first nine months, it reported EPS of $4.02, up 13% over the same period last year. It provides electricity and gas to 9 million customers in Georgia, Mississippi, Alabama, Tennessee, Virginia, and Illinois. Southern is seeing a huge uptick in electrification needs for new data centers in the South, with commercial sales rising 52% year over year in the third quarter.

Both companies, compared to competitors, have low debt-to-earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) levels, giving them plenty of room to fund expansion.

Con Edison operates almost entirely as a pure-play regulated delivery utility. New York's regulatory framework includes revenue decoupling, which insulates Con Edison's top line from fluctuations in customer usage or mild weather. Its multiyear capital plan drives steady, predictable rate-base growth.

Southern is experiencing significant growth and has already paid for a lot of its expansion. Because Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4 are in full operation, it has moved past the multiyear construction overhangs and cost-overrun risks that weighed on its balance sheet. Those nuclear units are now generating stable, regulated base-load returns and improving free cash flow generation.

Both dividends yield nearly three times the S&P 500 average of 1.11%. ConEd's dividend yield is 2.98% at its current share price, while Southern Company's is 3.24%.

ConEd's annualized dividend of $3.55 per share is comfortably covered by 2026 adjusted earnings guidance of $6.05-$6.15 per share, keeping its payout ratio around 58%, well below the utility sector average, leaving a wide cushion against economic downturns.

Southern Company projects full-year EPS of $4.70. Its annualized dividend this year is $3.02, so its payout ratio is slightly higher at 64%, but still within the safety range for utilities.

ConEd is the better stock for stability. It has a low beta, and its history of dividend growth is unmatched within the industry. It may also be priced lower at this point, with a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 19.48 to Southern Company's 20.10.

Southern Company, however, has a slightly higher dividend yield and operates in states experiencing more rapid population and industrial growth than New York can expect. In 2026, Southern Company achieved its strongest weather-normalized retail electricity sales growth in nearly two decades (up 2.3% year to date), propelled by a 50% surge in data center electricity usage and more than 17 gigawatts of contracted large-load projects in its pipeline.
