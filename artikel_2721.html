Fernando Mendoza found ‘something special’ in unlocking Raiders rising star

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

The first touchdown pass Fernando Mendoza threw in a Raiders uniform went to Jack Bech. It happened in a preseason game, so there was nothing particularly consequential about it in the grand scheme of things.

As it turns out, it might have been the first glimpse of something that could have lasting ramifications for the Raiders' offense.

A friendship that has blossomed away from the football field is beginning to manifest itself on it. And in the process, Mendoza might be helping unlock a player the Raiders invested a second-round pick in last year but have struggled to consistently incorporate into their offense.

Denver Broncos cornerback Riley Moss (21) tackles Las Vegas Raiders wide receiver Jack Bech (18) during an NFL game in Denver, Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. AP Photo/David Zalubowski

"Jack is a great guy. He's one of my best friends on the team," Mendoza said. "He's someone who's notorious for staying after practice and catching extra balls. He's been a fantastic player and teammate."

There is something to be said about the comfort level a quarterback develops with a receiver he spends time with away from the facility. Especially two young players navigating the early stages of their NFL careers and figuring out where they fit in the grand scheme of an organization.

What is becoming increasingly apparent is that Bech has a connection with Mendoza that he never seemed to establish with any of the other quarterbacks he has played with in Las Vegas.

And the implications of that extend well beyond the individual success of either player.

Consider what happened Sunday in Denver, when Bech caught eight passes on nine targets for 88 yards and a touchdown against one of the NFL's best defenses. Five of those catches produced first downs. Bech accounted for nearly half of Mendoza's 18 completions.

That is a pretty significant development for a young quarterback trying to find his footing in the NFL and a young wide receiver trying to establish his value in it.

And none of it surprised Raiders coach Klint Kubiak.

"Well, I think a quarterback and receiver relationship builds when there's adverse times and the guys are making plays," Kubiak said. "So, those plays that Jack made last night, those are things that he's done in practice with Fernando. He has the trust of the quarterback."

It's important to understand something about Bech as the Raiders continue constructing an offense around Mendoza.

He might never develop into the legitimate No. 1 wide receiver Raiders fans are clamoring for. He might not even fit the traditional description of a No. 2 receiver.

But that doesn't mean he can't be an immensely valuable piece of a championship-caliber offense.

Think of a bigger-bodied, more physical version of Hunter Renfrow in terms of situational value. Someone who understands leverage, finds soft spots in coverage, operates effectively in congested areas and becomes almost instinctively aligned with his quarterback.

The guy Mendoza looks for on third-and-7. The guy he trusts to come down with a contested catch in the end zone. The guy who understands where he needs to be when a play breaks down.

Factor in Bech's special teams contributions and the physicality and willingness he brings as a blocker in the run game, and it's easy to envision him becoming the type of player coaches struggle to take off the field.

Las Vegas Raiders quarterback Fernando Mendoza (15) throws against the Denver Broncos in Denver, Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. AP Photo/Jack Dempsey

Kubiak has consistently pointed out how much you can feel Bech's physicality.

"He's always been gritty, always been a great teammate," Kubiak said. "Then you get opportunities against a really good defense, and he made plays. So I'm happy for him. I'm not surprised."

Great football teams aren't exclusively populated by star football players. They're supplemented by capable, dependable role players who understand precisely what is required of them and consistently deliver.

That distinction sometimes gets lost in our obsession with draft positions, depth charts and statistical production.

If Bech becomes that kind of player, nobody should apologize for the Raiders using a second-round pick on him.

Especially if he develops alongside Mendoza over the next several years, complementing the star power of Brock Bowers and Ashton Jeanty and whatever high-end receiving talent the Raiders eventually add.

Bech, interestingly, deflects attention from his individual relationship with Mendoza to emphasize the broader connection the rookie quarterback has established throughout the locker room.

"Not only has he gotten better on the field from April, but the way he's just taking that leadership role," Bech said. "We all have faith and confidence in him."

Mendoza, meanwhile, traces his connection with Bech back to that preseason touchdown.

"Even though it was a preseason game, it's still a great game, and at that moment I realized that we had something special," Mendoza said.

Las Vegas Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak speaks during a news conference after an NFL football game against the Denver Broncos in Denver, Sunday, Nov. 22, 2026. AP Photo/David Zalubowski

What makes their friendship particularly intriguing is that neither appears inclined to take the football component of it for granted. There are extra passes to throw and catch after practice, nuances to master and a level of trust to continue earning.

The Raiders drafted Mendoza to be the face of their franchise.

If his arrival also helps Bech become one of the foundational pieces surrounding him, that would represent a pretty significant bonus.
