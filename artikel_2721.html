Stony Brook-bound QB leads Ward Melville to Suffolk championship at his future field

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Hudson Philbrick has been a revolutionary figure for the Ward Melville Patriots.

The Stony Brook University-bound quarterback, set to graduate early in January to get a jump with his NCAA squad, doesn't have time for senioritis, but instead is looking to go out in style — and undefeated in his last dance.

"It would just feel amazing. That's all I could say," he told The Post ahead of the Suffolk County championship against Sachem North that will be played Friday night at his future college just four miles away.

Quarterback Hudson Philbrick stands for a portrait at Ward Melville High School in East Setauket, N.Y., on Nov. 18, 2026. Heather Khalifa for NY Post

"Right on the home turf," Philbrick said.

He led the Patriots to their second county title since the 1980s with a 31-28 win over William Floyd last season, then fell narrowly in the Long Island Championship to Massapequa 14-12 a week later.

Getting back to the LIC means everything to the star, who now owns several records for the 10-0 Patriots — and is one end zone toss away from setting a new school single-season passing touchdown record at 25.

"Everybody knows that we're setting our eyes on one particular goal and one challenge that we know that we can overcome," he said.

Philbrick has carried a lot on his shoulder pads since starting varsity as a 10th grader with the long-term goal of playing college football and, hopefully, pro someday.

Quarterback Hudson Philbrick laughs before football practice at Ward Melville High School in East Setauket, N.Y., on Nov. 18, 2026. Heather Khalifa for NY Post

"I wouldn't be doing this if I didn't have my eyes set on the biggest goal possible."

He overloaded his schedule as an underclassman, skipped lunch periods to graduate early, and then tackled hours of homework to maintain an A average while staying dedicated to the gridiron.

"It was a lot of pressure," Philbrick said. "But it's nothing that I was ever scared of doing."

With the larger-than-life expectations, his mom, Sharon, and little sister Oceana came up with a creative way to ease the tension and keep Philbrick loose before his first varsity start as a 10th grader.

"We jumped into the Long Island Sound," he recalled.

Philbrick won 21-14 over Sachem North later that day, so naturally they had to do it again, and again, and again for good luck as a newfound game-day ritual.

Brothers Hampton Philbrick (left) and Hudson Philbrick stand for a portrait at Ward Melville High School in East Setauket, N.Y., on Nov. 18, 2026. Heather Khalifa for NY Post

"It does seem to work," the soon-to-be Sea Wolf said. "All the way from the first game when it's 80 degrees and sunny, all the way until now when it's 40 degrees, and the water is freezing cold."

They even went in during last week's nor'easter, as Philbrick may have taken coach Chris Boltrek's advice that weather should not dictate how a game goes a bit too literally.

"It's brutal, but whatever it takes," said Philbrick, now 6-foot-3 and 205 pounds — 3 inches taller and 35 pounds heavier than during the first polar plunge. "Just having the courage to do something like that helps boost your mindset a little bit before the game."

Senior year is especially touching for Philbrick because of another family football legacy — his ninth-grade little brother, Hampton, a quarterback who was called up to varsity for the playoffs.

"I really think he's gonna be better than me," Hudson said. "I know he'll grow, and he'll get the job done. He'll win a lot, so it feels good."

Hampton took his brother's place in a pair of blowout playoff wins in a moment that felt surreal to the duo, especially after getting advice from his big bro on "never giving up."

"I'm very excited to just continue Philbricks' reign of terror on the rest of Long Island for many years to come," Hampton said confidently.

However, there is one thing Hampton plans on doing differently from Hudson, as the ninth grader is "not into the cold."

"I've never jumped in the Sound before, so I don't know if I'm going to carry it on," Hampton said. "Maybe I'll make a new tradition, like sitting in a hot tub or something."
