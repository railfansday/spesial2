Anthropic Stock Just Broke Below Its $83 IPO Price. Is It a Bargain Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Anthropic fell below its $83 IPO price on its fourth trading day.

By Johnny Rice – Nov 24, 2026 at 2:55PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›

On Thursday, the frontier AI lab Anthropic (ANT -6.54%) made its public debut, with the offering bringing in an incredible $93.5 billion at closing. That makes it the biggest in history -- bigger even than the record set by SpaceX (SPCX -5.65%) in June. With investors scrambling to get shares on day one, the stock opened at $111.50, well above the offer price of $83.

But today, on its fourth trading day as a public company, Anthropic stock is trading below that initial offer, hovering below $80.

So, is this a golden buying opportunity? Here's what you should consider before jumping in.

Before we look at Anthropic itself, it's useful to look at what the historical record says about how stocks perform after an IPO.

University of Florida professor emeritus Jay Ritter -- known as "Mr. IPO" for his research -- looked at more than 9,000 U.S. listings from 1980 through 2024. He found that the average stock gained about 19% on its first day. Investors who got in at the offer price trailed the broader market by 3.3 percentage points over the study's three-year window, while those who bought at the first-day close trailed it by 20.5 points.

OK, but that's a pretty wide data set. A narrower one with companies that are at least somewhat comparable -- admittedly hard when dealing with a company as unique as Anthropic -- will tell us more.

Companies already doing at least $1 billion in annual sales fared better, though they still finished 2.1 points behind the market when measured from the first-day close.

At about $80 a share, Anthropic is worth roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis that includes shares reserved for future issuance. The company's trailing 12-month revenue comes in at $35.3 billion, meaning the stock is trading at about 41 times sales. That is expensive.

Bulls argue that the company is at the very forefront of a technological revolution and its product is so valuable that it will grow into this valuation. It's a fair argument, especially given the pace of that growth -- revenue jumped from $1.4 billion to $17 billion year over year in Q3. That's basically unheard of at this scale.

If we assume last quarter's sales pace holds, you're paying about 21.5 times annualized sales at today's price, or about 16 times based on shares outstanding. That's still expensive -- Nvidia (NVDA -5.86%) trades at about 8 times annualized sales on that same outstanding-share basis -- but more in the realm of reason.

My concern is twofold -- one, spending, and two, moat (or lack thereof).

Anthropic's compute and infrastructure spending ate up 58% of sales in Q3, up from 48% in Q2, and Q2's $974 million operating profit turned back into a loss of $567 million. I'm still not convinced that this business can reliably turn a profit over time.

That's especially true if the company is forced to compete on price with other model providers in order to defend its market share. That's the problem with having, in my view, very little moat. The company's current advantage comes from having the best models.

That makes for a fairly shallow moat. Not only will it need to innovate relentlessly and spend incredible amounts training new models, but it may find itself with fewer and fewer customers willing to pay premium prices for those latest models when cheaper open-weight models can do the job.

So, while the company's dizzying growth makes today's price look like it could end up a bargain given a few years of expanding its top line at lightning speed, I would question whether the growth of the last two years is at all indicative of the growth in the next two. And if it is, what will earnings look like down the line?
