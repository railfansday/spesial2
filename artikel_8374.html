Since you are not out at ease by the Boldin analysis (which has SORR built on as part of its Monte Carlo) you may be a good candidate for a liability matching TIPS ladder that will guarantee a minimum inflation adjusted floor.
For many (I did this) a place to start is to calculate your social security at your claiming age target (70?) in today's dollars. The spreadsheet by Neurosphere on this forum as well as some online tools can help you do that fairly easily and https://opensocialsecurity.com/ can both advise you on when your wife and you should claim and while the total SS between the two of you will be. Is that enough of a floor for you? If it covers th essentials like housing, food, healthcare and transportation it's likely a good time bought floor.

You can then purchase a TIPS ladder with enough between 2025 and 2029 that when added to your wife's salary provides the floor equal to SS. Then make the rungs from 2029 on equal to your total SS. There is no rule that says all rings have to have the same size

If you want to be sure you can even add more to the early year leaders to cover kids, etc ..

You would still withdraw from the portfolio for the less essential expenses, but you can cut back when the market is down.


This is where I need to plug in TPAW (tpawplanner.com) or even VPW or ABW. Those tools draw on the portfolio in an amortized way so it'll last to an age you pick (100 is a good start). In good years your draw more, in bad years you draw less, but it doesn't matter because your basic expenses expenses are covered.

Some people use a guardrails method to vary how much to get from the portfolio when the market changes and beat SORR.

All of these variable withdrawal methods can be used without a floor, but for the peace of mind you seek, using them on top of a TIPS ladder floor may warm those feet up
