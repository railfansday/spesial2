‘GTA VI’ Just Might Turn Carly Rae Jepsen Into A “Late in Life Gamer”

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The singer's 2015 pop song "Run Away With Me" is experiencing a second life thanks to its inclusion in one of the game's scenes: "I have no idea if this is just a quick moment, but I'm really enjoying it."

For years, legions of Carly Rae Jepsen fans have bemoaned how cult classic “Run Away With Me” never quite had the moment it deserved as a full-fledged arena pop anthem. Eleven years, five albums and one consequential video game needle drop later, it finally has that chance thanks to a most unexpected champion: Grand Theft Auto VI.

“It’s kind of mind-blowing to be honest,” the pop star says of the past week she’s had, where “Run Away With Me” has picked up significant momentum soundtracking a standout mission in the game, where main characters Jason and Lucia jubilantly ride jet skis through the game’s fictitious Miami locale. With hundreds of millions of players in the game already — plus millions of views of the scene online, the song’s already seen a notable bump, as her streams on the song continue to double by the day. Now, it’s pushed the song now past the 200 million stream mark. “I did know that it was coming. It was sort of a top secret thing, but I didn’t expect this,” she tells THR on a Zoom call. Jepsen remembers being in the studio with collaborators Noonie Bao, Mattias Larsson and Robin Fredriksson when they were making the song.

Jepsen explains that people often ask her if she “had a feeling” about songs, as they are now. In her eyes, every song she makes feels incredible at that moment. “I got asked that a lot for ‘Call Me Maybe.’ I’m like, ‘I had no clue. How were we supposed to know?'” she says.

“With this song, we didn’t know it would have a moment or not. We just knew we were all really thrilled with the feeling it gave us, and we were all on our feet when you’re writing it,” she continues. “We were dancing it into fruition.”

The song, a later single on her critical darling third album Emotion, was popular upon release, but unlike some of her other hits like “Call Me Maybe” or “I Really Like You,” it never found its way to the Billboard Hot 100. At the time, she was just happy it had found its people. “Anytime that I think that there’s a moment where a song gets revisited, it does feel kind of like lightning striking. It does feel sort of special and magical to me,” Jepsen says.

Jepsen likens it to the moment Kate Bush’s “Running Up That Hill (A Deal With God)” found a second life in the wake of a Stranger Things needle drop.

“I remember feeling a little bit like Kate Bush was mine. I’m a little bit confused that now everybody was finally jumping,” she says jokingly. “I have no idea if this is just a quick moment, but I’m really enjoying it. My favorite part about being in a career like this is just how many surprises get thrown at you and how unexpected life can be.”

Jepsen says that her mother often tells her that something weird happened the day she was born because strange things happen to the singer. “I’m starting to think she might be right,” she says when the idea that “Run Away With Me” might finally get its moment on the charts — the song was re-released this week. “I would be so delighted. From my past catalog of songs, this is probably the one I would hope would get some attention. I’ve always loved it.”

Jepsen is currently preparing for her 2027 Day and Night North American tour, in support of her recently-released double album. “Run Away With Me” had historically been the opener for her shows, barring a change up the last few years. She’s always loved having it open the show to serve as an invitation to check one’s troubles at the door.

“I am actually currently working through setlist right now and transitions and just how we build out our show, which sounds early, but I feel like we’re trying to build a whole world here,” she says. Jepsen credits her recent work writing for the 10 Things I Hate About You musical as shifting the way she sees the flow of her live shows.

Jepsen admits she hasn’t actually played GTA VI, but she has seen the scene in which the song’s featured. “I’m going to have to [play it]. My assistant, Katie has been waiting. We’re going to have a night,” she says.

The singer thinks it’s a game she could get into, given its cinematic nature. “It’s everything that I want to put into the music that I make,” she says. “I’m going to become a late in life gamer.”
