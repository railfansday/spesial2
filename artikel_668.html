‘Best Pancakes in the County’ Review: Nicolas Cage Goes Gonzo, Again, in a Dismal Action-Comedy That Fails to Match His Energy

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A remote diner is the setting for Ken Sanzel's movie featuring a stand-off between corrupt FBI agents and a gang of criminals.

You have to admire Nicolas Cage’s penchants for loyalty and consistency. His latest effort reunites him with director-screenwriter Ken Sanzel, with whom he previously collaborated on 2019’s Kill Chain, a film that few saw and even fewer liked. And it features the sort of gonzo trademark Cage performance for which he’s become both admired and ridiculed, in this case featuring a degree of misplaced commitment to the bit that definitely doesn’t pay off. The only saving grace of Best Pancakes in the County is the excellent recipe advice about the titular food that will definitely improve your breakfast gatherings. This is the sort of movie that is routinely described as “Tarantinoesque” (a word that’s actually included in the Oxford English Dictionary, don’t you know) thanks to its convoluted plotting, eccentric characters, jokey dialogue, and propensity for Mexican standoffs. These movies are, with a few exceptions, invariably bad, but this one takes the pancake. Best Pancakes in the County The Bottom Line The worst movie in the multiplex. Release date: Friday, November 20Cast: Nicolas Cage, Justin Long, Shelley Hennig, Ravi Patel, Brandon Mychal Smith, Lauren German, Kelly GreysonDirector-screenwriter: Ken Sanzel 1 hour 38 minutes

Cage clearly participated in creating the look for his character, labeled in an onscreen graphic as “The Son of a Bitch.” Playing a ruthless criminal who doesn’t hesitate to shoot his partner while eating ice cream after a safe-cracking burglary goes awry, the actor wears hideous ‘70s-era clothes and a terrible perm wig, his make-up featuring extensive facial scarring and a porcine nose. He also employs a speaking style that sounds like he inhaled helium before every take.

Cage, absent from the proceedings for long stretches, gives it all and then some, to the point that you can feel his delight upon repeatedly uttering the phrase “I can’t find my thumb!” after a particularly gruesome incident. You half expect him to reprise his line “I’m Nicolas friggin’ Cage!” from The Unbearable Weight of Massive Talent.

His efforts, as gonzo as they are, are all for naught, with the movie’s incoherent plotting, risible dialogue, and subpar filmmaking making it a torturous experience with nothing memorable about it other than its title. That the distributor doesn’t have much confidence in its prospects is evident from its simultaneous release in “select” theaters and availability on digital platforms.
