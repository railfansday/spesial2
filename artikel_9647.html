What I Learned Building a Face-Recognition Attendance System as a Student

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

I'm a computer science student graduating in June 2026, and I want to share what building FaceMark - a face-recognition attendance system - actually taught me, beyond the tutorials.

Attendance at lectures is still paper sheets or shouted names. It's slow, easy to fake, and the data dies on paper. The idea behind FaceMark: a camera at the door, students get marked present automatically, and the records land in a database you can actually query.

Students enroll once with a few photos. At the door, a camera feed detects and recognizes faces, matches them against enrolled students, and writes attendance records with timestamps. A web dashboard shows who's present, per session, per course.

1. Recognition accuracy is a UX problem, not just a model problem. A 95% accurate model means roughly 1 in 20 students has a bad experience every single session. You need a manual override path from day one - the professor must be able to correct a record in seconds, or the system loses trust faster than it saves time.

2. Lighting will humble you. The model that worked on my desk failed near the glass door at 2pm. Real-world testing in the actual location, at different times of day, is not optional.

4. The dashboard is the product. The recognition pipeline feels like the hard part, but the thing people use every day is the attendance list. Fast loading, clear absence marking, export to a spreadsheet - boring features that decide whether the system gets adopted.

Angular for the dashboard, Node.js on the backend, a face-recognition pipeline handling detection and matching. The portfolio with more of my work: https://ziadtaham.github.io/ziad-taha-portfolio/

Happy to answer questions about the approach. And if you've built something similar, I'd genuinely like to hear how you handled the lighting problem.
