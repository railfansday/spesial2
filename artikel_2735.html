Patriots QB options to replace Drake Maye, from Jimmy Garoppolo to Will Levis

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The New England Patriots lost their starting quarterback Drake Maye to a serious ankle injury at what could only be described as the worst possible time.

Maye was injured in the Patriots' first game following the 2026 NFL trade deadline after being sacked by Aidan Hutchinson. He did not return to the game after being injured and remained in Germany for further tests on his injury even as the Patriots flew home.

The Patriots haven't yet revealed the severity of Maye's injury. However, ESPN and NFL Media insider Ian Rapoport reported the 24-year-old quarterback's injury may be severe enough to cause him to miss the rest of the season.

And later, ESPN's Adam Schefter reported Maye may have "fractured his ankle" and will undergo further testing to decide if surgery is necessary.

Regardless of the extent of Maye's injury, the Patriots appear likely to be without their star quarterback long-term. That will leave the organization relying on the duo of Tommy DeVito and Behren Morton to carry it, and coach Mike Vrabel acknowledged the team isn't necessarily looking to add outside help because of that duo.

"I don't see that being the case right now. But we'll have conversations, and we'll discuss it, and we'll look and see," Vrabel said when discussing the possibility of adding a quarterback at a news conference on Monday, Nov. 16. "Tommy and Behren Morton have been in our system, have been working. Behren Morton's been improving. He's been working each week, and I know that if he has to play for us, that he'll be ready to go."

"And I'm excited for this challenge, and I hope that everybody else is, because that's really what we're at," he added. "We're 5-5 in the middle of the season, just past the middle of the season, and we've got a bye, and looking forward to the challenge."

Even despite Vrabel's assertions, the Patriots may look to add depth to ensure they are three-deep at the quarterback position.

With the trade deadline in the rearview, the Patriots will only be able to dip into the free-agent market to add depth and talent at quarterback. Here's a look at the top options they could consider, including a potential reunion with an old friend.

If the Patriots want to add a potential plug-and-player starter, Garoppolo would be the only fit. The 35-year-old remains unsigned after considering retirement during the 2026 NFL offseason, but he may still be willing to play in the right circumstance.

The Patriots could provide that. Garoppolo spent parts of his first four seasons in New England before being traded to the San Francisco 49ers. He also played under Josh McDaniels with the Las Vegas Raiders in 2023, so he has five years of experience in McDaniels' offense.

Garoppolo could quickly sign, get back up to speed on McDaniels' system and then prepare to guide the 5-5 Patriots to a playoff berth. Despite their up-and-down performance, New England remains just one game back of a playoff spot, so Garoppolo could potentially steer the team to a second-consecutive postseason berth.

Garoppolo would also provide the Patriots with valuable experience. He has a 43-21 record and a 97.6 passer ratiing across 64 career regular-season starts and helped lead the 49ers to an appearance in Super Bowl 54, which they lost to the Kansas City Chiefs.

If Garoppolo isn't interested in joining the Patriots, New England may have to scour practice squads across the league to find potential quarterback depth.

In looking at those options, Levis stands out as a potential fit for the Patriots. The 2023 second-round pick spent his rookie season playing under Mike Vrabel with the Tennessee Titans. The results weren't necessarily pretty, as Levis led the team to a 3-6 record while completing just 58.4% of his passes for 1,808 yards, eight touchdowns and four interceptions, but Vrabel's familiarity with him could allow him to quickly settle into a role with the Patriots.

While Levis could be a depth option for the Patriots, he wouldn't likely be viewed as a plug-and-play starter over DeVito. That could incentivize him to stay with the Jets, who would likely elevate him to the team's 53-man roster to serve as a backup if Geno Smith's ankle injury is serious.

That could complicate New England's path toward adding Levis, short of offering him a chance to compete for playing time.

Mullens is another veteran quarterback with ties to New England's current coaching staff. The 31-year-old spent most of the 2022 NFL offseason with the Raiders when McDaniels was in his first season as Las Vegas' coach. The Raiders traded him to the Minnesota Vikings in advance of the season, but he still has some familiarity with McDaniels' offense.

Mullens also has starting experience, as he has made 20 total starts across five different NFL seasons. The Southern Miss product has a 5-15 record in those starts but has posted respectable counting stats, completing 66% of his passes for 6,448 yards, 34 touchdowns and 31 interceptions.

Like Levis, Mullens wouldn't be guaranteed to overtake DeVito, but he would provide some needed experience to New England's quarterback room. Mullens is currently on the Jacksonville Jaguars' practice squad, but he could be ready to jump ship if given the opportunity to lock down a backup role after spending the start of the season as the third-string option behind Trevor Lawrence and Quinn Ewers in Jacksonville.

In 2023, many viewed Browning as a high-quality backup quarterback who could eventually emerge as a solid stopgap starter. He posted a 4-3 record in relief of an injured Joe Burrow with the Cincinnati Bengals, completing a league-high 70.4% of his passes for 1,936 yards, 12 touchdowns and seven interceptions while showing a strong command of Cincinnati's offense.

Browning was again given an opportunity to play again last season, but he struggled, completing 64.8% of his passes for 771 yards, six touchdowns and eight interceptions. The Bengals eventually benched him in favor of trade acquisition Joe Flacco and Browning left the Bengals in the offseason to join the Tampa Bay Buccaneers.

Browning failed to beat undrafted free agent Jalon Daniels for Tampa Bay's backup job behind Baker Mayfield. He spent the start of the season on the Buccaneers' practice squad but eventually landed on the unit for the Los Angeles Chargers.

Browning would likely jump at a chance to rejoin a 53-man roster, and the Patriots could be interested in seeing if the 30-year-old can rekindle the magic he enjoyed during his first starting action with the Bengals.

Reed spent Week 9 of the 2026 NFL season with the Ravens after injuries to Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley ahead of a "Thursday Night Football" game. He was quickly dispatched after joining the depth chart and remains available to sign.

Reed has never played an NFL snap, but he enjoyed a quality campaign during the 2026 UFL season. He completed 56.3% of his passes for 1,923 yards, a league-high 21 touchdowns and nine interceptions playing for the Dallas Renegades, which he parlayed into an opportunity to return to the NFL.

Reed spent all of the 2024 season with the Chicago Bears after going undrafted out of Western Kentucky. Patriots passing game coordinator Thomas Brown served as Chicago's passing game coordinator, offensive coordinator and interim coach during that season, so he could be interested in reuniting with Reed as a potential depth piece.

If the Patriots eschew options with NFL experience and instead target a rookie to help push Morton, Altmyer could draw consideration. The undrafted rookie is currently on the Lions' practice squad, but the Patriots could add him to serve as a third-stringer on the 53-man roster.

Altmyer enjoyed a solid college career at Illinois and completed 67.4% of his passes for 3,007 yards, 22 touchdowns and five interceptions during his final season in 2025. The 24-year-old doesn't have a cannon of an arm and is more of a game-manager type, but he could still provide the Patriots with valuable organizational depth, not unlike DeVito did when he came out of Illinois and served as the New York Giants' third-string quarterback.

McDaniels also worked on-staff with Illinois coach Bret Bielema for two years when Bielema was an assistant for the Patriots. That familiarity could help sell the offensive coordinator on Altmyer if New England can't bring in a more veteran option.
