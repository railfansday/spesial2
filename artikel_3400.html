40 Years Later, Stephen King’s ‘IT’ Officially Returns With a Massive Anniversary Release

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

Pennywise has had plenty of help making people afraid of clowns, but IT had already been doing that job for years before Tim Curry put on the makeup. Stephen King’s 1986 story gives the monster an entire town to hide in. The kids know something is wrong with Derry, but getting an adult to listen is another problem, and by the time the Losers’ Club grows up, most of them have learned to forget it, too. Coming home means remembering why they wanted to leave in the first place.

That’s a lot to ask of seven people, and King gives them more than a thousand pages to work through it. Anyone coming to the book after the movies has a much longer summer with Bill, Beverly, Richie, and the rest waiting for them. There’s also considerably more Derry to explore, including the history of a town that keeps losing children and finding ways to look elsewhere. Forty years later, the anniversary releases are giving readers another excuse to spend some time there.

A deluxe 40th-anniversary edition of Stephen King’s IT releases November 26 from Hodder & Stoughton, featuring a new introduction by Stranger Things creators Matt and Ross Duffer. The 1,088-page hardcover is limited to 1,010 hand-numbered copies, with Pennywise printed across the page edges. It’s part of a wider anniversary celebration that also includes a separate U.S. edition from Scribner.

Four franchises, four completely different ways of getting underneath your skin. This is the one built out of your particular fear, why it works so well on you, and what it would leave behind.

Your nightmare does not wait outside the door. It waits for you to close your eyes, which you are going to have to do eventually, because nobody has ever won that particular argument.

Your nightmare is not really the doll. It is the face of the person you tell, and the way they decide, very kindly, that you have imagined all of it.

Your nightmare has your number, knows your name, and has almost certainly been inside your house before. The worst part is that you already know them.

Your nightmare is not really about you. It is about the people asleep down the hall, and the feeling that whatever is here is not interested in leaving.

The UK limited edition comes in a clothbound, die-cut slipcase, with illustrated endpapers and a ribbon marker. It was listed at £75, but the publisher’s store currently lists the edition as sold out. The seller also excludes shipping to the U.S. and Canada, among other territories. King’s website still directs readers to the preorder page, so the store’s current availability is worth checking before planning a purchase. For U.S. readers, Scribner’s anniversary hardcover is already available, having been released August 4. That edition runs 1,168 pages, with new cover art, stained edges, and a $50 list price.

The Duffers' involvement adds appeal to the UK addition, though. The Duffers have spent years working with a setup King helped make famous: a group of kids who have to face something monstrous while the adults around them fail to understand what’s happening. Their introduction gives Stranger Things fans a reason to pick up IT, especially if they’ve only seen the movies.

The 40th-anniversary edition of IT releases November 26.
