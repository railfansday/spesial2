Chinese Tech Giant Huawei and Subsidiaries Convicted of Sanctions Violations, Fraud, Conspiracy to Steal Trade Secrets, and Money Laundering

A federal jury in the Eastern District of New York returned guilty verdicts following a three-month trial against Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd. (Huawei) and three of its subsidiaries for violating U.S. sanctions involving Iran, lying to banks, money laundering, and stealing trade secrets from American companies. As proven at trial, Huawei and several of its subsidiaries, including Chinese subsidiary Huawei Device Co. Ltd. (Huawei Device) and U.S. subsidiaries Futurewei Technologies Inc. (Futurewei) and Huawei Device USA Inc. (Huawei Device USA), engaged in a decades-long scheme to enrich Huawei and grow its telecommunications business, including in the United States, through criminal activities.

“No person or company is above the law,” said Attorney General Todd Blanche. “For decades, Huawei and three of its subsidiaries conducted their business in the United States on the basis of lies and theft. Thanks to the work of the FBI and our federal prosecutors, those who engaged in these criminal activities will be held accountable.”

“For over two decades, Huawei built its global empire on a foundation of fraud, theft, and deception,” said Assistant Attorney General Tysen A. Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “Huawei’s crimes threatened the U.S. financial system, took advantage of American companies that compete honestly in the global telecommunications market, and undermined our national security. Today’s verdict sends an unmistakable message: America will defend its businesses, protect its workers, and safeguard its national security against corrupt foreign corporations that lie, cheat, and steal to get ahead.”

“Huawei violated our laws, empowered our enemies, and harmed our national security,” said Acting Principal Deputy Assistant Attorney General for National Security M. Scott Proctor. “This conviction shows that the National Security Division and its partners will not rest until companies like Huawei are held to account for their bad conduct in an American court of justice.”

“Few crimes are as serious as Huawei’s decades-long pattern of endangering U.S. national security, violating the sanctity of U.S. commerce, and impeding the enforcement of U.S. laws,” said U.S. Attorney Joseph Nocella Jr. for the Eastern District of New York. “Today’s verdict is a step towards accountability for Huawei’s criminal activities, which includes yearslong collaboration with sanctioned entities in Iran, repeated lies to banks, and blatant thefts from U.S. technology companies.”

“For years, Huawei and its subsidiaries carried out an egregious campaign to expand their international enterprise through theft of trade secrets, fraud, and other criminal conduct at the expense of U.S. companies and the rule of law,” said Assistant Director Roman Rozhavsky of the FBI’s Counterintelligence and Espionage Division. “This case serves as a stark reminder that the Chinese government continues to pose a serious threat to our economic and national security, but today’s guilty verdict proves the FBI and our partners will aggressively investigate, expose, and hold accountable those who target our nation. No company is too big, too powerful, or too well connected to exploit the U.S. and its citizens and expect to escape American justice.”

Huawei and its subsidiaries assisted the government of the Islamic Republic of Iran by providing turnkey services with respect to an internet backbone and broader accessibility for mobile networks in the country. Huawei also helped the Iranian regime and a component of the Iranian intelligence apparatus, Zaeim, to monitor and censor its users’ access to the internet. Testimony further established that Huawei provided surveillance cameras that enabled the Iranian regime to monitor protest activity.

Huawei conducted its business in Iran under the name “Skycom.” Huawei repeatedly lied about the nature of its business in Iran, falsely telling banks, journalists, and the U.S. government that it did not assist Iran’s government, military, or intelligence community; that it began restricting its business in Iran in 2011; that Skycom was a business partner, rather than a wholly-owned subsidiary; and that Huawei complied with all applicable sanctions. Huawei knew that all of those statements were materially false and misleading.

Huawei lied about its activities in Iran to banks to obtain billions of U.S. dollars and access to U.S.-dollar clearing services, which enabled Huawei to be a sophisticated participant in a global marketplace for telecommunications services — in part because of its lies.

Huawei and its subsidiaries also engaged in a long-running conspiracy to steal intellectual property of U.S. companies, including Huawei’s competitors and some purported business partners. The jury convicted Huawei and three subsidiaries of conspiracy to steal trade secrets, conspiracy to commit wire and bank fraud, wire fraud, bank fraud, conspiracy to defraud the United States, conspiracy to violate the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), substantive violations of IEEPA, and money laundering conspiracy. When sentenced, the defendants face financial penalties including criminal fines, forfeiture, and restitution. Sentencing has been set for Sept. 14, 2027. A federal district court judge will determine any sentence after considering the U.S. Sentencing Guidelines and other statutory factors.

The investigation is being conducted by the FBI’s New York Field Office.
