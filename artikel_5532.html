Nvidia employees alleged Jensen Huang threatened and harassed staff, 2007 suit shows

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Claims by two staffers, including Nvidia's first in-house lawyer, surfaced as Huang seeks to return as CEO; his representative called them false

Two Nvidia $NVDA employees accused Jensen Huang, then the company's CEO, of threatening staff and making sexually explicit remarks, according to allegations in a 2007 California lawsuit described by Bloomberg.

Stephen Pettigrew, Nvidia's first in-house lawyer, stated in California Superior Court filings that he witnessed Huang berate and menace employees. Pettigrew claimed his dismissal came after he voiced worries about how executives behaved. The company pointed to shortcomings in his job performance as the reason it let him go.

"He would scream at them, threaten them with extreme bodily harm and make sexually oriented comments while thrusting his hips in a demonstration of the sex act," according to a brief filed by Pettigrew's attorneys in the age discrimination and wrongful termination suit.

Court filings in the case also describe an unnamed "senior executive at Nvidia" accused of making sexual comments and of vowing to tear an employee's arm off and hit them with it, according to Bloomberg. Pettigrew demanded upward of $2.6 million in compensation. Nvidia once offered $175,000 to resolve the matter, according to court records.

Nvidia filed counterclaims of its own, demanding that Pettigrew give back company records. The dispute concluded with a private agreement reached in December 2009.

Michael Winston, Nvidia's senior vice president for human resources, lodged a 2005 complaint alleging discrimination. He claimed he was dismissed for protesting "Huang's abusive treatment of employees, including conduct he believed constituted sexual harassment." Details of that complaint appear in documents from Pettigrew's case. Deposition testimony in that case indicated Winston was brought on in September 2004 and remained for only four weeks.

A representative for Huang said: "These false allegations are from over 20 years ago and were made by two fired employees. The thousands of employees who have shown support for Jensen over the last week show they are false." Nvidia representatives did not respond to requests for comment. Pettigrew and Winston have both died, and lawyers who represented them or their relatives did not respond to attempts to reach them.

Huang, 63, stepped down as CEO earlier this month in the wake of revelations about two extramarital affairs, according to Bloomberg. He is now petitioning directors for permission to come back, and both the board and senior management have publicly said no. Huang has acknowledged "torturing" the people closest to him in order to draw out their best efforts, Bloomberg said.

Some Nvidia employees have urged the board to bring Huang back. An internal suggestion-box post asking directors to weigh employee sentiment has drawn more than 1,600 replies, and a number of them call for restoring him to the role. Nvidia named Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress interim CEO, and the board is searching for a permanent chief executive.
