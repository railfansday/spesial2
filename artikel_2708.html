Five takeaways from No. 7 UCLA's 49-point win versus Long Beach State

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The No. 7 UCLA Bruins men's basketball team had another comfortable win, as they defeated the Long Beach State Beach 105-66 at the Pauley Pavilion in Los Angeles, Calif. on Monday.

The win marked the Bruins fourth of the season and their third-consecutive home win in a five-game homestand. UCLA didn't give Long Beach State a chance with a 33-point lead heading into halftime, but the Beach were able to cut into the lead in the second half by scoring more points than the Bruins last opponent did in their entire game. Here were five takeaways from UCLA's Monday night blowout win.

UCLA just couldn't keep the ball out of the basket and they racked up 53 first half points, their highest first half total of the season so far. It didn't slow down in the second half either. UCLA had 52 points in the second half and ended the game with five players in double-digit scoring. The result was another blowout that allowed the Bruins to achieve their third consecutive win of the season scoring at least 100 points.

The Bruins controlled the game for nearly the entirety of it and in large part it was due to forcing early turnovers. UCLA forced 19 turnovers at the Pauley Pavilion and added 33 points off of the turnovers. Early miscues could have given Long Beach State some hope at an upset, but they stood no chance when the Bruins stole the ball 10 times. Five of those steals came from one of the Bruins new additions, sophomore guard Jaylen Petty.

Long Beach State was another victim of the Bruins' defense suffocating their opponents at the Pauley Pavilion, but a defensive breakdown in second half gave the Beach a bit of life. Long Beach State put together their best 2010s Golden State Warriors Splash Bros impression and got hot from beyond the arc to cut into the Bruins lead. UCLA needs to be a bit more locked in defensively in second halves, but when the result is still a blowout, then maybe there isn't too much cause for concern.

Despite the Bruins going against 7-foot-2 center Noah Amenhauser, the Bruins dominated in the paint. UCLA scored 48 in the paint compared to Long Beach State's 18. The Bruins have proven to be able to score anywhere on the court and on a night where UCLA shot 63 percent from the field, it proved to be devastating for the Beach.

Senior forward Eric Dailey Jr. put together a clinic and deserves his flowers with a game-leading 25-points. Even more impressive was the fact that Dailey made his first nine attempts from the field. Dailey has been a steadying presence for UCLA this season and also helped the Bruins with five rebounds, an assist and a steal to bolster the Bruins to their third consecutive home win.
