How Fin Smith's carrying is giving England an extra dimension

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

With a grin and an expletive, Fin Smith describes Ben Earl as "greedy".

"It takes some doing to get just one carry when he's on the pitch," jokes Smith to BBC Sport. "He hogs them all."

Earl's performance in Saturday's win over New Zealand certainly caught the eye. The number eight carried a phenomenal 32 times in the 37-12 victory, more than twice as much as anyone else on the pitch.

But, further down the standings, Smith also stood out. He carried the ball 11 times against the All Blacks, the third-highest total of any England player.

That stat comes with context. The fly-half racked up his total in just 55 minutes on the pitch before cramp forced him to the bench.

For Smith, 5ft 10in tall and less than 14 stone, such physicality is not unusual.

A fortnight before beating New Zealand, against Australia, he carried nine times, his team's fourth-highest total.

According to stats from Oval Insights, he has carried more than any other fly-half in a Six Nations side this year.

If you include Rugby Championship teams in the mix, only three fly-halves - New Zealand's Richie Mo'unga, South Africa's Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu and Argentina's Tomas Albornoz - have backed themselves more with the ball.

Smith admits he does not haven't the athletic ability of some of those rivals.

But the Northampton man says his attempts to play through the defence, rather than in front of them, helps him lead the team to a more effective attack.

"I do like the physical side," he adds. "It gets me into games, but also I think it is just my character.

"I would hate to be someone that is asking everyone else to go and be physical and put their body on the line, if I wasn't particularly willing to do it. I think that's how I've been brought up."

With George Furbank, Henry Slade and Seb Atkinson all able to step in at first receiver, Smith being buried at the bottom of the breakdown is a price England are able to pay for a phase or two.

"It's important for me to be a threat myself," he adds.

"The way we're playing at Northampton and England, and the way I feel like teams attack best when I'm in them, is when there are almost no numbers on backs.

"You play as quickly as possible and whoever ends up in the slots just does that job. You've got to be interchangeable.

"If the right thing to do is carry or the right thing to do is hit the breakdown then so be it, and then someone else can drop in as the first playmaker.

"I think that just makes us a harder team to play against. Our scrum-halves are running a lot more than they have and all of a sudden defences have got to think about do they tighten up to defend the nine and then if the 10s are running the ball a bit more they have to think about me.

"Then spaces open up and you've become so much less predictable."

After a miserable Six Nations, England have found a new attacking edge in the Nations Championship. They have scored 44 tries across their six games in the tournament, 10 more than any other side.

On Saturday, they are hunting a sixth-straight win as they face a rematch against New Zealand in the final's weekend's third-place play-off.

Smith says England's current streak, after the five straight defeats that preceded it, has coincided with the team becoming more introspective, both on and off the pitch.

"The big thing that's changed would be we've been a lot more inwardly focused," Smith said.

"We're confident that if we're at our best, it should hopefully take care of whatever the opposition brings to the table.

"I think that was a big learning we got from the South Africa game [July's 45-21 defeat] is actually 'let's just do what we want to do and do it really well'.

"Standing on the pitch after we'd lost in Rome [to Italy in the Six Nations in March] was not that long ago and that's probably as low as I've been in my career.

"To now beating the All Blacks and playing well against Australia... just wanting to end the year on a good note is a massive one for for us and me this weekend.

"I've honestly absolutely loved these last few weeks from the feel around the training ground to the meetings, to the way we're training hard for each other, to dinners we're having in the evenings and then what we then put on the pitch.

"It's been as enjoyable a rugby experience as I can remember for a long time."

After the New Zealand rematch concludes their autumn campaign, England's next matches will be in the Six Nations where they travel to Ireland in the opening round on 5 February, before facing defending champions France the following weekend.
