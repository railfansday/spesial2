UnitedHealth Keeps Raising Guidance. Here's What a $10,000 Investment Could Be Worth if the Turnaround Sticks.

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

UnitedHealth has raised its 2026 outlook three times as its Medicare Advantage and Optum segments recover.

UnitedHealth Group (UNH -5.42%) may finally be turning a corner after a difficult stretch. These days, with management repeatedly raising earnings guidance (most recently on Oct. 13) and profitability showing signs of recovery, could the healthcare giant be poised for a comeback?

Here's what a $10,000 investment could become if the turnaround holds.

For much of the past two years, UnitedHealth has struggled to convince investors it could turn its business around. But that picture is starting to change. In fact, UnitedHealth has now raised its full-year earnings guidance three times in 2026.

Its latest third-quarter results offered another encouraging sign that management's efforts are starting to pay off. Specifically, the company reported adjusted earnings of $4.69 per share, compared with $2.92 in the same quarter last year. But more importantly, management raised its full-year 2026 adjusted earnings outlook to at least $20.50 per share, up from its previous range of between $19.50 and $20.00.

Still, for investors, the bigger question now is whether these improvements represent a temporary rebound or the beginning of a more sustainable recovery. So far, the latest results have given more reasons to be optimistic.

One of UnitedHealth's biggest challenges has been rising medical costs across its insurance business, particularly in Medicare Advantage. In response, the company had to begin making big changes to the business. These included raising premiums, reducing certain benefits, and exiting less profitable markets.

The strategy meant sacrificing short-term membership growth, but the results suggest that this new approach is working. In fact, in the third quarter, the health insurance segment generated $2.8 billion in operating earnings, compared with $1.8 billion a year earlier. Plus, its operating margin improved to 3.3% from 2.1%.

Looking ahead, management sees opportunities to further improve its Medicare Advantage business in 2027. Specifically, the company is focusing on markets and products where it believes it can achieve more durable margins, including special needs plans for seniors with more complex healthcare needs.

Meanwhile, UnitedHealth's healthcare services business is turning into a strong growth driver.

Perhaps one of UnitedHealth's most important competitive advantages comes from Optum, which operates across healthcare delivery, pharmacy services, and technology segments. This diversification is valuable as the healthcare industry looks for ways to trim costs, improve efficiency, and coordinate healthcare.

There is an additional long-term opportunity in Optum's technology operations. For example, automation and artificial intelligence (AI) could help streamline administrative processes and improve the way patients and providers interact with the healthcare system.

All told, if existing operations continue to recover and productivity improvements from automation and AI materialize, UnitedHealth would be better positioned to deliver stronger earnings growth. That raises the question of what stronger earnings growth could mean for investors.

Ultimately, if the company executes its turnaround plans well, investors could be rewarded. To illustrate, starting with its 2026 adjusted earnings per share (EPS) projection of at least $20.50 and applying the low end of management's long-term earnings growth target of 13%-16%, adjusted EPS could reach around $23.17 in 2027.

From there, applying a 22 price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio produces a price target of around $510 per share, which would turn a $10,000 investment into about $12,400.
