BigBear.ai vs. Cerebras Systems: Which Artificial Intelligence Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Federal defense AI software or wafer-scale AI hardware: which stock belongs in your portfolio as 2027 approaches?

By Robert Izquierdo – Updated Nov 27, 2026 at 1:51PM EST

10 stocks we like better than BigBear.ai ›

The rapid expansion of artificial intelligence has created a divide between software providers and hardware infrastructure giants. Choosing between BigBear.ai (BBAI +6.92%) and Cerebras Systems (CBRS +5.08%) requires weighing government contracts against massive computing power.

BigBear.ai focuses on decision AI for defense and intelligence agencies, while Cerebras Systems builds massive wafer-scale processors to accelerate artificial intelligence model training. These companies represent two distinct paths into the growing AI market, offering investors a choice between specialized software and high-performance hardware.

BigBear.ai focuses on AI-powered decision intelligence software, positioning itself as a unique player among tech stocks serving the defense industry. It uses a land and expand strategy by deploying minimally viable products to evaluate the potential for long-term government contracts. Because it derives significant revenue from a limited number of defense and intelligence agencies, customer concentration adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $142.1 million, reflecting a decrease of roughly 1.5% compared to the prior year. The company reported a net loss of $180.2 million during this period. This performance highlights the challenges of scaling software services within the rigid budgetary cycles of government clients.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was zero, indicating a very low level of total debt relative to shareholder equity. The current ratio, which measures a company's ability to cover short-term debts with assets that can be converted to cash within a year, was 1.8x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was negative $46.3 million.

Cerebras Systems builds massive semiconductor infrastructure, including wafer-scale systems designed to train large-scale AI models more efficiently than traditional chips. Its systems are used by organizations in medical research, cryptography, and energy, while its infrastructure is deployed in data centers worldwide. This specialized focus helps the company target niche markets that require high-density computing power.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $755.8 million, which represents growth of 79.9% over the previous twelve-month period. Despite this rapid top-line expansion, the company reported a net loss of $651.4 million. The significant growth illustrates the high demand for specialized AI hardware as developers race to build more complex models.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company had a debt-to-equity ratio of -0.5x, which indicates that total liabilities exceed shareholder equity. The current ratio was 2.1x, suggesting a solid ability to meet short-term financial obligations. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was negative $392.8 million.

BigBear.ai faces substantial revenue concentration since a few government customers provide most of its funding, making it vulnerable to budget shifts or contract terminations. The company has also navigated operational hurdles, including a $76.7 million goodwill impairment in late 2026 and legal challenges related to historical accounting restatements. Furthermore, it depends on third-party cloud infrastructure from providers like Microsoft (MSFT +0.60%) and Amazon (AMZN +3.96%), to deliver its software.

Cerebras operates in a highly capital-intensive industry where it must compete against established giants and rapidly evolving technology standards. The company is currently reporting significant net losses and negative cash flow as it invests heavily in its unique wafer-scale technology. Because it seeks to disrupt a market dominated by massive competitors, any delays in manufacturing or shifts in AI architecture could threaten its market share.

BigBear.ai appears to be the more conservatively priced option because it carries a much lower P/S ratio, which measures market value against sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

Between BigBear.ai or Cerebras Systems for exposure to the artificial intelligence market, I would pick Cerebras. The company's proprietary semiconductor chip technology enables AI systems to operate at faster speeds than those offered by larger competitors. This contributed to Cerebras gaining AI tech giants OpenAI, Microsoft, and Oracle as customers.

Meanwhile, BigBear.ai saw sales sink in 2025 after the U.S. government performed budget cuts. The company's revenue is rebounding in 2026, with full-year sales forecasted to come in between $150 million to $165 million, up from 2025's $127.7 million. However, the fact that the federal government could cut funding to BigBear.ai last year suggests its technology is not critical.

Moreover, the software company's reliance on federal contracts keeps it exposed to sudden shifts to its top-line growth. Its stock's lower valuation reflects this vulnerability, and until BigBear.ai can expand outside government dependence, Cerebras' innovative chips provide a better path to a return on your investment despite its stock's higher valuation.
