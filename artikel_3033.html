CoreWeave Stock Surges as Revenue Soars. Is It Too Late to Buy the Stock?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

CoreWeave stock jumped after delivering strong revenue growth.

Neocloud stocks have seen considerable pressure this year, but CoreWeave (CRWV -6.13%) just gave investors something to cheer about when it reported its third-quarter results. The sector has been hit with a trio of headwinds in 2026, which is why it may not be a good idea to chase the current rally.

First, the group was hit with questions about how the space will finance the current build-out, especially as interest rates have begun to rise. Second, IT consultant Gartner predicted that 80% of neoclouds would cease independent operations by 2030, as artificial intelligence (AI) revenue would not support the build-out and, with high financing costs, they would not generate sufficient returns on investment. Finally, midterm elections saw many anti-data center candidates win office and quickly call for a data center moratorium and tougher permitting requirements.

Going into its earnings report, CoreWeave shares were down 32% year-to-date and 65% from their 52-week high. Let's dig into the artificial intelligence infrastructure provider's Q3 results to see whether the rally can continue or if this is a good time to sell.

CoreWeave has become one of the top neocloud providers, and that showed up in its Q3 results. The company's revenue surged 168% from $1.37 billion a year ago to $3.66 billion. That came in ahead of the $3.55 billion analyst consensus, as compiled by LSEG.

CoreWeave also raised its full-year revenue guidance, taking it to a range of $13 billion to $13.3 billion, as it expects more than 1.85 gigawatts (GW) of active power by year-end. It is projecting to end the year with run rate revenue of between $20 billion and $21 billion. The company's previous guidance was for full-year revenue of $12.4 billion to $13.2 billion, with a year-end annualized revenue run rate of $18.5 billion to $19.5 billion.

The company's backlog, meanwhile, rose 138% year over year to a record $132.5 billion. It currently has $9.3 billion in construction in progress and reduced its cost of debt by 250 basis points to 7.9%. CoreWeave has been working to lower its debt costs, despite higher interest rates, by paying down expensive early-stage debt and replacing it with secured loans and customer prepayments. It expects to receive $9 billion in customer prepayments within the next three months.

Importantly, CoreWeave said that it is seeing quick payback periods on its investments. On short-term contracts, it is getting a payback within six to 18 months, while on longer-term commitments, it seeing a payback in one-to-two years at current rates. It is currently getting $40 million per megawatt annually under short-term deals and $20 million to $25 million per megawatt under four- to five-year contracts.

CoreWeave also saw a nice jump in adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) in the quarter, which climbed from $838 million a year earlier to $2.26 billion. However, it continues to burn cash, and its debt continues to grow.

It generated operating cash flow of $2 billion in the quarter and $5.6 billion through the first nine months of the year. However, free cash flow was negative $7.5 billion for the quarter and negative $17.9 billion through the first nine months of 2026 thanks to $23.6 billion in capex). It ended the quarter with $67.4 billion in debt and $6.4 billion in cash and restricted cash as of Sept. 30.

CoreWeave is demonstrating that the neocloud business can work and that the returns are quite robust at current pricing. However, its growing debt load, backed by rapidly declining assets, remains a major risk, and neoclouds are highly vulnerable to construction delays stemming from the political shift following the mid-term elections. Whether short-term rates remain so robust in the future is also a considerable risk as more computing capacity comes online.

As such, I wouldn't be chasing the rally in CoreWeave or any other neoclouds, and I think that the big cloud computing stocks are the better way to play the growing infrastructure-as-a-service market.
