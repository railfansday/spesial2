Michael Burry Cashed In Caterpillar Puts After a Nearly 4x Gain Even as CAT Stock Fell 10% in Its Worst Week in 18 Months. Is Caterpillar Still Overvalued, or Did Burry Just Lock In the Easy Money?

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Caterpillar's valuation got out of hand and is now coming back down to earth.

Caterpillar (CAT -4.95%) has quickly fallen 44% from its highs, which is the largest drawdown for the construction equipment company in the last 10 years. The stock was riding high because of the company's importance to the artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure build-out but has since fallen along with many other thematic stocks.

Famous investor and short seller Michael Burry publicly bet against Caterpillar using put options, and his position increased by about 4x in less than a year. He has since closed most of his position, which he wrote about in his newsletter this week.

Does that mean now could finally be a good time to buy the dip on Caterpillar, or is Burry just getting out while the easy money has been made?

Spending on construction has grown due to the need for data centers, computer chip factories, and other industrial sites in the United States that are fueling the AI boom.

Caterpillar directly services the construction of these sites with its myriad vehicles and equipment, such as bulldozers. It has many other business lines related to construction, such as equipment rental, financing, and turbines. It is the go-to brand for building things.

Last quarter, revenue for power and energy grew 18% year over year, driven by the AI boom. However, its other large segment, basic construction services, grew 18% year over year as well.

More important was the booming backlog, which grew 96% year over year to $78 billion. Caterpillar is receiving orders from construction companies faster than it can fulfill them, giving it short-term business predictability and pricing power. This is why at one point Caterpillar's shares were up over 400% in the last five years.

The problem with a business like Caterpillar is cyclicality. Today is a booming time, with strong growth in demand, so it wants to increase its manufacturing capacity to meet it.

However, this can turn ugly if a downturn ever materializes. Construction booms almost always come with an eventual bust, and the AI infrastructure trade may be no different. This is why many stocks associated with AI infrastructure have fallen over the last few months: Investors worry that these boom times are nearing an end.

At one point this year, Caterpillar stock traded at a price-to-earnings ratio (P/E) of over 50, which is significantly higher than at any point in its history. This meant that a lot of the AI benefits to its business were priced in, and perhaps even more.

Burry bet against Caterpillar shares using put options, which are similar to shorting but with a specific expiration date. As Caterpillar has fallen around 50% from its highs, the value of these option contracts has risen substantially.

Investors like Burry rightly saw that Caterpillar was being valued as if the boom in AI demand for its construction products and services would be permanent. It turns out Wall Street is now worried it will be temporary, with profits coming back down to earth.

Does that mean investors can now buy Caterpillar stock? Or is it set to fall further?

Right now, Caterpillar has a P/E ratio of 23, which is much more reasonable than its previous peak of 50. At the same time, it should still see solid earnings growth in the quarters ahead, driven by this booming backlog and price increases it can implement due to the construction equipment supply shortage.

That still does not mean one should run out and buy up a bunch of Caterpillar shares. Smart investors buy for the long term, not the next few quarters. Operating in a highly cyclical industry, Caterpillar is a difficult stock to own. There are much better ones you can buy for your portfolio to hold for a decade.
