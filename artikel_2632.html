Grant Nelson making impact amid Nets injuries

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

TORONTO — There is an NBA axiom about staying ready so one doesn't have to get ready. It's what Grant Nelson has been doing this season, really, the past two, with G League Long Island, awaiting his chance with Brooklyn.

That chance arrived with the Nets' big man woes that saw Nelson — who had five rebounds and four assists in a 124-100 loss to the Raptors on Friday — finally called up.

Brooklyn's struggles at the backup center spot, bottoming out with Moritz Wagner's knee injury, have given Nelson his opportunity. And he's cashed in to give the Nets some much-needed hustle.

"Just being [ready], staying ready, being out in Long Island under those coaches, just learning the system throughout the two years I've been here., being out in Long Island under those coaches, just learning the system throughout the two years I've been here. And when my name was called, being ready," Nelson said. "So, it's just great all the preparation that I put in, finally seeing that unfold on the basketball court. And it's just good to be with this team, get some minutes."

Grant Nelson walks down court during the Emirates NBA Cup Group Play against the Milwaukee Bucks at Fiserv Forum on Nov. 25, 2026 in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. Getty Images

Nelson, 24, got the call-up at the start of the Nets' four-game road trip, making his season debut in a brief cameo at Minnesota. But when Wagner, one of just two proven centers on Brooklyn's roster, left with knee soreness Wednesday in Milwaukee, that's when the 6-foot-10 Nelson had an opportunity to step up.

In the first meaningful non-tanking minutes of his career, Nelson actually helped the Nets to a 104-97 victory. He had just one point but added six rebounds, including three on the offensive glass, and was a plus-six in 13:04.

"You saw Grant: You plug him in, and he does everything well. His size, how smart he is, skilled, tough — really, really tough," head coach Jordi Fernández said. "And since we've had him here, he's been himself, and he's a very, very good basketball player. He was a big reason why we won. You see the rebounding and the activity and the physicality defensively, and I expect him just to play hard."

Of all the things Nelson did, playing hard was the most important. It's something that stood out on a Nets team that hasn't done it enough.

"He's just coming in and playing as hard as he can. He's not gonna be perfect and not make mistakes, but he makes up for it with effort," Julius Randle said. "He's just doing all the little things, the dirty work, the things that we need to win that might not show up on the stat sheet. So he's playing really hard, playing with a lot of good energy, and it's contagious."

Grant Nelson (16) and Egor Demin (8) play defense during the game against the Toronto Raptors during the 2026 Emirates NBA Cup on Nov. 27, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

For a Brooklyn team that has shown a disappointing lack of hustle and energy, Nelson provided plenty.

He led the team in Defensive Rating, Offensive Rebound percentage and box outs, with five of the latter. For perspective, the rest of the Nets combined for just six while the Bucks finished with seven.

Nelson was an undrafted 2025 rookie, and other than four NBA cameos he spent the season in the G-League working at forward and center. But this year he was exclusively a center, averaging 16.8 points and 11.8 boards on 57.4 percent shooting before getting called up.

"Continuing to work on my game, still making that transition to a five man, so being able to guard in the perimeter, still working on that jump shot. I've taken a step this year getting rebounds, so just doing that at this next level," Nelson said. "Just continuing to be able to guard ball screens, guard guards, being able to switch onto them when I have to. And then being in every action offensively and defensively, having the conditioning for that and being physical with the fives.

"How I move kind of gives me an advantage. So continuing to be able to move my feet defensively on guards.So continuing to be able to move my feet defensively on guards. … I feel like I still have a lot to work on, but just continuing to show that."
