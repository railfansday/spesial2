Fed's Waller puts a half-point December rate hike on the table if inflation runs hot

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Waller said he leans toward a quarter-point increase if October inflation improves; the Fed meets Dec. 8-9

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said the central bank ought to weigh a half-point interest rate increase at its December meeting if October consumer price data looks like the figures from the prior two months, according to Bloomberg.

Waller made the remarks Tuesday in a speech in London. "If, as I expect, the monthly rate of inflation improves in October, I am leaning toward supporting an increase in our policy rate of 25 basis points," he said, according to Bloomberg.

"But if the October CPI inflation data out later today are similar to the past two monthly readings — with core CPI inflation around 0.3% or even higher — then a stronger policy move may be needed," Waller said, according to Bloomberg. "In that case, I believe a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting."

He said a quarter-point move in that scenario would damage the Fed's standing. "Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take," Waller said, according to Bloomberg.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics is scheduled to release the October consumer price index on Tuesday. The Fed's next policy meeting is Dec. 8-9. The 2-year U.S. Treasury yield climbed 4 basis points during Waller's remarks.

Swaps moved to fully price a quarter-point hike for December following Waller's remarks, versus odds of roughly 85% ahead of them, according to Bloomberg.

Questioned afterward by Bloomberg's Francine Lacqua, Waller said the Fed needs to act. "We are looking forward and saying we need to do something; at a minimum, probably take back the three insurance cuts that we put in last year," he told Bloomberg.

Waller said higher rates "will be needed, sooner rather than later," according to CNBC. He said carrying out the hikes matters more than their timing, and that "it is not necessary, for example, to raise rates at consecutive meetings."

The Fed held its benchmark rate at 3.75% to 4% at its October meeting. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point increase. The central bank raised rates by a quarter point in September, its first increase since July 2023.

Other officials had said after the October meeting that rates need to rise. St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said "additional firming of monetary policy will be necessary, and soon." Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan, who votes on the December decision, said the Federal Open Market Committee "needs to implement at least two more 25-basis-point rate increases."
