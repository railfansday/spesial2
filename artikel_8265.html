Personal Investments • Re: Mathematical Error in 15% Savings Rule

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The 15% savings rule (which, incidentally, used to be the 10% rule when I entered the work force) and the 70% spending in retirement rule are more like rules-of-thumb... heuristics to help people with first order planning. But I would argue that each person's situation is different so those rules may not apply to every situation. Personally, I would argue that the 70% rule wildly inaccurate, but I'll digress.

That said, I wanted to focus on the core of your post, which is, "How much do I spend now vs save for the near future vs save for retirement?"

As you point out, spending now can help make memories with your children when they are still in the nest. Saving for the near future might help you reach goals like downpayment on a house or college for your children. Then of course saving for retirement will allow you to life and hopefully thrive when your working life comes to a close.

How you balance those things depends on your own personal values and how you judge your current needs vs future needs. And much of that is unknown since you don't know what the future holds. Man plans, and God laughs! Some people might want to roll the dice, live for today, and hope that the future works itself out. Others might have the position that "hope is not a strategy". They over save for retirement to give themselves a 99.8% success rate in their Monte Carlo simulator of choice. Many people fall somewhere between these two extremes.

What's the right answer? What's the right "rule" to use? I don't know. Nobody here knows. No math will solve this equation with certainty. If that's what you seek, good luck in that endeavor.

The truth is that the right answer usually only reveals itself on our deathbeds as we look back on our circumstances. But since we don't have time travel at our disposal, we just have to do our best to at least get close to the target.
