The Giants’ play-call that still has Matt Nagy ‘pissed’ a week later

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Matt Nagy could've summed up his message to the Giants defense in two words: My bad.

Still haunted a week later by two three-and-outs that kept the Commanders alive for a game-winning rally after two fourth-down interceptions could've sealed a win for the Giants, the offensive coordinator regrets most calling a quarterback sweep for J.J. McCarthy on third-and-3 that lost two yards and led to a punt.

"I'm pissed at myself for that play call," Nagy said before Thursday's practice. "Just to be real and put it out there. I know I can be better with that call. Now, have we practiced it a lot? Yeah, we have. But, in that moment, there's a balance of how aggressive we want to be versus conservative."

New York Giants Offensive Coordinator Matt Nagy speaks at a press conference on Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026, at Quest Diagnostics Training Center in East Rutherford, N.J. Robert Sabo for NY Post

Nagy was much more aggressive on third downs in Week 1 while running out the final 5:21 of a win against the Cowboys. The difference is he had Jaxson Dart under center then instead of McCarthy.

"There's no reason why we should lose that game after what our defense did with those two interceptions," Nagy said. "We need to end the game as an offense, and it starts with me making the right call in the right situation. I want to make sure I take that accountability with the guys. They know that."

John Harbaugh's coaching staff has placed an emphasis on accountability and have called out players when effort or execution lacks. It's a two-way street when a coach messes up.

Giants quarterback J.J. McCarthy (18) runs the ball past Washington Commanders linebacker Sonny Styles (52). AP Photo/Frank Franklin II

"Very clear," Nagy said. "That's important for them to know. It's not just that third-and-3 play. There are other plays throughout the game. If you can't take accountability or accept responsibility in this process, you'll never gain respect and trust from your peers. I wouldn't say that if I didn't believe it. But I'd also be a fool if I didn't learn from it. I need to be able to use that as a play-caller, let those guys see it, feel it and trust it, when the play is called they believe in it and make it happen, and we have a better success story."
