What we learned as De'Anthony Melton's sharp shot fuels Warriors' win vs. Kings

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

What we learned as De'Anthony Melton's sharp shot fuels Warriors' win vs. Kings originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SAN FRANCISCO – Thanksgiving dinner came early for the Warriors on Wednesday night as they feasted on the Sacramento Kings' defense early to enjoy a big enough lead that lasted throughout the game.

The Warriors scored just 88 points Sunday against the Boston Celtics. They then scored half of that in the first quarter against the Kings in an eventual 129-110 win at Chase Center.

After leading the Kings 44-19 through one quarter, the Warriors lost the final three quarters 91-85.

Overall, the Warriors shot 53.3 percent from the field, made 20 threes and tallied 37 assists with just 15 turnovers.

De'Anthony Melton led the way with 24 points, tying his career high of eight 3-pointers in a game. Melton in the Warriors' previous six games had made a total of eight threes.

This was not a one-man show, though. The Warriors had seven players score in double figures, and they won the bench battle 61-19 behind Gui Santos' 22 points.

Here are three takeaways from the Warriors' second straight win.

They play different positions and have much different roles, but whenever Yaxel Lendeborg plays another top pick making a name for himself, eyes instantly will turn to a rookie showdown. On Wednesday night, that meant pinning him against Kings rookie point guard Darius Acuff Jr.

The start of the game wasn't pretty for Lendeborg. Within the first four minutes, Lendeborg already airballed a three, was called for traveling and an offensive foul. He was replaced by Santos after the offensive foul, who instantly drained a 3-pointer.

However, Lendeborg did have three steals in the first quarter and was up to 11 points at halftime while Acuff was held to four.

Though Acuff (22 points) had more points than Lendeborg (15 points), the Warriors rookie had a much more efficient night. Lendeborg was a plus-8 thriving at the cup, as a cutter and screening for others, and Acuff was a minus-23, going a lowly 9 of 24 from the field and 3 of 12 beyond the arc.

The wait is over. Brandon Williams has been itching to make his return from a mild hamstring strain and immediately made an impact off the bench.

Williams watched the Warriors dominate the first quarter, and quickly took advantage of the Kings' lack of defense. On the first play of the second quarter, Williams used a ballscreen from Charles Bassey and a backscreen from Will Richard to blow by his defender and finish at the rim. He then went coast to coast, zooming past the Kings for another layup on the Warriors' next possession.

After a strong start, Williams missed his next four shots, failing to finish at the rim. A blocked shot on Keegan Murray got him going again in the third quarter, as Williams quickly converted on the other side.

Some rust had to be scraped off Williams throughout the night. His 14 points in 13 minutes and ability to attack the Kings' defense also played a big role in the Warriors' bench outscoring them by 42 points.

As the Warriors felt multiple times during their recent winless five-game road trip, this game was over in a hurry. It's a four-quarter game, but the doors were closed after the first quarter in favor of the Warriors. Melton catching fire from deep early was a big reason why.

His 3-point shooting was a huge plus to start the season as Melton had a 52.8 3-point percentage (19 of 36) through the first seven games of the season. Melton then went 0 of 9 over his next two games and has struggled to find his shot as of late. He missed all five of his 3-point attempts Sunday night against the Celtics, and then went 6 of 7 against the Kings in the first half, tying a season high for an entire game.

Melton made four of the Warriors' nine threes in the first quarter. The veteran combo guard then tacked on another two threes in the second quarter, and missed his only attempt in the third. The first time he fired from downtown in the fourth quarter might have been his most difficult attempt of the night, and it still swished through the net.

Scoring bursts always are an added bonus for Melton. Needing to find his way out of a funk, Melton's strong start propelled the Warriors to another home win.
