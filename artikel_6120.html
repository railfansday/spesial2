Air Canada Launches 12 New International Routes: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Air Canada is set to expand its international network considerably in 2027, with 12 new routes scheduled that were absent from its network in 2026. Aeroroutes reports that the additions will include nine routes that are entirely new to the airline and three returning services, including one that Canada's flag carrier last operated a quarter of a century ago.

Europe accounts for half of the additions, while Air Canada is also expanding its presence in the US, Mexico, China, and Israel. The growth is being facilitated by new aircraft deliveries, most notably the Airbus A321XLR. However, the airline's expanding international network is also benefiting from additional widebody capacity.

Montréal-Trudeau International Airport (YUL) is the biggest beneficiary of the expansion by route count, with six of the 12 additions. Toronto Pearson International Airport (YYZ) follows with four, while Vancouver and Edmonton each gain one. All but one of the routes will launch before the end of June, with Montréal–Seville following in September.

Of the three returning routes, Toronto–Louisville has had the longest absence, having last operated in June 2002. Toronto–Shannon returns after it only had a single summer season in 2018, when it was operated by the Boeing 737 MAX 8. Meanwhile, Montréal–Tel Aviv was inaugurated in June 2017 before being suspended in October 2023 following the outbreak of conflict in Israel.

Among the entirely new routes, Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport (CAN) will become Air Canada's third mainland Chinese destination, complementing Beijing and Shanghai. Basel will similarly become its third Swiss destination, while Seville will gain its first nonstop transatlantic service. Mark Galardo, Air Canada's Chief Commercial Officer, said the additions reflect the airline's ambitions to reach underserved markets:

"Summer 2027 marks the most extensive intercontinental expansion in Air Canada’s history. Enabled by the next-generation capabilities of our expanding Airbus A321XLR fleet, the likes of Seville builds upon recent additions to Palma de Mallorca and Tenerife."

Four of the 12 additions, including two-thirds of the new European routes, will be operated by the Airbus A321XLR. Air Canada took delivery of its first of 30 examples in April this year, with the aircraft making its transatlantic debut between Montréal and Toulouse in June. It has since enabled further expansion into secondary European markets.

Configured with 182 seats, including 14 lie-flat business class seats, the aircraft allows Air Canada to serve markets where demand might not justify a widebody. Speaking at last week's Scotiabank Transportation & Industrials Conference, CFO John Di Bert revealed that the airline's 2026 route launches were performing in the upper half of its margin range:

"They have been accretive new routes, and we are confident that the next batch of routes will also be accretive."

XLR Routes Introduced Or Scheduled In 2026

Berlin, Lisbon, Lyon, Nantes, Porto, Tenerife, Toulouse

Amsterdam, Basel, Dublin, Edinburgh, Seville

Air Canada currently plans to deploy the A321XLR on 20 transatlantic routes in 2027. Its role extends beyond opening new markets, with Air Canada also replacing some Boeing 787 services with A321XLRs. This releases widebody capacity for longer or higher-demand routes, particularly across the Pacific, where the Dreamliner is central to the airline's expansion.

The airline is optimizing its network and growing all-around, with both types becoming integral to its plans.

Air Canada's growth extends well beyond the 12 new routes. Next summer, it plans to operate more than 125 international routes outside of routes to the US, offering up to 169,000 weekly seats across more than 85 destinations. This represents an increase of over 8% in available seat miles (ASMs), driven partly by increased frequencies and extended seasonal services.

Alongside additional A321XLR deliveries, the imminent arrival of Air Canada's Boeing 787-10s will provide further opportunities for international expansion. The airline has 14 examples on order, with two scheduled for delivery before the end of this year and six more in 2027. Management expects approximately two-thirds of next year's incremental capacity to be allocated to international markets.

Under its New Frontiers growth strategy, Air Canada is targeting more than 130 billion ASMs by 2030, up from approximately 108 billion this year. Having already substantially expanded its international network in 2026, the carrier has indicated that further network announcements will accompany its incoming aircraft deliveries. As such, the 12 additions reported here are likely just the starting point for an even fuller 2027 expansion.
