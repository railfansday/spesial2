Pro Golfer Arrested, Accused of Attempting to Smuggle 111lbs of Drugs in Luggage Into Nigeria

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

South African professional golfer Henlo Grobler has been arrested at an airport in Nigeria, according to authorities.

Nigeria's National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) said in a statement on Sunday, November 22, that Grobler, 37, and a Kenyan chef, Abubakar Faiza Njambi, 25, had been taken into custody after allegedly attempting to smuggle 111 pounds (50.40 kilograms) of strong cannabis called "Loud" into the country.

It is unclear what charges Grobler, who is a member of the Professional Golfers Association South Africa, could face. As of Monday, November 23, no plea or update has been publicly reported in the case. Under the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency Act, marijuana is illegal in Nigeria and trafficking convictions can carry a sentence of up to life in prison.

Grobler was arrested by operatives of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) at the Murtala Muhammed International Airport (MMIA) in Ikeja, Lagos, on October 11.

The golf pro had been flying in business class from South Africa before he was intercepted at the Terminal 2 arrival hall. Authorities allegedly discovered two boxes upon searching Grobler, which had 46 parcels of "Loud."

The boxes had been tagged with Njambi's name while she allegedly took two boxes tagged with Grobler's name from the baggage claim area and left the airport, as per the NDLEA.

"The swap of luggage tags was a deliberate ploy by the syndicate to frustrate the identification of ownership, so that each courier could deny the boxes bearing his or her name if intercepted," the agency claimed.

According to the NDLEA statement, during a preliminary interview, Grobler claimed he was "recruited by a South African associate" and introduced to other people of the syndicate "he never met in person."

He was allegedly picked up from his home in South Africa by a driver who "handed him four traveling boxes and took him to the airport, where he was instructed to meet the Kenyan lady and swap luggage tags with her."

The NDLEA claimed that "tracking devices had been installed on the traveling boxes as well as on his mobile phone," showing "how closely the cartels monitor their couriers and consignments."

"He claimed he was promised 5,000 South African Rand [$287] upon successful delivery," the statement added.

Njambi, meanwhile, was arrested days later, on October 15, at the boarding gate of Lagos airport as she attempted to board a South African Airways flight to return to Kenya. Njambi's passport had been profiled and placed on the NDLEA watchlist following Grobler's arrest.

The chef claimed she left Kenya for South Africa on October 4. She then "stayed in a hotel for about a week, and met the golfer at the airport in South Africa on 11th October, where he handed her two of the four boxes before they checked them in under their respective luggage tags," as per the NDLEA statement.

Grobler's arrest comes after a basketball player from the United States allegedly tried to smuggle 75 pounds of marijuana into Nigeria earlier this month.

A man identified as Greene Da'monte Khiree flew into Lagos from Thailand on November 11 and was arrested when he tried to leave the airport after abandoning two checked bags, NDLEA announced.

The bags contained 32 parcels of marijuana and a tracking device, according to the agency.
