Bill Belichick is 'bad, bad.' UNC football must consider a $10 million decision

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

North Carolina lost by 17 points to Virginia, and that didn't even supply the most stinging rebuke of the day of Bill Belichick's program.

That came in Wake Forest's blowout win against Georgia Tech.

Wake Forest's Gio Lopez keeps surging as a Heisman Trophy contender. All Lopez needed to do was get away from Belichick.

After Lopez transferred, he described his experience playing for Belichick in 2025 as being in a program with "no air."

Indeed, the Belichick balloon has deflated at North Carolina (5-6).

If you're wondering, playoff contending Wake Forest (10-1) hired Jake Dickert exactly one week after UNC hired Belichick in 2024.

There's your direct contrast between what it means to get a hire right, versus what it means to whiff.

In Belichick's succinct six-minute news conference after the 41-24 loss to Virginia, he used the word "bad" nine times to sum up how his team played.

"Bad, bad performance all the way around," he said. "Outplayed, out-coached, out-everythinged."

UNC wasn't helped in this Virginia loss by the absence of starting quarterback Billy Edwards Jr., out with an injury. Replacement Travis Burgess threw two interceptions in his first career start, but he wasn't the primary issue.

North Carolina did not portray a well-coached team. The Tar Heels suffered from special teams mistakes, and Virginia repeatedly gutted UNC with explosive plays.

UNC must have thought it was getting a defensive guru when it hired Belichick off the NFL scrap heap. Virginia shredded the guru's defense for 464 yards.

What did the Cavaliers do to give UNC fits?

North Carolina's administration must consider whether this is bad enough to fire Belichick after two lackluster seasons.

Belichick turned down the temperature on his hot seat by beating rival Duke last month. All he's done since then is lose four of his past five games. He'll need to beat NC State for the Tar Heels to make a bowl game.

Put aside all of the off-field drama, and there are football reasons to question the longevity of this tenure. North Carolina is only marginally improved from last season. With a recruiting class ranked No. 50 nationally on the way, even the staunchest Belichick acolyte must be losing faith.

By firing Mack Brown and hiring Belichick, North Carolina took an average program and made it worse.

Brown never won fewer than six games in any of his six seasons in his sequel stint at UNC. He also never embarrassed the university. Can't say the same for Belichick, whose relationship with Jordon Hudson introduced "Happy BANniversary" into college football's lexicon.

Makes you wonder whether UNC would've been better off sweeping Belichick out with Michael Lombardi and Belichick's son, Steve, two months ago.

With Belichick's tenure seemingly hanging in the balance in September, the university opted to back its head coach. A university investigation into the culture and climate of Belichick's program uncovered problems, but UNC deemed there was no "systemic failure" but rather some "isolated" misconduct.

After the investigation concluded, Chancellor Lee Roberts said Belichick "continues to have our support."

When the day comes that Belichick no longer has UNC's support, how much will it need to pay to be rid of him? He'd be owed a buyout of $10.8 million after this season if he's fired immediately without cause. The language within UNC's post-investigation statement in September made it sound as if the university would not be keen on pursuing a for-cause firing of Belichick.

That matched expert lawyer Martin Greenberg's read on the situation when I spoke with Greenberg in September, after Lombardi and Steve Belichick had resigned, and UNC's investigation had concluded.

"Based on my review and looking at the literature as far as (the controversies under Belichick), I don't think there's a sustainable termination for cause," Greenberg, a former law professor at Marquette, told me earlier this season.

In other words, if UNC wants Belichick out this year, he'd be owed severance.

There remains, however, another option, and this is perhaps the most logical route. If UNC fires Belichick after next season, he'd be owed no buyout.

Belichick's contract is uniquely structured so that the final two seasons of his five-year deal are backed by no severance.

His $10 million buyout after this season would dip to $0 on Dec. 31, 2027.

"If they want to get rid of him (this year), they're going to end up paying this guy," Greenberg said, when we spoke earlier this season. "Or, they're going to end up putting up with whatever the antics are until next year, where they don't have to pay him if they terminate him."

The pool of strong replacement candidates likely would be pretty shallow in this carousel cycle if the Tar Heels job opened.

Is UNC willing to endure 13 more months of mediocrity to avoid paying Belichick failure money? If that's the plan, Belichick would need another boatload of transfers to have a chance at being more competitive next season.

But, that raises another question: Who would want to play for a program with no air?
