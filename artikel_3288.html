Forget 'Slow Horses,' This Sci-Fi Thriller Is Gary Oldman’s Greatest Role

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

If you only know Gary Oldman as the lethargic Jackson Lamb in TV hit Slow Horses, it may shock you to know that much of his career was spent bringing to life characters who were high energy, operatic, and filled every moment of screentime with their voice. There’s no greater example of this than The Fifth Element, Luc Besson’s 1997 sci-fi thriller that became an instant classic thanks to its unique vision of the future and memorable performances. As Jean-Baptiste Emanuel Zorg, a flamboyant industrialist working for the film’s evil forces, he becomes larger-than-life in the best way possible. And with the film charting among Tubi's top 10 most-watched films, now's the perfect time to revisit.

Set in the 23rd century, the film stars Bruce Willis as Korben Dallas, a New York City taxi driver and former special forces operative who is stunned when a mysterious woman named Leeloo (Milla Jovovich) falls into his cab. He learns that Leeloo is the key to fighting a great evil that is making its way toward Earth, and uniting her with stones representing the four classical elements is the only way to save humanity. With the government, as well as crazed mercenary Zorg (Oldman), on their tail, Dallas must do everything he can to save Leeloo, and become the hero he was always meant to be.

In a summer that featured dinosaurs (Jurassic Park: The Lost World), aliens (Men in Black), and kick-ass presidents (Air Force One), the French filmmaker's whimsical sci-fi tale stood out amongst traditional Hollywood fare to create a worldwide hit that retained the record for admissions in France for over a decade. The film's impact was largely due to its unique look, which captivated audiences regardless of whether they knew of Besson or understood the film's concept. Those visuals were the product of Besson collaborating with equally imaginative co-creators.

Having made his first American-set movie with Léon: The Professional, Besson would go on to develop a story he first created when he was just 16 years old. To match his unique story, filled with prophecy, philosophy, and unknown cultures, he hired artists who would make the visuals unlike anything previously seen on the big screen. For the production design, he recruited French comic creators Jean Giraud and Jean-Claude Mézières to envision 23rd century New York City, where classic iconography met eye-popping futurism. This was symbolized by Korben Dallas’ flying yellow taxi cab, and skyscrapers that seemed to have no beginning or end.

The groundbreaking costumes, such as Leeloo’s orange suspender ensemble and the wild leopard print chic of radio host Ruby Rhod (Chris Tucker), were designed by celebrated fashion guru Jean Paul Gaultier and gave every character their own unique style. Besson already knew how to tell a compelling action story, having built his name on movies such as La Femme Nikita and Subway. By bringing in these iconoclastic artists, he would make every frame look like a comic book come to life, giving both the sci-fi and action genres a new perspective.

The counter to Willis’ sardonic, laid-back hero is Oldman’s Zorg, a character who doesn’t have a drop of subtlety in his blood stream. Elaborately dressed and prone to violent tantrums, Oldman plays Zorg with a manic intensity that borders on cartoonish. Parading around his premises like an emperor, he tries to dominate everyone he comes across, betraying his cowardice only when presented with an opponent he truly can’t overcome. The emotions are right on the surface, reflecting the actor’s previous collaboration with Besson in Léon: The Professional, where he played the corrupt, abusive, and unhinged police officer Norman Stansfield.

In a film where the effects could easily dominate proceedings, Oldman's magnetic presence becomes one of the film’s highlights. As well as giving a human face to the threat Dallas faces, he also provides a villain that feels right at home in the gaudy excess of the future that Besson brings us. A vain capitalist who is used to everything going his way, there is something in his portrayal of a powerful man with deep-seated insecurities that will resonate with today’s society as much as it did in 1997.

The mark of a great actor is the ability to tell a story in multiple ways, and Gary Oldman has made a career of sidestepping expectations. Jean-Baptiste Emanuel Zorg could not be further away from Jackson Lamb, and yet both characters showcase the commitment and ability of a star who can find a way to become part of whatever world he finds himself in. If you’ve just finished the latest season of the Apple TV hit and can’t wait for the next, check out this cult classic to see just how far your favorite spy’s range can stretch.
