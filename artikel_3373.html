How ‘GTA VI’ Got Its Vice City Sound

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Five years is a long time to wait for another hit. In Grand Theft Auto VI, that’s where we find Bae-Luxe and Roxy, the longtime friends who rap as Real Dimez in the game’s story­line. They’ve signed with a fictional label called Only Raw Records after an early success with local rapper DWNPLY. Their new label boss, Dre’Quan Priest, used to book acts at his business partner Boobie Ike’s strip club. Dre’Quan has a sensible enough approach to finding records: “Dancers are like my A&Rs,” he says in one scene.

GTA has been funny about the rap business for years. In 2004’s San Andreas, players stole a rhyme book from Madd Dogg, a star voiced by Ice-T, so that the hapless OG Loc could have a career. DJ Pooh helped write the game, and Young Maylay and MC Eiht voiced CJ and Ryder. Even before that, GTA III’s Game Radio FM had real-life DJs Stretch Armstrong and Lord Sear playing songs by real-life MCs Royce da 5’9″ and Sean Price. Recognizing a voice on the radio was part of getting comfortable in a city you were supposed to tear apart.

In the game’s previous installment, GTA V, released in 2013, the music took on a new dimension as influential figures in hip-hop and R&B helped curate the sound of the game. DJ Pooh hosted a ­station called West Coast Classics; Los Angeles radio veteran Big Boy was the voice you heard on Radio Los Santos. In 2017, Frank Ocean brought his Blonded radio show to the game’s ever-expanding ­universe. His station perhaps sums up GTA’s musical sensibility as it’s taken shape over the years: Future’s “Codeine Crazy” fits naturally on the same dial as Aphex Twin and Curtis Mayfield.

By 2021, Dr. Dre was releasing six new songs through GTA Online: The Contract, a downloadable content update in which players chased down a stolen phone containing his unreleased music; Eminem and Nipsey Hussle were among the featured guests on the records. For the return to Vice City, as Rockstar Games has long called its in-game version of Miami, Trick Daddy and Trina will host a channel called Dirty South Classics — including JT Money and Solé’s 1999 hit “Who Dat” and DJ Khaled’s 2006 song “Born-N-Raised,” with verses from Trick Daddy, Rick Ross, and Pitbull. Slip-N-Slide Records founder Ted Lucas helped curate the station, CBS Miami reported. And the six stations that were previewed make up only part of the lineup you’ll hear in the new Vice City.

This is familiar work for the pair, who have been ­representing Miami to the world for years, and who teamed up to launch an ­actual morning show on the city’s 99 Jamz radio in 2020. Their voices ought to make Vice City feel a little less invented. “We’ve lived this culture, we helped shape it, and now ­people around the world get to ­experience some of that ­history and energy with us,” ­Trina adds.
