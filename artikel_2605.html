Steelers stock report after 38-18 loss to the Bengals

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

The Pittsburgh Steelers were absolutely dominated by the Cincinnati Bengals on Sunday Night Football by a score of 38-18. The final score doesn't do it justice, as Pittsburgh scored most of their points after the game was already out of reach. The Bengals outgained the Steelers by 241 yards and picked up 30 first downs compared to Pittsburgh's 17.

As always, here is my weekly stock report from the Steelers' third loss of the season.

Well, this game does not bode well for the Steelers' chances at winning the AFC North. The Steelers were outplayed and outcoached for three straight hours on Sunday Night. The Steelers' lack of weapons and overall passing game showed up big in this one. It certainly didn't help that their defense did not show up in this one either.

On a night where not much went right at any phase, I came away feeling fairly positively about the linebackers. Queen posted the Steelers' lone sack of the contest. Wilson was all over the field and once again led the team in tackles. Both Queen and Wilson continue to put together their best season in black and gold under new defensive coordinator Patrick Graham.

Joe Burrow and the Bengals offense threw the ball all around the yard. Rookie third-round pick Daylen Everette eventually replaced Samuel Jr. in the third quarter. Samuel Jr. returned to the field in the fourth quarter, where he promptly let up a 42-yard catch to Ja'Marr Chase. The Steelers traded their best corner early in the season and did nothing to address it at the deadline, tonight was a cold reminder of that.

The Steelers' blitz had been dominant over the course of their four-game winning streak, but that was far from the case tonight. Joe Burrow was able to quickly diagnose the blitz and pick apart the Steelers' secondary. What was a reliable formula so far this season was not the answer tonight, as Burrow finished the game with 409 yards and four passing touchdowns.

Aaron Rodgers started this game 0/7 passing. Admittedly, it wasn't all his fault, which is why we will talk about the entire offense here. The Steelers' passing game is incredibly limited, and not really built to play against teams like Cincinnati that can light up the scoreboard. Their receivers struggle to separate, and that won't get better over the course of their last eight games. The Bengals were more than prepared for the Steelers' run game, which they didn't even try to establish until they were already down multiple scores. From the quarterback to the playmakers to the coaching, something is going to need to change drastically to take this team seriously against any quality competition.
