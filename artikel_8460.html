Personal Finance (Not Investing) • Re: Roth conversion at 10% bracket seems like a no-brainer

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Please check my assumption.
We are unlikely to greatly benefit from large Roth conversions, but it seems like a no-brainer to do Roth conversion into the 10% bracket. This makes the conversion a wash, 10% tax now verses 10% later for a tIRA withdraw. I know inflation, etc. can all skew this, but I'm not seeing a downside. We should have a couple of years with almost zero income (no SS) so we will do up to the standard deduction for sure because that's free money, but the extra 10% also seems like discounted money? RMDs and SS will likely just push us into what is the 22% today, and 24% for a widow.

We have a small amount of Roth now about 4%, so adding a little more wouldn't hurt.
Married, already retired, higher earner not yet claiming SS benefits.

I've planned to age 100 for my wife and 85 for me. Family history
suggests these numbers. This means my wife will file
single with large RMDs if we don't pare down tax deferred.

For me, conversions to the top of the 12% bracket is optimal
up to age 75. Pralana agrees with me under my conservative
assumptions.

This produces the most disposable income after taxes and
medical. The conversions are only a few thousand a year above
withdrawals needed for living expenses.

We are already beyond the threshold for 85% of SS being taxable,
so each extra thousand only generates an extra $1200 in taxes that we would have to pay eventually.

By making the conversion from 65 to 75, the tax deferred is reduced to the point where we can then drop back to just withdrawing the RMD, which allows less than 85% of SS to be taxed while we are both alive and for the survivor.

An advantage of this is that we can direct tax deferred accounts to
just distribute the RMD, both while I am alive and after I pass.
This plus SS and a small annuity will meet bare bones expenses,
and my wife can pull from Roth for discretionary spending.
