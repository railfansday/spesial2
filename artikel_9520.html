Shared PostgreSQL Container vs Per-App Databases in Docker Compose (Measured)

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Posted on Nov 24 • Originally published at vpsfolio.com

Per-container RAM: gitea 180.4 MB · umami 150.5 MB · caddy 42.5 MB · db 28.1 MB (sandbox verified 2026-11-24)

View the interactive benchmark plate and full VPS sizing comparison or compare all measured stacks on VPSFolio Benchmarks.

Two apps, one database engine. Gitea and Umami both speak PostgreSQL, both are happy on 16, and both sit idle most of the day. On a 1 GB VPS the question is not whether you can run two Postgres containers — you can — but whether the extra ~30 MB per idle instance is worth the operational isolation you give up.

This stack puts a single postgres:16-alpine container behind both apps. An inline init script creates two databases (gitea, umami) and two least-privilege roles (gitea_user, umami_user) with separate passwords pulled from the environment. Caddy fronts both apps on 127.0.0.1:18080 and routes by Host header, so git.local hits Gitea on :3000 and analytics.local hits Umami on :3000.

The trade-off is real: both apps now share a Postgres major version. If Umami someday requires 17 and Gitea pins 16, you either fork the container or migrate one app out. That is the cost of the RAM savings. The measured numbers below make the decision concrete.

Sizing note: in our sandbox run, the single shared postgres:16-alpine container idled at 28.1 MB RSS serving both Gitea and Umami. Compare that against our verified solo guides, where separate PostgreSQL containers consumed 70.6 MB combined (24.2 MB for Gitea's postgres:16-alpine plus 46.4 MB for Umami's postgres:15-alpine). On a 1 GB VPS, saving ~42.5 MB (roughly 4% of RAM) is modest for two apps, though it compounds if you pack four or more Postgres-backed services onto one box.

The init SQL only runs when pg_data is empty. If you change it later, you must recreate the volume — which wipes both apps. Plan accordingly.

2. Ports. Publish 80 and 443 on Caddy instead of the loopback-only 18080:

3. Gitea URLs and Caddy cert persistence. Set GITEA_DOMAIN=git.example.com in .env, change GITEA__server__ROOT_URL in compose.yml from http:// to https://${GITEA_DOMAIN}/, and add named volumes caddy_data:/data and caddy_config:/config to caddy (plus top-level volumes:) so Let's Encrypt certificates persist across container recreates.

For remote backups, add a pg_dump sidecar or a cron job on the host. A minimal dump of both databases:

Ship those to object storage with rclone or restic. Do not rely on the volume alone.

Upgrades. Postgres major upgrades are the sharp edge here. pg_upgrade works, but you are upgrading both apps at once. Test on a copy of the volume first. Gitea and Umami both tolerate 16 today; if either pins a different major later, split the database out for that app and accept the extra ~30 MB.

Blast radius. One Postgres means one failure domain. A corrupted pg_data takes down both apps. Mitigate with pg_dump on a schedule and a tested restore path. Restore into a scratch container before you trust the backup:

Connection limits. Default max_connections is 100. Gitea and Umami together use a handful. If you add more apps, watch pg_stat_activity and raise the limit in postgresql.conf via command: in the compose file rather than editing the volume.

Measured RAM: shared vs. per-app PostgreSQL. Here is the side-by-side comparison from our disposable Docker sandbox runs:

Consolidating two PostgreSQL instances into one saved 42.5 MB on the database tier (from 70.6 MB down to 28.1 MB) — coincidentally matching the 42.5 MB idle footprint of Caddy itself in this run, so the complete 4-container proxied stack (401.5 MB) still sat 8.7 MB below the two unproxied separate-DB stacks (410.2 MB). Even if both apps already ran postgres:16-alpine (~24–28 MB each), sharing only frees ~20–25 MB (~2% of a 1 GB VPS). For two apps, separate Postgres containers are usually worth that small tax; sharing starts paying real dividends once you run four or more Postgres-backed services on one small VPS.

When to split. Split when: the apps need different Postgres majors, one app's load starves the other, or compliance requires separate database instances. Until then, one container with two roles is the simpler operational story on a 1 GB VPS.

Measured in our sandbox run: total memory 401.5 MB (sum of per-container peaks over a 30s idle window after startup) across 4 containers; largest container 180.4 MB; published port(s) 18080; HTTP probe returned 200, 200. The run took 113.0s including the image download. Required secret(s) APP_SECRET, GITEA_DB_PASSWORD, GITEA_INTERNAL_TOKEN, GITEA_JWT_SECRET, GITEA_SECRET_KEY, POSTGRES_SUPERUSER_PASSWORD, TWO_FACTOR_ENCRYPTION_KEY, UMAMI_DB_PASSWORD were generated for the test run, as the tutorial instructs. Container breakdown: gitea 180.4 MB, umami 150.5 MB, caddy 42.5 MB, db 28.1 MB. Verified routes: Gitea on shared Postgres (HTTP 200), Umami on shared Postgres (HTTP 200).

Verification: we ran this docker-compose stack in a disposable Docker environment and recorded the measurements above. They describe that run, not a guarantee for your host.
