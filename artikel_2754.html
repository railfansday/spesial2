The Seahawks offense is not all Brian Fleury’s fault

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

The Seattle Seahawks bye week has come and gone, and now the Super Bowl champions must begin a sprint to the finish line to make the playoffs. While the defense has remained incredibly difficult to crack (except in the red zone, bizarrely), the offense has been inconsistent at best and self-destructive at its absolute worst. If the Seahawks fail to reach the postseason, the failures of the offense will likely be the main culprit.

First-year offensive coordinator Brian Fleury has come under heavy criticism for his unit's performance. Despite retaining most of last year's Super Bowl offense (with one very notable exception), the Seahawks have not performed nearly as well as they did under Klint Kubiak in 2025. It's certainly not inconceivable that Fleury is a one-and-done OC if things don't take a positive turn over the final seven weeks of the regular season.

And yet, as we have previously written, the cracks in Seattle's offense did not start with Fleury. And we have numbers to back it up.

A fantastic article by Ted Nguyen and Seahawks beat reporter Andrew Destin of The Athletic outlined the quantifiable decline in Seattle's passing game. The inflection point is the loss to the Los Angeles Rams, in which Sam Darnold was picked off four times and the offense didn't score a touchdown until late in the fourth quarter. It was a game that looked nowhere near the red-hot previous 10 weeks in which Sam Darnold looked like a darkhorse MVP candidate. The Rams might have lost subsequent games to the Seahawks, but they laid out the blueprint for things to come.

The good news is Seattle, believe it or not, is statistically faring better through 2026 than the back-end of 2025. It is nowhere close to the gaudiness of Weeks 1-10 of last year. You know what changed? The coverage. More specifically, the evidence is clear that Seahawks opponents are leveling up on their two-high shells. Again, the EPA/pass is better than how 2025 ended but not at the same level of Weeks 1-10 2025.

The article doesn't have the splits between Drew Lock and Sam Darnold when factoring in the passing stats, although I suspect Lock is carrying the bulk of the success by virtue of not turning the ball over. What is clear is that the passing offense, even with Jaxon Smith-Njigba performing like he's completely solved football, is not very explosive.

You know what can help replace some of the lack of explosives in the passing game? Running the ball better against two-high looks and lighter boxes. Seattle was not necessarily otherworldly efficient from Week 11 through the Super Bowl, but the explosives went up and the negative rushes went down. In 2026? Not explosive, not efficient, not good at all.

Such is life when Kenneth Walker walks and the only plan at running back was hoping Jadarian Price, Emanuel Wilson, and George Holani could hold down the fort until Zach Charbonnet came back. Price got injured, Wilson is a capable back but not an explosive threat, and Holani is a fine third-down option who has a single 10+ yard run to his name. Charbonnet and Price are thankfully healthy and have both had good moments against very bad defenses, and they will be relied upon heavily in the home stretch.

I don't think that Brian Fleury should be treated as the main culprit for an offense that was wobbling under Klint Kubiak. Does anyone here remember the zero touchdowns scored against the Philip Rivers-led Indianapolis Colts? Or the 6-6 halftime score against the Atlanta Falcons? Or going 3-3 at the half versus the Carolina Panthers? Or not scoring an offensive touchdown until garbage time against the Minnesota Vikings? That's what the Seahawks offense looked like for most of the end of last season. They literally won the Super Bowl scoring only one offensive touchdown while Sam Darnold could only complete half of his 38 pass attempts!

Fleury can take culpability, however, for the sloppiness of the operation. Seattle is on pace for more false starts than all of last year (playoffs included). Fleury is the one constantly calling first-and-goal run plays to mostly unsatisfying results. Ted Nguyen's article highlighted ongoing issues that ultimately fall at this feet:

The offense has also seen a lack of precision and detail, leading to poor spacing on pass concepts, shoddy blocking and turnovers. Taking care of the details is imperative to their improvement.

But perhaps another aspect of the offense's struggles is the harsh truth about Seattle's receiver room, which looked like a deep and talented group but isn't performing like one. Nguyen's assessment of Seattle's non-JSN targets stops short of being scathing but the stats seem telling:

Smith-Njigba is still one of the best receivers against man, but the rest of the team has struggled. Last season, every skill player other than Smith-Njigba averaged -0.04 EPA per target with an explosive rate of 11.9 percent. This season, they average -0.19 EPA per target with an explosive play rate of 7.8 percent.

The Seahawks do not have a second receiver who can reliably beat man coverage on his own. Shaheed is a gadget player who can win deep occasionally. Kupp has been efficient, but he's too slow to be a high-volume target against man. Horton flashed last season, but he's struggled to create separation. Barner can be a mismatch against some teams but hasn't had enough plays designed for him this season.

The Seahawks' auxiliary players are what they are, and they need help from the scheme to get open.

Fleury doesn't appear to be doing enough to counter man coverage by scheming guys open, which the Seahawks have struggled with all season, but if your receiving group outside of JSN constantly needs scheme help to be effective, you've got a bigger problem on your hands. I don't think it's far-fetched to say that Seattle's wide receiver group could and arguably should look very different next year given Kupp's sunsetting career and the disappointing start to Rashid Shaheed's new contract.

The Seahawks offense isn't functioning well in part because opposing defenses (well, good ones at least) have long figured out how to slow them down for the better part of 1.5 seasons. It was happening under Kubiak but the Seahawks kept winning anyway, and indeed they still had some brilliant playoff performances, more specifically the NFC Championship. Injuries, offensive line struggles, and having a poor run game for most of this season have exacerbated the problems, and they have certainly not helped Sam Darnold in any of his starts. Drew Lock has admittedly passed his tests in Darnold's absence, but it'd be very optimistic to assume that he'll continue playing well when he's not facing teams whose coordinators have since been fired.

The fact that Darnold's once gaudy numbers have dipped under two coordinators once teams started to increase two-high shells may also speak to an exposing of Darnold himself. Getting "greedy" cost Seattle a win against the Denver Broncos when Darnold was picked off forcing a bad ball to Shaheed against a Cover 2 look. If this is what nerfs Darnold consistently regardless of coordinator, then we've somehow managed a new version of the collapse of Let Russ Cook. Maybe that costs this Brian his job, too.

I'm not saying Brian Fleury is faultless nor am I going to push back on those who want him fired, but these problems did not just show up overnight, and Fleury hasn't exactly been put in favorable spots considering the season-long injury woes. Frankly, I think I've had my fill of several years worth of constantly blaming the coordinator for the shortcomings of the incumbent quarterback. The question is whether or not Fleury (and, frankly, anyone in Seattle's QB room) should be the one to solve these issues beyond 2026.
