‘Never seen anything like it’ – Stephen A. Smith says NBA player is stealing a living

Tanggal: 2026-11-04

Stephen A. Smith has officially reached his breaking point with NBA injury absences.

The NBA's injury bug has gotten completely out of hand, and fans are frankly tired of checking injury reports just to see if a game is worth turning on. Night after night, the best matchups go missing as guys like Anthony Edwards and Tyrese Haliburton find themselves sidelined.

Even LeBron chimed in, admitting the level of conditioning guys need just to survive today's speed is crazy. The problem is so obvious that Adam Silver is actually floating 40-minute games to keep players in one piece. But shaving off a few minutes won't magically solve how punishing this game has gotten.

Taking to his YouTube show, Stephen A. Smith went completely off on Kawhi Leonard. He's clearly fed up with Kawhi living on the injury report, looking straight into the camera and insisting the Raptors star is robbing the franchise by pocketing hundreds of millions while barely seeing the floor.

He said: "If he heard about an injured list, he was going to make sure he's on it. If he heard about it, he was going to make sure he's on it. So, we knew he was going to be on it. Now, the excuse this time is swelling in his left knee. I know it's legit. I'm not accusing him of faking it. I'm just saying perpetually there's always some injury with him. I'm telling y'all right now.

"The man is an absolute pro of getting paid without working. I've never seen anything like it in my life. I've never seen anything like it ever. It's just unbelievable. I want him to give classes on that. This guy is something special. How do I get paid without working? Kawhi Leonard. That's all you got to name the class."

Kawhi actually made his return debut for Toronto in late October, only for his knee to flare up immediately after.

It's the exact same nightmare that plagued him with the Spurs and Clippers. He's unbelievable when healthy, but forever betrayed by chronic inflammation and surgeries that keep him sidelined.
