I'm trying to wrap my head around the differences between TPAW and SPAW. Outside of the asset allocation adjusting based on future income, it also adjusts away from stocks and into bonds based on CAPE ratio, correct? Is there a formula or methodology somewhere that explains how much it adjusts?
Yes, TPAW adjusts asset allocation based on future income and the equity premium. At the default setting, the equity premium is estimated using TIPS yields for the expected return of bonds and CAPE based estimates for the expected return of stocks. The asset allocation is calculated using Merton's formula, which takes into account risk aversion, the equity premium and stock volatility. Merton's formulas is available here: Merton's portfolio problem. For these formulas entered into a spreadsheet, see this post.
Do you have a recommendation of TPAW vs SPAW (or risk setting for TPAW) for someone who is retiring early, such as in their 40s, who can more easily return to work if their portfolio starts heading south? I currently have TPAW with a 19 risk setup, and it's suggesting 21% stocks, which seems way too conservative to me. Even if I turn the risk up all the way, it's suggesting only 44% stocks. Does that seem appropriate for someone in their 40s considering retirement? The only fixed expense I entered was my mortgage, which is only about 10% of the suggested monthly withdrawal.
Early retirement doesn't impact the decision very much. It's more about what you believe about expected returns and whether you're comfortable with changing asset allocation targets.

TPAW's asset allocation will be countercyclical if the expected returns are based on TIPS yields and CAPE (as in the default). This is why the default setting gives bond heavy allocations right now. Bond yields are high (3.2% real)—higher in fact than the historical average of 2.8% real. Conversely stock valuations have soared leading to earnings yields much below the historical average. If you believe that this means that the equity premium is low right now, then TPAW's countercyclical asset allocation may be right for you. If you don't believe that, you can change the expected return assumptions (in Advanced -> Expected Returns and Volatility) to reflect your beliefs, including fixed expected returns that aren't based on current valuations. The asset allocation will still move around with portfolio performance if there is future income acting as bonds. (This post goes over an example of why that is, and why it's useful.) If instead, you prefer the simplicity of a predetermined asset allocation, then use SPAW.
