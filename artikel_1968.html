Sources: Tottenham's chances of signing Georges Mikautadze in January revealed

Tottenham Hotspur will find it difficult to prise striker Georges Mikautadze away from Villarreal in January, sources have told Football Insider.

The Lilywhites are chasing a deal for the 26-year-old alongside Everton, with both clubs hoping to bolster their attacking options this winter.

However, Villarreal are reluctant to lose their frontman in the middle of the campaign and are set to make life difficult for Spurs to snatch him away.

Sources say that the La Liga side are under no pressure to cash in on Mikautadze, and won’t even consider a sale while they remain in the Champions League.

Tottenham are expected to have money to spend in January once again, but may even struggle to tempt Villarreal with a big offer.

Mikautadze is one of the first names on the team sheet for the Yellow Submarine, after notching 15 goals and six assists in all competitions last term.

Villarreal’s participation in the Champions League is creating a huge hurdle for Tottenham to overcome if they want to sign Mikautadze in January.

Inigo Perez’s side have struggled in Europe’s elite club competition this term, having won just one of their opening four league phase games so far.

They also have difficult tasks against Fenerbahce and Manchester United on the horizon before their league phase campaign ends on 27 January.

If the Spanish side finish in the bottom 12 of the Champions League table as expected, then it could leave Spurs with a last-minute window to be able to sign Mikautadze ahead of deadline day.

However, it would have to be a truly swift move for the 26-year-old, given that the winter window is due to close for Premier League clubs on 2 February.

Former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness told Football Insider that Tottenham are only likely to have made a few hundred thousand in revenue from the England team using their Hotspur Way training ground last week.

Thomas Tuchel’s side prepared for their game at home to Croatia by training on Spurs’ pitches, and staying in the hotel at their training ground.

That has been a regular arrangement between the two parties in recent years, but Wyness believes it will not be a money-spinning deal for Spurs by any means.

He pointed out that the money netted from England’s stays in North London will have barely scratched the surface when compared to what Spurs’ facility cost to build.
