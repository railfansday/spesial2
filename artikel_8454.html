Personal Investments • Re: Optimizing donation strategy via TLH and DAFs?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

I’ve been getting into donating appreciated shares and discovered this is better than donating cash. I typically give $40k–$50k a year, mostly to one large nonprofit, and I have roughly $100k–$200k in appreciated holdings in my taxable brokerage accounts.
I’m also buying about $50k a year in taxable accounts to build up future lots for giving. The idea is to donate appreciated lots once I’ve held them for more than a year and harvest losses on lots that are down when it makes sense.

I’m curious how others handle a few things.

1. Ongoing purchases and tax-loss harvesting. If you buy regularly while also harvesting losses, how do you handle the timing of your purchases and avoid wash sales? Do you have a simple rule for which lots to donate, harvest, or keep?

2. Daffy vs. Fidelity Charitable. I currently use Fidelity Charitable and generally make one grant a year to the same nonprofit, so the money doesn’t sit in the DAF for long. For anyone with a similar giving pattern, did Daffy offer enough of an advantage to switch? Has anyone found it simpler to donate shares directly to the nonprofit?

3. Market downturns. My older lots have substantial gains, but a downturn could shrink the dollar value available to give and leave newer lots underwater. At some point I will "run out" of older lots. If you aim to give about the same dollar amount each year, do you set aside some cash for giving or simply use older lots as needed?

I realize this is hyperoptimizing, but it's fun - curious on other's thoughts on this?
I do the same.

1. I handle the timing to avoid wash sales. It's easier if you don't buy too regularly. Actually, livesoft is right as usual. Charitable giving has pretty much nothing to do with wash sales. But you might just be talking about the TLHing part.
2. I use Vanguard. But the other two are excellent and have lower minimums which is helpful for many people. Doubt I'd bother switching.
3. I'd give appreciated shares before I gave cash. When I didn't have appreciated shares, I gave cash.

We're currently hyperoptimizing on the charity side too so don't beat yourself up too much. But we think using a charitable trust for a unique situation might result in us being able to give substantially more to charity over our lifetime than not using it. It's more complexity and won't help us a bit, but we might still do it.
