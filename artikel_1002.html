England are tasked with defeating world champions Spain on their own turf as Thomas Tuchel’s men look to snatch top spot in their Nations League group.

The Three Lions have already secured their place in the quarter-finals after dispatching Croatia 2-1 on Thursday, with Harry Kane scoring from the spot on his record-breaking 126th cap to prove the difference.

They now travel to Madrid for a winner-takes-all clash at the pinnacle of Group A3, with England hoping to exact some revenge after suffering defeat to Spain at Wembley in their group-stage opener in September.

Questions surrounding anti-racism protocols have overshadowed the build-up to the match, with Tuchel angered by his remarks being “twisted” after they sparked a barbed response from Spain head coach Luis de la Fuente.

There are perhaps a couple of surprises in that England XI, with Thomas Tuchel opting to play Ben White in place of Trent Alexander-Arnold. The Arsenal man will presumably be slotting in at right-back.

Jarrell Quansah makes a start – again, presumably at centre-back – with Ezri Konsa and Lewis Hall making up the rest of the defence. Jordan Pickford drops to the bench in place of James Trafford too.

Otherwise, it’s the same front six that Tuchel used for the win over Croatia last time out, with Elliot Anderson and Declan Rice anchoring midfield and Anthony Gordon and Morgan Rogers on the wings.

England XI: Trafford; White, Konsa, Quansah, Hall; Rice, Anderson; Rogers, Bellingham, Gordon; Kane

Subs: Pickford; Alexander-Arnold, Spence, Burn, Gibbs-White, Bellingham, Hill, Calvert-Lewin, Ngumoha, Eze, Lewis-Skelly, Steele.

Spain XI: Simon; Pubill, Cubarsi, Laporte, Cucurella; Zubimendi, Ruiz, Merino; Yamal, Baena, Oyarzabal

The line-ups should be released in the next five minutes or so, so here’s a reminder of the early team news for both sides...

Luis de la Fuente has said that Lamine Yamal is fit to feature for Spain having been left out of the squad for the 2-0 victory over Czechia on Thursday.

“Lamine is suffering from inflammation in his knee, he has fluid buildup,” he said, “Our medical staff, along with those of his club, assessed the situation, and I decided that, to avoid this trip. His recovery is going well, and we believe he’ll be with us on Friday”.

Mikel Oyarzabal could also feature in the squad missed the Czechia match due to illness.

For England, Thomas Tuchel has no further issues following the 2-1 win over Croatia. Dan Burn was called up to the squad to replace Marc Guehi and is likely to start once again. Djed Spence and Ezri Konsa should be fit again having missed out on the previous game.

Tuchel could also hand out debuts to Josh King, James Hill and Jobe Bellingham.

“Last year I probably felt it more than anything in terms of the amount of games and the toll it takes on your body. I am realising as you get older you have to take the recovery side of it a lot more seriously,” explained Rice.

“When you are 21, 22 and 23 you can get up and gallop around the golf course the next day. Even now I am young at 27, the demands of the game and all the competitions. You get up after a game and you feel your legs.

“It is not just the physical side, it is also the mental side. The pressure of delivering for a big club like Arsenal and being in every competition is the goal and we have built a squad where we can rotate but when you are playing well you don’t want to be dropped,” he added.

The England and Arsenal midfielder, who has been accused of playing ‘old man football’, is only just returning to full fitness after a demanding season for club and country. By Miguel Delaney in Madrid

As Declan Rice is discussing how he had an “average World Cup”, and talks at length about the challenges modern players have in ensuring prime fitness, a seemingly more trivial topic comes up: the punditry of Graeme Souness. The former Liverpool captain has in recent weeks criticised Rice for playing “old man’s football” and “cheating” in his midfield role, while insisting he’s a “centre-back”.

Rice, of course, handles it diplomatically – “there would be no disrespect there, I’d shake his hand, I’d chat with him” – but with some edge, too. The England vice-captain asserts this is what the modern game demands, and it’s completely different to Souness’ time.

Spain host England in the final group fixture of the Nations League at the Metropolitano Stadium on Sunday 15 November, with kick-off at 7.45pm GMT.

Viewers in the United Kingdom can watch the match live on ITV1, with coverage from 7pm GMT. A live stream will be available via ITVX.

“I prefer the Spanish model. It's very recognisable, the players are perfectly adapted," said Luis de la Fuente in his pre-match press conference.

“The English model is very good, but you have to have that type of player. Neither England can play like Spain, nor Spain like England,” he explained.

“Spanish footballers surprise me that, compared to what is paid in England, they are very cheap," he added. "I see them as better, in my opinion. Everyone in their market can set the prices."

The Spain boss added that Lamine Yamal is “ fit to play”, though “we will see if he is a starter”.
