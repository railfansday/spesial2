Trump threatened to ban or tax U.S. companies that build AI data centers abroad

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The president gave no details on the penalty plan at a White House press conference and said he had not told his own officials

President Donald Trump outside the White House. (Tasos Katopodis/Getty Images)

President Donald Trump said on Friday that American technology firms would be hit with "a tremendous tax" for constructing AI data centers overseas.

Trump described the penalty as either a ban on building data centers abroad or a tax, according to CNBC. He gave no details on how the policy would work, according to The Associated Press.

"I don't know if that's a breaking story," Trump said at a White House press conference, according to CNBC. "I haven't even said that to my people. But I'm telling them now they're here."

Before announcing the policy, Trump said, "We're going to fight very hard for the data centers."

The comments were made one day after Trump's trilateral summit in China with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, according to CNBC. Trump said Xi "congratulated me on how well we're doing" in artificial intelligence.

Trump told Americans they should accept data center projects where they live and push developers for concessions, according to the AP. "You ought to take advantage of the data centers. Make them pay you, make them pay your community, but don't shut them off," he said.

Opposition to data centers loomed large in the midterms, which gave Democrats control of Congress, according to the AP. Officials with Google $GOOGL and Meta $META told the news service that they are working to ease distrust by being more open with the communities where they hope to build.

Trump urged data center companies in October to pay local communities a "dividend" to win support for new facilities. In a Truth Social post, he said projects that were unpopular in an area became popular once a financial deal was struck.

Local resistance has forced developers to call off at least $260 billion in planned U.S. data center projects this year, according to Heatmap Pro. A survey by the University of Pennsylvania's Annenberg Public Policy Center in August found that 61% of Americans are somewhat or strongly opposed to new data centers where they live, up from 49% about four months earlier.

Microsoft $MSFT has said it will no longer seek tax breaks in the places it builds. More than 300 companies have signed a non-binding White House pledge to cover the cost of their electricity.
