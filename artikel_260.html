YouTube’s view counts will now include the number of people that it estimates are watching a video together on TV. In an update on Monday, the platform says it’s bringing “co-views” to public view counts across long-form videos, Shorts, and live content to provide a more “complete picture” of how people are using its platform.

This marks yet another update to YouTube’s view count system. In August, the platform began counting a view as soon as a video starts playing. The company said at the time that creators will notice their view counts increasing faster as a result of this change, and it seems like adding co-views could boost these numbers as well.

YouTube notes that it will only apply co-views to new views on TV going forward, and that they won’t appear retroactively. Co-views also won’t affect a creator’s ad revenue, YouTube Partner Program eligibility, or revenue.
