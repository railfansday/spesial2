Trump Says He Wants to Protect ‘My Friend’ Putin From International Courts

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Donald Trump was grinning like a Cheshire cat Thursday morning, exclaiming “that’s my friend!” before rushing to greet one of his favorite autocrats: Russian President Vladimir Putin. The two met shortly before being joined by the third strongman invited to the Temu-Yalta trilateral summit, Chinese President Xi Jinping.

The three men are expected to discuss everything from tech policy to nuclear non-proliferation. But looming over the summit are Trump and Putin’s plans to continue negotiations for an end to Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. Notably, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has been excluded from these talks.

It’s been clear for some time now that Trump favors the Russian position in the conflict, but on Thursday, he made explicit how little he actually cares about the war.

“I get along with both [men],” Trump said. Claiming, with no real-world basis, that the war would never have happened had he been elected president in 2020.

The war “doesn’t affect us. We sell equipment to the European Union,” Trump added. “In fact, I guess you could say, we make a lot of money with that,” he added, listing off the different arms sales agreements the U.S. has in Europe.

Clearly, the war is affecting the United States. At a baseline, the president is expending an immense amount of energy and government resources attempting to butter up Putin, and offering him huge concessions in an attempt to mitigate other domestic crises. Earlier this year, Trump removed sanctions on the sale of Russian oil in response to the energy crisis caused by the Iran war, and demanded that Ukraine cease its targeting of Russian energy infrastructure. The money made off of those sales will — in part continue to support Iran’s Russian-backed offensive against the United States.

The Russo-Ukraine war has been devastating for both countries. Millions of combatants and civilians have been slaughtered in the now four-year long war of attrition. Russia has been accused numerous times of committing war crimes against enemy combatants and civilian populations.
