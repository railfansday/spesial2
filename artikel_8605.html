But if you get enjoyment or comfort from spending your time and treasure on emergency preparedness, go for it.
I think it is reasonable, and prudent, to be prepared for some "emergencies." It is impossible to be prepared for everything, but preparing for the "typical disaster" might be a good idea

Most preparedness does not cost much time or money. A few 5 gallon water containers, plus the water in your water heater, can last a long time if you ration. If you have a tankless hot water heater, you may want a few more containers, but even 20 gallons could be handy. You need to buy food anyway, so no cost in keeping extra around (it might even save you money.) The exception might be electricity, and depending on your situation you could probably get by without electricity.

I find as I get older I value comfort and convenience more as well. I don't particularly enjoy being prepared, but it makes me happy knowing I have at least tried. So maybe I do enjoy it

We used to camp a lot. Still do occasionally. Being prepared is always a good thing. Having camping gear helps us be more prepared. But I'm not going to throw stuff into my backpack and walk to someplace to find more supplies. I feel more comfortable knowing that is not likely to b needed.

Last time the supply issue was TP. You don't need TP, but it is nice to have. Who knows what it will be next time. No harm in keeping some supplies on hand. If nothing else, it cuts down on trips to the store.

I think it is a good idea to prepare for "likely disasters." @smitcat has the right idea. Decide what you are preparing for, and go from there.

We've been in this house for 35 years. We have lost water only once. And that was planned while they were installing new water pipes. They suggested we fill up bathtubs. I did not bother, I had water in the basement. We have also lost natural gas once. Again it was while they were connecting new pipes. We loose electricity almost every year. Sometimes for 5 minutes, sometimes for 5 days. So I concentrated on electricity as our most likely supply problem.

There is a thread here about electricity backup. Unfortunately I can not find it right now. So here is my story.
I started with a single battery that could power our furnace for a couple of days. Single pipe steam heat, electricity is required only to work the gas valve. We need heat to keep our pipes from freezing. Otherwise we'd have to shut off water.
When the ground water in are area kept going up, I added another battery so we could run the sump pump. If I can run the sump pump, I have plenty of water to flush the toilets.
Then I added a small generator, so I could recharge the batteries. The problem was, it needed gas. About a gallon a day. So when I needed a new car, I bought a hybrid with a 15 amp 120 volt outlet. I did not buy that car to supply electricity, but I spent a little extra so it could. It is also handy on road trips. My car can boil water, run an induction cooktop, and has a 16 gallon gas tank. The problem with these small systems is you have to run extension cords, and they will not keep the house cool. Again, we value comfort.
Eventually we bought a whole house generator. It runs on natural gas. So we have electricity as long as we have natural gas supply. It kicks on after about a minute of no grid supply. No need to do anything. No extension cords, no buying gas, it is automatic. We didn't need it when we were younger, but it sure is nice now. I can leave home thinking that the darling one will be OK. That makes me happy. I'm not sure how you put a price on that.

If memory serves it was about $10k. Probably more than the value of what is in our freezer.
