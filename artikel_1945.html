Sources: Man United set aside cash for Folarin Balogun transfer

Manchester United may set money aside to sign Monaco striker Folarin Balogun late in the January window, sources have told Football Insider.

The Red Devils are clear admirers of the USA international, but intend to prioritise moves to strengthen their defence first.

Mauricio Pochettino's side are set to spend the bulk of their winter budget on defensive reinforcements, before turning their attention to the other end of the pitch.

Sources say that the addition of a new striker is a secondary priority right now, but the club are likely to look at Balogun if they have money left over.

The 25-year-old came within inches of joining Everton in the summer, only to fail a medical in the final stages.

Monaco are happy to sell Balogun this winter in order to raise funds, and United may yet pounce on that situation.

Football Insider revealed on Monday that Balogun moving to Old Trafford this winter could be a deal that suits all parties, given Monaco's desire to sell.

The Red Devils' interest is being driven by their new boss Pochettino, who was a "big fan" of the frontman during his time in charge of the USA.

Balogun and Pochettino share an "ongoing relationship," and United are hopeful that their bond could see them climb to the front of the queue to sign him.

The former Arsenal academy graduate netted 19 goals in all competitions for Monaco, and has largely been prolific since first moving to France back in 2022.

He hasn't played in English football since a loan spell with Middlesbrough in 2021-22, but United could soon be about to change that.

Former United chief scout Mick Brown told Football Insider on Monday that Pochettino will face the same "massive problem" as his predecessors when it comes to the club hierarchy.

The ex-recruitment man is adamant that the decision-makers have caused the club to fail in recent years, and he doesn't expect it to be any different for Pochettino.

Brown pointed out that all of United's most recent managers have failed for "similar reasons," which have all stemmed from above.

He has no doubt that the hierarchy is the common denominator behind the issues, and feels that nothing will change until there is an alteration at the top.
