Rams' five best WR options after failing to land NFL trade deadline deal, from Tyreek Hill to Brandon Aiyuk

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

The Rams opted to stay pat at the trade deadline, spurning the desire to make a move for one of the many wideouts made available during Tuesday's spectacle.

That proved a controversial choice among segments of Los Angeles' fanbase, many of whom wanted to see Los Angeles stay aggressive as it attempts to mount a challenge on the NFC West standings.

Things got harder for the Rams on Monday when Puka Nacua was placed on injured reserve with a knee injury. His absence, coupled with Davante Adams, has made Los Angeles' wide receiver room — one of its biggest strengths entering into the year — look decidedly more flimsy.

That wasn't enough to coax the powers that be in the Rams' front office to make a move. With the deadline now slammed shut, here's a look at some players the Rams could consider to fill the void after Nacua and Adams were ruled out with long-term injuries.

Aiyuk is already considered Public Enemy No. 1 (or close to it) by segments of the 49ers organization. Why not continue to turn the knife by joining San Francisco's biggest rival?

The 28-year-old pass-catcher's high-profile fallout with the Niners and dodgy injury record could scare some teams away. But if the Rams want to buy low on a potential top-of-the-line pass-catcher, it would behoove them to consider Aiyuk. The Arizona State talent produced 75 catches for 1,342 yards and seven touchdowns in his final full season in San Francisco. A big-play threat when healthy who ranked second in the league in yards per catch among wide receivers targeted more than 50 times in 2023, Aiyuk could help supplant some of Nacua and Adams' lost production. Aiyuk will likely need some time to get his legs under him after missing the better part of two seasons with injury. The allure of bringing him into Tinseltown — and back to his best — could be too much for Sean McVay to pass up on. One would have to imagine Aiyuk wouldn't mind the chance to shine in Los Angeles colors, either; the Rams face off against the Niners on Dec. 13.

Hill might have more question marks around him than Aiyuk given his age and the severity of his last malady — a left knee dislocation that severed several of his ligaments in the joint, namely the ACL. A 32-year-old coming off major knee surgery and character concerns is hardly an ideal option for a WR-needy side. Then again, the Rams are bordering on desperate. Hill has proven to be a reliable pass-catcher when healthy, adept at burning opposition defender with his route-running and speed. It's fanciful to think that Hill's injury — coupled with his age — wouldn't diminish his world-class speed. Nevertheless, Hill is a crafty and malleable player that found relative success even in less-than-ideal circumstances, particularly in Miami.

Hill is still weeks away from getting back on the field. If he does, he should prove appealing enough for Los Angeles to consider taking a swing.

Now, things get a little precarious. Samuel was a solid, if unspectacular option with the Panthers and Commanders, eclipsing 800 yards once and 600 yards four times. In 2025, he landed on the Bills but failed to make an impression, getting released one season into his three-year deal.

Samuel is shifty, explosive and versatile, occasionally lining up in the backfield during his first few seasons. The Ohio State product would not be the most enticing option, in truth. But his skillset does lend itself to Samuel carving out a secondary or tertiary role on the Rams' attack, something that could be compelling if Los Angeles can stay afloat while Nacua and Adams are on the mend.

The former Giants pass-catcher is arguably unlucky not to have a job after enjoying a steady run in the Big Apple that saw him amass at least 700 yards on four occasions. Slayton is not the most glamorous option, especially when compared to the likes of Aiyuk and Hill.

And yet, there might not be a player better positioned to take the mantle from Nacua and Adams than him. Injuries scuppered Slayton's final season in Big Blue, leading to his release. But Slayton's consistency and recent body of work suggests he could explode (at least somewhat) in a better offensive environment. Slayton should receive some interest in the coming few days.

Father Time bests all, even three-time All-Pro wideouts who place inside the top-20 in all-time in catches, receiving yards and receiving touchdowns. Hopkins was a non-factor during his lone season with the Ravens and struggled to produce much of anything during his last postseason trip in 2024.

Despite this, Hopkins' reputation precedes him. He is a runner of inch-perfect routes who has found a way to make success even as his tires start to give out a bit. Hopkins will hardly make-or-break being a Super Bowl contender at present. But he could aid in helping right the ship as the Rams attempt to keep their nerve following Nacua and Adams' injuries.
