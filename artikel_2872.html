History Says Stocks Tend to Rise After a Midterm Election: 3 Stocks That Should Serve Investors Well Over the Next Year and Beyond

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

These consumer names are in a strong position to resume their moves higher.

Investors are in a stronger position now that the 2026 midterm elections are finished. Over the history of the market, it has risen in the year after every midterm election since 1946.

That makes this an opportune time to put money to work. However, as investors generally have focused on artificial intelligence, many strong consumer stocks have been overlooked. Thus, rather than considering trendy investments, it could be profitable for investors to turn their attention to these three consumer discretionary stocks.

Consumers have increasingly been concerned about affordability in recent years, but one wouldn't know it from the recent prosperity of the cruise industry in general, and specifically the largest cruise line by market cap, Royal Caribbean (RCL -2.41%).

Record demand has given the company strong pricing power, and advance bookings for 2027 are selling faster than usual despite the fact that the company is not offering discounts.

These are remarkable results for a company that began the decade with a COVID-19 shutdown that lasted for more than a year and left it with a debt load of more than $22 billion, well above the $9.7 billion in Q3 2019, just before the pandemic.

Fortunately, today's Royal Caribbean shows few signs of this struggle despite that higher debt load. In an industry that defines 100% occupancy as two people per cabin, occupancy has averaged 111% in 2026, confirming the tight supply.

To address this demand, the company boosted capacity by 8% over the last year with the launch of one new vessel, and will increase it further with the debut of an additional ship in 2027.

With that expansion, its revenue in the first nine months of 2026 increased by 9% from year-ago levels to nearly $15 billion. Also, Royal Caribbean's net income rose 8% to $3.8 billion, even as higher payroll, fuel costs, and interest expenses from the debt were a drag on earnings.

Despite all this, the stock trades at 20 times earnings, even though it commands a valuation premium above Carnival (CCL -2.29%) and Norwegian (NCLH -1.16%) for its higher profit margins. Given that the cruise line is now sailing smoothly, that low valuation may give travel investors all the more reason to buy Royal Caribbean stock.

MercadoLibre (MELI -1.75%) is the leading e-commerce company in Latin America. It also offers fintech, logistics, and advertising services, and has thrived by turning the political and economic challenges in that region into business opportunities.

Admittedly, the stock has suffered as the company has invested heavily in its efforts to expand the reach of its e-commerce business, which has reduced its net margins in that part of the business. It has also faced rising bad-loan costs as it has rapidly expanded its loan book. Both have weighed on profitability.

However, these efforts will likely solidify MercadoLibre's position in the market over the longer term. In the first nine months of 2026, revenue increased by 48% year over year to nearly $30 billion. Meanwhile, net income fell 7% to $1.3 billion.

Still, the stock price's more than 40% slump from its July 2025 peak has dragged its P/E ratio to below 40, a multiyear low for the stock. Once the investments in the business begin to translate into growing profitability, it is likely that MercadoLibre stock will resume its upward trajectory.

Dutch Bros (BROS -2.13%) has built a compelling competitive advantage. Although it must compete with Starbucks, numerous independents, and other coffee chains, the company's people-first culture, rapid-service drive-thrus, and proprietary beverages have won over customers.

Additionally, it is in the middle of a rapid regional-to-national expansion, following a similar script to the ones that drove the growth of Starbucks, Chipotle, McDonald's, and others.

As of the end of its latest quarter, the chain operated 1,273 shops, and its plan to grow its footprint to 2,029 locations by 2029 is on track. That expansion continues despite rising input costs and intense competition, which have slowed its revenue growth. Consequently, investors have punished the stock.

Still, the slowdown is relative, as its revenue in the first nine months of 2026 rose by 31% to nearly $1.6 billion. Also, a boost from "other income" allowed Dutch Bros' net income to grow by 38% to $81 million.

The aforementioned headwinds have pressured the stock this year, and its P/E ratio has consistently fallen over the last two years to around 48. Nonetheless, amid the company's revenue growth and rapid expansion, such a valuation could indicate a buying opportunity.
