In its efforts to appeal to the heartland of America, liberal elite Hollywood has produced a movie titled — checks notes — Heartland. The market was cracked wide open by Taylor Sheridan's sprawling neo-Western series Yellowstone, which Netflix famously passed on a decade or so ago. The show went on to become a platform-defining hit for Paramount+ and Sheridan cemented himself as something of a mogul with a spectacularly successful streak. In recent years, Netflix and other streamers have tried to replicate Yellowstone's success with a string of shows and movies aimed at the same demographic, but the results have been hit or miss.

For instance, Prime Video's The Terminal List, which star Chris Pratt said was made for an audience that Hollywood has historically ignored, simply spent too long between seasons. It recently returned with its second season after a four-year gap. As expected, The Terminal List failed to crack the standard benchmark of 1 billion minutes watched upon its return, according to Nielsen. Netflix's Yellowstone alternatives — Ransom Canyon and The Waterfront — have also struggled to break out, with the latter being canceled after one season.

More recently, Netflix has had success with old-fashioned Westerns such as East of Eden and Little House on the Prairie — the latter has been renewed for a second season, while the John Steinbeck adaptation will be followed by an adaptation of Lonesome Dove. Netflix's latest original movie, starring Jessica Chastain, comes on the heels of not only these hit shows, but also a series of mid-budget genre titles that have found great success this year amid the streamer's creative pivot from mega-budget tentpoles.
