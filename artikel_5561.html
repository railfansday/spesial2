4 steps plumbers say can prevent burst pipes in a freeze

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Texas plumbers list 4 freeze defenses: disconnect hoses, drip faucets, insulate exposed pipes and find the main shut-off valve.

During a cold snap, the part of a house most likely to fail is the plumbing hidden in its walls and under its floors. Pipes can burst well before the weather turns truly frigid. Post and Courier reports that a South Carolina plumber's customer had a pipe burst before temperatures were steadily below freezing, in a house with the heat turned off and a small draft. The danger begins with the first cold night, not the worst one, and the house itself can leave a pipe open to the cold. Pipes in exterior walls, especially north-facing ones, are the most vulnerable to cold winds, according to plumbers in Amarillo, Texas, who spoke to KVII. That means much of the risk is something an owner can control.

A homeowner whose own pipes burst should expect to pay for the damage. In Charleston, S.C., Charleston Water System takes no responsibility for damage to a homeowner's plumbing, according to Post and Courier. That leaves the owner of an unheated or drafty house paying for the repair. Most of the defenses against that bill cost little and need no plumber, which makes skipping them harder to justify. Each defense guards a different weak spot: faucets outside, pipes along cold walls, pipes in unheated spaces, and the minutes after something breaks. None of them protects the whole house, and a homeowner who handles only one remains exposed everywhere else.

CBS Texas laid out the steps homeowners can take to keep their plumbing from bursting in a freeze, from wrapping pipes in garages and attics to knowing where to shut off a house's water fast. The steps carry no rank, and homeowners should treat them as parts of one plan rather than as options to choose among. Some keep water from freezing in the first place, and one limits the damage once a pipe has already given way.

The faucet on a home's outside wall is the only pipe in the house that sits fully in the open air, and homeowners often set it up to fail. CBS Texas reports that outdoor spigots are among the most common plumbing failures in a freeze, because water held in by an attached hose can't drain from the pipe. That water freezes where it sits and splits the pipe from the inside. "That section couldn't drain and so it popped," Anthony Ladd of Baker Brothers Plumbing said. Homeowners in South Carolina get the same instruction to disconnect and drain their hoses, according to Post and Courier. An owner who takes the hose off removes the trapped water that causes one of the most frequent breaks in a freeze.

Protecting the tap itself takes no special skill and almost no money. Post and Courier recommends insulated covers for outdoor spigots, but a homeowner doesn't need to buy one. David Crow, president of Benjamin Franklin Plumbing, says a towel with a cup on top protects the spigot just as well. Nearly every household already has both. With a fix this simple and a failure this common, the main way an owner loses a spigot is by forgetting to protect it.

A covered tap protects the pipe only at the point where it leaves the wall. It does nothing for the water lines that run through the garage, the attic or the walls on the cold side of the house. An owner who drains the hose and covers the spigot has closed only the outermost gap, and the pipes deeper inside need protection of their own.

This is the first layer, not the whole job.

Homeowners can keep a pipe from freezing by keeping the water inside it from sitting still. A slow trickle keeps water moving and lowers the odds of a freeze, especially for faucets on exterior walls, according to CBS Texas. That puts the drip at specific taps, the ones whose pipes run along the cold outer walls of the house, not at every faucet. The cabinet doors under a dripping faucet should stay open, according to Post and Courier. Open doors expose the pipe to the heat of the room. A homeowner who drips the tap with the cabinet shut leaves the pipe in a closed, unheated box and has done only half the job.

Homeowners need only a bare trickle to protect a pipe. They should set the drip as slow as the faucet allows, according to Post and Courier. With so little water involved, dripping is one of the cheapest ways to protect a house, and a little water through a cold stretch is the whole cost. "When it gets below freezing, that slow trickle can make a big difference," Clayton Robinson, co-owner of CR Plumbing, told CBS Texas.

Homeowners can't use a drip to protect the most exposed taps of all, the ones outside. David Butler, Responsible Master Plumber at Milestone Electric, A/C, & Plumbing, says only indoor faucets can be left dripping, never outdoor ones. Outdoor spigots need their hoses disconnected and their covers on instead. Pipes in garages and attics, which no faucet trickle reaches, need insulation of their own.

Pipes that run through garages, attics and crawl spaces need protection in place before the cold arrives, since those rooms get no heat from the rest of the house. Homeowners can shield those lines by wrapping them ahead of a freeze, according to CBS Texas. The insulation for the job is an ordinary item sold at hardware stores, according to KVII in Amarillo, Texas. A defense this easy to find and put on is where a homeowner's winter preparation should start.

Wrapping covers only the pipes a homeowner can reach. The insulation goes on accessible pipes, according to KVII. In a garage or an attic, those are the runs in plain view. Some breaks still happen out of reach, and RJ Marquez, owner of The Drain Guys, says his company gets calls about lines that broke beneath houses and inside walls. No homeowner can wrap a pipe sealed behind a finished wall. A homeowner who wraps every pipe in the garage lowers the risk to the house without removing it.

In the crawl space, wrapping and sealing are one job. Homeowners with pier-and-beam crawl spaces should seal the drafts there, according to KVII. That is also one of the spaces where CBS Texas advises wrapping pipes, so the owner has to keep the cold air out as well as put a layer between that air and the pipe. The sealing, unlike the insulation, need not cost anything. "Some of the things you can do for free to handle some of the actual freezing problems is to seal up any drafting," said Alex Marquez, master plumber with The Drain Guys. The wrap and the seal together make up this step, and a homeowner who does only the wrap still leaves the cold air a way in to the pipes.

The main shut-off valve is the step for the night a pipe breaks anyway. The other three steps try to keep a burst from happening, and this one limits the damage once it has. Plumbers call knowing where the valve is critical, yet it is one of the most overlooked steps, according to CBS Texas. Once a pipe breaks, the faster an owner turns off the water, the less damage the house takes. Many homeowners treat the valve as an afterthought, but how quickly they reach it determines how much a burst costs them.

The valve is the only defense that covers every pipe in the house. Wrapping, dripping and covering each protect one spot, and a pipe can still break at any spot they missed. Anthony Ladd, a plumber with Baker Brothers Plumbing, says the main valve cuts off the water supply to the entire house. When the owner closes that valve, no new water reaches a broken pipe, wherever in the house the pipe broke. That protection helps only if the owner can find the valve and turn it while water is spraying. "It's very important to know where it is and that it's working," Ladd said. A valve that won't turn is no help in an emergency. Owners should do a dry run while nothing is broken: find the valve, turn it and confirm that the water stops.

Owners who skip that dry run pay for it in the worst minutes of a freeze. David Crow, president of Benjamin Franklin Plumbing, says his plumbers get frantic calls from customers who never got ready and can't find the shut-off. The water stays on through every minute they spend searching and waiting for help, and that damage is exactly what the valve is meant to prevent.

Even the step meant for after a break has to be done before the cold arrives.
