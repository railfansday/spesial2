Jermod McCoy shows promise during 1st start for Las Vegas Raiders in Week 11 vs. Denver Broncos

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The Las Vegas Raiders have been searching for answers at cornerback throughout the season, often deploying a three-man rotation between Darien Porter, Hezekiah Masses and Jermod McCoy at one of the two starting spots on the perimeter this season. However, in Week 11 against the Denver Broncos, the coaching staff decided to let McCoy take all of the reps, for better or worse.

According to Pro Football Focus, the numbers on the fourth-round pick's first start were a mixed bag. He was targeted five times and gave up two catches (one drop) for 55 yards while recording a pass breakup. So it was far from a perfect outing, but the Tennessee product showed promise on tape where the stats leave out some context. With that in mind, let's dive into the film.

Let's get the negative reps out of the way to end on a high note. And while the result here is bad, this is far from a terrible rep.

The problem is that when the Broncos motion the slot receiver across the formation to get into a three-by-one set, the Raiders respond by getting into a single-high coverage that leaves McCoy one-on-one with Jaylen Waddle, who has a reputation as one of the best deep threats in the NFL.

Still, the rookie corner does a decent job of staying patient at the line of scrimmage by not completely opening his hips until Waddle commits to the outside release. Then, McCoy uses his inside hand to help slow the receiver down a bit and run with the receiver stride for stride, meaning this is perfect coverage against a go route. However, it isn't, and the problems begin at about the 50-yard line.

That's where Waddle starts to tip his route by picking his shoulders and head up to hit the breaks on the curl route. But McCoy doesn't, or is late to see that, and ends up running right by his man, giving up plenty of separation for a 19-yard catch.

To cut the fourth-round pick some slack, especially with no safety help over the top, this is a tough assignment with Waddle's speed. However, he can still use this rep to build confidence, knowing that he was able to run with Waddle. This can be a learning moment for McCoy, recognizing that he can trust his speed and look for the route indicators that receivers give him moving forward.

This is a similar rep, just against Troy Franklin, who can also fly with a 4.41-second 40-yard dash time at the 2024 NFL Combine.

Again, McCoy shows decent patience at the line of scrimmage to avoid opening up the gate and getting burnt. But he does get turned around a bit with Franklin using an outside jab step to set up his inside release. Still, McCoy isn't out of position initially by using his hands to help stay in phase. The issue is he holds on too long, which slows him down and leads to the receiver breaking free later in the route.

Luckily, the wideout had one of the more comical drops of the season, with the ball doinking off his helmet for an incompletion. So, the corner gets away with this one. Also, it looks like he was supposed to have some sort of help over the top.

The Raiders are running a unique coverage here, where the outside corners (McCoy and Eric Stokes at the bottom of the screen) are playing man while the four other defensive backs (Hezekiah Masses, Treydan Stukes, Jeremy Chinn and Taron Johnson) are executing a pattern match zone coverage.

Notice how both Johnson and Chinn converge on the receiver in the middle of the field despite the pass going over the top of the defense. It's impossible to know for certain without having the exact play call and coverage rules, but I'd imagine one of those two is supposed to help against the deep route.

This is a rep that goes down as a negative one on the ledger since the Broncos get an explosive play out of it, but it isn't nearly as bad as the scorebook shows for McCoy.

Not only does Denver run a flea flicker, but they also have the outside receiver (Marvin Mims, who runs a 4.38-second 40-yard dash) motion across the formation for a wheel route while executing a switch release with the receiver at the top of the screen. But the rookie isn't fooled, staying at his landmark in the Cover 4 call and passing off the outside receiver to pick up the wheel route.

McCoy is in a good spot to defend the route, squeezing the receiver toward the sideline and staying in the receiver's hip pocket throughout the rep. However, he starts to stumble around the 12-yard line and trips right before the ball arrives, while Chinn can't get there in time to help, resulting in the big gain that set up a Broncos touchdown.

What's most encouraging about this play is that in previous games, McCoy had a lot of issues against these types of concepts in zone coverage. In his limited playing time prior to this past Sunday, he often looked confused or like he was thinking too much and unsure who to cover when playing zone, especially against route combinations where he had to pick up and pass off receivers.

So, despite this being a big gain, the rookie is showing growth here. He just needs to be better at staying on his feet to finish the play.

Moving onto the positive reps, while the incompletion here is a result of Bo Nix making the wrong read and Courtland Sutton running into Johnson, it's still solid coverage from McCoy.

Denver dials up a mesh concept, where Sutton is working across the field on a deep drag or over route to set a pick for the slot receiver (No. 13 Pat Bryant). That's a good play call by Sean Payton because it catches Las Vegas in man coverage and Bryant is open, which is where Nix should go with the ball.

But from McCoy's perspective, he's in a perfect position to defend the over route, avoiding any contact on the pick and running stride-for-stride with Sutton. In fact, the corner might get an interception if the receiver's route doesn't get cut short. It's the difference between his eyes being on his man (which is where they should be in man coverage) and on the quarterback, making it more difficult to adjust to the ball in the air.

As a side note, Klint Kubiak went viral for saying at halftime that the referees needed to call the game both ways. Kubiak has a good point with this play because if Cody White was called for offensive pass interference in the end zone — a penalty that wiped away a touchdown — then Sutton should get flagged here, too.

Here, we'll get another rep from McCoy in press coverage against Franklin on a go route. This time, he does an ever better job of staying patient at the line of scrimmage against the inside jab step from Franklin to avoid opening the gate too early.

Once the receiver commits to the outside release, then the corner opens his hips to turn and run. He manages to stay in the receiver's hip pocket throughout the route while widening the wideout toward the sideline to give the quarterback less room to complete the pass. So, Nix tries to throw back shoulder, but McCoy recognizes that Franklin is slowing down and throttles with his man to contest the catch for an incompletion.

That's perfect coverage against a go route, taking away the deep shot and the back shoulder throw. The problem is that Johnson gets flagged for illegal contact against Waddle, making McCoy's rep go all for naught.

I saved the best play McCoy made for last so that we can end on the highest of high notes. Ironically, this is the first target that came his way during the game.

Part of what's so impressive is that Rob Leonard calls the rookie's number, leaving him one-on-one against Waddle, the Broncos' best receiver. Denver even gets a coverage indicator pre-snap, motioning a receiver from across the formation, which Masses follows to tip that Vegas is in man.

A lot of coordinators would have the secondary rotate, where McCoy widens with the motion to stay on the outside receiver and avoid showing the defense's hand. But Leonard shows faith in the fourth-round pick, and the latter proves why his coach had confidence in him.

Again, McCoy does a good job of staying patient on the line of scrimmage while playing press, not opening the gate until Waddle commits to the outside release. Also, he uses his hands well to help stay in phase throughout the rep, running stride-for-stride with the receiver against the slot fade route. Nix still tries to drop it in the bucket, and McCoy recognizes that Waddle is looking up for the ball, turning his head to locate it in the air too. That allows him to come up with a PBU and keep the Broncos out of the end zone on third down.

While the fourth-rounder still has plenty to prove over the next couple of months, he showed why he was considered a draft steal and the most talented corner on the roster in Denver. That should keep McCoy in the starting lineup for the foreseeable future, especially since the defense has spent the entire season searching for cornerback help.
