Oklahoma State football's rally vs. Arizona State reminds Cowboys what defense could be

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

TEMPE, AZ — In overtime, Oklahoma State coach Eric Morris didn't hesitate after a third-down pass to Wyatt Young fell incomplete.

Instead of keeping his offense on the field for a fourth-and-2, Morris sent out kicker Sam Keltner for a chip shot field goal to take a three-point lead.

The decision rewarded him at the first opportunity. Arizona State tried a roll out misdirection play. Freshman quarterback Jake Fette, who hadn't thrown an interception in four starts this season, threw across his body toward the middle of the field. OSU safety Quinton Hammonds stuck one arm out behind his head and intercepted the pass, calling game in a 30-27 win.

"It just shows the trust coach that Coach Morris and Coach (Skyler) Cassity have for our entire defense," Hammonds said. "We were playing really good that entire second half, so we just tried to ride that momentum into overtime. We have such a good kicker, Sam Keltner he's just so consistent that we can trust him to go out there and execute every time he's on the field."

At times this season, it's been tough to trust OSU's defense. At Iowa State, OSU's offense scored too quickly at the end of the game and the Cyclones hit a big play to set up a walk-off field goal. In three conference losses, the Cowboy defense has allowed 55 points to Texas Tech, 51 to Iowa State and 35 to Houston.

That's the unit that Morris allowed to decide the game. Morris said he didn't even consider going for it, in part because the defense had been so strong in the second half. The Cowboys allowed just 88 total yards in the second half. Arizona State (6-5, 4-4 Big 12) averaged 2.9 yards per play after halftime.

Hammonds still doesn't know how he made the game-ending interception. His job on the final play was to blow the quarterback up, but as soon as he saw Fette roll out and throw the ball he decided to hang back.

"I was like, might as well throw a hand out there and see if I can catch it, and ended up catching it," Hammonds said.

For the interception to develop, it took an entire defense being on the same page and communicating together in coverage. That's been a weakpoint for OSU (7-4, 5-3) this season.

Making a game-winning play, instead of the other way around, gave senior linebacker Isaiah Chisom confidence in his unit.

"I think it's just reassuring to understand that we have the right players on the field, we have the talent," Chisom said. "It's just a matter of us continuing to do our job and just make big plays."

For a change, the Big 12's No. 14 defense had the No. 1 offense's back.

As Hammonds intercepted the pass and hopped out of bounds, quarterback Drew Mestemaker was in the horde of teammates running to swarm Hammonds.

Mestemaker said it was the most genuine joy he's had for someone else in his life.

"Those guys have kind of taken some heat the past few weeks and I'm just so proud of how they responded," Mestemaker said. "The work they put in every day, how they go about their practice habits and all that it might not show up on gameday, but those are the hardest-working guys on the team and they get heat for plays that they could have made and they're the hardest on themselves for it and to see a guy like Quinton go up and make a play to end the game, that's my guy and I'm just so proud of him."

For 30 minutes in the second half and one defensive play in overtime, everything came together for OSU's defense.

"(It's) just awesome, showing what we can be when we're locked in and focused and everybody's doing their job," Morris said.
