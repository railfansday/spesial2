One of the notable features in the 2027 Nissan Rogue Hybrid is the e-Power system, a series hybrid that uses electric motors to drive the wheels while the engine serves as a generator. As such, the compact crossover delivers an EV-like driving experience minus the range anxiety.

But aside from the smoothness of EV-like operation, Nissan is championing a new feature called Smooth Stop. Inspired by a technique known as a “limo stop,” the feature automatically reduces braking pressure just before the vehicle comes to a complete stop, minimizing the forward-and-backward jerking sensation passengers typically experience.

With Smooth Stop, drivers can simply maintain pressure on the brake pedal without manually adjusting it. Similar comfort technology has been available in BMW models like the E90-generation 3 Series, but Nissan claims Smooth Stop is a first-in-class feature, giving the Rogue Hybrid an advantage over direct rivals like the Honda CR-V and Toyota RAV4. Perhaps Nissan can bank on Smooth Stop to distinguish the Rogue Hybrid, especially considering it has added other comfort features.

For one, Nissan made the cabin quieter by adding sound-deadening materials in noise-prone areas like the wheel wells and reducing wind noise through concealed windshield wipers, door-mounted side mirrors, and slim roof rails. The cabin also features Zero Gravity seats, soft-touch materials, and generous front headroom that contribute to comfort, while the available ventilated front seats were redesigned to minimize operating noise. The Japanese marque claims that normal conversations can be maintained between front and rear passengers, even at highway speeds.

Another comfort feature is the improved ProPilot Assist, the brand’s advanced driver-assistance system, similar in purpose to Ford’s BlueCruise. The system comes with a new front camera with a 20% wider field of view, increased object-detection range, and improved steering control for centering the vehicle in the lane. Moreover, it is available with Hands-Off Traffic Jam Support for hands-free steering at speeds below 30 mph and Auto Lane Change, which requires the driver’s hands to remain on the wheel. The system was developed and tested in California.

While the Nissan Rogue has been the brand’s best-selling model in the U.S., its sales still trail those of its main rivals by a wide margin. In 2025, Nissan sold 217,896 Rogues, significantly fewer than the 479,288 Toyota RAV4s and 403,768 Honda CR-Vs sold. But for the 2027 model year, Nissan is switching up the formula with a new series hybrid system and a more comfortable driving experience.

How that will pan out remains to be seen. The 2027 Rogue Hybrid will arrive first in the SR AWD trim with the SR Technology Package in limited quantities in late November 2026, with a $41,490 MSRP. More affordable trims and the more expensive Platinum trim will follow in early 2027.
