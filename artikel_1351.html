Euro 2028: Hosts, Stadiums, TV Rights and More

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

The European Championship is widely seen as one of the hardest competitions in the world to win. Although in the Premier League, you must be at your very best for 38 matches, teams need some form of luck to win the ultimate prize in international European football.

That's never guaranteed, as Germany showed at Euro 2024 as they disappointingly crashed out to Spain in the quarter-finals by losing 2-1 after extra-time. That sense of danger, unpredictability and intrigue is what makes it one of the best tournaments in the world — and Euro 2028 is expected to continue that trend when it arrives.

With the 2026 World Cup in the books, the countdown to the next major tournament on home soil is well underway. Euro 2028 will run from Friday 9 June to Sunday 9 July, with 51 matches staged across England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland.

Spain will go into the tournament as the holders after they won the previous tournament in Germany. They beat England 2-1 in the final in Berlin, and the Three Lions will be hoping it's third time lucky after losing back-to-back Euros finals. It's more than possible on home soil — and we have outlined everything you need to know about the tournament.

Some of the greatest players in football history have competed at the European Championships. Here is the competition's star-studded top scorers list.

Euro 2028 will take place in four countries: England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland. That is more hosts than any previous edition except Euro 2020, which was deliberately spread across Europe. Northern Ireland were part of the original five-nation bid but lost their hosting role when Casement Park was dropped (more on that below). The hosts are mostly easily connected, with a short flight across the Irish Sea giving fans access to any match if they wish to support their nation.

This will be the third time England has hosted matches at a European tournament. They put on a show at Euro 96 by reaching the semi-finals and narrowly losing to Germany on penalties. Their bitter rivals went on to win the tournament, but the Three Lions brought the country together after years of frustration.

Twenty-five years later, they hosted several matches, including the semi-finals and final, at Euro 2020, which was delayed a year due to the COVID-19 Pandemic. England reached the final, but it ended in yet more heartbreak as Italy beat them on penalties. The Three Lions will be hoping it is third time lucky on home soil, yet nothing is ever guaranteed in football. However, their 2026 World Cup showings and UEFA Nations League performances ensure they will be among the favourites.

Wales and the Republic of Ireland have never hosted a match at the Euros. Scotland even played two of their matches at home at Hampden Park, but just as it seems to happen at every single tournament they play at, it ended in disappointment. They comfortably lost both matches to Czechia and Croatia to see them knocked out in the group stages again. Euro 2028 will offer a chance of redemption, with Scotland set to play all three group games at Hampden if they qualify.

Surprisingly, the home nations' bid for the tournament was relatively straightforward. Russia also made a bid for it, although that was quickly made ineligible due to its invasion of Ukraine, and Turkey withdrew their efforts to focus on a joint bid with Italy for Euro 2032. It meant that the UK and Ireland were the only option, making it an easy choice for UEFA. The decision was confirmed in October 2023.

England have only won one major tournament — but we have outlined their best performances at the World Cup or Euros.

Euro 2028 starts on Friday June 9, 2028, with the opening match at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff. Wales will take the A1 slot in that game if they qualify and the final, the 51st match of the tournament, is at Wembley Stadium on Sunday 9 July.

Wembley will also host both semi-finals, on 4 and 5 July, and one of the quarter-finals. The other three quarter-finals will be played at the Aviva Stadium, Hampden Park and the Principality Stadium.

Each host already has a set position in the group draw if they qualify: Wales A1, England B1, the Republic of Ireland E1 and Scotland F1. England's opening group game would be at the Etihad Stadium, with their final two group matches at Wembley. Kick-off times will be confirmed after the final tournament draw in December 2027.

No teams have qualified for Euro 2028, and that includes the hosts. UEFA has scrapped automatic qualification for the hosts, so England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland will all go through qualifying like everyone else.

There is a safety net, though. Two places at the finals are kept back for co-hosts who fail to qualify through their group. If three or more of the hosts miss out, those two places go to the hosts with the best qualifying records. Northern Ireland are not covered by the safety net now that they are no longer staging matches.

The qualifying draw takes place in Belfast on Sunday December 6, 2026. The 54 teams will be split into 12 groups: six groups of four and six groups of five. The seeding pots are based on the 2026/27 Nations League, with England among the top seeds in Pot 1. Wales, the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland are in Pot 2, and Scotland are in Pot 3. The co-hosts and Northern Ireland will be kept apart in the draw.

Teams in the Nations League quarter-finals, England among them, will be drawn into groups of four so they have room in the calendar for those games. Qualifying starts in March 2027 and the group stage ends in November 2027. The 12 group winners and the eight best runners-up qualify directly. The last places are decided by play-offs in March 2028, and the format of those play-offs depends on how many host safety-net places are used.

There are nine stadiums hosting matches at Euro 2028. Surprisingly, Liverpool's Anfield will not be hosting matches as its pitch dimensions fall short of UEFA's requirements, whilst Man United were unable to guarantee whether the stadium would be ready in time, as they look at rebuilding the area. Celtic Park, Ibrox and Croke Park were also not picked.

Wembley and the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, one of the best stadiums in the world, will hold matches in London, Everton's new Hill Dickinson Stadium also makes the list, and Scotland, Wales and Ireland each have one venue.

The original plan had ten stadiums. Northern Ireland's venue was supposed to be Casement Park, a GAA ground in west Belfast that has been derelict since 2013. Windsor Park, the home of the Northern Ireland team, was too small to meet UEFA's 30,000-seat minimum.

Casement Park was a controversial choice from the start. Local residents had fought the redevelopment in the courts for years, objecting to the size of the stadium and the traffic it would bring, and lost their final challenge in 2022. Picking a Gaelic games venue over the home of Northern Irish football also drew criticism from unionist politicians, who questioned how much public money was going into the project.

In September 2024 the UK government refused to fund the rebuild in time for the tournament. It said the estimated cost had risen from £180m when the bid was awarded to potentially more than £400m, a figure the GAA disputed. The Irish government pushed for Croke Park or Páirc Uí Chaoimh in Cork to replace it, but UEFA chose to share Belfast's five matches out among the nine remaining venues.

Around three million tickets will be available for Euro 2028. Unlike at previous tournaments, they will only go on sale after the final tournament draw in December 2027, so fans will know which teams they could be watching before they apply. General sale will start with a ballot instead of first-come, first-served.

UEFA has promised to broadly freeze prices at Euro 2024 levels, with no dynamic pricing. More than 40 per cent of tickets will be in the two cheapest "Fans First" categories. At Euro 2024 those categories cost €30 and €60, and UEFA is aiming to keep them below £30 and £60 in 2028. Prices for the more expensive categories have not been confirmed. At Euro 2024, group-stage tickets in those categories cost €150 and €200.

Each qualified nation will get 10,000 tickets for every group-stage match. Disabled fans will be able to buy tickets at the cheapest price and request a free ticket for a companion. Tickets sold on UEFA's official resale platform will be capped at face value. We will update this article when the full price list and sale dates are confirmed.

Interestingly, there are differences from how FIFA created their price point's for the 2026 World Cup, which drew heavy cricitisim over its' ticket prices.

One of the most exciting competitions in the world will be shown live on ITV and the BBC in the UK. The organisations agreed a deal with UEFA in 2022 to show Euro 2024 and 2028, giving as many people as possible the chance to watch it. When the decision in the UK was made, Niall Sloane, ITV Director of Sport, said:

This deal keeps the Euros available on free-to-air television for viewers for the coming years. Having delivered the biggest ever UK single channel football audience of 27.6 million at the 2020 Euros, for England’s semi-final victory over Denmark, we’re looking forward to bringing to the widest audiences the very best coverage of tournaments that have such broad appeal.

The BBC and ITV deal covers all four nations of the UK. Fans in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland can watch the tournament free-to-air.

The road to the finals is split between broadcasters. ITV has exclusive rights to England's qualifiers. The BBC shows Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland qualifiers across TV, iPlayer and BBC Sounds. In Wales, S4C shows Wales qualifiers live with Welsh-language commentary, and BBC Cymru Wales covers them in English.

In the Republic of Ireland, RTÉ holds the rights to Euro 2028 and to Ireland's national team matches, so the tournament will be free-to-air there too.

Elsewhere, in America, you will be able to watch the tournament on Fox Sports. TVA Sports and TSN holds the rights in Canada, whilst it is yet to be confirmed who will be showing the tournament in Australia. No one will want to miss it as the pressure and anticipation builds.

All focus in international football has already turned to the European Championships in the United Kingdom and Ireland in 2028.

UEFA has not yet set the prize money for Euro 2028. The amounts for Euro 2024 were confirmed in December 2023, six months before the tournament, so the Euro 2028 figures are likely to follow a similar timeline.

The previous two tournaments give a good guide. Euro 2020 and Euro 2024 both had a total prize pot of €331 million. Every team got a participation fee of €9.25m, and the maximum a team could earn was €28.25m. Spain took the full €28.25m in 2024 after winning all seven of their matches on the way to the trophy.

Clubs will also be paid for releasing players. UEFA has set aside €140m for clubs that send players to Euro 2028, out of a €244m programme for the 2024–28 cycle.
