Netflix's Heartbreaking 4-Episode Fantasy Takes Less Than 4 Hours to Binge

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

It's easy for shows on Netflix to get lost in the shuffle sometimes, with the streamer releasing so many on a regular basis that it can be tough to keep track of the good ones. Fortunately, the cream rises to the top, and the best shows will find a way to either break through or leave a legacy. One of those shows gets brought up a lot around the fall and holiday times, and folks continue to revisit it on a regular basis — even though it's a completely heartbreaking watch.

Lost Ollie, released back in 2022, is a four-episode limited series that follows a stuffed toy on a journey to find the little boy who lost him. It's a live-action/animation hybrid that has a lot in common with Disney's Toy Story franchise, except that it gets a lot more intense with the emotions and will likely make you cry even more than you did at Buzz and Woody holding hands in a garbage furnace. If you need a good cry, or just want to watch some of the most beautiful storytelling you've ever seen on Netflix, Lost Ollie is a quick little watch that will certainly check off both boxes.

While it plays with some big ideas, Lost Ollie is able to pack a ton into a very short amount of time. The series is just four episodes long, with each of them running between 41 and 51 minutes. When it's all said and done, Lost Ollie takes about three hours to watch, which is about the same as a pretty long movie. But the series makes great use of its episodic format, leaving plenty of opportunity to take a break or have some conversations about the deeper aspects of the story (if you're watching with younger family members).

From top to bottom, everything about Lost Ollie feels like a home run. It's a funny and adventurous story that also brings in big swells of emotion throughout, giving viewers a plethora of emotional experiences. It's also got some of the most fantastic CG animation we've ever seen on any form of television. Ollie and the other toys in the show are put into a live-action format, and they don't come off as wonky or even all that computer generated. They look about as close to real walking and talking toys as you'll see on the screen. Combined with some masterful cinematography and a lot of great production design, it's hard to look away from everything Lost Ollie has going on.

A journey from the comfort of your couch.

When you look at the credits for Lost Ollie, it's easy to see how it turned into something so magnificent. The series is based on a children's book by William Joyce, who was a concept artist on the original Toy Story and creator of the series Rolie Polie Olie. Some of his other books were adapted into animated features like Meet the Robinsons and Robots. Shannon Trindle, who previously worked on Foster's Home for Imaginary Friends, created the series for television, while Oscar-winning Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse director Peter Ramsey helmed all four episodes.

As if the creative team wasn't interesting enough, they put together a stellar cast that really takes Lost Ollie to the next level. New Girl's Jake Johnson and Jane the Virgin star Gina Rodriguez star as the parents of Billy, the main character played by Kesler Talbot. The titular Ollie is voiced by Tony winner and Frozen star Jonathan Groff, with Tim Blake Nelson and Mary J. Blige also lending their voices to the other main toys in the movie.

Lost Ollie is what happens when a lot of immensely talented people get the opportunity to pour everything they can into a collaborative project, and it's a series that a lot of families have revisited on more than one occasion. Sure, it's going to make you cry, but it's also going to make you smile an awful lot.
