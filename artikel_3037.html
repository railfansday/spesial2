MercadoLibre vs. Uber Technologies: Which Consumer Network Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

MercadoLibre's 47% revenue surge stands out, but Uber's cheaper earnings multiple and steadier balance sheet make the 2027 call far closer than the growth numbers suggest.

Investors searching for high-growth platforms often find themselves choosing between regional dominance and global ubiquity. Whether MercadoLibre (MELI +1.11%) or Uber Technologies (UBER +0.83%) fits your portfolio better depends on your specific growth objectives.

MercadoLibre serves as the primary digital commerce infrastructure for Latin America, providing essential retail and fintech services. Uber connects millions of consumers with transportation and delivery options worldwide. While both operate platform-based models, they navigate different competitive landscapes and regulatory environments, making this a classic match-up for growth-oriented investors.

MercadoLibre operates a vast digital ecosystem that includes its marketplace, the Mercado Pago fintech platform, and a comprehensive logistics network. The company maintains key partnerships with major media brands like Disney+ for its loyalty program and utilizes cloud infrastructure from Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN -0.29%). It also works with card associations like Visa Inc. (V +1.30%) and Mastercard (MA +1.32%) to facilitate payment services for its millions of active users.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $38.5 billion, which represents a growth rate of approximately 47% over the prior year. The company reported net income of roughly $1.9 billion during this same period. This significant growth is a result of expanding electronic commerce adoption and the rising volume of digital payments throughout the region.

The debt-to-equity ratio, which measures total debt relative to shareholder equity, is nearly 1.7x. As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio of 1.2x indicates its ability to cover short-term liabilities with current assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was about $10.8 billion, providing capital to reinvest in the business among retail stocks.

Uber Technologies connects over two hundred million monthly users with mobility and delivery services through its expansive global platform. The company has established significant partnerships with Tractor Supply Company (TSCO -0.49%) and Amazon to broaden its reach into grocery and retail delivery. It also relies on third-party mapping and infrastructure software from Alphabet to ensure its services run smoothly across thousands of cities.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, Uber recorded revenue of about $56.5 billion, an increase of roughly 14% compared to the previous twelve-month period. Net income for the company reached approximately $3.5 billion during this time. These results reflect the scale of the platform as it moves toward consistent profitability and stronger bottom-line performance.

Uber maintains a debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 0.4x as of its December 2025 balance sheet. Its current ratio is approximately 1.1x, which measures its capacity to pay off short-term debts with its available short-term assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow reached about $9.8 billion, allowing the company to strengthen its financial position and explore new growth initiatives.

MercadoLibre faces intense competition from global entrants and regional rivals. It also relies on mobile platforms controlled by Apple Inc. (AAPL +0.39%) and Alphabet Inc. (GOOG -0.96%) for its app distribution. Furthermore, the company manages credit risk within its lending solutions for merchants across Latin America.

Uber Technologies operates in a crowded field against rivals like Lyft (LYFT +2.23%) and DoorDash (DASH -0.62%). The platform must also manage complex legal challenges regarding how it classifies its drivers. Additionally, inflationary pressures on fuel and labor costs may impact its ability to maintain driver supply and net revenues.

Uber appears cheaper on an earnings basis, though MercadoLibre trades at a lower multiple relative to its sales over the past 12 months.

MercadoLibre has evolved from the so-called eBay of Latin America to something more akin to a combination of Amazon and PayPal Holdings Inc. (PYPL +1.68%). In 2025, the company made large investments in its Brazilian logistics network, cut down the free-shipping threshold in Brazil in June, and nearly doubled its consumer loan book. This drove impressive growth for the company and investors, with an increasing focus on financial services over delivering goods.

Yet the immediate concern with left-of-center Luis Lula winning the Brazilian presidential election last month is that the new administration will start addressing Brazil's high government and consumer debt levels, which will keep interest rates there around their current very high levels. That's bad for a consumer-loan-focused business like MercadoLibre, and shares have been hammered this week as a result.

Long-term, however, such fears are probably overblown. In fact, in the current fiscal year, revenue is expected to jump 45% to $41.9 billion, with slightly higher free cash flow and slightly lower net income. Overall, it's a good picture for MercadoLibre.

Uber revolutionized the taxi industry when it rolled out its ride-sharing network, allowing anyone with a car to earn a side gig. Both the industry and the company have come a long way.

Uber Technologies is by far the largest player in the developing ride-share market in terms of revenue. Wall Street expects Uber's sales to increase by $6 billion from last year to $58 billion this year, bringing with it net income of $6.4 billion. But as a sign of the expenses Uber faces in expanding its business model, that would be roughly $3.6 billion less income than in 2025. In the long run, its global ride-sharing, delivery, and autonomous solutions business will make for a strong business.

There is a lot that is appealing about MercadoLibre, but it comes at a premium P/E ratio that may not be warranted given the historically lower valuation the market puts on Latin America-reliant stocks. Right now, Uber is only slightly richer than the average S&P 500 stock (which trades around 19 times forward earnings). Based on valuation, the better choice for 2027 is the ride-sharing giant, Uber.
