Why ADI Global Distribution Stock Plunged This Week

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

ADI Global Distribution lost nearly half its value in a week, and the real problem isn’t revenue.

A $659 million goodwill write-down and a lowered full-year profit outlook sent ADI Global Distribution (ADIG -0.66%) tumbling this week.

The shares were down 46.4% from Friday, Nov. 6's close to Thursday, Nov. 12's close, according to data from S&P Global Market Intelligence. Over the same period, the S&P 500 gained 2.9%, and the Nasdaq-100 was up 4.8%.

Management reported a third-quarter net loss of $655 million on Thursday, Nov. 12, after recording a $659 million non-cash goodwill write-down due to persistent weakness in residential construction. Top-line revenue increased 9% year over year to $1.31 billion, topping Wall Street consensus estimates of $1.27 billion. However, profitability shrank as a larger share of orders shifted to lower-margin national accounts.

The stock's sell-off wiped out nearly half its value in five sessions, trimming its market capitalization to $745 million. That drop left the equity down 68% (as of the close on Thursday, Nov. 12) from its Aug. 11 52-week high of $30.99.

ADI Global Distribution distributes security, fire safety, audio-visual, and low-voltage electronic products to commercial contractors and equipment installers who use them in building systems. The company generates revenue by selling alarm hardware, surveillance systems, and fire protection equipment through its distribution branch network. This week's earnings report showed that while demand for commercial security remains steady, higher freight costs and facility consolidation expenses are squeezing operating margins.

Management lowered full-year 2026 revenue guidance to a range between $4.91 billion and $4.93 billion, while reducing full-year adjusted stand-alone earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) expectations to between $230 million and $240 million.

ADIG was spun off from Resideo Technologies in August 2026. The distributor faces stand-alone public company expenses alongside softer residential equipment sales. This week's goodwill write-down and outlook cut invert the spin-off thesis as attention shifts from independent growth to margin contraction and integration costs.

At a trailing price-to-sales (P/S) ratio of 0.2, the stock appears inexpensive on top-line revenue, but that depressed multiple reflects market concerns over negative net income and margin compression.

Investors will watch whether management can stabilize operating margins during the fourth quarter, where revenue is projected between $1.11 billion and $1.13 billion. The company held $208 million in cash and short-term investments as of Oct. 3, 2026, against $1.2 billion in total debt, including lease obligations, providing liquidity as it navigates operational changes. The business faces an uphill path to restore market confidence following its post-spin sell-off.

Investors may want to wait for the dust to settle before considering a position in ADIG. It continues to grow, which is a positive. A stock falling on declining revenue would be a red flag, but the main culprit here appears to be weaker profits.

Management is focused on positioning the business for long-term profitability. It is expanding into commercial markets while also targeting cost reductions. It expects $60 million in annualized cost savings by the end of 2026.

The reason investors may want to wait before buying is the fluid residential market, which remains weak and may continue to weigh on the company's results in 2027.

The next catalyst to watch is the company's fourth-quarter 2026 earnings results. Investors will want to see whether margins are stabilizing and management's outlook for 2027.
