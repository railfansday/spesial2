CAVA vs. lululemon athletica: Which Consumer Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

CAVA's 27% revenue jump and Lululemon's 1.1x sales multiple sit side by side, but one stock's premium valuation raises a question about how much growth is already priced in.

By Seena Hassouna – Updated Nov 22, 2026 at 12:17PM EST

CAVA Group (CAVA -4.67%) and lululemon Athletica (LULU -2.76%) offer investors two distinct ways to play the consumer landscape. This comparison explores whether CAVA's rapid expansion or Lululemon's established profitability makes for a better buy.

CAVA is disrupting the fast-casual segment with its Mediterranean-inspired menu, while Lululemon leads the technical apparel market through high-performance gear and global brand loyalty. Comparing their financials and risk profiles helps clarify which stock suits your long-term goals.

CAVA Group operates a Mediterranean fast-casual restaurant chain known for its pitas, salads, and customizable bowls. The company serves a diverse customer base, with a significant focus on Millennial and Gen Z diners who prioritize healthy, convenient options. The brand has quickly become a standout within restaurant etfs as it scales across nearly 500 locations and grocery retailers nationwide.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 4, 2026, CAVA generated nearly $1.4 billion in revenue. This represents an increase of roughly 27% compared to the prior 12-month period. During this period, the company reported net income of nearly $64.7 million and a net margin of approximately 4.5%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.6x, calculated by dividing total debt by shareholder equity. The current ratio is near 2.7x, comparing current assets to current liabilities to assess short-term liquidity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 28, 2025, free cash flow totaled nearly $26.1 million.

lululemon athletica designs and retails technical athletic apparel and footwear across a global network of more than 800 stores. It is a dominant force among technical apparel stocks, utilizing an omni-channel model that balances physical locations with a robust e-commerce presence. The company also fosters brand loyalty through an ambassador program and services like its technical footwear lines.

In the 12 months ended Aug. 2, 2026, Lululemon generated close to $11.1 billion in revenue. This figure represents an increase of approximately 1.7% compared to the previous 12-month period. The company achieved net income of nearly $1.4 billion during this period, resulting in a net margin of roughly 12.8%.

As of its February 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.4x. The current ratio is roughly 2.3x, indicating the company has significantly more current assets than current liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Feb. 1, 2026, free cash flow was nearly $921.7 million.

CAVA faces intense competition from established national restaurant chains, such as Chipotle Mexican Grill, as well as local dining options. The company must manage challenges in scaling operations, including the high costs of construction and labor for new locations. Additionally, its reliance on third-party delivery partners and digital channels exposes the business to potential technical failures or cybersecurity risks.

Lululemon Athletica faces risks related to its supply chain concentration, as it relies heavily on fabric suppliers and vendors in Mainland China and across the broader APAC region. The company is also managing legal and reputational risks arising from ongoing securities class action lawsuits over past statements about store traffic. Furthermore, Lululemon must compete with global giants like Nike while navigating shifts in consumer preferences and macroeconomic volatility.

Lululemon appears significantly cheaper on both earnings and sales multiples compared to the high-growth premium currently assigned to CAVA shares.

Turnarounds ask you to pay today for a fix that may take years, and that is the real gap between these two stocks. Lululemon needs a new CEO who just started, a healthier Americas business, and a better read on what shoppers want, all at once. Any one of those can slip, and the stock can stay cheap while they do. The latest quarter shows the problem. Americas sales fell as fewer shoppers visited and fewer bought, and a one-time tariff refund hid an earnings drop of about a third. Management itself expects the pressure to continue through the rest of 2026 and beyond.CAVA asks for something simpler: keep opening restaurants that work. That still makes the stock volatile, because a premium price leaves little room for a miss, and shares will fall hard if growth cools. But the business already does what the bull case needs, and you can watch that risk show up in traffic and new-restaurant results as it happens. I would rather own a risk I can see coming than a repair I have to hope for.I lean CAVA for 2027. Size it small enough that one bad quarter doesn't force you out. If Lululemon's low multiple tempts you, wait for Americas sales to stabilize before you buy, because cheap alone doesn't give a stock a reason to rise.
