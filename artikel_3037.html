'Big Short' Investor Michael Burry Thinks This Chinese Stock Is Worth About 60% More Than Its Price. Should You Buy?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Burry has stopped betting against the Nasdaq and is putting his money into cheap stocks overseas, led by one Chinese e-commerce giant.

By Jonathan Ponciano – Nov 27, 2026 at 1:33PM EST

Michael Burry made his name betting against U.S. housing before 2008, but lately, he's been betting on something far less popular: Chinese stocks.

In his Substack newsletter, Cassandra Unchained, Burry said he has closed out his remaining bearish bets on the Nasdaq-100 and now holds roughly a quarter of his portfolio in cash. What's left is tilted abroad. Among his largest positions are MercadoLibre (MELI +4.47%), Australian online retailer Temple & Webster and JD.com (JD +3.68%). Separately, stakes in Hong Kong-listed Tencent (TCEHY +3.85%), BYD (BYDDY -1.05%) and Meituan make up a little over a fifth of the portfolio.

Burry explained the overseas tilt simply by saying that U.S. stocks, in his view, are still expensive.

Michael Burry. Image source: Getty Images.

JD.com is one of China's largest online retailers, known for owning its own warehouses and delivery network. Burry puts its intrinsic value at roughly $45 a share, and with the stock at $27.35 on Friday, that implies upside of more than 60%. JD also trades at about 0.17 times sales, its lowest level on record. Burry's exposure includes call options that expire in 2029.

His timing has looked good so far. Chinese stocks posted their biggest gain in two years on Friday after Beijing signaled more support for its markets, and JD rose about 4%. The shares remain well below their 52-week high of $34.30.

Burry isn't done with semiconductor stocks, either. He sold most of his bearish put options on Nvidia (NVDA +0.80%) as the stock fell this month, while saying his original thesis "still has legs." His plan, he wrote, is to bet against Nvidia again "nearer $200/share during a period of market optimism. That time is not now." Nvidia closed Friday at $150.49.

Chinese stocks also carry risks U.S. stocks don't, including shifting government policy, U.S.-China trade fights, and accounting rules that give investors less visibility. JD's low valuation partly reflects those risks, along with fierce price competition in Chinese e-commerce.

Meanwhile, Burry can change course in days, and he often does, but the core idea is worth borrowing. When a profitable market leader trades at a record-low valuation because of fear rather than collapsing fundamentals, patient investors can be paid for waiting. If you want exposure to e-commerce stocks in China, JD is a reasonable place to start, as a small position you're prepared to hold for years. Investors who'd rather avoid China-specific risk can apply the same lesson at home: build a watch list of quality tech stocks that have been knocked down, and buy in stages.
