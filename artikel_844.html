Verstappen wins wild Las Vegas GP as Ferraris collide again

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Max Verstappen wins wild Las Vegas Grand Prix; Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri claim double podium for McLaren after late Mercedes collapse saw George Russell and Kimi Antonelli drop back; Charles Leclerc penalised for causing collision with Ferrari team-mate Lewis Hamilton

Please use Chrome browser for a more accessible video player

Max Verstappen produced a remarkable drive to win a wild Las Vegas Grand Prix that saw him withstand extreme pressure from George Russell and Kimi Antonelli before a late Mercedes collapse.

Pole-sitter Verstappen retained the lead at the start but the enormous power of a slipstream on the long straights of the Las Vegas Strip Circuit made it impossible for the leader to pull clear.

First Russell, and then later newly crowned world champion Antonelli were each able to pass Verstappen on several occasions but the Red Bull driver calmly retook the lead within a few corners every time, expertly managing the deployment of his power.

Russell, trying to pass his team-mate for second with three laps remaining, ran into a barrier after overshooting a corner and dropped down the order, before Antonelli appeared to suffer a deployment issue at the start of the final lap that allowed Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri to snatch a double podium for McLaren.

There was more unwanted drama for Ferrari as team-mates Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc collided again as they battled for sixth with 12 laps remaining, with the latter given a 10-second penalty by the stewards for causing the incident.

Pierre Gasly took fifth ahead of his team-mate Franco Colapinto to secure a major points haul for Alpine, with Hamilton recovering to seventh, having served a 10-second penalty himself earlier in the race for overtaking Norris off track.

Carlos Sainz snatched eighth from Russell on the run to the finish line to equal Williams' best result of the season, while Leclerc claimed the final point in 10th.

The result all but secures second in the drivers' standings for Verstappen, who made it four wins in six races to pull 37 points clear of third-placed Hamilton with two rounds to go in Qatar and Abu Dhabi.

McLaren's late gains enabled them to close within nine points of Red Bull in the battle for third in the constructors' standings, with Verstappen's team-mate Isack Hadjar unfortunate to finish outside the points after a front-wing failure - moments after a Safety Car stoppage that had bunched the field - dropped him from third to last.
