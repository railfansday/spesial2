Former world No 1 Jannik Sinner has undergone surgery on his troublesome knee, casting doubt over his hopes of playing at the Australian Open.

The 25-year-old has not played since ​winning his fifth Grand Slam ‌title at Wimbledon in July.

The Italian world No 2 ​said this month he was confident of returning in time for the Australian Open, which starts on January ​17, but would not rush his ⁠recovery.

Sky Italy are reporting he could be out for up to three months.

Five-time Grand Slam champion Sinner posted on social media: "Over the past few months, together with my team we've tried everything to treat my injury without surgery. After some very encouraging progress early on, my knee unfortunately didn't respond as we'd all hoped, that's why today I underwent a minor surgery to improve the condition of my iliotibial band.

"I really miss being on court and will now begin my recovery process together with my team. Thank you for all your support. See you soon."

Sinner set a new record by winning the first five Masters 1000 tournaments of the year, becoming only the second man after Novak Djokovic to complete the career Golden Masters.

But he appeared exhausted after suffering a shock early loss at the French Open before rebounding strongly at Wimbledon.

His absence has allowed Alexander Zverev to overtake him at the top of the rankings, while Carlos Alcaraz is now close behind in third after a hugely impressive comeback from his own injury issues.

Sinner was operated on in Turku, on the southwest coast of Finland, by renowned surgeon Lasse Lempainen.

The procedure involved lengthening the iliotibial band through arthroscopy, using small Z-shaped incisions to reduce tension in the area.

His knee will initially be immobilised before he begins intensive physiotherapy to regain mobility and strength, with his return to full fitness for 2027 unclear.

Sinner said he had lost a significant amount of weight after having his ⁠tonsils removed and was in ​the process of rebuilding muscle.
