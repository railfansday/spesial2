The Stock Market Just Fell 10% From The Highs. Here’s Exactly What I’m Doing With My Own Money.

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

I’ve invested through plenty of downturns since 2003, and the lessons from the last big one are guiding what I’m doing now.

By Matt Frankel, CFP® – Nov 23, 2026 at 3:12PM EST

The S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average both fell 10% from their record closes last Friday, putting them in correction territory. At the end of last week, the tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 had fallen about 18% from its peak and is approaching a bear market, typically defined as a 20% decline. Most of the damage has come from a sharp reversal in AI stocks, and the past couple of weeks have included Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation as Nvidia's (NVDA +31.48%) CEO and an 11% drop in Anthropic (ANT -1.43%) shares on its second day of trading. Then, Huang was reinstated on Monday morning, causing even more volatility (but to the upside).

I'm a Certified Financial Planner®. I've been investing since 2003, and I've lived through the financial crisis, the COVID-19 crash, and the 2022 bear market, among others. With that in mind, here's what I'm doing with my own money right now, and just as importantly, what I'm not doing.

First, I'm not dumping stocks or moving my portfolio to cash. By the time things feel calmer, prices have already recovered. I personally know a lot of people who regret bailing out of the stock market in 2008 due to panic.

It also helps to remember how common corrections are. The S&P 500 has gone through about 38 corrections and 14 bear markets since 1950, and every one of them eventually recovered. The index has had an average intra-year decline of about 14% in every year since 1980, yet its average calendar-year return over that span was positive 13.3%. In other words, drops like this one are the price of admission for long-term stock market returns.

Warren Buffett once said to "be greedy when others are fearful." And while I didn't do that during the financial crisis (I was a graduate student at the time with little money to invest), how I handled it in 2020 is one of my proudest moments to look back on as an investor.

The COVID-19 crash initially caused most stocks to plunge, but it hit real estate investment trusts (REITs) especially hard. Hotels sat empty, malls were shut down, and movie theaters went dark, and some investors openly questioned whether these businesses would survive. That's when I took a deep breath and bought shares of Ryman Hospitality Properties (RHP +1.68%), Simon Property Group (SPG +1.21%), Tanger Factory Outlets (SKT +1.99%), and EPR Properties (EPR +1.88%).

It was a nerve-wracking time to buy, but I kept asking myself whether these well-run companies, with valuable properties and solid balance sheets, would still be standing in five years. I believed they would. Well, Ryman has gone up roughly tenfold from its 2020 lows, and the others have delivered excellent returns as well. The lesson I took away is that some of the best opportunities show up when the headlines are at their worst.

Having said all that, I'm not just sitting on my hands. I'm actively looking for discounted opportunities, especially in AI stocks, since most of the selling has been concentrated there.

What stands out to me is the gap between how AI stocks are trading and how AI businesses are performing. Nvidia just reported third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier. Yet heading into that report, the stock traded for about 13 times forward earnings, below the S&P 500's multiple of about 18. Alphabet's (GOOGL +6.34%) Google Cloud more than doubled its revenue in the third quarter, and its backlog reached $561 billion.

Alphabet (GOOG +5.88%), Oracle (ORCL +12.34%), and Forgent Power (NYSE: FPS), all of which I already own, are at the top of my list. I'm also keeping an eye on stocks like Marvell Technology (MRVL +18.37%), KLA (KLAC +14.96%), and Sterling Infrastructure (STRL +12.44%). Of course, I have no idea where the bottom is, so I plan to buy in stages over the coming weeks and months.

Finally, I'm not touching my emergency fund to buy stocks, no matter how cheap they look. Any money I might need over the next few years stays out of the stock market, which is what allows me to ride out drops like this one without being forced to sell at a bad time.

Nobody knows whether this correction will turn into a bear market or how long it will last. To be clear, if I buy any of these stocks in the near future, it's entirely possible that I'll be "too early." And that's OK. I didn't hit any of the real estate stocks at their exact bottoms in 2020, and I'm not trying to time it this time either. But I'm confident that five years from now, I'll be glad I used this sell-off to add to great businesses at lower prices. That's what happened in 2020, and I think it's a good bet that it will happen again.
