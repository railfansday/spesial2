DC’s New Batman Series Is Officially Here, And It’s Changing Superman Forever

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

After over two months of Bad Seeds and a plethora of Batman updates including the climactic latest chapter of Absolute Batman, the Caped Crusader is entering a new era with Detective Comics. To close out the run of writer Tom Taylor and artist Mikel Janin, Detective Comics is entering a new six-issue storyline starting today in which Batman must clear the name of Clark Kent, who's been accused of murder. It's a fascinating storyline in which Superman's principled values make him all too cooperative in the investigation, surrendering himself in his civilian persona while ultimately leaving a Superman-sized gap in Earth's protective forces. Thus begins Clark Kent: Murderer.

The new issue released today with Detective Comics #1115 confirming the death of Esme Brennan, daughter of Smallville's mayor. The issue made a keen point out of not just the grisly nature of the killing, with two shotgun blasts to the victim, but the lack of clarity on even Superman's mind about whether he committed the crime. Batman enters the case entirely trusting of his fellow superhero and friend, but by the end of the issue, the investigation takes a turn that should make for quite the landmark case as Detective Comics investigates whether Clark is indeed a murderer.

For those who just finished Lanterns, the scene of the crime is eerily familiar. The crime appears to have originally taken place at the Smallville Ravens stadium, at which Esme is confronted and brutally shot to death. As Superman voluntarily surrenders himself as Clark Kent, who was named to be the primary suspect, Batman investigates to find Esme's body at the disposal area. This culminates with unearthing what makes the sheriff's department suspect Kent to be the culprit: his business card, and his name showing as last call from Esme's mobile phone. Things aren't looking good for Clark already.

The issue addresses the tension and logistical nightmare of Earth's most stalwart protector surrendering himself, in his Earthling alter ego while Darkseid and other threats continue to loom. Batman also faces the potential erosion of his trust in Clark as, during a tense moment where he meets with Clark in secret in his cell, Clark is unable to deny outright that he committed the crime. What this means has yet to be fully explored, and everyone in the world is fairly assuming the story is more complicated than the Big Blue Boy Scout suddenly committing cold-blooded murder during his downtime. But the lingering question remains to be how this will affect the World's Finest duo.

With subsequent issues teasing Batman's growing concerns about Superman's connection to Esme Brennan's murder, and even teaming up with the Question in Detective Comics #1117, the final pages showed simmering tension between DC's premier duo. Batman himself rationalized away the need to interrogate his suspect as usual in Clark's presence, but Clark refused to be treated with kid gloves, and asked Bruce to pose the question, point-blank, what he's too cautious to ask about: whether Clark did the deed.

Superman's upbringing is a familiar tale to most people worldwide, but this particular addition noticeably upstages his old origin story.

It already stands as a good hint that Batman is having difficulty separating his personal connection to Superman from his reputation as the World's Greatest Detective, but the other five issues will give fans the answers they need. It has hints of Identity Crisis' primary plot, similarly affecting the Justice League as a result, but with the biggest current threat being irreversible damage, however big or small, to the bond of trust shared between Batman and Superman.
