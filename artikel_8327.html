Personal Finance (Not Investing) • Re: Turning 65 in January in SoCal: Kaiser convenience vs. broader Medicare network options?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

I was going to respond to this post earlier this morning, but the phone rang or something. Anyway…

I’m 63, have had a bronze ACA plan with Kaiser SoCal (San Diego) for the past decade. I’m also a (2x now) cancer survivor. First, a rare and aggressive cancer, now something also aggressive but much more common. Just the luck of the draw, I guess. As I’m getting closer to 65, and thinking about what I’m going to do, I think I’m probably going to just stick with Kaiser.

I think in these discussions we spend a lot of energy pondering about edge cases. Scary rare diseases that you’re probably not going to get, because, well, they’re rare. My experience is that if the Kaiser doc doesn’t know, they’ll find a colleague who does, even if the colleague is outside of Kaiser. In my case, when things got complicated, they set up a consult for me with Johns Hopkins. Paid for by Kaiser, I assume, because I never saw a bill. I also discovered that is is considerably cheaper to private pay for a consult or two every year at UCSD, than it is to pay for insurance that covers UCSD, and if the world class guy at UCSD makes a recommendation, my Kaiser doc is perfectly capable of executing on it. Honestly, for most life threatening things that you’re likely to come down with, the standard of care is basically a flow chart that everybody follows. When I went to UCSD for the second opinion, even for the rare cancer, the treatment recommendation was exactly the same as what Kaiser offered, and the UCSD doc said to stick with Kaiser. He also said that if the cancer became advanced, he might have things to offer, and he could get Kaiser to refer me then. Which fortunately hasn’t happened yet.
