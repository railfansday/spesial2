Nike Stock Just Plunged Despite the Company's New Turnaround Plan. Here's What Spooked Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Nike laid out an ambitious plan to more than double its profit margins by 2031. But the fine print shows two more years of shrinking sales first.

Nike (NKE -7.04%) investors got a look at the company's long-term plan on Tuesday, and at first they seemed to love it. Shares climbed as high as $40.68 in the morning, up about 6% from Monday's close, as Nike held its first investor day in nine years.

The excitement didn't last. By early afternoon, the stock had reversed to around $37, down about 3% on the day.

Nike calls its new strategy "Full Stride Ahead." In its investor day release, the company set a 2031 framework that looks strong on paper:

In addition, a cost-cutting program called Pace is expected to deliver about $2.5 billion in annual savings by 2031, though it will cost about $1.3 billion in charges to carry out.

The plan is also about focus. Nike is concentrating on five priority sports (running, training, global football, basketball and outdoor) plus its sportswear brands, Nike Sportswear, Jordan and Converse, across 23 priority countries. The company expects those areas to drive more than 90% of its revenue growth through fiscal 2031. It also plans to restore Jordan's "premium positioning and marketplace health."

"Sport has never played a bigger role in people's lives," CEO Elliott Hill said.

The plan makes it clear that this isn't, by any means, an immediate turnaround story. Nike reaffirmed that revenue will fall by a high-single-digit percentage this fiscal year, with operating income falling even faster. Next year, it expects revenue declines to "moderate," not reverse.

Nike isn't targeting a return to growth until fiscal 2029, and even then only at the pace of the overall market. The company called fiscal 2027 and 2028 a period of investment, and acknowledged the short-term pain. “We recognize that 2028 will be a challenging year financially, this is where the work to reposition the portfolio and strengthen the business becomes increasingly important,” the firm said.

That means two more years of shrinking sales before the turnaround shows up in the top line. Nike's own CFO, Dave Denton, acknowledged the gap: "Our current financial performance does not yet reflect the full potential of those assets."

The company is also still working through a "reset" in Greater China, one of its biggest markets.

At about $36 a share, Nike trades at roughly 30 times the midpoint of this year's adjusted earnings guidance. That's expensive for a company with falling sales.

But if Nike hits the midpoint of its 2031 target, $3.25 a share, today's price would be only about 11 times those earnings. Investors are essentially being asked to pay for a recovery that's five years away.

The dividend, of course, helps while you wait. Nike expects to pay about $2.4 billion to shareholders this fiscal year, roughly a 4% yield on its current market value of nearly $55 billion.

Nike's brands remain among the strongest in the world, and the plan is specific, with clear milestones that investors can track. The stock is also far below its 52-week high of about $69, and vastly below (roughly 80%) its all-time closing high of $161.97 in November 2021.

But long turnarounds carry real risks. Consumer tastes can shift, tariffs and inflation could squeeze margins, and rivals won't sit still while Nike resets.

For patient investors, Nike at these prices could be a reasonable bet on a recovery among consumer stocks, with a solid dividend for the wait and strong upside if the firm gets close to prior highs. But just don't expect quick results. The real test comes in fiscal 2029, when Nike says growth should finally return.
