The killer mistake Vikings must avoid at 2026 NFL trade deadline

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The killer mistake Vikings must avoid at 2026 NFL trade deadline appeared first on ClutchPoints. Add ClutchPoints as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

The Vikings' biggest risk at the November 10 deadline is standing pat with a 6-1 record and an offense that still has obvious problems. Minnesota has a top-tier defense and a rare chance to win now, so a bold move for a quarterback or a playmaker like Jonathan Taylor makes sense.

Mandatory Credit: Brad Rempel-Imagn Images

Minnesota is 6-1, but the offense is the weak link. Kyler Murray has struggled, and the Vikings have not ruled out any quarterback option, including a trade. J.J. McCarthy is no longer an option after the team dealt him to the Giants in late September for a fifth-round pick. Murray signed a one-year contract in March after Arizona released him, which shows the Vikings never viewed him as a long-term answer.

Murray also dealt with hamstring tightness at the end of Sunday's game, which put his Monday night status in doubt. Carson Wentz is the backup, but he is not a long-term fix either. A team with possibly the best defense in the league cannot count on luck at the most important position.

Taylor would give the Vikings a clear offensive identity. He has rushed for over 1,000 yards with Philip Rivers, Carson Wentz, Anthony Richardson, Joe Flacco and Daniel Jones, so he does not depend on the quarterback. He would also take pressure off Murray, whose best trait this season has been making plays with his legs.

Minnesota has been linked to him from the start. The Vikings and Giants are considered the most aggressive teams looking for a back. The price is high and it will take at least a second-round pick plus more. There's expectations that the Giants could meet Indianapolis' demands. If the Vikings want Taylor, they cannot wait for the price to drop.

Taylor would not fix everything. The Vikings could add him and still have trouble blocking for him. But a clear upgrade at running back helps a quarterback who is struggling. If the Colts price Taylor out of reach, Titans back Tony Pollard is a fallback.

The cap is the reason to act. Taylor is owed $16.02 million in 2027, but the money is not guaranteed until five days after the Super Bowl, so a team can trade for him with no commitment beyond this season. After the season, Minnesota will have to pay Jordan Addison and Dallas Turner, among others, and may need a quarterback.

The Vikings also cannot fit Taylor under their cap right now. They would need to rearrange a deal or two, or ask the Colts to absorb part of his salary, which raises the price. That is a real hurdle, but the options are not mutually exclusive. The cap squeeze is the reason to move now, because a 6-1 team with the league's best defense will not have a better chance to win a championship.

Minnesota's front office should not treat a good record as proof the roster is complete. A move for Taylor, even at a steep price, helps the offense, eases the burden on Murray and shows the team is serious about this season. Doing nothing risks wasting a rare window.
