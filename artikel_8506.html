Who Counts as Your Dependent? A Child or Relative Who Passes the IRS’s Tests

Claim someone as your dependent only if they pass every IRS test for a qualifying child or a qualifying relative, tests built on how you’re related, their age and income, where they live, and who supports them. Check each one before you file, since a dependent can bring tax credits and a better filing status.

A qualifying child under 17 can bring the child tax credit, and other dependents can bring the credit for other dependents, worth up to $500 each. Either kind can also help you file as head of household, usually at lower rates than filing single, or claim the child and dependent care credit.

A qualifying child can’t pay over half of their own support, but anyone can cover the rest. A qualifying relative must get more than half of their total support from you, though relatives who together pay over half can agree in writing that one who paid over 10% claims them.

A qualifying child is your child, stepchild, foster child, sibling, or stepsibling, or a descendant of one, such as a grandchild. They must live with you over half the year, and temporary absences, like time at school, count as time with you. Unless permanently and totally disabled, they must also be under 19 at year’s end, or 24 if a full-time student.

A qualifying relative must be a relative on the IRS’s list, such as a parent, or live with you all year, and have gross income under a yearly IRS limit.

A parent beats anyone else claiming the same child. If parents on separate returns both claim, the IRS picks the one the child lived with longer, then the one with the higher adjusted gross income (AGI), total income minus certain adjustments. A nonparent can claim a child the parents pass up only with a higher AGI than every parent who could.

Say your widowed mother lives on untaxed Social Security and you pay most of her bills. Those benefits don’t count toward her gross income, but IRS Publication 501 counts what she spends on herself in her total support. If you pay more than half of that total, you can claim her and the credit for other dependents.

Before you file, run each person through the dependent interview in the IRS’s Interactive Tax Assistant. It asks what support you provided, so total what everyone spent on them first.
