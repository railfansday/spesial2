I'm not seeing how you got more in SPYD divs than the RMD. I didn't do an exhaustive study but recently the yield is about 4%, was the NAV significantly lower in the past possibly bumping the yield up to say 8%?
My RMDs as a percentage of the balance as of the end of the previous year have been about 4%. The NAV is not directly significant. The market price at the end of each year is directly significant because it affects the dollar balance of the IRA, which affects the RMD amount to be taken during the following year.

As I mentioned in my first post to this topic, the approach will not last indefinitely. If I live long enough, for some future years, the RMD as a percentage of the balance at the end of the previous year will be greater than the distribution yield relative to the market price at the end of the previous year.

With the first year that I had a RMD, the RMD as a percentage of the balance at the end of the previous year was 4.05%. On that date the closing market price of the fund was $39.19 per share. During the year, the sum of the four dividends was $1.8637 per share. That makes the distribution yield relative to the market price used to determine the RMD amount 4.75% (=1.8637/39.19), which is greater than the RMD percentage.

That is a slight simplification because I take at least the RMD amount after the dividend for the third quarter and before the dividend for the fourth quater. The sum of the four dividends from the one for December of the previous year through the one for the September of the year was $1.8509. That makes the distribution yield relative to the market price used to determine the RMD amount 4.72% (=1.8509/39.39).

Another slight simplification is omitting the very small effect on the end of year balance from the return on the cash in the account.
