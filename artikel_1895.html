'Scottish football has a serious problem, and Rangers are part of the reason' - ex-chief scout

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Scottish football is facing a serious problem with developing talent to perform at the top level after the latest controversy at Rangers.

For the first time ever, there were no Scottish players named in either side's starting XI as Rangers beat Motherwell 2-1 in a close-fought clash at Fir Park on 21 November.

That has led to outrage from supporters and pundits, but underlines a bigger concern for Scottish football and the future for young talents coming through.

Former Man United chief scout Mick Brown told Football Insider that Rangers and other SPFL clubs are looking abroad in their efforts to improve their starting XIs.

Concerns have grown behind the scenes about the lack of top-class talents coming through in Scotland, and the lack of trust shown by their leading clubs in young players.

Sebastien Pogocnoli's national team side are also facing a decline given the low number of top emerging talents coming through, and something must be done to resolve it.

Brown told Football Insider during the summer that Rangers have to be clever with their recruitment, with scouts working across Europe to identify top young players.

While recruiting from within Scotland remains a big part of their plans, the increasing globalisation of the game means there are better deals to be had signing players from abroad.

Scottish players are being priced out of moves to top clubs, with Motherwell also part of the discussion, while clubs feel they cannot afford to take gambles.

"I think it's become clear that Scottish football has a serious problem at the moment," Brown told Football Insider.

"For there to be a game between two of the top Scottish clubs, Rangers and Motherwell, without a single Scottish player in either starting XI, that has got to be a worry.

"We've spoken before about the makeup of this Rangers squad and where they have to look to recruit players, and unfortunately, they're looking outside of Scotland.

"It can be pricey to sign domestic players, who come at a premium in every country, but at the same time, how many top young talents are coming through at the moment?

"There aren't any standout players in Scotland who you'd look at and think, Rangers have to go and sign him. That's the problem at the end of the day.

"These players aren't being produced at the moment, they're not coming through, and that's a problem not just for the clubs, but for the national team as well.

"It's hard to say what they can do about it, you can't force clubs to sign certain players if there aren't players of that top quality coming through.

"But it's certainly a concern and I'm sure discussions are being held about their next steps behind the scenes, because something has to be done to help the issue."

Rangers spend £35million to improve their squad during the summer, and will likely have to spend big again in January to address major weaknesses in Derek McInnes' side.

That is part of the problem in Scottish football, the top clubs are forced to look elsewhere to improve their squads if they wish to continue competing at the top level.

Football Insider revealed last month that Rangers are weighing up potential January transfers to improve their squad after assessing a number of key positions.

There are concerns that further injuries, which have been an issue this season, or unforeseen circumstances could mean Rangers are forced to act in the market.

There are also concerns about a number of players on the fringes of the squad, and potential departures or opportunities to upgrade are unlikely to be ignored.
