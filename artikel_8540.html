Investing - Theory, News &amp; General • Re: I Bonds Mega Thread (I Bond Heads Rejoice!)

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Well, I no longer "rejoice" for I Bonds, and can't recommend it in good conscience to anyone.

I hold I Bonds, my spouse holds I Bonds, and I convinced my parents to hold I Bonds. But dealing with their customer service and unacceptable backlog of 10+ months, and the difficulty of accessing anything, I've concluded it just isn't worth the trouble.

One parent passed, and despite multiple phone calls, I can't get anywhere with them. They can't confirm anything beyond the existence on the account, even though I have a death certificate and executor paperwork from the courts. They require certain forms for me to fill out, but since they won't confirm whether or not there is beneficiary (even though by law executors can request this information), I can't know which form to fill out--because there's one form to complete if there is a beneficiary, and there's a different form to fill out if there is no beneficiary. Each would require different accompanying documentation. It's like a Catch-22. So one representative said to fill out all of the forms just in case, and include all of the accompanying documentation. They only take certified copies, so I need to get additional copies from the courts for the just in case.

Additionally, every form I submit needs to have a signature guarantee. One parent's bank does not provide them anymore. It took me some time to find a place that would do it.

Next up, surviving parent how has a new bank account since their family member needs to help them manage finances. It should be a simple thing to add it on TreasuryDirect.gov, but there is an error. Customer service says we need to open a new account, and then submit transfer documentation. That also takes 10+ months. Surviving parents has very large medical and caregiving bills.

After multiple phone calls over the past two months, I asked if I could speak to a supervisor. They said supervisors have no contact information or phone extensions. I asked where I could file a complaint. They said I could write a letter, but there is no department that receives these types of letters.

By the numbers, I Bonds have some great attributes. But in practical reality, their organization is completely broken and if my parents can't depend on them whatsoever, I don't see how my spouse and I could either--or anyone for that matter. It's like a game of roulette. I'm sure it works for many people just fine, but I made the same assumptions for my family and here we are.

I am pulling all of our I Bonds out regardless of what interest is due, and I will look at anything but Treasury Direct for my fixed income and cash.
I am following up on my post earlier to update the forum on how this process is playing out.

A month ago, I received two nearly identical emails from Treasury Direct, each of which included my customer number and the request number of the documents filed. As a reminder, the first was to distribute deceased parent’s iBonds into surviving parent’s bank account, and the second was to simply withdraw the iBonds of the surviving parent’s and deposit it into the same bank account.

The emails state: “Dear Customer, This is a system generated email to communicate the Request that was previously received in our office has been assigned. Once it has been fully reviewed, you will be contacted if additional information is needed to process the Request. Assuming everything is in order to complete your Request, we expect to complete it in the next 2-3 weeks. That time will be extended if we have to contact you for additional information. We appreciate your continued patience. Thank you, Treasury Services.”

I was surprised, because over the phone they said it could take 10+ months, and it had only been about three months. Optimistically, I thought they had gotten their act together, and that as the email noted, within 2-3 weeks they would distribute that money to surviving parent. As it has been a month now since the email was sent to me, I called this morning to inquire. What the customer rep told me was that the case had indeed been assigned to someone, but that that person would then review it and assign it to our “actual” case manager/reviewer, and that it would take the full 10+ months.

When I read them the email and asked what I got wrong, they admitted that the email was misleading. In fact, she said that it had been pointed out many times before, and that they already know the email is problematic. And yet, it still goes out.

Because of the email stating 2-3 weeks, I told my out-of-state lawyer that it looked like we wouldn’t need to request the court an extension to keep deceased parent’s estate probate process open. But now we will indeed need to contact the judge to ask for an extension.

Their system is broken not only in terms of their untenable 10+ month delay, but their communications processes are as well. This is increasing the amount of time I am spending communicating with my legal team, and the courts, and costing real dollars. Purchase I Bonds at your own risk. If you’re young and single, the last thing you are probably thinking about is estate planning. However, for those who take care in making estate plans, I can’t in good conscience recommend they buy any fixed income products through Treasury Direct. When I calculated the amount I might save by deferring taxes with I Bonds, it’s just not worth the trouble for my beneficiaries, should something happen to me.

For those of you with Treasury Direct accounts, I pray you never die before getting to withdraw all of your funds.
