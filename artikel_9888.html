Replaying Yesterday: Designing a Date Picker for a Daily Puzzle

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A daily puzzle is a timestamped resource with a very particular lifetime. It exists once, for everybody, and then it is gone. That is the design, and it is a good one, because it is what makes the set shared.

It also creates a demand the game itself does not serve: "let me have that one back". People want to replay a set they enjoyed, look up a card they were unsure about, or try a different approach on a puzzle that beat them.

The answer is a replay view, and the interesting part is not the view. It is the clock.

When a daily puzzle rolls over, it does not roll over at midnight where your player lives. This one changes at five in the morning in Toronto. That means "today" is a property of the puzzle, not of the device, and two players in different timezones can be looking at two different sets and both be correct.

Get this wrong and the replay picker becomes a source of bug reports that are impossible to reproduce: the player says yesterday is missing, and for them it genuinely was, because the set had not been published yet in the puzzle's timezone when they went looking.

The other design decision is that a replay should look like the original. The moment the replay opens a different board with a different layout, players stop trusting the scores they got and start wondering whether the replay is the real thing. daily orbs unlimited opens past sets in the same board you already know, which is the only version of replay that feels honest.
