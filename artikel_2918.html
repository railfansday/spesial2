A SpaceX-Tesla Deal Could Come in December. Here's Why 1 Group of Investors Should Be Worried.

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

A merger is likely to make Tesla's robotaxi more competitive in the future, spelling trouble for its rivals.

By Lee Samaha – Nov 24, 2026 at 4:07PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Space Exploration Technologies ›

A merger between Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -5.65%) and Tesla (TSLA -0.20%) would have far-reaching consequences, not least by combining a leader in physical artificial intelligence (AI) -- Tesla's electric vehicles, robotaxi, and Optimus robots -- with a company that has a rapidly growing AI infrastructure services business (SpaceX).

That combination could have profound consequences for the major near-term catalyst for both companies, namely robotaxi, and that should be considered a threat to Uber Technologies (UBER +0.98%). Here's why.

To be clear and consistent, I don't think aggressively pursuing a merger is in the best interest of Tesla shareholders right now. In truth, we don't know if a bid will occur and what the terms will be. However, it strikes me that Tesla shareholders have waited for years and endured plenty of missed robotaxi timelines, the disappointments of Cybertruck, and industrywide downward revisions to electric vehicle sales, only to sell out just as Tesla is set to scale up its robotaxi rollout.

That's an issue for Tesla shareholders, as they could end up with a larger share of the new company than the 47% ownership stake Bank of America analysts estimated in an all-stock deal. Still, I think there's another group of shareholders who should be more worried: Uber shareholders.

A merger would create even better conditions for Tesla to realize its ambition to become a transportation-as-a-service (TaaS) company running a fleet of low-cost-per-mile Cybercabs. That's a direct threat to Uber's marketplace, at least in the areas where Cybercab has regulatory clearance. The threat has not gone unnoticed, and there's a reason Uber already has multi-partner autonomous robotaxi arrangements with companies like Waymo, Zoox, and Wayve.

When Tesla CEO Elon Musk says the "vast majority" of Cybercab will be "Tesla-owned and Transport-as-a-Service," this is highly unlikely to encompass a partnership with Uber.

In addition, merging with SpaceX would bring many benefits for Tesla's robotaxi.

All told, everything points to a merger making it easier for Tesla to ramp robotaxi and drive Cybercab's total cost of ownership (TCO) per mile lower. The latter is the key metric to follow, as it would facilitate a virtuous cycle in which low ride fares encourage further Cybercab fleet expansion. That's a real risk for Uber, because if its robotaxi partners can't achieve a comparable TCO, then Tesla's robotaxi could make significant inroads into some of its most profitable geographies.
