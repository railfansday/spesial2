Alleged Member of “8-Digits Team” Ransomware Group Extradited from Italy for Role in Major Data Extortion Campaign Against U.S. Hospitals, Healthcare Entities, and Schools

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Roman Khlynovskiy, 43, a Kazak national, made his initial appearance in Knoxville, Tennessee, federal court today after being extradited from Italy to face charges for his alleged role in a ransomware and extortion conspiracy that targeted U.S. schools, hospitals, and healthcare entities.

“The defendant is charged with participating in one of the largest healthcare data breaches in decades,” said Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “The indictment alleges a conspiracy that targeted hospitals and healthcare providers, stole vast quantities of sensitive patient information, and then sought to profit by threatening some of the most vulnerable members of our society. These alleged crimes exploited the deeply personal medical information of millions of people.”

“Khlynovskiy and other members of the ‘8-Digits Team’ repeatedly targeted hospitals and schools, extorting victims and threatening to release highly sensitive information,” said FBI Director Kash Patel. “This widespread extortion campaign resulted in over $500 million in ransom demands. With the help of our Italian law enforcement partners, Khlynovskiy has been extradited to the U.S. to face trial. This is part of this FBI’s most aggressive work in history to work with our partners and return fugitives to the U.S., including 84 Foreign Transfers of Custody (FTOCs) over the last 21 months, up 71%, and 157 extraditions, up 121%. Those who victimize Americans cannot evade justice by operating overseas, and the FBI will continue to pursue them wherever they may be.”

“The U.S. Attorney’s Office is committed to protecting Americans from wrongdoers who seek to exploit others for profit,” said U.S. Attorney Francis M. Hamilton III for the Eastern District of Tennessee. “Technology may permit cybercriminals to perpetuate crimes remotely, from far outside the District, but distance offers no refuge. This Office, in collaboration with our agency partners, will aggressively pursue those who attempt to victimize our citizens wherever they may be found.”

According to the indictment, Khlynovskiy was a member of the “8-Digits Team,” which was affiliated with the INC and Lynx ransomware. According to court documents, members of the conspiracy targeted victims in at least six states, including both schools and hospitals. After gaining access to victims’ networks, the co-conspirators exfiltrated data and then extorted the victims by demanding payments in exchange for promises not to release the stolen information. The conspirators boasted that they possessed over 50 terabytes of data consisting of more than 70 million confidential health records. The conspirators also threatened to publicize this highly sensitive medical information to increase pressure on victims to pay ransom demands. The coconspirators’ actions resulted in over $500 million in ransom demands.

Khlynovskiy is charged with conspiracy to commit computer fraud and identity theft, conspiracy to commit wire fraud, extortion involving computers, conspiracy to commit money laundering, and threats to disclose nonconsensual intimate images in violation of the TAKE IT DOWN Act. If convicted, he faces a maximum penalty of 27 years in prison.

Senior Counsels Thomas Dougherty and Bryce Rosenbower of the Criminal Division’s Computer Crime and Intellectual Property Section and Assistant U.S. Attorney Jeremy Dykes for the Eastern District of Tennessee are prosecuting the case. The Justice Department’s Office of International Affairs worked with the government of Italy to secure the arrest and extradition of Khlynovskiy. The Ukraine’s State Bureau of Investigation and Prosecutor General’s Office and the Lithuanian authorities provided substantial assistance.

CCIPS investigates and prosecutes cybercrime and intellectual property (IP) crime in coordination with domestic and international law enforcement agencies, often with assistance from the private sector. Since 2020, CCIPS has secured the conviction of over 220 cyber and IP criminals, and court orders for the return of over $500 million in victim funds.
