The last 7 permanent Swansea City managers ranked from worst to best

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Swansea City's eight years outside the Premier League have been a rather mixed experience, with the club struggling to rediscover the consistency and ambition that once made them such an established top-flight outfit.

Since Graham Potter's arrival in 2018, following their relegation, the Swans have experienced some encouraging highs but plenty of frustrating lows. However, those lows have been better than many of their Championship peers, having finished in the bottom half of the Championship just twice.

In fact, Swansea City placed 15th in 2021/22 and 14th in 2023/24, while their four other finishes have seen them end 10th or 11th. Their best opportunities for a return to the Premier League came from 2019–2021, after one particular manager guided them into the play-offs in consecutive campaigns, finishing sixth and then fourth.

Swansea remain an ambitious club with genuine aspirations of returning to the Premier League, but they lack the financial muscle and resources of many Championship rivals. Their recruitment budget, wage structure, and ability to retain their best players can all hold them back, particularly when competing against clubs with parachute payments and greater spending power.

That said, there had also been genuine optimism surrounding the club again this season, with Vitor Matos overseeing an encouraging start that had Swansea competing around the automatic promotion places in 2026/27. However, his recent departure for Ligue 1 side Strasbourg has thrown their campaign into uncertainty, leaving the club searching for another permanent appointment recently.

It would be unfair to judge them until they have had 10 games or more in charge, but now, with seven permanent managers having taken charge since Potter's arrival and until Matos' exit, it feels an appropriate time to assess how each has performed and rank them from worst to best.

It is hard to look past Alan Sheehan as the bottom-placed pick of the seven here. While the Irishman deserves plenty of credit for steady caretaker spells — including that five-game winning streak in early 2025 that landed him the permanent gig with Swansea — the step up to full-time Championship management proved to be a bridge too far for him and he didn't make it far into 2025/26 after taking on his first senior role.

Appointed on a three-year deal in April 2025, his tenure unravelled alarmingly quickly once the pressure of expectations set in. Managing just four league wins from the opening 15 games of the 2025/26 campaign, his side looked devoid of identity, culminating in a dismal 4-1 home defeat to Ipswich Town. Without a distinct tactical blueprint or the pull to attract interest from elsewhere, Sheehan's permanent spell was a textbook case of the interim bounce fading fast.

On paper, Michael Duff arrived at the Swansea.com Stadium with serious pedigree after guiding Barnsley to the League One play-off final. In reality, however, his appointment was a complete cultural mismatch from day one. Swansea supporters take immense pride in their possession-heavy, progressive footballing identity, yet Duff immediately pivoted toward a more direct, physical approach that saw possession figures plummet and fans quickly turn.

Playing 'The Swansea Way' is just one factor, though. With just 21 points collected from 19 league matches — the club's worst return at that stage since 2018 — and a miserable run of one win in eight, the hierarchy had little choice but to pull the plug after five months. Duff is a fine EFL operator, as shown by his subsequent stints at Huddersfield Town, Wycombe Wanderers, and now at Bradford City; but he was never right for SA1.

Luke Williams edges ahead of Duff primarily because he actually understood what Swansea City are supposed to look like on a football pitch. Having previously worked as Russell Martin’s assistant and torn up the lower tiers with a hyper-attacking Notts County side, Williams returned to South Wales in January 2024 with a clear commitment to front-foot, possession-based football. There were genuine flashes of quality during his 13 months at the helm, and he steadied the ship effectively in the second half of the 2023/24 campaign.

Ultimately, though, a lack of ruthlessness in both boxes proved to be his undoing. His side had streaks of strong form to look like serious challengers to the top six, but also poor runs far from their purple patch best. A bleak run after the turn of the year in early 2025 saw Swansea slide toward trouble, leading to his dismissal in February. Respectable, stylistically aligned, yet ultimately unable to sustain momentum.

Graham Potter may have only spent 11 months in South Wales, yet his impact on the club’s modern identity was profound. Inheriting a bloated and demoralised squad following Premier League relegation in 2018, Potter stripped things back, promoted fearless academy graduates like Dan James and Connor Roberts, and restored 'The Swansea Way' in breathtaking fashion. They may not have made the play-offs in their first Championship campaign after relegation, but building blocks were put in place by Potter.

A tenth-place Championship finish and a thrilling FA Cup quarter-final clash with Manchester City only told half the story; Swansea played some of the most fluid, intelligent football in and out of possession in the second tier. Unsurprisingly, Brighton & Hove Albion swooped in to prise him away in May 2019, and his subsequent rise to Chelsea and international management underlines just how elite a tactician Swansea had on their hands. Given their young core, Potter could have achieved plenty with more time at the club.

Arguably the most debatable positioning when comparing him to Potter, as few managers have polarised opinion in SA1 quite like Russell Martin, yet his place on the podium is thoroughly deserved. Across nearly two years in charge, Martin doubled down on extreme possession-based principles, producing some unforgettable afternoons — none more so than completing Swansea’s first-ever South Wales derby double over Cardiff City.

While his stubbornness occasionally led to frustrating defensive lapses, he oversaw clear year-on-year progression, guiding the Swans to a 10th-place finish in 2022/23 while developing stars like Joel Piroe and Nathan Wood. When newly-relegated Southampton paid compensation to make him the centerpiece of their Premier League promotion push in June 2023, it served as ultimate validation of his methods and upside when given the time and resources.

Few managers have transformed the mood in SA1 quite as dramatically as Vitor Matos. Inheriting a confidence-shot side from Alan Sheehan in November 2025, the Portuguese tactician immediately injected high-octane pressing, technical sharpness, and clear tactical structure to rescue the 2025/26 campaign. Armed with a full summer window and working alongside Jamestown Analytics’ data-led recruitment model, Matos elevated Swansea into genuine top-six contenders.

They were playing exhilarating, progressive football that had the Swans firmly on course for at least a first play-off finish since the Steve Cooper era. That trajectory was only halted because Ligue 1 outfit Strasbourg, backed by BlueCo’s financial muscle, moved decisively to poach him in recent weeks. Had he stayed in South Wales, a top-six Championship berth felt virtually assured, making second place thoroughly deserved.

There is simply no debating top spot. Steve Cooper remains the gold standard of Swansea's post-Premier League era, delivering back-to-back Championship play-off finishes in 2019/20 and 2020/21 — including a trip to the Wembley final — despite operating under strict financial constraints and constant squad turnover. While his pragmatic, counter-attacking setup wasn't always purist 'Swansea Way' football, Cooper possessed a rare knack for squeezing every ounce of quality out of players.

He used his England youth connections to get plenty out of loan signings like Rhian Brewster, Marc Guehi, Morgan Gibbs-White, and Conor Gallagher. He made the Swans fiercely difficult to beat and instilled a winning mentality that hasn't been matched since. His subsequent achievements taking Nottingham Forest back to the Premier League only cement his status as the standout boss on this list. Perhaps Matos could have surpassed him had he remained in charge, but Cooper simply has to top the seven.
