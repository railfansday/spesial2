Costco Hasn't Split Its Stock Since 2000. Here's Why 2027 Could Be the Year.

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

A four-digit share price has investors wondering if a stock split is on the table next year.

By Neil Patel – Nov 25, 2026 at 12:00AM EST

10 stocks we like better than Costco Wholesale ›

Forward stock splits receive a lot of attention from investors. It might be because this move reminds shareholders how well the business has performed in the past. And it's a signal of confidence from the management team that executives are optimistic about the future. Investor psychology gets a boost.

If we focus just on the fundamentals, nothing really changes. Operations resume as normal. The same employees sell the same products and services to the same customers. It's business as usual.

The data is still worth highlighting. According to Bank of America and Bloomberg, companies that undergo a forward stock split see their share prices rise an average of 25% over the subsequent 12 months. Naturally, investors try to figure out who the next big candidates are, so they can potentially capture sizable gains in their portfolios.

This brings up Costco (COST +2.23%). It hasn't split its stock since 2000, but it could take action next year. Here's why.

Costco's board of directors approved a 2-for-1 split on Dec. 2, 1999. It took effect on Jan. 14, 2000, turning a roughly $98 stock into a $49 one.

The business was performing well. Net revenue jumped 16% year over year in Q1 2020, and net income totaled $129.3 million.

Shares were pricey, too. The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio hit 44.3 at the start of 2000. In that era, split-adjusted prices were the standard way to keep an expensive stock within reach of everyday investors.

Fast-forward to today. Costco closed at $1,097.91 on Nov. 23, more than 11 times the price that triggered the last split. In fiscal 2026 (ended Aug. 30), net sales rose 10.1% to $297.2 billion, and diluted earnings per share (EPS) climbed 14% to $20.76. The trailing P/E is near 53.

Record share price. Healthy growth. A premium valuation. Today's setup rhymes with 2000.

Rival Walmart also split 3-for-1 in February 2024. This was partly to help employees buy shares.

But management hasn't tipped its hand. On the fiscal 2025 first-quarter call in December 2024, chief financial officer Gary Millerchip said, "There isn't a plan at this time for a stock split." He pointed to fractional-share trading, but said Costco would keep evaluating the idea.

Executives are clearly confident in the company's position. On Nov. 10, the board declared a $30 per-share special dividend worth $13.3 billion. With the share price above $1,000, however, the split question probably won't go away in 2027.

Front-running a stock split is a short-term game. Long-term investors can avoid it. Bank of America's research still found that about 30% of split stocks fell over the following 12 months.

There's a much simpler approach to follow: Find businesses worth owning for five years or more. Costco makes the grade.

Its massive scale lets it buy in bulk, keep markups low, and still run lean. Overhead costs ate up just 8.9% of Q4 merchandise sales.

Members are fiercely loyal. The U.S. and Canada renewal rate hit 92.3%. And paid memberships grew 3.8% to 84.1 million households in the fourth quarter. Demand is steady, with worldwide traffic up 3.3%.

And profits keep rising. Net income grew 46% from $6.3 billion in fiscal 2023 to $9.2 billion in fiscal 2026.

This company belongs on every long-term investor's watch list. But the price tag causes hesitation.

Costco's P/E ratio is 159% more expensive than the S&P 500. The market seemingly always places a premium on this retail stock. A wonderful business can still be a disappointing investment at the wrong price, however.

With Costco, quality is never in doubt. The real question is always valuation.
