Dog Food Is Being Recalled Over A Possible Salmonella And Listeria Risk For Dogs And Humans

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

It could already be sitting in your fridge or freezer.

If you recently stocked up on grass-fed ground beef, here's one grocery item you might want to double-check before dinner.

According to the U.S. Department of Agriculture's Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS), Idaho-based Mountain West Food Group, LLC is recalling about 2,855 pounds of raw ground beef that may be contaminated with E. coli O26. The agency says the issue popped up during routine FSIS testing, which detected the bacteria in a sample.

The product in question is sold in 16-ounce (1-pound) vacuum-sealed packages labeled "FORWARD FARMS GRASS-FED GROUND BEEF." FSIS says the packages have a "Use or Freeze By 01/13/26" date, and you’ll also see "EST 2083" printed on the side of the packaging. The beef was produced on December 16, 2025, and shipped to distributors in California, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Pennsylvania, and Washington for retail distribution.

As for what you should do, the FSIS is keeping it simple: don't eat it. If you have the recalled product, throw it away or return it to where you bought it. Even if it looks and smells fine, that doesn't mean it’s in the clear.

As for symptoms, E. coli infections can range from rough stomach issues to more serious complications. FSIS notes that certain groups, including young children, older adults, and people with weakened immune systems, are at higher risk for severe illness. If someone in your household recently ate ground beef and then starts feeling seriously unwell, it's worth calling a healthcare provider.

FSIS also reminds consumers that when ground beef isn’t recalled, the safest move is to cook it properly to 160 degrees Fahrenheit (and yes, a food thermometer is the only way to be sure).

So, if your next burger night involves Forward Farms packaging, maybe sit this one out—or double-check that label before firing up the grill.
