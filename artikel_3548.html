Lionsgate's terrible 2024 is a distant memory, thanks to the remarkable 2026 that the studio has had so far. This summer, Lionsgate released the music biopic Michael, which grossed over $1 billion worldwide and more than $370 million domestically. The movie missed the coveted $100 million mark in its domestic debut by a fraction. However, Lionsgate's latest release will comfortably top this benchmark this weekend, delivering the studio's biggest debut in over a decade.

We're talking, of course, about The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping. It's the sixth installment in the dystopian sci-fi franchise based on Suzanne Collins' bestselling novels, and the second prequel following The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes. That movie was produced on a comparatively modest reported budget of $100 million, and it went on to gross around $360 million worldwide.

This was by far the lowest box-office haul in the franchise's history. The series began with director Gary Ross' original The Hunger Games film, which made nearly $700 million worldwide against a reported budget of under $70 million in 2012 and turned Jennifer Lawrence into a star. It was followed by three direct sequels directed by Francis Lawrence: The Hunger Games: Catching Fire, The Hunger Games: Mockingjay — Part 1, The Hunger Games: Mockingjay — Part 2.

Lawrence also helmed The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, which tells the origin story of the villainous President Coriolanus Snow, and Sunrise on the Reaping, which focuses on the tragic hero Haymitch Abernathy. The character was played by Woody Harrelson in the original films. The new film has received relatively muted praise from critics and is sitting at a 67% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, although its audience score is perched at a "Verified Hot" 95%.

Fan appreciation is certainly going a long way in pushing the film past expectations this weekend. Sunrise on the Reaping generated $21 million in Thursday previews, exceeding The Odyssey's preview haul and setting itself up for a domestic debut in the $120 million range. The movie is projected to break past the $200 million mark worldwide by Sunday, potentially recouping its reported $150 million budget in three days flat. Sunrise on the Reaping stars newcomer Joseph Zada in the lead role, along with a celebrated supporting cast including Jesse Plemons, Kieran Culkin, Elle Fanning, Glenn Close, and Ralph Fiennes.
