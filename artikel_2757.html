Leclerc Says Ferrari Lost One Second on the Straight in Vegas—and Both Cars Missed Q3

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton qualified 11th and 12th in Las Vegas on Friday, Ferrari's first double Q2 exit since Imola in May 2025.

Amid the chaos of tow and no-tow up and down the grid, Leclerc missed Q3 by 0.111 seconds and Hamilton by 0.167 seconds, while Kimi Antonelli took pole for Mercedes ahead of Saturday's Las Vegas GP.

Hamilton lacked grip throughout qualifying, while Leclerc was third-fastest in Q1 only to make a mistake in Q2. It is the first time this season that neither Ferrari has reached Q3. The last double Q2 exit was the 2025 Emilia Romagna GP, also in 11th and 12th, and before that the 2024 Canadian GP, with Leclerc and Carlos Sainz.

Hamilton said floor damage early in Q1 cost him three or four tenths in downforce, and he told Sky Sports he lost more time to a lack of energy deployment at the end of his final lap. "Yeah, we lost a piece of the floor on the like first or second lap of Q1 and the car balance shifted," he said after qualifying.

"I think it's like three or four tenths of the downforce we lost, and then after that we were struggling more so with sliding. But it was basically from the get-go, and then my last lap was really good at the end, but the best I could with what I had.

"Then we lost something like six tenths or something around that time, so I think with that we would have been in Q3, but otherwise I was happy with my lap."

Asked about getting the tires warm on a cold Las Vegas night, he added: "These tires have been very, very difficult this year to get working, even on hot circuits, so it's definitely been difficult."

Lewis Hamilton at the Las Vegas GP. Image Credit: Scuderia Ferrari

Asked about both cars missing Q3, Hamilton said the gap on the straights was bigger than he had expected. "I don't know, I mean, we knew long straights and stuff was going to be challenging," he said. "The gap seems to be a little bit more than I anticipated in the straight line.

"But yeah, I don't know what it is; it's not insignificant. It's a big chunk on the straights, and then I don't know; just grip-wise, it didn't feel like we had a ton of grip, but we were struggling to get the tires working, and we were just kind of nowhere."

Leclerc blamed his mistake in Q2 and a session spent managing traffic. "Yeah, I mean, I'm very disappointed as a whole," he said.

"Of course, there's the mistake in Q2 that we paid the price for, but I think that is following a lot of frustration building up with a lot of mistakes with the way we managed everything, traffic and other things, and that doesn't help, particularly on a track like this, where it's all about tires.

"You've got to put tires in the right window. I was looking more in my mirrors and trying to understand the traffic myself, which makes it very difficult to do anything. So, yeah, just very disappointed."

Charles Leclerc at the Las Vegas GP. Image Credit: Scuderia Ferrari

Leclerc said Ferrari's straight-line speed was the bigger problem, though he still expected to reach Q3. "I think we lost a second down the straight. Not I think; we lost one second down the straight, which is crazy," he said.

"It's expected, and that makes it very difficult to recover anything at the end of Ferrari power units, and on a track like this, it is the most power-sensitive track of the season, so we expect to have a hard time. But I was expecting to go through Q3, though."

Asked whether Ferrari had planned a tow between its two cars, as other teams did, Leclerc said keeping tire temperature came first. "I mean, that was not, yeah, kind of discussed in a way that we thought that tires were just everything, so keeping just the temperature of the tires was the most important for us. We know it's our weakness," he said.

He said Ferrari had a lot of GPS problems on Friday morning and didn't yet know whether that explained the team's timing problems in qualifying. Asked whether a team like Ferrari should still be making this many errors, he said: "Before commenting further, I think we need to look at what the issue is today. If it's a GPS issue, which doesn't necessarily come from us, then it's something that we cannot do much about.

"And today was painful, and that's, yeah, if it comes from us, I do agree we need to look at it and obviously improve, because, particularly in critical moments like this, we need to be there, and we didn't."
