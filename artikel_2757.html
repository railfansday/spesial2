Sixers’ bench proving its worth early on

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

It's been a common concern amongst Sixers fans for the better part of the last decade. So often, we're asking the same questions in the spring when the playoffs roll around. Do they have enough shooting? Who is going to fill the minutes in the frontcourt when Joel Embiid is not on the floor? Just how much do they have to shrink the rotation from the regular season to the playoffs to avoid getting exposed?

While NBA fans across the world were probably excited to watch all the star power the Sixers have, fans who have followed the Sixers religiously know that the team was going to have to have a good enough crop of reinforcements behind the stars. If not, another second-round exit wouldn't come as a surprise to any loyal Sixers fan. But, so far, some of the new players for Philly who are not starters have shown to be worthy investments.

Dean Wade did start on Monday night against Brooklyn, but Wade has combined for 20 points in his last two games on 7-of-12 shooting and 4-of-7 from beyond the arc. Perhaps more importantly, Wade had a total of 18 rebounds in Philadelphia's last two games, both wins. Wade has never been a high-volume offensive player anyway, but if he's picking his spots wisely on when to shoot and hitting the glass a lot, he'll keep earning minutes. Remember, a lot of times when Wade is going to be on the floor for the Sixers, it's probably because LeBron James isn't on the floor so there's certainly a rebounding void to be filled without James.

How about Jabari Walker? He's another player that's making the most of his minutes and got the start on Sunday night against Indiana with Embiid unavailable. For a player listed at 6-foot-7, Walker has been rebounding at a high level of late as well, which again should have fans feeling better about lineups in the regular season and playoffs that lack one of James or Embiid in the frontcourt.

Dominick Barlow remains sidelined for the Sixers, but he looked like one of the few trustworthy bodies when it came to frontcourt reserves in 2025-26, so if he can get on the floor sometime soon, that's another piece that should battle for playing time off the bench. Then factor in a reliable two-way veteran wing in Kentavious Caldwell-Pope and a sharpshooter like Anfernee Simons and you can start to see an 8-10 man rotation developing nicely for Philadelphia.

Oh, and let's not forget Labaron Philon. He might not be playing a ton right now, but do we really think his entire rookie season is going to be a de facto redshirt season? Simply put, one of the biggest goals for the Sixers in the regular season, aside from keeping the stars healthy, was going to be to find four or five players who they could reliably trust to come off the bench in the playoffs for what they hope is four rounds of postseason action. This isn't to say that Wade, Walker, Simons and Caldwell-Pope are guaranteed to be those four guys. But even if they're not, Barlow could eventually return and be one, Philon could grow into a bigger role, or there may only be one move Mike Gansey needs to make at the trade deadline instead of three or four.

Gansey might have caught a few strays with the team's backup center situation in the season's first couple weeks. But no one could have forecasted the Ariel Hukporti injury, and based on the way the bench is currently playing, getting to Dec. 15 with the status quo on the bench seems like an attainable goal for Philadelphia. In case you needed a refresher, Dec. 15 is the date that players who signed free agent contracts in the summer are eligible to be traded. Gansey's options should widen in less than a month, but more importantly, he might not need to make sweeping changes to the team's bench, which is something the franchise has been stuck trying to treat with lots of band-aids all too frequently in previous seasons.
