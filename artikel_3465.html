Heat Meets Ocean's 11 In Apple TV's New Thriller Series Releasing Through Christmas

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

There's a new thriller on Apple TV that's a little bit Ocean's Eleven, a little bit Heat, and it's releasing over Christmas for those looking for a new holiday watch. Apple TV has become a streamer for must-watch television. It's a long way from its beginnings, marked by plenty of missteps.

Now, Apple TV is reaching the HBO level, where you can hear that a show is coming to Apple, and just by virtue of being on the streamer, it's likely worth watching. Speaking of which, there's a new heist thriller series that began airing on Apple TV on November 13, 2026, with a finale set for December 25, 2026.

12 12 12 is the latest thriller series on Apple TV, and it combines the cat-and-mouse game from the point of view of the thief and the police, like Heat, while also featuring a creative, ensemble heist, just like in Ocean's Eleven. This 8-episode series comes from Dudi Appleton and Jim Keeble.

Anthony Mackie and Jamie Dornan star as FBI Special Agent Jack Strickland and Paul Galvin, respectively. Paul is a renowned criminal and thief who Jack believes is responsible for the death of his partner. When Paul goes for one last heist in Zurich, Jack will do just about anything to apprehend him.

12 12 12 currently has a 59% on Rotten Tomatoes, probably not what the showrunners were hoping for. Most critics agree that the show gets going towards the latter half of the series, as the end stage of the heist is shown and the final part of Jack's hunt for the criminals is revealed, but there's some disagreement about whether the journey there is worth it.

The shifting timelines and three-act approach do seem to offer a unique take on a fairly standard storyline. For others, it only made things more unnecessarily complex and confusing. The large ensemble cast that changes in between storylines also does help the chaotic nature of the show.

The unique title of 12 12 12 comes from the FBI's Principles of Crime, which states that, for a high-asset robbery, like the one Paul pulls off in Zurich, it takes 12 months of planning, 12 hours to actually do the heist, and then the police have 12 days to solve it. 12 12 12's audience is reminded of this at the top of every episode.

Furthermore, each episode is broken up into three distinct sections. One part takes place during the planning stage, another during the heist, and the next during the hunt. What we learn in each section informs what we learn in the next, and sometimes, information is revealed that changes the way we understand what we've already seen.

12 12 12 is a fairly straightforward heist show, and while the three-timeline approach is somewhat novel, it's not actually all that different from a story that fans of the heist genre will be familiar with. There's a thief trying to leave the criminal life but is forced back in for one last job, and a bitter detective who needs to catch him.

What will determine whether you'll like the show or not will probably have to do with your relationship with the two leads, Mackie and Dornan. They're both doing fine, capable work, and anytime they're on screen 12 12 12 is better for it. If those two actors don't particularly interest you, you might be better off watching Heat or Ocean's Eleven.
