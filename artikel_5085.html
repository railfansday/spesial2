29th October’s Binance hack is still in the news, making waves today thanks to North Korean hackers who are now attempting to launder stolen cryptos.

Rather than immediately converting the stolen assets into cash, the hackers are moving them through different cryptocurrency services to make their transactions harder to trace.

According to blockchain analytics firms Chainalysis and Elliptic, stolen cryptos worth more than $600 million have already passed through THORChain. Another $342 million have reportedly moved through a CoinJoin service.

Since THORChain does not require Know Your Customer (KYC) checks or censor transactions, it does not require users to verify their identities before making swaps. This has made the protocol useful for hackers attempting to move the stolen Binance funds.

THORChain is Financial Freedom The Cypherpunk Way.

The FBI has already identified wallet addresses associated with the attackers. It recently urged crypto service providers, including THORChain’s network operators, to block transactions linked to them.

And yet, despite this warning, the protocol has continued to process transactions associated with the laundering operation. In fact, THORChain has generated up to $1 million in fees from these swaps as per TRM Labs estimates.

Additionally, hackers have also used CoinJoin, a transaction-mixing technique designed to conceal transaction trails.

Well, to cash out, the hackers will still need to transfer the cryptocurrency through additional services. These would potentially include over-the-counter (OTC) brokers, where digital assets can be exchanged for traditional currencies or other forms of value.

In the words of Ari Redbord, Global Head of Policy at U.S blockchain analysis company TRM Labs,

They don’t have to get a lot ​off in order to make this all worthwhile for them.

These additional transactions create further opportunities for investigators and law enforcement agencies to identify and freeze the funds.

Around $2.85 billion has already been frozen by token issuers. However, the remaining assets are still in circulation, raising concerns. Also, TRM Labs has estimated that the laundering operation could continue for months or even years.

This update comes on the heels of the United States’ DOJ filing a forfeiture action to recover more than $1.1 billion in cryptocurrencies stolen in the hack.
