What we learned as Warriors' valiant comeback attempt falls short vs. Blazers

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

What we learned as Warriors' valiant comeback attempt falls short vs. Blazers originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

During Steve Kerr's pregame news conference before his Warriors faced the Trail Blazers on Friday night in Portland, he raised a caution flag big enough to cover Oregon.

"We're finding our rhythm offensively since the (road) trip where we really struggled," Kerr told reporters at Moda Center. "But this team is a different animal defensively. They're going to test us way more than the last couple games felt. We're going to feel that pressure. We're going to see how we can respond to it."

Not well at all, as the Warriors' one-game road trip ended with a 116-94 loss that eliminated them from NBA Cup competition.

Four Warriors scored in double figures, led by Yaxel Lendeborg's 18 points. De'Anthony Melton had 17, Brandin Podziemski 16 and Charles Bassey 12.

Portland, playing without Damian Lilliard and Ja Morant, limited the Warriors to 36.4-percent shooting from the field, including 33.3 percent from distance.

Here are three observations from the loss that dropped Golden State's record to 10-9:

The same Warriors who have won eight consecutive games at home have lost six straight as visitors because they are not the same Warriors.

The "home" Warriors apply ball movement and accurate shooting, and they turn up their defense when needed. The "visitor" Warriors enter an arena and immediately fall apart on both ends.

Golden State fell behind so quickly and decisively that its 14th point didn't arrive until the game's 15th minute. The Blazers by then had piled up 47 points. The Warriors were singing the same song as on their last road trip, when they lost the last four games by an average of 27.3 points.

The Warriors once again didn't match the energy of the home team, seemingly taken aback by Portland's intensity on both ends. They fell behind by 24 within nine minutes before finding enough offense in the second quarter to trim a 36-point deficit to 23 (67-44) at the half.

The first step to curing this ailment is to start taking their Chase Center vitality on the road.

While the first quarter was reminiscent of Golden State's first quarters on its last road trip, there was a striking difference that might be significant in upcoming games.

This time, the Warriors would not allow themselves to be humiliated into submission.

It was as if looking at the scoreboard early in the second quarter and seeing a 36-point deficit pumped wolverine blood through the Warriors' collective bodies.

Putting some screws into their loose defense and regaining familiarity with the hoop, Golden State won the second quarter by three (33-30) behind an offense buoyed by Melton's three triples.

The Warriors came out after halftime and rallied behind Lendeborg's 12 points in 10 minutes to win the third quarter by 10 (34-24) and enter the fourth trailing 91-78 – close enough for a fighting chance.

Ultimately, with the Blazers regaining their first-quarter zest, it was not enough. For that, the Warriors can blame their first 14 minutes.

Bassey made his fifth consecutive start at center, and there is no reason to believe he will be heading back to the bench anytime soon.

First off, Bassey at center allows Draymond Green the freedom to stray from the paint, defend perimeter players and play the passing lanes. That versatility tends to help the team's overall defense.

Secondly, Bassey's soft shooting touch makes him a potent scoring threat in in the paint. He has scored in double figures in all five starts, averaging 12.2 points per game.

His 12 points on this night came on 6-of-9 shooting from the field. He added five rebounds and is averaging 8.4 per game since moving into the starting lineup.

Bassey continues to work on his screening, which often isn't as bad as it looks. His illegal-screen turnovers should decrease with better timing with his ball-handling teammates.
