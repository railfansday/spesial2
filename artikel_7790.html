Good noise-reducing curtains help absorb sound waves and reduce noise pollution, so they’re ideal for a bedroom, living room, home office, or home theater. Many can even double as blackout curtains or thermal curtains to help reduce natural light pollution and heating or cooling costs.

Shopping for soundproof curtains starts with choosing one of three types: insulation curtains, audio insulation sheets, and industrial blankets.

Insulation curtains look similar to regular window curtains found in most homes and can come in a range of designs as well, from neutral colors to bold patterns. The main difference is that insulation curtains are made with heavier materials that block some noise. Insulation curtains also often function as blackout curtains since the thicker material that insulates the room from sound also works to block sunlight.

Thermal insulation curtains also help lower energy bills. The same thick material that works to absorb sounds can contain thermal energy, adding a layer of insulation that prevents air from entering or escaping through windows.

While audio insulation sheets aren’t technically curtains, they can be hung to function like curtains. Audio insulation sheets are typically designed with grommets to allow them to hang from a curtain rod, making them easier to install than acoustic panels.

Audio insulation sheets can block more sound than insulation curtains and industrial blankets because they are constructed with multiple layers of heavy fabric, which makes them a nearly soundproof fabric. They also function as blackout curtains due to these multiple layers. However, the amount of fabric required to craft audio insulation sheets makes them a more expensive option.

Industrial blankets are primarily intended to protect furniture and other fragile items during the moving process. Because of this, they are made with thick and padded material, which allows them to serve as sound-eliminating curtains. Though you probably don’t want to use them to soundproof an office or other professional space long term, they can serve as excellent temporary soundproof divider curtains.

Some industrial blankets are designed with grommets, which makes them easier to hang. Otherwise, you’ll have to find a way to drape them over windows or rig up a hanging mechanism so that they can help absorb outside sounds. Industrial blankets are one of the most budget-friendly options for soundproofing walls or creating a soundproof doorway curtain to reduce outdoor sounds in your indoor space.

Before purchasing soundproof curtains, check to confirm that the product is available in the appropriate size. Measure the length and width of the window, and look for a curtain that is wide and long enough to sufficiently cover it. Ideally, soundproof curtains should cover a portion of the wall, in addition to the window, especially in small spaces like apartments, to be most effective.

If possible, choose a curtain that is up to three times as wide as the window and 10 or more inches longer. Floor-to-ceiling soundproof curtains can be even more effective at noise reduction. Heavier soundproofing curtains are more effective at absorbing sounds and keeping your space quiet than lighter ones. This is especially true for lower-frequency sounds. If deciding between two soundproof curtains, the heavier option may be the better choice.

The materials used to make soundproof curtains will affect not only the way they look but also how well they block and absorb sounds. More fibrous options, such as velvet curtains, can be more effective at noise reduction. Suede and polyester are also quality options to consider.

Many soundproof curtains on the market are crafted with triple-weave fabric, making them denser and more effective. Thermal soundproof curtains also offer additional insulation as well. Thermal insulation can both help to reduce energy costs and also make the curtains more effective at absorbing sounds. A general rule of thumb to keep in mind when looking for soundproof curtains is that more material usually equals better soundproofing capabilities.

Before hanging a new set of soundproof curtains, consider the curtain rods currently installed to see if they’ll work. Since soundproof curtains are heavier than traditional curtains, make sure the curtain rod can support the weight of heavier curtains, or consider installing new curtain rods.

Depending on their dimensions, soundproof curtains could weigh 8 pounds or more, compared to about 2 pounds for traditional light-filtering curtains. In addition to rod strength, shoppers may also wish to consider buying a wraparound curtain rod. This style of curtain rod is more effective at blocking sounds since it attaches to the wall at a curve and allows the curtain to provide a more complete sound barrier.

The color of the sound-blocking curtains won’t affect their noise reduction, but it will certainly visually affect a living space. Based on personal preferences and needs, consider acoustic curtains that match the aesthetic of the room—such as modern, vintage, or farmhouse, for example—and coordinate with the decor as well.

Finally, consider how easy it will be to install and maintain the soundproof curtains. One of the easiest ways to help soundproof a space is with insulation curtains that have grommets along the top for simple installation from a curtain rod.

Depending on their design, audio insulation sheets and industrial blankets may be more challenging to install. If possible, try to preview the manufacturer’s installation directions before making a purchase. Also, take note of the manufacturer’s care and cleaning instructions for each potential purchase. Machine-washable options are easier to maintain.
