So AI indicated that your tax bill is $12,000, so it looks like you are paying all your taxes out of RMD withholding like me. So my solution is this....

Put all of your ex-wife's inheritance into a separate IRA (that you own of course). 20% of $1.74M is $348K. You (and your sister) have all the stocks in the other 80% of your portfolio. The ex-wife's fixed income earnings are enough to easily pay your taxes. So take part of your RMD from that IRA, and let your ex-wife pay your taxes. That seems like a win to me!

If stocks go down, you might even take the whole RMD from there so she doesn't get more than the 20% of your portfolio. Another win?

If stocks gone up, you win, and you might have to replenish the ex-wife IRA to get it back to 20%, but who cares, the stocks went up!

I can't think of any other solution because an IRA is tax deferred. Uncle Sam is going to get it sooner or later.

This really is all about your own attitude about paying taxes you have deferred paying. Make you asset allocation such that you can sleep at night, and do the small things you can do to pay a little less (like QCDs, tax efficient placement, the 100% treasury MM, etc).
