Dutch Bros vs. CAVA: Which Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Dutch Bros trades at a steep discount to CAVA on forward earnings and sales, but its Rebel energy drink reliance and Western concentration raise questions.

Fast-growing restaurant brands often fight for the same investor dollars as they scale nationwide. Choosing between Dutch Bros (BROS +5.67%) and CAVA Group (CAVA +0.80%) requires looking at different paths to market dominance.

Dutch Bros focuses on high-speed drive-thru beverage service with a culture-heavy approach. CAVA Group is bringing Mediterranean flavors to the fast-casual space with customizable bowls. Both companies are expanding their footprints rapidly, but they offer distinct risk profiles and valuation levels for investors looking ahead to 2027.

Dutch Bros operates a network of 1,273 shops across 27 states, focusing on a drive-thru model that prioritizes speed and customer service. A significant portion of its revenue is tied to its Rebel energy drinks, which represented nearly 22% of systemwide net sales in 2025. This reliance on specific co-packers like Portland Bottling Co. for its energy drinks creates a concentrated supply chain risk for its business among retail stocks.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $2.0 billion. This represented revenue growth of roughly 30.7% compared to the previous twelve-month period. The company also reported net income of nearly $102.0 million for this period, with a net margin of approximately 5.1%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 1.6x, indicating that total liabilities exceed shareholder equity. The current ratio, which measures the ability to cover short-term liabilities with short-term assets, is nearly 1.5x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow, which is cash from operations minus capital expenditures, was approximately $54.4 million.

CAVA Group serves guests through a network of 500 restaurants, providing a Mediterranean-inspired menu of customizable bowls and pitas. The company also maintains a retail presence, selling its branded dips and spreads through grocery channels. To maintain its offerings, it relies on more than 50 grower and rancher partners along with third-party manufacturers.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 4, 2026, revenue reached close to $1.4 billion. This performance reflected revenue growth of approximately 27% over the prior twelve months. For the same period, the company generated net income of nearly $64.7 million, resulting in a net margin of roughly 4.5%.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is nearly 0.6x. This figure indicates that shareholder equity comfortably exceeds its total debt. The current ratio is roughly 2.7x, suggesting it has significant liquidity to cover short-term obligations, while free cash flow for the fiscal year ended Dec. 28, 2025, was approximately $26.1 million.

Dutch Bros faces significant geographic concentration in the Western United States, which makes its shops vulnerable to regional conditions like wildfires. The company also deals with intensive competition in the coffee and food service industries from quick-service restaurants and delivery platforms. Furthermore, inflationary pressures on commodities such as dairy and coffee beans could impact its operating costs.

CAVA Group encounters high competitive intensity in the restaurant industry, where many rivals possess better locations and larger financial resources. The company depends heavily on digital channels and third-party delivery services, where service quality is largely outside its direct control. Rapidly expanding while trying to maintain its unique culture and unit-level profitability remains a primary execution risk for the brand.

Dutch Bros appears more attractively valued than CAVA Group as it trades at a significant discount on both a Forward P/E and P/S ratio basis.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with Dutch Bros. The growth story here is really exciting: It has turned an Oregon coffee pushcart into a drive-thru chain of about 1,300 shops, and says it has the real estate lined up to reach 2,029 by 2029. Sales at existing shops have also grown quarter after quarter for years. Profit is growing faster than sales, a sign the model gets more efficient as it scales, and new markets are opened by people who started on the coffee line and worked their way up.

To CAVA's credit, it is a well-run chain with a healthy balance sheet and a brand that has caught on across the country. But it is smaller and growing more slowly, with fewer openings planned, and it missed estimates in Q3 as growth at existing restaurants cooled.

If you plan to buy and hold for years, Dutch Bros has what you want: fast growth, rising profits, and lots of room to expand, and that outweighs the cost of building new shops. Wall Street agrees, with most analysts rating the stock a buy.
