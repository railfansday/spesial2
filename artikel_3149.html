Ares Capital Turned $10,000 Into Nearly $31,000 Over 10 Years. Can Its Dividend Do It Again?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The leading BDC is still a reliable income investment for compounding total returns.

Ares Capital (ARCC -0.26%), the largest publicly traded business development company (BDC), pays a massive forward dividend yield of 10.2%. That high yield might seem like a red flag, but it's actually maintained stable or increasing dividends for the past 17 years.

Over the past 10 years, Ares' stock price only rose about 20%. But with reinvested dividends, it delivered a total return of 209% -- which would have turned a $10,000 investment into about $30,900. Should investors count on Ares to replicate those gains over the next ten years?

BDCs provide debt and equity financing to smaller "middle market" businesses that struggle to secure loans from conventional banks because they're considered riskier borrowers. In exchange for taking on more risk, BDCs charge higher interest rates than banks.

Ares has invested in 636 companies across its $29.2 billion portfolio. To reduce its overall risk, it allocates 58% of its portfolio to first-lien secured loans and 4% to second-lien secured loans.

To grow at a steady rate, Ares needs the Fed to keep interest rates in a "Goldilocks" zone between 3% and 4%. Lower rates will help its portfolio companies but reduce its net interest income, while higher rates will hurt its portfolio companies but boost its net interest income.

The Fed funds rate, at 3.75%-4.00%, is currently in that ideal range. But if inflation stays hot, another rate hike could squeeze its portfolio companies.

As a BDC, Ares continually issues new shares to finance its loans and investments. However, it also needs to pay at least 90% of its taxable income as dividends to maintain a lower tax rate. Therefore, most of the profits from Ares' investments flow directly into its massive dividends.

BDCs usually fund their dividends with their net interest income (NII) per share rather than their earnings per share (EPS). Its net asset value (NAV) per share also gives us a clearer view of how its investments are faring, and whether its stock is overvalued relative to those assets.

From 2015 to 2025, Ares' NII per share grew from $1.62 to $2.02, its annual dividends rose from $1.57 to $1.92, and its NAV per share increased from $16.46 to $19.94. At $19, it trades at a discount to its most recent NAV per share of $19.31 in the third quarter of 2026.

Ares' core growth rates seem modest, but they're enough to fund its massive dividends. As long as its NII per share covers its dividends and its stock doesn't become overvalued, it could replicate its impressive gains from the past decade through reinvested, compounded dividends.
