Barings BDC (BBDC) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Management details a CLO refinancing and a leverage reduction plan.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

Barings BDC, Inc. (BBDC +0.34%) reported net asset value of $10.92 per share for the third quarter, while net investment income of $0.26 per share fully covered the quarterly dividend. Management indicated that the company is utilizing a new $400 million collateralized loan obligation to refinance $350 million of maturing unsecured notes and diversify its funding base. The company stated that it remains focused on middle-market corporate credit and observed increasing manager dispersion across the private credit industry as individual credit performances vary. Management reported that the investment portfolio grew to $2.5 billion at fair value, supported by $196 million in new fundings. The company also confirmed that it does not intend to pursue investments in the artificial intelligence infrastructure ecosystem, citing a preference for traditional corporate credit exposure.

Over the past several years, we have deliberately built a capital structure designed to hold up through a full cycle, a laddered maturity profile, access to multiple funding markets, and revolver capacity to support our borrowers and pipeline. With this transaction behind us, our team can stay focused on investing and portfolio management. Credit performance remains a key area of focus across the private credit market. For BBDC, nonaccruals increased modestly this quarter, but when excluding assets covered by the Sierra CSA, nonaccruals remained below 1% of the portfolio at fair value at 0.7%, and weighted average interest coverage remains healthy at 2.6x.

We are actively managing each of these situations with a focus on maximizing recoveries. One of the themes we have emphasized this year has been our expectation of greater manager dispersion in private credit, and we believe that dispersion is now clearly underway. For several years, abundant capital and a benign credit backdrop allowed most managers to post similar results, masking meaningful differences in underwriting discipline. That is changing. As idiosyncratic credit issues surface and sponsors extend hold periods, differences in nonaccrual trends, NAV stability, dividend coverage, and recovery outcomes are becoming more visible across the industry, and we expect that gap to widen as the cycle matures. Manager selection, underwriting, portfolio construction, and workout experience matters.

In our views, the managers who perform from here will be distinguished by a few things: the quality of underwriting and documentation at origination, the depth of sponsor relationships when portfolio companies need additional support, disciplined diversification and position sizing, and the experience and resources to work through stressed credits when they arise. These are not capabilities that can be built quickly in response to a turn in the cycle. Barings has invested through multiple credit cycles, and the credit metrics I just described reflect that approach. We believe investors will increasingly differentiate between managers on these factors and that BBDC will benefit from that scrutiny.

There are certainly attractive investments in the AI ecosystem, but investors should not look to BBDC for that risk profile. To sum up, credit requires active management, and we remain focused on a small number of specific situations. Overall, the portfolio is diversified, senior secured, floating rate, and positioned to generate attractive current income. And with that, I will turn the call over to Elizabeth.

The CLO is expected to close mid-November, and the floating rate notes carry a weighted average interest rate of SOFR plus 1.64%. Strategically, the transaction removes our largest near-term refinancing risk ahead of maturity, adds long-dated floating rate funding that is well matched to our 93% floating rate portfolio, diversifies our funding base, and preserves revolver capacity for new investments and unfunded commitments.

Because the 3.3% notes were issued in a very different rate environment, any refinancing will carry a higher cost. We believe the CLO is attractive relative to the alternatives available today. Turning to the balance sheet. Leverage remains within our target range of 0.90x to 1.25x.

Looking ahead, we expect leverage to move back toward the middle of our target range by year-end, driven by anticipated sales of portfolio investments to our joint venture with Jocassee and expected repayments.
