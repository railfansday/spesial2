University of Birmingham pauses plans to move Shakespeare Institute after outcry

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Vice-chancellor halts proposed relocation from Stratford-upon-Avon but offers no guarantees over future

The University of Birmingham has paused plans to move its much-loved Shakespeare Institute from Stratford-upon-Avon to its city campus after an outcry from scholars, actors and enthusiasts around the world.

Prof Adam Tickell, the university’s vice-chancellor and principal, issued a statement on Tuesday evening saying proposals to relocate the institute and its renowned 60,000-volume library to the university’s Edgbaston campus would be put on hold while its future was explored.

“Over the past few days, we have heard very clearly how much the Shakespeare Institute matters not only to the University of Birmingham but also to Stratford-upon-Avon, to our partners at the Royal Shakespeare Company (RSC) and the Shakespeare Birthplace Trust, and to people who love Shakespeare around the world,” Tickell said.

“I have therefore decided to pause the planned move as we explore how and whether the institute can have a vibrant and viable future in Stratford-upon-Avon.” He pointed out that interest in studying in Shakespeare’s home town has been low in recent years and made no promises about the institute’s future.

Prof Michael Dobson, the director of the Shakespeare Institute, who said he would resign if the move went ahead, described the announcement as “a start”, but said he would have preferred the decision to be reversed rather than paused.

The original decision to relocate, announced to devastated institute staff and students last week, was lambasted on social media as “near-philistine”, “unbelievably stupid”, “extraordinarily sad and shortsighted” and “an appalling act of cultural vandalism”.

A petition protesting against the move, led by the actors Samuel West and Dame Janet Suzman, rapidly gathered 15,000 signatures, as supporters of the institute around the world registered their anger at the proposal.

Tickell said: “The university’s commitment to Shakespearean studies has never been in doubt but we need to be honest about the challenges and opportunities the institute faces.

“Interest in studying in Stratford has remained very low for several years. At the same time, we have seen significant growth for related subjects on our Birmingham campus and the thriving creative industries sector in Birmingham creates real opportunities for Shakespearean research and scholarship.

“Over the next six months, we will work with the Shakespeare Institute, the RSC, the Shakespeare Birthplace Trust, and other partners, students, and alumni to ensure that the Shakespeare Institute re-establishes itself at the heart of scholarship, performance and reception for England’s greatest poet in both Birmingham and Stratford-upon-Avon.”

Dobson said: “I look forward to continuing to try to persuade the university management that we can find a viable, permanent future for the institute in Stratford-upon-Avon.”
