Moonlight Blade classes tier list

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

A guide to all the available Moonlight Blade classes

Updated on November 7th, 2026 - Version: 0.13.436

Moonlight Blade finally found its way to the West and is now available for Android and iOS as well as PC. In this article, we will break down all the available classes to help you make an educated decision as you start your adventure in the world of Jianghu. If you enjoy Wuxia games, this is a must-try.

Individual player skill, and equipment level, to name a few - all these things can make a world of difference.

That said, before I wrote this article, I played every class in the game up to max level, and I spoke with other players and did research in forums.

Alright, with all that out of the way, let's get into our Moonlight Blade tier list.

- The Strategist is a melee class that excels in PvP, especially in small-group skirmishes and 1v1 scenarios. If you are someone who enjoys PvP and you already think that's the class for you, I should tell you that if you are not planning to spend real money on this game, the Strategist cannot unlock its full potential. That doesn't mean that it's not a very good option for a beginner. That said, even though the Strategist class does indeed have a lot of burst damage, they do lack a sustained output. You need to master the combos because the cooldowns are rather long.

When it comes to PvE, the Strategist's role in Dungeons and pretty much anything team-related will be that of a Damage dealer. You will have to learn to be smooth with the combos, and that might require a little bit of practice.

- The Swordsman is a melee damage dealer. This class is similar to Strategist in that regard, but the main difference is their survivability. Unlike strategists, the Swordsman cannot heal themselves. Instead, they get around thanks to their mobility and multiple combos that, once you master them, can be very satisfying.

One-versus-one PvP situations and small skirmishes are the Swordsman's favourite type of content. Naturally, in Dungeons, you will assume the role of the damage dealer if you choose to go with this class. Overall, this is an easy class to learn, which makes Swordman perfect for new players.

The Enchanter is a ranged class that can dish out a lot of damage, potentially the most damage in the game (depending on the equipment). That said, the Enchanter's something of a glass cannon. Even though it has high amounts of damage, it has no defence. For that reason and the fact that you need to control and keep an eye on your Shadow, I wouldn't recommend this class to new players.

It takes a veteran to be able to control the shadow and be aware of their position in PvP. To that, I should also add that you need to know your combos. Long story short, this class has a high learning curve.

- If you enjoy the gameplay of support characters, then you should seriously consider picking up the Fairy class. As a healer, the Fairy is always an integral part of anything group-related. Be it Dungeons, PvP, or otherwise, every party needs a healer - that's how this game works. Here I should say that this is the only class in the game that is gender-locked (Female/Girl).

That said, large-scale PvP battles require some experience as it pertains to positioning and keeping the proper distance from your enemies. Even if in smaller battles and 1v1 situations, Fairies can keep themselves healthy due to their recovery skills, they are not very tanky.

The Flutist is a ranged class focused on crowd control and buffs. In PvE, the Flutist doesn't lack the necessary damage to breeze through content. In Dungeons, as a the Flutist, you can join either as a Damager dealer or a Healer, which makes it a lot easier to find groups. Because of its ability to buff the crit damage of the party, this class is always sought after when it comes to Dungeons and group activities in general.

In PvP, Flutists can hold their own in 1v1 scenarios but they assume a more support role in group fights.

Overall, I'd say that this is the best class, especially for beginner players (but not only).

The Phoenix school in Moonlight Blade is a mid‑range DPS class built around fire and elemental balance. What makes it stand out is summoning the phoenix spirit for flashy AoE bursts, which is great for clearing mobs in PvE or punishing enemies in PvP scenarios.

The trade‑off is survivability as it’s nowhere near as tanky as Guardian or as supportive as Fairy/Flutist, so positioning and timing matter a lot. Because of that, I'd say it's not a very beginner class and it takes a bit of time to master it..

Overall, If you want a stylish, high‑impact class that lights up the battlefield, Phoenix is the pick. If you prefer durability or straightforward melee, you’re better off elsewhere.

The Beggar can be a fun class to play, but I wouldn't recommend it to any new players. Veterans won't have a problem, though. Even though this class doesn't have a lot of damage, it's sought after when it comes to Dungeons and other team activities. Overall, this is a very balanced class, both in terms of PvP and PvE, as it possesses excellent crowd control skills and survivability. This is the only class in the game with the ability to summon (a wolf!).

Naturally, how strong a Beggar can be comes down to equipment and player skill, but that's true for every class. But, I'd say that Beggar requires a heavy investment in the gear department.

The Shadow class in Moonlight Blade is a melee assassin with dual blades, stealth, and poison mechanics, offering a high-skill-cap playstyle. It’s designed for precision and mobility, so if you are someone who enjoys tactical, rogue-like combat, maybe this class is for you. Keep in mind that this is not exactly a beginner-friendly class, and it also requires some investment to reach its full potential.

In PvE, Shadow excels at single-target burst and sustained damage via poison, making it a boss-slaying powerhouse, though its weak AoE limits its wave-clearing ability. In PvP, it has a very high potential, but its fragility and cooldown reliance is the reason why I don't recommend this class for beginners.

In dungeons, Shadow's role is that of a single-target DPS, focusing on shredding bosses with burst damage and DoTs. It lacks group utility or AoE, so it depends on tanks, healers, and crowd-control teammates.

The Guardian is the melee tank class of Moonlight Blade. There's not a lot to say about this class, really. If you enjoy the role, then this class is for you. With proper investment in gear and whatnot, Guardians can be extremely hard to take down.

This class is very sought after in Dungeons, Boss fights, and PvP. If you don't care about being the carry of your party, then Guardian is a great class to pick. In 1v1 situations, Guardians can be very potent. Even though they have slow attack speed and long cooldowns, their single-target skills can deal a decent amount of damage. In group PvP, you'll be mostly looking to frontline your team and absorb the enemy's damage.

These are all of the Moonlight Blade classes in the game at the moment. Of course, we will update this tier list with new classes if they get released.
