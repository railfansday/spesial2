NFL Thanksgiving halftime shows: Who is performing during Cowboys, Lions, Bills games in 2026?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

The NFL has sunk its craven teeth into numerous holidays over the past few years. None are more closely linked with the league than Thanksgiving, for better or worse.

For masses of spectators, Turkey Day has been defined by three pillars: food, family and football. The NFL has attempted to conjoin the latter with another beloved outlet: music.

Thanksgiving matchups have been accompanied by a number of memorable displays of artistry, from Mariah Carey and Destiny's Child to Dolly Parton and Creed.

Unsurprisingly, there is a new crop of artists looking to leave their imprint in the 2026 iterations of the event.

Here's what you need to know about the halftime performers for the 2026 set of Thanksgiving games.

Leading off the Thanksgiving Day proceedings is beloved New York City-based rap collective Wu-Tang Clan, who will deliver their haunting vocals when the Lions take on the Bears from Ford Field.

The matchup marks the 87th time Detroit has taken the field on Turkey Day. Few matchups have proven as decisive — the winner of Thursday's game will surge to the top of the NFC North standings, if only for a fleeting moment.

Forged in Staten Island, Wu-Tang Clan is one of rap's grandest-ever acts. Known for haunting production, gripping lyricism and a passion for kung-fu, the Wu are widely considered to be one of hip-hop's most formative entities.

The group — made up of RZA, GZA, Inspectah Deck, U-God, Method Man, Ghostface Killah, Raekwon, Masta Killa, Cappadonna and the late Ol' Dirty Bastard — originated in 1992. In 1993, Wu-Tang Clan released its debut album, "Enter the Wu-Tang (36 Chambers)". The project is considered one of rap's most seminal works, taking its mantle alongside other noteworthy East Coast releases like Nas "Illmatic", The Notorious B.I.G.'s "Ready to Die" and Mobb Deep's "The Infamous."

Wu-Tang Clan was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame earlier this year.

Miranda Lambert will lead the charge as the halftime show performer during the Cowboys' clash against the Eagles.

The matchup has lost considerable luster given both side's underwhelming efforts this season. Nevertheless, Lambert's presence should give the matchup a little more legitimacy. The contest will kick off at 4:30 p.m. ET promptly.

Lambert is a country music star, having drawn praise — and numerous accolades, including three Grammys — for her singing and songwriting chops.

The Longview, Texas native first drew acclaim in the early-2000s with the release of her debut album, "Kerosene." The project spawned four singles that landed inside the Top-40 country charts, eventually landing Platinum distinction after selling more than one million copies.

Lambert has maintained her place on country music's perch in the decades since her debut project, receiving a Grammy nod for Best Country Album with her 2014 release, "Platinum." She returned to the spotlight last year after co-writing (and co-producing) Ella Langley's "Choosin' Texas", the longest-running No. 1 single in Billboard Hot 100 history.

In the final game of the holiday, famed rock band the Goo Goo Dolls will take the stage halfway through the Bills and Chiefs' duel.

The "Iris" performers are Buffalo natives and should receive plenty of love from the Highmark Stadium faithful when they take the field. Buffalo has rarely hosted Thanksgiving games, its cold climate seemingly sub-optimal for such contests. The combination of Josh Allen, Patrick Mahomes and the Goo Goo Dolls' signature brand of punk-infused rock should be enough to set things alight come the 8:20 p.m. ET window, however.

The Goo Goo Dolls are a Buffalo-based band that came to prominence during the late-1990s and early-2000s. The band emerged as one of punk's most popular acts during their pomp, amassing more than 15 million record sales, four Grammy nominations and three top-10 singles: "Iris", "Slide" and "Name."

Now just a duo, Goo Goo Dolls garnered plenty of interest ahead of the season, with Buffalo fans far and wide requesting that the group perform Highmark Stadium's first concert. With the announcement that Goo Goo Dolls would be lending its services to the Bills Thanksgiving game, they did their supporters one better.
