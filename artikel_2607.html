If Warriors want to trade Kristaps Porzingis by December 16, who are potential partners, targets?

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Before the world turned in the Bay Area, before the biggest swing of Mike Dunleavy's tenure as general manager of the Golden State Warriors, he laid a stepping stone on Dec. 15, 2024. In came Schröder, a veteran who - at the time - gave the Warriors obviously-needed guard. But that fit was so rough that Golden State jettisoned Schröder as part of the Jimmy Butler trade just two months later. A new era was born.

But the Warriors would never actually be sitting here with Butler, two years later, if they hadn't traded for Schröder on the exact day they did.

This remains one of the most prominent examples of the modern NBA's "re-aggregation rule" - and a blueprint for the Warriors' current best-case scenario with Kristaps Porzingis. Under the league's collective bargaining agreement (CBA), a player traded before Dec. 16 can be used as part of a potential future trade to match salaries in a deal at February's trade deadline. And Golden State specifically planned for this with Schröder, whose former $13 million contract the organization acquired and then packaged as part of the eventual Feb. 2025 deal for Butler.

The Warriors, now, will have an incentive to find a deal for Porzingis before Dec. 16 for the same reasons. If they're able to find a trade partner, a smaller fish acquired in a salary-match with Porzingis could be packaged for a bigger fish in February - and theoretically then have more trade value than Porzingis, a player who may never see a basketball court again.

The issue, of course, remains Porzingis's current lack of any tangible trade value beyond his semi-expiring contract. Teams are much more likely to want to move off a bad contract in February than they are in December, so early in the season. A team source previously told the Chronicle earlier in November that Golden State will look at all options but wouldn't force a deal solely for better bargaining power by the re-aggregation deadline.

"I definitely think they have a chance to move him, just a matter of their level of urgency to do so," a league personnel source told the Chronicle, asked whether Golden State could find a partner for Porzingis by Dec. 16. "Might be easier when teams know their direction and just want the contract and talk buyout."

Golden State Warriors center Kristaps Porzingis reacts after making a 3-point basket during a game against the Washington Wizards, Friday, March 27, 2026, in San Francisco. (Godofredo A. Vasquez/AP)

Swinging a move for Porzingis before that deadline, though, would allow the Warriors to add immediate help - perhaps another center to pair with the ascending Charles Bassey, perhaps another offensive creator - while building out a package for a major target in February. Anthony Davis, now with the Wizards, remains a clear game-changing option, who the Warriors could land by aggregating salaries with a combination of Porzingis (or a December acquisition in his place), Draymond Green and Moses Moody.

For now, here's a look around the NBA at four potential December swings for Golden State, and the Porzingis-laid financial path it would take for the Warriors to land them.

Total contract: Unrestricted free agent in 2030-31, $25 million average salary

Charlotte Hornets center Naz Reid moves the ball against the Memphis Grizzlies during a game in Charlotte, N.C., Monday, Nov. 23, 2026. (Nell Redmond/Associated Press)

Reid is a good player having a bad season with a bad contract, which could land directly in Golden State's Porzingis-holding wheelhouse. After years in Minnesota thriving as one of the best sixth men in the NBA, a trade to Charlotte (in the LaMelo Ball deal) in July hasn't been kind to Reid, who's averaging 17.7 points on 17.5 shots per game and was benched at halftime in one game early this season.

Reid isn't the rim protector that the Warriors originally coveted with Porzingis, but he'd bring a similar effect offensively. He's a 6-foot-9 center who has shot 38% from three on 5.7 attempts per game across the past four seasons, and could be a dynamic offensive weapon who would allow Golden State to lean fully into its five-out offensive concept.

Simplest path to a deal: Package Porzingis with a bench player like De'Anthony Melton (who becomes trade-eligible Dec. 15) or Gui Santos and then add a couple draft picks, depending on Charlotte's asking price.

Comment from the league personnel source: "Contract looks tough to have now, and think Charlotte would want multiple assets as he's been bad but LaMelo has been good so for optics (sic)."

Total contract: Unrestricted free agent in 2029-30, $27.2 million average salary

Bucks center Myles Turner reacts during a game against the Atlanta Hawks, Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026, in Milwaukee. (Aaron Gash/Associated Press)

The 30-year-old Turner is averaging 6.3 rebounds and 8.3 points on 38% shooting in 26.8 minutes per game. He was a key contributor on a Pacers team that nearly won the NBA Finals in 2024-25, but has looked lost in Milwaukee the past two seasons. The Bucks would likely want to get out of Turner's contract, but would the Warriors value Turner enough to absorb his salary the next few seasons? Doubtful.

Simplest path to a deal: Package Porzingis with Moody. That would bring little value from the Warriors' perspective, though.

Total contract remaining: Unrestricted free agent in 2029-30, $18.1 million average salary

Mavericks forward Daniel Gafford throws down a slam dunk against the Golden State Warriors guard De'Anthony Melton at the American Airlines Center in Dallas, November 18, 2026. (Tom Fox/The Dallas Morning News)

The 28-year-old Gafford has been a solid contributor in Dallas for years now, and would give Golden State rim protection (1.6 blocks in 22.1 minutes per game) while allowing Draymond Green to comfortably play power forward. Gafford is a lob threat and capable facilitator and could form an intriguing center tandem with a similar player in Bassey.

Trade Porzingis straight up and add some kind of draft capital. This would depend if Dallas wanted to clear future money off its books.

Comment from the league personnel source: "Gafford is good and on a good contract, would be an interesting target."

Total contract: Unrestricted free agent in 2029-30, $28 million average salary

Pelicans forward Trey Murphy III works the ball against Phoenix Suns guard Jalen Green during a game in New Orleans, Monday, Nov. 23, 2026. (Gerald Herbert/Associated Press)

Perhaps no player in the NBA - besides the LeBron James/Anthony Davis rumors in the summer - has been more heavily linked to Golden State since last season than Murphy, a 6-foot-8 sharpshooter who averaged 21.5 points per game last season. While dealing with knee troubles this season, though, Murphy has dropped to 15.1 points per game on a lost Pelicans team.

Package Porzingis, Moody and draft capital for Murphy, who would have league-wide interest if he's truly on the trade block and would likely command a significantly higher asking price.

Comment from the league personnel source: "Trey is a whole other level of swing, good pairing with Yaxel for the future of the team as well, would cost multiple firsts. ... If I'm GS, I would part with picks for Trey."
