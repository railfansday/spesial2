Malik Nabers a top Giants receiver without knowing ‘what’s a catch nowadays’

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

One of the NFL's best receivers doesn't know a catch from a non-catch.

Malik Nabers knows he makes a lot of big receptions for the Giants, but he is just as confused as every NFL fan once replay review gets involved. The 100-catch weapon is trying to figure out how in just the last six days … he lost a first-down catch, the Jaguars' Parker Washington lost a touchdown catch against the Giants, the Rams' Konata Mumpfield lost a touchdown catch, and the Chiefs' Rashee Rice lost a game-changing first down.

"When you go to the ground, the ball is going to move. It's going to move," Nabers said. "It doesn't mean that you dropped the ball. I don't know what's a catch nowadays. I guess you just have to not let the ball even touch the ground. You just have to hope it doesn't. Roll your body up so it doesn't. I guess you have to be more conscious now."

Nabers admittedly was nervous when an airborne Washington dragged both feet in the end zone as the Jaguars rallied against the Giants last week. But the ball moved as he crashed to the ground.

"When Parker made that catch, everybody was like 'Not in!'" Nabers said. "I watched that. I was like, 'No, he's in. Oh, my God.' When they called it [incomplete], I literally was like, 'What is a catch if that's not a catch?'"

Head coach John Harbaugh and tight end Isaiah Likely, who both joined Nabers in New York this season, understand Nabers' confusion on a personal level. Harbaugh's Ravens lost to the Steelers last season when a 13-yard catch was ruled incomplete because Likely didn't finish a third step before the ball was jarred loose.

Does Harbaugh know what a catch looks like?

"I thought I did," Harbaugh said. "Now it goes to replay, and I don't know. Once it goes to replay, it gets all real gray. That was the same way last year, so that hasn't changed."

That's not going to stop the Giants from throwing the ball to Nabers, especially when he is lined up in the slot.

Nabers (65 catches for 747 yards and three drops) is averaging 14.8 air yards per target out of the slot, ranked No. 5 in the NFL among wide receivers with at least 15 slot targets, per NextGenStats. He has caught nine of 14 downfield targets (10-plus air yards) for 247 yards (No. 3) and three touchdowns (tied for No. 2).

"It's just tough to match him up in there," Harbaugh said. "He's good against zone. He's good against man. I think we've schemed some really good things up for him, and Isaiah also has been really good in the slot. It's tough to find a way to cover those guys in the slot, so we try to get them in there and get them open."

Nabers has accumulated 47 percent (353) of his receiving yards from the slot despite nearly one-third as many targets on the inside (26) as out wide (70). It seems to be the go-to way to feature Nabers in the red zone.

"It's a natural way to try to remove some of the cloud [coverage], some of the double-teams," Nabers said. "I still can get doubled in the slot — I still do. Putting me in the slot, motioning me … creates a better matchup — a wider split, a two-way release, giving me an opportunity to do stuff on their nickel or on their safeties or on their linebackers."

Case in point: The Giants drew up a mismatch of Nabers in the slot against Jaguars safety Antonio Johnson last week. Easy touchdown.

"The coaches spend a lot of time studying defense trying to get us open," Nabers said. "I didn't really think they were going to play that defense. Me being in the slot, I thought somebody was going to follow me or about to double me. When I got that coverage, I was like, 'Oh, it's just like practice.' And I took advantage of it."

The Giants had Wan'Dale Robinson in the slot during Nabers' first two seasons. Now, with rookie Malachi Fields and Darnell Mooney, there is more opportunity for Nabers to get creative.

"It's a credit to him," quarterbacks coach Brian Callahan said. "Not every receiver can handle that. It puts a lot of mental stress on with formations, motions, shifts, where you have to be, what route you are running. To see him able to handle that and still go out the way he played has been awesome."
