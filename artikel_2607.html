How Texas football can beat out Alabama, Tennessee to reach 2026 SEC championship

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Texas football sat near the bottom of the SEC standings in October. The Longhorns could now hoist the conference's championship trophy in December.

Steve Sarkisian's team took another step toward qualifying for the SEC championship game with Saturday's 45-3 win over Arkansas. Texas still sits tied with Alabama and Tennessee for the No. 2 spot in the SEC standings at 7–2, and all three teams will sit tied at 8–2 given they win their respective season finales next week.

How can Texas overtake its SEC foes and face Georgia in the Dec. 5 conference championship? The path is surprisingly simple.

The Longhorns will reach the SEC title game with a loss by any of the following four teams to close the regular season:

A loss by either Alabama or Tennessee creates a two-way tie which the Longhorns advance past both the Volunteers (who they beat in September) and Crimson Tide (who lost to Tennessee, and therefore lose the tiebreaker to Texas). As for LSU or Mississippi State, both are factored into the three teams' cumulative opponent conference winning percentage, and a loss by either would give Texas the edge over Tennessee and Alabama in that metric.

Each of Alabama, Tennessee and LSU are 12-plus-point favorites, per various sportsbooks. Mississippi State is more than a two-touchdown underdog at Ole Miss.

Texas' path to the SEC championship isn't complicated. But should the Longhorns even want to play in Atlanta? .

Say both Texas and each of the above SEC teams win next week. That would keep the Longhorns at home on championship Saturday, but it would also guarantee them a bye in the College Football Playoff. The Longhorns will very likely move up to the No. 4 slot in the CFP on Tuesday night after UCLA's loss at Michigan. It would then likely enter the CFP as either the No. 3 or No. 4 seed depending on the following two weeks of games, then rest up until the CFP quarterfinals on Dec. 30 or Jan. 1.

Play one more game, then sit and prepare until New Year's Day is an appealing path. Greater danger comes with an SEC championship appearance. Let's assume the Longhorns go into Atlanta and get battered by the Bulldogs. A playoff spot is still guaranteed, but the selection committee would the have to decide between the following teams for the final first-round bye (assuming three spots go to Georgia, Utah and, in this scenario, a 10–2 Alabama, one already ranked ahead of Texas):

Perhaps Texas would still earn the bye over Indiana given its five wins over then-top 25 teams. But would even the greatest strength-of-schedule sympathizer give a three-loss Texas the final bye over a one-loss UCLA, a Big Ten champion with its lone defeat coming in overtime at Michigan? That would infuriate college football fans nationwide, at least those not in the nation's premier conference.

Texas fans can triangulate playoff scenarios and championship paths all week. Sarkisian has no interest in such exercises. He says his team is more than happy to play one more game before the playoff, where the Longhorns could win the 35th-and-final SEC championship.

"This is a highly competitive building," Sarkisian said Saturday. "This is a group full of people that have a super high competitive spirit, and the competitor in all of us wants to win and wants to win a championship

"If we're fortunate enough to compete for an SEC championship, we'll be all in on that."
