The State Of REITs: November 2026 Edition

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

The REIT sector eked out a small gain in October (+0.94%), ending a 3-month slide. The average REIT total return exceeded both that of the S&P 500 (-0.9%) and NASDAQ (-5.3%), but fell short of the Dow Jones Industrial Average (+1.8%). The market cap weighted Vanguard Real Estate ETF (VNQ) significantly outperformed the average REIT in October (+4.81% vs. +0.94%) and has also outpaced the average REIT year-to-date (+9.20% vs. +6.14%). The spread between the 2026 FFO multiples of large cap (16.5x) and small cap REITs (11.3x) widened in October as multiples expanded 0.3 turns for large caps and was unchanged for small caps. Investors currently need to pay an average of 46.0% more for each dollar of FFO from large cap REITs relative to small cap REITs. In this monthly publication, I will provide REIT data on numerous metrics to help readers identify which property types and individual securities currently offer the best opportunities to achieve their investment goals.

Dividend yield is an important component of a REIT's total return. The particularly high dividend yields of the REIT sector are, for many investors, the primary reason for investment in this sector. As many REITs are currently trading at share prices well below their NAV, yields are currently quite high for many REITs within the sector. Although a particularly high yield for a REIT may sometimes reflect a disproportionately high risk, there exist opportunities in some cases to capitalize on dividend yields that are sufficiently attractive to justify the underlying risks of the investment. The only REIT property type to trade at a premium to NAV at the end of October was Health Care (+10.3%). Malls (-2.4%), Data Centers (-4.4%), Hotels (-4.5%) and Self-Storage (-5.6%) trade at modest NAV discounts. Timber (-41.9%) and Farmland (-40.3%) trade at the steepest NAV discounts in the REIT sector.

Health care REITs account for seven of the eight largest premiums to NAV and are led by Welltower with a 95.3% premium. The stellar fundamentals across a wide swath of health care real estate have fueled strong earnings and NAV growth. The only non-health care REIT in the top 8 is data center REIT Iron Mountain (IRM), which reported robust AFFO/share growth of 17% in Q3.

As of the end of October, 4 REITs (across 4 different property types) traded at less than half of NAV. Although two of these REITs, Service Properties Trust (SVC) and Industrial Logistics Properties Trust (ILPT) operate in favorable property types, they consistently trade well below NAV due in large part to being managed by the infamous external manager The RMR Group (RMR). The majority of the most discounted REITs, however, are in the oversupplied office and multifamily sectors.

The REIT sectorâs at-the-market (ATM) share issuance remained elevated in Q3 as the few REITs trading at a premium aggressively raised accretive new equity capital.

Health Care REITs raised the vast majority of ATM proceeds dwarfing all other property types combined. Single Tenant Triple Net and Data Center REITs also saw strong ATM usage in Q3.

In the 3rd quarter there were new large forward ATM sale agreements from American Healthcare REIT (AHR) (14,385,140 shares), Ventas (VTR) (7.8M shares) and Realty Income (O) (5.2M shares). The three largest total ATM issuances came from Health Care REITS trading at substantial premiums to NAV: Welltower (WELL) ($3.07B), Ventas ($1.40B) and AHR ($996.9M).

On the flip side of issuing shares at a premium to NAV is the similarly accretive opportunity available when trading at a discount to NAV: share repurchase. Paying less than NAV/share to buy back stock can be immediately accretive to NAV and for the 4th quarter in a row this strategy was heavily utilized in the REIT sector.

Q3 share buybacks were spread across a broad range of property types led by Communications ($615M), Self-Storage ($411.1M), Single Family Housing ($325M) and Industrial ($311.1M). Despite being a large property type within the REIT sector, Health Care ($58.1M) saw less share repurchase activity than Timber which is composed of only 2 REITs.

The REITs that bought back the highest proportion of their outstanding shares in Q3 were Hotel REIT Sunstone Hotel Investors (SHO) (5%), Office REIT Piedmont Realty Trust (PDM) (4.2%) and Land REIT Farmland Partners (FPI) (3.8%). In dollar terms, the largest repurchases were executed by Self Storage REIT Extra Space Storage (EXR) ($327.1M), Tower REIT American Tower (AMT) ($315M) and Industrial REIT Rexford Industrial Realty (REXR) ($308.7M).

In addition to the share buybacks executed during Q3, 5 REITs announced new repurchase programs or expansions of existing programs. The largest was from REXR ($1B) followed by Healthpeak Properties (DOC) ($500M), UMH Properties (UMH) ($100M), Centerspace (CSR) ($50M) and BRT Apartments (BRT) ($10M).

Disposing of assets at fair value and using the proceeds to repurchase shares at a wide discount can be highly accretive to NAV and potentially to earnings as well. Similarly, issuing shares at a huge premium to NAV and using the proceeds to purchase quality properties at fair value can similarly be accretive to both NAV and earnings. With a litany of high quality REITs currently trading at either substantial discounts or premiums to NAV, these strategies are allowing some of them to create material shareholder value over time.
