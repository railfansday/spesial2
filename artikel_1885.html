Rangers and Celtic 'part of the problem' as Scottish football faces growing concern

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Rangers and Celtic are unable to compete with European clubs when it comes to signing Scottish players, and it is becoming a concern.

The Old Firm rivals' difficulties in recent seasons have added to the concerns about a lack of top players coming through the ranks at Scotland's biggest clubs.

This comes after Rangers' 2-1 win against Motherwell saw the first time in history that neither side had named a Scottish player in the starting XI of a SPFL game.

Former Man United chief scout Mick Brown told Football Insider that the decline of Rangers and Celtic in recent years highlights the problem Scottish football is facing.

Alongside the concern about the lack of talent coming through, a number of Scottish players have also sought moves abroad, rather than joining one of the Old Firm clubs.

The likes of Lewis Ferguson, Robbie Ure and Aaron Hickey have moved abroad in recent years, while Scott McTominay and John McGinn are playing at the top level.

Brown warned Football Insider on 24 November that Scottish football is facing a serious problem regarding the level of players coming through their academy systems.

Sebastien Pogocnoli's national team side are also facing a decline given the low number of top emerging talents, and something must be done to resolve it.

Rangers and Celtic have also been mentioned as potential reasons, with neither club able to compete at their previous levels, and therefore missing out to bigger clubs.

"Like it or not, a lot of whether Scottish football is doing well depends on Rangers and Celtic," Brown told Football Insider.

"If they're at their best, they're competing in Europe, then players are going to want to go and play for them, but at the moment they're missing out on those players.

"That's part of the problem, because their decline means they're not luring the best Scottish players to come and play in Scotland.

"What you have to look at is; who are the best Scottish players right now? You'd probably say Scott McTominay and John McGinn, but neither of them are playing in Scotland.

"Historically, you'd always think that Rangers and Celtic can compete to sign the best Scottish players, but that's not the case any more.

"They don't have the finances to beat some of the top European clubs, and they're not playing at a high enough level to be a draw in that respect, so that is a problem.

"The best players coming through in Scotland are having to move abroad to further their careers, we've seen that a lot recently and that's never a good sign for the state of the SPFL."

Much of the issue surrounding top Scottish clubs' signing younger domestic players comes down to the fees they are willing to spend on rival players.

Instead, Brown told Football Insider during the summer that Rangers have to be clever with their recruitment, with scouts working across Europe to identify top young players.

The Ibrox club were scouring areas including Scandanavia and Eastern Europe for talent, having had success when recruiting from there in the past.

Interestingly enough, Rangers CEO Andrew Cavenagh explained during the summer that the club would operate with a "Scottish bias" as part of new-look transfer plans.

Lawrence Shankland, James Penrice and Ross McCrorie then all completed moves to Ibrox, but the debate still lingers about the quality of talent coming through.
