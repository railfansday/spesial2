GigaChat Omni vs SaluteSpeech: API Pricing and Call Costs

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Posted on Nov 28 • Originally published at altrouter.ai

Setting up call analytics for a new business entity hits an unexpected roadblock: buying standard minute bundles in SaluteSpeech is no longer possible. The classic pipeline pairing standalone ASR with a text LLM breaks down at the billing stage.

This guide examines the unit economics of switching to GigaChat Omni, with token math and clear scenarios where processing raw audio costs less than two-stage transcription.

A 20,000-minute package previously cost 12,000 rubles. That put asynchronous recognition at 0.6 rubles per minute. A company processing 2,000 minutes a month spent 1,200 rubles. On July 15, 2026, minute packages and pay-as-you-go access were discontinued for new accounts, as detailed in the SaluteSpeech documentation.

Existing corporate accounts retained post-paid billing, but with a catch: a minimum monthly invoice of 15,000 rubles. If a team sends those same 2,000 minutes each month, the effective unit price surges: 15,000 divided by 2,000 equals 7.5 rubles per minute. That represents a 12.5-fold increase over the old rate. Reaching the original baseline of 0.6 rubles per minute requires consuming at least 25,000 minutes monthly: 25,000 multiplied by 0.6 yields 15,000 rubles. Small services and pilots cannot hit those volumes.

New businesses are directed to register through Cloud.ru. For engineering teams, that shift involves rewriting auth routines, integrating another SDK, signing new contracts, and clearing security reviews. Developers must maintain two separate environments: one for text generation in GigaChat API, another for speech recognition on Cloud.ru. The architecture grows complex, and sequential network round trips between distinct platforms add latency.

Corporate rates are published openly, as confirmed by official GigaChat API pricing. The entry-level Lite tier costs 0.065 rubles per 1,000 tokens. The Pro tier runs at 0.5 rubles, and the flagship Max costs 0.65 rubles per 1,000 tokens.

On average, one minute of spoken phone conversation produces 120 words. In token terms, Russian speech translates to roughly 160 tokens. A five-minute call yields 800 input tokens of transcript. Instructions in the system prompt take another 200 tokens. The structured JSON summary consumes 200 output tokens. That brings the total request volume per call to 1,200 tokens.

Audio recognition accounted for the lion's share of the budget. Processing 50,000 minutes in SaluteSpeech at the former package rate of 0.6 rubles cost 30,000 rubles: 50,000 multiplied by 0.6. Paired with Lite, total system expenditure reached 30,780 rubles, where speech recognition absorbed 97.4 percent of the spend. Text processing took under 3 percent. Processing 2,000 minutes under the 15,000 ruble post-paid invoice makes this disparity sharper. Analyzing 400 five-minute calls with Lite costs only 31.2 rubles: 400 multiplied by 0.078. Speech transcription devours over 99 percent of the budget. A detailed breakdown of text generation costs is documented in our guide on GigaChat API pricing.

Free individual quotas of 365 million tokens per year carry strict constraints, as analyzed in a detailed token limits breakdown. While that total looks generous, the quota splits into rigid model pools: 250 million for Lite, 50 million for Ultra, 40 million for Pro, and 25 million for Max. These allocations do not pool together. Burning through the 25 million tokens for Max blocks subsequent requests to that model. You cannot pull unused capacity from Lite to fund calls to Max. At that point, developers must rewrite prompts for lighter models with lower analytical accuracy or move to a paid enterprise agreement. Broader patterns for estimating token bills appear in our guide on neural network API pricing.

Batch speech recognition at 0.6 rubles per minute drops acoustic context, stripping out pauses, volume swings, pitch shifts, and vocal inflections. Background noise vanishes from text transcripts. When a customer remarks, "The service is just wonderful," standard ASR outputs flat neutral words. The text LLM classifies this as positive sentiment. If the phrase was delivered with biting sarcasm, the true situation is the exact opposite. Stripping audio nuance generates false positives in automated quality monitoring.

Sber unveiled the multimodal GigaChat Omni on November 27, 2026, according to an iXBT report. The architecture connects an LLM backbone with the GigaAM speech recognition module and the InternViT visual encoder, outlined in an architectural overview on ItsNew.

The model processes raw audio directly without intermediate speech-to-text conversion. It tracks emotional state, catches sarcasm, separates distinct speakers, and registers ambient sounds like car horns or street noise. In audio comprehension benchmarks, GigaChat Omni took top honors across three out of five public evaluations. In contact center QA pipelines, this changes the diagnostic outcome: the system flags disputes by voice tremors and shouted words, even when both parties maintain formal politeness. Direct audio evaluation catches escalations before customers file formal complaints.

At one frame per second, a ten-minute video yields 600 frames, demanding up to 300,000 tokens when uncompressed. That volume exhausts context windows and inflates inference latency. Processing lengthy video files quickly becomes prohibitive.

In GigaChat Omni, the InternViT visual encoder handles this load. The compression algorithm packs visual frames roughly threefold. As token consumption per second drops, teams can submit long recordings without degrading context retention. The model pinpoints actions down to the second, such as a dropped parcel in a warehouse or a customer entering the frame. Compression trims GPU memory demands and accelerates generation.

On Russian handwritten text recognition benchmarks, Omni scored 90.4 points, documented in iXBT test data. This aids CCTV audits in retail branches and pickup points where staff complete paper forms by hand. The model extracts text straight from the video stream, bypassing manual transcription steps.

Cloud platforms charge for video processing by the minute or second. In our catalog, Gemini Omni 1.1 costs $0.092925 per video second at 720p and 1080p, which totals $55.75 for a ten-minute clip: 600 seconds multiplied by 0.092925. Scaling up to 4K resolution increases the rate to $0.238508 per second, raising that same clip to $143.10: 600 seconds multiplied by 0.238508.

The GigaChat Omni architecture accepts up to 20 files of mixed formats in a single request, confirmed by Hamidun coverage.

This enables packing full incident context into one payload: a call audio recording, an agent screen recording, two chat screenshots, and a scanned PDF contract. The model aligns agent speech with on-screen behavior while comparing verbal promises against contract clauses. Engineers avoid building custom orchestration pipelines just to merge disparate data streams.

Sber has not yet published conversion formulas translating audio and video minutes into tokens for the commercial API. The model debuted on November 27, 2026, but multimodal billing rules remain unreleased.

Official docs do not clarify whether files incur flat per-item fees or bill internal tokens generated by the GigaAM encoder. Billing mechanics for video feeds remain equally unclear: whether charges scale by duration, raw byte size, container format, or compressed frame counts. Calculating the exact cost of an API call in advance is currently impossible. Without transparent multimodal token rates, budget forecasts stay speculative. Teams must hedge against the risk that an audio minute in Omni ends up costing more than standalone ASR combined with a text model.

Weights for GigaChat Omni are publicly available under the MIT license, as reported in the iXBT announcement.

Hardware requirements present the primary obstacle. Multimodal inference with video processing demands considerable GPU memory, as flagged in the Hamidun article. The authors have not yet committed exact VRAM figures to documentation. Running visual encoders requires enterprise-grade accelerators, which carry steep hosting fees.

A detailed hardware payback calculation is available in our self-hosted LLM vs API cost analysis. A continuous 24/7 call stream makes self-hosted infrastructure economical. With uneven traffic, dedicated GPU instances accumulate fixed costs while hardware sits idle. When incoming call volume concentrates during business hours, a pay-as-you-go cloud API is usually cheaper.

Selecting an architecture comes down to three technical criteria.

Account status and billing access. Building new pipelines on direct SaluteSpeech accounts makes little sense: package purchases closed on July 15, 2026. The 15,000 ruble post-paid threshold inflates per-minute rates for modest call volumes. Without an active Cloud.ru deployment, testing alternative ASR engines or routing raw audio straight to GigaChat Omni is a cleaner path.

Need for acoustic and emotional context. If the pipeline only extracts keywords, plan names, account numbers, or contract dates, transcription followed by text LLM parsing works fine. When monitoring caller frustration, vocal irritation, passive aggression, or sarcasm, switching to GigaChat Omni is justified because standard ASR eliminates those signals. Direct audio ingestion cuts false positives in automated compliance checks.
