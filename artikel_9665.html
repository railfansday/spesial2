What Canadian CTOs should check before engineers send code and logs to Cursor, Claude Code or Copilot: PIPEDA, Law 25 and the records to keep.

This guide is for CTOs and CISOs at Canadian software companies of 10 to 100 people.

PIPEDA applies to personal information that private-sector organizations collect, use or disclose in the course of commercial activities. It does not regulate a tool by name. It regulates what happens to personal information (OPC, PIPEDA in brief).

PIPEDA's ten fair information principles appear in Schedule 1. The first three questions below concern those principles; the fourth concerns the Act's separate breach obligations.

1. Accountability: who is responsible when data goes to a vendor?

You are. Principle 4.1.3 says an organization is responsible for personal information transferred to a third party for processing, and must use contractual or other means to give a comparable level of protection.

The OPC's cross-border guidance explains how this applies to vendors outside Canada. PIPEDA does not prohibit transfers for processing outside Canada. The organization stays accountable, must take all reasonable steps to protect the information while the third party holds it, and should be satisfied that the vendor has adequate safeguards. The OPC also says a transfer for processing is a "use", so the data can only be used for the purposes it was originally collected for (OPC, Guidelines for processing personal data across borders).

2. Safeguards: are controls appropriate to the sensitivity?

Principle 4.7 requires security safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of the information, including organizational and technological measures. A production database dump in a prompt is more sensitive than a stack trace with a request ID.

Evidence to keep: which repositories hold customer data, how agent read access is limited (for example, Cursor Read Access set to Workspace), and how secrets and personal data are redacted in logs and session records.

4. Breaches: what if personal data leaks through an agent?

Under PIPEDA, organizations must report to the OPC breaches of security safeguards that pose a real risk of significant harm, notify affected people, and keep records of all breaches for two years, even breaches that are not reported (OPC, What you need to know about mandatory reporting of breaches).

If an agent sends a customer database credential or customer records somewhere they should not go, assess it under your breach process. The session record is often the best evidence of what was sent and when.

These principles are guidance, not new statutory duties. They show how the regulator reads the existing law.
