3 Genius Artificial Intelligence (AI) Stocks to Buy Hand Over Fist Before Year-End

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

While AI and memory stocks get hammered on bubble fears, these three companies just posted sold-out demand, record revenue, and raised guidance.

Artificial intelligence (AI) stocks have been getting hammered for weeks now, and the headlines all sound the same: bubble fears, AI financing anxiety, investors suddenly nervous that the hundreds of billions pouring into data centers won't pay off. None of that is made up; the sell-off is real, and some of these stocks have dropped 20%, 30%, even 40% from their highs. Here's the thing I keep coming back to, though: Price drops driven by fear tend to be the ones worth paying attention to, because they often have almost nothing to do with whether the underlying business is actually struggling.

When a stock falls because its own numbers disappointed, that's one kind of signal. When it falls because the whole sector got spooked by a headline, that's a different kind of opportunity. These three companies are showing you exactly that gap right now, between what the stock price is doing and what the business is actually doing.

Micron Technology (MU -5.78%) makes the memory chips that power AI processors, and it's been caught up in the broader memory-stock rout, like the rest of the industry. Here's what that sell-off is ignoring: Micron's high-bandwidth memory is sold out for all of 2026, the company has already locked in roughly $100 billion in take-or-pay contracts and $22 billion in customer deposits, and it still can't fill half the AI-driven demand coming its way. That's not a company bracing for a slowdown; it's really a company turning away business. Micron posted record quarterly revenue of $41.46 billion and guided to around $50 billion for the following quarter. A stock sliding on bubble fears while its own order book says the opposite is exactly the kind of disconnect worth buying into.

Astera Labs (ALAB -11.22%) makes the connectivity chips that let AI processors talk to each other and to memory inside a data center rack, unglamorous plumbing that happens to be essential. Second-quarter revenue grew 104.5% year over year, and the company just unveiled a new rack-scale architecture, Orion, built to support up to 160 accelerators in a single pod, a sign it's racing to keep pace with how fast AI hardware is scaling. The honest risk here is customer concentration: a handful of hyperscalers account for most of Astera's revenue, so any pullback from one of them would sting. But a company growing revenue that fast while expanding its product line isn't the profile of a business that's run out of room, it's the profile of one that just got caught in a sectorwide mood swing.

Vertiv Holdings (VRT -3.55%) sells the power and cooling systems that keep AI data centers from overheating, which sounds boring until you remember that every AI chip ever sold needs somewhere to run and something to keep it cool. Vertiv's third-quarter results beat estimates, and management raised full-year net sales guidance to $13.5 billion to $14 billion, yet the stock still sits well below its earlier high this year, dragged down by the same hyperscaler spending worries hitting everything AI-adjacent.

The company has also been buying its way into adjacent capabilities, picking up a microgrid developer and two liquid-cooling specialists this year, building out a fuller stack of what a next-generation data center actually needs. A business raising guidance while its stock gets punished for sectorwide anxiety is the definition of a gap between price and reality.

I'm not going to pretend none of this carries risk. AI capital spending could genuinely slow, and all three of these stocks would feel it if that happens. But that's a different risk than the one the market is currently pricing in, which is closer to panic than analysis. Each of these companies backed up its business with real numbers this earnings season: record demand, raised guidance, sold-out production, while the stock price moved in the opposite direction. That gap doesn't last forever. Buying into fear only works when the fear turns out to be bigger than the problem, and based on what these three companies just reported, that's exactly where we are right now.
