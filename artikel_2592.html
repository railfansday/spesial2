The Washington Wizards May Have Ruined Their Rebuild With One Signing

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Washington Wizards may have killed their rebuild this past offseason without even realizing it. Coming into this season, Washington was hopeful that their losing ways would be flipped, finally pushing for a postseason spot for the first time since 2021. Now, just 15 games in, Washington sits at 4-11 and is riding an eight-game losing streak, putting them 12th in the Eastern Conference.

What's happening in D.C.? After a summer full of hope, the Wizards still seem far from their contention goals. Instead of prioritizing young players this offseason, Washington went all in on one move, and it may be a mistake that costs them everything.

In January, Washington would take a massive swing, trading for veteran point guard Trae Young from the Atlanta Hawks. The Wizards not only set the NBA media ablaze, but shifted their rebuild timeline up exponentially. After building their roster primarily through the draft, the Wizards would finally trade for a true leader and a player capable of leading their young core.

After declining his player option in free agency, Washington would make the 28-year-old a contract Young couldn't refuse. Young agreed to return to the nation's capital on a four-year, $212.8 million deal, which includes another player option in 2029.

Washington was riding on the former all-star to become the true leader and first option for the young Wizards. However, just 15 games in, Young isn't playing up to standard, and Washington may have made a truly horrible mistake that looks worse with every passing game.

While it is still early in the long NBA season, Young's struggles are eye-popping for a player of his caliber. He's shooting at career-low marks, leading to a seven-point drop from his career average. On the court, he seems to have lost his explosiveness and oftentimes forces shots up instead of moving the ball around. Washington is currently at -10.2 in offensive rating compared to opponents with Young on the floor, but only -1.6 with him off the floor.

His ineffectiveness as a scorer this season has hurt Washington greatly. He's second in the NBA for assists, but his inconsistent shooting has allowed teams to focus on others defensively. With his lackluster shooting and slower first step, Young has effectively lost everything that makes him a force on offense. He's still an effective passer, but to truly lead this young core to their first playoff berth, Young has to become a threat to score.

On defense, Young is still an easy target. When on the floor this season, Washington has been outscored by 94 points in just 15 games. His defensive rating sits at a horrid 122, putting him 10th on the Wizards roster. Additionally, this season marks a career-low in Young's defensive win shares.

Unfortunately, Young's addition to the roster has been nothing short of horrible. It's still early, so Young has plenty of time to fix his offensive issues. However, right now, it looks like Washington may set their rebuild even further back, and the Wizards' depth chart may be in for another overhaul.

While Washington may be hoping for improvement, the Hawks couldn't be happier. Just eight months after trading their star player, Atlanta looks primed for another playoff push in their first full season with Young. Two major contributors to their success this season are CJ McCollum and Corey Kispert, who were both traded for Young.

McCollum helped lead Atlanta to a playoff appearance last season and became a focal point offensively during their playoff series against the New York Knicks. Now, he's become a steady veteran contributor for the Hawks, who currently sit fifth in the Eastern Conference with an 8-6 record.

While Kispert isn't the main show in Atlanta, his consistency has been off the charts this season. Through his seven appearances this season, the 27-year-old is shooting a remarkable 60% from the field and 54.2% from three. Kispert is shooting the ball at near-historic marks, making Washington's end of the trade in January look worse and worse.

No one should hit the panic button just yet, but Young's current struggles may prove too hard to overcome in D.C. The Wizards not only signed Young to a player-friendly contract, but traded two steady contributors on a contending team. In due time, Young and the Wizards may figure things out. However, as of right now, it's looking like another long season for fans, and there are many questions ahead for the Wizards.
