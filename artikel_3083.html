KVH Industries (KVHI) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

LEO now drives 58% of service revenue as legacy customers migrate.

Need a quote from a Motley Fool analyst? Email [email protected]

KVH Industries, Inc. (KVHI -15.09%) reported a strategic realignment toward Low Earth Orbit (LEO) connectivity and managed services, leading to a substantial increase in recurring service revenue. Management indicated that the company is successfully transitioning its maritime fleet customers to hybrid satellite solutions while launching new value-added services such as managed IT, cybersecurity tools, and live crew content. The company is actively optimizing its cost structure by reducing legacy geosynchronous satellite (GEO) network obligations, which is expected to improve margins in 2027. Management stated that the primary objective is to expand the recurring revenue base while converting operational growth into consistent cash flow despite evolving LEO hardware and data pricing dynamics.

Brent Bruun: Good morning, everyone, and thank you for joining us. Our third quarter results reflect the work we have done to reshape KVH around LEO connectivity and managed services. With much of the heavy lifting done, we are pleased to see the steady growth in our recurring revenue. Today, we give customers a choice of leading satellite networks so they can use the connectivity that best fits each of their vessels. And we are actively working to expand that choice. Beyond connectivity, we provide services our customers rely on to operate their vessels. Across the maritime industry, fleets are shifting to LEO connectivity, and their expectations of connectivity providers are changing alongside this transition.

Starting this quarter, we are reporting active subscribing vessels so that our vessel count better reflects the vessels contributing to our recurring revenue. We now exclude vessels in suspension and vessels on plans with very low monthly recurring fees, which today consists primarily of approximately 1,000 small fishing vessels in a single regional market. These vessels are not material to our recurring revenue, gross profit or EBITDA. On this basis, we ended the quarter with approximately 9,300 active vessels, up 6% from approximately 8,800 last quarter. This follows an exceptionally strong second quarter and is consistent with the seasonal trends we typically see in the third quarter.

As our customer base grows, we are expanding what we provide to each customer. This quarter, our first customers began subscribing to our managed IT service. Those first customers are part of a growing pipeline of fleets that want a single partner for both connectivity and onboard IT management. In September, we strengthened that offering by adding enhanced cybersecurity and IT tools to CommBox Edge Pro. As cybersecurity requirements continue to increase, more fleets are looking for solutions that help them protect their operations. We see the growing demand for managed IT and cybersecurity services as an opportunity to deepen our customer relationships and expand the services we provide. We are also investing in crew welfare.

Link Live, which we launched in September, adds live TV streaming to our crew content service. For fleet operators, keeping crew connected at sea and providing access to news, sports and other content during long voyages has become a priority, creating an opportunity to broaden the adoption of our crew content offerings and build closer relationships with fleet operators. Together, managed IT and content strengthens our relationships with customers, makes the services we provide harder to replace, and helps us grow recurring revenue from the fleets we already serve. Beyond maritime, our land-based business continues to perform well. We now support more than 1,800 sites, applying the same managed connectivity capabilities we have built at sea.

Turning to Starlink pricing, Starlink has announced price increases across its maritime services, affecting providers throughout the industry. Accordingly, we will be facing higher Starlink data costs, and as a result, we'll be repricing our Starlink plans from December 1st. We expect these changes to increase service revenue and reduce service gross margin as a percentage, but we do not expect a material change in gross profit. Looking ahead, our priority is to keep growing our LEO customer base and the services we provide to each customer, building a recurring revenue base that is both larger and more durable. With that, I'll turn it over to Anthony to walk through the financials and our updated outlook.

Anthony Pike: Thank you, Brent. Total revenue for the third quarter was $38.4 million, up 35% from a year ago and 14% from the second quarter. Service revenue was $33 million, up 30% year over year and 11% sequentially, with LEO airtime now representing 58% of service revenue. Service gross profit was $11.1 million, up 28% from a year ago and 5% from the second quarter. Service gross margin was 34% compared with 36% in the second quarter, reflecting plan mix as several large, high-usage customers came onto lower-margin plans. Airtime depreciation, a non-cash charge included in service gross profit, represented 6% of service revenue this quarter, compared with 7% in the second quarter.

Moving into 2027, service gross profit should benefit from the step-down in our legacy GEO network commitment, with a material reduction in GEO bandwidth costs expected next year. Turning to shipments, we shipped over 4,000 connectivity terminals in the quarter across a range of terminal types. This included more than 1,600 Starlink High-Performance terminals, which serve the core of our commercial maritime business. We also continue to ship OneWeb and VSAT terminals alongside Starlink Mini and Standard terminals, reflecting the breadth of our connectivity portfolio. Those shipments drove product revenue of $5.5 million, up 79% year over year.

Product gross profit included a $2.3 million non-cash write-down of our TV receive-only inventory, reflecting reduced demand and pricing, and which is excluded from adjusted EBITDA. Operating expenses were $9.7 million in the third quarter compared with $10.4 million in the second quarter. Adjusted EBITDA for the third quarter was $3.9 million compared to $3 million in the second quarter and $1.4 million a year ago. Capital expenditures for the quarter were $1.5 million, including $0.2 million of non-cash expenditure on VSAT antennas used in our Agile rental program that were purchased in prior periods. Turning to capital allocation. We ended the quarter with $50.7 million in cash and no debt.

Cash declined approximately $7 million during the quarter, primarily as a result of $10.7 million of share repurchases, which completed our $15 million program. We do not currently have a repurchase authorization in place. Our priorities are growing our LEO customer base, expanding the managed IT, content and other services that keep those customers with KVH, and pursuing selective acquisitions that add recurring revenue. Our Board continuously evaluates the best use of our cash within that framework. Finally, our outlook. With 3 quarters of the year complete, we are narrowing our full year 2026 guidance and raising the low end of both ranges.

We now expect revenue of $137 million to $143 million and adjusted EBITDA of $13 million to $15 million. At the midpoint, that represents year-on-year total revenue growth of approximately 26%, including a modest lift from Starlink repricing in December, and adjusted EBITDA growth of roughly 73% as the operating leverage in our business comes through.
