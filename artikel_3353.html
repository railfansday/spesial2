Hulu’s 36-Episode Psychological Thriller Series Is Perfect From Start to Finish

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

With three Emmy wins for his role as Jesse Pinkman, Aaron Paul confirmed his pop culture icon status by starring in Breaking Bad. Even though Vince Gilligan initially intended to kill Jesse off, Paul’s inherent likability created the glue that held the AMC drama together. The actor earned his accolades as the traumatized meth cook who was just another victim of Walter White’s (Bryan Cranston). As iconic and dark as the role was, however, Paul followed up Breaking Bad with something even more harrowing.

After the conclusion of Breaking Bad, Paul starred in the Hulu series The Path. The show was set in an entirely different world than his previous one, but no less serious. Paul portrayed the role of Eddie Lane, an acolyte of the fictional Meyerist movement. The Path views this movement non-judgmentally, as the series is portrayed from the perspective of those in the cult. At its core, Meyerism is a self-help philosophy that draws on many real-life cults. Paul’s character is a heavily indoctrinated believer, along with his wife, Sarah (Michelle Monaghan), who grew up in the movement.

Only after going to a retreat does Eddie question his faith in Meyerism. As a convert, Eddie’s relationship with the so-called movement is shaken, but Sarah is much more stringent in her beliefs. This conflict leads to a dark character arc for Eddie, which rivals his role in Breaking Bad. A decade after its 2016 premiere, the 36-episode drama remains one of Paul’s more overlooked television performances.

Cults have been a source of fascination for a long time, but viewers won’t catch the actors saying such a thing about Meyerism. Michelle Monaghan, Aaron Paul, and Hugh Dancy, who plays a ranking official, Cal, were so immersed in the series' culture that they were reluctant to label the movement a cult.

“I could never call it a cult,” Dancy told E! News. “We are a movement. I think that’s relevant and that’s, in a way, the core of the show.” The far more interesting way to portray the series is to create it from the perspective of its acolytes and how insidious this type of thinking is. As Dancy says, no one in a cult thinks that they’re in a cult.

These ideas are prominent in many real-life movements, whether they are legally acknowledged as a cult or just a church. Regardless, Paul’s role in the series gets so close to reality that it can sometimes be uncomfortable. While on ayahuasca, Eddie receives a vision that leads him to believe there is no “Light,” a core tenet of Meyerism. His doubts lead to friction in the movement and eventually separation.

Called “deniers” in The Path, people who separate from these types of churches or movements are shunned by the community and often separated from their families. The Path shows what happens behind closed doors and the violence used as punishment. Ambition is also at the center of Meyerism as Cal becomes adversarial with Eddie. Cal is poised to become the next leader of Meyerism and has always been in love with Sarah. Considering Eddie’s controversial views, he is an easy target for Cal.

The Path is mostly disturbing because it shows the ramifications of boutique beliefs such as these. Though there are characters who want to dismantle such a system, Eddie goes through a harrowing character arc that doesn’t tie everything up in a bow. At least Jesse escapes his torment in a powerful Breaking Bad performance. The Path is even more morally ambiguous and questions everything the viewers have seen.

All 36 episodes across three seasons of The Path remain available to stream on Hulu in the U.S. as of November 2026.
