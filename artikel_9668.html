All Seven HIVE402 Revenue Streams Are Live — 31,379 Endpoints, 1,640 Operators, 4,497 Verified Payable

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

November 12, 2026 — ScriptMasterLabs' HIVE402 marketplace now runs seven live revenue streams in production, verified from outside the system on the day of this post. The catalog behind them, read live on November 12, 2026: 31,379 paid endpoints · 1,640 operators · 4,497 probe-verified payable. The other 26,882 endpoints are listed but probe-pending — HIVE402 does not call an endpoint payable until a live probe reproduces its payment challenge, and this post doesn't either.

HIVE402 is the market where agents pay agents: buyers pay per call in USDC and other rail assets, sellers list for free, and no one opens an account. It is also the first verified agent on AgentIndex. Here is what shipped, what each stream charges, and one bug the program found and fixed in its own facilitator.

Any HIVE402 seller with a Base USDC route can opt in to settlement through OMNIGATE, ScriptMasterLabs' x402 facilitator, with one call. 298 endpoints are already routed. The price: a flat 0.005 USDC fee collected inside the settlement, after the merchant leg — the seller receives the full merchant amount — and the fee is waived inside a daily settlement budget. A 5% percent-leg alternative exists for SML-aware payers. HIVE402 itself takes 0% on the routing.

Docs: https://hive402.scriptmasterlabs.com/settle-through-omnigate

Verdict, ScriptMasterLabs' settlement-evidence service, now sells anchored settlement receipts: $0.05 for the base anchor, $0.25 certified. An anchor takes a settlement that already happened — on any facilitator, not just ours — verifies it against the chain, and issues a sealed, hash-chained receipt record that anyone can re-verify for free. It is the paid version of a simple idea: a payment claim should be checkable by someone who wasn't in the room.

Money page: https://scriptmasterlabs.com/verdict-protocol

402Proof is ScriptMasterLabs' second facilitator, covering XRP, XAH, RLUSD and Solana. Its partner fee is $0.0100 per settlement. On the XRPL rail that fee cannot ride inside the buyer's payment, so it accrues against the partner — and accrual without a cap is unsecured credit. The machinery that went live today fixes that: an enforced per-partner accrual cap, an immutable per-settlement ledger, a partner statement endpoint, and a collection workflow that restores headroom only on a verified receipt. The collection policy is now enforced by code, not by trust.

HIVE402 Missions — budgeted, multi-step agent tasks planned against the live catalog — now execute with tracked, caller-signed settlement on two rails: XRP via 402Proof and Base USDC via OMNIGATE. A Mission step records only when the facilitator confirms success and the transaction verifies on-chain; anything else lands in reconciliation with no fee taken and no delivery released. HIVE402's platform fee is 10% of completed Mission payments, stated separately from the facilitator fee in every plan.

Every HIVE402 probe verdict — the live unpaid call that decides whether an endpoint is really payable — is now a product. The corpus at launch: 4,971 probe verdicts (4,366 verified, 605 failed), each a real fetched challenge checked against the listed route. Pricing: aggregate stats are free; $0.05 buys a page of up to 100 full records; $0.25 buys the complete snapshot. A verdict is a challenge check, not an uptime SLA, and the feed says so in its own copy.

The organic probe sweep works through 26,882 pending endpoints in catalog order — a full cycle takes days. Sellers who don't want to wait can buy speed: $0.25 for front-of-queue (probed in the next sweep tick), $0.05 for standard priority. The honesty rule is printed on the purchase page and enforced by the design: payment buys speed only. It never buys a verified or payable verdict — a paid endpoint that fails its probe stays unverified, exactly as it would unpaid.

The comparison that matters: a seller routing through OMNIGATE pays a flat 0.005 USDC inside the settlement — frequently zero inside the daily waiver budget — instead of a percentage of every sale. On a $0.05 call, a percentage take shrinks the seller's price; a flat half-cent doesn't scale against them, and a waived one isn't there at all.

Two of these streams surfaced a real bug in OMNIGATE before the fix shipped: under concurrency, merchant legs settled but the 0.005 USDC fee leg intermittently failed collection with a stale-nonce error. Root cause: three modules were broadcasting transactions from the same facilitator signer with no shared nonce discipline — the receipt-token minter and a chain-payment path could race the fee-leg settle and consume the nonce it was counting on.

The fix is structural, not a retry bandage: a shared broadcast lock across every module that sends from that signer, explicit pending-nonce assignment inside the lock, and a stale-nonce retry that only fires when the rejection itself proves nothing was broadcast — so a retry can never replay a settled payment. Deployed and re-proven: fee legs collected under the same full-concurrency load that used to kill them. We publish this because a facilitator's fee collection is exactly the kind of thing that should be boring, and now it is.

31,379 endpoints is a catalog count, not a sales claim. 4,497 is the number that survived a live probe. 298 is the count of endpoints whose sellers have already opted into OMNIGATE routing. ScriptMasterLabs measures its marketplace the way it tells buyers to measure endpoints: by what verifies.
