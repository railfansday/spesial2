Park Hyatt Phu Quoc, Vietnam, Opening Soon, And I’m Intrigued!

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Back in 2019, plans were announced for an exciting new Park Hyatt resort in Vietnam. The property has been massively behind schedule on its planned 2022 opening. Several months ago, the hotel started accepting reservations for stays as of March 2027. There’s now a positive update, as the timeline has been moved forward to December 2026, so it’s just weeks away.

The Park Hyatt Phu Quoc is now accepting reservations for stays as of December 8, 2026. The delays on this property have been endless, so it’s nice to see the timeline moved forward for once. Here’s to hoping that there are no last minute delays.

For those not familiar with Phu Quoc, it’s Vietnam’s largest island, and half of the island is in a national park and UNESCO site. The Park Hyatt Phu Quoc is expected to feature a total of 168 keys, including 114 rooms and suites, and 54 private pool villas. This is Park Hyatt’s first resort in Vietnam, and second property in the country, after the Park Hyatt Saigon.

The resort is located on the southwestern tip of Phu Quoc Island, and is developed on 160 acres of land, bordered by a mile-long white sand beach on one side, and lush hills on the other side. The Park Hyatt is a 30-minute drive from Phu Quoc International Airport (PQC).

With the Park Hyatt Phu Quoc bookable, what are rates like? If you’re looking to redeem World of Hyatt points, this is a Category 7 property, meaning that a free night redemption will cost anywhere from 25,000 to 55,000 points per night, assuming a standard room is available. I value World of Hyatt points at around 1.4 cents each, so that puts the “cost” of a redemption at $350-770 per night.

When it comes to cash rates, they seem to start at just over $700 per night, and that’s before the 13.4% in taxes and service charges, so we’re talking about entry level rates of over $800 per night all-in.

If you are looking to book a cash stay here, I’d recommend doing so through the Hyatt Privé program, which offers extra perks like room upgrades, a hotel credit, and more.

Depending on the award pricing for the nights you’re looking at, redeeming points here could potentially be a very good value.

While I’ve visited major cities in Vietnam several times before, I’ve never gone there for a beach vacation, so I’m intrigued by this property, especially given the value of World of Hyatt points, plus of World of Hyatt Globalist status.

Based on what I’ve heard, currently the top properties on the island are the JW Marriott Phu Quoc, the InterContinental Phu Quoc, and the Regent Phu Quoc. Many people have told me that these properties are among the best of their respective portfolios.

I’m curious, though, for those who have been to Phu Quoc, is it worth a trek from the United States? I’m sure it’s gorgeous and I’ll certainly take Vietnamese food over Caribbean food, but is it otherwise worth traveling that far out of the way?

It’s funny how my preferences in that regard have changed over the years. 15 years ago, I’d almost exclusively take beach vacations in Southeast Asia, given the generally higher standard of everything (service, hotels, etc.), plus the more reasonable prices.

However, as I’ve gotten older, my opinion has changed. For one, the jet lag becomes more challenging the older I get, and can take away from relaxation. Furthermore, flight award options just aren’t as exciting as they were over a decade ago. Like, easily being able to always snag Cathay Pacific first class awards was a great reason to vacation in Asia, as far as I’m concerned. 😉 But that’s no longer so easy.

But I also have another motive here. I’m intrigued by airline startup Sun PhuQuoc Airways, which is just taking delivery of some Airbus A330s. So if I can fly an ex-American A330 directly to here, I’d sure find that to be amusing! For that matter, I really love the Capella hotel brand, and I’ve heard the Capella Hanoi is among the best in the portfolio, so I’d really love to visit that, and pair it with a visit here.

A Park Hyatt property is expected to open in Phu Quoc, Vietnam, and it’s now accepting reservations for stays as of December 2026. This property has been under development for eons, and it’s over four years behind schedule on opening. With the opening being just weeks away, here’s to hoping that the timeline finally actually sticks.
