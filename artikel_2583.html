Sean McVay has NSFW reaction to Rams flattening Packers on Thanksgiving Eve

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

By the time Sean McVay and Matthew Stafford got a good look at what they had left to work with on the sideline Wednesday night, they might as well have been in a garage trying to get a race car back on the track after blowing a tire and snapping a belt.

Oh, and somebody had already borrowed half their tools.

Los Angeles Rams head coach Sean McVay points to his defense during a game against the Green Bay Packers at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, Calif., Nov. 25, 2026. Jon SooHoo/UPI/Shutterstock

The Rams went into their game against the Packers without Puka Nacua and Davante Adams, the two receivers Stafford generally looks for when he needs somebody to go make a play. They lost cornerback Jaylen Watson weeks ago.

Then left guard Bill Murray went down. So did right tackle Warren McClendon Jr. Whatever comfort McVay took in having a couple of tight ends to help out his makeshift offensive line lasted only as long as Davis Allen and Tyler Higbee did.

Over on defense, safety Kamren Kinchens got himself thrown out for unnecessary roughness on a hard hit on Packers wide receiver Christian Watson.

Somewhere in all that, McVay and his staff found a way to run the ball down Green Bay's throat for 241 yards in a must-have 26-13 win.

Matthew Stafford of the Los Angeles Rams calls a play against the Green Bay Packers at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, California, Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026. Getty Images

And when Stafford needed a receiver, there was second-year wideout Konata Mumpfield, who caught four passes for 69 yards.

Meanwhile, a defense that had every reason to feel as shorthanded as the offense spent most of the night getting itself off the field. Three takeaways. Eleven first downs allowed. All of 244 yards for the Packers, three more than the Rams managed just handing the ball off.

Talk about figuring it out on the fly. Wednesday night and darn near the whole season.

It got McVay thinking about his old college coach, Terry Hoeppner, who had three pieces of advice for him back in the day: Have a plan. Work the plan. Plan for the unexpected.

He's getting tired of the last one, albeit a master at dominating it.

"We've had a lot of that third s––t come to life to plan for the unexpected this year," McVay said.

Consider their five-game winning streak, including Wednesday's win over the Packers, evidence of the Rams' ability to survive and thrive.

By any means necessary. Which sometimes means drawing up formations on the sideline.

Los Angeles Rams running back Blake Corum (24) eludes Green Bay Packers cornerback Brandon Cisse (2) on Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026, at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, Calif. Jon SooHoo/UPI/Shutterstock

"The biggest thing is, how do we figure out what's the best way to use the group that we have to play the opponent that we're going against? That was what we felt like it was," McVay said.

Imagine telling McVay beforehand he'd go jumbo against Green Bay, then start losing the very guys who make it possible to play that way. Now imagine telling him the Packers would know exactly what was coming and Corum would still be running through them.
