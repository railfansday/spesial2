Giants-Commanders matchup: New York defense needs to stay on the attack

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

The 5-2-1 New York Giants host the 2-5-1 Washington Commanders on Thursday Night Football. The teams tied 27-27 in Week 5. New York is favored by just 1.5 points, and the Over/Under is set at 42.5 points.

Both the Giants and Commanders allowed victory to slip through thier fingers in Washington. Cam Skattebo's fumble led to a subsequent six-snap game the following week, and Washington's botched final ten seconds of overtime will be notorious in Commanders' lore for years to come.

Still, the Giants are now starting a different quarterback in J.J. McCarthy, and sitting comfortably at the top of the NFC East. This is an important home game on a short week for John Harbaugh and the Giants, who are looking to go on their first three-game winning streak of the season.

Washington ranks 20th in total offense, 22nd in passing offense, and 11th in rushing offense, largely due to Jaydeon Daniels' 252 yards on the ground, which leads the team. The Commanders average 21.5 points per game, which is 21st in the league. Despite the underwhelming numbers, the Commanders aren't a pushover team; they defeated Seattle in Week 3 and made their Week 6 loss in San Francisco kind of interesting in the second half. Still, the Commanders lack an identity on offense.

Washington lost star receiver Terry McLaurin three weeks ago, and the Washington rushing attack has struggled all year. Jacory Crosky-Merrit had an early 33-yard touchdown run against the Giants in Week 5, but he's still on I.R., and the rushing attack hasn't exactly taken off with Richaad White and Austin Ekeler.

Rookie wide receiver Antonio Williams has 26 catches for 284 yards on the season, but he hasn't exactly stepped up yet post-McLaurin injury; he has talent, and a big game could be coming for him soon. Williams has led the Commanders in snap share since the McLaurin injury.

Stefon Diggs plays 60-65% of the snaps, and he leads the Commanders in receiving yards. He has 30 catches on 49 targets for 303 yards with a team-high five touchdown receptions. Treylon Burks has been playing through injury, and Luke McCaffrey is a good special teams player who doesn't receive snaps on offense every week.

Van Jefferson played 58% of the snaps against the Rams in Week 9, catching five of seven targets for 58 yards with a touchdown. Jefferson seemed comfortable, and Washington looked for him in key situations; it's something to pay attention to on Thursday night.

Tight end Chig Okonkwo was used early and often against New York, and I expect more usage in Week 10. Ben Sinnott and John Bates are the two-and-three tight ends. Washington's offensive line consists of (from left to right) Brandon Coleman, Chris Paul, Lucas Patrick, Sam Cosmi, and Josh Conerly Jr.

ATTACK! Dennard Wilson dialed up pressure against Jayden Daniels and forced four three-and-outs in the game. Wilson blitzed on 62% of the Giants' dropbacks; 40.5% quick pressure rate, which is a 99th percentile performance. They blitzed on 23 dropbacks and got a quick pressure on 12 of those. Dru Phillips had 5 quick pressures on 7 pass-rush snaps as a blitzer.

New York used Cover-0 in critical moments toward the end of the game. Washington offensive coordinator David Blough is going to have better contingencies than he did in Week 5. New York out-coached a young coordinator, and the Giants should continue to show pressure, but be willing to change it if Blough's adjustments start to yield success.

The Giants surrendered a 33-yard touchdown to start the game against Washington, but the team has been much better with their run fits over the past two games. Disciplined football up front and better-angle discernment by the safeties in the alley can help mitigate an already struggling rushing offense; Jason Pinnock and Ar'Darius Washington have been solid so far, displaying those traits.

Blough's offense used simplified concepts against Wilson's heavy man defense. A lot of simple half-field curl/flat to slant/flat reads with stacks/BUNCH in third-and-short to create rubs/picks upon the release. New York must be dialed in spatially in man coverage and very communicative if they're passing routes off to one another; pick/wheel and post/wheel were used several times in Week 5.

When Blough uses condensed formations, New York should — and will — load the line of scrimmage and bring defensive backs on five-man pressure blitzes. Expect Fire-Zone blitzes and a lot of interior isolation for Arvell Reese and Micah McFadden against the interior offensive line. Reese vs. a running back in protection on third-and-longs will be a mismatch to exploit — as it was in Week 5.

The Giants must be cognizant of Jayden Daniels' legs. His ability to extend plays and evade defenders is difficult to handle, but Wilson incorporated these rush-spies that forced offensive linemen to commit to blocking but were tepid enough to watch Joe Milton as he tried to pick up yards with his legs. Arvell Reese's second sack was one of these rush-spies.

New York needs to continue to send six rushers at the snap, with two bailing into underneath coverage half a second after the sack. Wilson has used defensive linemen and linebackers in the rush this season; some of those underneath players and zones can help contain the quarterback. High-side rushes can't just abandon rush responsibilities with no spy if it is man coverage.

The Giants defense is playing like a top-10 unit, and the potential of these high picks is being actualized. Containing Daniels is difficult, but doable, and I expect the Giants to make Washington's offense uncomfortable. Giants win, 20-13.
