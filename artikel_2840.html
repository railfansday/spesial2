Winners & Sinners: Week 4

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Welcome to Winners & Sinners, where we reflect on the week that was for the Milwaukee Bucks. Week 4 saw the Bucks go 0-4, with blowout losses to the Boston Celtics, New York Knicks, and Los Angeles Lakers, before a more palatable closer loss to the Orlando Magic. The Bucks don't really deserve any hardware, but they're already engraved, so here goes.

Note: For scheduling reasons, this week's Winners & Sinners has been posted after the Atlanta Hawks game but does not include it. That game will contribute to Week 5's installment

Despite a rough first contest against the Celtics, Jaime Jaquez Jr. earns his second MVB, tying Tyler Herro with two apiece on the season. In the three games to end the week, Jaquez put up 21.0 PPG, 5.3 RPG, 3.3 APG, and 1.0 SPG, while hitting 47.7% of his shots from the field and 47.4% of his shots from deep (on 6.3 attempts per game). Beyond the numbers, Jaquez earns this win because of his willingness to move to the bench in a bid to bolster the team. During postgame media availability following the Lakers loss, Jaquez spoke of the move, saying, "I just try to do what I can to help this team in any way possible, whether that's starting, coming off the bench, just trying to, you know, try to jump start something for this team." No fluff, no ego, just leadership.

The Contenders: Kyle Kuzma, Nate Ament, Ryan Rollins.

The Decision: Kuzma made life tough for Jayson Tatum, helping restrict him to 6/18 shooting, but he was largely ineffective against Luka Doncic and got destroyed by Franz Wagner. Ament had some moments too, getting in the passing lanes and showing some surprising defensive versatility (including some juice against Jalen Brunson). But it's Rollins who has been the team's most consistent defender, and he hit the boards hard this week: a team-leading 5.3 defensive rebounds per game and 35.6% defensive rebounding percentage. That, along with his customary work at the point of attack, is enough to dust off the belt.

The NBA: I really wanted to highlight the Bucks' opening defensive possession against the Knicks—a defensive masterpiece so mean it'd make your coach's coach blush—in "Run That Back." Of course, the only thing more restrictive than the defence on display in that possession is the NBA's clip control. Consider this another warning.

Myles Turner: Yes, he's an easy target at the moment and, yes, he was legitimately good (or at least at expectation) against the Magic. But when you're the team's second-highest paid player and force the coaching staff into giving you a "rest" game, then there are problems. Even with his bounce-back game against the Magic, Turner shot just 29% from the field for the week and, despite playing in only three out of four games, tied for second on the team in total turnovers. Not going to cut it.

If Ryan Rollins can't improve his lead guard skills, then he might find himself permanently cast as a backup. While he has improved the shooting woes that inhibited his effectiveness to begin the year, shooting 42% from the field and three this week (albeit on reduced volume), Rollins continues to struggle orchestrating the offence, particularly with his passing, telegraphing what should be regulation passes on set plays during both live action and from inbounds.

That's all for Week 4. Share your winners and sinners in the comments!
