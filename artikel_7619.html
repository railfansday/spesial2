Sheinbaum signals trade progress after Trump meeting in China

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum and U.S. President Donald Trump met briefly at the APEC forum in China against a backdrop of U.S.-Mexico trade tensions.

MEXICO CITY (CN) â Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum briefly met with U.S. President Donald Trump on Wednesday in Shenzhen, China, where both leaders attended the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum.

“We held a very good meeting with President Donald Trump and his team. We continue to make progress on our shared agenda,” Sheinbaum said Wednesday on X.

Sheinbaum, via video link from China in her morning press conference Wednesday, added that Trump was in very good spirits and the two will meet again in Florida next month for the G20 Leaders’ Summit.

“President Trump was briefed on the progress of the talks that the secretary of economy has been holding with his counterparts in the United States. And we can say that we are very far along,” Sheinbaum said Wednesday.

The U.S. is Mexico’s largest trade partner and China is Mexico’s second-largest. Mexico and China do not have a free trade agreement and the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement was put on ice when the U.S. declined to renew the agreement in July.

The three countries will continue to review the agreement on a yearly basis until all three countries agree to sign an extension, which largely depends on U.S. cooperation.

Trade between Mexico and the U.S. was valued around $900 billion in 2025 and is on track to reach $1 trillion in 2026, according to some estimates.

Sheinbaum’s meeting with Trump in China came a week after Trump threatened to double tariffs on Mexico if Sheinbaum does not stop migrant caravans heading to the U.S. from southern Mexico. Trump called the caravans illegal on Thursday.

Current Trump tariffs on Mexico include 25% tariffs on imported passenger vehicles and light trucks as well as a 50% tariff on steel, aluminum and copper. Goods that do not meet rules-of-origin requirements under the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement are also subject to 25% tariffs.

Mexican Economy Secretary Marcelo Ebrard has been advocating for a reduction on these tariffs since October, including in a government economic report he gave to the Mexican Senate.
