Done Deal! Netflix Buys Radford Studios For Almost $400M; Streamer Calls Purchase “A Meaningful New Investment In California’s Film Industry”

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Executive Editor, Legal, Labor & Politics

Ted Sarandos has never hid his love for Old Hollywood, and now the Netflix co-CEO has a 55-acre and 98-year old piece of Tinseltown property.

After months of negotiations, speculation and reporting by Deadline that things were moving ahead, Netflix has bought the iconic Radford Studio Center in Studio City. The anticipated auction of the property today proved a bit of a non-event as Goldman Sachs-led lenders pulled the lot back and sold it directly to the streamer.

A CBS hub over the decades and once dubbed Republic Studios decades ago, Radford has “22 stages, 210,000 square feet of office space and 172,737 square feet of support space,” according to its specs. Watch on Deadline

As part of today’s purchase, with paperwork being filed as we speak, Netflix also paid the pricey ULA transfer tax that goes with the acquisition, I hear.

With 1,000 Netflix employees let go last month, execs such as Content chief Bela Bajaria ousted last month, dissent among the ranks, and the stock still struggling, the optics around such a big outlay of cash might not look so good. But this is Hollywood, where perception is reality and the Sarandos and Greg Peters-led streamer is leaning into a narrative of being good industry citizens in today’s purchase to give some gloss to the whole thing.

“There’s still a long way to go, but we are hopeful that with the partnership of the State of California and the City of Los Angeles, this will be the start of a meaningful new investment in California’s film industry,” Netflix said today of the purchase and the future it envisions for Radford as a production hub in what has been some very tough times for the home of Hollywood the past five years.

With facilities in various states of readiness in New Mexico, plus tax credits-rich New York and New Jersey, the streamer Tuesday went full Randy Newman about L.A.

“This city is home to us,” they exclaimed. “We’re excited to keep investing here and finding more ways to entertain the world, while supporting the writers, builders, electricians, drivers, designers, and talent who make L.A., California, and the entertainment industry so special.”

To the over 50,000 industry workers who have seen their jobs disappear since 2020 that can’t help but sound like hope. The statement (see the full missive below) also can’t help but remind some of the threats to leave L.A. that Skydance was leaking like crazy in its ultimately successful effort to seal the $111 billion merger of Paramount and Warner Bros Discovery earlier this summer.

Netflix plans extensive renovations at the 1928-opened Radford, especially being that previous owner Hackman Capital Partners, who bought the lot off what was then ViacomCBS for $1.85 billion in 2021, never finished their own upgrades before losing the property to creditors. To that, Netflix has engaged real estate and infrastructure company ZVantage and gear group Cinelease to shepard the literal and figurative re-branding of the sprawling joint.

Having said that, nothing’s gonna change for a while at either Netflix’s Sunset Blvd-leased HQ or for the current renters at the former CBS hub. Sticking to their current leases, the streamer plans to stay at the Hollywood locations until around late 2031. As for those already at Radford, a source close to the situation tells me that “business will continue as usual on the lot in the near term, including for productions that are currently filming on the lot.”

Business as usual, in an ever-changing business — and Ted finally got his lot.

Read the full statement from Netflix on the purchase Of Radford Studio Center here:

Unless you work in entertainment or live in Los Angeles, you may not have heard of Radford Studio Center. But you’ve probably seen something that was made there.Opened in 1928, at the end of the silent film era, Radford has been home to a long list of classic shows — from Gunsmoke and Gilligan’s Island to The Mary Tyler Moore Show, Seinfeld, and That ‘70s Show, among many, many others.Today, we’re proud to share that Netflix is purchasing Radford to build on that history and strengthen our investment in Los Angeles and California.Netflix was founded in California, and we opened our first office in L.A. more than two decades ago. Since then, many of our shows like early favorites Grace and Frankie and GLOW have filmed in L.A., as have more recent series and films like Nobody Wants This, The Lincoln Lawyer, The Rip and The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth. This year alone, more than 45 of our shows and movies are confirmed to film across L.A.We’ve also engaged ZVantage, Zello’s entertainment advisory division, to support the transition and next chapter of the iconic lot through its operating platform, Backlot Studio Services. As part of the engagement, Cinelease will serve as a production equipment provider at the studio, further enhancing the infrastructure and services available to the production community. Working together, our goal is to maintain operational continuity, retain the existing studio team and strengthen Radford’s position as a premier production destination.There’s still a long way to go, but we are hopeful that with the partnership of the State of California and the City of Los Angeles, this will be the start of a meaningful new investment in California’s film industry.This city is home to us. We’re excited to keep investing here and finding more ways to entertain the world, while supporting the writers, builders, electricians, drivers, designers, and talent who make L.A., California, and the entertainment industry so special.
