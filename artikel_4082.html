Rayman Legends Retold Developer On Bringing Rayman’s Personality Back, Crafting The Glade Of Dreams, And The Mysterious Villain

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Marco Renso opens up about Retold’s new focus on story and characters, its returning voice actors, and the hopeful future of Rayman.

It’s no secret that Rayman Legends Retold is a bit of a strange project. Fans have been waiting a long time to see the limbless wonder return with a new game, and yet here we are 13 years later with a remake of what is still the newest entry in the series. The initial response from everyone was complete confusion, but those reactions mostly subsided after we all saw what Ubisoft Milan and Montpellier were trying to do.

Beyond adding new content and significantly changing up an art style that we’ve all been staring at for more than a decade, one of the main things that Retold sets out to achieve is adding a cohesive world, narrative, and characters to Legends. It’s a bigger undertaking than you might imagine, considering how exclusively gameplay-driven the original was with its disconnected worlds and their disparate themes and gimmicks.

While that mechanic-focused format has become synonymous with Rayman for many (especially since Legends is easily the most successful entry in the franchise), it wasn’t always the case. Every Rayman game before Origins had plenty of lore, worldbuilding, and character focus alongside all the slapstick and silly moments. Legends Retold is an attempt to unite every era of Rayman and, if all goes to plan, work towards something new.

“First of all, it's a general sense of looking at the future,” realisation director Marco Renso tells me. “We knew that we wanted storytelling to be a part of Rayman, and when we looked at Legends, it’s an awesome game, but from a storytelling point of view, it’s a bit disjointed. There’s no real purpose. We knew that we wanted to create a thread to make the player feel the whole experience a bit more creatively, to give purpose to the heroes moving through these realms and why they’re doing what they’re doing. That was one of the early choices we made. Since we wanted this to be a reimagining of Legends, it was natural that we wanted to tell something more.”

As Renso confirms, a renewed focus on storytelling and the world of Rayman was one of the first and biggest decisions made during the development of Legends Retold. That may sound simple, but the original Legends is so far removed from the rest of the series, and from even having a story in the first place, that there’s more work involved than you might think, especially since the team wanted to respect the whole history of Rayman and not just the modern era.

“It started with analysing the whole Rayman lore, which is quite complicated because it’s all over the place,” Renso says. “Trying to make sense of it, to create some order before extending it, and trying to understand what we should focus on. So even before starting to work on the new story, it was a matter of understanding the past lore, the characters and who they are. There was a lot of research in the beginning, and there’s the balance of it still being a platformer game. It’s still Legends, so it’s not suddenly becoming a storytelling game where we have four hours of cinematics.”

While a proper narrative is one of the biggest new additions to Retold, the developers didn’t want that to overshadow the in-and-out nature of the original Legends and still wanted players to be able to “get to the action fast”. That meant keeping the game’s original choose-your-own-path structure that offered as much freedom and variety as the player wanted. However, Renso tells me that in itself was complicated since it meant having to make the story somewhat non-linear to account for all types of paths.

One of the ways that all of this effort is most apparent is through Legends Retold’s world map, which finally adds cohesion to the Glade of Dreams and makes it a tangible place instead of just unrelated locations. It’s not just the areas you’ll be visiting in Retold, though, as the world map includes nods to Rayman’s past adventures like the Rabbid landing site and Origins’ Jibberish Jungle. As it turns out, the world map isn’t just referencing Rayman’s history.

“It's no secret that we want to bring the brand back, and a lot of the work went into trying to build a foundation for whatever could be the future,” Renso admits. “You never know, of course. But it was a lot of work, and we had in mind, okay, maybe it's something that we can build upon. The world map was one of our goals. We tried to create something creative while also taking into account past games. We know we have very passionate, longtime fans that are super, super into the details of the lore. So we tried to put some details in for them as well.”

Renso also revealed that the world map was used by the developers for the new Dragon Ride segments, and that its geography was considered as you travel between the realms.

Retold’s big push for story is made clear with the introduction of voice acting, which has been missing from Rayman games for too long. The limbless wonder himself is voiced by David Gasman, who returns to the role once again after lending his talents to the Phantom Show DLC for Mario + Rabbids: Sparks of Hope and previously starring in Rayman 2 and 3.

Considering his past appearances, I asked Renso if Gasman is the voice of Rayman going forward, to which he cheekily says, “Right now, he’s the voice of Rayman”. Renso also explains that when the team decided to implement voice acting, Gasman was the first one considered in the English dub since he “just feels right” as the character.

It’s not just Rayman’s voice that’s notable in Retold, though, as his personality has drifted away from the slapstick and goofy character in Origins and Legends. Rayman is once again a heroic and good-natured hero like he was in earlier games, albeit with the same laid-back nature and a fondness for naps.

“Our goal was to make the characters feel more rounded,” Renso tells me. “There were some traits in the early games that were kind of lost in Origins and Legends, where they became a little bit more one-dimensional. Super funny, of course, which is something that we also didn't want to lose and that was great about Legends, but we also wanted to retrieve part of what made Rayman, especially in 2 and 3, special. That's why we also decided, for example, to have the four heroes on screen at the same time to show them acting and reacting to each other.”

Retold also brings back Billy West as Murphy, last seen in Rayman 3, which Renso says is “perfect” since you need the right mix of annoying and helpful for the character.

Retold also finally gives Rayman fans a bit more to chew on when it comes to Barbara, one of the four main characters of Legends who was just kind of there with no explanation in the original. Now she not only has a voice thanks to Elana Dunkelman, but also a fierce personality to go along with her backstory, which Renso is hesitant to give me too much information on.

“It was one of our goals to be able to give her a bit more space,” Renso says. “It's a story-driven game, so we cannot dig too much into that, but we took the chances here and there to add something about her past and show how she reacted to the other characters. In fact, you see that she's quite present because of that. We felt that other heroes were better known - she was there in Legends, but there's not much known about her. And now I think she’s probably my favourite character.”

Known characters getting revitalised isn’t all that Retold is about, though, as it’s also introducing a brand new villain that’s literally cloaked in mystery. Since it was hinted early on that the villain has some ties to Rayman’s past, fans have been guessing who they might be for months now, with some popular picks ranging from Mr Dark, The Magician, a Rabbid, and even Ed from Tonic Trouble. As much as I pried, Renso wasn’t giving anything away.

“The mystery is the point, really,” Renso laughs. “We knew right away that we needed a villain because of the story and the fact we needed a main threat. The Dark Teensies were very fun, but having a main antagonist gives a real goal and adds clarity to the whole progression of the story. So we knew that, but we wanted also to have a mystery that’s also connected to the future. I don't want to spoil, but if you play the game, you'll see that there are some hints there. And actually, there are some hints also in his character design about who he might be, but I can't go much further than that.”

We’re not far off from learning the truth behind the cloaked figure’s identity for ourselves, but the presence of a dark villain with an axe to grind is pretty representative of how Retold refocuses Rayman’s tone. The original game was bright and vibrant like a Saturday morning cartoon come to life, while Rayman 2 took a darker and more dramatic direction.

Then Rayman 3 flipped everything on its head with a truly bizarre and comedy-led adventure with some hints of its predecessors' more fairytale tone. We don’t really talk about the Rabbids era of Rayman, but Origins and Legends doubled down on the slapstick style and removed any consequence or thought to focus on the gameplay, for better or worse. Now, Retold is trying to bring all of those eras together for one unified tone.

“We want Rayman to be a brand that brings emotions to the player, and in a broader sense of the spectrum,” Renso tells me. “We want players to be able to feel joy, of course, and have some laughs, but at the same time to be moved, to feel tension. Each game has a part of these feelings, but at the same time, they're all part of the same brand. In Retold, we want to embrace that. We don't want to lose, for example, the humour and how bizarre it can be to go full epic and dramatic. That's not Rayman. But the other way is also true. We don't want it to become suddenly just slapstick humour, and that's it. It's something we really want to be at the core of the brand going forward.”

From my chat with Renso, as well as my chat earlier in the year with brand producer Loic Gounon, it’s clear just how much love has been put into Rayman Legends Retold. While most Rayman fans are fully on board at this point, it’s more than fair to say that a lot of gamers are still sceptical about remaking the latest game in the series. Sure, it’s 13 years old, but it’s still the newest Rayman game we have.

“I can understand, because it’s common feedback,” Renso says. “We can understand the thought process there, and I get those feelings. But on the other hand, there are several reasons why we picked Legends. We tried to have something that is not erasing what the original game was. It’s more of a reimagining and a different take on it that will hopefully feel familiar and new. We really tried to add a new voice to it, to bring something from us, the team, to the game, because we know how much Rayman matters to the fans. The team did not take it lightly, and we did everything we could in order to give it its own character, to make it unique in its own way.”

As a lifelong fan of the vegetable thingamajig, I’m just as hopeful as Renso that everyone takes a chance on Rayman Legends Retold, especially with how clear it is that the series’ future rests on the response to this game. We already know that the team has ideas for where to take Rayman next and, while we won’t find out what they might look like for a long while, I couldn’t help but ask Renso what direction he’d personally like to see Rayman take.

“With my role as realisation director, I would love to have the chance to be able to work on a 3D game because of the possibility of how to immerse the player, just to make the Glade of Dreams feel like a real place,” Renso admits. “But Retold was also a blast, so to be honest, there’s no wrong choice here.”

Two hours with Retold were enough to convince me that it’ll be the definitive way to experience Legends, especially with a bit more time in the oven.
