6 Airlines With 40-Inch Premium Economy Seat Pitch

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Although some airlines, like Air New Zealand, offer more, 40 inches (102 cm) is the golden number for seat pitch in premium economy. Any more is meaningless for all but the tallest of passengers. It represents a significant step up from the 30 to 32 inches (76 to 81 cm) that you can usually expect in economy class.

Seat engineers can use this space to produce deep recline and leg and calf rests for a supremely comfortable seat without passengers needing to pay for lie-flat business class. The additional space also makes it possible for passengers to be productive throughout cruise on their laptop or tablet. Examples of airlines that offer 40 inches of seat pitch in premium economy include All Nippon Airways, Cathay Pacific, Emirates, and ITA Airways.

Not all airlines offer 40 inches of seat pitch in their premium economy cabins because it takes effort, along with a reduction in the overall number of seats that can be sold. For its 787-9, ANA took the plunge and increased seat pitch by two inches (five cm). ANA also grew the recline range by the same amount, achieving a total of nine inches (23 cm) of recline. These seats are available on ANA's 206-seat 787-9s and not on its 246 and 215-seat Dreamliners.

Another airline that has gone to great lengths to give its premium passengers access to 40 inches of pitch is Cathay Pacific, although it insists that its premium economy is more than just extra legroom. The carrier has retrofitted some of its aging 777-300ERs to bring in this capability. “For our Boeing 777-300ER, we have a very clear goal, which is to design Premium Economy as a cabin that thrives on its own merits – a product that reflects the customer’s needs and the premium differentiation,” says Vivian Lo, General Manager, Customer Experience & Design at Cathay Pacific.

All premium economy seats on Cathay Pacific's A350s also give passengers 40 inches of pitch. The room is matched by a full-length leg rest and an adjustable armrest. You can access a near business class level of customizability in these seats. Cathay Pacific's business class is also known for the "raft of useful features" incorporated within it. Examples include a dimmable reading light, slide-out coffee table, and a petite amenities storage space.

Flying on the world's largest commercial aircraft, Emirates' premium economy gives passengers access to 40 inches of pitch and 19.5 inches (49.3 cm) of width. Eight inches of recline provides additional comfort. This product was unveiled in 2020. Sir Tim Clark, Emirates president, said: “The Emirates A380 is already one of the most sought-after travel experiences in the skies, and now we’ve made it even better. While others cut back, Emirates is working hard to restore the products and services that we’ve had to suspend or adjust."

ITA Airways has premium economy products on its A321neo, A330-200, A330-900, and A350-900. The A330-200 and A350-900 are older aircraft with less space in premium economy. The carrier's A321neo and A330neo offer the best products, giving passengers access to a spacious 40 inches of pitch. Both jets are equipped with Safran seats, with the A330neo using the Z535i and the A321neo deploying the Z600. These products are relatively similar, but with minor alterations made to make them compatible with widebody and narrowbody planes, respectively.

As with any great premium economy product, there is much more to ITA's premium economy than space. You'll find a generous touchscreen measuring 15.6 inches (39.6 cm), along with power ports, a coat hook, a legrest, and a footrest. You'll also be able to enjoy food inspired by Italy's legendary cuisine, as pictured above.

The Italian carrier's premium product has been well-received by trip reviewers. Upgraded Points traveled on an ITA A330neo and wrote: "The service from the flight attendants didn’t rise to the wow factor, but it was good. The hard product on the flight was superior to the soft product, and I would definitely fly ITA Airways again. The cabin interior looked amazing, everything felt new and clean."

The comfort available onboard Project Sunrise flights will extend beyond the premium economy seat. Qantas will dedicate space to a wellbeing zone. Both premium economy and economy passengers can use this space for hydration and self-serve refreshments, along with spaces for stretching and guided movement. Although this area is essential for economy passengers who would otherwise spend over 20 hours in a cramped seat, it will also provide fantastic comfort and variety for premium economy passengers.

Premium economy has grown significantly in popularity over the past decade.

Premium economy onboard Starlux's A350-900s can be found over the aircraft's wings front section, right behind the second door. It includes 36 seats in a 2-4-2 configuration, while row 24 has just two outer pairs of seats. These seats, which are RECARO PL3530, all have 40 inches of seat pitch. They also have a width of 18.7 inches (47.5 cm), which is a small but noticeable step up from the 18 inches (45.7 cm) you can expect in standard economy.

Sleep and entertainment are the priority for the airline's product. All passengers can enjoy bedding from the Taiwanese brand Sleepy Tofu. This includes a luxury pillow with a quilted design. Entertainment comes from the 15.6-inch (39.6 cm) high-definition entertainment screen on the back of every seat. These screens are loaded with countless TV shows, music, movies, and games, and come with noise-canceling headphones so you aren't disturbed by the surrounding passengers.

Finally, premium economy passengers are given amenity kits from SMILEY Company. These kits are co-branded with Starlux Airlines and include socks, an eye mask, lip balm, and hand lotion. Please note that the kit is only available on routes to North America.

Starlux's marketing of this product focuses as much on the vibe that has been created in the cabin as the hard product. "Passengers are welcomed with warm and uplifting feel. The subtle color combinations of warm brown and gray tones are at the same time calming and comforting but also expressive, creating a very specific atmosphere of relaxed elegance to the whole premium economy cabin," Starlux writes.
