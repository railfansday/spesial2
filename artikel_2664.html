2026 CIAC boys soccer tournament semifinal previews, predictions, tickets

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Only 16 boys soccer teams are left standing as we enter the CIAC semifinals, which begin on Tuesday. Of those 16 teams, two defending champions remain in Staples of Class LL and Tolland of Class M.

Three No. 1 seeds have reached the semifinals, and just one No. 2 seed. Three 2025 CIAC championship runners-up still have a shot at returning to a final, in Greenwich (LL), Haddam-Killingworth (S) and Stonington (S but was in M last year).

Here is what you need to know about the semifinals scheduled for Tuesday and Wednesday.

WHEN: Tuesday, Nov. 17, 7 p.m.WHERE: Municipal StadiumTICKETS: GoFan $10 for adults (plus service fees); $5 for students, senior citizens (65 and older), military members with ID; free for children 5 and under.

HOW THEY GOT HEREStaples: BYE; def. No. 16 Danbury 5-0; def. No. 9 NFA 3-0.Glastonbury: BYE; def. No. 20 Southington 3-0; def. No. 5 Trumbull 2-1.

OUTLOOKA pair of conference champions will go head-to-head in what will arguably be the most highly anticipated match in the tournament, and could be considered a battle between the top two teams in the state. Staples, the No. 1 team in both the GametimeCT Power Rankings and the Coaches' Class LL/L Poll, has not lost a game this year, and has only allowed seven goals (three of which came in the FCIAC tournament). The Wreckers, led by second-year head coach Liam Witham, successfully defended the FCIAC title and are looking to now defend the state title. Striker Adrian Rodriguez has been one of the most prolific goal scorers in the state, and Staples has dominated its two tournament opponents so far. Glastonbury, which won the CCC championship this year behind a massive group of seniors that were hungry after a mediocre 2025 season, will have its work cut out for it. The Guardians took out a top FCIAC team in the semifinals, and did defeat the FCIAC runner-up (Stamford) 4-1 in the regular season. This will likely be the best game across all four tournament semifinals. ALDAM'S PICK: Staples

WHEN: Wednesday, Nov. 18, 6 p.m.WHERE: New Canaan High SchoolTICKETS: GoFan $10 for adults (plus service fees); $5 for students, senior citizens (65 and older), military members with ID; free for children 5 and under.

HOW THEY GOT HEREBrien McMahon: def. No. 18 Fairfield Warde 2-1 (3-2 PKs); def. No. 2 Xavier 3-2.Greenwich: def. No. 27 Kennedy 6-1; def. No. 11 Wilbur Cross 5-0; def. No. 14 Hamden 2-1.

OUTLOOKGreenwich is no stranger to the final rounds of the CIAC Class LL tournament. This is the fifth year in a row that the Cardinals have reached the semifinals, a stretch that includes two championship appearances and one title in 2022. Last year the Cardinals lost to Staples in the championship, and are eager to get back. Its opponent, No. 15 Brien McMahon, has had the opposite story. This is McMahon's first CIAC semifinal appearance since 1989. The program has not reached the finals since 1988 and hasn't won a title since 1974. Still, McMahon is as deserving to be here as any team, having been the biggest spoiler across all four tournaments. The Senators took out No. 2 seed and SCC champion Xavier, handing the Falcons their first and only loss of the season in penalty kicks. Then McMahon took down SCC powerhouse Fairfield Prep in the quarterfinals behind a hat-trick from Matthew Chen, who has been one of the top postseason performers. After not even earning a bid in the FCIAC tournament, McMahon will now face a Greenwich squad that it lost to 5-0 in the season opener back on September 9. ALDAM'S PICK: Greenwich

WHEN: Wednesday, Nov. 18, 7 p.m.WHERE: Municipal StadiumTICKETS: GoFan $10 for adults (plus service fees); $5 for students, senior citizens (65 and older), military members with ID; free for children 5 and under.

HOW THEY GOT HERENew Milford: BYE; def. No. 17 Windsor 2-1; def. No. 8 Wilton 2-0.Berlin: def. No. 19 Platt Tech 5-0; def. No. 13 Bassick 4-0; def. No. 12 Newtown 5-0.

OUTLOOKNew Milford is hoping to reach its first CIAC final since 2009, which is the only time in program history the Green Wave have gotten to the end. In two years head coach Steven Carneiro has turned the program into a fledgling SWC team into one of the top teams in the state. New Milford was undefeated heading into the SWC championship, which it lost to defending champion Weston. New Milford, led by Anthony Diaz (who has scored 25 goals this year), has recovered from that championship loss to reach its second semifinal in program history. Berlin will be New Milford's opposite, and has a completely different story this year. After beginning the year 1-4-1, the Red Coats bounced back to win nine of its final 10 regular season games, and then took down defending CCC champion Tolland in the first round of that tournament before falling to eventual 2026 champion Glastonbury in penalties during the semifinals. Berlin is led offensively by Thomas Francalangia and defensively by Cole Pike. Berlin has never reached a CIAC state championship game. ALDAM'S PICK: Berlin

WHEN: Wednesday, Nov. 18, 4:30 p.m.WHERE: Municipal StadiumTICKETS: GoFan $10 for adults (plus service fees); $5 for students, senior citizens (65 and older), military members with ID; free for children 5 and under.

HOW THEY GOT HEREBethel: BYE; def. No. 14 Lyman Hall 2-1; def. 11 Simsbury 1-0. Middletown: def. No. 26 Cheney Tech 8-1; def. No. 23 Platt 2-1; def. No. 15 E.O. Smith 3-2.

OUTLOOKThe SWC vs. CCC theme of the Class L tournament continues with Bethel vs. Middletown. Bethel has not reached a CIAC final since 2010, but has earned the state title crown in all six of its championship appearances. The Wildcats have been a top 10 seed in the Class L tournament every year since 2019, but have struggled to get through the quarterfinal round in that time. Ironically, in its first time not reaching the SWC championship game in four years, it makes its deepest run in states. Bethel is led by Kevin Ly, who has scored the majority of Bethel's goals this season. On the other side is Middletown and its prolific goal scorer in Andrew Edward. Middletown is looking for its second CIAC championship game appearance in program history, the last being in 1976. The Blue Dragons did reach the Class L semifinals in 2022, but lost 5-1 to Xavier. Middletown suffered a first-round exit from the CCC tournament, and is extremely battle tested from a difficult regular season schedule that includes losses to Glastonbury, Tolland, and Berlin. ALDAM'S PICK: Middletown

Valley Regional High SchoolTICKETS: GoFan $10 for adults (plus service fees); $5 for students, senior citizens (65 and older), military members with ID; free for children 5 and under.

HOW THEY GOT HEREWaterford: def. No. 32 Griswold 8-0; def. No. 16 Watertown 7-1; def. No. 8 Lewis Mills 3-1.Hand: def. No. 28 Bulkeley 10-0; def. No. 12 Lakeview 5-0; def. No. 4 Nonnewaug 3-2 (OT).

OUTLOOKWaterford has broken onto the scene in a big way this season, going undefeated in the regular season with just a tie against NFA as its only blemish. After a bye in the first round of the ECC tournament however, the Lancers fell to Bacon Academy in penalty kicks and were unable to win the conference title. Waterford has bounced back by outscoring opponents 18-2 in its three tournament games so far, as it attempts to reach its first CIAC final since 1987. Waterford has never won a CIAC championship, but with an trio of exceptional goal scorers this could be the best team in program history. Jaydev Villa (23 goals and 11 assists), Mateus Fontoura-Oliveira (22 goals and 14 assists) and Bray Michonski (19 goals and 13 assists) have made Waterford a dominant force this year. On the flip side is Hand, which lost in the SCC semifinals to eventual champion Xavier and is now the only SCC team left across all four tournaments. Hand is looking to return to a CIAC final for the first time since 2019, which was the fourth year in a row that the program had won the Class L championship. Hand is led by a couple of 20+ goal scorers in Charlie Mannix and Timmy McSweeney. Hand has outscored its opponents 18-2 in the tournament as well, but needed overtime to defeat Nonnewaug 3-2 in the quarterfinal round. With no schedule overlap between these two opponents, this one is truly a toss-up from a prediction standpoint. ALDAM'S PICK: Hand

WHEN: Tuesday, Nov. 17, 4:30 p.m.WHERE: Municipal StadiumTICKETS: GoFan $10 for adults (plus service fees); $5 for students, senior citizens (65 and older), military members with ID; free for children 5 and under.

HOW THEY GOT HEREWeston: def. No. 31 Whitney Tech 9-1; def. No. 15 Plainville 2-0; def. No. 7 Bacon Academy 1-0. Tolland: def. No. 30 Amistad 6-0; def. No. 19 New Fairfield 5-1; def. No. 6 Suffield 4-1.

OUTLOOKThe story in Class M is whether Tolland will be able to turn its back-to-back championship run into a true dynasty with a third straight title this year. The Eagles, led by Jonathan Adadjo (who has been a key part of both of his team's previous championship runs), were stunned by Berlin in the first round of its attempted CCC title defense, but has outscored its Class M opponents 15-2 in three games. It will be taking on a familiar team in the semifinals, the team it beat in 2-1 in the 2024 Class M championship game. In that game Adadjo (a sophomore at the time) scored the game-winner with just over three minutes left. No doubt Weston remembers that game, and after losing in last year's Class M semifinal to Stonington in overtime, the Trojans finally get a shot at redemption. Weston is coming off a successful defense of its SWC title with a dominant 4-0 win over New Milford, the only team it had lost to in the regular season. This will be the third semifinal game across all four tournaments that pins a CCC team against a SWC team. ALDAM'S PICK: Tolland

WHEN: Wednesday, Nov. 18, 6:30 p.m.WHERE: Waterford High SchoolTICKETS: GoFan $10 for adults (plus service fees); $5 for students, senior citizens (65 and older), military members with ID; free for children 5 and under.

HOW THEY GOT HEREStonington: BYE; def. No. 20 Lyman Memorial 7-0; def. No. 5 Old Saybrook 1-0.Valley Regional: def. No. 24 Innovation 2-0; def. No. 8 Shepaug 2-0; def. No. 1 Northwestern 1-0.

OUTLOOKFor seven of the last eight seasons, Stonington has reached a CIAC semifinal. Until this year, those had all been in the Class M tournament, a stretch in which the program reached four championship games and won two of them. Last year it lost in the final against Tolland, but this year has returned to the semifinal in Class S. Despite losing its top goal scorer from last year, Stonington has fought its way into familiar territory. Brayden Wolfradt has been an important offensive weapon in the postseason for Stonington, while goalie Matteo Panciera has not allowed a goal in the tournament (both are also baseball players who helped lead Stonington to the S championship game in the spring). Stonington lost its first round game in the ECC tournament, but has posted a pair of shutouts in the state tournament so far. It will get a tough test in a Valley Regional team that finished as the runner-up in the Shoreline Conference and also has a dominant goalie in sophomore Paul Malaguti. Valley Regional has not allowed a goal in three tournament games, including in a 1-0 takedown of the tournament's top seed. Valley Regional is one of the three remaining Shoreline Conference teams left standing in Class S, but will have to get past the one out of conference team standing for another crack at a title. If it does so, it will be the Warriors' first CIAC finals appearance since 2010. ALDAM'S PICK: Stonington

HOW THEY GOT HEREH-K: def. No. 26 Wheeler 8-2; def. No. 10 Morgan 3-1; def. No. 2 Cromwell 3-1. East Hampton: BYE; def. No. 14 Woodland 5-4 (OT); def. No. 11 Canton 4-1.

OUTLOOKThis is a second take on the Shoreline Conference tournament for both teams, after both were eliminated by Valley Regional in penalties in the actual tournament (East Hampton in the quarterfinals and Haddam-Killingworth in the semifinals). Haddam-Killingworth is hoping to reach the finals for a second straight year, after losing to Somers 3-1 in last year's championship. To get here, the Cougars have mostly taken down conference opponents like the Shoreline Conference champion Morgan and the tournament's top seed Cromwell. Now it will face an East Hampton team that it defeated 3-1 in the regular season. East Hampton is looking for its first CIAC championship game appearance since 2022, and has not claimed a share of the title since 2014. East Hampton's path to the semifinal has been easier (on paper) than Haddam-Killingworth's, but the Shoreline Conference was filled with circular defeats at the top. This should be one of the best rivalry semifinal games across all four tournaments. ALDAM'S PICK: Haddam-Killingworth
