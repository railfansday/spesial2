Undrafted rookie could be the Rams best option at tight end

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

After making a move towards multiple tight ends late last season, it was not surprising that the Los Angeles Rams brought eight tight ends into OTAs and kept them all through training camp. L.A. finally decided to keep five, a higher than average number, on the opening roster. They also kept around another tight end, prospect Dan Villari, on the practice squad.

Spring forward to the end of November and the Rams deep tight end room has been thinned substantially by injuries. Terrance Ferguson is out for the year; Colby Parkinson has missed one full game, parts of another half-dozen, and obviously impaired in many others; Davis Allen has a high ankle sprain, which carries a normal recovery time of a minimum of four weeks; and now Tyler Higbee has been designated as week-to-week with a bruised chest. Against the Green Packers on Thanksgiving Eve, the Rams installed offensive tackle David Quessenberry as tight end in the "13" personnel groupings.

Villari, 24 years-old, was signed by the Rams as a 2026 undrafted free agent out of Syracuse, but his journey to the NFL did not begin as a tight end. Originally recruited and signed as a quarterback by Michigan, he spent two seasons with the Wolverines. After a redshirt, he appeared in four 2021 games, recording one completion in three attempt and running for 35 yards on nine carries.

Transferred to Syracuse for 2022 and the Orangemen converted him to tight end in summer workouts. Over four seasons, he played in 44 games with 25 starts, catching 64 passes for 671 yards. In 2023 and 24, Syracuse, lined him up all over the field, collecting reps at running back and quarterback to go along with tight end duties. For his career, he chipped in 484 rushing yards at a 4.9 per carry clip and completed 23 of 33 passes for 177 yards.

It's fair to label him as a receiver first prospect. At 6'4" 243 lb.Villari is not a towering tight end like the rest of the unit. He sports just-above average arm length at 32 1/4" and owns good-sized hands, 9 3/4". On film, Villari looks like a fluid athlete. At his Pro Day, he didn't test in any of the explosive/strength drills, vertical, broad jump, or bench press. Bit of a discrepancy in the reporting of his forty yard dash times, RAS reports 4.77 with a 1.66 10-yard split, while others put him at a hand-timed 4.60. A 7.04 3cone shows stellar agility and flexibility. Burst and balance were excellent as well, 4.31 shuttle.

College film shows, even with his lack of experience, the looks of a natural fit at tight end. Primarily lined up in the slot, he gets to his spot and gives his quarterback a nice square target. Nice feel for open space. I have to describe his hands as iron in velvet, soft because the football just seems to be absorbed into his grip and strong because he plucks passes with his hands away from his body and tucks them away quickly. Solid wingspan and body control to haul in awry balls. Fearless over the middle and catches well through contact. As an open-field runner, I'd use "competitive" as the descriptor, he gets north/south in a hurry and runs low behind his pads, often right through first contact, Blocking needs a lot of technique and strength work. He's willing, but doesn't have much pop in his strikes and lacks the needed handwork/arm extension.

Came into the pros as a developmental prospect, but you can see he has the base traits. Villari has been on the receiving end of passes just three short years. He offers interesting versatility, although it would be highly suspect if the Rams would build a package of plays around an undrafted player joining the team this late in proceedings. He did however, run the ball out of wildcat formation, throws pretty well, and has very good hands. Like almost all youngsters, he needs route-running polish, but Villari's short area agility and natural move skills hint that he can handle a bigger route tree. Most of his targets were of the simpler variety, pivots, look-in/curls, and up the seams. In actuality, the current L.A. tight ends don't appear to be running a particularly complicated route tree either.

The Rams preseason lack of urgency in adding wide receiver depth hinted they're were going to make use of tight ends in the slot akin to receivers as a regular part of the rotation. Things have since changed due to all the injuries. Of the remaining two fully-available tight ends remaining, Parkinson can, and has been split out, but he's a bit heavy-footed and best suited closer to the action, while Max Klare is more natural to the slot, in skills, size, and athletically. Villari game appears more apt to fit in with Klare's.

Circumstances have certainly conspired against the Rams ball catchers, both wide outs and tight ends. Sean McVay would really be up against it if he were to try shapeshifting the offense again, back into three wide receiver sets. There doesn't appear to be many answers there. The L.A. braintrust looks limited in their options. At tight end, they will likely need to add a body. It could be a street retread or giving Villari a shot.

At $45k guaranteed, Villari was the Rams highest paid undrafted rookie in 2026, there must be more than "roster filler" interest, Yes indeed, he joined the team needing development and strength/conditioning work, but he's now been in the program for seven months, working on strength, technique and getting a grasp off the playbook. There is likely little or no upgrade available at this point.

In theory, McVay and tight ends coach Scott Huff could kick the tires on Villari, he has three practice squad elevations to the active roster, before a permanent decision needs to be made on his future. Elevated or signed, the Rams need to activate Dan Villari.
