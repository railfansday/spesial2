How weird vibes from the Springboks could have huge impact in Nations Championship final

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

To whatever extent the words of Rassie Erasmus were lost in translation last week, South Africa used their team announcement press conference on Monday to make a clear point. With Erasmus unable to conduct media duties under the terms of his ban from World Rugby, assistant coach Mzwandile Stick stood in at the Springboks' hotel in southwest London – taking questions in English but answering only in Xhosa, his first language.

It was explained that the coaching staff wanted to ensure nothing was misinterpreted. With the dedicated Kuhle Sonkosi – a former lock who now works for South African broadcaster SuperSport – acting as an impromptu translator, Stick spent a slightly stilted half-an-hour explaining a heavily-changed team for the Nations Championship final against France. He espoused the virtues of Manu Tshituka, the Congo-born back rower compared to Pieter-Steph du Toit primed for an international debut at Twickenham on Sunday.

Mzwandile Stick conducted his press briefing entirely in Xhosa (PA)

At one point, Stick cracked a joke about his good looks being the reason he attracted more attention than other coaches from the cameras in Dublin, insisting that it had been a collective effort to replace Erasmus. To Sonkosi's credit, the remark drew a chuckle even on translation.

Clearly, the coach was entitled to converse in his mother tongue if he wished to; South Africa has 12 official languages, of course. As is generally the case, he was engaging company.

Stick (left) stood in for Springboks head coach Rassie Erasmus (Getty)

Yet one could should not shake a slightly peculiar feel. In part, that has to do with a side going into a final, with two trophies potentially on the line, on the heels of consecutive defeats. "Obviously, it's not ideal to go into the final having lost back-to-back losses," Stick admitted. "I think the more we're frustrated at is that we haven't played to our standards. For us, it's very important to stop this run." A shot at revenge against France is welcome after a game of fine margins; the Springboks coach rightly noted that his side had come out on the right side of three one-point contests on their way to World Cup glory two years ago.

But the weirdness pervaded beyond the oddities of this new competition. Just a quarter-of-an-hour before Stick sat down to speak, Erasmus was posting on social media not about the team about to be named, or the Springboks nominated for World Rugby awards, but about his plans to once again livestream his reaction on Sunday afternoon.

"Watchwithrassie" had proved a success of sorts, with more than 100,000 people in South Africa signing up to watch a silent stream of the South African supremo sat on his own in a hotel room and occasionally appearing to speak into his phone. "Sound will remain off until Rassie wants to speak," a chyron said – the unmute button was never pressed. At least someone can silence the double World Cup-winning coach. Oddly, despite issuing a media release suggesting they wanted a full misconduct hearing following his ban, SA Rugby never actually formally applied for one.

Rassie Erasmus has not actually requested a full misconduct hearing (PA Archive)

It is worth thought, too, that Stick himself could yet face action for his remarks about Sam Prendergast that followed the Ireland fly half's disciplinary hearing last week. The coach had suggested that the player would have been suspended for a hip-drop tackle had he been playing for Fiji after it was concluded that his challenge had not met a red-card threshold. Both World Rugby and the Nations Championship are said to be aware and considering his comments.

All 12 head coaches and captains from the competing nations on the inaugural finals weekend are expected to attend a launch at Twickenham on Wednesday evening to build interest and try and shift a few more tickets. It is not yet totally clear to what extent South Africa will attend; of course, they would point out that their head coach cannot under the terms of his ban. That complicates the flow of the week, and their on-pitch preparations will come first.

South Africa will get another chance to face France (Getty)

One wonders, though, what twists and turns this week might have in store if the weird vibes continue. It is strange, clearly, to have the world champions seemingly at odds with the rest of the game – indeed, the Erasmus saga has served to distract from both how good the Springboks have been, and how they might look to escape what, for them, might be regarded as a rut.

With Damian Willemse injured, fly half Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu shifts to full-back and Manie Libbok comes in at fly half. "I think the key thing is converting opportunities," Stick explained. "I think the forwards have given us a great platform over the last two weeks and we've just not been able to take those chances." A fearsome bench includes two of those World Rugby Player of the Year nominees in Malcolm Marx and Ox Nche; Eben Etzebeth, a previous winner of the award, lurks among the replacements, too.

Ox Nche and Malcolm Marx have been nominated for World Player of the Year (Getty)

Having been thrown, slightly, by the French selection in Paris, it is helpful in a sense for South Africa to have more certainty that Matthieu Jalibert will begin at No 10. "You want to play against the best," Stick stressed. "France had those injuries before and now they're close to full strength. We can see where we're sitting before we go into 2027, which is a World Cup year."

The Springboks appear ready for anything; the rest of us may have to be, too.
