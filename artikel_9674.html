GitHub Octoverse 2026: AI Agents, Citizen Developers, and the New Security Reality

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Posted on Nov 3 • Originally published at blog.gitguardian.com

Every year, we look forward to GitHub's State of the Octoverse report. It is one of the best leading indicators of where software development is heading, and what the rest of the technology industry can expect in the coming years. This year's report is especially exciting, and the numbers are staggering.

Over the past year, developers generated 34.3 billion commits across GitHub, up from 12.9 billion the previous year. That is nearly three times the activity in just one year. Even more remarkable, over 40% of pull requests now start with an AI agent, up from just 17% six months earlier. GitHub Actions also executed more than 45 billion jobs, over three times the previous year's total.

These numbers signal a fundamental change in who creates software and how quickly it gets built. Professional developers are working faster, citizen developers are creating applications with AI, and autonomous agents are taking on more development tasks.

These numbers are also startling when you consider trends already reported in other reports like GitGuardian's State of Secrets Sprawl 2026, which documented a record 28.65 million exposed secrets in public GitHub commits during 2025.

The trends emerging on GitHub today show where we are headed as an industry and what organizations everywhere will soon be managing. Here are the five findings that stood out to our team and what they tell us about the future of software security.

GitHub's latest Octoverse report reveals how quickly AI is transforming software development. Here are the top stats that popped out to our team:

GitHub recorded 34.3 billion commits in 2026, up from 12.9 billion the previous year. That is not a small increase. That is an explosion. Code pushes increased by approximately 167%, reaching 17.9 billion.

GitGuardian's State of Secrets Sprawl 2026 had already documented a 43% increase in public commits during 2025, alongside a 34% increase in exposed secrets. The research showed the highest number ever recorded last year, at 28.65 million new secrets in public GitHub commits. While GitHub Push Protection does affect the rate, by their own numbers, they ran 5.8 billion scans this year vs the total 17.9 billion pushes. This implies the majority of code pushes go unchecked, which aligns with our conclusion that Push Protection is no silver bullet.

Across almost all recent security reports, we have seen a consistent trend of rapid growth. As software production accelerates, organizations need secrets detection and credential security controls that keep pace with development.

In March 2026, approximately 17% of pull requests started with an AI agent. By September, that figure exceeded 40%, representing approximately 110 million agent-started pull requests in a single month.

We have previously identified security implications of AI-assisted development. Claude Code-assisted commits exposed secrets at approximately twice the baseline rate, with 3.2% containing a secret compared with 1.5% across public commits. We believe that this implies that people are not reviewing code deeply enough to catch security issues that the AI introduces.

AI coding agents create many chances for unintended credential exposure and access to sensitive systems during otherwise legitimate development tasks. Preventative security controls need to operate during agent execution, alongside traditional source code scanning.

GitHub reports more than 225 million developers on its platform. Public repositories associated with identifiable industries grew from about 9 million in 2022 to 25 million in 2026.

AI-powered development tools are making it easier for employees without traditional programming experience to build applications. Citizen developers can now create internal dashboards, automate business workflows, and integrate external services using natural language instructions.

These applications frequently require access to business systems through API keys and service credentials. Citizen developers may have limited experience with secrets management, application security, or cloud permissions.

Organizations need secure development processes that are accessible to employees across departments. Safe defaults for credential handling let citizen coders build useful applications while reducing exposure. The right guardrails in the right workflows can prevent a lot of issues.

AI coding assistants most commonly operate directly within a developer's environments. They can read project files, inspect application configurations, and execute commands as part of routine development work.

Research into the second major wave of the Shai-Hulud supply chain attack identified 33,185 unique secrets across 6,943 compromised machines. Separate endpoint research found that 40% of high and critical secrets identified on developer laptops were located in AI tool directories and logs.

Discovering and eliminating unnecessary local credentials reduces exposure. Preventative controls such as git hooks provide additional protection when agents interact with files and tools.

GitHub Actions executed approximately 45.4 billion jobs during the latest reporting period, compared with 14 billion the previous year. This represents growth of more than 220% in automated development activity.

As automation increases, organizations must manage a growing number of credentials and their associated permissions. This includes moving towards shorter-lived authentication mechanisms.

More than 64% of credentials, originally found to be valid, stayed valid for more than four years, according to our research. The report also showed that 46% of critical secrets could be missed when prioritization relies exclusively on provider validation.

As software development and automation continue accelerating, detecting exposed credentials, remediating existing risks, and preventing new exposures must become part of everyday development workflows.

Embedding preventive controls directly into agent workflows lets developers benefit from AI automation with greater confidence. This supplements pre-commit git hooks to catch secrets out of code, which many developers have already adopted.

Citizen developers need straightforward ways to connect applications to business systems securely. Centralized secrets management and appropriate access permissions provide a foundation for safer development. AI-assisted development is accelerating the creation of applications and infrastructure. Credentials used by repositories, build systems, and deployment pipelines can provide access to critical parts of the software supply chain. Professional developers are producing code at unprecedented rates, AI agents are taking on increasingly complex tasks, and citizen developers are building applications that would have required specialized engineering skills just a few years ago.

The number of applications, integrations, and credentials organizations must secure has never grown this fast. The attack surface is much larger now.
