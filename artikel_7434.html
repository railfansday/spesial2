Mondlane’s trial follows the four-year prison sentence given to Tanzanian opposition leader Tundu Lissu earlier this month for inciting people to disrupt a 2025 election. Lissu, who said he was prosecuted for calling for electoral reform, was acquitted of treason in that case.

One of Uganda’s veteran opposition figures, Kizza Besigye, is on trial for treason and faces a potential death penalty if convicted.

Zambia’s leading opposition figure and several supporters also face treason charges in their southern African country following another troubled election this year that rights groups say was characterized by a campaign of harassment against the opposition.

In Mozambique in eastern Africa, Mondlane lost to President Daniel Chapo of the Frelimo party in the 2024 election. Frelimo has now governed Mozambique for 51 years since independence from Portugal in 1975 and has been accused of rigging elections and suppressing opposition, which it denies.

Mondlane rejected the 2024 results as fraudulent and called for protests and people to stay away from work after the vote. Postelection protests by thousands of people lasted months in the largest popular uprising against Frelimo. They were exacerbated by the killing of Mondlane’s lawyer and another official in their opposition Podemos party, who were fatally shot in a car in what Mondlane called political assassinations.

Mondlane left Mozambique for a period following the killings, claiming his life was in danger.

Amnesty International, citing local civic groups, said more than 300 people were killed in the protests and criticized the Mozambican authorities for what it called a brutal crackdown on protesters. Human Rights Watch alleged that security forces used live ammunition against protesters.

Election observers including a team from the European Union questioned the validity of the elections and said there were irregularities in counting and the alteration of some results.

Mondlane, who has since formed a new party, has denied the charges against him and said he was advocating for “electoral truth” with his messages on social media calling on people to protest and boycott work.
