Players in limbo after NZ netball league collapses

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

All Parris Mason has known is playing netball. But with a World Cup just nine months away, she and four other New Zealand players have been left in limbo after the collapse of their domestic league.

"There's a high potential I will not be playing netball heading into a World Cup," she told BBC Sport.

While a significant number of her Silver Ferns team-mates play professionally in Australia and England, Mason stayed with her home league.

The 23-year-old defender, who has been a full-time professional for six years, found out her netball fate when the news was leaked to the media.

"It shattered me," she said. "The influx of emotions; is my career over? What do I do next? It was just so much to handle all at once."

Netball New Zealand admitted the way events unfolded publicly was deeply regrettable and has pledged to support the affected players.

"We know this period of uncertainty has been extremely tough for players, coaches, teams and everyone involved in the domestic competition," a statement said.

"Their wellbeing is our priority, and we are working closely with the New Zealand Netball Players' Association to support them."

It comes as the Silver Ferns are on the cusp of becoming the world's number one ranked side, after winning gold at the 2026 Commonwealth Games and beating Australia in the Constellation Cup.

The World Cup will be held in Sydney, Australia, between 25 August and 5 September, with New Zealand looking to win the title for a sixth time.

There had been uncertainty over the future of the ANZ Premiership for a couple years. The league had struggled to secure long-term broadcast deals which impacted the league financially.

In 2026, the league recorded a loss of around $NZ 3.7m (£1.5m). There was an exodus of the country's best players.

Ten of the 12 Silver Ferns who won gold for New Zealand at the 2026 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow have moved overseas to play - primarily in Australia.

Businesswoman Anna Mowbray and her husband, former All Blacks player Ali Williams, pitched a new private league for 2027, but negotiations fell through just eight weeks after signing a term sheet.

"I don't understand my life without netball," said Mason. "It's been a big part of my identity, and [there is a] mental toll to [being] continually knocked down.

"I think of everything in this whole situation as just completely sad."

Nathan Limm, who has been following the story for the New Zealand Herald said: "The Ferns' success feels a little bit fragile. They get a few injuries, where's the depth underneath if they're not playing domestic netball?

"I'm not confident there will be a league next year. The communication between Netball NZ and the Players Association, with their investors, with the public, has been so rocky.

"I think there is a real distinct lack of trust between the players and Netball NZ."

When asked whether there are plans for a domestic netball competition next season New Zealand Netball said: "Work on an elite domestic competition for 2027 is well under way. Our goal is a sustainable, professional competition for 2027 and beyond."
