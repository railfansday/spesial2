3 Dirt Cheap Artificial Intelligence (AI) Stocks to Load Up on Before 2027

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

AI spending continues to rise, and three of its biggest beneficiaries still trade at bargain valuations.

By Prosper Junior Bakiny – Nov 19, 2026 at 2:14PM EST

Many investors and analysts still aren't convinced that the artificial intelligence (AI) industry will continue expanding rapidly. That's partly why some AI companies, particularly those spending a small fortune on capex, haven't performed that well this year. But what if Wall Street is wrong and the AI market maintains its recent momentum over the medium term? In my view, there is a good chance that will happen. And if it does, several AI stocks could be great bargain buys right now. Let's consider three of them: Alphabet (GOOG -2.49%) (GOOGL -2.66%), Amazon (AMZN -3.92%), and Micron Technology (MU -1.70%).

Alphabet's shares are down 11% this year. Investors are worried about the company's capex and declining free cash flow, which has been negative for two straight quarters. However, Alphabet's AI-related investments are paying off. The company's core advertising business remains strong, and cloud computing sales continue to accelerate.

In the third quarter, Alphabet's total revenue came in at $127.6 billion, up 25% year over year. The company's Google Cloud sales were $30.7 billion, up 103% compared to the year-ago period. The tech leader's earnings per share declined to $2.12, down from $2.87, while its free cash flow was a negative $9.1 billion.

That said, Alphabet ended the quarter with $561 billion in cloud backlog, up 262% year over year -- and it expects to recognize 55% of it, or $308.55 billion, over the next 24 months. The company will likely receive new orders as well. That suggests Alphabet's cloud sales could continue to grow at a good clip in the foreseeable future, especially given the AI industry's continued growth.

The near-term negative free cash flow is worth the massive opportunities Alphabet is tapping into. The stock may have lagged broader equities this year, but Alphabet's prospects look bright from current levels. The company's price/earnings-to-growth (PEG) ratio of 0.5 is well within the undervalued range, which typically starts below 1.

Amazon has remained the leader in the cloud computing market, despite stiff competition from Alphabet and Microsoft (MSFT -1.51%) in recent years. What's more, AI is also helping boost the company's Amazon Web Services revenue. In the third quarter, Amazon's net sales increased by 12% year over year to $201.9 billion.

AWS revenue was $46 billion, up 39% year over year -- the strongest growth rate it has recorded in 18 quarters. Amazon's EPS decreased 3% year over year to $1.89. AWS continues to account for most of Amazon's operating profits, and demand for the company's services remains strong, as evidenced by its $578 billion AWS backlog, which the company said grew in the triple digits year over year.

Amazon could make significant margin gains in the medium term by doubling down on custom AI chips, which tend to be more cost-effective than comparable GPUs (graphics processing units). Further, the company's AI initiatives could also improve its e-commerce business, which remains its largest source of sales. Lastly, Amazon's PEG ratio is only about 0.6, which makes the stock look like a steal at current levels. Long-term investors bullish on AI's prospects can hardly go wrong with this stock.

Micron Technology designs memory chips, which are critical components in the AI value chain. In fact, a shortage in this industry has granted the semiconductor leader significant pricing power. Its revenue and earnings have skyrocketed as a result. In the fourth quarter of its fiscal year 2026, which ended on Sept. 3, Micron's revenue grew by 379% year over year to $54.23 billion.

The company's non-GAAP earnings per share were $33.42, up 1,003% compared to the year-ago period.

How long will the memory chip shortage last? Perhaps until 2028 or 2029, according to some projections, potentially providing a strong tailwind to Micron through the medium term. And although the company faces the risk of slowing top- and bottom-line growth -- as well as potential regulatory problems -- the market has already factored these in. Micron's PEG ratio is only 0.2, which makes the stock dirt cheap right now.
