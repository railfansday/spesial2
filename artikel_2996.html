Is Avalanche’s New Deal With the New York Stock Exchange a Threat to Hyperliquid?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Avalanche just managed to secure an incredible opportunity that Hyperliquid seemed well positioned to capture.

On Nov. 9, Ava Labs, the developer of Avalanche (AVAX -0.41%), said that its blockchain will be the technology backend for the New York Stock Exchange's (NYSE) planned new venue for trading tokenized stocks. The new venue will be owned by Intercontinental Exchange (ICE -3.04%), which also owns the NYSE.

This new development is both expected, as many financial institutions are exploring tech solutions for their tokenized asset operations, and surprising, as Avalanche isn't exactly one of the most competitive blockchains for attracting institutional interest.

Plus, ICE inked another big crypto deal just 11 days before its announcement to bring derivatives built on Hyperliquid (HYPE +0.03%) to the U.S. Now, with the Wall Street exchange giant pledging its support for two chains, Hyperliquid holders are likely wondering whether Avalanche's deal with the NYSE forecloses an opportunity for their investment.

So is the Avalanche deal a threat to Hyperliquid?

The new NYSE venue is envisioned by ICE as a 24/7 market for tokenized shares, with the important stipulation that the tokenized stocks will have the same entitlements for holders as the real shares that back them, like dividends and voting rights.

In terms of the collaboration's structure, once the venue goes live sometime in Q4 2026, NYSE's order book will be used to match buyers and sellers of the tokens, and Avalanche's technology, but not its actual network, will then be used to settle each trade. Avalanche will be the only blockchain tech used for that purpose, so there's no room to split the work with a competitor like Hyperliquid.

ICE's deal with Hyperliquid, which was announced on Oct. 29, works differently. It covers a derivatives market instead of a spot market, and ICE is also buying a minority stake in the company it's partnering with.

ICE will exclusively operate a regulated version of TradeXYZ's perpetual futures markets. It's a company that's closely linked to Hyperliquid itself and operates most of the largest markets on Hyperliquid's platform. Perpetual futures, or perps, resemble conventional futures contracts without an expiration date, and they usually involve using leverage. The Hyperliquid company will supply the network infrastructure, but it isn't directly involved in the agreement.

The target customers differ between these two deals too. ICE's Hyperliquid efforts are meant to attract retail customers seeking short-term price exposure and speculation on asset prices, while the NYSE venue working with Avalanche is meant for the selling of actual shares to a less differentiated group of investors. However, Hyperliquid's platform also offers spot markets, including tokenized shares, making it hard to avoid the conclusion that it was passed over.

It's true that Hyperliquid technically could have handled all of the activities that the NYSE chose Avalanche for. It's also true that Hyperliquid's low transaction costs and quick settlement times are better than those of Avalanche's public network, though the NYSE's private Avalanche backend will likely perform differently.

Still, Hyperliquid holders don't need to be concerned about Avalanche imminently eating its lunch, even if this episode signals the onset of a tougher competitive environment for signing similar deals going forward. For the 30 days ending Nov. 13, Hyperliquid brought in $89.6 million in fees, while Avalanche only brought in $8.1 million. Hyperliquid has plenty of business from trades on its platform regardless of what the NYSE chose.

As for the two coins competing for investor dollars, Avalanche doesn't pose much of a threat there either. It's unclear whether the new NYSE ledger will actually use Avalanche's coin for anything, and because of the structure of the private chain its technology will be used for, it's very likely that holders of the coin will not get much in the way of upside at all, even if many billions of dollars of value pass through. There isn't necessarily any new reason to buy Avalanche on account of the deal.

Therefore, Hyperliquid investors should be aware that while this event isn't a problem, any future integrations that the network might seek with traditional financial institutions will doubtlessly be contested by its competitors vying for the same goal. And sometimes, a project like Avalanche can take the day despite not even being on the radar most of the time. As this shows, getting a foot in the door at one institution doesn't guarantee getting the other foot in.

With all that said, Hyperliquid is still worth buying today, but its total addressable market is likely a bit smaller than before.
