Top 7 best value-for-money EFL League One signings this season

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

League One clubs are always looking for value-for-money signings.

The majority of clubs in the third tier don't have millions and millions to throw around, so smart additions that can either be flipped for big profits in the future, or even those you bring in to perform one role and who can perform it perfectly, go a long way toward achieving success in the division.

This list won't just run down the best signings in League One so far this season, but instead look at those who have made such a massive impact that you can't help but feel the selling side should be given a little more money in retrospect.

Here are the seven best 'value for money' signings in League One this season.

A 32-year-old Jayden Stockley moving from Port Vale after their relegation to AFC Wimbledon isn't going to grab headlines at the time, but for a Dons side who know their backs are against the wall and will be forced to play direct, the frontman has been perfect for them.

Stockley reunited with ex-Charlton Athletic boss Johnnie Jackson in the summer, and was handed one role: win aerial duels and get us up the field. Now 33, the target man has been brilliant.

He's been involved in 220 aerial duels, 81 more than anyone else in the division. The 112 he's won is 27 more than Paudie O'Connor in second place.

Plus, he's scored six league goals, which amounts to 60% of his side's total output. He's one of the main reasons why AFC Wimbledon are currently above the drop zone, and if they stay there, he'll be a key part of why, too.

Wigan Athletic had a big task on their hands this past summer in replacing academy graduate Sam Tickle after the goalkeeper made his well-deserved step-up to the Championship with Bristol City.

It seemed inevitable that the man to do so would be Joe Whitworth, whom Tics boss Gary Caldwell had loaned twice while at Exeter City from Crystal Palace, and they did so in a rather interesting deal.

The 22-year-old joined on a free transfer, with Palace holding a sell-on fee, meaning that Wigan will only have to pay the Premier League side a share of a future deal, rather than part ways with money now.

So far this season, Whitworth has prevented the most goals in the division, saved shots at a 70.2% rate and kept three clean sheets in 12 games, while also having above-average distribution numbers.

He's quickly establishing himself as one of the best in his position, and for a free transfer, that's not bad at all.

It'll be mentioned a little later on in this list, but the ability to bring in someone who had such a great loan spell the season prior on a permanent deal for a cut-price, or in this case, for free, is incredible business.

Jon Russell has been revived under Nigel Clough at Mansfield Town after a strangely poor half-year at Barnsley under Conor Hourihane, after he netted 11 in 2024/25. He spent the latter half of last year at Field Mill, scoring five and assisting one in 21 league games.

The 26-year-old made his move permanent for free and has five goals and three assists in 13 games in all competitions so far this year for the Stags. He's been one of the best goalscoring midfielders in the division, and Clough seems to be playing to his strengths.

Mansfield continue to upset the odds and break new ceilings, and it's deals like this which help them do so.

Bradford City were among the best defensive sides in League One last season. Only four teams conceded fewer goals. They arguably didn't need to replace Sam Walker in goal, but if the right deal was there, Bantams fans were open to the idea.

In came Jon McCracken from Dundee FC on a free transfer, who had recorded the most saves and prevented 7.53 goals in the SPL last season. He was even linked with Celtic back in April. So, getting him for nothing was already a good start.

In his first 17 games as a Bantam, the Scotsman has registered 12 clean sheets, including nine in 12 league outings. Before shipping three against Huddersfield Town, he'd conceded just four goals in 11 games.

That's quite exceptional, and the only thing perhaps limiting the amount of praise he should rightly get is the fact that Bradford as a whole are so great defensively. McCracken has only had to make 19 league saves all season, but he's still pulling through when called into action.

Derensili Sanches Fernandes has been the best player in League One this season. The man coined the 'dribble king' in the Dutch top flight at Excelsior has, unsurprisingly, been electric going forward against third-tier opposition, and has 13 goal involvements in 14 games.

Those involvements are as evenly split as you can imagine, too, with seven goals and six assists. Not only is he a great finisher, but he sets up chances better than anyone else, too. Indeed, no player has created more chances, nor big chances, than the 25-year-old.

He's even been capped by Cape Verde for the first time due to the quality he has shown at the Accu Stadium, and scored on his debut against Liberia in AFCON qualifiers.

But as good as Sanches Fernandes has been, he wasn't exactly cheap. Reports state that he moved to Huddersfield from Excelsior for "a significant" undisclosed fee, which could indicate a seven-figure sum. From that point of view, there are better value-for-money signings this season.

For the impact the wideman has made at his new club, though, Town fans feel like they may want to send the Dutch side a little more.

Luton Town's upturn in form towards the end of the season last year was not only down to Jack Wilshere becoming more settled in the dugout, but also due to the brilliance of Kasey Palmer, who joined on loan from Hull City.

In their 11-game unbeaten run to end the campaign, Palmer netted eight and assisted once. He also started in their EFL Trophy final win at Wembley against Stockport County. The Jamaican international had never played below the Championship before in his career, and was showcasing why.

It was imperative that Palmer made a permanent move if Luton were to continue their momentum from the latter stages of the previous year and go at least one place better than their seventh-place finish, and they managed to secure his services for less than £500,000.

For an attacking midfielder in his prime years, whose level is above League One, that's a brilliant bit of business, and he already has three goals and four assists in 12 league starts this season.

If he could help Jack Wilshere's side get back into the second-tier, it would be money well spent.

In terms of signing someone with big potential to be worth a lot more than the six-figure sum they initially paid, and who could help their side in the meantime push for promotion, no one comes close to Kyreece Lisbie.

Reports over the summer before a deal was made between Reading and Colchester United suggested that a fee between £500,000 and £750,000 would be needed, as Lisbie entered the final year of his deal at the League Two side, and already, he has the potential to be worth a decent seven-figure sum come the end of the campaign.

His twin brother, Kyrell, was sold to Millwall for between £2.5m and £3m this past summer after scoring 11 and assisting seven for Peterborough United. Kyreece is already over halfway to that goal-contribution tally and is performing for a side higher up in the division.

If he can keep this up, not only could he help Reading reach the second tier, but he could also bank the Royals a healthy profit immediately. Evidently, he has been the best value-for-money signing in League One so far.
