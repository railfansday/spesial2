Insider reveals Leeds outlay for latest Elland Road change: 'I know a lot about this...'

Tanggal: 2026-11-08

Leeds United are likely to have spent around £500,000 to install safe standing in the South Stand at Elland Road.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who has previously spoken about safe standing as part of "parliamentary enquiries."

Leeds installed the new seating in several areas of the South Stand ahead of Saturday's 1-0 win over Tottenham, and will complete the rest of the stand during the international break.

Wyness believes that the Whites' hierarchy need to work closely to understand the wants and needs of their fans during the continued upgrade of Elland Road.

The decision to install safe standing has been met with mixed feelings from the supporters so far, as their home ground continues to change.

Work is already underway to upgrade the capacity of Elland Road to 53,000, with the Whites' home set to be transformed in the coming years.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that Leeds need to engage in regular consultations with their fans to ensure that they get the redevelopment process right moving forward.

The Scottish businessman pointed towards an issue that emerged in the summer, with several season ticket holders displaced by the building work in the West Stand.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness outlined how Leeds must be mindful of what their fans want from the ground.

"Well, I know quite a bit about the safe seating. I actually spoke at some parliamentary enquiries when it was first coming out, and I understand it reasonably well," he said.

"If you're looking at per-seat cost added up to the number of fans they've got on safe standing, by the time they've got certification done for safety and consultancy fees, you're probably looking at about an investment of about £500,000 in terms of getting that done.

"Now, I know that there's been a lot of fan issues about moving people around with season tickets and all that sort of thing.

"What Leeds have to do is recognise this as an opportunity to show customer service and to really get into it in detail, work with each fan and solve it.

"That will be important going forward because they've got to look at this as a pilot about the whole redevelopment in general, and how they handle the fans' problems will be key to how everybody will look at this redevelopment going forward.

"So they've got to get really into the detail and work on each individual fan, and show that they're listening."

Football Insider revealed earlier this week that Leeds are unlikely to make a genuine attempt to sign Minnesota United striker Kelvin Yeboah in January.

The 26-year-old is on the radar of several Premier League clubs, with Everton, Ipswich and Crystal Palace all keen to sign him this winter.

Sources say that Leeds' interest in Yeboah is not advanced at all, despite the obvious family connections linking him to Elland Road.

Yeboah is the nephew of Leeds legend Tony Yeboah, who built a goalscoring legacy for the club during his two-year stint from 1995 to 1997.
