Inside the Tape: Jeremiah Trotter Jr. Is Becoming a Cornerstone at LB

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Inside the Tape: Jeremiah Trotter Jr. Is Becoming a Cornerstone at LB originally appeared on NBC Sports Philadelphia

Welcome back to our weekly film series, where we bypass surface-level stats to isolate the schematic tweaks, matchups, and micro-details that define the Eagles from week to week.

After diving into Drew Kendall, Willie Lampkin's first NFL start against the Jaguars in London, and rookie wideout Makai Lemon's expanded opportunity against Dallas on Monday Night weeks ago, the focus today once again returns to Trotter, whose ability to capitalize on opportunities has continued to stand out as the season has progressed.

With Zack Baun sidelined against Pittsburgh, Trotter took over green dot play-calling duties and was absolutely everywhere for 60 minutes. Whether it was attacking gaps in the ground game, finding ways to get a player to the turf in the open field, or showcasing his ability in coverage, every time his number has been called Trotter has found a way to make an impact, and then some.

With a short week ahead as Dallas awaits on Thanksgiving, let's take a closer look at what made Trotter's performance against Pittsburgh pop.

You want to talk about an explosive way to get on the stat sheet early, how about this play on 4th and 1 for your first tackle of the ballgame.

First, one thing that stands out here and throughout the ballgame was the usage of five and six man fronts at times from the Eagles defense. It's not a new thing as they've shown it in weeks prior, but it was a staple on Sunday against a Pittsburgh offense that prides itself on its ability to create movement up front for RB Jaylen Warren. Not in Week 11.

For Trotter, this is textbook linebacker play in a spot where he is initially aligned head up on Steelers center Zach Frazier (54). His ability to not only scrape over a gap, but to the far side of the strength where he ultimately works downhill off the shoulder of TE Pat Freiermuth (88) is an unbelievable showcase of instincts and one-on-tackling ability. There are linebackers in the NFL that could also identify what Trotter saw at the snap, sure, but executing and finishing the play is a whole different ballgame. Having the explosiveness and athleticism to beat RB Rico Dowdle to the spot on a 4th and short opportunity is a play only a select few LBs in football can make. This is outstanding football from Trotter.

See it and attack it. It's something Trotter does on every run play, and it's personified once again here in a violent manner against Warren. At its core, the linebacker position since the dawn of football is a spot where defenders are asked to set the tone. Some guys do it vocally, while Trotter lets his pads do the talking.

This is an Eagles defense that has played some decent football over the last month or so, but there was always more – another level that they could elevate their game to. With Trotter in the middle, the physicality and aggressiveness of the entire front seven moved up a few rungs on the ladder against Pittsburgh.

You all are familiar with the standard Philly has on the defensive side of the ball where the Eagles are expected to dictate terms, not react to them. On Sunday, led by Trotter with pop plays like this, they dominated a Steelers front that has had its way up front for a majority of the 2026 campaign.

I'm not sure Dowdle wants to see Trotter for a long time after this ballgame where No. 54 was consistently able to get the versatile ball-carrier to the turf all afternoon long.

There's good open-field tacklers, and then there's what we've seen from the young Eagles LB all season long. Seemingly, all Trotter needs is to get his hands on a single strand of clothing and guys fall to the turf. It doesn't matter their size or athletic profile, Trotter consistently invents ways to force ball-carriers to the ground – an elite trait at the position.

Making up for the missed tackle from Riq Woolen here, you can only imagine what type of chunk play this could have turned into had Trotter not been able to bring Dowdle to the ground. It's a nice display of his ability to understand ideal pursuit angles, because if Trotter takes the route to work behind Jihaad Campbell (11) who's engaged with Freiermuth (88), Dowdle potentially is able to accelerate and turn that corner before Marcus Epps (29) likely meets him up near the Eagles' 30-yard line, 15 yards from where Trotter ultimately is able to get the Steelers RB to the ground. This is an excellent example of not only covering for a teammate's missed opportunity, but the range and open-field ability to run number-to-number at his position.

This is called a 'spot drop' at the LB position. In zone coverage, you're asked to gain depth quickly where you're not too shallow where QBs can layers throw behind you (and in front of the safety), but also not too deep where it allows YAC (yards after catch) opportunities on designed screen plays underneath for quicker, shiftier athletes in space. It's a great play to rally to the football and to limit any yards after the catch against Freiermuth, but the one thing that stands out here has nothing to do with the play itself.

Watch Trotter's feet… they never stop. For young linebackers, oftentimes in zone coverage you'll see their feet come to a stop when they believe they've reached their necessary depth in coverage. That stops any momentum you have, and in the NFL where there are world class athletes scattered on every depth chart, that extra second it may take to get your lower half going again could be the difference. For Trotter, his feet pitter-pat on and off the turf like a well-oiled piston before quickly accelerating downhill towards the Steelers TE. Footwork is everything in the game of football, no matter the position, and it's the foundation for Trotter in what makes him so impactful in the heart of the Eagles' defense.
