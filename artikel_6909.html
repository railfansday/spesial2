The Low-Profile Sneaker Trends Defining Fall 2026

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Every item on this page was chosen by an ELLE editor. We may earn commission on some of the items you choose to buy.

Slim-sole mania is here to stay, but these looks are far from tired.

Of all the popular footwear trends in recent years, few have proved as enduring as the low-profile sneaker. And ever since iconic silhouettes such as the Adidas Samba and the Onitsuka Tiger Mexico 66 helped spark the craze, the category has seemed to evolve nearly every season. Lately, soles are slimmer than ever, with dancewear-inspired jazz flats and scrunchy, second-skin sneakers taking center stage. Bold accents like double laces and metallic finishes only add to the many ways these sleek kicks are being reimagined, making it easy to liven up even your simplest outfits.

To keep you up to date on the latest low-profile sneaker trends, we’ve gathered eight key looks below, pulled from recent runways and embraced by street style mavens and A-list celebrities alike. Read on for the full list, plus some of our favorite pairs for nailing each aesthetic.

Perhaps the slimmest interpretation of the low-profile phenomenon, jazz shoes are one of this year’s biggest breakout trends. As seen on fall 2026 runways from Celine, Jacquemus, and Tod’s, classic lace-ups offer a little extra support. Sneaker-like details such as rubbery soles and foam cushioning underfoot take the comfort level up a notch. And for something slightly sportier (and more substantial), try a cool split-sole pair like Adidas’s Taekwondo Mei Elite or Keds’s famous Champion Low in smooth leather.

Add instant pizazz to your closet this season with a pair of animal print sneakers. In keeping with an understated silhouette, try leaning into a streamlined or darker-hued print—whether snakeskin, leopard, deer, or tiger—that avoids veering too flashy.

Largely credited with popularizing the slim-soled, double-lace sneaker back in 2014, Miu Miu continues to release variations of the striking fan-favorite—including color-blocked designs on its latest spring/summer 2027 runway with yet another New Balance collaboration. And dozens of other brands are following suit with their own takes. Think not only contrasting laces stacked atop one another but also dual laces strung side by side and even a second lace layered across the entire shoe, à la brands like Vans and Puma.

A low-profile silhouette offers a decidedly elevated way to dip your toe into the metallic sneaker waters. For fall 2026, Cecilie Bahnsen’s silver-trimmed pairs felt especially balletic, teamed with cozy leg warmers, while even Shiatzy Chen’s bright red style seamlessly complemented sharp leather trousers and a coordinating fur-trimmed coat.

There’s something unmistakably cool about black sneakers, and they’re more of a staple than you might expect. They’ll add gravitas to looks that traditional white pairs can’t, while meshing perfectly with this season’s deeper palette. Appearing across autumn runways at Jil Sander, Patou, and Tod’s, simple noir lace-ups prove an equally sophisticated alternative to flats, easily styled with everything from baggy jeans to polished knitwear.

Though gorpcore has long been dominated by chunky, trail-inspired sneakers, some brands are making the case for slightly slimmer treatments—in a somewhat similar vein to those controversial Vibram FiveFingers adopted by the street-style crowd. On its fall/winter 2026 runway, Loewe debuted a swim-shoe hybrid, while Lacoste drew upon the idea of a tennis ball hitting a net to create a barely-there, caged sneaker for spring/summer 2027. For a nice switch-up from the usual lineup, try a snug slip-on or low-profile style with hearty overlays or a rubber toe bumper.

Vibrant yellow sneakers don’t belong solely in summer. Designers at Fendi, Loewe, Cecilie Bahnsen, and Onitsuka all debuted versions for the fall/winter season, providing a serotonin-boosting jolt of color against fall staples in muted tones.

Falling in line with the nostalgic return of scrunched heels and flats, slim, elasticized sneakers are all the rage right now. Prada jump-started the trend with its now-viral Collapse sneaker for spring/summer 2025, and the style returned in multiple iterations on both of its 2026 runways. Meanwhile, Nike’s Moon Shoe collaboration with Jacquemus continues to be a hit, as spotted on Rihanna earlier this year. Other satin favorites, like Miu Miu’s beloved Plume sneakers and Puma’s latest ruched-toe H-Street Etoile, offer an even daintier spin on the comfy aesthetic.
