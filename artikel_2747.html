Players Era Recap: What we learned, and questions for the future

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Through the first two weeks of the non-conference slate, there were many questions that surrounded this year's edition of Iowa State men's basketball. After dropping their season opener against Memphis, the Cyclones picked up three victories against admittedly weak competition with Southern Miss, Southern and Niagara.

Just by the scoreline, none of those wins look particularly alarming. But those who watched the games live would tell a different story. There were a noticeable amount of missed rotations, which everyone should expect with the amount of minutes being given to the various transfers. My biggest concern throughout was the subpar three point shooting. The Cyclones entered Las Vegas connecting on just 24% of their three point attempts. It doesn't take a rocket scientist to know that won't cut it, and would also likely improve due to the law of averages.

I would also not be the only person to express some individual concerns. Jamarion Batemon was ice cold from beyond the arc, going 5-27 in the first four games, including 0-7 through his first two games. After scoring 12 points against Memphis, Tamin Lipsey failed to score in double figures in the three home games. Blake Bucchanan looked much more sluggish throughout, which likely caused the switch to Dominykas Pleta into the starting lineup.

After a full week of preparation, would any of these questions be answered in Las Vegas?

While I would typically use the rose and thorn format for performances, we will change the format to pumpkin pie and cranberry sauce (I have no idea how some of you can eat this, fight me).

Their first bout against the Aztec was a solid enough outing against a squad that was picked to finish third in a new Pac-12 conference. The difference would ultimately be decided early with Iowa State notching a 10-0 early to take a 17-10 lead. That would be the only "kill shot" (any unanswered run of 10 or more points) for either team as the margin stayed between 6 and 10 for much of the afternoon.

The famous Otzelberger defense forced 18 turnovers, the second highest total of the season behind an absurd 30 for Southern. Even when the Aztecs were able to put up shots, they struggled through 38% from the field and 23% from distance.

Pumpkin pie: Tre "Ole reliable" Singleton

22 points, 5 rebounds, and 2 steals? Solid day at the office. That performance earns first serving at the team dinner.

The only stat that can largely ignore matchups and in-game situations, 77% from the line will certainly suffice.

Cranberry Sauce: Jaquan Johnson 3-pt shooting

I don't know what changed from last season, but Jaquan Johnson is ice cold from distance, and went a gaudy 0-6 from distance. Maybe just stick to driving to the hoop. The rest of the roster went 7-19 for 3-pt attempts, which is perfectly fine.

Don't commit those? Very insightful analysis I know, but there is no reason to give opponents extra free throws.

Pumpkin Pie and Cranberry Sauce vs. Tennessee

I'm fine with never playing Tennessee again. At least until all of our players are 6-8 or taller. There were times where the offense looked like the early Otzelberger years where the offense was Izaiah Brockington, Gabe Kalschure, or a prayer. The Cyclones only scored 19 points in the first half, the lowest first half total of the Otzelberger era. For those of you wondering, Iowa State still scored 20 first half points in that infamous 36-53 loss against Oklahoma State.

Just hot grease to the eyes. Iowa State had two separate 3+ minute stretches without scoring, and the size that Tennessee brought made it feel like they were shooting through tree branches on the way to the hoop. Just an ugly performance all around.

"Lose first half = bad, Win second half = good". Yes, but there is more to this. Everyone knew/hoped that the Iowa State shooting would improve, but they were able to create some more separation to take high percentage shots. The Cyclones cut the lead to five with just four minutes to go, but it was not meant to be. Alas, we at least know that this team can get out of a funk when they fall into one, and certainly gave themselves the opportunity to win late in the game.

Cranberry Sauce: 5-21 on layups, 10-18 on free throws

Gross. I would rather eat the full can of cranberry sauce than watch all 21 attempts again. 56% from the FT line is also quite subpar.

The Robert Morris transfer was the only player that appeared comfortable shooting the ball throughout the entire game. 17 points, 7 rebounds, and no turnovers was the only reason why the Cyclones were even competitive late in the game.

Pumpkin Pie and Cranberry Sauce vs St. John's.

It was a pretty safe bet that Iowa State would be able to take care of SDSU and then struggle against Tennessee. The third matchup would be the real litmus test for this Cyclones team that is currently missing their marque win. In a reversal of the previous game, Iowa State had a very solid first half that looked more dominant than the two point lead would suggest. Unfortunately, the second half was also a reversal from yesterday as the Cyclones went cold, allowing St. John's to run away with the game. It was also a sloppy affair that included 44 total turnovers.

Pumpkin Pie: A solo 7-0 for Leon Bond III

Leon Bond III entered the game with four total points scored this season. In the span of three minutes, Bond connected on three shots to provide a solo 7-0 that certainly awarded him additional minutes in the second half.

The first game where multiple players looked comfortable from deep, the Cyclones shot a fantastic 42% from distance.

For just the second time this season, the German big man recorded double digit points and was a tremendous force throughout the first half. He would slow down in the second, but his overall performance does give some optimism for the rest of the season.

It pains me to write this, but I don't recognize the current Tamin Lipsey that has been playing this season. Lipsey recorded three fouls in the first half and failed to score a bucket for the full contest. Thank you for your eight assists today, but scoring a few would also be appreciated. It feels like watching freshman Lipsey.

Cranberry Sauce: 6 minute scoring drought early in the second.

St. John's stormed back to take the lead with a 13-0 run that included the Cyclones shooting 0-9 and five turnovers. It was a bad time to have a bad stretch, and Iowa State would not regain the lead for the rest of the contest.

Pumpkin Pie: Tre Singleton in the second half

A 25 points outing should never go to waste, especially with 17 in the second half. When everyone else started to freeze, Tre Singleton kept the Cyclones alive in the second half, accounting for Iowa State's only nine points over a stretch that eclipsed 7 minutes.

That's too many, plain and simple. You can't force 21 turnovers and still lose the overall margin.

Overall thoughts: This season will certainly be uglier than the recent past.

The roster clearly has the potential to stay competitive with very good teams. But it has become abundantly clear that turnovers and long cold stretches will be the achilles heal for the season. Between the Tennessee and St. John's games, Iowa State played 40 total minutes that were unquestionably great basketball against very difficult opponents, and then a separate 40 minutes raise real concerns for if this team will be able to achieve its potential.

If you need one bit of assurance in these trying times, remember that these two losses were against top-15 teams, and they were able to beat a solid SDSU team relatively comfortably. But these were two opportunities to grab marquee non-conference wins, and they missed. I don't know if they have another opportunity to get that type of win before the conference season starts due to Purdue struggling thus far and nobody really knowing if Iowa is good.

Just fill yourself with leftover turkey and beer for the final (consecutive) farmageddon on Saturday, and come back to Ames on Tuesday for a reset game against Alcorn State. Maybe we will score 132 points again.
