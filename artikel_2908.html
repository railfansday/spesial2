Hendricken's Super Bowl win didn't happen by accident. Here's why.

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

After seeing a 14-point lead dissolve in under five minutes, it would have made sense if Hendricken's sideline turned into a war zone and with La Salle energized on the other side.

But if you haven't learned by now, that's not what the Hawks do.

No, what they do is what they did on Friday night, Nov. 20. After giving up what looked like a commanding lead, Hendricken put together a drive it might as well copyright at this point. It was a 12-play, 73-yard masterpiece, with Ryan Santo Jr. doing the honors on the go-ahead score in the 20-14 win over the rival Rams, giving the Hawks their fourth straight State Championship.

"I believed we were going to win," Hendricken's Elijah Guertin said. "I believe we're the team that's going to win and I have no doubt in my mind."

"We never expected to win," said Santo, who earned MVP honors after his two-touchdown performance. "They tell you at practices that this is going to be a battle. It can go the other way.

"You have to make your plays, stay disciplined … and everybody has to do their job."

When winning is in your blood, you know how to handle the big moments. That's why Hendricken wasn't worried about its season after gagging away a win against Chaminade in the opener, or taking a beating to Connecticut powerhouse Daniel Hand. Those losses were part of the journey, and the regular-season and playoff success that followed brought them to Bryant University confident, but hardly cocky, going up against La Salle.

Confidence was high after the Hawks opened the second half with a nine-play, 65-yard drive that ended with a Santo 1-yard run. Even with a quarter-and-a-half left, the 14-0 lead seemed impossible for La Salle to come back from.

Nobody bothered to tell the Rams. With Hendricken selling out in the secondary, La Salle used its numbers advantage and let Elijah Bento chew his way down the field before Sam Perry connected with Jabari Jackson on a 20-yard score.

The Rams followed with a stop, and on their ensuing drive, Perry skipped a pass to Brendan Cardoso that turned into a 76-yard touchdown. Perry then hit Daniel Lynch on the conversion pass, and with 9:30 left, the game was tied and La Salle's sideline was rocking louder than Beirne Stadium had ever seen.

"We believed in each other," Perry said. "We just trusted each other. I knew I had to play better than the first half and we were going to be fine."

While an eerie quiet swept over the bleachers, the sideline was relaxed.

"The first thing we said was we were good," Santo said. "In the end, they're going to make plays. It's football – they're a great team, they have so many athletes, big plays are going to happen and it just matters if we come back.

"We came to the sideline and said we're good, everything is going to work out."

Hendricken quickly worked upfield, but then stalled near midfield. Facing a third-and-nine, Isaiah Brito went deep to Javien Valle, who was covered by Jackson. There was contact, Jackson's arm extended and while the ball was uncatchable, it didn't matter – because an uncatchable ball is not part of NFHS rules on pass interference.

Four plays later, Hendricken had the lead. Santo went for 17 yards to get the ball into the red zone, Thomas Correia brought it to the seven and Santo did the rest, scoring from seven yards out.

"I didn't want it – they gave it to me," Santo said. "I would have been more than happy to hand it to somebody else and let them score.

"If I was blocking and they get in the end zone, it's a team victory."

Hendricken missed the extra point, and with 3:50 left, La Salle had plenty of time to put together one final drive.

But the best player in Rhode Island wasn't going to let that happen.

Guertin, a Penn State commit who will officially sign with the school next month, could have easily earned MVP honors for his performance on Friday night. He was a menace off the edge, coming up with early sacks and occupying offensive linemen that allowed his teammates to take advantage and make plays of their own.

In the second quarter, Guertin made a one-armed tackle of Bento on a 4th-and-1 run that stopped a La Salle drive on the plus side of the field. Now, in the fourth, the Rams were facing another 4th-and-1. They went to Bento and Guertin made the same play, stopping Bento in his tracks and securing the victory for the Hawks.

"One thing about this team is we never flinch," Guertin said. "A quote from our coach is from Creed 3, when you put your foot on the tire you keep swinging and don't take the foot out.

"That's what we did – we kept our foot on the tire and kept going."

"The backbone of every team is the defense," said Santo, who flew all over the field on the defensive side of the ball. "My dad always tells me offense gets the girls, but defense gets the trophy."

Last year La Salle was outclassed in its Super Bowl loss to Hendricken. This year the Rams showed up in a big way. The defense, led by Lucca Sisto on the edge and Bento at linebacker, answered any questions about its ability to handle the run in the first half. When things looked bad, La Salle's big players made big plays and turned a potential rout into a heck of a game.

"I'm just so proud of this team. We could have easily gone down, and it shows how together we are," Perry said. "I just love this team. It's tough the way it ended, but you can only control what you can control, and I'm proud of how we fought."

Hendricken didn't look like Hendricken at the start of the season, and with the entire state rooting for its demise, they worked to ensure that wouldn't happen. They stayed together when things were at their lowest, and that paid off by them being together when things were at their highest.

Friday could have been the end of the dynasty. The opportunity was certainly there.

But moments like that are what makes Hendricken Hendricken. The Hawks were prepared for ups, downs and everything in between, and when it came time to make plays, in front of the largest crowd anyone's seen at a high school game in well over two decades, they made them.

"It feels amazing. It's weird – I'm playing at this unbelievable stadium at Bryant, a Division I field, I felt like I was at Michigan vs. Ohio State," Santo said. "It was awesome."
