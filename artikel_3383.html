17 Greek Mythology Books For Adult Percy Jackson Fans

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Percy Jackson & The Olympians is one of the most popular Greek mythology book series since the turn of the century, which is saying something because our fascination with the old stories has never completely ceased. The stories continue to be reimagined, whether on the screen or in books, every day. However, there’s something unique and special about the Percy Jackson books. Luckily, it’s a great time to be a fan.

Rick Riordan continues to expand the Riordanverse, which includes Percy Jackson and other mythological series like the Kane Chronicles, all the time. Plus, the story is back on the TV screen. Disney+’s Percy Jackson season 3 has received overwhelmingly positive reviews, with special praise for its fight sequences and its darker tone.

What’s more, publishers have noticed Percy Jackson's popularity over the past two decades, and they’ve catered to fans of this series and general Greek mythology lovers. Plenty of books exist that are perfect for Percy Jackson fans who read them when they were younger and are now adults. These seventeen stand out for their spirit of adventure, their imagination, and their ability to capture Greek myths in new ways.

One of the most thrilling parts of the Percy Jackson books is that they put Greek mythology and characters into our world, rather than taking the reader back to their world. These modern books function on the same principle, imagining how the Greek gods and their children would fare if they existed nowadays. Lightning & Thunder, which I’ve had the chance to read ahead of its release, has a more optimistic view of that prospect, while Gods Behaving Badly has a darkly comedic view of the gods struggling. Either way, though, it's extremely entertaining.

Stone Blind by Natalie Haynes, Andromeda by E.S. McLeod, Clytemnestra by Costanza Casati, Circe by Madeline Miller, I, Medusa by Ayana Gray

Many of the best retellings of Greek myths center on overlooked, villainized characters, matching Percy Jackson’s core values. The series has always been about outsiders, and Rick Riordan amplifies voices of characters typically silenced. What’s more, he encourages empathy for characters like Luke, who could easily be dismissed as villains. These five are standout choices.

The Games Gods Play by Abigail Owen, Blood of Hercules by Jasmine Mas, ODY-C by Matt Fraction & Christian Ward, Arch-Conspirator by Veronica Roth

Some of the most thrilling examples of Greek mythology books place the gods and heroes in an entirely new fantasy or science fiction setting. It’s a fun thought experiment. How would these characters we know and love fare in a different world. This leads to entertaining high-stakes stories like The Games Gods Play and Blood of Hercules, where the characters are underdogs trying to survive. Meanwhile, ODY-C and Arch-Conspirator not only reimagines the gods but also the technology of the world. Luckily, they all share the same spirit of adventure as Percy Jackson, but with adult maturity.

Now is the perfect time to dive into The Games Gods Play and the rest of Abigail Owen's The Crucible series because the third book in the series, The Wrath Gods Reap, came out just this month, and it is the best book of the series. It's faster-paced, higher-stakes, and it finally gives readers an emotional payoff that's 3 books in the making.

A Thousand Ships by Natalie Haynes, The Song of Achilles by Madeline Miller, The End Crowns All by Bea Fitzgerald, Troy: Lord of the Silver Bow by David Gemmell

The Iliad and The Odyssey are probably the most popular Greek myths in existence, continually being brought to the screen or the page in new ways. Even Percy Jackson had its own take on the latter epic poem with Sea of Monsters. These four books offer new perspectives and fun adventures that will be perfect for folks who want more adventure and tension.
