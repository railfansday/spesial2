Is Micron Undervalued?

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

A record year and a 38% slide have left Micron trading at a price usually reserved for shrinking businesses.

By John Ballard – Nov 18, 2026 at 3:21PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Micron Technology ›

Micron Technology (MU -1.98%) just finished the best year in its history. Fiscal 2026 revenue reached $133 billion, and adjusted earnings hit $75.52 per share. Yet the stock closed at $778.31 on Friday, Nov. 13, about 38% below its June 25 high of $1,255, and trades at just 4.4 times next year's expected earnings.

That looks cheap, and management's own comments about demand and supply for memory chips suggest it is. But valuation multiples that low usually signal that investors expect a slump, and memory has burned shareholders before. Here's why the market is so skeptical and why Micron could still be worth more than double today's price.

At an Oct. 21 technology webinar, Micron President Scott DeBoer argued that memory is now the main bottleneck holding back the performance of artificial intelligence (AI) systems. He said the company doesn't "see a period of time where memory is not going to be in short supply in the next two years."

Part of the reason is high-bandwidth memory (HBM), the stacked chips that sit beside AI processors. HBM uses far more factory wafers than standard memory to deliver the same amount of storage, and that gap will continue to widen.

Micron is designing custom HBM with customers, and its next manufacturing generation, called 1-delta, should ramp in the second half of 2027. It plans to spend about $10 billion over the next decade on a long-term research lab to discover new growth opportunities.

Analysts expect Micron to earn $176.50 per share in fiscal 2027 and $203.48 in fiscal 2029. The stock is trading at just 3.8 times the 2029 estimate. Based on last year's earnings, the stock trades at about 10 times.

If the memory market continues to expand, those valuation multiples are too low. Bank of America sees global sales of DRAM, Micron's main memory product, at $573 billion this year and $917 billion in 2027, then still growing to nearly $1.4 trillion by 2030.

If Micron keeps its trailing price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of 10 and earns $176.50 per share in fiscal 2027, the stock would be worth about $1,760, or 2.3 times Friday's close.

Memory is a boom-and-bust business. Micron's annual report notes that over the past five years, yearly swings in its average selling prices have ranged from "an increase of approximately 200% to a decrease of approximately 50%." Micron expects to spend about $25 billion on new factories and equipment in just the first half of fiscal 2027. That funds growth, but Micron warns that as it and rivals add supply, prices may fall.

Micron is also concentrated. Over half of its fiscal 2026 revenue came from its top 10 customers, and about 60% came from data centers, so a pullback in AI spending would hit hard.

Still, a forward P/E of 4.4 already prices in a bust that management doesn't expect over the next two years. If Micron holds its trailing multiple and hits analysts' fiscal 2029 estimate, the stock would trade over $2,000, about 2.7 times Friday's close.

The risk remains, but the upside potential makes Micron worth holding in a diversified portfolio alongside other top AI stocks.
