One week after trade, Nique Clifford takes blame for falling out of Kings' favor

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

One week after trade, Nique Clifford takes blame for falling out of Kings' favor originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

SACRAMENTO – Nique Clifford's quick career turn went from being out of the rotation for one of the worst teams in the NBA to being needed for a team that considers itself a championship contender.

One week after the Kings traded Clifford to the Oklahoma City Thunder, he was back at Golden 1 Center on Sunday taking on his former team, helping deal the Kings their eighth straight home loss as they fell to 1-12 on the season.

"It's crazy how fast it just happens," Clifford said before tipoff. "It felt like I was here one day ago and then I was gone."

The Kings traded Clifford weeks into his second NBA season after going 24th overall in the 2025 NBA Draft. Sacramento initially acquired Clifford in a draft-night trade with the Thunder, sending a top-16 protected draft pick in 2027 they acquired from the San Antonio Spurs in the De'Aaron Fox trade in February 2025.

Last week's trade, which came exactly seven days before Clifford's Sacramento return, netted the Kings two future second-round picks. The move allowed Clifford to rejoin an NBA rotation after falling behind rookie Emanuel Sharp, Daeqwon Plowden and Leaky Black in Kings coach Doug Christie's pecking order.

The net results of the two trades involving Clifford: the Thunder received Clifford and the Spurs' 2027 first-round pick, while the Sacramento ended up with second-round picks in 2028 and 2030.

The Athletic reported the Kings moved on from Clifford because they feared his value would only go down while he fell out of the rotation, because Christie is coaching for his job rather than prioritizing Clifford's development.

For his part, Clifford on Sunday took the high road and blamed himself for falling out of the rotation.

"There's so many different things that I could go back to," Clifford said. "I just like to take accountability. I got to be better. I didn't play well enough. I fell out of the rotation, and part of that is definitely on me. I could have been better for the team, and the decisions they made were what they felt was best for the organization and for the team. And, you know, I respect that. So I wish them nothing but the best and I just want to continue moving forward with my career and getting better."

Before the trade, Clifford appeared in eight of 10 games for Sacramento, averaging 1.6 points in 6.3 minutes. In four games with the Thunder, he has played 20, 16, 12 and a season-high 23:51 on Sunday.

Clifford is still learning the ropes of the Thunder's system under coach Mark Daigneault, who needs depth in light of long-term injuries to pivotal players Chet Holmgren, Cason Wallace and Jalen Williams.

Sunday was Clifford's fourth game with his new team. He scored a season-high nine points with three rebounds while he works to establish a role. The Thunder snapped their three-game losing streak amid their spate of injuries while Clifford hopes to continue to grow in his new surroundings.

"Honestly, just focusing on all the little things," Clifford said. "Being a connector for the team. Doing all the little stuff, rebounding, defense, cutting, running the floor hard. The offensive stuff is going to come as I get more comfortable with the team and all those different things will come to fruition."

Clifford isn't expected to suddenly become an NBA All-Star with the Thunder. But Daigneault pointed to their ability to integrate players like Jared McCain, who went from averaging 6.6 points in a limited role with the Philadelphia 76ers to 10.4 with the Thunder after being acquired midway through last season.

"There's an assessment phase with every player," Daigneault said, "and obviously (Clifford's) got a body of work in the NBA, but he doesn't have a body of work with us, and so when we get a new player, we try not to rush the process of anticipating what they're going to be or what they're going to look like. We give them a period of time where they can kind of declare themselves, and especially when they're playing. And he's got that opportunity right now."

The Kings will look to avoid losing each game of their season-long seven-game home stand on Wednesday when they host the Phoenix Suns.
