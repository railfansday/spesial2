How the 49ers are uniquely equipped to beat the Vikings

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The Week 11 NFL slate leaves a lot to be desired. Two games on the schedule involve playoff teams. The big one is an international outing between the Minnesota Vikings and the San Francisco 49ers.

The Vikings saw their season circling the drain, so they went out and traded for a familiar face in Kirk Cousins. Nobody was happier with the trade than Justin Jefferson, who caught a season-high nine passes in Cousins' debut last week.

Missed opportunities defined Raheem Morris's stop unit last week, as he explained. The Niners defense will look to bounce back after a game in which they finished in the 6th percentile in points allowed per drive.

The other side of the ball features two of the very best at what they do. The mad scientist Brian Flores boasts one of the most impressive defenses we've seen through 10 weeks. Minnesota bottled up a high-powered Lions offense, kept the Bills in check, and turned Jordan Love and the Packers offense into a pumpkin last week.

The only team to slow down the 49ers offense this year is themselves. They've surpassed 30 points five times this season, including a season-high 46 points last week against the Cowboys. Big plays were the name of the game for San Francisco, as they were in the 99th percentile in EPA per play against Dallas.

The biggest edge in this game is on special teams. Minnesota ranks first in schedule-adjusted efficiency, while the Niners rank 29th. The Vikings are fifth or better in field goals, kickoff return, punt return, and are sixth in hidden points.

The 49ers are smack dab in the middle at 16th in hidden points, largely because of their punter. But it shouldn't surprise anyone to see the punt return unit rank second-to-last, or the field goal team come in at 28th. The third phase of the game is generally what decides these one-possession contests.

Let's dig deeper into each offense and defense to see how this game could play out.

Contrary to what you might have seen online leading up to this game, the 49ers defense and Vikings offense do exist. Flores and Shanahan are not the only play-callers in this game.

The Vikings scored 30 against the Packers, so the initial thought might be that Kirk Cousins was all that was needed to jumpstart an offense that has been dormant all year.

After going three-and-out and losing a yard to begin the game, the Vikings had a drive that started on Green Bay's 10-yard line. They lost six yards and had to settle for a field goal.

On the next drive, the Vikings had another short field after the Packers missed a field goal. Myles Price caught a 38-yard touchdown on 3rd and 23 on a play that should have never resulted in a completion.

After going three-and-out on their ensuing drive, Cousins threw an interception in the red zone, but his knee was down. On 3rd and 15, Green Bay was in press man coverage with no safety help, and gave up 24 yards. Minnesota scored on the next play.

Thirty points. One drive that was legit. Zooming out, the Vikings had an EPA per play in the 54th percentile, a success rate in the 52nd percentile, and a yards per play in the 28th percentile. Cousins was money on third downs, which flipped the variance in the Vikings' favor.

Functionally, the Vikings struggle to run the rock. Minnesota is running more gap and man concepts than ever under Kevin O'Connell, which may explain some of the struggles. Despite ranking fifth in the league in yards before contact, Minnesota is 25th in rushing success rate and 29th in EPA per rush.

They had a 12-yard run last week on a pitch to a wide receiver. Aaron Jones shook free from a 19-yarder. But he only averaged 4.3 yards per carry. Their rushing leader, old friend Jordan Mason, had 16 carries but could muster only 3.7 yards per carry.

It's easy to chalk up a subpar run game to poor quarterback play, but the Vikings are 27th in team rush yards over expectation (- 73) and 21st in 10+ yard rushes.

I've always appreciated Mason for being a tough, hard-nosed runner. What the Niners do well is exactly how you keep Mason under wraps. All the slanting and stunting the defensive line does will force Mason to redirect. That's what you want. If you let Mason pick up some steam, he'll pick up 5-7 yards, easily. But if you make him change directions, he turns into a mediocre back.

It's tough to use 5-down fronts against a wide-open offense like the Cowboys, who live three wide receiver sets. Minnesota uses 11 personnel nearly 40% less than Dallas. O'Connell uses two tight end sets 27.7% of the time, the 9th-highest rate in the league, and runs plays from under center at the sixth-highest clip.

Against 5-down fronts last week, the Vikings had 15 carries and averaged 3.8 yards. I'd expect Raheem Morris to deploy more 5-down fronts than usual, especially on early downs, to keep the Vikings in longer down-and-distance situations. It also means more Jaden Dugger, which is a good thing, who only played 11 snaps last week.

Something smells about the Vikings' run game. The 49ers have to win this facet of the game. The defense has allowed five rushes of 10+ yards this season, far and away the best in the league (second place has allowed 16).

They're second in rushing EPA on the season. Minnesota's offensive line is far from a bully ball unit. Their tight ends aren't effective in the run game, which could lead to multiple negatives to force obvious passing downs. They ask 180-pound receivers to block in the fit, and the results are as you'd imagine. This is the type of game where you notice Nick Bosa and Mykel Williams control the line of scrimmage.

Christmas came early for Justin Jefferson last week. He saw a season-high 13 targets. Kyler Murray acts like it's against the rules to throw the ball over the middle of the field, which is why Jefferson only has nine targets in the middle third this season. His 49 yards are tied for 146th in the NFL, per Next Gen Stats. That'll change with Cousins, who has the second-most yards this season over the middle of the field.

The Packers tried to bracket Jefferson at times last week, but to no avail. Credit Cousins for throwing the ball on time before the double team could get there. But the Packers had opportunities for turnovers on Jefferson targets. They dropped an interception.

You'll live with the underneath throws, like the curls and the screens—but you better make a tackle and limit the damage after the catch. What you have to take away are the deep over routes or any kind of crossing route. You're not going to shut down a focal point of the offense like Jefferson. However, you can eliminate throwing zones schematically and ensure he doesn't beat you over the top.

Fred Warner has erased the middle of the field this entire decade. Teams simply do not target the intermediate part of the field with No. 54 on the field. Dak Prescott had 51 attempts in Week 10. Six of those were beyond 10 yards over the middle. Warner lurking in that area should impact how the Vikings want to attack San Francisco.

If Price or Felton beat you, then so be it. There isn't a George Pickens at WR2 for Minnesota. The 49ers are much better equipped to slow down this unit than they were last week.

The most impressive part about Brian Flores is his ability to dictate what an offense does. He makes coordinators tuck their tails and get out of what they want to do offensively. He speeds up quarterbacks with the blitz, but limits their options because extra blockers have to stay in and protect the pass.

Flores' scheme takes immense pressure off his coverage unit, while creating 1-on-1s across the board for his pass rush. It's fascinating to watch.

Last week, the Packers had five eligible receivers run a route on 33.7% of their dropbacks. It felt like Jordan Love left a few plays on the field because he either didn't have, or wasn't aware of, where his outlet was when the pressure got to him.

If we reference the teams since the Vikings' bye, the Colts used five eligibles on 25.9 percent of their plays, the Lions were at 24.1%, while the Bills were at 47.2%.

The team that used the most eligibles at the highest clip scored the most against the Vikings, you say? Per Sumer Sports, Brock Purdy has 298 dropbacks this season. The 49ers have used five eligible receivers run a route on 239 of those dropbacks. That's good for a league-high 80.2% of the time.

Against Minnesota, you have to fight pressure with pressure. You're going to take your fair share of licks. But if you can withstand enough jabs and avoid the haymakers, you can land a few of your own.

One way to do that is by getting all of your eligibles in a route. For the 49ers, that's simply running their offense. Buffalo turned quick play-action and screen passes into a profit. Quick dump-offs to tight ends for eight-yard gains are big wins against this defense.

To me, if you're an EPA (big play) team, like the Packers, the Vikings are the worst possible matchup. Whereas a success-rate-driven offense, like the 49ers, can thrive against Minnesota. Love has been conditioned, by way of his offense, to hunt the big play. Brock Purdy is undoubtedly as aggressive, but there are far more built-in outs at every level in a Kyle Shanahan offense than there are in a Matt LaFleur offense.

Green Bay had several plays where Dallas Turner or Andrew Van Ginkel were in a bind in coverage, but they couldn't take advantage. Instead, Love was sacked because his eyes were downfield.

Those will show up this week with Christian McCaffrey 1-on-1 in the open field, or Keenan Allen settling into the vacated area of a blitzer, or George Kittle and Deebo Samuel keeping the offense ahead of the chains on a screen pass.

There's no need to inundate you with numbers about the Vikings offense. They're excellent. They're the best in the league in EPA per play, success rate, points per drive allowed, and third in schedule-adjusted efficiency. But for as much of a step up in class as this is for the Niners offense, the same is true for Flores and company.

Mike Evans is expected to play. They have not seen a No. 1 receiver in the same stratosphere as Evans. The Bills are an elite running team, but that is a choppy passing game short on weapons. The Lions are the closest passing attack to the 49ers, but one quarterback thrives under chaos, while the other has cement feet and can't get himself out of trouble.

Just as Dallas was uniquely equipped to harass the Niners' defense, I feel the same way this week, with this iteration of Shanahan's offense going up against Flores. Purdy knows the offense inside and out. We've seen him get to the third and fourth progressions, scramble for first downs when nothing is there, or manipulate space in the pocket to help buy time for his wideouts.

It felt like it had been a few weeks since we saw the 49ers run a screen. That changed last week. This season, the Vikings have faced 17 screen passes. They're allowing 8.59 yards per play with a 64.7% success rate. That'll be a key cog for the Niners this week, especially to help slow Minnesota's pass rush down.

There will be sacks. There might be turnovers. But it's not a game where Connor Colby should be overwhelmed. There is no Quinnen Williams for him to go against. Minnesota's defensive tackles are more of the stout variety, which are not the kinds that give Colby issues.

Dallas Turner exclusively lines up on the edge. If the ball is on the hash, that's where Turner will line up. So, there won't be any guessing games as to where Turner will be. You cannot say the same about who is coming. Purdy can expect as many as six players around the line of scrimmage and won't know who's blitzing until the ball is snapped.

The Vikings are the only team in the league with a double-digit sack percentage. They get pressure on nearly half of their dropbacks, and only one team generates quicker pressure than Minnesota. There are two sides to every coin, though. Brock Purdy has been pressured the third-least of any quarterback and, even after last week, is still the third-least sacked quarterback in the league.

Free-access throws outside the numbers are out there for the taking, and teams have shied away from them. Whether it's something the 49ers look to exploit early on in the game or in the second half, it's another area where this team, unlike the others Minnesota has faced, can lean on to stay ahead of the chains and into manageable third downs.

Good luck running the ball on this defense. Minnesota is sixth in EPA per rush, but is stingy on a down-to-down basis, ranking third in success rate.

It's difficult to generate any zone concepts, since the majority of defenders are at the line of scrimmage. It's all 1-on-1s, nobody can climb to the second level, and it's a jumbled mess at the line of scrimmage.

It'll be interesting to see if Shanahan uses more draw concepts. That's not something the Vikings have seen much. Counter plays haven't found much daylight. Since Week 7, Minnesota allows a 21.4% success rate and only 2.93 yards per carry.

There is one run concept where teams have found success against Flores's defense. That's running power. During that same stretch, it's the one play you can get over on the Vikings defense.

Skol fans found that tweet and chalked those runs up to being late in the game when the Vikings defense was tired. Try again. The Packers had an 11-yard gain in the first quarter running power last week. They did not run it again until the third quarter. The Bills had a pair of 7-yard gains in the second quarter. The Lions had an 8-yard gain in the first quarter.

Power has been the 49ers' go-to run in short-yardage and goal-line situations this season, which is why they lead the league with a 55.6% success rate. To me, you run the ball against the Vikings to stay out of third-and-oh no. That's when Flores gets you. Those downs are where your offenses go to die.

The Colts had 33 carries for 154 yards. The Lions ran for 230 yards. The Bills had 44 carries. Buffalo only averaged three yards per carry, but they stayed on the field.

Shanahan has to be willing to run the ball on third down. That's when you can gash the Vikings. While that's a possibility, the goal should be to wear them down, stay on the field, and make a unit that's closer to a sum of its parts than a star-studded one…play football.

The Niners have the upper hand at the skill positions. They're not going to score 40 points, but picking them to score fewer than 23 is something that we will do once we see it happen.

In a game between two potential NFC squads battling for playoff positioning, we'll take the team that resembles what a contender looks like. The Vikings will make it tough, but there will be some late separation as the Niners offense will be too much when it's all said and done.
