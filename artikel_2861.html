What we learned as lopsided first quarter dooms Warriors in blowout loss to Pels

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

What we learned as lopsided first quarter dooms Warriors in blowout loss to Pels originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

Life went from bad to worse for the Warriors on Monday night in The Bayou.

Getting blown out by the San Antonio Spurs without Steph Curry is understandable. But being torn to pieces by the New Orleans Pelicans is not.

The Warriors beat the Pelicans by 51 points the last time these two teams met one week ago. The Pelicans had lost six straight games, yet snapped that streak by beating the Warriors 115-92 for a 21-point beatdown at the Smoothie King Center.

Golden State trailed by as many as 31 points. Monday's game, like Friday night in San Antonio, was over within the first few minutes. The Warriors scored a season-low 11 points in the first quarter, putting them in a 24-point hole they never could dig out of.

Rookie Yaxel Lendeborg followed his stinker against the Spurs by scoring a game-high 28 points on 9-of-23 shooting. Brandin Podziemski scored 15 points on 3-of-8 shooting, but the rest of the starting five scored a total of nine points.

Here are three takeaways from the Warriors' third straight loss.

With two games to go on this five-game road trip, the Warriors are begging for a normal start in Dallas or Memphis. Monday's first quarter in New Orleans was about as bad as it gets after ugly starts in Houston and San Antonio.

They trailed 35-20 after the first last Wednesday night against the Rockets in a game where Curry left in the first few minutes from sustaining a right oblique abdominal tear. The Spurs then raced out to an 18-0 start in the Warriors' first game following Curry's injury, which turned into an eventual 38-point loss. The Rockets and Spurs compete at the top of the standings, and the Pelicans are parked at the bottom.

The Warriors missed their first 10 shots. Their first make was a De'Anthony Melton three from the right corner that made it a 23-5 game. The offense settled for threes and had three times as many turnovers (six) than shots made (two). They went 2 of 18 overall while the defense looked lost.

In Houston, the Warriors rallied back in the second quarter to make it a five-point game at halftime. They couldn't in San Antonio, and not even in New Orleans. The Warriors were a bad first quarter team with Curry, and they're a shell of themselves without him.

More lessons were had for Lendeborg. The rookie said he'd never forget his performance in the Warriors' blowout loss to the Spurs, but he played like someone still stuck in the past instead of focused on the present.

Steve Kerr let Lendeborg have it when he was forced to call his third timeout of the first quarter after two errant passes from the promising forward. Then at the end of the first half, Lendeborg had his jumper blocked from behind, slumped in response and Pelicans point guard Jeremiah Fears drew a foul with 0.5 seconds left.

Whatever was said to Lendeborg at halftime turned him into a different player. He entered a new level of aggressiveness, and left the quarter somehow up to 22 points after an eight-point first half. Lendeborg went 4 of 9 from the field, but also a perfect 4 of 4 from the free-throw line to help him get to a 14-point quarter.

Lendeborg started the fourth quarter and went right to the rack. Later in the quarter, he gave everyone the highlight of the night after throwing down a huge dunk on Derik Queen.

A slow start became Lendeborg's eighth 20-point game after going 7 of 14 from the field in the second half, and he made all four of his free throws. Lendeborg also had one more turnover (four) than assists (three).

Gravity, or the loss of it, is by the far the biggest adjustment the Warriors have to make without Curry. His presence alone changes the geometry of the court. So does his absence.

Yet the Warriors kept letting it fly from deep, counting their misses while the Pelicans' lead grew. The Warriors took 18 shots in the first quarter, and 13 of them were threes, to which they made one. They followed that lowly first quarter by going 2 of 11 on threes in the second quarter, bringing them to 3 of 24 (12.5 percent) in the first half.

LJ Cryer was given the starting nod and then missed his first seven threes. The Warriors shot 23.9 percent (11 of 46) beyond the arc. They shot six more threes than the Pelicans, and made four fewer than them.

One of their only advantages of the game was the free throw line. The Warriors scored their first two points of the night from there, and slowing the game down was their best offense. Five Warriors made multiple free throws, and only four made multiple threes.

The free-throw line has to be their friend, especially without Curry's nightly 30 points.
