NextEra Energy vs. Vistra: Which Utilities Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Florida's largest regulated utility meets a booming renewables business at NextEra, while Vistra bets on competitive wholesale markets and data center demand.

Rising power demand from data centers and regional electrification is fundamentally reshaping the utility landscape. Deciding between NextEra Energy (NEE -0.69%) and Vistra Holdings Inc. (VST -2.02%) depends on your preference for regulated stability or competitive market upside.

NextEra Energy dominates the renewables space while operating a massive regulated utility in Florida. Vistra operates as an integrated retail and power generation company, navigating the more volatile but potentially lucrative competitive wholesale markets. Both companies are positioned to benefit from shifting energy needs, yet they offer distinct profiles for investors.

NextEra Energy operates primarily through Florida Power & Light (FPL) and NextEra Energy Resources. Florida Power & Light is a regulated utility serving over six million customer accounts in Florida. In the broader market for electric utility stocks, the company stands out for its massive investments in renewable energy and battery storage.

In FY 2025, revenue reached about $27.5 billion, which was an 11% increase over the previous year. The company reported net income of approximately $6.8 billion for the period. This resulted in a net margin of close to 25%, a decrease from the 28% net margin reported in the prior fiscal year.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was roughly 1.8x. This ratio measures total debt, including short- and long-term obligations, against shareholder equity to assess financial leverage. The current ratio, which evaluates the ability to pay short-term debts, was approximately 0.6x, and the company generated about $3.2 billion in free cash flow during the year.

Vistra is an integrated retail electricity and power generation company serving roughly 5 million customers across 16 states. It operates in competitive wholesale markets, selling energy and capacity from its diverse fleet of power plants. The company has recently focused on securing long-term offtake agreements to supply power to large facilities like data centers.

For FY 2025, the company reported revenue of about $17.7 billion, representing growth of approximately 3% compared to the prior year. Net income for the fiscal year was around $944 million. The company achieved a net margin of roughly 5.3% during this period.

Based on the December 2025 balance sheet, Vistra carries a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 4.0x. This indicates a higher level of total debt relative to shareholder equity. The current ratio reached nearly 0.8x, while free cash flow for the fiscal year was approximately $129.0 million.

NextEra Energy faces significant regulatory and legal risks, particularly regarding its ability to recover costs through state-approved mechanisms. The company also deals with operational uncertainties at its nuclear generation facilities and potential climate impacts from severe hurricanes. Furthermore, project development remains sensitive to equipment supply chains and rising construction costs.

Vistra is exposed to wholesale power price volatility and commodity price fluctuations, which can affect its generation revenue. The ownership of nuclear facilities introduces specific operational and financial risks, including potential changes in environmental regulations. The company also competes in competitive markets against large peers like Dominion Energy (D -0.47%).

Vistra appears to be the more value-oriented choice for investors as it trades at a lower multiple of both its sales and its future earnings estimates.

The P/S ratio measures a company's market capitalization relative to its sales over the past twelve months. The Forward P/E ratio compares a company's share price to its future earnings estimates.

Vistra has built itself into a sizable provider of nuclear and other power generation through aggressive acquisitions in recent years. The company has inked deals with Meta Platforms (META -0.53%) and Microsoft Corp. (MSFT -2.46%) that account for 55% of its nuclear energy capacity, making future revenue much more predictable. The outlook for the other 45% is considered to have improved, as Wall Street expects clean energy to again become a priority at the federal level, with the Democratic Party controlling both houses of Congress. That promise for Vistra could be countered, however, if a ban on new data center construction is implemented in Texas, where the company would otherwise benefit from AI-driven construction.

Revenue for fiscal 2026 should rise about 24% to $22 billion, with net income more than quadrupling to $3.1 billion. Growth should moderate in 2027, but still deliver high-single-digit percentage expansion.

NextEra Energy is a regulated utility and one of the world's largest operators of renewable energy assets, with about one-third of its 4 gigawatts (GW) of generation capacity coming from wind and solar. As energy demand continues to soar, NextEra is well-suited to meet it. The typical FPL residential bill remains approximately 30% below the national average and is projected to increase by only 2% annually on average through the end of the decade.

Energy storage is also a growth area for the company. Battery storage represented 3.7 GW of additions to its backlog in the third quarter of fiscal 2026. NextEra can build battery storage as a stand-alone project or co-locate storage across the company's existing renewable sites. NextEra can also develop batteries as grid solutions, and can expand 4-hour batteries to 8 hours at its existing storage sites to accommodate customer growth.

All of that is helping the business increase sales to nearly $31 billion in fiscal 2026, according to Wall Street analyst consensus. Net income should rise more than 20% to close to $8.5 billion. Free cash flow remains a problem, with FCF projected at negative $19 billion, and that deficit is expected to jump to a gut-wrenching $79 billion in 2027, while sales and profit will increase in the high single digits.

NextEra's position in the still-growing Florida market is appealing to long-term investors, but Vistra's nuclear fleet and its much more reasonable valuation at a 15.3x forward P/E ratio make it the better option for investors seeking to fill out their portfolios for 2027.
