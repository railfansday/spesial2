Forget 'Peaky Blinders,' Titus Welliver's 8-Episode Crime Thriller Is Perfect From Start to Finish

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Jeffrey is a freelance features writer at Collider. He is an MPA-accredited entertainment journalist and a Tomatometer-approved critic based in the LA area. He graduated from the University of Texas at Austin with a Bachelor of Science in Radio, TV, & Film and a Bachelor of Arts in Theater.

If you're in the mood for a deliciously fun drama and a fan of shows like Peaky Blinders, it's time to check out The Westies. Set in the early 1980s, the MGM+ series presents a fictionalized account of the real-life Irish crime syndicate that operated out of Hell’s Kitchen. A combination of slick storytelling with crime drama thrills, along with a terrific cast of compelling characters, makes this show a must-watch experience.

The Westies mixes fact and fiction, with the series' main characters drawn from members of the real crime syndicate. Although the Italian mafia outnumbers the Westies five-to-one, the construction of the Javits Center in the latter's territory stands to give them a lucrative payout. J.K. Simmons stars as Eamon Sweeney, inspired by real Westies mob boss, Mickey Spillane. One of Sweeney's top lieutenants is Jimmy Roarke (Tom Brittney), based on real-life mobster James Coonan, whose loyalty to both the gang and its leader appears to be reaching its limits in the first season.

What did it take to bring the infamous Westies to television? J.K. Simmons, Tom Brittney, Titus Welliver, and co-creator/showrunner Chris Brancato pull back the curtain on how The Westies recreated 1980s Hell's Kitchen while balancing real history with dramatic storytelling.

When The Westies begins, Sweeney is attempting to navigate the finer points of the Javits construction deal while maintaining an uneasy peace with the Gambino crime family, led by Paul Castellano (Ron Lea) and underboss John Gotti (Hamish Allan-Headly) — and the series nicely juxtaposes these real-life figures against the story's more fictionalized interpretations. Although The Westies isn't aiming for a straightforward history lesson, with some elements of the show changed for storytelling purposes, series co-creators Chris Brancato and Michael Panes have crafted an immersive crime drama that exceptionally transports the audience back to the early 1980s.

Alongside the crime thriller's many twists and turns, The Westies boasts an incredible ensemble cast. The show has to juggle characters, factions, and subplots, but each still receives proper room to breathe. The actors wonderfully bring their characters to life, inhabiting a brutal world where one wrong move can bring ruin, and you are never sure who to trust. In addition to the intriguing chess game between Sweeney and Jimmy, another riveting subplot follows Jimmy's girlfriend, Bridget Walsh (Sarah Bolger), a former IRA soldier who now resides in the U.S. and runs into trouble when her former IRA contact and lover, Brendan Cahill (Allen Leech), arrives in New York City, seeking assistance with the IRA's ongoing activities.

Titus Welliver of Bosch fame is another standout within the cast as Glenn Keenan, a morally ambiguous NYPD officer who has a long-standing relationship with Sweeney. When FBI agent Birdie Polk (Jessica Frances Duke) recruits him to join her task force with the intention of bringing down organized crime, Keenan is torn between his ties to Sweeney and his love for his son, who is becoming involved with the Westies. Instead of choosing one, Keenan plays both sides of the law, and the actor portrays his character’s moral complexity exceptionally well. It's a different side of his acting strengths, and fans can watch him play someone totally different from his arguably most iconic role as Detective Harry Bosch.

The Westies hasn't yet been renewed for a second season, so its future is still up in the air. The first season ended on a shocking note with huge story implications, and it would be a shame if the finale served as the end of the road for the series. Brancato spoke optimistically about the show's fate in a July interview with Deadline, stating, 'We're very confident there will be a second season of The Westies."

The titular gang's rise to power has only just begun, and based on the first season's ending, there are many more great storylines to come. Nevertheless, The Westies provides a throwback crime drama experience reminiscent of movies like Goodfellas and Casino, albeit in a nostalgic 1980s setting.
