Netflix's 6-Part Dystopian Series Is A Masterclass In Updating Classic Sci-Fi

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

While one of Netflix’s best dystopian sci-fi shows had a long and tricky road to the small screen, the investment was worth it in the end thanks to the show’s triumphant debut season. Sci-fi TV is currently enjoying a golden era in the 2020s. While the ‘90s had classics like The X-Files and Star Trek: The Next Generation, the advent of streaming services has opened up a whole new world of small-screen sci-fi storytelling.

On Apple TV alone, there is Invasion, Monarch: Legacy of Monsters, Foundation, Dark Matter, Severance, Murderbot, and the Colin Farrell vehicle Sugar, a high profile hit that doesn’t even reveal its sci-fi elements until an audacious plot twist midway through season 1. On Netflix, Stranger Things, Black Mirror, and 3 Body Problem have all expanded the possibilities of small-screen sci-fi’s scope and scale, while Hulu’s Margaret Atwood adaptation The Handmaid’s Tale managed to expand on the iconic novel's story without losing the show’s critical acclaim.

Not one to be outdone, Netflix offered viewers a dystopian adaptation of its own in early 2025, and this six-season season had a historically long and rocky road to the screen. Based on the classic 1957 comic book of the same name, The Eternaut is an Argentinian sci-fi show that follows a group of survivors navigating a sudden, catastrophic alien invasion of their home city of Buenos Aires.

Penned by Héctor Germán Oesterheld (HGO) and Francisco Solano López, the comic series El Eternauta was first published between 1957 and 1959. The comic follows a group of survivors in the aftermath of a deadly alien invasion, and the comic was remade in 1969 with a revision that made its political messaging more explicit. After HGO was disappeared by Argentina’s fascist government, the rights to his most influential work went to his widow.

There was a failed attempt to mount an animated adaptation of El Eternauta during HGO’s life, but only an expensive pilot of this proposed adaptation was ever produced. Despite plentiful interest in a potential movie adaptation, complex copyright issues and the author’s uncertainty about translating the story’s political allegories to an English-speaking, non-Argentinian context stalled any potential developments.

In the years that followed, the dystopian masterpiece The Eternaut was repeatedly almost adapted, only for various practicalities to stall its production. In 1995, a live-action miniseries was almost funded before it was abandoned, while, in 2007, The Headless Woman director Lucrecia Martel was hired to write a screenplay, only for producers to leave the project in limbo after over a year of development.

Finally, As Luck Would Have It director Álex de la Iglesia began working on an adaptation of The Eternaut in 2018 and, although this wasn’t the version viewers received from Netflix, its development kick-started the show that was finally released in 2025. Created by Pizza, biro, faso director Bruno Stagnaro, Netflix’s The Eternaut maintained de la Iglesia’s choice of leading man, Ricardo Darín, in role of Juan Salvo.

Perhaps emboldened by the critical success of HBO’s dystopian shows Station Eleven and The Last of Us, Netflix released The Eternaut to widespread critical acclaim in 2025. Although season 1 did depart from its source material with the addition of Ariel Staltari’s new character Omar, The Eternaut was still widely celebrated as a faithful and intelligent, but also enthralling and exciting adaptation of the seminal comic.

Now that HGO’s iconic comic has finally found its way to the screen, viewers of season 1 will be delighted to hear that the show will only go from strength to strength in its sophomore outing. Netflix renewed The Eternaut for a second season in May 2025, and the source material offers more answers to its many mysteries as its story continues.
