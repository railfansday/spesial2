Vestas Wind Systems stock drops after profit and revenue miss expectations

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Vestas narrowed its full-year revenue range to 20.5 billion-21.5 billion euros and its margin range to 7.5% to 8.5%

Vestas Wind Systems narrowed its full-year guidance on Wednesday after third-quarter operating profit and revenue came in below expectations. Vestas stock fell 7.7% in early European trading.

The Danish turbine maker posted 483 million euros in earnings before interest, tax and special items, while sales totaled 5.68 billion euros. A company-compiled consensus had called for 526 million euros in earnings and 6 billion euros in revenue, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Vestas now expects full-year revenue of 20.5 billion to 21.5 billion euros, narrowed from a range of 20 billion to 22 billion euros. It sees an EBIT margin before special items of 7.5% to 8.5%, from a prior range of 7% to 9%. The company also lowered its expected investments for the year to around 1 billion euros from around 1.2 billion euros. The company said it is narrowing the outlook around the previous midpoint.

The stock dropped by up to 9.4% at its session low, its steepest decline since Feb. 5, according to Bloomberg. Vestas said expenses rose at the He Dreiht project, located in German waters of the North Sea, after a turbine blade broke. Chief Financial Officer Jakob Wegge Larsen said the hit would not extend beyond a single quarter.

The third-quarter EBIT margin, excluding special items, climbed to 8.5%, up from 7.8% a year earlier. Group order intake totaled 4.1 billion euros for the quarter, a decline from 4.6 billion euros a year before.

Revenue in the main wind turbine business rose 4.9%, as more offshore turbines were delivered and each megawatt fetched a higher price, while fewer onshore deliveries dampened the gain. The average price at which its turbines sold climbed to 1.22 million euros per megawatt, compared with 1.01 million euros previously. Service revenue grew 13%, and the backlog of service orders stood at 42 billion euros in anticipated future revenue under contract.

"Vestas has delivered a weak result," Janne Vincent Kjær, an analyst at Jyske Bank, wrote in a note, according to Bloomberg.

Vestas raised its profit forecast in August. CEO Henrik Andersen told Bloomberg Television he was taken aback by the share-price drop and believes the company is progressing well.
