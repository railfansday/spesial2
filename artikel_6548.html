Dirty Martini Gnocchi

Tanggal: 2026-11-10

When my fellow food editor Brooke Caison tried this recipe, she said, “This gnocchi skillet makes me want to drink.” So why can’t you do both? A dirty martini happens to be the perfect template to turn into a savory recipe, as we’ve riffed in many other ways: dirty martini dip, dirty martini deviled eggs, dirty martini pasta… shall I go on? This dirty martini gnocchi is my latest attempt to transform this iconic drink into dinner, and I have to admit—it’s a success.

The dirty martini:To make this recipe, you only need the vermouth you’d actually put in a dirty martini, Castelvetrano olives in brine (that’s the dirty part of the recipe), pimento peppers, garlic, lemon, Parmesan, and, of course, the gnocchi. You may also be asking: Why not use pimento-stuffed olives instead of having to buy two different ingredients? Well, Castelvetrano olives are superior in texture and flavor, so it was a no-brainer to include them. But this pasta truly wouldn’t be complete without some pimento peppers. Trust me—the extra step makes a lot of difference.

The gnocchi:Personally, I like using Colavita or DeCecco’s store-bought gnocchi. Once you cook some garlic, add the vermouth and broth, then cook the gnocchi until it swells up and gets a little tender. You don’t want to overcook it, which is why I recommend adjusting the amount of broth as you go. If you’re wondering when your sauce is done, check by seeing if you can coat the back of a spoon with it.

Storage:If you don’t end up eating the entire skillet as I would, then you can store the leftovers in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 3 days.
