10 Game Boy Advance Metroidvanias That Respect Your Time

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Josh Speer has been writing about games for more than a decade, and has been a fan his entire life, since getting his NES.

The Game Boy Advance marked the beginning of the Metroidvania genre as a respected and more commonplace one. Not only were there many great games that were released on the handheld, but developers took some real chances to expand it in unusual ways.

Partly because of the genre, and partly because of being on a handheld, all the Metroidvanias on this list are ones that do a good job of respecting your time. That means they're relatively short burns and that they're pretty streamlined.

Metroidvanias can take a long time to get through, but these ones deliver all the punch of a long game in a package that fits your life.

There's a wide variety of games here too, some well known titles, others more eclectic. No matter what, though, they're all great and fun little Metroidvanias which do a good job of respecting us gamers. So join me as we return to a wonderful handheld that I truly adored.

Back in its heyday, Cartoon Network had a lot of amazing shows, but one of the best was Samurai Jack. And even though I don't typically enjoy adaptations of those programs to videogames, they did a surprisingly good job with Samurai Jack: The Amulet of Time.

Not only did it feature everyone's favorite samurai, but it also has him hopping through time to procure fragments of elemental gems. Eventually they'll allow Jack to return to his own time period, but in the short term, they unlock new powers, such as a battle hammer and super jump.

While Samurai Jack: The Amulet of Time features great visual style and is a fun time, it's also more than a bit derivative of better examples of the genre. Still, for fans of Samurai Jack, this is an excellent adventure.

As a big fan of Spooky month, I adore The Nightmare Before Christmas. But as iconic as the movie is, many of the video game adaptations have been less than stellar. Probably the best example was on the Game Boy Advance in an adventure called Tim Burton's The Nightmare Before Christmas: The Pumpkin King.

The game serves as a prequel to the movie, and showcases how Jack Skellington became the Pumpkin King. There's all sorts of crazy, including a bug infestation, as well as the the schemes of Oogie Boogie to become top dog.

As for how it plays, it's more of a platformer than a full throated Metroidvania, but it's just enough of an example to include it here. Jack gets new powers and upgrades as it proceeds, there's challenging boss battles and the like. Combined with a really vibrant visual style, this is one handheld Metroidvania that's worth your time.

Everyone looks to The Prince of Persia: The Lost Crown for when the series finally embraced Metroidvanias. But it actually happened much, much earlier on the GBA in Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time. And while the game had multiple console releases, the handheld one did the best job of embracing the genre years before The Lost Crown.

The GBA game has you running around the palace of Azad in 2D, fighting baddies and saving citizens. You'll get new powers, rewind time itself, and get powerful upgrades for helping out the people of Persia.

Most interesting, you'll also switch between the prince and another playable character named Farah. Which isn't that common in the genre, but it makes sense thematically and helps this relatively basic Metroidvania stand apart.

When most of us think about Konami and Metroidvanias, we naturally think about Castlevania. But they actually tried their hand with other series, including Shaman King. The massively popular manga turned out to be a pretty fun little Metroidvania in Shaman King: Master of Spirits.

What's wild about it is that you can equip a variety of different spirits, both with passive and active abilities. Meaning that it plays out a lot like Castlevania: Aria of Sorrow and Dawn of Sorrow, as well as Order of Ecclesia.

You'll get spirits by doing all sorts of things, and can even equip different decks of them that you can swap between on the fly. While it's fun and visually attractive, this is also a pretty basic Metroidvania which you can beat in 10 hours or less.

Unlike the other games on this list, Shantae Advance: Risky Revolution has one unique characteristic – it came out in 2025 for GBA as well as other consoles. WayForward had always wanted to make this adventure, and finally found an excuse when Limited Run agreed to partner with them for physical releases.

The game takes place between the original game and Risky's Revenge, meaning that our favorite genie girl can still transform into a variety of animal forms. Those include classics like monkey and elephant, as well as stranger ones like spider and harpy. Each of them provides new ways to explore, as well as unique ways to attack.

The biggest throwback aspect of this adventure is using Tremor Engines to shuffle around the position of the background, allowing Shantae to find new paths forward. Throw in a short run time, fun multiplayer mode, tons of humor and some challenging boss battles, and this is one amazing Metroidvania adventure.

Fans of the PS2 console probably need to check out this list of poorly known Metroidvanias worth playing on the system.

Sometimes I think about what could have been, and one off games that should have been the beginning of a legacy. One of those games was Scurge: Hive. It was released on both GBA and Nintendo DS, but I played it on the older system, and was utterly bewitched by it.

Jenosa is afflicted by the parasitic Scurge, and has to not only face the horrible mutations it wreaks on planet Inos, but on her as well. You're constantly racing against the clock, and eventually even have to face the infection inside you. Along the way, you'll get upgrades and fun new mechanics like a mechanical grapple.

While most Metroidvanias play better in 2D, I found the isometric style worked very well in Scurge: Hive. Mix that with elemental weapons and a paper/rock/scissors system, and it's very addictive. It's also quite short, as you can beat the game in a little more than 10 hours.

I'll acknowledge that Drill Dozer is a hard game to narrow down stylistically. It features lots of platforming, intense boss battles, unique upgrades and a very powerful drill mech. But given that it's also one of the best games on the GBA, I felt worth highlighting it here, even if it's not a traditional Metroidvania.

You'll use the shoulder buttons to control the direction that you drill, and it can do more than just burst forward. Jill can use her Dozer to unscrew bolts, destroying blocks and fighting enemies. You'll even get upgrades that let it traverse watery sections and fly through the air.

It's pretty rare for any game to stay with me for more than a decade or so, which is part of why I had to include Drill Dozer on this list. I haven't forgotten it since I played it more than two decades ago, and I still fervently hope that somehow this amazing adventure gets a modern follow up.

Inti Creates wasn't on my radar until I played the first Mega Man Zero. A wholly original, super challenging take on the series that bridged the gap between the X and Legends series. And while this is still a platformer, it also features a mission-based system and the ability to level up your gear.

Better yet, beating some bosses provides elemental chips that allows Zero customize his attacks. Then there's Cyber Elves, which are hard to find but vital upgrades that are also single use. Finding them can drastically change how you play the game, meaning that purists would go out of their way not to overuse them.

One thing that also helped distinguish Mega Man Zero from earlier entries was how brutally hard it was, especially the boss battles. The series did start to tone things down in latter entries, but not enough you could call any of the series easy. A really remarkably different Metroidvania.

Hot on the heels of Castlevania: Symphony of the Night, we got Circle of the Moon. A game that was more of a classic example of the series than a pure Metroidvania. It was also incredibly dark and hard to see. Which is why it was a brilliant move on Koji Igarashi's part to make the next game, Harmony of Dissonance, a bright, bold festival of color.

Even though it's a GBA game, Castlevania: Harmony of Dissonance is a beautiful game that still holds up today. It features Juste Belmont, who is capable of powerful magical attacks by fusing sub weapons with magical books. And while that's a lot of fun, it also makes this one of the easiest of the Igavanias.

Still, this is a very fun Metroidvania that features some surprising boss battles and a fascinating premise. But if you know what you're doing, it's also one you can easily beat in 8 hours or less, at least once you figure out the convoluted maps.

After Super Metroid's success, Nintendo could have played it safe. Instead, they gave us one of the best games in the series on the GBA in Metroid Fusion. Sure, it's linear and thus pretty short. But it also was the first game in the series to really embrace a coherent narrative. It also injected a bit of survival horror into the series, a trend that was later embraced in Metroid Dread.

The X-Parasites brought a lot of terror into the adventure, and totally changed how Samus fought back. So much so that she had to get a brand new suit, which makes her stronger but also more susceptible to cold. Like other games in the series, beating bosses will provide powerful new upgrades, which you'll need to deal with the deadly doppelganger called SA-X.

This isn't just a great Metroidvania you can beat in less than 10 hours. It's an amazing handheld adventure that's just as fun today as before, and it efficiently laid the groundwork for the series to evolve in exciting ways, including the upcoming Metroid Ravenous.

If you're looking for unexplored gems in video games, I recommend these ten Metroidvanias that are unexpected masterpieces.
