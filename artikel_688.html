Why Does The Mars Volta Think a Live Album Recorded in Michigan & Oklahoma Will Connect With Fans in Mexico?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Omar Rodríguez-López explains how fan-selected live album LUCRO SUCIO; UNFINISHED BUSINESS reflects band's deep connection to Mexico.

The Mars Volta released a new official live album on Friday (Nov. 20), its first since Scabdates (2005). To choose the songs for LUCRO SUCIO; UNFINISHED BUSINESS, the U.S.-Puerto Rican band invited fans to vote through recordings made available on a website. The album’s 13 songs, which include live versions of tracks from the band’s 2025 studio LP Lucro sucio: Los ojos del vacío, were recorded last year during its U.S. tour. The shows took place in places like Michigan and Oklahoma — sites that represent the very essence of the country — as well as cities with significant Latino populations, such as Miami and Los Angeles.

“In almost 30 years of touring, we’d never felt a tour that was so special, so in sync,” Rodríguez-López says over the phone as he prepares for the band’s return to Mexico, where The Mars Volta will play two shows next week. “At first, the idea was to take my favorite nights and make a sort of Frankenstein, but that led me to [come up with] a campaign that involved the fans and create a site where they could vote for the tracks.”

Mexico will be the first place where the band — also featuring Mexican-born vocalist Cedric Bixler-Zavala, and Marcel Rodríguez-López, Eva Gardner, Linda-Philomène Tsoungui, Teri Gender Bender and Leo Genovese — presents LUCRO SUCIO; UNFINISHED BUSINESS. The choice is no coincidence, given Rodríguez-López’s deep connection to the Latin American country.

That helps explain how a live album recorded in U.S. cities can connect so deeply with The Mars Volta’s Mexican fans. In 2003, the band gave two of its most celebrated performances at Circo Volador in Mexico City; they were included in the 2023 documentary Omar and Cedric: If This Ever Gets Weird.

“It’s difficult to communicate the feeling or the connection I have with Mexico, because it’s an extremely unique relationship,” the musician says.

“Obviously, I’m Boricua, but I feel like my blood is in Mexico — I don’t know how to say it. My roots, my heart are in Borinquen [the Taíno name for Puerto Rico], in Naranjito, but my blood has everything to do with Mexico.”

Rodríguez-López, 51, spent part of his childhood in Puebla, Mexico with his family, before moving to El Paso, Texas — a border city with a large Mexican population — where he founded the post-hardcore band At the Drive-In in 1994.

“I was raised in Mexico, then we moved to El Paso, which is practically Mexico, where Spanish is spoken,” he adds. “As an adult, I lived in Guadalajara… I’ve always said, I feel adopted by Mexicans.”

Mexican culture has also found its way into The Mars Volta’s songs. One example is “Mictlán,” whose name means “place of the dead” in the language of the ancient Mexicans and is part of the Aztec worldview. The track first appeared on Lucro sucio: Los ojos del vacío and was later revisited in a live version for the band’s new album.

LUCRO SUCIO; UNFINISHED BUSINESS brings together the powerful progressive-rock guitars that define Rodríguez-López’s sound and his unmistakable Latin jazz influence. It also features songs such as “The Iron Rose,” “Mito de los trece cielos,” “Morgana” and “Cue the Sun.”

“My opinion is that people will find an album that represents the experience of seeing the band today,” the guitarist concludes. “In a very genuine and honest way, it’s conveying the experience to the fans, made by the fans themselves. It’s the closest thing to seeing the band live. It will never be the same, but it’s a document, and I’m super pleased and happy with the result.”

The Mars Volta will perform Nov. 28 at the Tecate Comuna festival in the Mexican state of Puebla, followed by a Nov. 29 show at Pepsi Center in Mexico City.
