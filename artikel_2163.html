Scott Parker: The real reason for rapid West Ham appointment revealed by source

West Ham are convinced Scott Parker is the right man to spearhead a return to the Premier League, sources have told Football Insider.

The Hammers opted to part ways with Nuno Espirito Santo earlier this week after a frustrating start to the season which had seen the fans calling for a change.

Their next manager was announced less than 24 hours later, after he emerged as the clear front-runner, and West Ham moved quickly to reach a full agreement.

As I revealed on the latest edition of Football Insider's Transfer Insider podcast, Parker's previous record in the Championship made him the ideal candidate at the London Stadium.

The 46-year-old has previously led Fulham, Bournemouth and Burnley to promotion, and there is optimism that he will do the same with the Hammers come next summer.

Parker is also already well-liked at West Ham after his time there as a player, and will take charge of his first game in the dugout against Blackburn Rovers tomorrow.

Former West Ham scout Mick Brown told Football Insider last month that the club had been clear with Nuno that they expect to finish in the automatic promotion places.

Having slipped down the table after a poor run of form, they are now hopeful that Parker is the right man to help them reach their goals between now and May.

The boss' previous Championship experience was key to the decision, and there is now optimism that he will be the right man to pick up where Nuno previously struggled.

Speaking on the Transfer Insider podcast, I explained: "Well, he's got all the ingredients and the experience to be a success at West Ham.

"He's got promotion on a CV from the Championship to the Premier League. He previously led Fulham, Bournemouth and Burnley all to the Premier League as well.

"And as I said, that's the goal for West Ham right now, to win immediate promotion back to the Premier League.

"Scott Parker is highly regarded at West Ham after his four years there as a player where he did really well for the Hammers, and won multiple player of the year awards.

"He's an experienced pair of hands for West Ham who are sitting in eighth place, and they are hoping Parker can inspire them to go on a run and get them over the line."

While Nuno's departure came quicker than many at West Ham had expected, but it had been coming as frustrations continued to build at the London Stadium.

Large sections of the supporters had been vocal in their discontent with the manager's role, the style of play, and their results in the early stages of the season.

As a result, Brown warned Football Insider on 2 November that West Ham were holding internal talks about Nuno's future, and he was then sacked the next day.

The growing feeling of unrest at the London Stadium has become a concern, and after a surprise defeat against West Brom, Nuno's position became untenable.
