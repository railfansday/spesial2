Inside Joseph's remarkable return to England stardom

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Sky Sports looks at Will Joseph's sensational return to the England fold four years after making his first cap; Joseph has overcome injuries, the liquidation of his boyhood club and a loss of hearing in one ear all while caring for his twin; he is set to start vs New Zealand

Four years on from his first cap, Will Joseph lit Twickenham up with a stunning five-try outing for England in their 78-15 demolition of Japan - but his remarkable international comeback is the result of so much more than his on-pitch performance.

The winger is only the fifth player in England history to score five times in a Test match - managing the feat in his first appearance since 2022 - with the display set to have secured him a starting berth in the crucial Nations Championship clash against New Zealand on Saturday.

His return marked the culmination of a odyssey back into Steve Borthwick's side that has seen him battle injury, permanent hearing loss in one ear and the liquidation of his boyhood club - all while caring for his twin brother, Alex, who suffers with serious health issues and severe learning difficulties.

"I've been on a bit of a journey from when I was first here," Joseph told Sky Sports. "I've learned a lot about myself, and I've got a better understanding of what it takes to probably be in this environment, and to be at this level."

Joseph's "journey" has seen him overcome more in four years than many players face over the course of their entire lives - let alone their careers.

After a promising beginning to his professional career with boyhood club London Irish, he missed much of his third season with an infection, before Irish were liquidated due to financial difficulties in 2023. A Lisfranc injury then put a halt to a solid start at new club Harlequins.

But the lasting damage caused by those ailments - while significant - paled in comparison to his next injury in 2024, when he suffered a permanent loss of hearing in his left ear.

"I had a training session and then the whistle went and my ear felt a bit funny," Joseph told Sky Sports. "We had a pool session afterwards: I got into the swimming pool, got out, and then I couldn't hear anything out of my left ear.

"My understanding is that it's an autoimmune disease - my body overreacted to something that's gone in my ear and just shut off the nerves to be able to hear.

"I remember thinking it's going to be really hard [to play], because I felt like I couldn't communicate to anyone outside me - everything felt a little bit off.

"I was definitely a little bit worried [about being able to continue playing], but I got through it week by week and then got to a place where I said actually, I can live with this. This is going to be alright."

His resilient attitude has shone through, coming through a second Lisfranc injury and a wrist surgery to score four tries in seven games for Gloucester this season, with his red-hot form earning himself the long-awaited second cap that allowed him to dazzle on the international stage.

That tenacity is something England fans will recognise as a Joseph gene. His older brother, Johnathan, made 54 appearances for his country in an eight-year spell in the side between 2012 and 2020.

"I felt a big sense of relief," Johnathan told Sky Sports of his brother's performance against Japan.

"He had his first cap four years ago, and then hasn't been seen [for England] since.

"[Before the game] It was almost like if he doesn't go well now and if things go badly, it could have been his last chance.

"It was a real sense of relief [to see Will perform like that] because I know he's good enough to be in that England team."

Will has undoubtedly been influenced by the sparkling rugby career of his older brother, but it is also his twin brother, Alex, who provides a source of daily inspiration.

The winger speaks warmly of being motivated by taking care of Alex, who suffers with severe learning difficulties and epilepsy.

While focusing on recovering from his own injuries, Will also had to contend with his twin being temporarily put into an induced coma as a result of uncontrolled seizures.

"Growing up with him, I almost felt like I had to look after him," Will added. "He's a big motivator for me because he's probably not able to do things like most people in this world.

"I want to make him proud. He's a big why for me."

His older brother Johnathan echoed this sentiment, attributing Will's composure in high-stakes situations in part to the skills learned when caring for his twin.

"[Will] is a very humble kid. He's very caring - he puts people before himself.

"He's obviously got a twin brother who's got special needs, so he's always had to take care of him, which sort of transfers over to the bloke he is today.

"[Will] was obviously buzzing after [the Japan game], but he'll be right back down to earth now and he'll be working hard to prepare well for New Zealand, I'm sure."

Starting against the ever-vaunted All Blacks - who will be looking for a reprieve after their damaging 35-28 loss to Wales in Cardiff - will undoubtedly be the most high-calibre challenge of Will's career.

But with his track record of overcoming adversity, you would be daft to bet against the Gloucester man putting on another show-stopping performance.
