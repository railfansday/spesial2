AI in Your SOC 2 Environment: 3 Worked Examples, From Risk to Audit Evidence

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Posted on Nov 16 • Originally published at throughlineaudit.com

AI shows up in SOC 2 environments in three ways: it supports the people who operate the system, it performs compliance controls, and it acts directly in production. Each one changes the risk differently, so each changes what a credible control looks like.

Below is one worked example of each, written from the auditor's side: the risk, the control, how an auditor tests it, and the evidence that shows it worked. These are illustrative designs, not a mandatory checklist. Your approach depends on your scope, risks and service commitments.

Ask how much the AI acts on its own. The more it acts without a person in between, the more your controls have to govern the AI itself, not just the people using it.

This looks like nothing has changed. A person still owns each change, and a separate person still approves it before it reaches production.

But the risk has shifted. AI raises the volume of code and the size of each pull request, so reviewers are stretched. People over-trust AI output, and their experience tells them where humans make mistakes, not where AI does. AI makes confident assumptions with nothing behind them, and those are easy to wave through.

An auditor will notice rubber-stamping patterns, like a 2,000-line change approved within a minute. Approval timing is a signal to investigate, not proof on its own. Relevant criteria: CC8.1 (change management) and CC7.1 (vulnerability detection).

An agent compares access lists against current staff to find leavers who still have access. In some setups it reports to a responsible person who acts. In others, it can remove the access itself.

This can make the control stronger than the human version. AI doesn't get tired or skim a long list. But it makes mistakes people wouldn't. If someone changed roles and that never reached a system the agent can see, the agent may judge their access appropriate. People pick up that context in the office. AI only has what's recorded.

That last row is the one auditors care about most. When AI performs a control, the auditor needs comfort over the AI itself: how it's configured, who can change it, and whether its outputs are complete and accurate. Relevant criteria: CC6.2, CC6.3, CC4.1, CC3.2 and CC8.1.

An agent runs part of a support desk as a data administrator. When a customer needs a record fixed, it makes the change in production data while talking to them. Fast and effective, and now part of your system.

AI can do more and more of the work, but people must own the outcomes. A control is only as good as the context, scope and oversight around the AI performing it.
