The White House says the AI data center buildout will continue

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Free daily briefing on global business news.

National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett said ballot referendums won't affect projects scheduled over the next couple of years. Trump reiterated his support

An aerial view of a 33 megawatt data center in Vernon, Calif. A surge in demand for AI infrastructure is fueling a boom in data centers across the country. (Mario Tama/Getty Images)

National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett said Friday that he anticipates the buildout of data centers will proceed along its projected path, even after voters backed curbs on the facilities in Tuesday’s midterm elections.

Hassett acknowledged that voters enacted restrictions in dozens of local and state ballot initiatives aimed at data centers. "We respect the localities that decide that's what they want to do," said on CNBC. "But America is a big place, and right now, the data centers scheduled over the next couple of years aren't affected by these ballot initiatives."

He said he expects the facilities to keep multiplying where governors are friendlier to them. He suggested states that are hostile to the facilities could change course, saying "the benefits of the data centers will be so obvious that people decide to change their minds about the way they regulate them."

Hassett also pointed to "uncertainty about what the political change is going to mean for data centers going forward" as the reason stocks sank sharply on Thursday. The Nasdaq $NDAQ Composite fell 5.5% on Thursday before rebounding Friday. Hassett said markets are anxious about a new congressional majority. "You could take something that's growing as fast as the data centers and make it grow a little bit slower, and you still have a blockbuster economy," he said.

President Donald Trump posted Friday on Truth Social that he is "100% in favor of Super Intelligence (SI)" and will work with states "that want to become RICH by having them." He wrote that "only foolish leadership will say 'NO' to the riches they bring."

An NBC News exit poll showed that 56% of midterm voters rated data centers a major or crucial concern. Among those who called the issue crucial, the generic Democratic candidate drew 62% support.

Voters in more than 30 towns and cities across nine states backed bans, moratoriums and other curbs on data centers on Tuesday. Developers had called off at least $260 billion in planned U.S. data center projects as of mid-October, and most of those projects died at the local level.

Other industry and political figures have described a harder road. Meta $META Platforms President Dina Powell McCormick said Wednesday at the WSJ Tech conference that starting new data center build-outs in the United States will be very challenging. She said expanding existing projects should be easier.

Texas Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa, who defeated Republican Gov. Greg Abbott $ABT, said Thursday evening, "We will have laws to protect Texans, and we need to stop what we are doing with data centers until we have those protections." Republicans hold the majority in the Texas Legislature.
