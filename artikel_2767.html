Hot start lifts Braves over Vikings in girls basketball

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

Host Terre Haute South opened its 2026-27 girls high school basketball season with a 33-1 lead Tuesday night, but it wasn't enough to make West Vigo quit.

The last 19 minutes of the nonconference game were fairly even, South winning the game 62-26 — not close enough to scare the Braves, and not nearly enough to mollify the Vikings' new coach.

"For the first game, and for the girls being a little bit nervous, I thought our start and our intensity was really great," coach Jason Setliff of the Braves said afterward. "It was a good tuneup for us."

Longtime observers of this rivalry are familiar with its pattern: South steals the ball, turns defense into offense and shoots layups. The Braves had 12 steals in the first eight minutes, five by Moose White, and shook off a slow start to score 17 points in the last 3:50 of the quarter for a 22-1 lead.

"We got great guard play," Setliff said. "We anticipated very well on the defensive end." And when Shaundrea Sanders took over the steal department with four thefts in the second quarter, the Braves got its first 11 points.

That's when the Vikings got off the mat, not nearly early enough for coach Tommy Roberts.

"We've got to get it out of [the Vikings'] heads that those big schools are going to beat us up," he said afterward. "We're going to stop playing soft basketball, and it starts tomorrow."

The score was 40-4 in the waning seconds of the second quarter, and a lead of 35 or more at halftime starts a running clock for the entire second half. So the fun part of the game for Viking fans came at that point, when Lydia Jones hit a rebound basket before the end of the half and West Vigo kept the margin under 35 for most of the third quarter.

"We had bright spots in the third quarter," Roberts agreed. "But then [the Braves] got a couple of baskets and we hung our heads again."

A 6-0 run sparked by a pair of steals by Samarra Newburn made the score 52-14 and the clock was running the rest of the way. But the Vikings still won the fourth quarter, and had just eight turnovers after halftime while South had 14.

White led three statistical categories for the game with 18 points, eight rebounds and seven steals. Newburn scored 13, Sanders 10 with five steals and Ashaya Cooper eight points plus a game-high four assists and five steals for the Braves.

Lydia Jones scored 17 for West Vigo, and Abi Jones had six steals.

The statistic that pleased Setliff the most was rebounding. The Braves, with no starter taller than 5-foot-6, had 41 to 21 for the taller Vikings.

"We have to rebound by committee," Setliff agreed, "but we fight, and we're gonna continue to fight. Our girls compete, and when we're locked in we can be a very good team."

"We're going to have a mentality change," Roberts promised.
