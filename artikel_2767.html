Raiders’ plan for Fernando Mendoza starts with four yards on first down

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

Fernando Mendoza took responsibility for all six sacks the Seahawks hung on him Sunday. Klint Kubiak might want to get a word in before the rookie quarterback owns up to the Raiders' run game, too.

There's a reason the Raiders coach included himself among the people who need to do a better job helping Mendoza. By the time the rookie quarterback is trying to escape a free rusher on third-and-9, Kubiak has already had two chances to make his life easier.

That became priority number one as Kubiak and the Raiders began preparing Mendoza for his second career start on Sunday against the Broncos.

Las Vegas Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak speaks during a press conference after an NFL football game against the Seattle Seahawks in Las Vegas, Nov. 15, 2026. AP Photo/David Becker

"Just coach and player working together. Just getting him into the right calls," Kubiak said. "He did a great job, and it's always on myself to help the whole operation be better."

Start with what four yards on first down would do for everybody in Denver. Against Seattle, Ashton Jeanty carried eight times for 15 yards in the first half. Mendoza completed 11 of 13 passes before halftime, and even at that clip, it was an awfully hard way to move the ball.

Give Kubiak second-and-6 and he can hand it to Jeanty again. He can also show that same handoff, pull a linebacker toward the line and give Mendoza a throwing lane behind him.

Give Broncos defensive coordinator Vance Joseph second-and-10 often enough, and he can afford to let somebody else worry about Jeanty while his best pass rushers get after the quarterback.

Why leave Mendoza in the same spot for those players to find him? Some of his best throws Sunday came when Kubiak rolled him out, something the Raiders got away from in the second half. And with Mendoza's live arm and Tre Tucker's speed, there's reason to dial up a deep ball. Get a safety to give Tucker another five yards of cushion and see what that does for Jeanty underneath.

The uncertainty about Brock Bowers' ankle complicates things. Take him off the field, and Kubiak loses a pretty good target on those rollouts. He can still move Mendoza around, take his chances downfield, and make Denver honor the run.

It takes more than calling a run to get those four yards. If a defensive tackle drives a guard into the backfield, Jeanty has to make his first cut before he reaches the line. Give him room to press the hole and make the linebacker commit, and now he has a say in where the play goes.

Seattle Seahawks safety Julian Love (20) and defensive tackle Rylie Mills (98) tackle Las Vegas Raiders running back Ashton Jeanty (2) on Nov. 15, 2026. AP Photo/John Locher

The communication inside matters just as much on passing downs. Say Denver walks two linebackers up over the center. Mendoza and his linemen have to agree on which one the protection accounts for. Kubiak can help by giving him a receiver breaking quickly into the space the other linebacker leaves if he comes. Then it's on Mendoza to deliver the ball.

"All of those sacks were on me," Mendoza said. "I believe the offensive line played great, and I'm excited to get back out there this next Sunday."

An admirable sentiment. The tackles can still do him a favor by running the edge rushers past him, and the guards by leaving him somewhere to step up.

Mendoza's first film session as the starter gave him and his coaches a chance to sort through all of it. Kirk Cousins could bring 14 seasons of Sundays to those conversations. With Mendoza, even the way he relays the call in the huddle merits a second listen.

"Detailed, detail-oriented. Asks questions. He's a professional," Kubiak said of Mendoza. Becoming the starter means "definitely more things, more questions, more conversations that are geared to him."

"I would say it was a little bit more leaving no stone unturned, making sure we cover all the coaching points, as there can be no assumptions made because this is my first time doing this," Mendoza said.

Fernando Mendoza (15) of the Las Vegas Raiders warms up before a game against the Seattle Seahawks at Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas on Sunday, Nov. 15, 2026. Getty Images

And then making sure mistakes get corrected.

"I'm looking forward to getting those right," Mendoza said of the adjustments.

The complication is that Joseph gets to watch the Seattle tape, too. A pressure Mendoza spent Wednesday learning to recognize might look familiar Sunday, right up until the linebacker he expects to rush drops underneath his throw. Kubiak can give him that look in practice and make him find his second receiver.

Come Sunday, that picture might look entirely different.

"So, the learning just gets accelerated when you're doing it," Kubiak said.

Mendoza is not running from that challenge.

"I believe getting thrown in the fire is the best way to learn, and the Seahawks and the Broncos both have considerably elite defenses," Mendoza said.

There's a good way to tell Sunday whether the work paid off.

"Well, the proof always comes in the production on the field, so we get another week to go produce," Kubiak said.

Watch the scoreboard for the down and distance when Mendoza breaks the huddle. If it says third-and-3, that rookie suddenly has an awful lot of the playbook at his disposal. So does the guy talking into his helmet.
