Storms Cancel Flights As 80 Million Americans Travel For Thanksgiving

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

FlightAware data shows hundreds of canceled and delayed flights in the US today due to poor weather conditions. There have been 396 cancellations already for flights within, into, or out of the United States. More have been delayed, with 1,053 flights within, into, or out of the US facing this type of disruption.

The troublesome weather is the result of two areas of low pressure arriving at American shores, one off the northwest coast and one off the northeast coast. It comes at the worst possible time, with over 80 million Americans expected to travel across the Thanksgiving weekend.

The FAA has said it is the busiest holiday travel weekend in 16 years, yet it will be heavily disrupted by the weather. On the East Coast, streets are submerged in the Miami-Fort Lauderdale area. To the west in Huntington Beach, California, floodwaters have also covered roads and entered homes. Coastal areas have been hit particularly hard, with huge waves striking much of the coastline. Anybody who has experienced the ongoing poor weather will be unsurprised that aircraft have not been able to begin their scheduled services. "[It is] absolutely craziness, but the end goal is what it is all about, just to get home," one traveler told TODAY.

The FAA had previously warned that forecast rain and winds made delays possible "throughout the week" due to the poor weather. On Wednesday, FlightAware data showed over 6,800 delays and over 60 cancellations.

The most eye-catching and worrying event from Wednesday was the overshoot of a runway at Miami International Airport (MIA) by a McDonnell Douglas MD-11, operated by FedEx Express. The incident involving a 34-year-old aircraft led the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) to impose a ground stop, disrupting many Thanksgiving travelers hoping to beat the weekend rush.

MIA reopened on Wednesday night, but was still short of full capacity with two runways available. Delays overnight exceeded three hours. By early morning today, the average delay was three hours and 34 minutes.

New York has one of the most complex and congested airspaces in the country.

The US government had taken steps to minimize disruptions during the Thanksgiving weekend following the four-hour nationwide ground stop on November 13. Top officials from the FAA, the Department of Defense, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, and the Department of Transportation (DOT) were put together in a "Tiger Team" that met weekly in the run-up to Thanksgiving. With the DOT refusing to rule out further ground stops while systems are updated, their role was to ensure that any disruption came outside of Thanksgiving.

White House spokesperson Allison Schuster told the Daily Wire:

“President Trump and [Transportation] Secretary [Sean] Duffy have brought together the best and the brightest across the government to ensure what happened last Friday can never happen again."

Preventing nationwide ground stops caused by human or technological issues is possible. However, the impact of severe weather is harder to mitigate. While any cancellations and ground stops caused by this week's storms will be localized rather than causing a nationwide halt to aviation, there is next to nothing that the top team set up for this week can do about it.
