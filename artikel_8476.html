You have comparable sales data and competitive bids. The market is relatively efficient, which probably is the term I should have used instead of transparent. Where it gets irrational is if a buyer falls in love with a house and is willing to pay a seller more, or in a divorce or estate sale where the seller wants a quick sale and is willing to accept something fast and easy. Those do happen but they aren't the norm.
That tends to be true where the home market is very similar within the same area in all aspects. Some areas have larger differences in one or more of the following items making them much harder to predict with comparable sales data: school districts, home construction, home size, age, plot size and desireability, finish level, and adjacencies both positive and negative.
If the location is mostly track housing with cookie cutter design/build/age etc these are typically not an issue at all.
