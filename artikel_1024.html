Springboks boss Erasmus given six-game ban

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Rassie Erasmus issued an extraordinary criticism of the officiating of his side's 21-19 Nations Championship defeat to France; the South African coach will play no part in their upcoming match against Ireland, after being banned for six games with immediate effect

South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus has been suspended for six international matches with immediate effect, following the extraordinary comments he made about the officiating of his side’s 21-19 defeat to France.

SA Rugby has requested a full hearing in response to the sanction.

Erasmus delivered an outspoken press conference on Monday, in which he suggested officials had missed 17 illegal tackles against his side during their crunch Nations Championship clash. He went as far as questioning whether "manipulation" of the result was behind why those incidents were not picked up.

World Rugby confirmed they had reviewed the comments made by Erasmus, and an independent panel had imposed the sanction under World Rugby's Match Official Abuse Sanction Process, a framework that was introduced in July.

In a statement released online, World Rugby said: "The panel determined that the comments suggest that match officials acted with bias/prejudice or deliberately influenced the outcome of a match, and when the conduct is considered as a whole a reasonable person would conclude that the conduct constituted an unacceptable attack on the credibility and competence of the match officials.

"In respect to the sanction, the panel determined that the conduct clearly amounts to disrespecting the authority of the match officials by impugning their integrity.

"Therefore, the panel reviewed a number of elements of the commentary, including the timing and reach of the statements, combined with the allegations of bias or bad faith, and found that the comments attracted a six-match automatic suspension."

On Monday, Erasmus questioned decisions made by the officials during his side's loss to France, claiming he saw three occasions where opposition players should have been sin binned for dangerous tackles.

"Let's say 11 of those were at least a penalty and three I thought was at least three yellow cards. And not one was sanctioned and not one was showed out or cited," Erasmus said.

"I'll send you the playlist but I'll get into trouble… So I can't send the playlist to anybody and we can only send five clips to World Rugby. So we'll probably query it but the answer will probably be: 'Yes, it was a mistake, it should have been penalised'.

"On numerous times we mentioned to the referee 'we think there's a knee-drop tackle'. I'm saying to you my feeling is that knee-drop tackles is out. They happened 17 times in our game. Not one was penalised."

"And if the TMO didn't see it, then maybe technology must be better. If he saw it and ignored it, what does that boil down to? Manipulation of the game or the result? Or players getting injured?

"So, all I'm asking is don't take the TMOs out and discourage them to get involved. Then the wrong team will win and players will get injured. That's all I'm saying."

Erasmus was previously banned from all forms of rugby for two months in 2021 after he released an hour-long video, criticising referees for their officiating of the first Test of the British and Irish Lions tour to South Africa, a game in which his side were beaten 22-17 by Warren Gatland's tourists.

He was handed a two-game suspension in 2022 after publishing a string of X posts, again criticising officials following a defeat to France.

In a statement released immediately after the announcement, a spokesperson for South Africa Rugby said:

"SA Rugby is requesting a full misconduct hearing for Springbok coach Rassie Erasmus following his sanctioning by an independent World Rugby panel for comments made in a press conference in Dublin on Monday.

"The organisation respected the process but had requested an opportunity for its coach to contextualise his comments and assure the panel that it was not his intention to allege that any match official had acted with ulterior motives.

"SA Rugby trusted that such a hearing could be expedited considering the seriousness of the charge.

"SA Rugby will not make any further comment until the hearing could be completed."

Should the suspension be upheld, Erasmus will not be allowed to have any match-day involvement in his side's clash against Ireland on Saturday, or the following five games.

Those include the Nations Championship Finals, commencing the weekend of Friday, November 27 and he will also be unavailable for South Africa's opening four internationals of 2027, which are yet to be confirmed, but are likely to be the opening fixtures of his side's Rugby Championship campaign.

It is expected that he will be able to return for the 2027 Rugby World Cup in Australia.

Under the ban Erasmus will also be prevented from entering a stadium, having any direct or indirect with South Africa's players or management team, and has been suspended from engaging in any 'feedback' processes with match officials.

World Rugby say the 54-year-old coach is allowed to bring the matter before a full misconduct hearing.

"Respect is fundamental to rugby, and match officials are central to the sport," World Rugby's statement added.

"Protecting their wellbeing and integrity, and their right to perform without abuse or intimidation, is a responsibility World Rugby and unions take seriously. Coaches, players, unions and everyone in the game must uphold these standards.

"Robust, respectful debate is natural, but teams have access to well-established and supported channels and protocols for raising officiating matters before and after matches. Challenging a decision is not the same as questioning an official's integrity or making comments that expose them to personal abuse."
