Compass ‘Welcomes’ Open Real Estate, Sees Record Quarterly Earnings

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

During Compass’ third quarter earnings call on Nov. 5, CEO Robert Reffkin touted the firm’s success amid an array of market challenges, from increasing mortgage rates to decreasing home sales.

“Against this backdrop, I am pleased to say that our company delivered record Q3 revenue of $3.9 billion, record Q3 net income of $62 million, record Q3 adjusted EBITDA of $306 million, which was also above the high end of our guide and reflects more adjusted EBITDA than either standalone Compass or Standalone Anywhere generated for the full year of 2025. We also delivered record Q3 free cash flow of $240 million, bringing cash on hand to a very healthy $841 million.”

Compass’s adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) of $306 million for Q3 surpassed the company’s original expectations of $275 million to $305. While down from $363 million in Q2, EBITDA rose from $94 million in Q3 2025 to set a new Q3 record.

Other Q3 records for Compass include revenue, which came in at $3.9 billion for Q3. While this marks a dip from last quarter’s $4.31 billion, it represents a whopping 111% year-over-year increase from $1.85 billion in Q3 2025. Pro forma revenue was up 11.6% year-over-year from $3.49 billion.

Net income was also at a record Q3 level, coming in at $62 million. While down from $92 million in Q2, the increase is significant compared to the $4 million net loss year over year. The company has now been net income positive for three consecutive quarters.

Much of the quarter’s success was attributed to improvements in Compass’s operating expenses (OPEX), which were down 6% year-over-year. Free cash flow was also improved at $240 million in Q3, which the company noted was a record for any quarter or year in its history.

Looking ahead, Compass stated it expects a Q4 revenue of $3.150 billion to $3.350 billion, and a Q4 adjusted EBITDA of $100 million to $130 million. For the full year of 2026, the company stated it expects free cash flow to be positive, and OPEX of $2.71 billion to $2.73 billion.

Breaking down the business Looking at the brokerage business, gross transaction volume for Coldwell Banker, Compass, Corcoran and Sotheby’s International Realty came in at $138.9 billion, down from $155.2 billion in Q2 but up 96.5% year-over-year from $70.7 billion in Q3 2025. Pro forma, transaction volume was up 7.5% year-over-year to $133.1 billion. This is also compared to an average 0.7% increase in gross transaction volume for the entirety of the U.S. residential real estate market over the same period (as sourced from the National Association of Realtors®’ existing home sales data).

Brokerage transactions were up 106.4% year-over-year, from 67,887 in Q3 2025 to  140,118 this quarter. Pro forma, transactions were actually down 0.3% year-over-year, but this is compared to a 1.8% decrease across the entirety of the U.S. residential real estate market (as sourced from NAR’s existing home sales data).

For franchise business, gross transaction volume was at $108.9 billion, a 1,181% year-over-year increase from $8.5 billion in Q3 2025. Pro forma, transaction volume was up 2.3% year-over-year to $104.2 billion.

Franchise transactions were up a whopping 2,073% year-over-year, from 8,825 in Q3 2025 to 191,809 this quarter. Pro forma, transactions actually decreased 3.6% year-over-year.

The mortgage business improved as well, credited to Compass’s acquisitions. Total mortgage transactions were up 406% year-over-year to 38,757 (up from 7,666 in Q3 2025). This was also up pro forma by 1.7% year-over-year. Loan volume was $3.30 billion, up 9.9% year-over-year on a pro forma basis. “We’re now a top 10 mortgage lender, which is incredible after such a short amount of time,” said Reffkin. “In Q3, even though it was a very tough mortgage market, our overall funded volume grew by 10% year over year in Q3 and year to date by 32%…Longer term, we want to be a top five retail lender.”

Agent retention and recruiting also improved for Compass due to what the company and Reffkin outlined as a strong value prop and industry positioning. For agents producing more than $20,000 in gross commission income, retention is above 98%. Recruiting-wise, brokerage agents specifically increased by 2,292 in Q3, and total agent count grew by 6,390.

The ‘Home’ platform Reffkin also attributed Compass’ Q3 success to the rollout of its “Home” platform, an end-to-end proprietary technology for real estate professionals.

Across Coldwell Banker, Corcoran and Sotheby’s International Realty, Compass stated it saw 33,950 agents adopt the available platform, bringing the total number of users across the full company to 68,349 agents. The plan remains to roll out the platform to the full portfolio of Compass brands in Q1 2027. Through the platform, all participating agents will have access to the company’s Private Exclusive and Coming Soon inventory.

Looking ahead Reffkin reported that the company will take a conservative approach in the months ahead as the market continues to be challenged.

“There are reasons to be optimistic, but obviously we’re not planning for that,” he said. “We continue to focus on costs and plan for being conservative. But I think what can help in our favor (is) one, we have more inventory right now than any point since…2019. More inventory means you can create more sales.”

When asked about future merger-and-acquisition opportunities, Reffkin indicated the door is open.

“We continue to be a company that wants to support great operators and continue to have those conversations.”

Maria Patterson contributed to this report.
