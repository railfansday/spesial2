Wife of former Cowboys star questions ruling that barred son's team from playoffs

Tanggal: 2026-11-05

The wife of a Dallas Cowboys legend is fed up and took to social media to dispute her son's high school football team's ban from the Texas Association of Private and Parochial Schools (TAPPS) Division 1 playoffs.

On Tuesday, the governing body for Texas private school football ruled that Argyle Liberty Christian would not be allowed to compete in the postseason because it used an ineligible player during the season. The Liberty Christian Warriors are ranked No. 1 in TAPPS state rankings and feature five-star linebacker Cooper Witten, an Oklahoma Sooners commit and the son of Cowboys alum Jason Witten. Cooper Witten is also the No. 3-ranked player in the state for the Class of 2027.

The board's sanctions include forfeiting the games in which the ineligible student participated, resulting in Liberty Christian's removal from the TAPPS playoffs. The board has not identified the student. However, Witten does not appear to be involved, aside from playing on the same team as the student.

"We strongly disagree with the TAPPS ruling and we provided clear and unambiguous evidence to TAPPS which demonstrated our innocence against the false assertions that were made against our football program," Argyle Liberty Christian wrote in a letter to families of the football team on Tuesday. "This unfair ruling ignores the clear and obvious facts in this case, and we are considering our various legal options moving forward."

The TAPPS executive board found the school in violation of the governing body's "follow the coach" rule.

The bylaw states that a student who transfers to a TAPPS member school is not eligible for varsity competition in a sport for one year if a coach in that sport at the new school coached or trained the student during the previous 12 months. The rule includes AAU, select, club, 7-on-7, 6-on-6 or similar teams, along with individual or group instruction to the student. According to reporting by The Dallas Morning News, Jace Johnson, a 2026 transfer from Abilene Cooper to Liberty Christian, is believed to be the player involved in the violation.

Argyle Liberty Christian wide receiver Jace Johnson hauls in a touchdown reception against Parish Episcopal. (Steve Nurenberg/DMN)

Johnson, a 6-foot-5 senior wide receiver, holds offers from programs including Southeast Oklahoma State, Texas A&M-Texarkana and Central Missouri, according to Rivals.

"It was determined that a transfer student-athlete participated on a 7-on-7 team coached by Liberty Christian assistant football coach Antonio Griffin in the year prior to transferring to Liberty," TAPPS said in a news release. "Furthermore, the athletic department leadership and football coaching staff knew or should have known of these facts prior to the start of the 2026 football season."

Griffin, a 2012 Liberty Christian alum, serves as the team's defensive backs coach. However, Griffin also runs Lionhearted Elite, a 7-on-7 program in nearby Colleyville, where Johnson participated. Greg Riddle, of the Dallas Morning News, reported that a local district executive committee has recommended the state executive board declare Johnson ineligible and suspend Griffin from coaching for the remainder of the season.

While Griffin owns Lionhearted Elite, former Baylor Bears wide receiver Quan Jones currently serves as the program's head coach.

"I have served as the head coach of Lionhearted 18U for the past several years, and this is documented," Jones said on X. "Antonio Griffin is the owner of Lionhearted, but I have always been the head coach responsible for overseeing and leading the 18U team."

"I believe the accusations against Antonio Griffin are wrongful," Jones continued. "Ownership of an organization should not be confused with coaching responsibilities. Lionhearted also merged with WTX Elite this year. I ask that the facts be verified before conclusions are made."

Witten's mother, Michelle, took to social media, alleging that email documentation submitted to TAPPS may have been tampered with.

"The original email, shared directly with me tonight by a fellow Warrior football mom, says 'Owned by Coach Griff,'" wrote Michelle Witten on Wednesday. "The excerpt reportedly submitted to TAPPS says 'Owned and Coached by Coach Griff.' Why does that version include 'and Coached'? I'm also sharing the full email so the omitted context can be seen."

The Witten family's ties to Liberty Christian run deep. According to Michelle, her son Cooper has played for the program since age 3. Notably, Jason, now the Sooners' interim offensive coordinator, coached Liberty Christian to back-to-back TAPPS state titles in 2023 and 2024. Cooper's brother CJ is also a Liberty Christian alum and is currently a freshman linebacker for the Rice Owls.

"Griffin NEVER coached the student in question," Michelle wrote on X. "We believe clear evidence was ignored and disputed evidence accepted. Do five voters grasp the consequences for these boys, their futures and Liberty Christian? This goes beyond football."

A TAPPS spokesperson declined to comment on the differences between emails with Chron on Thursday afternoon.

It's unclear whether Liberty Christian's legal actions can make a difference in such a short timeframe, as TAPPS has re-bracketed its eight-team playoff and delayed the first round until November 14 to give teams time to prepare for their new opponents.
