YouTube Hits Back At Rivals With New Slate Featuring Rising Creators, Established Stars & Hot New Directors

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

As rivals such as Netflix and Disney start encroaching on their world of creators, YouTube has returned fire with a new slate of programming from a slew of burgeoning creators such as Nick DiGiovanni and Emma Chamberlain, established stars including Phil Rosenthal and Aryna Sabalenka and one director that insiders have dubbed the “next Kane Parsons”.

The Google-owned streamer held its second annual Creator Premieres event at The Hollywood Roosevelt in Los Angeles, where it unveiled a raft of new titles.

DiGiovanni, who is behind the world’s largest food YouTube channel, is launching Chef. The series, which comes from his production company, Knife Drop Studios, is following fellow professional chefs, offering an unfiltered look inside kitchens around the globe after launching the first full length episode Inside the World’s #1 Pizza Restaurant.

Chamberlain is launching What Are You Waiting For? Next year. The series, which comes from Amplify Pictures and exec producer Joe Lewis, sees the creator try to understand more about people who wait in line, particularly in New York. Watch on Deadline

Last year, Saturday Night Live star Grace Reiter, who also stars on Netflix’s Minimum Wage, launched The Hunger Games (but better) parody. She is following that up with her second unscripted parody film: Twilight (but better). Produced by But Better Productions, Reiter directs with Andie Schlenker and Anderson Appleby as producers, and Cindy Schlenker as co-producer.

Tennis star Aryna Sabalenka is launching Aryna’s Arena: Unfiltered next year. The reality series will follow her as a Gucci ambassador designing her own $250K diamond necklace, a bride-to-be planning a wedding while living out of a suitcase for 300+ days a year and a daughter flying home to Belarus between slams. It comes from UltraBoom Media.

Former late-night host Lilly Singh returns to YouTube with Eligible Aunties, an unscripted docu-follow series that trades shallow villa romances for mature women with real emotional intelligence and emotional baggage. It comes from Singh’s Unicorn Island Productions.

As Deadline revealed in March, after Netflix ended his food show Somebody Feed Phil, he is taking it to YouTube with season nine premiere in spring 2017. It will see the Everybody Loves Raymond creator head to Greece, eating at sun-soaked tavernas. It comes from Lucky Bastards in partnership with Banijay Americas and Banijay Rights with Rich Rosenthal as showrunner and exec producer.

Finally, YouTube is highlighting the world of director Wesley Wang, whom the company hopes can replicate the success of Backrooms director Kane Parsons. Wang will premiere three projects on YouTube including Response, a sci-fi horror short film. The film begins when a routine welfare check at a remote desert preserve turns terrifying when four park rangers discover an otherworldly presence inside a woman’s tent, leaving the youngest ranger separated from his own body. Response is produced by Wesley Wang Media with Jason Rodelo serving as director and writer.

He also has two series, Extract 14 and Absolute Cinema, launching in the spring. Extract 14 is a six-part interactive murder-mystery series that puts viewers in the role of detective, with $100,000 on the line for the first person to crack the case. Told through found footage, digital breadcrumbs and hidden clues woven throughout each episode, the mystery unfolds over six weeks on YouTube, challenging viewers to pause, rewind, decode and collaborate to uncover the truth. The first viewer to correctly solve the case wins $100,000! Extract 14 is directed and written by Wang and produced by Wesley Wang Media.

Absolute Cinema is a competition series that follows ten aspiring filmmakers, chosen from thousands of internet strangers, who are brought to New York for an immersive, seven-day filmmaking bootcamp where they’ll compete for $100,000 to bring their dream movie to life. It also comes from Wesley Wang Media.

While YouTube doesn’t directly fund the vast majority of its slate, it does offer promotional opportunities and a direct route to advertisers, a move that it cemented at the Upfronts in May.

At the time, CEO Neal Mohan told Deadline, “We fundamentally do two things: one is we help amazing creators like this build an audience, no matter where they are in the world, and that takes an enormous amount of investment in the technology and the capabilities to do that. Then, the second thing we do is we help them build businesses.”

Sean Downey, Google President of Americas and Global Partners, said at tonight’s event, “YouTube creators are not just shaping modern culture, they are redefining the entire entertainment landscape. This slate of shows demonstrates how creators are commanding primetime attention on the big screen and building unmatched fan loyalty. For brands, this isn’t about buying thirty seconds of ad space; it’s about moving past standard commercial breaks and partnering directly with the storytellers who are defining modern culture.”
