Tottenham have been given another reason to remain interested in Eli Junior Kroupi after the Bournemouth striker marked his return from a serious injury with his first senior goal for France.

The 20-year-old had been sidelined for around three months after undergoing surgery on a foot injury during the summer, raising understandable questions over how quickly he would rediscover his previous level.

Those concerns are beginning to disappear. Kroupi has returned to club football and then made an immediate impression internationally, scoring in France’s 2-1 Nations League victory over Turkey on Sunday.

Kroupi remains a major target for Tottenham as Roberto De Zerbi looks for a more reliable goalscorer.

According to TEAMtalk, Spurs are even considering offering players as part of a potential deal, with Wilson Odobert, Mikey Moore and Luca Williams-Barnett discussed as possible makeweights.

Tottenham had already identified Kroupi as a leading target during the summer, but his injury effectively ended any possibility of a transfer at that stage.

His comeback has now strengthened his case considerably. Kroupi earned his second France cap against Turkey after replacing Bradley Barcola in the 63rd minute and scored 17 minutes later, converting after Rayan Cherki played him through.

It represented his first goal for Les Bleus and another major milestone in what has been a rapid rise.

From Tottenham’s perspective, the timing could hardly be better.

De Zerbi’s side have lacked consistent goals from recognised strikers this season, and Kroupi offers youth, Premier League experience and significant long-term upside.

However, Bournemouth remain in a very strong negotiating position.

A CaughtOffside report recently revealed that Bournemouth have no intention of selling Kroupi in January and could demand more than £80million if their stance changes.

That makes a winter deal extremely ambitious.

Spurs should still continue working on the transfer. Kroupi scored 13 Premier League goals as a teenager last season, and his first France goal suggests the summer injury has not permanently disrupted his momentum.

The biggest question is therefore no longer his fitness. It is whether Tottenham can produce an offer creative enough to convince Bournemouth to negotiate.
