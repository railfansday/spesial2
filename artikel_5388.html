Thailand met with Apple over mercenary spyware alerts targeting politicians

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Minister Chaichanok Chidchob says an alert does not mean a device was compromised; talks with tech firms are set for Thursday

Thailand's Digital Economy and Society Minister Chaichanok Chidchob met Apple $AAPL representatives on Wednesday about spyware warnings that politicians and others in the country received, according to Bloomberg.

In response to questions from Bloomberg, Apple said it had detected activity consistent with mercenary spyware attacks and sent the notifications on Oct. 29. The notifications went out worldwide, landing with recipients across 99 countries, according to Bloomberg.

Chaichanok said that getting a warning does not always indicate a device was actually breached, according to Bloomberg. He said officials in Thailand have nothing linking the attempted attacks to Pegasus, a surveillance tool built by Israel's NSO Group.

Some Thai politicians had questioned whether Pegasus was used after the alerts arrived, according to Bloomberg. Researchers reported in 2022 that the spyware had been used against activists campaigning for democracy.

Thai authorities are due to sit down with technology companies on Thursday to weigh the threat and potential responses, with Android-related risks also on the agenda, according to Bloomberg.

Chaichanok described the Apple meeting as one in a series of discussions, according to AppleInsider. The ministry intends to collaborate with the National Security Council, the National Cyber Security Agency and Thai companies that build computing equipment and programs. He said the goal is to understand the full scope of the attacks before any security plan takes effect, and he expects the plan to be finished during the week beginning Nov. 16.

Apple would not disclose what prompts it to send an alert, arguing that revealing those details might let attackers modify their tactics and avoid being caught, according to Bloomberg. Apple advises anyone who gets a threat notification to turn on Lockdown Mode and get help from security experts.

Apple describes the alerts as "high-confidence alerts" and says it cannot achieve absolute certainty, according to AppleInsider. The company has said that people in more than 150 countries have received its warnings since the program started in 2021.
