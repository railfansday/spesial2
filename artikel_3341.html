5 'Tulsa King' Actors Who Also Appeared in 'The Sopranos'

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Sylvester Stallone's Dwight “The General” Manfredi may be a fish out of water in Oklahoma, but Taylor Sheridan's popular series Tulsa King is a homecoming for anyone who spent the late ’90s and 2000s in the company of one of HBO's best series, The Sopranos. The key to this show's immense success is, without a doubt, co-creator and showrunner Terence Winter, who was an original writer and executive producer on The Sopranos. In 2010, he went on to create another hit for HBO, Boardwalk Empire, bringing some Soprano alumni along with him.

Similar to Boardwalk Empire, Winter has also brought several stars from The Sopranos into the fold of Tulsa King, including Max Casella, who played Benny Fazio, and Annabella Sciorra, who fans remember for her role as Tony's stunning but troubled gumad, Gloria Trillo. While there's still a chance for more familiar faces to show up in Paramount+'s latest success, this list highlights five stars from Tulsa King (almost no one remembers #5) who also appeared in The Sopranos!

Known for starring alongside Neil Patrick Harris on the hit sitcom Doogie Howser, M.D., Max Casella made his first appearance in the Season 1 premiere of Tulsa King as Armand "Manny" Truisi, an ex-soldier for the Invernizzi crime family who fled New York and settled down to start a quiet life with his wife and kids in Tulsa, where he started working for Margaret Devereaux (Dana Delany) at Fennario Ranch. After squashing the past with Manfredi, Truisi joined his crew, but his broken marriage and financial desperation set him on a course of betrayal and self-destruction that ended with him taking his own life at the end of Season 3.

Before he was the Oklahoma Kid, Casella starred in The Sopranos as Benny Fazio, a loyal soldier who worked his way up in the Soprano/DiMeo crime family. Casella made his first appearance in Season 3, Episode 3, "Fortunate Son," as Fazio, who shows up after being released from county jail and is now working for his friend and recently made man, Christopher Moltisanti (Michael Imperioli). Out of the 20-plus episodes he appeared in, Casella's character is mostly remembered by fans for his hilarious tiff with Artie Bucco (John Ventimiglia) in Season 6, which landed him in hot water with Tony.

In Season 1, Episode 4, "Visitation Place," fans meet Manfredi's younger sister, Joanne Manfredi, played by Annabella Sciorra, who had previously starred alongside Stallone in the 1997 crime thriller Cop Land, which also stars other Soprano alumni including Edie Falco, John Ventimiglia, and Tony Sirico. Throughout the show, Joanne has since become a major player in her brother's extensive operation and is one of the few people who can keep Manfredi in check and level-headed. While Joanne is not a woman to be messed with, she has nothing on Sciorra's character from The Sopranos, Gloria Trillo.

Sciorra made her debut in Season 3, Episode 8, "He Is Risen," as the strong-willed and fiery Gloria Trillo, who meets Tony Soprano (James Gandolfini) in Dr. Melfi's (Lorraine Bracco) office and eventually becomes his gumad, but their passionate affair is short-lived after Tony sees her true, toxic nature. Despite her trauma and baggage, fans remember Gloria and Tony's relationship as one of the most revealing and intense dynamics in the entire series, which also led to Tony having a significant breakthrough about his relationship with his mother, Livia (Nancy Marchand). Even though her time on the show was brief, Sciorra delivered an unforgettable performance, and I know I speak for every fan when I say it's nice to see her back in the spotlight on Tulsa King.

In Season 3 of Tulsa King, Manfredi has his work cut out for him as he goes head-to-head with a powerful Dixie Mafia leader and boss of a moonshine empire, Jeremiah Dunmire, who is played by the underappreciated Robert Patrick. Widely known for his role as T-1000 in James Cameron's Terminator 2: Judgment Day, Patrick gives an intense and merciless performance in Tulsa King and is easily one of the series' best characters, but before he was setting house fires and terrorizing the people of Tulsa, Patrick appeared on The Sopranos as the degenerate gambler, Davey Scatino.

In Season 2, Episode 6, "The Happy Wanderer," fans meet Scatino, the owner of a local sporting goods store who has known Tony and Artie since they were kids. When word reaches Scatino that Tony is taking over Uncle Junior's (Dominic Chianese) executive card game, he weasels his way into the game (unaware that he was manipulated by Tony), but he makes the grave mistake of thinking that his past with the mobster and the friendship between their kids would get him a pass on the massive debt he accumulated. Even though Dunmire and Scatino couldn't be more different, Patrick executes both roles with incredible ease, which essentially showcases his underrated range and versatility as a performer.

Chris Caldovino stars in Tulsa King as Dennis "Goodie" Carangi, a former high-ranking member of the Invernizzi crime family who joined Manfredi's crew as a consigliere and de facto second-in-command after the General severed ties with the Family. Caldovino delivers a powerhouse performance as Goodie, who is known for his unwavering loyalty, cheeky humor, and quick wit, and has since become a recurring fan favorite. Before he became the General's right-hand man, Caldovino briefly appeared in The Sopranos as Phil Leotardo's (Frank Vincent) younger brother, Billy Leotardo.

In Season 5, Episode 3, "Where's Johnny?," viewers meet Billy Leotardo, who is an associate of the Lupertazzi crime family as well as his older brother's partner in crime. As tensions continue to rise between New York families, Tony's cousin, Tony Blundetto (Steve Buscemi), seeks revenge after his mentor, Angelo Garepe (Joe Santos), is murdered by the Leotardo brothers in retaliation, and ends up killing Billy, essentially dragging Tony and his crew into the chaos. Caldovino appeared in a total of nine episodes of The Sopranos, and even though it would have been nice if he stuck around a little longer, it's great to see him front and center in Tulsa King.

Best known for his breakout role as the charming Charles "Lucky" Luciano in Boardwalk Empire, Vincent Piazza stars in Tulsa King as Vince Antonacci, a former capo turned consigliere in the Invernizzi crime family who initially views Manfredi as a threat to his territory after being released from prison and is a driving force behind sending the General out West. Since establishing himself in Tulsa, Antonacci has landed himself on The General's bad side and has been a constant pain in his ass, operating as the middleman between Manfredi and New York's Quiet Ray Renzetti (James Russo). The majority of people might think Piazza followed Winter from Boardwalk Empire, but only die-hard Sopranos fans know that isn't the case.

Piazza made his debut in Season 6 of The Sopranos as Hernan O'Brien, a wealthy and reckless friend of AJ Soprano's (Robert Iller) who gets the mob boss's son involved in the party scene and plenty of trouble. He made his first appearance in Season 6, Episode 8, "Johnny Cakes," appearing in a total of three episodes, with Season 6, Episode 13, "Soprano Home Movies," being his last. In fairness, Piazza was pretty young when he appeared in The Sopranos, and since he only briefly appeared in several episodes, it's easy to understand how some might forget (or not even recognize) that he was in the groundbreaking series.
