Lacey Baker breaking barriers for women in skateboarding

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Lacey Baker is a professional skateboarder who has spent her career challenging expectations and fighting for greater recognition of women in a sport traditionally dominated by men. For her, skateboarding is more than a competition or a way to earn a living; it represents freedom, creativity, individuality, and the ability to express herself without limitations. She believes women in skateboarding continue to face an invisible glass ceiling, something she has personally experienced throughout her career. Her journey began when she was just two years old, while she was in foster care. Watching her foster brothers skate on a miniature ramp immediately captured her imagination, and she quickly became fascinated by the sport. What started as childhood curiosity developed into a lifelong passion. By the age of eleven, she was already competing, steadily building her skills and establishing herself in the skateboarding community. Years of dedication eventually brought her to the 2014 X Games in Austin, where she won a gold medal at the age of 22. That achievement demonstrated her talent on an international stage, but it also highlighted the larger challenges women continued to face within professional skateboarding.

According to Baker, women in skateboarding often exist within a smaller subculture of an already distinctive sporting community. Despite their talent, dedication, and willingness to compete at the highest level, female skateboarders have historically received less attention and fewer opportunities from parts of the skate industry. One of the clearest examples is the difference in prize money offered to male and female competitors. Baker describes a competition series that had only recently begun including women, yet the financial rewards remained dramatically unequal. While male competitors could receive $200,000 for first place, female competitors were offered just $30,000. She believes this difference is difficult to justify when women train just as hard, demonstrate comparable commitment, and dedicate enormous amounts of time to improving their performance. For Baker, equality means more than simply allowing women to participate in competitions. It also means providing them with fair opportunities, financial recognition, professional support, and the same respect afforded to male athletes. She believes that if the skateboarding community genuinely wants equality, women cannot always wait for existing organisations to create opportunities for them. Instead, they must take the initiative and build the spaces they want to see.

One example of this approach is Meow Skateboards, a skater-owned company created to support women in skateboarding. Baker contributes by designing skateboard graphics for herself and other female riders, helping create a brand that reflects the creativity and identity of the community it represents. What makes the company particularly meaningful to her is that it is built by skateboarders for skateboarders, rather than being controlled by large corporate interests. It provides women with an opportunity to support one another, develop their own creative identities, and participate in an industry where they have not always received equal recognition. Through projects like this, Baker demonstrates that meaningful change can begin with small, independent initiatives that give people greater control over their own opportunities. Ultimately, she believes that even if her professional skateboarding career does not continue forever, the sport itself will always remain part of her identity. Skateboarding has given her a sense of belonging, freedom, and confidence that goes beyond medals, competitions, or financial rewards. Her philosophy is rooted in being authentic, expressing individuality, and refusing to let other people's expectations determine what she can achieve. For Lacey Baker, skateboarding should be a space where everyone can participate, create, and enjoy the freedom to be themselves.
