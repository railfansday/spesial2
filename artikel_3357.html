All the Country Stars in Taylor Swift’s CMA Awards Performance, Explained

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

We already knew that when Taylor Swift returned to the CMAs for the first time in 13 years, the night was going to be packed with easter eggs. What we didn’t know was that her performance would be far more than just that, filled with appearances from country stars who have all played an important role in her storied career since it kicked off in Nashville with her self-titled debut in 2006.

Swift began the performance of her nominated song “I Knew It, I Knew You” in her dressing room (with outfits from her Taylor Swift era scattered across the room, mind you) before making her way backstage at the Bridgestone Arena en route to the stage. Here, we walk through all the artists she encountered along the way.

As Swift rounded the corner from her dressing room, there he was: Brad Paisley, polishing a handful of CMA Awards. Paisley had already made a surprise appearance earlier in the night to join Lainey Wilson for a musical monologue, but his significance in Swift’s country music story is substantial. Swift opened for Paisley on his Bonfires & Amplifiers Tour in 2007, where he apparently played a prank on the young artist, releasing crickets on her tour bus. “For months, I couldn’t sleep,” Swift said in October when she was inducted into the Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame. Clearly there’s no, er, bad blood remaining.

Up next in Swift’s stroll were Little Big Town, the foursome of Karen Fairchild, Kimberly Schlapman, Phillip Sweet, and Jimi Westbrook. Little Big Town had a hit in 2016 with a version of Swift’s song “Better Man,” which was originally crafted for Red (Swift eventually recorded her own take of the solo write as a vault track). “I’m grateful for her friendship through the years,” Fairchild told Rolling Stone, “and we love her so much. She found her better man.”

Swift did a little dance with Kristian Bush and Jennifer Nettles of Sugarland, a duo with whom she has a longstanding relationship. Swift opened for Sugarland in 2007, and even owns a signed copy of their debut EP, Premium Quality Tunes. A decade later, when Swift was looking for someone to sing a song she’d written, “Babe,” she called up the twosome. They ended up recording it for their album Bigger. Swift also served as a guest on the track, even appearing in the dramatic music video. Swift grabbed a burgundy cowboy hat from Brooks & Dunn as she passed them in the hall, a duo she would later honor with a speech when she presented them with the Willie Nelson Lifetime Achievement Award. “That’s the thing about Brooks & Dunn,” Swift said. “They don’t take very much too seriously, except for all the important stuff: their families, their fans, and country music.” Swift is clearly a fan of Kix Brooks and Ronnie Dunn, covering songs like their Number One hit “Ain’t Nothing ‘Bout You” live.

That burgundy hat? Swift didn’t keep it for long, dropping it on the head of CMA Awards host Wilson. The two artists posed on the red carpet together before the show in complementary yellow dresses (an easter egg? Maybe!), and have performed together before, sharing the stage for a version of Swift’s “Love Story” at her husband Travis Kelce’s Tight End University event earlier this year. Wilson also sung the same Swift classic at her induction into the Nashville Songwriter’s Hall of Fame.

The next cameo is one drenched in a bit of Swiftian lore: Eric Church. Swift popped on a pair of aviators as she passed the Chief, bumping fists as he leaned against a column. One of Swift’s biggest opportunities early in her career was opening for Rascal Flatts, a gig she snagged only after Church, who refused to conform his set to the band’s time requirements, was fired. Though he may not be as recognizable as the others — at least outside of Nashville, where he’s revered as country music royalty — the moment of “Whispering Bill” Anderson handing Swift her guitar was truly touching, and loaded with lore. The family friend and Country Music Hall of Famer invited Swift to play at his fan club party in 2004 when she was just a teenager, sharing the stage at the Grand Ole Opry in 2010 where Anderson is currently the longest standing member. This move may be the most symbolic of all when it comes to the potential of Swift returning to her country music roots, and one that shows how deep her love and knowledge of the genre really runs. The duo blowing each other a kiss was, well, the chef’s kiss of it all.

When Swift finally took the stage for her grand return to the CMA Awards, she wasn’t going to do it alone. Keith Urban, on his trusty ganjo, joined Swift for the last few bars of “I Knew It, I Knew You” before launching into “Our Song,” which she also performed at the CMAs in 2007. Urban and Swift go back to 2009, when she opened his tour, and he also appears on Fearless (Taylor’s Version), lending vocals to “That’s When” and “We Were Happy.” Urban was more than content to just be a supportive player for his old friend, picking at his instrument and hopping dutifully through the dance moves. Are all of these reunions a tribute to her past, or a hint to her future? Knowing Swift, it’s surely some version of both.
