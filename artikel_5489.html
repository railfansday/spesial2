From Hamilton to Salt Lake City: 10 NHL franchises that changed cities

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Free daily briefing on global business news.

These 10 NHL teams that moved left cities such as Hamilton, Quebec City and Hartford, and here is where each franchise started and where it ended up

A hockey franchise is not always tied to the city on its jersey. Several current NHL clubs began life somewhere else, often under a different name and always in a different arena. The New Jersey Devils started in Kansas City, Missouri. The Calgary Flames started in Atlanta. The Dallas Stars started in Minnesota. Tracing those paths shows how much of the league's map has changed since its early decades.

Some cities appear on both sides of the ledger. Winnipeg lost its original Jets to Arizona in 1996. It then gained a different franchise from Atlanta in 2011, and that team took the Jets name. Atlanta has lost two NHL teams, the Flames in 1980 and the Thrashers in 2011. Denver hosted the Colorado Rockies from 1976 to 1982 and later gained the franchise that became the Colorado Avalanche. Minnesota lost the North Stars to Dallas in 1993. It received a new team, the Wild, which began play in 2000.

This guide to NHL teams that moved covers a selection of 10 relocations, not every move the league has had. Each slide names the city a franchise left, the city it went to and when the move happened. It also traces what happened to the team afterward, including any Stanley Cup won in its new home.

A few patterns run through these NHL franchise relocations. Three of the clubs — the Quebec Nordiques, the original Winnipeg Jets and the Hartford Whalers — entered the NHL in 1979 from the World Hockey Association. All three later left their original markets in the 1990s. Several franchises that changed cities won a championship after the move, including the Avalanche in their first season in Colorado. One franchise, the California Golden Seals, moved to Cleveland as the Barons and merged with the Minnesota North Stars in 1978. Another, the Kansas City Scouts, moved twice before settling in New Jersey.

The slides run in chronological order of the moves. The list starts with the Hamilton Tigers, whose players went on strike over pay in 1925. The franchise was then sold and became the New York Americans. It ends with the 2024 move of the Arizona Coyotes' hockey operations to Salt Lake City. The team played the 2024-25 season there as the Utah Hockey Club, and the Utah Mammoth name was announced in 2025. In between are moves from the 1970s, 1980s, 1990s and 2010s.

The NHL franchise based in Hamilton, Ontario, moved to New York in 1925 and became the New York Americans. The move followed a pay strike by the Hamilton Tigers players and their suspension by the league.

The Tigers joined the NHL in 1920. Hamilton sits in southern Ontario, at the western end of Lake Ontario. The club played five seasons in the city before a labor dispute ended its stay.

The dispute was about wages. In 1925 the Tigers players went on strike over their pay. The NHL responded by suspending the players who had walked out.

The franchise was then sold to New York interests. That sale took the club out of Canada and into the U.S.

In its new city, the franchise took the name New York Americans. The team played its home games at Madison Square $SQ Garden in Manhattan.

The Americans stopped operating in 1942, 17 years after the club left Hamilton. The franchise did not relocate again or resume play under another name. No current NHL club traces its history back to the Tigers.

That ending sets this move apart from many other NHL teams that changed cities. The Calgary Flames and the Dallas Stars, for example, still play under the names they took on after relocating.

Hamilton has not had an NHL team since the Tigers left in 1925. The city's time in the league is limited to those five seasons in the early 1920s.

The New Jersey Devils began in Kansas City, Missouri, as the Scouts in 1974, moved to Denver as the Colorado Rockies in 1976 and relocated to New Jersey in 1982. Each move brought a new name. As a result, the club's history runs through three identities.

The Scouts entered the NHL as an expansion team in 1974. They played two seasons in Kansas City before the franchise left Missouri. That made the Missouri chapter the shortest of the franchise's three stops in the NHL.

Denver became the franchise's second home in 1976. The team took the name Colorado Rockies there and played six seasons in the city. That stay was longer than the one in Kansas City, but it also ended in a relocation.

The second relocation came in 1982. The franchise moved east and became the New Jersey Devils. Its new base was the Meadowlands in East Rutherford, New Jersey. The move took the club from the Mountain West into the New York metropolitan area.

The Rockies are not the same franchise as today's Colorado Avalanche. The two clubs share a state but not a history. Each keeps its own records.

The Devils won the Stanley Cup in 1995, in the franchise's 13th season in New Jersey. The club had needed more than a decade in its third city to reach that point. Two more championships followed, in 2000 and 2003. All three came within a span of nine seasons. That stretch remains the only period in which the franchise has won the Cup.

None of those titles came during the Kansas City or Denver years, which covered the franchise's first eight seasons. Every championship in the club's history belongs to the New Jersey era. The two earlier cities produced a name change each, while the Stanley Cup arrived only after the second move.

The NHL franchise that began in Oakland, California, in 1967 moved to Cleveland in 1976 and became the Barons. Two years later, in 1978, the Barons merged with the Minnesota North Stars and stopped playing as a separate club.

The franchise entered the league in 1967 as an expansion team. It was first called the California Seals. It later took the name California Golden Seals, which it carried until it left the Bay Area.

The Minnesota North Stars also joined the NHL as an expansion team in 1967. They played in Bloomington, Minnesota. The two clubs that eventually combined had entered the league in the same year.

The 1976 relocation took the franchise from Northern California to Ohio. It arrived with a new name and played in Cleveland as the Barons. That made it one of two NHL teams that changed cities in 1976. The Kansas City Scouts moved to Denver that same year and became the Colorado Rockies.

The Barons lasted in Cleveland until the 1978 merger. After that, the franchise no longer existed as its own club. Its history continued inside the Minnesota organization rather than under the Barons name. Cleveland has not had an NHL team since then.

That ending sets this franchise apart from most of the NHL teams that changed cities in this selection. The others in the list either still play in their new homes or kept operating for many years after relocating. This one moved once and then folded into another club.

From its 1967 start in Oakland to the 1978 merger, the franchise played 11 seasons as its own club. It spent nine of those seasons in Oakland and two in Cleveland.

Any link between the old Seals and a current NHL team runs through that merger. The North Stars, the club that absorbed the Barons, later left Minnesota for Texas and became the Dallas Stars.

The Atlanta Flames moved from Atlanta to Calgary, Alberta, in 1980, and the franchise kept its name in the new city. The result was the Calgary Flames, who went on to win the Stanley Cup in 1989 against the Montreal Canadiens.

The franchise entered the NHL in 1972. It was one of two expansion teams added to the league that year. The other was the New York Islanders.

In Atlanta, the Flames played home games at the Omni Coliseum. The downtown arena opened in 1972, the same year the team arrived.

The name had roots in Georgia history. It referred to the burning of Atlanta in 1864, during the Civil War, when Union forces under William Tecumseh Sherman burned much of the city.

That historical reference did not travel to Alberta, but the name did. The team's logo changed from a flaming letter A to a flaming letter C.

Calgary had hosted major professional hockey before the Flames arrived. The Calgary Cowboys played in the World Hockey Association from 1975 to 1977. The relocated Flames gave the city its first NHL team.

The club's first Calgary home was the Stampede Corral, an older arena on the city's Stampede grounds. In 1983 the team moved into the Olympic Saddledome. The building was constructed in part for the 1988 Winter Olympics, which Calgary hosted. It was later renamed the Scotiabank Saddledome.

The Flames reached the Stanley Cup Final in 1986 and lost to Montreal. Three years later, the same two teams met again in the Final.

This time Calgary won. The Flames clinched the 1989 championship in Game 6 at the Montreal Forum. They became the only visiting team to win the Stanley Cup on Forum ice against the Canadiens.

Defenseman Al MacInnis received the Conn Smythe Trophy as the most valuable player of the playoffs. Veteran forward Lanny McDonald scored in the deciding game. It was the final game of his NHL career.

The Minnesota North Stars left Bloomington, Minnesota, for Dallas in 1993 and became the Dallas Stars. The franchise dropped "North" from its name and carried the Stars identity to Texas.

The North Stars joined the NHL in 1967 as an expansion team. Bloomington, a suburb of Minneapolis, was their home. The club played 26 seasons in Minnesota before the move.

The franchise also absorbed another club that had changed cities. In 1978 the Cleveland Barons, which began in Oakland, California, merged with the North Stars. Through that merger, the Dallas Stars' lineage connects to two of the relocations in this list.

Dallas won the Stanley Cup in 1999. The Stars beat the Buffalo Sabres in the final. The title came in the franchise's sixth season in Texas. It was the club's first championship under either name.

Minnesota went seven seasons without an NHL team. The gap ended when the Wild, a new franchise, took the ice.

The Wild do not carry the North Stars' records. That history moved to Texas with the team. The 1967 expansion club and today's Dallas Stars are one franchise under two names.

The Quebec Nordiques left Quebec City for Denver after the 1994-95 season and became the Colorado Avalanche. The renamed team won the Stanley Cup in its first season in Colorado.

The Nordiques did not begin in the NHL. The club played in the World Hockey Association before the NHL admitted it in 1979.

Quebec City hosted the franchise for 16 NHL seasons, from 1979-80 through 1994-95. That run puts Quebec City among the former homes of current NHL teams.

The move came through a sale. A new ownership group bought the franchise and took it to Denver. There the team adopted the Avalanche name.

The change of city and owners did not create a new franchise. The club the NHL admitted from the WHA in 1979 is the same club that plays in Denver today. Its NHL history runs from Quebec City to Colorado without a break.

The 1996 Stanley Cup Final matched Colorado against the Florida Panthers. The Avalanche won all four games of the series.

That sweep gave the franchise its first Stanley Cup. The Nordiques' 16 NHL seasons in Quebec City had ended without one. The title arrived as soon as the franchise reached Denver.

The original Winnipeg Jets left Manitoba for Arizona in 1996 and became the Phoenix Coyotes. The move ended 17 NHL seasons in Winnipeg. That run began when the club entered the league from the World Hockey Association in 1979.

The franchise's history started before its NHL years. The Jets were a founding member of the WHA, which began play in 1972 as a rival major league. That year, the Jets signed Bobby Hull away from the Chicago Black Hawks. Hull was one of the NHL's top scorers at the time. The Jets went on to win the WHA championship, the Avco World Trophy, three times, in 1976, 1978 and 1979.

The WHA shut down after the 1978-79 season, and the NHL absorbed four of its clubs. Winnipeg entered the league alongside the Edmonton Oilers, the Quebec Nordiques and the Hartford Whalers. The Whalers had played as the New England Whalers in the rival league.

In the NHL, the Jets played home games at Winnipeg Arena. The team took Dale Hawerchuk with the first overall pick in the 1981 NHL Entry Draft. The center won the Calder Trophy as the league's top rookie in 1982. In the 1992-93 season, Teemu Selänne scored 76 goals for Winnipeg, which remains the NHL record for a rookie.

Phoenix had never had an NHL team before the Coyotes arrived. The relocated club opened in downtown Phoenix at America West Arena, a venue built for the NBA's Phoenix Suns. The Coyotes shared that building with the Suns until 2003.

The team then left downtown for a suburban arena in Glendale, Arizona, which opened in 2003. That building carried several sponsor names over the years, including Jobing.com Arena and Gila River Arena. The franchise also changed its own name in 2014, becoming the Arizona Coyotes.

Winnipeg's current Jets, who began play there in 2011, are a separate franchise with their own history.

The Hartford Whalers left Hartford, Connecticut, for North Carolina in 1997 and became the Carolina Hurricanes. The franchise spent two seasons in Greensboro, settled in Raleigh in 1999 and won the Stanley Cup in 2006.

The club's roots are in the World Hockey Association, not the NHL. It began play in that league in 1972 as the New England Whalers, based first in Boston before moving to Hartford. The Whalers won the WHA's first championship in 1973.

The team entered the NHL in 1979, when the WHA ceased operations and four of its clubs joined the older league. It took the name Hartford Whalers. Its home rink was the Hartford Civic Center in downtown Hartford.

Peter Karmanos, the team's owner, moved the franchise south after the 1996-97 season. Hartford has not had an NHL team since the Whalers left.

The Hurricanes did not open in their permanent home. Their new arena in Raleigh was still under construction when the team arrived. For their first two seasons, they played at the Greensboro Coliseum in Greensboro, North Carolina.

The team moved to Raleigh in 1999, when the new arena opened that fall. The franchise has played its home games in Raleigh ever since.

Carolina reached the Stanley Cup Final in 2002 and lost to the Detroit Red Wings. The team returned to the Final in 2006 and beat the Edmonton Oilers in seven games. The deciding game was played in Raleigh.

That series matched two former WHA clubs. Edmonton had joined the NHL in the same 1979 merger that brought in the Whalers. Cam Ward, a rookie goaltender, won the Conn Smythe Trophy as the most valuable player of the playoffs. Rod Brind'Amour captained the team.

The Hurricanes have also acknowledged the franchise's Hartford years. Starting in the 2018-19 season, the team has worn Whalers throwback jerseys for occasional home games.

The Atlanta Thrashers moved to Winnipeg in 2011 and became the current Winnipeg Jets. True North Sports & Entertainment bought the franchise that year and relocated it to Manitoba. The new owners gave the team the Jets name, which the city's earlier NHL club had used.

The Thrashers began play in 1999 as an expansion team. That gave Atlanta its second NHL franchise, after the Flames. The club took its name from the brown thrasher, the state bird of Georgia. Home games were at Philips Arena in downtown Atlanta. The building opened the same year the team debuted.

The Thrashers had several notable players during their 12 seasons. The club drafted Ilya Kovalchuk first overall in the 2001 NHL draft. Dany Heatley won the Calder Trophy as the league's top rookie in 2002. The team's postseason history was brief. The Thrashers won the Southeast Division in 2006-07 and made their only playoff appearance that spring. The New York Rangers swept them in the first round.

The 2011 sale brought the NHL back to Winnipeg. True North, led by chairman Mark Chipman with backing from David Thomson, moved the team into the downtown arena then known as MTS Centre. The building is now called Canada Life Centre.

The Jets name carried over, but the franchise history did not. The current Jets and the original Jets are separate franchises. The team playing in Winnipeg today traces its records to Atlanta in 1999. It does not claim the history of the club that previously wore the same name in the same city.

The relocated team spent its first two seasons in the Southeast Division, the grouping the Thrashers had played in. The league's 2013-14 realignment moved Winnipeg to the Central Division. That placed the Jets with teams such as the Minnesota Wild and the Chicago Blackhawks.

In 2018, the Jets reached the Western Conference Final, where the Vegas Golden Knights eliminated them. That run was the furthest the franchise had advanced in the playoffs in either city.

The Arizona Coyotes' hockey operations moved from Tempe, Arizona, to Salt Lake City in 2024. The NHL approved the sale of those operations to Smith Entertainment Group that year, and the team's players went with them to Utah.

The club's last Arizona home was a college building. In its final seasons in the state, the team played at Mullett Arena in Tempe, which Arizona State University owns. The arena is also home to the university's Sun Devils men's hockey program.

The transaction covered the team's hockey operations. The players moved with that side of the organization to Salt Lake City.

Smith Entertainment Group is the company led by Ryan Smith, and it also owns the NBA's Utah Jazz. In Salt Lake City, the hockey team began playing at the Delta Center, the downtown arena the Jazz already used. The move gave Utah its first NHL team.

The team played its first season in Utah, 2024-25, as the Utah Hockey Club. That name identified the club by its state and its sport, with no nickname attached.

A nickname followed the next year. The Utah Mammoth name was announced in 2025.
