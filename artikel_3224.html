A Recession Is Coming Eventually. Here's Warren Buffett's Best Investing Advice.

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

The U.S. hasn't had a real recession since the Great Recession of 2008.

Other than a two-month recession during the brunt of the COVID-19 pandemic, the economy has not experienced a recession since the Great Recession in 2008, which wiped out trillions of capital and will not soon be forgotten.

However, given that it's now been nearly 20 years since the Great Recession and there is mounting pressure on consumers, some investors are anxious, believing it's only a matter of time.

While it's difficult to time, a recession is certainly coming eventually. Here's Warren Buffett's best investing advice to cope with a recession.

I don't say that a recession is coming to scare people, but simply because history suggests it's a normal part of the business cycle. In fact, the U.S. economy is long overdue for a recession.

According to Kiplinger, since the end of World War II, the U.S. economy has experienced 12 recessions, averaging one every 6.5 years. It's hard to know why there hasn't been a recession since 2008, but I suspect all of the money printing ordered by the Federal Reserve has at least partly delayed one.

Either way, I don't think the economy has seen its last recession. While recessions are not fun while they are happening, they don't last forever, and the economy and stock market typically emerge stronger on the other side.

Investors should prepare by reviewing prior advice from Warren Buffett, the esteemed former CEO of Berkshire Hathaway. Buffett helped build Berkshire into one of the world's largest companies, and has made many shareholders rich along the way.

During his over six decades at the helm of Berkshire, Buffett steered the company through numerous downturns. One critical piece of advice from the Oracle of Omaha is to avoid panic selling. When a recession hits, many unprepared investors become emotional and fearful, instantly dumping their stocks, even if the long-term business fundamentals are sound.

When the market is struggling, many stocks sell off. But that's not because the companies are going out of business. Rather, investors are racing for the sidelines to try and avoid further losses. Investors should remember that many institutional investors are focused on 12- to 18-month returns. Their portfolios are often judged against the market, which they need to beat to show investors that the high fees they pay to invest with them are worth it.

Large institutional investors account for the majority of flows, so when they sell, stock prices move.

This doesn't mean you should never sell when a stock drops, but make sure you are selling because the long-term fundamental thesis no longer works, and not simply because you are panicking. That's why knowing what you own is critical.

The other great piece of advice for recession investing from Buffett is, "Be fearful when others are greedy and be greedy only when others are fearful." Long-term investors can treat recessions as opportunities, as they offer a unique chance to buy stocks at lower entry points.

If a company's thesis is not busted when the market is selling off, and it has a good management team and a strong enough balance sheet to withstand temporary headwinds, then this is the time to move in and buy the stock at a cheap valuation. One or two bad quarters don't make or break a stock.

Buffett has played contrarian on numerous occasions and been rewarded for his even-tempered patience. For instance, in the three years following the Great Recession in 2008, Buffett made contrarian bets on Goldman Sachs, Bank of America, and GE Aerospace (then known as General Electric), all of which paid off handsomely.

I would caution investors against interpreting this advice as simply buying the dip on any and every stock that falls. Often, stocks trade lower for good reason. Investors must determine why a stock is down, whether the headwinds are temporary, whether a company can survive a sustained economic downturn, and what the long-term prospects are.

But for patient, long-term investors willing to do the work and take the plunge during a market downturn, the rewards are often substantial.
