Cooley’s surprise return produces same result. What do the Mammoth need to change?

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

You know what's worse than a yo-yo? A broken yo-yo.

Both unravel from time to time, but the working one knows how to get itself back into place.

Utah Mammoth goalie Karel Vejmelka is a working yo-yo: He goes down and pops right back up. He gets a shutout one game, lets in five goals the next and bounces back with a net-positive game immediately thereafter, despite losing 3-2 to the league-worst Vancouver Canucks.

The Mammoth, on the other hand, are a broken yo-yo.

Their loss to the Canucks marks their sixth consecutive fumble — and there are plenty of reasons that shouldn't have happened. The Canucks were:

On track to record the worst record of any team in the last 34 seasons (they finished in last place by a wide margin last year, too).

On the second half of a back-to-back traveling back-to-back series.

Starting a goalie who hadn't played yet this season, and who recorded his second-worst save percentage of last season against Utah.

It was a must-win game, and the Mammoth "found a way to lose" it.

"We've got to find ways to win games. Right now, I feel like we're playing well. It's just (that) we're finding ways to lose instead of finding ways to win," said Vincent Trocheck, who scored one of Utah's goals on Monday. "It's just one or two mishaps here and there during a game that are too big."

One of those mishaps on Monday was Clayton Keller's late penalty.

During a Mammoth power play late in the game, where they had the chance to score a go-ahead goal, the captain skated through Canucks defenseman Zeev Buium's stick and popped it out of his hands with a sturdy follow-through.

The result? A penalty to Keller, ending the Mammoth's power play and putting the Canucks a man up shortly thereafter. Canucks forward Brock Boeser capitalized on the opportunity, taking the go-ahead goal for his team.

"It's a tough one," Keller told the Deseret News of the penalty after the game. "I'm just trying to get to my spot on the power play. I'm not, like, trying to fling his stick. ... It's just a weird one. Can't blame anyone for it."

"I think it's a soft call, but I don't think we should put ourselves in that position," he said.

Something needs to change. What exactly that is remains to be figured out.

"It's tough to say," Trocheck said when asked what the solution is. "I mean, I think the broadest message is just, 'continue to stay the course' and 'play the way that we have been playing,' for the most part. I think it's just a little bit more consistency inside of a game. Playing a full 60 minutes. ... But 5-on-5, it felt like it was our game the whole night."

"We can talk about stats — we win the advanced stats every night, but that doesn't matter," he said. "You need to win games. There's no moral victory. Tonight, we found a way to lose."

The one glimmer of hope on the horizon is the reinforcements that should be coming off injured reserve soon.

Tourigny said on Saturday that John Marino is "still a bit away," but "positive." He was a full participant in morning skate on Monday with a full-contact jersey. Dylan Guenther is "on schedule" (two to four weeks from Nov. 11) and the team "should have news soon" on Nick Schmaltz.

Logan Cooley made a surprise return Monday, playing 23:48 — the most of any Mammoth forward — despite the fact that he skated in a non-contact jersey at morning skate prior to the game. He had a scare late in the game, crashing into the end boards at full speed, but 14,000 people collectively exhaled when he came out for the ensuing power play.

That said, he had a somewhat similar crash last season, coincidentally also against the Canucks, where he played a shift on the power play afterwards but went on injured reserve for the next three months.

It's rare that any team has every single player healthy at the same time, but it's a real problem when a team's top three goal scorers and its entire second defense pairing are watching from the press box.

Utah is back in action at the Delta Center on Wednesday, when they'll meet the equally battle-wounded Colorado Avalanche: Nathan MacKinnon and Cale Makar, arguably two of the top three hockey players in the entire world, are both out long-term.

Even without their two best players, the Avalanche probably are better than the last three teams to which the Mammoth have lost. They will not be able to back their way into a win in this one.

It will take all hands on deck. Instead of "finding ways to lose," as both Trocheck and Tourigny put it, they need to find a way to win.

The yo-yo needs to stay raveled up for a while.
