Should the Seahawks hold Quenton Nelson out of the 49ers game?

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

Arguably, the biggest trade deadline acquisition of the 2026 season came when the Seattle Seahawks enticed the Indianapolis Colts to part with their All-World and likely future Hall of Fame LG Quenton Nelson for the measly sum of a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-rounder. This move was supposed to be a shot in the arm for both the pass and run game, and was likely not just a rental even though Nelson is in the final year of his contract. Moving last year's first-round pick Grey Zabel over to RG is a clear sign that Seattle views that lineup as the ideal long term scenario to build a studly OL.

Unfortunately, this new lineup lasted just one game, a rare home Monday Night Football loss to the Chicago Bears 34-17. It was one of the worst games I've seen the Seahawks play under Mike Macdonald, and a big part of that was the offensive line struggles. Sam Darnold was sacked three times, once by new Chicago Bear Jeffrey Simmons who was a problem all night. Zabel's struggles at RG threw some cold water on the plan. I figured there would be growing pains, so it wasn't necessarily a surprise. The theory is that the new lineup would be able to gel over the following two games against the Arizona Cardinals and Las Vegas Raiders, head into the bye feeling good, and be ready for the stretch run.

A sound idea, but during the week following the Bears debacle, it was announced that Nelson had aggravated a previous knee injury that he had with the Colts and needed a procedure. As I write this, Seattle is on its bye and Macdonald has characterized Nelson as "week-to-week" stating that if they had practice over the bye, Nelson would likely not be participating.

Seattle's next game is a huge division matchup in San Francisco, one they need to win to have any hope left to win the NFC West, no matter how slim the chances are. Nelson might not even be healthy enough to play, but even if he is, should the coaching staff hold him off for another week? That probably sounds like a really dumb idea and I should be tarred and feathered for even positing that question. The Seahawks traded draft capital for Nelson, he's one of the best guards in the NFL, and they need to decide whether or not he should be extended. Plus, every game he sits out would mean just postponing the inevitability of Zabel moving back to RG.

Believe me, I get all of that. Yet, maybe it's not so bad to shift that scenario back another week? Seattle's rushing attack has been better of late, with 150 yards or more in two of the last four games and 120+ in three of four, with the only stinker coming against Chicago…when Nelson was playing LG and Zabel at RG. Charles Cross will hopefully be back in the lineup, but backup Josh Jones might need to play RT since Abraham Lucas injured his ankle against the Raiders. Bringing Nelson back would put the starting LT and LG for the last two games at RT and RG. That kind of sounds like a disaster waiting to happen, particularly against a strong San Francisco defense.

When the Seahawks lost to the 49ers in Week 5, they gave up five sacks and turned the ball over 4 times which led directly to 21 points for San Francisco. The starting OL featured Cross, Zabel, Jalen Sundell, a platoon of Christian Haynes/Beau Stephens, and Abraham Lucas. And still Seattle arguably should have won. In this important rematch, swapping Anthony Bradford and Jones in at RG and RT couldn't perform worse than the Week 5 OL did (knock on wood). I don't think I'd feel as confident saying that with the projected lineup if Nelson returns given the results against Chicago.

Maybe letting Nelson get another week under his belt in the system wouldn't be such a bad thing? Seattle would have the best chance short-term to get a victory in Santa Clara. Long-term, insert Nelson into the lineup for the rest of the season and work out the kinks against the ¾ of the NFC East (Dallas Cowboys, New York Giants, and Philadelphia Eagles) before the two matchups against the Los Angeles Rams.

That idea might sound counterintuitive, but it could give Seattle the best chance to win as many games as possible, a necessity if they want to make the playoffs. Survive, advance, and play your best ball at the end of the season. Holding Nelson out until after the 49ers game could be the best way to achieve all of those goals and keep their dreams alive.
