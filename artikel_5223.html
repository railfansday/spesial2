Bitcoin hits four-month low as $820 million liquidated and $12.7 billion short squeeze risk builds

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

A sweeping liquidation wave erased more than $700 million in bullish leveraged positions, leaving traders watching whether Bitcoin can defend the $60,000 region.

Bitcoin fell to its lowest level in four months as an $820 million liquidation wave swept the derivatives market. The leading cryptocurrency fell to $61,196 on Nov. 20 before recovering to $61,901 as of press time, according to CryptoSlate data.

This price performance extended a sell-off that has repeatedly punished traders betting on a market recovery. Data from CoinGlass showed that 170,824 traders were liquidated over 24 hours, with total liquidations reaching $819.57 million.

Bullish positions accounted for $712.33 million, or nearly 87% of the total, while short liquidations reached $107.24 million.

Bitcoin accounted for $221.86 million in liquidations, followed by Ethereum at $122.49 million. The selling extended to derivatives tied to traditional assets, including gold, which recorded $62.53 million in liquidations.

The largest individual liquidation occurred on Bybit, where a BTCUSD position worth $9.26 million was forcibly closed.

The market turmoil has also triggered a sharp increase in expected volatility.

Block Scholes' 30-day Bitcoin Implied Volatility Index (BSIV) climbed to 51.6%, up 8.4 percentage points over the past week. Ethereum's equivalent reading reached 66.4%, while Solana's rose to 77.7%, reflecting growing expectations of sharp price swings across major cryptocurrencies.

Meanwhile, Bitcoin's 30-day at-the-money implied volatility reached 45.8%, its highest level since June, as options traders repriced risk after the latest downturn.

Notably, the pressure has also extended beyond derivatives markets.

US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) recorded another $582.3 million in net outflows Thursday, bringing November's cumulative withdrawals to approximately $3.47 billion.

These continued ETF withdrawals have weakened a major source of Bitcoin demand at a time when leveraged traders are also being forced to reduce exposure.

Bitcoin's latest decline has coincided with broader weakness in financial markets, as investors have sold technology stocks and traditional safe-haven assets.

Gold has declined for five consecutive sessions, according to Bitunix, with weekly losses potentially exceeding 9%.

The exchange's analysts attributed the simultaneous weakness in Bitcoin and gold to persistent inflation, elevated borrowing costs, and increasing demand for cash as investors reduce leverage.

However, he suggested that monetary tightening could be approaching its peak, pointing to changes in bond yields and market expectations as signs that investors are beginning to anticipate an eventual policy shift.

He identified Bitcoin as a potential early indicator of changing liquidity conditions, given its sensitivity to monetary policy and investors' risk appetite.

Against that backdrop, Bitcoin's derivatives market is showing signs of a significant shift in positioning, with leveraged bullish bets being cleared while bearish exposure accumulates above prevailing prices.

James Lavish, co-managing partner of Bitcoin Opportunity Fund, said the sustained decline had effectively wiped out accumulated monthly leveraged long exposure.

“Literally every single ounce of monthly leveraged longs in bitcoin has been blown out, and $12.7 billion of leveraged shorts are now stacked above.”

The buildup raises the possibility of a sharp reversal if Bitcoin recovers, as rising prices could force bearish traders to close their positions and accelerate buying pressure.

However, the immediate market structure still favors sellers.

Analysts at Bitunix said Bitcoin's open interest declined 2.7%, indicating traders continued to reduce leveraged exposure. Both spot and futures cumulative volume delta (CVD), a measure of buying and selling activity, showed net selling, with aggressive sell orders accounting for approximately 51% of trading activity.

The exchange's analysts also identified concentrated bearish positioning on Hyperliquid, where the Super Printing Machine trading group reportedly holds $778 million in Bitcoin short positions against $94.03 million in longs.

The imbalance creates additional short-squeeze potential if prices recover, although weaker spot demand and declining open interest suggest that buying pressure has yet to return convincingly.

For now, Bitunix identified buying interest between $61,250 and $61,400, with another demand zone around $60,550 to $60,600.

This means a sustained break below $61,250 could expose Bitcoin to further declines toward $60,000. Conversely, a recovery above $62,265 to $62,720, accompanied by improving buying activity, could begin to put pressure on accumulated bearish positions.
