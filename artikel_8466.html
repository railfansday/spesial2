Well... I'm not here to argue with you. If you're happy with your MA plan, then I'm happy that you're happy.

I am not on Medicare myself, so there is no "my" plan. But based on what my parents' and in laws' generation's experience-- MA plans vary a lot in quality, and your experience when you're relatively young and healthy may not necessarily carry forward to when you're older and sicker. It's worth remembering how the finances of MA work: MA plans get a risk-adjusted, locality-adjusted per capita fee for each person enrolled. They are then required to spend at least 85% of that on direct healthcare, prescription drugs, and "healthcare related expenses". That's where the money for your rebates, additional coverage (like medical or dental), and perks like gym memberships comes from. Put another way, every dollar that pays for those perks, is a dollar that's not being spent on actual medical procedures or drugs.

MA plans are required to cover emergency and urgent care while you're out of area, yes. But they are NOT required to cover anything else, and I mean, $0 coverage is permitted. Some MA plans will cover some out of network care, at some level of co-payment; but that's an optional perk. Likewise, lower OOP limits are a perk that some MA plans offer, but by no means all of them.

MA plans aren't actually required to cover drugs at all, let alone cover "all approved" drugs. Those that do cover prescriptions, will have a formulary which covers all required classes of drugs, but not every approved drug. They also can require you use their pharmacy chain, require preauthorization, step therapy, use of generics, limited quantities, and other rules. All drug plans are going to do this, but the specifics of your plan's formulary and how strict they are on cost control measures will make a huge difference in how you, specifically, experience it. The kicker is, with standard Medicare, you have your choice of Part D plans-- with MA, you take what they bundle with your plan.

MA plans are allowed to change their provider networks at any time-- not just at the end of the year. This has happened to multiple relatives of mine, and it can be very disruptive. The plans also may only have a single provider accepting new patients or a single practice for a specialty (like radiology, say). That's not just a problem with rural areas-- again, I have relatives struggling with this in an above average income suburb right now.

If you are disapproved for a drug or procedure, you have rights of appeal... but if you are slow-rolled, or given a complicated bureaucratic process to follow, or given exactly one possible option-- that's a lot harder to address. (My experience with CMS, advocating for my parents and in laws, has not been as happy as yours appears to have been.)

MA is often cheaper than Part B + Medigap + Part D... but not necessarily, if you have serious or chronic conditions, and may have significant limitations on where/how you get your care. Some MA plans are excellent, but I personally hear some "buyers remorse" from relatives as well.
