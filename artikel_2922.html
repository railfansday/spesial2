Fantasy Football Rankings: Players Hayden Winks is higher and lower on than consensus for Week 11

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Fantasy football analyst Hayden Winks shares the players he's higher — and lower — on than Yahoo consensus for Week 11.

These rankings will be tinkered with all the way until kickoff, so please check back in on our weekly rankings pages and Sunday AM sit/start show for the absolute latest.

These blurbs are also in The Blueprint, which is my weekly rankings and analysis column. The RB version is out on Wednesdays, while the WR version and the QB/TE/K/DEF version are each out on Thursdays before Thursday Night Football.

Bo Nix has more games below 10.0 fantasy points (three) than he does above 20.0 (two) so far. It's largely the same issues as in previous seasons, but to his credit, things historically have looked way better in the second half of seasons. I haven't noticed that he's a worse athlete coming off the dislocated ankle. It's more of just decision-making inside the pocket, but hopefully Sean Payton got into his head during this bye week. Hopefully Jaylen Waddle was listening, too!

To be fair, Nix has had the third-hardest schedule to date and the easiest schedule moving forward. This week kicks that off. The Raiders have been horrific on defense in both phases. They've allowed the second-most fantasy points to QBs and the second-most rushing yards to all positions. That's difficult to do! Nix has been the QB17, QB23, QB8 and QB8 against the Raiders historically, but this defense's current 6-game streak of allowing a top-13 fantasy QB is more important. Denver is set up well as 10-point home favorites with a 25.25-point team total (eighth).

Tate (shoulder) has been limited in practice after missing the last handful of games. This assumes he's ready to roll, while Wan'Dale Robinson was placed on IR and Calvin Ridley was demoted. It'll be Tate, Elic Ayomanor and Chimere Dike in 3-WR sets moving forward. That's not much of a competition for Tate, who has lived up to his NFL draft status to me. He can win on breaking routes and has great hands. His skill set stands to benefit from the changes the Titans have made offensively with Tate banged up:

Over his past four games, Tate is the WR25 on WR22 usage and WR20 targets per game. In good matchups, he can be viewed as a boom-bust WR2 now. The Cowboys are a great matchup. They've allowed the sixth-most fantasy points to WRs, while struggling to defend Keenan Allen and Deebo Samuel Sr. last week as the latest example. Michael Wilson is the closest real-life comp to Tate, and he hung 128-1 on them as the WR4 three games ago. Tennessee owns a 23.0-point team total this week, easily their highest of the season.

Hampton set season highs in fantasy points (17.0 half PPR) and usage (15.9 expected) last week, in their first win of the year. Hampton handled 22 carries in a run-heavy game plan that continued because the scoreboard finally allowed for it. The Chargers are first in neutral run rate (61%), so don't be surprised if Hampton's fantasy performances tick up if they can stay more competitive after a brutal schedule. You can see the extra carries in the second half of last week here:

On tape, Hampton was used slightly differently last week. He handled nine pitches, which is the second-most by a player in a single game all year. Instead of being pointed to the perimeter, many of them got him going straight downhill, where his attributes are maximized. They also ran more man concepts instead of the wide zone stuff OC Mike McDaniel typically calls for. It's a meeting in the middle between player and coach, one that was long overdue.

This week, the Chargers are favored. I repeat, the Chargers are favored against the Jets. They don't have their best run-stopping DT right now either, which has helped opposing teams rack up 154 rushing yards per game this month (fourth). James Cook (RB7) and Kenneth Walker (RB1) just went nuclear against them, but even Ollie Gordon had 103 total yards and a score in Week 7, too. This is easily the best it's looked for Hampton since the games started this year.

Diggs is quietly the WR21 on WR24 usage this season and could hold the line with Terry McLaurin out for the season. He's not a full-time player, nor as quick as he once was, but his game ages gracefully as a crafty player with excellent hands and toughness. Diggs has been manufactured some targets on top of being their most relied-upon underneath and intermediate target.

This is an excellent spot for him with the Bengals expected to put a beating on the Commanders' defense. Game script should be on his side, and he needs these 3-WR sets to get onto the field. On top of that, the Bengals have a fantastic CB1 in D.J. Turner on the perimeter, but Diggs will avoid him and settle into where Cincy has been the worst at defending: the middle. Similar WRs like Chimere Dike (WR8) and Zay Flowers (WR3) have had great games against them recently, while traditional WR1s have struggled. The Commanders project for 23.75 points (11th), too.

Egbuka has been more good than great on tape this year. He battled a toe injury in training camp and then has picked up new injuries along the way. Baker Mayfield said he "looks great right now" after hinting that the injuries have prevented him from taking off earlier this year. For what it's worth, Egbuka is not on the injury report at all right now and that was not the case earlier this year.

Across the entire season, Egbuka is the WR33 on WR19 usage. Those are probably closer to his floor. We'll see if the Bucs going to more Tez Johnson forced Eguka to play outside more, but to put things simply, the Buccaneers are desperate for Egbuka to turn into an alpha. Everyone else doesn't have the skills to do that, and the pass game has been unwatchable. I can argue he is still frequently the designed first read on a bulk of the Bucs' dropbacks. Everyone has to just play better.

The Lions are too injured at all three levels to be nervous about the matchup. The Bucs are unlikely to be in full run the clock out mode like they've been in the past, and we'll see if CB1 D.J. Reed (hip) can suit up. There should be time for whoever is starting at QB to deliver the ball with Aidan Hutchinson out. A lowly 18.25-point team total holds expectations in check. We're chasing volume more than anything.

Lawrence has been in a groove as fantasy's QB8 (20.7 PPG), generating +0.29 EPA per dropback (QB3) with 21 passing touchdowns across nine starts. The only issue at times is not having a ton of volume due to game script, as the Jaguars easily lead the NFL in point differential. They're actually 31st all time in that metric through nine games.

This matchup is expected to be a bit closer, partially because the Giants defense has outkicked expectations. They've still allowed the seventh-most fantasy points to QBs, despite getting breakouts from rookie LB Arvell Reese and CB1 Deonte Banks. They're well coached with solid safety play. Where they've been lit up is against the scramble, as both Tyler Shough and Jayden Daniels have run like crazy. That's unlikely for Lawrence, so a more mediocre output wouldn't be too surprising. Jacksonville travels as 6-point road favorites with an implied team total of 26.25 points (seventh).

Gordon is now playing with fire after ripping off explosives as a new starter back in September. He's down to RB25 on RB13 usage this past month, thanks to 20 carries and just 1.5 receptions per game. His efficiency could crater without Malik Willis, as it did last week when he was the RB18 on RB10 usage. Trickier defenses than the Colts will give Kyle McCord more trouble.

The Bills are waking up on defense with a new DC. They've seen multiple true breakouts from their DL, and they're more sured up at S with Amani Hooker in the mix and moving Cole Bishop more into the box where he's at his best. Even while going through some pains, the Bills are still only allowing 93.6 rushing yards per game (25th). It's a bad matchup for Ollie with a team total of just 14.5 points.

Never get too high or too low on Jamo. He's a boom-bust WR22 just about every season now. This year, Williams is the WR25 on WR31 usage with stretches of WR1 and WR4 play.

This is a matchup to be more concerned with. The Bucs have two great CBs, and Zyon McCollum is just as good an athlete as Williams is. That's partially why the Bucs have allowed the fourth-fewest fantasy points to WRs on 15+ air-yard throws this year. Tampa Bay is a pass funnel defense, but a lot of that is in the Amon-Ra St. Brown and Sam LaPorta zone. It doesn't mean that Williams can't take it to the crib on low volume, but there have been better matchups per-game than this one. The Lions still have a 25.25-point team total (ninth).

Smith is expected to return after a pair of injuries have derailed his 2026 season. Since 2025, Smith is the WR21 on WR25 usage. While he doesn't have A.J. Brown to compete with, he also plays in a bottom-5 offense now. It's hard to calibrate all of the moving pieces, so Smith is a boom-bust WR3 until we have a better clue for how Joe Milton is going to fit in with Smith.

On top of that, the Steelers have an excellent DL that can spoil the entire game. Philly only has a 17.5-point team total, and I totally get it.

Christian Benford (groin) hasn't practiced, but the Bills added S Amani Hooker at the deadline and have a more menacing DL in 2026. It'll be almost entirely zone defense for Douglas to sort through with an inexperienced QB. It'll be a tough spot. To his credit, Douglas has been the WR22 on WR50 usage since returning from injury. He's a boom-bust WR3/4 with better odds of flaming out here.
