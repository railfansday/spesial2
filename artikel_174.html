US admits ‘substantial’ change to extradition request translation for leftist activist

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

DoJ cites three examples of alterations from English into Spanish for James ‘Fergie’ Chambers after Guardian report

US authorities have admitted altering the translation of an extradition request for James “Fergie” Chambers from English into Spanish, attributing violent and illegal actions to the wealthy activist and philanthropist that were not in the original.

Chambers’s case is the first known instance of the US seeking to extradite a citizen over alleged support for Hamas, an accusation apparently tied to an international money-laundering charge in the criminal complaint underpinning the extradition request and donations to organizations helping Palestinians in Gaza.

In a notification sent to Spain’s ministry of justice, the US Department of Justice (DoJ) cites three of the examples of legally significant alterations revealed in the Guardian’s reporting on the issue, calling them “substantial [and] inadvertent”.

The notification arrived to defense attorneys, the prosecutor and a three-judge panel on Tuesday as the judges were deliberating whether to grant the US extradition request, leading the defense team to file a motion calling for a halt in the proceedings and a new translation.

The US has now admitted to three of the alterations detailed in the Guardian’s reporting, and calls the fourth “an acceptable translation”. One of them involves introducing the word “fire” in describing late 2023 protests against Elbit, an Israeli weapons manufacturer. Two of the three charges in the criminal complaint against Chambers are related to the protests – where nothing was set on fire.

Other changes involved adding the Spanish words for “violent” and “detonate” when the original English text did not have them.

According to documents seen by the Guardian, the Trump administration notified Spain of the alterations on 12 November, the day before a hearing involving Chambers’s attorneys, the three judges and the prosecutor – but the notification only reached all parties on Tuesday.

The unusual chain of events caps off weeks of unsuccessful efforts by the defense team to get the high court to pay attention to the issue and order a new translation.

The Guardian’s reporting on the issue was based in part on a 3 November motion that Chambers’s attorneys filed with the court, identifying a dozen alterations to the original criminal complaint underpinning the extradition request.

However, the judges responded to the motion by saying “the translation offered by the requesting country’s authorities should be considered trustworthy” and that no action was required, according to Aitor Martínez, a member of Chambers’s defense team.

Then, during the hearing, the judges denied the defense team’s request to admit into evidence a 345-page report prepared by a certified Spanish translator identifying what were now 30 “incriminating changes” the US had made to a machine-made translation.

The judges also denied the attorneys’ request to present the translator as a witness in the hearing. Meanwhile, the prosecutor dismissed the issue dismissively, referring to “supposed translation errors”.

These and other prejudicial notions with specific legal implications form part of the prosecutor’s report on the case, which in turn influences the judges deciding on the extradition request, Martínez, a member of Chambers’s defense team, told the Guardian.

“I think the fact that the US recognizes what it has done shows that they were trying to fool the judges,” said Martínez. “This should touch the pride of the Spanish justice system.”

Spanish law and the extradition treaty between the US and Spain require extradition requests to be translated into Spanish by the requesting country. Martínez notes in the defense team’s motion filed on Tuesday that a 2003 extradition request from Turkey wound up being denied due to errors in translation – after defense attorneys took the case to Spain’s constitutional court, alleging violations of due process.

“We might wind up doing the same,” Martínez said.

A self-described communist and anti-imperialist, Chambers is also heir of the Cox family, owners of Atlanta-based Cox Enterprises. He sold stock back to his family worth about $250m in mid-2023 and has been funding an estimated 100 progressive and humanitarian projects in 20 countries since then.

Chambers’s views and donations have been extensively profiled in US media, including his public comments on Israel and Gaza. A week after the 7 October 2023 Hamas attacks on Israel, he posted on X about Israel’s response: “Stop calling it ‘collective punishment’/There’s nothing to be ‘punished’ for/No faction of the Palestinian resistance, Hamas or other, has done *anything* wrong/It’s simply genocide/Like it has been.”

If the court’s decision on the US request is affirmative, Spain’s council of ministers can reverse the outcome. This means Chambers’s extradition may be among the last decisions made under Pedro Sánchez, the socialist prime minister, before national elections on 29 November.
