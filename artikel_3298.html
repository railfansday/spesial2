‘Sunrise On The Reaping’ Epilogue Scene Explained: How Everything Ties Together

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

SPOILER ALERT: This post spoils the end of The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping.

The epilogue scene at the end of The Hunger Games: Sunrise On The Reaping echoes the chapter that closes Suzanne Collins’ novel of the same name.

Woody Harrelson, Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson appear in the final moment of the film, reprising their roles of Haymitch Abernathy, Katniss Everdeen and Peeta Mellark, respectively, from The Hunger Games (2012), The Hunger Games: Catching Fire (2013), The Hunger Games: Mockingjay – Part 1 (2014) and The Hunger Games: Mockingjay – Part 2 (2015).

It was known that Lawrence and Hutcherson would return for the second prequel film. Harrelson could have been a given once they were confirmed, since his character centers the latest movie. In The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, which tells the villain origin story of Coriolanus Snow (Tom Blyth), Donald Sutherland’s voice was used in the end to tie the first prequel film to the original four, in which Sutherland portrayed the poisonous president. The Sunrise scene in particular is meaningful with these three actors behind their roles because it connects the past and present, bridging the gap between The Sunrise on the Reaping and Suzanne Collins’ original trilogy of books plus their film adaptations. Haymitch, in his Quarter Quell Hunger Games, did so much to advance the rebellion that would eventually bring down Panem, the Capitol and the barbaric tradition that killed children ever year. It was Katniss who ultimately gained the momentum from this spark — symbolized by Haymitch’s flint striker and more — to topple the dystopian society and cruel reign of President Snow (Ralph Fiennes in Sunrise on the Reaping and Donald Sutherland in the first four films).

Katniss and Peeta wish Haymitch a happy birthday in the ending scene, symbolizing that he did, in fact, reach a birthday where the sun didn’t rise on another reaping ceremony, which was his love Lenore Dove’s (Whitney Peak) dying wish. It took another 25 years after his games, which doubled the amount of tributes entered as is custom for a Quarter Quell, to overcome the tyranny. The 50th Hunger Games added a wrinkle, or extra rule, to make those games extra gruesome.

By then, Peeta and Katniss, who had already somewhat thwarted the Gamemakers by threatening to each eat poisonous nightlock berries and die for there to be no victor in their 74th games, experienced a terrible Quarter Quell of their own. The 75th Hunger Games — or third Quarter Quell — involved the wrinkle that each pair of district tributes would be reaped from their existing pool of victors.

The Catching Fire film made it seem like Plutarch Heavensbee (Philip Seymour Hoffman) and President Snow (Donald Sutherland) devised that wrinkle themselves to particularly torture Katniss and send her a message about her sedition. In the books, the founders of Panem left a box behind that laid out the rules of each Quarter Quell game, making it seem more like coincidence that this happened when a full-blown rebellion was close at hand.

While preparing for the third Quarter Quell, Katniss and Peeta leaned on Haymitch for his experience in the games in addition to meeting other victors turned mentors turned tributes once more like Mags (Lynn Cohen), Beetee (Jeffrey Wright), Wiress (Amanda Plummer) and others, younger versions of whom played roles in Haymitch’s story before the big Catching Fire revolt as well.

“The seeds of Haymitch Abernathy’s backstory were planted over twenty years ago when I began the original trilogy,” author Suzanne Collins wrote in a letter timed to the film’s release. “In Catching Fire, Katniss and Peeta watch the recap of his Second Quarter Quell games as he silently observes from the shadows. The Capitol had warped the true account and spun reality into vicious propaganda. For Haymitch to tell the truth about what happened would’ve been too dangerous for his tributes and too traumatic for him.”

At the end of the snippet, Haymitch goes to the meadow to feed his flock of geese, which he raises in memory of Lenore Dove, but not before Katniss and Peeta thank him, and he in return thanks them for their teamwork in dismantling the dictatorship they lived under for so long.

As for whether there could be more films in the franchise, it seems like it would come down to Collins and her decision to write another book or two. Fans have been particularly vocal about wanting more of one Finnick Odair’s first Hunger Games, and director Francis Lawrence as well as star Joseph Zada have expressed interest in diving into Plutarch’s (Jesse Plemons) story as well as the lore behind the mysterious District 13, which makes itself known in the Mockingjay books and films. Collins is known for writing these books only when she has something to say and express through them. The final scene at the end of Sunrise on the Reaping could make a great close to the story as is.
