OTD (16/11/1996): Sunderland Lose Their Spurs At The Lane

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

Peter Reid's side got off to a good start in our first season in the top flight since the big rebrand. The FA Carling Premiership was the place to be and, after a surprise promotion that followed a fight against relegation to the third tier, Sunderland were part of it.

After a victory over Coventry City at Roker Park via a Steve Agnew goal, we'd managed to win three of our first seven league games. We did, however, pay a big price to take three points from Ron Atkinson's side that day, because Niall Quinn was lost to a long-term knee injury that would see him miss most of the season.

A few weeks later, we lost our new signing between the sticks when Tony Coton suffered multiple breaks in his leg in a 3-0 defeat at The Dell. With our two big signings out long-term, things looked a little less rosy.

By the time we travelled to White Hart Lane to take on Gerry Francis' Spurs side on this day in 1996, we'd picked up just four points from the previous fifteen on offer and had begun to slide down the table.

Ahead of the game, Reid had a crisis in defence. With Richard Ord suspended and Lee Howey out with an ankle ligament problem, the assumption was that Kevin Ball would move from midfield to slot in alongside Andy Melville. In the end, it was Martin Scott who filled the void in central defence, and Dariusz Kubicki came in at left-back.

Spurs took the initiative in the early stages and, after forcing a good early save from Lionel Perez, it came as no surprise when they took the lead after twelve minutes. Andy Sinton beat Gareth Hall and David Kelly down their left before beating Perez with a low drive that went in off the post.

Less than ten minutes later, Spurs had the ball in the back of the net again, only to see it ruled out for offside, and Sunderland were hanging on. Teddy Sheringham missed a sitter not long after that, but despite their dominance, Ball could have levelled against the run of play but failed to hit the target from six yards out when it looked easier to score.

Reid's side were fortunate to go in at the break just one goal down, and fifteen minutes into the second half we made our lives more difficult when we were reduced to ten men. Paul Stewart was harshly sent off a few weeks earlier at Highbury, but this one was deserved after a two-footed challenge on Colin Calderwood.

With ten minutes left on the clock, the game looked to be over when Kelly tripped David Howells just inside the area and Mike Reed pointed to the spot. Sheringham stepped up but gave Sunderland hope of taking something out of the game when he struck the outside of the post.

Two minutes later, however, Sheringham made no mistake. Darren Anderton crossed from the right and the former Millwall and Nottingham Forest striker doubled the home side's lead.

[Sinton 13', Sheringham 82' – Stewart sent off 57']

Sunderland: Perez, Hall (Rae), Melville, Scott, Kubicki, Kelly, Bracewell, Ball, Gray (Smith), Bridges (Russell), Stewart. Substitutes not used: Preece, Agnew.

Tottenham Hotspur: Walker, Carr, Calderwood, Campbell, Wilson, Anderton (Rosenthal), Howells, Nielson, Sinton, Armstrong, Sheringham. Substitutes not used: Baardsen, Edinburgh, Nethercott, Allen.

Back in the days when it was a thing to announce how much managers had to spend in the market, Bob Murray revealed on this day in 1996 that his manager had £7m available to strengthen the team. It was also reported that this could rise to £12m following the planned flotation a month later.

Sunderland were to be the eighth club to float on the stock exchange, and the hope was that £10-15m would be raised in doing so, although part of the money was to be used to pay off the debts incurred during the building of the new stadium, with Murray stating:

We are taking a £7m turnover business to £20m within two years. That's what we are hoping to do. The stadium is the catalyst for everything.

With the new money burning a hole in Reid's pocket, the first name on the list to spend it on was Israeli international Ronen Harazi. On this day in 1996, Harazi was due to begin a week-long trial at Sunderland and was hoping to follow Eyal Berkovic, who made a similar move to Southampton weeks earlier.

The striker had been recommended by former Sunderland player and Darlington manager David Hodgson, and his club, Beitar Jerusalem, are believed to have wanted £400,000 for the 26-year-old.

Harazi had scored 18 goals in 36 international appearances after replacing Spurs' forward Ronny Rosenthal in the national side. David Pleat at Sheffield Wednesday had also been keeping tabs on the player, as he was pencilled in for a reserve team fixture in midweek, but Harazi seemed confident ahead of his week on Wearside:

I've only got until Friday to prove myself, but I am confident I will make it. There's no problem with my fitness. I was injured a month ago, but I came on as a substitute in Cyprus this week and I am in good shape.
