3 bold predictions for Freddy Peralta with Orioles

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

3 bold predictions for Freddy Peralta with Orioles appeared first on ClutchPoints. Add ClutchPoints as a Preferred Source by clicking here.

The Baltimore Orioles signed Freddy Peralta to a five-year, $125 million contract, the largest deal for a pitcher in franchise history. The right-hander gave the Orioles two very different versions of his 2026 season to evaluate. He posted a 4.99 ERA in 22 starts for the New York Mets, then recorded a 3.25 ERA in 10 starts for the Tampa Bay Rays and won ALCS MVP. Baltimore is betting that his late-season rebound carries into 2027.

Peralta led the National League with 17 wins for the Milwaukee Brewers in 2025. He'll match that total in his first season with Baltimore and finish atop the American League. It's a bold call for a team that won 79 games in 2026, especially with so many starts coming against AL East lineups.

The case starts with how often he takes the ball. Peralta has made at least 30 starts in four straight seasons, giving himself enough chances to pile up wins. Pete Alonso hit 43 home runs last season, and Gunnar Henderson added 24. If Baltimore scores more consistently around them, Peralta should spend more sixth innings pitching with a lead.

His four-seamer averaged 94.3 mph during the 2026 regular season. In October, Peralta sat around 97 and reached 98 or 99. He threw 33 pitches at 97 mph or faster in his second ALCS start. Carrying even part of that jump across six months would give hitters less time to recognize his off-speed pitches.

The WHIP prediction has a separate foundation. Peralta posted a 1.01 mark in 10 regular-season starts with the Rays and a 1.08 over a full season with the Brewers in 2025. At 30, the bigger bet is sustaining the extra velocity. The baserunner target is one he has already met across a full season.

Corbin Burnes gave the Orioles a 2.92 ERA over 194 1/3 innings in 2024. Peralta will give Baltimore that combination of workload and run prevention again. He came close in 2025, recording a 2.70 ERA over 176 2/3 innings.

The challenge is getting deeper into games. Peralta threw 169 innings in 32 starts last season. At the same number of starts, 180 innings would require him to average roughly one additional out each time. Retiring hitters earlier in counts would help him get there without a substantial increase in pitches. Those extra outs matter most when the bullpen has already worked hard earlier in a series.

The Orioles entered the offseason needing a proven starter. If Peralta gives them 180 innings with an ERA below 3.00, he'll have done more than fill a rotation spot. He'll be the pitcher Baltimore wants starting Game 1 if the Orioles get back to October.
