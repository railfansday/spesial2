Anthropic’s IPO Succeeds, But AI Stocks, Bitcoin & Asian Markets Sink – What’s Next

Anthropic's record IPO failed to lift AI sentiment, as Wall Street's sell-off spread across technology stocks and global financial markets.

Asian markets tumbled, with Japan, South Korea and Taiwan facing sharp losses amid continued pressure on semiconductor and AI-linked stocks.

Bitcoin fell below $63,000 as broader risk aversion, ETF outflows and macroeconomic uncertainty weighed on the cryptocurrency market.

The most-awaited Anthropic IPO went live, marking a successful debut, but the broader financial markets offered little sign of enthusiasm. Despite translating into renewed optimism across the technology sector, market sentiment remained under pressure. The US stocks, tech shares and Asian markets recorded sharp declines. South Korea’s KOSPI and Japan’s equity markets were among those caught in the broader sell-off. In the meantime, the Bitcoin price also slipped to $61,500, with the global market cap plunging to $2.13 trillion.

The simultaneous weakness across equities, technology stocks and crypto has put risk appetite back under scrutiny, turning what was a major AI industry event into a backdrop for a broader market downturn.

Anthropic delivered a record-setting IPO, raising approximately $81.3 billion through its public offering and pricing shares at $83 a share, above its initial price range of $70 to $78. The offering was expanded to 980 million shares, reflecting strong investor demand, and valued the Claude developer at approximately $1.5 trillion at the IPO price. The scale of the offering made it the largest stock-market debut on record, surpassing the previous record set by SpaceX.

The enthusiasm was also evident during its first trading session on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. Shares opened at $111.50, roughly 34% above the IPO price, briefly pushing the company’s market value to around $2 trillion. However, the stock later dropped and closed at $97.17, still 17% above its offering price. The debut marked a major milestone for the AI industry, offering investors their first opportunity to gain direct exposure to one of the leading frontier AI developers through public markets.

Anthropic’s debut did little to reverse the selling pressure across Wall Street, as investors continued to pull back from technology and AI-linked stocks. The S&P 500 fell 0.02% to 7,175.83, marking its sixth consecutive session of losses. The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 2.19% to 49,097.06, while the Nasdaq Composite declined 2.25% to 23,879.20. The sell-off extended beyond a handful of AI companies, with declining stocks significantly outnumbering advancing shares across both the NYSE and Nasdaq.

The technology sector also remained under pressure, with Nvidia losing 3.3% despite its upbeat revenue forecast, while Broadcam and Micron each fell 2.1%. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index declined 2.4%, and data-centre infrastructure companies CoreWeave and Nebius dropped 6.1% and 9.4%, respectively. The broader technology sector closed 1.9% lower, reflecting continued unease around AI-related investments.

Bitcoin extended its decline as the sell-off across equities and technology stocks coincided with continued weakness in the cryptocurrency market. BTC dropped below $62,000, touching approximately $61,400 during Friday’s session, marking its lowest level since July. The decline puts Bitcoin roughly 13.5% lower over the past week, with the asset struggling to recover amid weakening risk appetite and continued pressure on institutional flows.

The decline has also coincided with persistent outflows from spot Bitcoin ETFs, which have added to selling pressure. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded approximately $306 million in net outflows on November 18, while broader November withdrawals have reportedly reached billions of dollars.

At the same time, traders are reassessing the Federal Reserve’s interest-rate outlook, with expectations of another rate hike strengthening following hawkish signals from policymakers. Higher Treasury yields and a stronger US dollar have further complicated the outlook for risk-sensitive assets, including Bitcoin.

The simultaneous declines across US equities, AI-linked stocks, Asian markets and cryptocurrencies have put the broader risk environment under scrutiny. Investors will now look for signs of stabilisation across technology stocks and semiconductor companies, particularly as markets reassess AI valuations and the scale of capital expenditure supporting the sector.

For Bitcoin, reclaiming the $63,000 level could offer an early indication of whether selling pressure is easing, while further declines could expose additional support levels. Upcoming US economic data, Federal Reserve signals, Treasury yields and movements in the dollar may also influence sentiment across risk assets.

Anthropic’s successful IPO remains a significant milestone for the AI industry, but the broader market reaction highlights that a strong corporate debut alone cannot offset prevailing concerns across financial markets.
