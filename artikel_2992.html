Nvidia Just Reported Its First Quarter Without Jensen Huang. If the AI Trade Is Cracking, Warren Buffett’s Advice Comes Down to 1 Move.

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Buffett has age-old advice that can help you survive different market paradigms.

By Brett Schafer – Nov 23, 2026 at 1:00AM EST

It has been a rough few weeks for Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) shareholders. The stock has quickly fallen 47.5% from all-time highs due to founder Jensen Huang's ouster over an affair scandal, the potential sale of computer chips that deliberately circumvented government sanctions, and looming competition in artificial intelligence (AI) compute that could erode Nvidia's once-flaunted moat.

Some investors argue that the AI trade is cracking, and that Nvidia's future will be worse than its near past. Before buying the dip in Nvidia shares, investors should heed the long-standing advice of investing legend Warren Buffett given this latest earnings result and market volatility.

Nvidia's third-quarter numbers were fantastic. Revenue grew over 100% year over year to $116 billion, driven by growth in data center computer chips to $107.6 billion in revenue. Margins were good, with trailing operating margin hitting 66%, making Nvidia one of the most profitable companies in the world. According to the price-to-earnings ratio (P/E), Nvidia trades at just 13.5x its trailing numbers. For a business growing revenue 100% year over year, this figure looks quite cheap.

That is not what investors care about. They care about future earnings, and the predictability of future earnings just got muddied in an extreme way. First, Huang is not running the business anymore. He was wheeling and dealing to secure contracts for Nvidia computer chips in recent years. Second, the United States government is investigating Nvidia for potentially selling illegal chips to third parties that then sent them to China, and there's also a Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) investigation into its accounting statements.

Plus, its customers are either teetering on the brink financially or looking to replace Nvidia chips in their data centers. The so-called neoclouds like CoreWeave and Nebius Group have plunged amid funding concerns. These make up $47.5 billion of the $107.6 billion in data center revenue. Other revenue comes from hyperscalers that are all selling or researching how to build their own computer chips to lower costs for cloud customers.

There is a lot to balance when analyzing an investment in Nvidia today. Given the trading volume in the stock, many investors have looked to buy the dip, with shares down by almost half from the highs.

Buffett's advice is to avoid such complicated situations as an investor. In fact, the saying goes that Buffett wants businesses so good that even a bad CEO could run them, because if you are going to hold a stock for multiple decades, a bad leader will show up sooner or later. Therefore, you want to invest in high-quality businesses with good defensibility that are simple to understand. Nvidia is the opposite of this.

No stock better exemplifies this strategy for Buffett than Coca-Cola. The soda brand has been a long-running holding for Buffett and Berkshire Hathaway simply because it is the leader in a profitable category. No matter what happens technologically in the coming decades, people will likely still drink Coca-Cola. The same can't be said for Nvidia chips, which is why Buffett has never made it or rapidly changing technologies a part of his portfolio.

Lastly, investors should learn from Buffett's strategy more broadly to maintain wealth over the decades. He never liked participating in thematic bull markets, whether it was the dot-com bubble, housing in the early 2000s, or artificial intelligence today. Wall Street may love hot stocks for a time, and Buffett was willing to initially look dumb for avoiding them, but there is almost always a wash-out on the other side. Choosing not to get swept up in fear of missing out (FOMO) is one of the keys to Buffett's long-term success, and that will likely be the case with Berkshire Hathaway during this AI bear market as well.

The secret to Buffett's durability was not necessarily what he bought, but what he avoided as an investor over 75 years in the market.
