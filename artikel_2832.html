Commanders LB Sonny Styles’ processing power makes him more dangerous

Tanggal: 2026-11-18

The New York Giants faced a third-and-3 coming out of the two-minute warning, needing to move the sticks to maintain a slim lead and possession of the ball.

Commanders linebacker Sonny Styles understood that situation. His quarterback had thrown interceptions on consecutive series leading up to this moment, and Washington needed to give Jayden Daniels one more shot to go win it.

The stakes didn't change Styles' approach to the crucial play. The Ohio State product diagnosed it appropriately, sensed what was coming and used freak athleticism to make the defensive play of the day.

"Last time we played them and we had a third-and-4 situation around midfield and they had a crack toss to the back," Styles said. "When they went empty, just looking at the tight end, it looked like he was cracking. Charles said something like that, too, so I thought it was going to be a QB run. I just reacted."

He reacted well in a play you've certainly seen a bunch by now.

"Everyone understood that we had to get a stop right there," Styles said. "Guys bowed up."

Washington got the ball back and Daniels rewarded that effort with a decisive, 32-yard touchdown pass to Stefon Diggs. That play will lead the highlights from a 24-21 victory over the Giants that essentially saved Washington's season.

None of that happens without Styles and an overall defensive effort where Washington's foot never left the gas.

This wasn't a singular event. The No. 7 overall NFL draft pick has been a marvel in this early season, and not solely due to insane athleticism. He's a smart player who can read offenses well and make plays on the ball. While he's far from perfect and with room for improvement, his first nine game qualify as an excellent start to his NFL career.

Styles has 80 tackles and the season is just nine games deep. While number of tackles isn't the important stat that it used to be, that's still a sign that Styles is doing things right. Jeremy Reaves' 55 is the next highest total on Washington's defense. The rookie, Styles, has had 13 tackles in four of the past five games.

He has validated GM Adam Peters' decision to draft a non-premium position at No. 7.

Styles was critical of some plays, including some hesitance as a blitzer, which is keeping him a step behind.

His stat line, overall: 80 tackles, including five for a loss, a sack, an interception, four passes defensed and four quarterback pressures in 46 blitzes. He has allowed 30 catches for 288 yards and a touchdown on 45 targets, for an 82.5 passer rating allowed.

The Commanders will need him leading the defensive surge toward the better play and consistent wins that are required for a real turnaround. The scary part about Styles is that his athleticism will get more effective as he takes more snaps and processes information faster.

He's the primary reason for hope and the long-term success on defense, assuming, of course, that the front office can surround him with talent in ways they haven't quite done for Daniels.

That's a discussion for the offseason. Styles and the Commanders will continue competing for the NFC East title down the stretch, with that Week 10 win against the Giants as a possible pivot point.

"This win isn't going to turn the whole season," Styles said. "We have to keep working, keep pushing and get getting better."
