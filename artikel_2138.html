Top 10 tallest players in the EFL Championship

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

The Championship is well-regarded as one of the most physical leagues in the world, and having the tallest, strongest players has always given some teams an advantage.

Especially now, with set pieces and headers being relied on more than ever, having dominant players in the air can help a side grab the edge in a game.

Granted, the majority of tall players in a division find themselves in goal, but again, with the amount of crosses which get put right on a keeper's head, who then has to wade through a crowd of giant outfielders to punch clear or claim, it's important to have that size advantage.

Right now, though, who are the tallest players in the second tier? Football League World has taken a look to find out.

Kicking off the list with one of the premier hold-up men in the Championship, Wrexham's Kieffer Moore has made a living at this level by being able to hold off defenders, bring in teammates and win aerial duels.

Six of his 18 goals for the Red Dragons since signing last summer, ahead of their second-tier return, have been with his head, including one in the massive 2-1 win over Southampton back in September.

Moore always ranks right atop the aerial duel ranks among Championship strikers, and being one of the tallest outfielders in the division more than helps with that.

There are a few six-foot-six players in the Championship. We'll start with the goalkeepers and head to Derby County, where Jacob Widell Zetterstrom has been one of the best in his position in the division since he joined the Rams in 2024.

The Swedish international is better known for his shot-stopping, rather than his ability to claim high. Indeed, he only has six high claims in 11 Championship appearances this season, but his long frame allows him to get to shots that others in his position cannot.

He makes well over four saves per game, and with Derby struggling to keep their key defenders fit, he's needed to be called into action and use his height to try and earn his side some points in what has been a difficult campaign at Pride Park so far this year.

Josh Griffiths is yet to make his league debut for Stoke City after signing this summer, with Viktor Johansson undroppable when fit, but having the six-foot-six shot-stopper in reserve isn't a negative.

The Potters struggled massively in the second half of last season with the Swedish goalkeeper out of action. Stoke's slide down the table can be attributed to that, with the likes of Gavin Bazunu and academy graduate Tommy Simkin not proving to be at the same level.

Griffiths may not be, either, with him being dropped at West Brom last season after Max O'Leary signed in January, but he's an experienced, tall backup option, which may be helpful if the same happens again this season.

Portsmouth invested heavily in the transfer market this past summer, with head coach John Mousinho receiving what he was hoping for to try and transform Pompey into a play-off-chasing side.

The biggest of the lot, both in terms of transfer fee and sheer height, was Marko Milovanovic, and 'Marezi' has shown glimpses of quality at times so far this season.

Two of his three goals this season have come from the penalty spot, and he is underperforming his non-penalty xG by around 2.00, but most Pompey fans are, overall, happy with his contributions so far.

It can often be the case that foreign strikers need a little bit of time to settle in, and Marezi should stand to improve as his time at Fratton Park goes on.

Still standing at six-foot-six, but a little bit taller, is Millwall goalkeeper Lukas Jensen, who Lions fans can't wait to see back in action shortly.

It's been a nightmarish 20 months or so for the Dane, who ruptured his Achilles in April 2025 and then has battled quad injuries after recovering, his most recent one seeing him miss the last three months.

When fit, though, he's been immense in the Millwall goal. He kept 14 clean sheets in 41 games in his debut campaign in 2024/25, and his first three outings of the current season saw him keep clean sheets in the opening two league games and save every penalty in an EFL Cup shootout win against Queens Park Rangers.

His return couldn't come sooner for certain fans at the Den, who look forward to seeing his towering frame between the sticks once more.

Middlesbrough shot-stopper Radek Vitek had a real breakout year last season at Bristol City. He was one of, if not the best, goalkeepers in the league, and used his six-foot-six frame to perfection.

Vitek was in the top five for high claims per 90 in the Championship last year, averaging over one a game, made over three saves per game at a 71.3% rate and prevented a league-high 7.36 goals.

It's no wonder that Boro invested at least £7 million, but up to £14 million, in his services, and while he hasn't quite reached those heights at the Riverside, there's still plenty of time for the 23-year-old to showcase his quality in Kim Hellberg's side.

Arthur Okonkwo has one of the more recognisable frames in the Championship, with his six-foot-six stature almost contrasting with being one of the skinnier goalkeepers in the division.

You don't see the Charlton Athletic man dominate in the air, but his agile build allows him to get across the goal line quickly and make some key saves. Wrexham's defensive dominance through the lower divisions was mainly down to him keeping the ball out of the net.

Many felt that Okonkwo should have been given more of a chance in Phil Parkinson's squad in the Championship, but instead, he found himself out on loan in the final year of his contract. He's had a fine start to his career at the Valley, but he does have that next level to hit.

Jake Cooper stands alone as the only player in the Championship who stands a centimetre short of two metres, and he's been one of the most dominant aerial defenders in the division for around a decade.

Weirdly enough, the Millwall man hasn't scored with his head for well over a year now, with each of his last seven career goals (four last season and three this year) scored with his feet. Before then, though, you could guarantee that, if the 31-year-old was on the score sheet, it'd be a header from a set piece.

Only Jordan Storey and Billy Koumetio have a higher aerial duel winning percentage with 70 or more attempts this season than Cooper does, and he sits eighth in overall duels won in the air.

A dominant centre-back at this level throughout his entire career, he could enter the top five appearance makers in the division come the end of the season, which just shows how timeless he's been due to his physical dominance.

However, there are two six-foot-seven players sitting atop this list. One of them is goalkeeper James Beadle, who is enjoying a much-improved second season at Birmingham City, on loan from Brighton and Hove Albion.

The England Under-21 international came under some criticism amongst Blues fans last season, but this year, he's playing up to his height, using his long frame to tip shots wide that were destined for the corner and save penalties, too.

He's already got five clean sheets in all competitions for Chris Davies' side this season, three fewer than he managed all last year. Only West Brom's Max O'Leary has prevented more goals, too.

Beadle has always had the potential to reach the very top of the game, with his height more than playing a factor in that. Now, it seems like he's closing in on reaching some of that promise.

Lincoln City had to make a big splash in their first transfer window as a second-tier club since the 1960s this past summer, and they did, quite literally, sign the tallest player in the division, six-foot-seven Mikkel Ladefoged, for a club record fee.

The 23-year-old netted 19 in 29 Swedish top-flight games for Vasteras over the last couple of years, and is being bedded in slowly to the English game at Sincil Bank, which seems to be working wonders.

He has three goals and an assist in just 318 minutes of Championship action. Two of those efforts, the equaliser against Southampton and his second against Burnley, were with his head.

He's won 26 aerial duels already this season, which amounts to 7.36 per 90 minutes. He won nine in his last game against Birmingham, an impressive feat against Christoph Klarer and Phil Neumann, who are two of the best defenders in the division.

Ladefoged seems to be starting more games now, which can only bode well for the Imps and strike fear into opposition defenders, who will be giving up the height advantage against the Dane.
