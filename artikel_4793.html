Using AI to Avoid Thinking Harms Students

The conclusion of this blog post is so important for students that I want to start with it:

If you are learning something that makes you think, using AI to avoid thinking is a misuse. AI use is appropriate when it prompts you to think more or to help you accomplish “busy work,” which requires little thinking.

In light of this statement, helpful uses of AI in education include:

Students may suffer when their use of AI reduces or eliminates their need to wrestle with a problem. This impairs learning, because:

I asked seven consecutive adolescent patients who have come to my counseling practice how they have been using AI to help with their schoolwork. Unfortunately, I believe each one of them reported a potentially harmful misuse, which they did not even realize.

As we review these misuses, it should be kept in mind that major goals of education should not be attaining good grades or correct answers, but rather acquiring knowledge and how to better understand and generalize it.

A high school sophomore was given an assignment to take notes about his history reading, and then to summarize what he learned in three sentences. He dutifully read the material and typed his notes into an AI Chatbot. He then asked AI to summarize the notes in three sentences, which he turned in.

Missed Learning Opportunity: Taking notes is an insufficient tool for learning (especially when the notes are typed rather than handwritten). Rather, deciding which is the most salient information from the notes to put into a summary is the activity that best promotes learning from this assignment.

A high school senior was dealing with a difficult homework problem in her math class. She asked an AI to show her how to solve the problem. The AI showed her the step-by-step solution. After she felt she understood the explanation, she went on to study her other subjects.

Missed Learning Opportunity: Just because this student understood the AI-generated explanation did not mean that she could utilize this explanation in solving further problems. She should have worked through a few other similar problems without help of the AI to ensure that she understood how to apply the underlying principles (Bastani et al., 2025).

Another high school senior who was taking several advanced placement classes used AI to summarize the highlights of books he was supposed to read for English class. He then wrote essays for this class using the AI-generated material.

Missed Learning Opportunity: How to extract essential information from reading material is a learned skill. While it is tempting to use AI for this purpose, a human reader may find different salient information by reading an original source. For instance, an AI summary may fail to comment regarding how an author presented detailed subject matter intended to evoke a reader’s emotions.

A high school junior asked AI to compose e-mails to his teachers about missed assignments and potential make-up work.

Missed Learning Opportunity: Students should learn how adding a human element as part of a written communication is more likely to generate favorable response. For instance, I quickly delete emailed queries from students requesting the opportunity to work with me if they appear to be AI-generated.

A high school sophomore told me he has used AI to verify the news he has heard on social media.

Missed Learning Opportunity: Given generative AI’s propensities to hallucinate and echo what it has been told (sycophancy) using AI to verify the news is a poor practice (Fletcher & Verckist, 2025). Instead, this student should learn how to use primary sources to verify news, such as consulting reports by a few news organizations.

The last two examples of potential misuse were prompted by teachers rather than the students:

An 8th grader was told by his science teacher to use AI to make a poster that presents what he had learned about a particular topic.

Missed Learning Opportunity: While I appreciate the teacher’s desire to teach students how to apply AI in their lives, in 8th grade it might be much more important for students to learn how to apply the elements that make for an effective poster presentation. For instance, how to decide on an appropriate title, choose the effective top line summaries, and what information should be included while maintaining readability of the poster. As part of this process, students also would learn how to prioritize the information they gathered.

A 9th grader was given an assignment by his English teacher to define different words, identify their part of speech, and to provide their language of origin. The student completed the assignment without consulting AI, and was downgraded because he didn’t give the exact definition of the word as listed by an AI. Furthermore, the student was aware that his teacher used an AI to grade the assignment.

Missed Learning Opportunity: According to the student in this example, his teacher is enthusiastic about using AI, and teaching students how to do so. Unfortunately, his teacher’s whole-hearted adoption of AI is pushing the student to forego his independent thinking and learning process in favor of allowing AI to shape his work.

Students may be tempted to use AI as a short-cut to complete their assigned work. However, as demonstrated in this blog, learning can be greatly diminished by using AI.

Students’ use of AI to prompt their thinking is good. Their use of AI to avoid thinking is detrimental. Thus, when students face difficulty in understanding an assignment, they should ask for hints from their instructors or an AI, rather than answers.
