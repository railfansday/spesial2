I can't imagine ACA being cheaper than what you quoted, which is already ludicrous indeed.

I've lost a lot of hope so I haven't dealt with the ACA nightmare in a few years, but in the past when I got quotes on ACA, the cheapest "Bronze" plans were $1000-2000/month premiums, $6000+ deductible, then after that they cover like 30-40%. This is with $0 subsidies and $0 income, which $0 income one would think would be low enough income to qualify for an ACA subsidy, but that is not the case -- ACA rejects me for income and recommends I apply for Medicaid, and Medicaid rejects me for being an adult age 18-64 and asks if I've considered the ACA plans -- it's a nasty, vicious circle of both programs denying coverage and passing the buck. When I called them and spoke with the ACA Customer Service (Texas based), the lady was super nice but wasn't really knowledgeable about health care policy in USA despite working it as her profession. She had no idea that many states had not expanded their Medicaid program to cover adults age 18-64 and that this "gap" left countless millions of people left to rot and die, a completely forgotten cohort. My argument is that 18-64 year olds absolutely are the American economy and it seems like a no-brainer to get these people back on their feet when they fall down (cheaper to take care of the very people who comprise the US economy so they're working, buying houses, paying taxes, and making babies, no?), but when I talk about that online or in person, it falls on deaf ears. Health care policy is against forum rules so I don't know how to talk about this topic without voicing my opinion that our system is broken and headed for fiscal collapse at the hands of corrupt mega-corporations who choose greed over human lives.

Bottom line, it's cheaper to "self-pay" or just rot and die than for me to get an ACA "affordable" plan at close to $20,000/year just to say I am covered with health insurance off $0 income and $0 assets. It makes more sense to take that currency and pay for drugs directly (via GoodRx and Mark Cuban's Cost Plus Drugs Pharmacy -- mail order from Florida) as well as drive around and speak to different specialist offices and beg them to be upfront about their proprietary pricing for each service they provide. By eliminating the middle-man of health insurance, I've been able to actually get access to health care directly from a doctor's office without the administrative burden of the doctor or myself begging for permission for x, y, z procedure off x, y, z CPT code.
