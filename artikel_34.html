China issues Mount Fuji warning as family of missing climber offers reward

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The $75,000 reward offered by Chen Jie's family has sparked fears of more reckless climbing attempts

The Chinese embassy in Japan has warned its citizens against off-season ascents of Mount Fuji amid concerns that the hefty reward offered by a missing hiker's family could encourage more reckless climbing.

Chen Jie, 25, vanished on 8 November on the mountain. His family is offering 500,000 yuan ($75,000; Â£57,300) to anyone who finds him alive, and 100,000 yuan to anyone who recovers his body, say Chinese and Japanese media reports.

Strong winds and blizzards pose serious risks during the closed season, especially as shelter and first-aid centres are not operational, the embassy said on Tuesday.

Mount Fuji's official climbing season runs from around early July to early September.

Earlier this year, authorities of Yamanashi province, where the northern part of Mount Fuji sits, had said that people who attempt to climb Mount Fuji during off-peak seasons and then require rescue by helicopter will have to pay for the service from next year on.

Authorities will also require those looking to scale Fuji during the off-season to submit detailed climbing plans.

"We hope that these measures will cause people to rethink what climbing Mount Fuji during the winter is like," Kotaro Nagasaki, the prefecture governor, said at the time.

On Wednesday, Nagasaki told reporters that attempts to find the missing climber could lead to "secondary disasters"

According to Japanese news outlet the Mainichi Shimbun, Nagasaki appeared to appeal to Chen's family, saying: "I understand that your family is important, but please refrain from doing anything that could endanger others."

Some social media users in Japan have similarly expressed fears would entice people to venture into the mountains ill-prepared and lead to more accidents,

Yamanashi police said Chen had set out on his hike on 8 November with two friends, who turned back after they realised the mountain was closed and that they weren't suitably equipped for the conditions of the trails.

Chen pressed on alone from the fifth station of the Yoshida Trail, which sits at around 2,300m (7545ft) above sea level.

A friend last heard from Chen at 10:30 that day, when he sent a video message saying: "I'm going up the mountain... There's no one in sight."

The highest point of Mount Fuji, which is Japan's tallest peak, stands at 3,776m.

Local authorities launched a search for Chen on 10 November, but it had to be halted several times because of poor weather.

Chen's father reportedly arrived in Japan on 13 November. It is not clear when he posted the reward offer.
