Vance says he will decide 'sometime next year' about 2028 US presidential run

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

US Vice-President JD Vance has said he will make a decision "sometime next year" about whether or not he will run for president in 2028.

In an interview with Fox News, Vance would not provide a timeline on making a determination, but signaled he is not in a hurry to make up his mind.

"I haven't made a decision. We'll figure it out sometime next year," Vance said. When pressed by host Laura Ingraham, he added: "When I make a decision, I'll be all in on whatever that decision is."

The comments came amid growing interest in Vance's next moves in the weeks since voters handed both President Donald Trump and Republicans a resounding defeat in the midterm elections.

People in Vance's orbit who have spoken to the BBC said the November elections did not change the timeline around his decision-making process for 2028. He is expected to run but allies believe he could wait several months to make a final decision.

Speculation about Vance's White House aspirations began the moment President Trump selected him as his 2024 running mate. The pick positioned Vance, who entered the White House as one of the youngest vice-presidents in US history, as the most likely heir to Trump's Make America Great Again (Maga) movement.

But while serving in office, Vance has carefully avoided discussing his own political ambitions.

He kept that up during the midterms, when supporters frequently interrupted his campaign speeches to urge him to run in 2028. Vance's stock response was that Republicans needed to focus on the election at hand in November.

But with the midterms in the rearview mirror, and Republicans eager to move on from their 2026 drubbing, the next election at hand is now the 2028 presidential race.

Vance would likely jump to the top of the Republican primary field whenever he launches his bid, should he choose to run. But the contest is expected to be a competitive process with a large field of candidates.

Numerous other high-profile Republicans are rumoured to be interested in running or have said publicly that they are mulling a White House bid.

The list includes Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Senator Ted Cruz of Texas, outgoing Florida Governor and 2024 hopeful Ron DeSantis, and former New Jersey Governor Chris Christie, among others.

Trump has praised Vance and Rubio on several occasions, while also insisting that he is not sure who he will endorse in 2028 - or if he will weigh in at all.

The president's endorsement would be influential with the Maga base. But the midterms revealed widespread frustration with many of his second-term policies, complicating the path forward for potential candidates like Vance who are tied to Trump's agenda.

Vance blamed the midterm results on the war with Iran in a post-election social media post. In it, he also mapped out a plan for winning back Republican voters, which included ending the war and focusing on the economy.

The message was widely seen as a first step by Vance to distance himself from Trump ahead of a 2028 run.

In the Fox News interview, Vance reiterated his view that the war came with a "political cost", but said it has not damaged his relationship with Trump.

"There's no daylight" on the Iran war between Trump and senior administration officials including himself, Vance said.

He added that Trump would not let the conflict become a "forever war" and said the administration would not let the war, which was launched by the US and Israel in February 2026, even become "a two-year commitment".

Vance also discussed the nationwide air traffic outrage last week, which brought air travel across the US to a standstill for several hours. The vice-president called on lawmakers to fix the issue in the final weeks of the current congressional session.

He also touched on the scandal engulfing Nvidia, its former CEO Jensen Huang and its implications for US-China relations. Vance said there was still no "clarity" about the accuracy of reports that a woman with whom Huang reportedly had an affair with was indeed a Chinese spy.

"There is a lack of clarity about it. I don't know and [Trump] doesn't know whether this woman was a spy or not," Vance said.
