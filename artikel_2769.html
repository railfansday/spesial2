Warriors again prove they can't afford slow starts with Steph Curry sidelined

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Warriors again prove they can't afford slow starts with Steph Curry sidelined originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

A four-quarter game again turned to a one-quarter outcome for the Warriors on Friday night.

The Warriors outscored the Portland Trail Blazers 83-79 over the final three quarters at the Moda Center. They still lost by 22 points, 116-94.

Two nights prior, the Warriors rested soundly from the cushion of a 25-point lead through the first quarter to hold off the Sacramento Kings the rest of the game. The roles were reversed against much better competition. This time, it was the opposition who could catch its breath after a 26-point first quarter lead.

In losing their sixth consecutive road game, the Warriors received yet another reality check without Steph Curry: The first quarter might be more important than the other three.

By the end of the first quarter Friday night, the Warriors already built their own demise in a game where they trailed by as much as 36 points. Game over. Same song and dance as their previous road losses without Curry.

"This has been a theme in the early part of the season here. We're just not bringing the necessary force and focus to start games," Warriors coach Steve Kerr said to reporters in Portland after the loss. "We're getting killed in the first quarter, over and over again."

When the Warriors suffered a winless 0-5 road trip after Curry sustained a right oblique abdominal tear minutes into the first game against the Houston Rockets, those outcomes were finalized in a hurry. They were one-quarter games, not four-quarter games.

The Warriors trailed 35-20 in the game Curry got injured, and 38-14 the next game. Their opponents outscored them 174-94 in the first quarter during those five games. How did the Warriors respond in their first road game since then?

They trailed 7-0 in the first two minutes and 10 seconds, and 20-5 at the 5:30 mark of the first quarter in Portland. The Blazers won the first quarter 37-11, meaning the Warriors now have been outscored by 106 points in the first quarter of their last six road games.

The early gap in its simplest form was too big for a Golden State team without its superstar (Curry) and his co-star (Jimmy Butler). While the Warriors were without Curry, Butler, Moses Moody and Kristaps Porziņģis, the Blazers didn't have Damian Lillard, Ja Morant or Shaedon Sharpe. But the Blazers still had the firepower of Deni Avdija, Toumani Camara, Jrue Holiday and Scoot Henderson.

That quartet was too much for the likes of Yaxel Lendeborg, Brandin Podziemski, De'Anthony Melton and the rest of the Warriors.

A tone was set from the start for whose game this was going to be. Avdija scored nine points on 4-of-6 shooting in the first quarter. Camara added seven, Holiday had five and Henderson brought eight from the bench. How the game started was quite the contrast to the Warriors' top scoring options.

Lendeborg was scoreless in the first quarter, as was Melton. Podziemski scored four points, but also went just 1 of 4 from the free-throw line.

The Warriors in the first quarter shot 16.7 percent from the field (4 of 24), 15.4 percent from 3-point range (2 of 13) and gave up six turnovers for seven Blazers points while being outrebounded 20-11. As Kerr said, this has become a theme. And with that, blame gets placed every which way.

"It's us. It's 100 percent on the Golden State Warriors. We're a team, so we figure things out together, we collaborate," Kerr said. "It's on all of us. That's what practice is for. That's what game film is for. I've got to do better, they've got to do better.

"We're going to get there. I'm very confident that we will, but we're not there yet."

Looking back at the Warriors' previous two games, both being home wins, is proof of what kind of team they need to be and how to do so.

First, they beat the Boston Celtics 88-87 on Sunday night off tenacious defense, inhaling offensive rebounds and building a first-quarter lead. The Warriors led 27-20 through the first quarter against the Celtics. In those 12 minutes, they attempted six more shots than the Celtics and made three more than them. They also grabbed six offensive rebounds in that span and held the Celtics to 3 of 11 on threes (27.3 percent).

Then came the Kings game, where they were the ones who could afford to be on cruise control after the first quarter.

The Warriors scored 44 points in that first quarter, their most in any quarter this season. They had 12 assists on 14 made shots and their bench provided a punch of 19 points, the same amount the Kings scored as a team. The Kings came within four points in the third quarter, but the story already had been told. The Warriors wound up scoring 129 points after failing to score 100 in their previous five, dished 37 assists and their bench racked up 61 points.

But the blues followed them back on the road.

Friday night's blowout loss gave the Warriors five consecutive road losses by 20-plus points, which is tied for the second most in NBA history. They've scored an average of 17.5 points in the first quarter of their last six road games, while allowing an average of 35.2 first-quarter points. That has led to them trailing by an average of 17.7 points in the first quarter of those six road games, making them the first team to lose by 20 or more in five consecutive road games since 1998.

There is no Curry Card to pull out of the Warriors' back pocket. Hours before tipoff, the Warriors announced Curry would miss at least three more games. To make up for their step back in Portland, the Warriors need to keep their foot on the pedal from the start the next two games against the lowly Los Angeles Clippers and Utah Jazz.
