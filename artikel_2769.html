5 Dolphins to watch at Bills

Tanggal: 2026-11-21

The Buffalo Bills host the Miami Dolphins on Sunday in a game of two franchises headed in completely opposite directions. The 8-1 Bills sit atop the AFC, leading the conference by one game over the Jacksonville Jaguars and Kansas City Chiefs (the Denver Broncos are also 7-2, but they are currently the No. 5 seed in the playoff picture). Miami, on the other hand, is 1-8 and tied for the AFC's worst record with the Los Angeles Chargers and Tennessee Titans.

On paper, this a game that Buffalo should win running away. However, games are played on the field, not on paper, and Miami has kept plenty of games close this season. Half of Miami's losses have come in one-possession games, and they've held great offenses like the Chiefs and the Detroit Lions under their season scoring average.

If the Bills want to avoid the pitfalls of a textbook "trap game," they'll need to lock in and focus on stopping Miami's key players. Here are five Dolphins to watch in Sunday's tilt with the Bills._____________________________________________________________________________

After De'Von Achane went down with a knee injury earlier this season, it was easy to assume that Miami's run game would suffer mightily. Thanks to Gordon, though, Miami has been able to run fairly well throughout the course of the year. With former Syracuse Orange quarterback Kyle McCord set to make his second career start this week, Miami will want to try to establish the run and maintain possession of the ball to keep their young quarterback out of obvious passing situations. Shutting Gordon down and keeping Miami in continuous second-and-long or third-and-long scenarios will go a long way towards driving the Bills to victory this week. Buffalo has been good against the run, as they are No. 8 in yards allowed on the ground. Gordon averages 4.2 yards per rush. If the Bills can prevent him from breaking big runs, they'll be in great shape.

Buffalo's secondary is beaten up, and while the Dolphins don't boast a great passing attack, they do have some sneaky-good weapons who can beat you if you aren't careful. Chief among those weapons is Washington, who leads Miami in targets (51), receptions (36), and receiving yards (400). At 5'8" and 192 lbs., he operates out of the slot more often than not, so it will be up to whomever Buffalo plays inside to contain him. Given their injuries in the secondary, that responsibility could fall to Dee Alford, Jordan Hancock, Damar Hamlin, or a combination of multiple defenders, including linebackers. Washington is particularly good after the catch, as 123 of his 400 receiving yards have come as "YAC" gains. If he catches a short pass, that's fine; what the Bills can't do is let him slip tackles and pick up big gains after he makes that grab.

The 2024 first-round pick is on pace for a career year, and good for him. It took Robinson some time to put it together as a pro, but he has five sacks already on the season, which is one shy of the career-high six sacks he notched as a rookie. He also has 13 quarterback hits, which is just one shy of his career-high total from that same 2024 rookie season. Both Spencer Brown and Dion Dawkins are likely to play this week, so Buffalo should finally be back at full strength along the offensive line after playing some different combinations due to injury over the last three weeks. Making sure that Robinson stays out of the offensive backfield needs to be a major focus of the game plan.

While the Rookie of the Year hype that came with Rodriguez in the season's early going has faded a bit, what hasn't faded is the quality of Rodriguez's play. The rookie out of Texas Tech has looked every bit the part of a long-term starter at middle linebacker for a Miami defense that has played better than the team's 1-8 record would suggest. Rodriguez was a fan favorite for many Bills-connected folks during the draft process last spring, and his play so far has justified that hype. He has 80 tackles, four tackles for loss, one sack, one interception, and one forced fumble so far this season. Head coach and offensive playcaller Joe Brady needs to try to give the rookie some different looks by using motion to throw off his keys. That should help Buffalo to make him hesitate just enough to wall up some blocking around him. Buffalo is the league's second-best rushing offense, but the Dolphins allow just 3.9 yards per rush thanks in large part to Rodriguez's talent. Neutralizing him in the middle could make for a long day for Miami.

Taaffe is someone I really liked for Buffalo in April's draft. He's a hyper-athletic safety who reminded me a little bit of Cole Bishop. Like Bishop, he's made some plays here in the early portion of his career, but he's also been burned for some big gains. Taaffe leads the Dolphins with three interceptions, but he's also allowed three touchdowns and a passer rating against of 100.7 this season, as opposing quarterbacks have completed 64% of their passes for a whopping 329 yards when he's the closest man in coverage. If Buffalo can run the ball effectively, that will lead Taaffe to creep closer to the line of scrimmage to help out in run support. When that happens, the play-action pass will be on, and Buffalo could find some big plays through the air. As long as Josh Allen isn't forcing the ball, he might be able to exploit a young player in Taaffe at the back end of Miami's defense. If he's not careful, though, Taaffe is more than capable of turning what looks like a big offensive play into a turnover.
