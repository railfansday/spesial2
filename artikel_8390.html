I'd love to know more about the skill of the many advisors used by friends and family members. Every single one of my close relatives and close friends use advisors--including Raymond James, Wells Fargo, Fidelity, and Edward Jones. However, nobody is particularly interested in discussing the details. I suppose their general lack of interest is why they use advisors.
Part of the reason they don't want to talk details is they actually don't know. I've talked to a few people who have advisors and they have no idea what they are paying. If most people had to write a check out to their advisor each quarter for what they are being charged instead of it being taken out of their account quietly, I bet you at least 25% or more would stop using their advisors.
I think you are both correct. Lack of interest in details makes you vulnerable to the sales pitch. Ignorance of investment basics makes it impossible to ask questions that would reveal whether your advisor is smart or just reading a script.
