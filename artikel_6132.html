Airbnb Makes Concessions on House Rules After Host Pushback

Hosts got through to Airbnb on their gripes and Airbnb bent. But many of the policies that stoked host anger, such as a limit on guest checkout chores, a ban on contracts, and collecting guest IDs, remain in place.

Airbnb is giving hosts more control over their house rules after they pushed back on a policy, announced in October, that limits what they can ask of guests. Under a House Rules policy that was updated November 3, hosts can now set checkout rules guests are expected to follow, such as putting dirty dishes in the sink, and can require a separate rental agreement when their insurer demands one.

A general restriction on contracts between hosts and guests, however, and a prohibition against collecting guest IDs — both of which made hosts angry — remain in place.

The restrictions that drew the most attention in the mainstream media will still be in effect starting November 20: Hosts still won’t be able to ask guests to wash dishes or strip beds. Airbnb also laid out, for the first time, when it will review listings for compliance and how hosts can appeal.

Airbnb has partially walked back a House Rules policy announced in October after hosts objected publicly, but the core restrictions take effect as planned on November 20. Under the November 3 update, what Airbnb had called "Requests" — instructions guests were only "encouraged" to follow — are now "additional rules" that guests "are expected to comply with," covering items such as putting dishes in the sink, gathering used towels, and sorting recycling. Hosts can also require a separate rental agreement when an insurer demands one, alongside existing exceptions for laws, homeowners associations, and building rules. Airbnb published a review timeline for the first time: listing reviews begin November 20 and run through March 2027, with at least 20 days to fix a flagged rule and one appeal per rule, supporting documents allowed. The limits that drew the most attention remain: no checkout chores such as washing dishes or stripping beds, no host-imposed fines, no rules added after booking, and no collecting guest IDs, emails, phone numbers, or signed agreements absent a legal, building, or insurer requirement. Hosts can cancel without penalty only when a guest breaks one of the seven standard rules. Airbnb declined to comment for this story.
