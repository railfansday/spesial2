How the NBA Cup works in 3 stages, from group draw to the Indianapolis final

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Free daily briefing on global business news.

The NBA Cup runs inside the regular season. See how the draw, group play and knockout rounds work, ending in Indianapolis.

The NBA Cup has no schedule of its own. The league plays it inside the regular season, so a single night in November can count as a tournament game and as a game in the standings at once. Each group game also goes into the standings as a normal regular-season game. The league built the two calendars around each other, and the schedules it published in August listed only 80 games for each team, according to ESPN. The league held back the rest of the calendar until group play had decided which teams would keep playing in the tournament. That makes a November loss count twice for a team. A team that drops a Cup game falls back in its chase for a trophy and in the playoff race on the same night.

The format puts those stakes into very few games and gives teams little room for a bad night. Each team plays only one group game against each rival, so it has almost no time to recover after a loss. Group play closes on Nov. 27, 2026, according to The Gazette, and the teams that fail to qualify, most of the league, are finished with the tournament on that date while a small group plays on into December. Players have a personal reason to care about getting through, because the league pays prize money only to players on teams that reach the knockout rounds. A few autumn games decide which teams get a chance at a trophy, and the regular-season standings keep every one of those results.

NBA.com explained how the in-season tournament was organized and how a champion was decided, from a draw that sorts teams into pots to the Friday "Cup Nights" of group play and a title game at Hinkle Fieldhouse in Indianapolis. Each stage sets up the next. In the draw, teams learn which rivals they must beat. In group play, they find out whether they survive, and in the knockout rounds, the league crowns its champion. Nearly every game along the way counts in the regular season too. For a fan, the question worth asking goes beyond who wins the trophy to how a few nights of tournament basketball change a team's whole season.

The NBA sets up its Cup draw so that every group holds one team from each level of strength. Teams come out of five pots in a random drawing, according to NBA.com. ESPN reports that a team's place in last season's standings decides its pot within its conference, and each of the six groups then takes one team from every pot. Luck decides only which team from a given pot lands in which group. The strength of each pot was settled months earlier. A group's strength therefore reflects last season's standings rather than the rosters teams bring this autumn. When a team has changed a lot since then, a group can end up lopsided even though the drawing followed every rule.

Last season's finish can misjudge a team that played it without its best player. Indiana went through all of 2025-26 without Tyrese Haliburton, who was out with a torn Achilles, according to ESPN. The Pacers' place in this year's draw rested on a season they played without their star.

East Group B shows how crowded a group can get. New York, Cleveland, Philadelphia, Miami and Indiana all landed together, five teams with designs on the East, and the group has no easy opening for any of them. The narrow gap between those teams showed when Indiana beat New York 106-104 on Nov. 13, according to ESPN. A single basket decided a game between two contenders. Close games like that one carry a cost because only the group winner is sure to advance. In a group with five strong teams, one narrow loss can leave a contender hoping for a wild card.

Group play decides most of the Cup in a few weeks, and it leaves teams almost no room to lose. The league plays the group stage on Friday "Cup Nights" from Oct. 30 to Nov. 27, according to NBA.com. Yahoo Sports reports that every team to make the knockout rounds since the Cup started in 2023 has gotten there with fewer than two group defeats. That record is a pattern rather than a rule, and a two-loss team can still advance in theory, but none has yet. A loss in mid-November carries the weight of a late-season elimination game, at a time when most fans don't yet think of the season as urgent.

Winning the Cup last season gives a team no protection in this season's groups. The Knicks, the defending Cup champions, are 0-2 in East Group B, according to Yahoo Sports. They have already taken the two losses that no team has come back from, and their run is in serious danger, though they are not formally out. A champion and a team that went home early last year start group play with the same few nights to prove themselves.

The draw gives every group the same mix of last season's strength, and in a group that has outgrown that seeding, even a contender can be out before the knockout rounds begin.

When records match, the margin of each win can decide who advances, and the rules reward teams that win by a lot in regulation. The Spurs have opened a large lead over their rivals in West Group C, going 2-0 with a +53 point differential, according to Yahoo Sports. The league counts overtime as zero toward point differential, so a team can build that kind of edge only during regulation. Some of a margin depends on who plays. The Warriors faced the Spurs without Stephen Curry, so a team can improve its tiebreaker standing by beating an opponent that is missing its best player. If teams remain tied after every other measure, the league settles the place with a random drawing, and a month of games can end with a knockout spot decided by chance.

In group play, a team needs to win early and win by a lot, because by the end a drawing may decide the rest.

Eight teams play for the NBA Cup in a single week of win-or-go-home games. The quarterfinals open Dec. 4 and the title game is Dec. 11, according to NBA.com, so a team can go from contender to spectator in one night. Getting into that field takes more than a good group record. Each conference fills its places with its group winners and just one wild card, which means most teams that finish second in their groups go home. A team can play well in November and still watch December from the outside, because the knockout stage rewards winning a group rather than finishing close.

A strong group finish earns a team home court as well as a place. The quarterfinals and semifinals are played in the higher seed's own arena rather than at a neutral site. In single elimination, a team has no second game to recover from a bad night, and the better seed gets to play that one game in front of its own fans. Teams that win their groups in November earn both a spot in December and the right to fight for survival at home.

Every knockout game except the last also counts in the regular-season standings, so a quarterfinal or semifinal loss costs a team in the playoff race as well as in the Cup. The championship is the exception, because it does not count as a regular-season game. That makes it the one Cup night with nothing at stake but the trophy. The league moves it away from both finalists' home arenas to Hinkle Fieldhouse on the campus of Butler University in Indianapolis, according to The Gazette.

After weeks of games that counted twice, the champion is decided on the one night that counts only for the Cup.
