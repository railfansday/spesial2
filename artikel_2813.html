Naugatuck football rallies past Torrington as Qwentarvous Lee delivers two huge plays

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

TORRINGTON - With the annual Thanksgiving battle with Ansonia within sniffing distance, Naugatuck football coach Chris Anderson preached to his team all week long that the Greyhounds couldn't look past Torrington on Friday night. He was right.

Naugy found itself trailing by a touchdown at the Robert Frost Athletic Complex, and the gap could have been even greater after another score was called back because of a false start by the Raiders.

"At halftime, we told the kids, 'Well, you've dug yourself a hole, but you've been resilient all season.' We were down to Holy Cross and Woodland, too, and won those games. We can do it again," Anderson remarked following a 29-14 victory that improved the Greyhounds to 9-0.

Torrington (6-3) controlled the first half with its ball-control single-wing offense. The Raiders ran 39 plays from scrimmage, more than twice as many as Naugatuck's 17, and ate up big chunks of the clock. Torrington even held the Greyhounds scoreless in the first half for the first time all season.

"Yes, we're high-powered, but when you don't have the ball, it doesn't matter," Anderson said. "When you're able to eat the clock like they can, they have the formula to beat us."

Naugy, which beat Torrington on the final play each of the past two meetings, needed a big play to turn the tide. Qwentarvous Lee provided exactly that and then did it again.

Ahead 8-0 after the opening half, Torrington was driving again on its first possession after intermission. But fullback Cameron Roxbury fumbled at the 22. The ball spun out of the pile to Lee, who picked it up and raced 78 yards to a touchdown. Brandon Andoh's conversion run tied the game for Naugy.

"Coach put me in on defense in the second half. I noticed we weren't getting to the ball. I was driving to the ball, and all the effort paid off. The ball came out right in front of me. Josiah (Loyd) stripped it out. The ball came right to me and I took off," Lee said.Nick Coleman got the momentum right back for Torrington with a 61-yard scoring run for his second TD, but Lee immediately answered. On Naugy's next play, he burst through the line and bolted 80 yards for a touchdown. Gabriel Bello's point-after kick put his team ahead for good.

"I got Llir (Mamudov) and (Logan) Washenko, two 300-pound guys, in front of me. I told them if you make your blocks and Josiah goes through and blocks the linebacker, I'm going to take off, and that's what happened," Lee said.

Torrington answered by driving to the Naugy 6-yard line, but Coleman was stopped short on fourth down.

"We just have to keep swarming to the ball. That was the big emphasis for the game," Loyd said. "It means a lot to get to 9-0, especially against a good team like this. They're coached well and have good athletes."

The Greyhounds then drove 96 yards in eight plays, capped by Andoh's 12-yard TD run with 6:29 left in the game. Following a Torrington fumble, he swept left end 22 yards for a 29-14 lead with 2:10 on the clock.

The Raiders scored in the first quarter after recovering a Naugatuck fumble. They went 86 yards in 13 plays with Coleman scoring on a 13-yard run. After forcing a punt, Torrington then held onto the ball for 20 plays. Coleman scored again on a 26-yard run, but it was negated by a false start penalty and the Raiders turned it over on downs.

Coleman finished with 190 yards rushing on 31 carries. For Naugy, Lee gained 133 yards on seven carries, while Andoh had 79 on 11 carries.

With the win, Naugatuck most likely clinched a home game for the first round of the Class L state playoffs. Torrington, ranked 10th in Class MM, needs to beat Watertown on Thanksgiving and hope other contenders such as Platt, Newtown and SMSA lose that day.
