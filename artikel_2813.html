Previewing UVA Football’s home finale versus North Carolina

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

The past few seasons have seen Tony Elliott repeatedly mention the month of November as time where teams separate themselves from pack. Of course, in the context of this season for the Virginia Cavaliers, that doesn't mean separating for a chance to play for an ACC title, but it could mean salvaging what's left of a season where the results have fallen well short of preseason expectations by going unbeaten in November.

This Saturday is the first of two massive opportunities that will heavily dictate how this season and program as a whole are viewed going forward. Two weeks, two rivalries, against two struggling teams that look much less intimidating than they did two or three weeks ago.

And if you were thinking Tony Elliott was one of those coaches who downplays the significance of rivalry games when he stepped in front of a microphone ahead of this two-game stretch, you would be wrong. In one of the better answers he's given as UVA's head coach, Elliott was asked about what rivalries mean to him even at a time when player movement has never been more hectic:

North Carolina enters this game with a 5-5 overall record of its own and 2-4 mark in the ACC. Bill Belichick's and co. have dropped two of their last three games, including an ugly 16-14 loss at home versus Louisville a week ago.

One way or another, someone is destined to earn a bowl bid on Saturday. Kickoff is set for 12:30 on the CW and here are three players, two keys to the game, and a predication.

Edwards Jr. remains questionable for Saturday after leaving last week's game against Louisville early in the third quarter. We won't know for sure until that first drive, but for a Bill Belichick-coached team, the idea that Edwards Jr. won't be playing in this game could easily be one giant facade.

On the field, Edwards Jr. has had a solid year with the Tar Heels. His best performance came on Halloween in an upset bid that fell just short against Miami, where he went 28-for-34 through the air for 341 yards and four touchdowns. He's tossed for over 2,200 yards on the season to go with 18 touchdowns and six picks on the season.

Interestingly, there's an alternate world where Edwards Jr. is taking snaps for the Cavaliers this weekend. Two Decembers ago, Edwards hit the transfer portal after starting his career at Maryland and was on a visit at UVA the same day Chandler Morris wound up committing to Tony Elliott and the 'Hoos. He enrolled at Wisconsin not long after, where he played in just two games due to a knee injury, then wound up back in the portal and eventually to Chapel Hill.

One of the bigger stories heading into this game revolves around Smith III and the suspension he'll be serving as a result of the scuffle at the end of that Louisville game. Smith III will be out for the first half of Saturday's game, which comes as a big break for UVA's passing game. Not only does Smith III lead his team with three interceptions, he also owns the fourth-most tackles on this Carolina team. The 'Hoos put up 424 total yards of offense against Cal last week, 223 of which came through the air. You would hope Beau Pribula and the receivers could find a way to take advantage of his absence for at least those 30 minutes.

No matter who is taking the snaps at QB, Thurman will be a huge factor in how UNC's offense fares. He's firmly second on this UNC team in catches with 50 and has hauled in eight touchdowns as a reliable safety blanket for Edwards Jr. at 6-foot-6, 245 pounds. He'll by far be the best athlete the 'Hoos have seen this year at the tight end spot. Thurman's well on his way to playing on Monday's, Thursday's, and Sunday's in the near future. He looks more like a receiver than a tight end once he catches the ball and gets downhill.

The 'Hoos were great at forcing Cal to play one-dimensional offense with how they played the run. UNC comes in averaging 114 yards per game on the ground, a mark that's 122nd in the country through their 10 games. They have a handful of guys in Benjamin Hall and Demon June who have shown flashes despite that statistical ranking. UVA will need to keep that rushing number low for a second-straight week, any potential sack yardage aside, without the likes of Landon Danley and Maddox Marcellus out there once again.

Based on the availability report, it appears Caleb Hardy will be back to add a dose of experience alongside Kam Robinson and as another body to slot into the linebacker rotation with Myles Brown and Justin Rowe.

It didn't accumulate to 40 points or anything extraordinary last week, but I thought it was one of the few games this season where the offense looked the way it was built to in terms of how they attacked Cal. 22 offensive points went up on the scoreboard thanks to Beau Pribula making things happen with his legs when needed for 67 yards, coupled with 223 yards of passing offense between both he and Eli Holstein. Four different running backs had 20 or more rushing yards and when Pribula needed short, reliable yards against pressure, Jekail Middlebrook was there to bail him out as a checkdown.

It wasn't the most attractive brand of offense, but those methodical drives for scores gave a glimpse into what this group can do when dialed in. The Tar Heels are average defensively from a yardage perspective. They're in the eighties in rushing and passing yards allowed and are second-worst in the ACC at getting off the field on third down. We simply shouldn't see the 'Hoos sputter on offense in the ways they did against the likes of SMU, Duke, and Wake Forest in this game, but can they take a bigger step forward against a Carolina team that has the athletes to punish Pribula and the offensive line if things aren't buttoned up?

UVA is 5-1 at home this season and 0-4 in games outside of Scott Stadium. It's a jarring split, especially looking ahead to next week. For now, the 'Hoos sit as a 1.5-point favorite; essentially a pick 'em with respect to this game being in Charlottesville.

UVA's pass rush found ways to stifle Jaron-Keawe Sagapolutele all game long, totaling four sacks from four different contributors. UNC has allowed at least one sack in all 10 of its games this season. They've given up three or more in five of those. Talk about good timing for this UVA defense.

This is another big oppurtunity for UVA to dictate the terms of the game through their time of possession. It's unlikely we see UNC produce enough on the ground to be able to lean on its running backs 60 minutes and their offensive line has its inconsistencies as well. They'll likely have to throw the ball to win here, meaning another good pass-rush performance would be big in putting UNC in more predictable passing downs behind the sticks to disarm their offense and get the ball back to Pribula to chew additional clock.

I feel weirdly confident in UVA in this spot overall. It would have been so easy to come and look flat last week on a three-game losing streak late in the season. This team clearly still cares, no matter how bad we think Cal really is. These games in November are about who wants it more and it appears UVA still has the right mindset. I'm on this team getting its sixth win here by a healthy seven points.
