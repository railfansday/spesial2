Microdrama Revenue to Hit $20 Billion in 2026, but Profits Outside China Remain Thin, Media Partners Asia Report Finds

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Global microdrama revenue will climb 24% to $20 billion in 2026, but outside China the business has yet to prove it can make reliable money, according to new research from Media Partners Asia.

The Asia-focused research and advisory firm’s second annual study of the sector, “The Microdrama Economy 2026,” projects the market will reach $32 billion by 2031, on annual growth of 9.8%. MPA’s figures measure spending by viewers, advertisers and brands before app stores and payment processors take their cut. Outside China, they count only dedicated microdrama apps and their web storefronts, not general social media feeds. China still dominates. The country will generate $15.2 billion this year, or 76% of the global total, after revenue jumped 90% in 2025. MPA expects growth there to cool to 6.7% annually as viewing levels off, taking Chinese revenue to $21 billion by 2031. The firm’s China figure is narrower than the National Radio and Television Administration’s RMB100 billion ($15 billion) estimate for 2025, which also counts producer outlays on AI models and platform investment.

Free, ad-supported viewing overtook paid viewing in China in 2025. Advertising will make up 69% of the country’s microdrama revenue this year and 79% by 2031. ByteDance, which operates Hongguo and Douyin, takes 56% of Chinese revenue, and ByteDance, Tencent and Kuaishou together command 82%.

Dedicated apps outside China will earn $4.87 billion in 2026, a third more than last year, with MPA forecasting $11 billion by 2031 on annual growth of 17.7%. The U.S. remains the biggest single market at $1.71 billion, though that is 2% lower than in 2025. Asia-Pacific excluding China, at $1.44 billion, is set to overtake the U.S. in 2029.

India, at $322 million, now outranks Japan and Korea as the biggest market in Asia after China. It is also the fastest-growing, with revenue more than tripling this year. Local players Kuku TV and Story TV both roughly tripled their takings, and JioStar launched its microdrama service Tadka on JioHotstar in April. India is the only big market where local platforms lead, selling cheap subscriptions that bypass app-store billing. JioStar’s existing streaming audience has reduced its need to pay for new users, and the company is now turning to retention and growing ad revenue.

Revenue is concentrated. ReelShort, DramaBox–GoodShort, Kunlun and NetShort together generate 65% of the total outside China, and each is either owned or financed from China. DramaBox and GoodShort announced a merger on Oct. 31, the first such deal among the sector’s top players, partly so the two would stop outbidding one another for advertising. Fox, Versant and NBCUniversal are among the U.S. media companies that have bought minority stakes in the sector, while India’s and Korea’s biggest local platforms are gearing up to go public.

Some homegrown companies take on the Chinese-owned apps directly, among them Korea’s Vigloo and Fuji TV’s FOD Short in Japan. MPA sees their edge in what the Chinese apps don’t have: built-in audiences, homegrown IP and talent, and a way to bill viewers without payment cards. Japanese and Korean broadcasters, studios and telcos mostly work as producers. Across Southeast Asia, telcos have sold heavily discounted passes to build reach, though MPA said it is unclear whether these bring in new paying customers or simply shift existing ones to a cheaper channel.

TikTok sits on all sides of the market. It sells advertising to the apps, carries their series and competes with them through its free app PineDrama. In Southeast Asia, ByteDance is repeating its China playbook, building audiences with free, ad-funded apps before cashing in. MPA expects TikTok to become a major player in the U.S. and Southeast Asia alike by 2031. YouTube stays open to creators and studios, while Meta mainly serves as the place where apps find new users. Even so, MPA doesn’t expect free platforms to push out the paid apps, which control the IP and the billing relationship with paying customers.

Profits are the sticking point. For each dollar of revenue generated outside China, 56 cents goes to marketing and another 18 cents to app stores and payment processors. In a typical month, just one in 12 users or so pays anything. The two largest groups each bring in more than $1 billion a year, yet DramaBox posted a 3.2% net margin in fiscal 2025. ReelShort lost $12 million in 2025 before swinging to a profit of roughly $8 million in the January-March quarter of 2026.

“Demand for microdrama is established in every major market, but outside China this is still an emerging business, and it has yet to show that it can earn a durable profit,” said Vivek Couto, CEO and executive director of Media Partners Asia. “The leading apps spend more than half of their revenue to acquire viewers, and most of those viewers do not pay. Consolidation has begun, and China has already moved to free, advertising-funded viewing. The companies that endure will be those that retain their viewers, bill them directly and invest in titles worth returning to.”

AI is reshaping supply far faster than demand. By MPA’s count, new titles listed on non-China apps will surge from around 100,000 in 2025 to 440,000 this year, with the AI-generated share climbing from 15% to 75%. Each listing represents a single title in a single language on a single app, so a series that runs in multiple languages or across multiple apps shows up more than once. Producing a title with AI costs roughly 10% of what a Los Angeles shoot would.

Audiences have been slower to switch. Live-action titles hold most of the top 50 spots on five of the six non-China platforms MPA tracks. In China, about nine in 10 titles released in 2026 are AI-made, but they bring in just 20% of the year’s revenue.

“What decides a platform’s economics is how many of its titles earn back the marketing spent on them,” said Myat Pan Phyu, MPA’s lead analyst for microdrama and vertical video. “That favors companies with established IP, stars and audiences of their own, which is why local broadcasters, studios and telecom operators matter to the next phase.”

Payments may shift the math. Apple and Google process 65% of the revenue earned outside China, though legal rulings and fresh legislation in the U.S., Japan and Korea have cleared the way for web payments. Direct billing can lift margins, although the savings depend on each market’s fees and payment route. MPA expects payments made outside the app stores, including through India’s UPI system, to deliver roughly half of the growth outside China in 2027.
