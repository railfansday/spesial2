20 beauty advent calendars for 2026 and what each one offers

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Free daily briefing on global business news.

These 20 calendars from British Vogue's guide range from £90 to £375. Each slide asks what the buyer is paying for.

A beauty advent calendar comes with two numbers on the box. One is the price a shopper pays at the till. The other is a much larger "worth," which retailers print in the most prominent spot to make the purchase feel like a steal. That worth is the seller's own figure, and no shopper can add it up before handing over the money. A shopper looking at The White Company's 2026 calendar, for one, sees a headline worth but would need a price for each item inside to check it. The space between the two numbers might mark a real bargain or a piece of marketing, and nothing on the box tells a shopper which.

People who review these boxes for a living have caught the worth figure overreaching. "Other times I have noticed mini-filled ones with deceptive worth values attached – determined by whether the products were actually full-sized," Ellie Davis, a senior beauty shopping writer, said. A more honest test looks past the total to the contents. It asks how many products are full size, how many are new launches, and whether a buyer would have paid for them anyway. Some shoppers don't want a bargain at all. They want the daily habit of opening a door each morning through December, whether it holds a candle, a bodycare product or a box worth keeping. No comparison of price and worth can measure that pleasure.

British Vogue, which employs Davis as its senior beauty shopping writer, went looking for this year's strongest beauty advent calendars, a search that took in a Korean skincare box from Boots, a Jo Malone fragrance countdown, Liberty's calendar and a Cowshed calendar packed in a wicker basket. Every calendar ahead faces the same question about what a buyer is actually paying for. Some answer it with full-size products, some with new launches and some with the ritual they bring to December. Each calendar passes a particular test. A shopper should weigh that test against the price, rather than any claim that a calendar is the best buy.

Sephora built its calendar around volume. Its 40 items carry a stated worth above £1,000 for a price of £235, according to British Vogue. A box with this many items leaves plenty of room for small sizes, and small sizes can push a headline value beyond what the contents justify. The fair question for this calendar is whether it covers its price without any help from the £1,000 figure.

It does, on five full sizes alone. Five named full-size products have a combined value of £264, which is more than a buyer pays for the whole calendar. The other 35 items come on top of a price those five products already cover, and a buyer can treat them as extras rather than as the reason to buy. That makes the £1,000 headline much less important than it looks, because the case for buying rests on products a buyer can name and price.

The calendar also offers access, because some of its products are sold only at Sephora. Those exclusives come from brands a beauty shopper will recognize, such as Tower 28, Makeup by Mario, Haus Labs, Topicals and Tarte. Because no other store sells them, a buyer has no outside price to compare them with, and that makes access part of what the money buys.

A regular Sephora shopper can justify this calendar's price on what is behind the doors, without believing the number printed on the front.

LOOKFANTASTIC asks buyers to trust the people who chose its contents as much as its price. Its £120 calendar contains £665 of products picked by experts, according to British Vogue. A wide gap between price and worth is common among beauty advent calendars, and the promise about who did the choosing is what sets this one apart. LOOKFANTASTIC pitches the box as a group's selection rather than one tastemaker's, with 25 contributors shaping what goes behind the doors.

A curated box is only as independent as its curators, and the makeup of the panel matters more than its size. The recognizable names are a hairstylist and creators, and the rest of the panel includes LOOKFANTASTIC's own buying team. The shop is helping to pick its own stock. That doesn't make the picks bad, because buyers know their products well. A reader should still treat this calendar as a store's edit with some outside voices rather than an independent jury.

The best test of any calendar is whether a buyer would use what's inside, and LOOKFANTASTIC makes that test easier to pass by selling three calendars this year. A buyer isn't stuck with a single mix. Of the three, the grooming box earns the strongest praise on value. "Last but the most value-packed of all, The Grooming Advent Calendar costs £90 and has £469 worth of products inside, according to the retailer," said Ellie Davis, British Vogue's senior beauty shopping writer. For a buyer whose routine centers on grooming, that lower-priced box may be a smarter buy than the flagship. The reason isn't a bigger claimed worth. It's that this buyer is more likely to find products behind its doors that they would have bought anyway.

A buyer can judge Space NK's calendar without taking its value on trust, because its full-size products settle the price question before the first door opens. Bought on their own, six of its full sizes would cost £276.50, according to British Vogue. Space NK charges £275 for the whole calendar, slightly less than a buyer would spend on those six products alone. Everything else among its 35 products comes on top of a price those six already cover, and that makes the rest of the box a genuine extra rather than padding built into a high price.

The calendar's headline worth of over £1,200 hardly matters here. No item-by-item breakdown backs that total, but a buyer has no need to believe it when six named products already justify the spend. Ellie Davis, British Vogue's senior beauty shopping writer, is wary of inflated worth figures, yet she makes an exception for this box. "No girl maths even needed," Davis said. She also finds that Space NK's calendar impresses her year after year with the balance between its full-size products and its minis. That suggests the retailer's generosity with full sizes is a habit rather than a one-off promotion.

The bargain is real only for a buyer who would use the six full sizes behind it, from the skincare and haircare brands Naturium, Color Wow, Augustinus Bader, Medik8, Olaplex and Summer Fridays. A buyer with no use for those products gets far less from the box. For that buyer, the case for the calendar rests on the headline worth, which nobody can check product by product.

Space NK's calendar passes the full-size test without question, and it passes the bargain test for anyone who already has those six names on a shopping list.

Harrods mixes luxury minis with some full-size products, the kind a buyer finishes rather than tries once. The usual worry with a luxury department store's calendar is that shoppers pay for the name on the box and then open door after door of small samples. A buyer can set that worry aside after counting the full sizes, which total £192.50 on their own, according to British Vogue. A shopper might otherwise pay for products of that size at a beauty counter. A buyer can judge this calendar by what ends up in the bathroom cabinet and treat the minis as a bonus rather than the whole reason to buy.

Part of what Harrods offers in the box is an invitation to spend more rather than a product to use. The calendar includes a £75 voucher toward a beauty device, and that voucher has value only for someone who goes on to buy a device. A shopper who never planned to buy a beauty gadget gains nothing from it and should leave it out when weighing the price. A shopper already looking for a device can count the full £75. The sound way to size up this calendar is to count the full sizes first and then decide whether the voucher matches a purchase already on the shopping list.

For most buyers, the case for Harrods rests on its full-size products alone.

ESPA sells a spa routine to recreate at home through December more than it sells a set of beauty products. That difference between price and worth is what makes the calendar feel like a bargain. It is also the claim a buyer should weigh most carefully.

The worth figure is the weakest reason to buy this calendar. The stated worth is £200 higher than last year's, a rise that makes the bargain look sharper but tells a buyer nothing about what changed inside. A bigger worth means something only if the products are large enough to earn it. "Are the value prices authentically based on the sizes of the formulas within?" said Ellie Davis, senior beauty shopping writer at British Vogue. A buyer can't answer that question by looking at the box, so the headline worth is the part of this calendar to discount, not the part to pay for.

What ESPA reliably delivers is a nightly habit. The calendar contains 25 products, so the buyer has something to open on every day of the countdown. The named contents are treatments to take time over rather than products to swatch and forget. A Pink Hair and Scalp Mud asks for the kind of slow, spa-style session that is the calendar's whole promise. With a Winter Spice Candle among the contents, the daily opening also becomes part of a December evening.

A month of small spa nights is what a buyer of this calendar is really paying for.

The case for this calendar has to rest on which products are inside, not on how big they are.

Someone who already wears Charlotte Tilbury would likely have bought many of these bestsellers anyway, and that is the strongest reason to buy the box, even though most of those products come as minis.

This is a 12-day indulgence for the brand loyalist, not a bargain for anyone counting full sizes.
