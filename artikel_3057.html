Applied Materials vs. Intel: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Intel looks cheaper on sales, yet its forward earnings multiple is more than double Applied Materials', and a sizable net loss complicates the picture.

By Brendan Coffey – Updated Nov 24, 2026 at 2:30PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Applied Materials ›

As chip demand evolves, investors must decide between Applied Materials (AMAT -6.66%) and Intel (INTC -8.15%). Both play vital roles in the global tech ecosystem but currently travel very different financial paths.

Applied Materials provides the essential machinery used to fabricate advanced electronics, while Intel designs and manufactures the chips that power computers and data centers. Comparing them highlights the choice between a steady equipment leader and a legacy manufacturer in the midst of a massive structural turnaround.

Applied Materials provides specialized equipment and software for the fabrication of semiconductors and advanced displays. It serves a global customer base including logic, DRAM, and NAND memory manufacturers. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, as two specific customers accounted for roughly 19% and 15% of net revenue in the latest annual report.

In the 12 months ended Oct. 25, 2026, revenue reached about $34.4 billion, representing growth of approximately 21.4% compared to the prior year. Net income for this period was about $10.1 billion, a significant increase from the roughly $7.0 billion reported in the previous year. This growth highlights the ongoing demand for sophisticated production tools among semiconductor stocks.

As of its October 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.3x. This ratio compares total debt to the value of shareholder equity, measuring how much a company relies on borrowed money. The current ratio, which measures a firm's ability to pay off its short-term obligations using assets it can convert to cash within a year, is roughly 2.3x. In the fiscal year ended Oct. 25, 2026, free cash flow was approximately $6.2 billion.

Intel designs and manufactures processors and accelerators for cloud, enterprise, and PC markets. The company is currently pursuing an IDM 2.0 strategy, which involves expanding its own manufacturing capacity to serve external third-party customers through its foundry segment. Its three largest customers are highly concentrated, accounting for approximately 43% of net revenue in its latest annual report.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 26, 2026, revenue reached about $59.9 billion, a growth of roughly 12.1% over the prior twelve-month period. Despite the rise in sales, the company reported a net loss of around $12.0 billion. This loss reflects the immense costs associated with Intel's efforts to modernize its fabrication facilities and regain a competitive edge in processor architecture.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.4x. The current ratio stands at roughly 2.0x, indicating a solid ability to cover near-term liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 27, 2025, free cash flow was a negative $4.9 billion. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented roughly 25.1% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Applied Materials faces risks from the highly cyclical nature of the semiconductor industry and significant geographic concentration in markets like China and Taiwan. The company relies heavily on export licenses for sales to China and remains vulnerable to supply chain disruptions and trade restrictions. It also manages ongoing regulatory scrutiny, including a $252 million settlement with the U.S. Department of Commerce in 2026 regarding export compliance.

Intel faces intense competitive threats in its product and foundry segments from rivals such as Nvidia Corp. (NVDA -5.86%) and other major fabricators. Financial pressures have led the company to cancel facility projects in several countries, including Germany and Israel. It also carries risks related to its reliance on third-party lithography tools and ongoing shareholder litigation concerning its financial disclosures and demand in the PC market.

Intel appears cheaper based on sales, but Applied Materials carries a significantly lower multiple relative to its future earnings estimates.

Intel is pressing on with its plan to revive its business. In the spring it announced a partnership with Space Exploration Technologies Inc. (SPCX -5.65%) and Tesla Inc. (TSLA -0.20%) to build Terafab, a massive AI chip-making plant aiming to produce as many as 200 billion AI chips a year. Intel is providing its foundry expertise and its 14A process, an effort to produce 1.4-nanometer chips it announced two years ago and is still developing.

Also this year, Intel signed a deal with Nvidia to help build a new AI GPU called Crescent Island that can work in tandem with Nvidia's Blackwell AI chips, and agreed to help develop an AI-centric chip for the Google division of Alphabet Inc. (GOOG -1.05%), too. Last year, the company raised investments from the U.S. government and SoftBank to improve its capital position.

All that has generated some optimism on Wall Street. For fiscal 2026, sales are seen rising 17% to $61.7 billion, though a large $11 billion net loss is projected, reflecting the cost of expanding its U.S. foundries.

Applied Materials, meanwhile, is the largest supplier in the world of wafer fabrication equipment for semiconductors. Its wide slate of offerings in chip manufacturing gives it inroads with customers by selling integrated solutions across technologies, making it harder for competitors to dislodge it. The business posted a larger-than-expected rise in sales and income for full year 2026, reported earlier this month. The business posted a 22% increase in revenue to $34.5 billion with a 44% jump in net income to $10.1 billion. Wall Street expects full-year 2027 sales to hit $48 billion, with a very healthy 50%-plus gain in net income to $15.2 billion.

For the next few years, management sees AI demand as driving the business's sales and widening its margins.

So which stock is the better buy for 2027? Intel's shares are priced at a premium, given that much-ballyhooed projects like Terafab may not come to fruition. Applied Materials is growing fast, with quick profit growth, so its 15.7x forward P/E ratio seems like a bargain, especially considering the average S&P 500 stock trades at around 19x forward P/E.
