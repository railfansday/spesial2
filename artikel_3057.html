AppLovin vs. Magnite: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Two ad-tech stocks, one with explosive growth and sky-high margins, the other a conservative bet on connected TV, with very different balance sheets.

By Sara Appino – Updated Nov 27, 2026 at 12:56PM EST

The digital advertising landscape is evolving rapidly, forcing investors to choose between specialized platforms. Should you bet on AppLovin (APP +5.35%) or Magnite (MGNI +2.42%) for your long-term portfolio growth?

AppLovin focuses on high-performance mobile app marketing, while Magnite operates as a sell-side platform connecting publishers with advertisers across connected TV and digital audio. Both companies benefit from the shift toward programmatic advertising, where software automates the buying and selling of digital ad space to reach specific audiences more efficiently.

AppLovin provides a marketing platform designed to help businesses advertise and measure their performance, primarily within the mobile gaming sector. The company utilizes proprietary technology to power major mobile games and is currently expanding its reach into broader consumer brands. It maintains strategic relationships with major industry players like Alphabet (NASDAQ:GOOGL) (NASDAQ:GOOG), Meta Platforms, Amazon (AMZN +3.96%), and Unity Software (U +4.27%), though it often competes with these same entities.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, AppLovin's revenue reached $7.6 billion--a 57.8% increase year-over-year on a continuing operations basis (excluding the divested Apps business). Net income was $5.0 billion, yielding a 65.9% net margin (the share of sales kept as profit after all expenses).

As of December 2025, its current ratio was 3.3x (over three times more current assets than short-term liabilities) and its debt-to-equity ratio was 1.7x (total debt relative to shareholder equity). Full-year 2025 free cash flow reached $3.95 billion after operating costs and capital expenditures.

Magnite operates as an independent sell-side platform, helping digital publishers monetize their content across connected TV, online video, and mobile displays. By serving as a bridge between media owners and ad buyers, the company facilitates ad placements in approximately 190 countries worldwide. It has established key partnerships with organizations like LG Ad Solutions and recently began working with OpenAI to integrate ad placements within advanced technology environments among communication stocks.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $766.7 million, growing by roughly 9.1% over the previous twelve-month period. The company reported net income of approximately $180.8 million, leading to a net margin of close to 23.6%. While revenue growth is more moderate than its peer, the positive net margin indicates the company is successfully converting a portion of its sales into actual profit.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.7x, suggesting a conservative level of total debt relative to shareholder equity. The current ratio stands at approximately 1.0x, indicating that current assets, such as cash and accounts receivable, roughly equal current liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was nearly $165.6 million. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 32.5% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Magnite is significantly dependent on advertising spend within the connected TV market, which could be impacted if programmatic auction adoption slows. The company also deals with high customer concentration, as two buyers accounted for approximately 44% of its revenue in 2025. Furthermore, it faces intense competitive pressure from larger participants like The Trade Desk (TTD +2.48%) and PubMatic (PUBM +3.18%) while navigating a complex legal landscape involving litigation against major tech firms.

While AppLovin carries a higher price relative to its sales, both companies trade at very similar valuations when considering their future earnings estimates.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with AppLovin. Advertisers are spending more on ads that deliver measurable results, and AppLovin's AI engine sits right in the middle of that, deciding which ad each person sees in mobile apps and, increasingly, online stores. And it's paying off in a big way. Revenue is up 55% year over year, and about two-thirds of every sales dollar ends up as profit.

The company isn't without baggage, though. It still leans heavily on mobile games, and it faces investor lawsuits and short-seller claims that it disputes. Still, I'm excited to see that its latest quarter beat the company's own targets by a wide margin.

Magnite is doing plenty right. It helps streaming services and publishers sell ad space, its streaming TV business is growing fast, and it earns a healthy profit. But it is a much smaller business growing more slowly, and a record midterm political-ad season gave last quarter a lift that is hard to repeat.

AppLovin, meanwhile, is growing much faster and has extraordinary profit margins, yet its stock isn't any pricier than Magnite's compared with the profits analysts expect. For long-term investors, an AI ad engine this profitable, at a similar price, makes AppLovin the better buy for 2027.
