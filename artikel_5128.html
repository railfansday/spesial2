Bitcoin faces sustained selling pressure as spot ETF outflows and derivatives market deleveraging weaken market sentiment.

Hawkish Federal Reserve expectations and upcoming US inflation data add to uncertainty surrounding BTC's recovery.

BTC's $58,000 support zone remains crucial, while reclaiming $60,000 is key to improving the near-term outlook.

The Bitcoin price yet again slipped below $60,000 and is struggling to reclaim the levels as persistent selling and uncertainty surrounding the US Fed’s monetary policy weigh on market sentiment. After briefly recovering above $64,000, BTC lost momentum and plunged below the psychological support level, extending its November decline. Adding to the uncertainty, investors are waiting for the PCE gauge and the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge.

Meanwhile, US spot BTC ETFs continue to record net withdrawals, underscoring weakening institutional demand. As the token shows signs of deleveraging in the derivatives market, investors are assessing whether the latest correction could extend toward lower support levels or whether an oversold market could trigger a recovery before month-end.

Ever since the BTC price breached below an important support zone around $62,000, the bulls have been struggling to keep up the rally above $60,000. Despite the latest push that elevated the levels above $64,000, the price quickly dropped below $61,000 and eventually below $60,000. Along with the bearish sentiment across the markets, these factors have been contributing to preventing the Bitcoin price from rising above the pivotal range.

Institutional demand through US spot Bitcoin ETFs remains weak. Spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded approximately $569.7 million in net outflows on November 23, marking the fifth consecutive session of withdrawals. November’s cumulative outflows reached approximately $4.71 billion, exceeding the previous monthly record. The chart also shows another $231.56 million in estimated net outflows on November 24, highlighting continued selling pressure.

The chart shows aggregated open interest falling from nearly $28 billion in early November to $20.59 billion. Meanwhile, the aggregated funding rate remains marginally positive at 0.0054, suggesting bullish positioning has not completely disappeared despite the price correction. The liquidation data also highlights continued forced closures of leveraged positions. This combination indicates markets undergoing deleveraging.

Expectations of further monetary tightening by the US Federal Reserve are adding to uncertainty across risk-sensitive markets. The Fed maintained its benchmark interest rate at 3.75%–4.00% at its October 27–28 meeting. The minutes indicated that almost all policymakers considered another rate hike in December appropriate under their baseline outlook. With the October PCE inflation report due on November 25, investors are also watching whether persistent inflation could reinforce the case for further rate increases.

Bitcoin continues to trade below the $60,000 psychological level after losing its previous support around $61,500 to $62,000. The daily chart shows a sustained sequence of lower highs and lower lows, with BTC falling sharply from its October highs near $90,000. The recent recovery attempt has also failed to establish a sustained move above $60,000, keeping sellers in control of the near-term price structure.

The RSI has dropped into the oversold range, which indicates intense selling momentum and suggests that BTC could be approaching conditions for a short-term relief rally. Buyers would need to regain control and push BTC above immediate resistance to establish a meaningful recovery.

Bearish scenario: If BTC loses the 57,800–58,200 support zone on a sustained basis, selling pressure could accelerate towards $55,000, followed by the major reference level near $52,881.

Bullish scenario: A recovery above $60,000 could provide initial relief. However, buyers would need to reclaim the 61,500–62,200 resistance zone to improve the short-term outlook. A sustained move above this region could expose $64,500.

Consolidation scenario: If buyers defend the $58,000 region but fail to reclaim $60,000, Bitcoin could remain range-bound between approximately $58,000 and $62,000 through the November close.

The Bitcoin price remains under bearish pressure as ETF outflows, derivative deleveraging and hawkish Fed expectations weigh on sentiment. Although the oversold RSI offers scope for a relief bounce, BTC needs to reclaim $60,000 to improve its recovery outlook.

The $57,800 to $58,200 support zone remains crucial. A breakdown could expose $55,000 and $52,881, while a sustained recovery above $62,200 could open the way towards $64,500. With US inflation data and the Fed’s December decision in focus, Bitcoin’s direction into month-end remains uncertain.
