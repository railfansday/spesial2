Not Alphabet. Not Meta. This Is the Digital Advertising Stock I'd Buy Right Now.

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

Amazon's ad revenue grew 12% last quarter, and management expects high-teens growth next. Here's what's driving it and how the stock is valued.

Alphabet and Meta Platforms may dominate digital advertising, but there's another company building an impressive advertising business. Amazon (AMZN +0.88%) is expanding its e-commerce ecosystem, artificial intelligence (AI) capabilities, and streaming platform, making it a top pick for investors in digital advertising right now.

In its third-quarter 2026 results on Oct. 29, Amazon reported advertising revenue growth of 12% year over year, excluding foreign exchange effects. Looking ahead, management expects advertising growth to accelerate into the high teens in the fourth quarter.

In fact, growth is becoming harder to overlook.

What makes Amazon's advertising business stand out is its direct access to shoppers who are already looking to buy something. That edge is what sets it apart from traditional digital advertising platforms.

The company has turned that advantage into a growing advertising ecosystem. For example, Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands allow businesses to promote their offerings directly in shopping results, while display and video advertising extend their reach beyond Amazon's marketplace.

Plus, the business is becoming increasingly sophisticated, combining shopping behavior, browsing activity, streaming engagement, and purchase information to help advertisers target consumers more effectively. With the rise of AI, Amazon's advertising capabilities just got an upgrade.

One of the biggest developments is Amazon's push to integrate AI into its advertising process. For example, in September, the company introduced Amazon Ads Agent, an AI-powered platform designed to help advertisers plan, create, and optimize campaigns. As a result, instead of requiring advertisers to manually manage multiple campaigns and advertising formats, they can outsource much of the work to Amazon's AI.

Today, its new Full-Funnel Campaigns feature brings sponsored ads, display advertising, video, and streaming television together in a single campaign. In other words, advertisers can simply provide their objectives, products, and creative materials, while AI handles campaign planning, execution, and optimization. This could be a game changer for smaller businesses that lack the resources and expertise to manage larger advertising campaigns.

Amazon is also using AI to simplify creative production. Its advertising tools can help businesses generate video advertisements, images, storyboards, and other marketing materials using existing product information. With that, Amazon isn't just selling ad space. It's also providing the technology that businesses need to advertise more efficiently.

Meanwhile, Amazon is expanding its advertising footprint through its other product lines. For years, Amazon's biggest advertising opportunity was closely tied to its e-commerce marketplace. Now, the company is going beyond that, with Prime Video being central to its new strategy.

By introducing ads to its streaming service, Amazon has created another way for brands to reach consumers beyond traditional search. Its growing sports programming also gives advertisers access to valuable audiences that are difficult to reach through traditional digital formats.

Thursday Night Football is one example. The program averaged 17.7 million viewers through the first six games of the 2026 season, marking its strongest start yet. Amazon is also expanding its NBA coverage, giving advertisers more opportunities to reach sports audiences.

But the bigger opportunity now is how Amazon connects these advertising formats. For example, a consumer might see a brand during a live sports broadcast, encounter a sponsored product while browsing Amazon, and eventually complete a purchase through the marketplace.

Still, Amazon offers something far bigger than traditional advertising: the ability to reach customers, influence their purchasing decisions, and measure sales within a single ecosystem. That raises the question of how much investors are paying for Amazon.

At a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of just 22, Amazon is reasonably priced, especially with many Wall Street analysts rating the stock a buy. Not only that, the average price target is $327, implying a 33% upside over the next year.

These targets are possible as Amazon becomes more than an e-commerce and cloud computing giant. Its advertising business is emerging as another powerful growth engine, making it a solid investment in the next phase of digital advertising.
