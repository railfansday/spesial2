How UTSA's recharged culture fuels championship contention beyond the stats

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

At some point during team meetings each week, UTSA coach Jeff Traylor stands in front of the Roadrunners and sorts through some updated stats and rankings. More often than not, the numbers aren't very flattering.

No. 89 in the FBS in passing. No. 35 in rushing. No. 97 in total defense.

Traylor is far from surprised. After UTSA pulled out a 19-17 win against UAB last week, Traylor said he didn't believe the Roadrunners boast any unit that stands as the best in the American Conference between offense, defense and special teams.

But for all the holes in their resume and statistical profile, the Roadrunners are No. 1 in the place that matters most - the league standings. UTSA hosts Tulsa at 11 a.m. Friday in the Alamodome with a chance to secure home field in the American Conference championship game, riding an unorthodox formula built on attitude and intangibles to push toward one of the program's best seasons.

"How are you winning? Well, you sure as heck know it's not coaching, right?" Traylor said. "It's got to be those players, and it's Ubuntu, 'I am because we are.' We have the best culture in the country."

The Roadrunners have stressed that "Ubuntu" mindset since the start of the season, leaning on the African philosophy stating that an individual's success and well-being is tied to the success and well-being of those around them.

What else could explain how well the Roadrunners cover for each other despite rarely performing at a high level in all three facets? The defense carried UTSA to a 16-14 win against Rice, a 35-16 drubbing of North Texas and Saturday's 19-17 survival against UAB, while the offense was the biggest driver in two shootout wins: 59-45 against Colorado State and 38-34 against Navy.

"That's why this is the greatest team sport, because it's not who has the greatest special teams, or the greatest offense or defense," offensive lineman Ben Rios said. "It's really just who collectively has the best unit, and that's what I like about this team."

UTSA Roadrunners head coach Jeff Traylor after the game against the Rice Owls in Houston, Texas, Saturday, Oct., 3, 2026. (Zach Del Bello/San Antonio Express-News)

For Traylor, the identity is another sign that his retooled approach to roster construction is paying dividends. The Roadrunners "just recruited a different kind of kid" this cycle, Traylor said, prioritizing championship experience and character over talent and athleticism.

With the new team solidified and mostly on campus by January, the Roadrunners jumped into their identity and culture talks immediately rather than waiting until the start of preseason camp. UTSA's "Triangle of Toughness" has been a major talking point for Traylor dating to his first season in 2020, and he wanted to be sure this year's Roadrunners understood that before kickoff rather than learning through the season.

"Our culture, and our togetherness, and the Triangle of Toughness, is just, it's back to what it used to be," Traylor said.

A longtime high school coach, Traylor had grown used to instilling that culture over a four-year cycle with each player, allowing the veterans to set the tone for the newcomers.

He admits he "didn't do a very good job" of adjusting as that timeline tightened the previous two years. With transfer portal windows open in both the winter and following spring practice, Traylor's roster wasn't solidified until the summer, leaving less time for culture building.

Though college football is still transient by nature, Traylor took advantage of the move this year to eliminate the spring transfer window. By the end of January, the Roadrunners had built the roster they'd ride with through the year, leaving more time for an identity to take hold.

"Night and day. You could just tell from the locker room talk, and just the culture, and the guys in general," Rios said. "I know each and every person in my position room. I know their background, and that's just something that we've had in the past, but I just think that it's another level this year."

As UTSA's seniors prepare for their final regular-season home game Friday against Tulsa, Traylor stressed that the veteran returners were also critical to the recharged identity. He highlighted defensive lineman Tai Leonard for embodying that mindset, as the fifth-year senior turned down a single-digit jersey and pushed focus away from individual stats to instead concentrate on his role as a thankless defensive anchor up front.

"We are here because of the triangle, our culture, some great leadership, and some unbelievable gutsy kids that have made some unbelievable plays in the perfect timing," Traylor said. "That's what I'm hoping to see again."

The result is a team that has erased deficits in five wins this season, including a pair of double-digit comebacks.

Once again, the Roadrunners are earning votes as a top 25 team in the country despite ranking in the top 25 in hardly any statistical category.

"We may not have the best certain units, maybe defense or offense," cornerback RJ Lester said. "But all-around team, I think we're one of the better ones."
