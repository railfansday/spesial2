What’s Your Vacation Vibe?

Tanggal: 2026-11-20

Whether brand new or reimagined, these four hotels offer distinctive stays. The only question is, which style to choose?

To be a discerning traveler is to have, in equal measure, preference and knowledge of the endlessly evolving hotel landscape. But even the greatest of globetrotters need a guide to what’s new every once in a while. Below, find four recently opened (or reopened) hotels, organized by travel preference: Looking for something in the city? We’ve got you. Country? We have that too. A place to hot air balloon? No worries. The toughest part will just be figuring out where to go first.

Time and Place: Perched atop the Spanish Steps, this 133-year-old mainstay is Old World luxe translated with fresh verve following a lavish update with revamped public spaces by ELLE Decor A-List designer Jacques Garcia.

Design Vibes: Delving into the archives for the Palm Court Garden redo, Garcia collaborated with Italian firm Colleoni Roberto & C. on a green-and-white-striped upholstery and with a Roman blacksmith on vintage-style lanterns.

Hide Out In… One of the damask-covered suites or at the renovated Michelin-starred Imàgo restaurant.

Time and Place: Some will recognize this property as the former home of the Standard, sans human fish tank at the check-in desk. Actually, sans any check-in desk. There’s also no room service, but there is a rooftop park with a gym, a neon blue pool, and 360° views.

Design Vibes: Legendary hotelier Ian Schrager, with his “luxury for all” philosophy, teamed up with minimalist maestro John Pawson on the architecture. Rooms feature custom-made plush seating, pale woods, and custom lighting systems for shifting moods.

Hide Out In… Your own private cineplex. Each room has a projector and 66-inch screen. Or disappear on the dance floor of the Studio, a speakeasy-style nightclub clad in marble and velvet.

Time and Place: Occupying a colonial-style former school in Old Bennington dating from 1829, this intimate inn was brought to life by Rafe Churchill, co-founder of the Elle Decor A-List firm Hendricks Churchill, and Casey Sunderland, Churchill’s partner in the new hospitality firm Place in Mind.

Design Vibes: The hotel leans into its links to a family of writers and to nearby Bennington College with an inviting library stocked with books by alums. Each of the eight unique guestrooms is cozily layered, mixing rich jewel tones and florals with antiques and art that ranges from the Hudson River School to Keith Haring.

Hide Out In… The gardens. Landscape designer Grace Fuller Marroquin lent her wild, cinematic touch to the grounds, which feature an array of native plants as well as a fruit orchard.

Time and Place: Steps from slopes that hosted ski events for two Olympics (1956 and 2026), the hotel occupies a former palazzo from the 1920s.

Design Vibes: Eschewing the traditional Alpine look, designer Achille Salvagni tailored the guestrooms with bespoke references to the local landscape and culture, like plaster bas-reliefs of mountains, ceiling motifs based on traditional clothing embroidery, and hardware in the shape of native wildlife.

Hide Out In… A hot air balloon. The hotel arranges private tours in the off season for unparalleled mountain views. Or, more down to earth, the spa includes a cryo chamber and a plunge pool.
