‘What if the input isn’t the news? What if the input is you?’ Bill Gross gave away 1.2 million AI business ideas for free—the Fortune 500 is next

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

A month ago, Idealab founder Bill Gross gave away 1.2 million AI-generated business ideas for free. Now he wants Fortune 500 companies to pay $250,000 a year to run the same system on their own patents.

“What if the input isn’t the news? What if the input is you?” Gross told executives at the Fortune 500 Innovation Forum in Detroit on Monday, in a session sponsored by Idealab.ai. He meant their patents, trade secrets and “all the things that you’ve shelved inside of your company.” Idealab plans to license the full system to companies at $250,000 a year, he said, and is offering five companies a one-year pilot for $25,000.

The pitch is aimed at a real problem: big companies hold a lot of patents that never become products. Large firms’ patents are tied to new product launches “far less so” than smaller firms’, according to research from Kellogg’s Sara Moreira and economists at Yale and the Atlanta Fed, which matched about 1.4 million products to patents. Moreira’s explanation complicates Gross’s pitch, though: big companies often use patents as a “defensive tool” to block competitors rather than to launch anything. Some of that shelved R&D is on the shelf on purpose.

Gross launched Idealab.ai in October after feeding an AI system news stories going back to 2000, every patent since 1980, product recalls, and complaints on Reddit and X, along with his own 31 methods for coming up with business ideas. He said it came back with 1.2 million ideas after about a month and roughly $1 million in AI costs, and that 131,000 people in 202 countries have searched them since launch.

To test the corporate version, Gross went to Caltech. He is a Caltech alumnus and trustee, and he said on stage that he sits on its board. He asked the tech-transfer office for four patents. “They said, ‘Why just four? We’ll give you all 9,000,’” he said. He said the system ran all 9,978 and generated 3,867 businesses that Caltech “should either build or license.”

For scale, Caltech’s tech-transfer office signed 59 licenses and options and helped form eight startups in fiscal 2024, according to its annual report.

For the forum, Idealab ran the public patents of 113 attending companies through the system and printed each a sheet of business ideas “that you’re not already doing,” Gross said. “Now that’s a teaser,” he said. “What we really want to do is work with you and have you give us the things that aren’t public.”

Earlier in the talk, Gross said companies would get their own instance of the system to run on their own servers, so their proprietary data would stay in-house.

The $250,000 corporate license is meant to pay for the free public version. “Big companies with 100 billion dollar year R and D budgets will pay for it, and that way we can give it away free to entrepreneurs,” he said.

Gross said he modeled Idealab, founded in 1996, on Thomas Edison’s lab, and quoted Edison: “I find out what the world needs, then I go ahead and try to invent it.” The test now is whether a corporate R&D department, which has had decades to find out what the world needs from its own patents, will pay Gross $250,000 to tell it.

Nick Lichtenberg is business editor and was formerly Fortune's executive editor of global news.
