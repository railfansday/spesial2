The Best Natural Gas Stocks to Buy and Hold Through 2040

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

With natural gas demand on the upswing, EQT Production and Kinder Morgan have the potential to reward long-term investors.

By Todd Shriber – Nov 28, 2026 at 2:05AM EST

Investing for the long term is the way to go, and 14 years certainly qualifies as an extended time horizon. Still, market participants shouldn't confuse patience with the importance of stock selection.

That's especially true with natural gas stocks, a notoriously volatile corner of the broader energy sector. The turbulence associated with natural gas equities is, of course, the result of natural gas being one of the bumpier commodities and one known to turn big gains into losses in the blink of an eye.

Natural gas demand is rising and that could stoke gains for these two stocks. Image source: Getty Images.

Consider "natty's" knack for volatility one of the costs of admission in this part of the energy patch and a reminder that stock selection is paramount for short-term traders and long-term investors alike. For those in the latter camp, there's a fair amount of appropriate natural gas stocks to consider, including EQT Production (EQT +0.64%) and Kinder Morgan (KMI +1.20%).

Already one of the largest producers of natural gas in the Appalachian Basin, EQT Production is cementing that dominance with the recently announced $13 billion acquisition of Range Resources (RRC +0.93%). That transaction creates the largest domestic natural gas producer and a company valued at more than $50 billion.

In essence, what buyers of EQT shares are embracing is the notion that scale is effective and that long-term natural gas demand will continue trending higher. Regarding scale, Range Resources controls over 750,000 acres in the Marcellus Shale in Southwest Pennsylvania. More importantly, the shelf life of Range's acreage in that region is superior to that of its competitors, meaning that when EQT closes the deal, it will continue pumping in the Marcellus Shale for years to come. At the same time, some rivals may be forced to look elsewhere.

Two-thirds of Range's acreage is natural gas, but a slice of the company's Pennsylvania land also includes natural gas liquids (NGLs), which is a higher-value energy source. That's likely not lost on EQT, and the data confirm the potential efficacy of this deal. Exports of NGLs have been surging for more than two decades, reaching record daily highs at various points in 2026.

Another consideration for prospective EQT investors is the $20 billion in free cash flow the combined company is expected to generate over the next five years. That's a big number, but it's not out of the realm of possibility because EQT generated almost $2.2 billion in free cash flow in the first half of this year.

Like many pipeline stocks, Kinder Morgan serves up a compelling income proposition. The shares yield 3.7%, and following a payout cut in 2016, the company has been on a nine-year run of dividend increases.

That is to say, this stock is one way for income-hungry investors to tap into the natural gas trade. Kinder Morgan operates 78,000 miles of pipelines in North America, and 40% of all natural gas transported in the U.S. flows through its infrastructure. So while it's not an exploration and production firm, it is intimately tied to the U.S. natural gas industry.

For long-term investors, that's attractive because some analysts believe Kinder Morgan can thrive even if the commodity's price sags. That may not happen. The dual tailwinds of NGL demand and rising electricity consumption could propel a 25% surge in domestic natural gas demand by 2030.
