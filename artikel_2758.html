UTSA film study: How an unlikely duo combined for a game-changing trick play

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Facing fourth-and-1 late in a critical game, UTSA coach Jeff Traylor elected to roll the dice, trusting a combination of freshman reserves who had never been called upon like this in their careers.

Freshman quarterback Mark Wiepert, an Oregon transfer, had only ever been used for tush push and punt return, and freshman tight end Ty Rupe was still searching for his first career catch in his second year with the Roadrunners.

But with less than seven minutes remaining and UTSA aiming to protect a two-point lead Saturday in Birmingham, Traylor dialed up a trick play that resulted in a 27-yard connection between the two unlikely sources.

UTSA's offense lined up in a tight huddle around the ball as if the Roadrunners were attempting a quarterback sneak, but Wiepert instead took the snap and rolled out to his right. Rupe, lined up on the right edge of the line, blocked the nearest defender at the snap but quickly disengaged, leaking into open space on an out pattern.

The connection to extend UTSA's drive allowed the Roadrunners to chew more time off the clock, helping UTSA escape with a 19-17 win Saturday at Protective Stadium in Birmingham, Ala.

"We have a lot of faith in Mark Wiepert and Ty Rupe, and the look was there," Traylor said. "I can't stand coaches when the look's there, and they don't have the intestinal fortitude to make the call."

Before the successful trick play, the Roadrunners lined up and attempted a quarterback sneak from the same formation, but Traylor called timeout to negate the play. The Blazers appeared to defend the initial attempt well, stopping Wiepert's push right around the line to gain.

Using the timeout to switch the call to the pass, Traylor shared some of the credit with UTSA athletics director Lisa Campos and president Taylor Eighmy, saying he never has to worry about the duo second-guessing his in-game decisions. The Roadrunners typically follow the modern analytics approach and attempt a conversion on spots like this fourth-and-1 from UTSA's 49-yard line, but Traylor said a punt would not have been out of character given the struggles of both teams' offenses.

The Roadrunners were also unable to turn to some of their usual short-yardage staples, as the absence of running back A'Marion Peterson through the second half due to injury left UTSA without its primary threat from the jumbo package that uses eight offensive linemen.

Watching from the sideline because of the substitution package, UTSA quarterback Owen McCown said he had faith in Wiepert, comparing him to longtime NFL utility player Taysom Hill.

"It was awesome. Mark's our Taysom Hill guy. He does it all," McCown said. "Wasn't surprised one bit when Mark executed that perfectly."

The Roadrunners also received unexpected contributions from a special teams unit that Traylor said played "beyond fantastic."

UTSA scored its second touchdown of the game after forcing a fumble on a UAB kickoff return. Safety Joel Rogers charged forward and punched the ball loose around the 20-yard line, and at least a half-dozen players had a shot at the recovery, pushing the ball backwards toward the end zone before Jamel Hardy jumped on it for a touchdown.

"What an unbelievable play causing the fumble," Traylor said. "And then Jamel Hardy, the most unselfish guy on the team, totally selfless, scores the touchdown. How happy I am for him."

Traylor also credited punter Caile Hogan, the American Conference's Special Teams Player of the Week, for downing five of his seven punts inside the 20-yard line, including a pair of kicks that forced UAB to start at the 6- and 1-yard lines.

"The special teams is what might've got lost in this story, how incredible Caile's been punting the ball," Traylor said. "I can't tell you how much he worked sky punt, and just really proud of Caile that he's executing that so well right now."

Cornerback RJ Lester was also honored as the American's Defensive Player of the Week after notching five tackles, two tackles for loss, a forced fumble and a pass broken up in the end zone.

Lester said the forced fumble, which led to a field goal early in the third quarter that gave UTSA a 17-14 lead, started when he read his assigned receiver was blocking rather than running a route. Lester had a clear path to pursue quarterback Ryder Burton around the outside, and Lester attacked the play with a forward lunge, jarring the ball loose with his shoulder pads.

"When the quarterback pulled, I just saw him and pulled the trigger," Lester said.

Lester also played a critical role in a safety early in the fourth quarter, giving UTSA more breathing room at 19-14. Taking possession at the 1-yard line after Zach Morris downed Hogan's punt, UAB elected a run up the middle to try to create some breathing room.

Linebacker Darion White attacked through a gap in the offensive line but stumbled to the turf, creating an obstacle that forced rusher Braylon McReynolds to look for an alternate path.

McReynolds moved left to escape White's grasp but was immediately met by Lester, who bought time for nose tackle Jusiah Sampleton to snare McReynolds around the ankles from behind before defensive lineman Eddy Smith swarmed to wrap up and finish the play.

"When you see a chance to get points on the board, you've got to seize it," Lester said. 'I just tried to hop on him and get our two."
