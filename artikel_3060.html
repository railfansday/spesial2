Darling: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-11

Accounting timing masks the fuel segment's true earnings power, but management expects deferred profits to resurface as margins normalize.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 29, 2026, Darling (DAR -1.83%) announced third-quarter combined adjusted EBITDA, a metric measuring cash earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization across core operations and joint ventures, reached $514 million. Cash generation from its renewable fuel operations allowed the company to rapidly reduce debt and fund share repurchases. Lower leverage gives the business flexibility to return more capital to shareholders over time. Three things management said stand out.

Darling reduced net debt by $420 million in the quarter while repurchasing $76 million of stock. Cash distributions from its fuel joint venture are helping lower its leverage ratio, opening the door for a potential dividend or expanded buyback program.

Third quarter results for Diamond Green Diesel, Darling's joint venture renewable fuel producer known as DGD, were impacted by short-term hedging losses and feedstock rules under standard accounting rules, known as GAAP. Management expects these deferred profits to flow back into financial results as fourth-quarter spot market margins improve.

Darling sees steady global demand for its core feed and protein ingredients. As the federal Renewable Volume Obligation, or RVO, mandates higher biofuel blending volumes, rebounding global fat prices will support cash flows in its core processing segments.

Cash flow remains high across global operations, and leadership isn't hiding its satisfaction with operational momentum. It maintains structural advantages in low-cost fuel production and global rendering scale while focusing on pricing discipline.

With leverage dropping toward targets, management is prioritizing organic growth and disciplined capital allocation.
