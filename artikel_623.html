Elizabeth Holmes’ Partner Billy Evans Writes Her an Emotional Thanksgiving Love Letter: ‘Our Most Beautiful Days Are Still Ahead of Us’

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Billy Evans, Elizabeth Holmes’ partner and the father of their two children, took to X on Nov. 26 to write an emotional Thanksgiving message to her.

The text is primarily about how much he misses her and how their kids need their mother. Holmes is currently serving an 11-year prison sentence for four wire fraud-related convictions tied to her high-tech health company, Theranos.

“Today is our fourth Thanksgiving with Liz in prison,” Evans wrote. “Despite everything, I have so much to be thankful for. Our two beautiful kids, a love that’s only grown stronger through all of this. The incredible kindness of people who have stood beside us. I’m thankful for all the life we still have ahead of us. The memories yet to be made, the good we can still do in this world. The post also included a picture of Holmes with one of the kids.

Evans and Holmes’ relationship was the focus of Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s hit meta-documentary “You Can See Everything,” which recently crossed $100 million at the global box office.

Evans wrote the Thanksgiving message on his personal X account. In the film, it is revealed that Evans had been controlling Holmes’ X account while she was incarcerated and writing combative messages that did not seem to be in her voice.

Read the full message and see the X missive below.

Today is our fourth Thanksgiving with Liz in prison

Despite everything, I have so much to be thankful for. Our two beautiful kids, a love that’s only grown stronger through all of this. The incredible kindness of people who have stood beside us

I’m thankful for all the life we still have ahead of us. The memories yet to be made, the good we can still do in this world. I really believe our most beautiful days are still ahead of us

But time is precious, and there will never be enough time together

Ivy was three months old when her mother went awayWills wasn’t even twoThey’re three and five now

I’ve spent the last 3 and a half years trying to make visiting their mother in prison feel normal. Building our own little worlds in that prison yard. Reading them her letters at bedtime. Trying to keep her a part of every little moment of their childhood

Liz is serving an 11-year sentence for a nonviolent crime. She was acquitted of every charge related to patients. Investors themselves acknowledged that their losses caused them no financial hardship

Whatever you think of Liz, what purpose does keeping her there serve? Who benefits from taking another year of their childhood away from them? How much good could she do with her freedom?

There’s so much beauty in our lives that I wish I could share with her. Every time something wonderful happens with the kids, she’s still the first person I want to tell

I would give anything to have her sitting beside us at the table today
