Huddersfield Town head coach Martin Drury on a win for the memory bank at Doncaster Rovers

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Despite their excellent start to the season, it remains to be seen if Huddersfield Town are toasting promotion come late spring and no-one is getting ahead of themselves just yet.

But if they do, they will remember events on a freezing Thursday night at the end of November in Doncaster, which might just get a special airing on the big screen.

Two-nil up thanks to goals from captain Cameron Humphreys and top-scorer Joe Taylor, Town were in cruise control before Bali Mumba conceded a penalty for the second successive time in this particular league fixture in 2026 – at the very same end of the stadium – and also received a second yellow card in the 34th minute.

Isaac Hutchinson converted the resultant spot-kick and it had the makings of a long final hour for the high-flying Terriers.

Instead, they drew strength from adversity.

Those in away jerseys had each other's backs, but critically maintained an attacking threat – confirmed when Ilias Bronkhorst scored the all-important third goal in front of 1,905 delighted away supporters.

Rovers pulled one back before the end, but it was Town's night at a venue where they hadn't so much as scored a goal in their previous four league visits, all defeats coincidentally.

After putting ghosts to rest at Valley Parade and the Club Doncaster Sports Village – following poor losses at their Yorkshire rivals last term – it's a case of two down, one to go on the derby front in 26-27. Town head to Oakwell on December 19.

On a keynote victory, head coach Martin Drury, whose side drew level on points with leaders Stockport County, said: "I loved it. I think in the past, you lose that game.

"Probably the biggest compliment I feel I can pay the players is I felt real belief and real confidence in them, even when we went down to ten.

"I really believed in them and I think they believed in themselves. I think they can continue to do that because they're a good group.

"I think we'll look back in months to come and go and say this result was a big one, a really important one.

"I thought they were well-connected, from start to finish.

"When we've had setbacks so far this season, we've responded really positively to it. So it gives us a lot of belief.

"They put a lot more players in the top line (with an extra man). You have a risk and drop in to make the same numbers up, but then you're really underloading the rest of the pitch.

"So we had to try and defend a line with one less player and move across the pitch as quick as we could. I felt the players did that really well. And that's probably why Doncaster didn't create as much as maybe they would have wanted."

Drury's opposite number Grant McCann pulled no punches after the loss and acknowledged his side are where they are in the table – 21st – for a reason.

The Rovers boss continued: "We need to be better at both ends of the pitch, that's the reality.

"We've conceded far too many goals and we're not scoring enough goals. I know we've scored four in the last two games but we need to be better.

"There's so much for us to do and improve on.

"At the minute, we look a little bit like a team that's sort of passing the buck on to each other a little bit and I felt that in the second half."
