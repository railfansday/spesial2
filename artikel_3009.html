Archer’s Anduril Drone Begins Full-Scale Flight Tests in 2027. Here’s Why Defense Could Be Worth More Than Air Taxis.

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Archer's defense push helps diversify while its commercial air-taxi business awaits FAA certification.

By Courtney Carlsen – Nov 22, 2026 at 1:15PM EST

10 stocks we like better than Archer Aviation ›

Air taxis may attract headlines, but Archer Aviation's (ACHR -9.06%) biggest opportunity could lie beyond commercial passenger flights. The electric vertical take-off and landing (eVTOL) aircraft developer has teamed up with defense technology company Anduril to develop hybrid-electric VTOL aircraft, with Thunder's first flight planned for 2027.

For investors, Thunder's debut reveals another side of the Archer story: a defense business that could eventually outgrow its commercial air-taxi operation. Here's why Archer's military push is worth a closer look.

Archer Aviation develops the Midnight aircraft, its flagship aircraft for its commercial air taxi business. The company is currently working through a multi-year Type Certification process with the Federal Aviation Administration and is in the final phase of the four-stage process. It has conducted piloted flights in Northern California, and CEO Adam Goldstein hopes to begin passenger flights in 2027.

While commercial air taxis get the attention, Archer is making a wise decision to diversify into defense with a hybrid-electric variant of its Midnight aircraft in collaboration with Anduril. Because military procurement operates under the Department of Defense airworthiness standards, Archer could generate revenue and validate its hardware with real-world flight hours before its commercial air taxis scale.

In July, Archer and Anduril unveiled the hybrid-electric VTOL military variant, Thunder, and a commercial cargo variant, Halo. What makes these VTOLs intriguing is that they give the military an aircraft that falls somewhere between small tactical drones and expensive helicopters.

Unlike helicopters, these aircraft can fly uncrewed into contested airspace without risking the lives of highly trained military pilots or the expensive Viper and Apache helicopters. Meanwhile, like helicopters, these vehicles can take off and land vertically, operating from small airfields, operating bases, or small naval decks without needing traditional runways.

It will take time for Archer Aviation's consumer air taxi business to take off and reach commercial scale, especially since it's an entirely new form of transportation. By expanding into defense, the company can get its aircraft flying and open itself to the potential to win major government contracts, which could provide long-term revenue visibility and stability.

Earnings from Thunder and Halo will likely be limited in the near term, as Archer ramps up test flights in 2027. However, Archer has expanded its defense footprint by acquiring Insitu, Wisk Aero, and SkyGrid from Boeing. Insitu adds $200 million in defense revenue immediately, and Archer also inherits its customer relationships and offices in the U.S., Australia, the U.K., and the United Arab Emirates.

Archer Aviation's air taxis could change how we travel across cities, and passenger flights will begin on a limited basis in 2027, with full commercial operation projected for 2028. That said, some experts think 2028 is overly ambitious, so in the near term, defense provides a financial bridge that will keep the company growing even if commercial air taxi rollouts slip past 2028.
