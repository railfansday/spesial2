Tanggal: 2026-11-18

Debt-free C3is posts a 249% revenue jump as tanker and dry bulk rates surge.

By Motley Fool Transcribing – Nov 18, 2026 at 2:53PM EST

Management reported significant growth in revenue and earnings for the nine-month period. C3is (CISS -30.93%) increased its total fleet capacity and diversified its operations through the addition of product tankers. The company maintained a debt-free balance sheet while funding its expansion. Executives indicated that favorable market conditions in both the tanker and dry bulk sectors supported improved time charter equivalent rates. Strategic focus remains on acquiring non-Chinese built vessels to avoid potential tariff impacts.

Slide five shows the dry bulk market and the time charter rates. The dry bulk market continues to shrug off relatively steady levels of fleet growth thanks to healthy demand and ongoing disruption, whether it be port congestion, canal diversions, or slower steaming.

The demand for iron ore and steel is attributed to the rapid urbanization, large-scale infrastructure development, accelerating electric vehicle production, expanding renewable energy installations requiring structural steel, and industrial manufacturing capacity expansion. The structural changes in the iron and steel industry will lead to lower life cycle waste and inefficiencies, higher construction productivity, and better opportunities to develop standardized yet flexible design across infrastructure, automotive, energy, and other industrial applications. Global coal demand is set to increase due to the crisis in the Middle East and an unusually strong El Niño weather pattern.

Although shipping disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz do not directly affect coal markets, tighter natural gas supply has pushed up prices, prompting some electricity systems to switch from gas to coal. Global grain demand and market conditions in 2026 are shaped by rising prices, constrained trade routes, and steady food and feed consumption. Saudi Arabia's grain demand is rising under Saudi Vision 2030, driven by rapid expansion in tourism, mega projects, hospitality, and domestic livestock requirements. The kingdom is positioning itself as a regional grain storage and distribution hub to insulate domestic food supply chains from geopolitical and shipping disruptions. Minor bulks are the commercial foundation of the Handysize markets.

A vessel trading the Atlantic or Pacific basin may carry several different cargoes within a single trading year. Examples include fertilizers from Baltic to Brazil and petcoke from the U.S. Gulf to India. Other cargoes include cement from Mediterranean to West Africa and steel products from China to Southeast Asia. This cargo diversity gives operators flexibility, but also exposes them to a wider range of commodity-specific market drivers. Bauxite stands out as the fastest-growing and strongest-performing minor bulk commodity, increasingly driving shipping and ton-mile demand. Q3 was a superb period for the dry market. The BDI averaged over 3,000, its best quarter since the COVID-driven boom of 2021.

Asia-Pacific dominates the market with the largest revenue share, and the global market for construction steel is anticipated to drive iron ore consumption worldwide. Bauxite flows by September 2026 had increased 20% compared to the same period in 2025. El Niño was already being identified as a potential support factor through three key channels: longer grain trade routes, increased coal demand as hydroelectric generation weakens, and potential disruption at the Panama Canal. The common denominator is not necessarily higher commodity volumes; it is higher ton-mile demand. The major Middle East conflict and disruption around the Strait of Hormuz have reshaped the shipping market, yet dry bulk has stayed relatively insulated and has benefited from longer routings.

Slide seven shows the Aframax LR2 rates and age. The U.S.-Iran war was at least notionally on hold in Q3, but trade routes are under increased attack. Transits via the strait rose significantly despite a surge in Iranian attacks. The adjacent Houthi-Saudi war spiraled, with tankers, ports, and pipelines hit. Meanwhile, ship attacks in the Black Sea escalated further. Despite more Hormuz transits, energy prices surged in Q3 due to continued attacks on energy infrastructure and trade, lower inventories, and fears that the Middle East conflict could re-escalate after the U.S. and Israeli elections. As of September 30th, 2026, Aframax spot earnings rose sharply across all reported routes.

On the Aframax fleet, by the end of the third quarter of the year, there was a 5.59% increase in the total fleet. The global Aframax fleet now stands at 1,266 vessels, of which 290 vessels are over 20 years of age, accounting for 22% of the total number of vessels. With a starting tally of 1,199 vessels, the current fleet represents a change of 5.6% in vessels number and around 5.8% in deadweight over the year so far. Over the last quarter, the fleet has increased by 22 vessels. The age of our Aframax tanker was 16.19 years by the end of Q3 2026.

The highest number of Aframax tankers are in this category of 15 to 20 years, which is around 27% of the total fleet. Slide seven shows the global oil demand and the product tanker rates. Our latest two new additions to the fleet, the product tankers Clean Fury and Clean Reaper, were delivered in Q2 and Q3 2026. Africa and Asia-Pacific account for roughly 95% of the global net capacity growth through 2030. The Iran war has disrupted flows of Middle East refining and Persian Gulf feedstocks, tightening product markets and shifting the advantage towards Atlantic Basin refiners. BloombergNEF sees limited impact on the medium-term refining capacity pipeline so far.

An increase in Gulf export via Hormuz or the Gulf of Oman put more barrels on the water, yet still below pre-war levels. Chinese export quotas were relaxed, leading to a rise in Pacific cargo. However, Russian exports were choked by refinery attacks, but volumes may start to recover slightly with a slowdown in attacks and the U.S. waiving sanctions on diesel exports. Product tanker fortunes in the near term will continue to be driven by the continued strength of the crude tanker market. Until something ends the current super spike in crude markets, cargo splitting and LR2s switching to dirty trades will continue to drag up rates for all product tanker sizes. The near-term outlook is healthy.

Even when the Arabian Gulf trade normalizes or more pipelines open, there will be more cargo with the added demand boost of enormous buying to restock oil or product inventories, both commercial and strategic.
