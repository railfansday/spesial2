"Short-Sighted": Delta CEO Vows To Fight Heathrow’s Move To Split It From Virgin Atlantic

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Delta Air Lines CEO Ed Bastian has publicly criticized Heathrow Airport's decision to move the airline to Terminal 4 in 2028, calling the decision "short-sighted" and "bad for airline consumers." The criticism comes after a once-in-a-decade review at Europe's busiest international airport resulted in five airlines being ordered to move terminals in 2028, including Delta.

This change has been met with upset after both Delta and Virgin Atlantic, in which the American carrier holds a 49% share, argued that it will move the US carrier away from its UK partner and put the airline at a disadvantage to its competitors. Bastian has vowed to "pursue all available remedies" to reverse the decision.

As part of Heathrow Airport Limited's review, Delta will be required to relocate to the airport's Terminal 4. This comes after more than a decade in which Delta and Virgin have cohabitated in Terminal 3. This one-terminal approach for the two airlines has enabled convenient connections, premium shared amenities, and a collaboration to provide travelers access to the airlines' two extensive long-haul networks.

Having the two airlines co-located in Terminal 3 has provided a valuable competitive alternative to other legacy carriers operating transatlantic flights. When separated, this will undermine the years of investment that the two airlines have spent in the last decade, and it is suggested that the decision has disregarded the customer benefits that are offered by the co-location. In a lengthy statement released on Monday, Bastian said:

"Heathrow Airport Limited’s decision to relocate Delta’s operation to Terminal 4, away from our partner Virgin Atlantic, is short-sighted, deeply disappointing and bad for airline consumers . . . Separating our operation undermines years of investment and disregards the tightly integrated customer benefits that we offer in our co-located terminal."

As detailed in Bastian's statement, both Delta and Virgin are planning to fight this decision, and Delta is urging Heathrow Airport Limited to reconsider the decision. Virgin is considering its legal options, and SkyTeam, of which both airlines are partners, has argued that the move would put its members at a disadvantage to other airlines sharing terminals.

For Delta, the move matters as they are arguing that the relocation to Terminal 4 would split the airline from its joint venture partner after more than a decade. This would result in connecting passengers between the two airlines being forced to connect across the airfield by bus transport.

Heathrow Airport Limited has said that the once-a-decade review would free up available capacity at the airport, while easing congestion at one of the most slot-controlled airports in the world.

Heathrow's decision to separate Delta and Virgin Atlantic sparks competition concerns and potential legal challenges.

For US carriers, Delta is tied second with United Airlines by total destinations served at Heathrow, with both airlines serving seven year-round US gateways. They trail behind American Airlines, who operate year-round to nine US airports, and add a sole seasonal service.

Virgin Atlantic operates year-round to ten destinations in the US, with a seasonal service added during the Northern Hemisphere summer season to Seattle. The full Heathrow network for both airlines is below:

In Heathrow Terminal 3 currently, Delta and Virgin share a number of facilities and infrastructure, including the Virgin Atlantic Clubhouse, providing an iconic lounge for eligible business class passengers and top-tier frequent flyers. Travelers also have access to the Virgin Revivals Lounge, which provides lounge facilities for arriving passengers.

Shared ground labor, check-in, and transfer coordination also see the two airlines working collaboratively for the benefit of passengers and airline operations.
