United Airlines Cancels 11 Boeing 757 Routes: See Full List Now

Tanggal: 2026-11-16

United Airlines has removed the Boeing 757 from 11 routes this winter, compared to its schedule from the 2025/26 winter season. Eight of the routes are domestic, including six serving Florida, while the other three are international services from United's Houston hub to Honduras and Costa Rica.

The biggest change is on its Newark–Orlando route, which accounted for almost half of departures across all 11 affected routes. Nonetheless, United will still operate the 757 on 52 routes this winter, covering various transcontinental, Hawaii and transatlantic services, as it begins replacing its 757-200 fleet with new Airbus A321XLR and A321neo Coastliner aircraft.

Over half of United's 757 route cuts involve Florida, with three Florida airports and six routes dropped since last winter. However, when measured by departures, Florida routes accounted for 272 of the 355 departures, representing just over three-quarters of flights. In addition to Newark–Orlando, United has removed the 757 from Chicago O'Hare–Fort Lauderdale, which recorded 48 departures last winter, and Newark–Tampa, which had 27 departures.

The other Florida routes are Los Angeles–Orlando with 18 departures, Los Angeles–Tampa with ten, and Washington Dulles–Orlando with six. The two remaining domestic routes are Washington Dulles–Houston with 19 departures, and Denver–Las Vegas. At 627 miles (1,009 km), the latter is the shortest route on the list.

All three cut international routes depart from Houston, serving San Pedro Sula and Comayagua in Honduras, as well as San José, Costa Rica. Each route recorded 19 757 departures last winter, with the aircraft last operating in January 2026.

United will continue operating the 757 on 42 domestic and 10 international routes this winter. Premium transcontinental services remain an important part of its domestic network, including Newark to both San Francisco and Los Angeles; Boston to San Francisco and Los Angeles; and Washington Dulles and Reagan National to San Francisco.

The 757 was originally planned to be phased out for the 161-seat A321neo Coastliner on Newark's California routes this summer. However, due to delays from Airbus, United said earlier this month that its first Coastliner will not enter service until early 2027.

The 757 also remains on several routes connecting United's hubs, including Chicago O'Hare to San Francisco and Los Angeles, and Denver to Newark, Houston and Washington Dulles. Internationally, Newark continues to use the 757 for flights to Edinburgh, Dublin, Porto, and Lima, while Houston will also see international 757 service to Bogotá, Lima, and Quito.

United and Delta have kept their aging 757s airworthy for years and years.

United operates 61 Boeing 757s, comprising 40 757-200s and 21 757-300s. The airline first introduced the 757-200 in August 1989, before inheriting its 757-300 fleet through its 2010 merger with Continental Airlines. While the 40 757-200s are targeted for replacement in the near future, the 21 757-300s do not yet have a confirmed retirement date and have been earmarked for a cabin revamp, suggesting they have many years of service left.

The 757-200s will be replaced by new Airbus A321XLRs and A321neo Coastliners. The carrier previously projected that the 757-200s would leave the fleet in 2026, but that timeline has since slipped due to delays with replacement aircraft. As it stands, none are scheduled for retirement in 2026.

However, United will begin stepping up the retirement of older aircraft across its fleet through 2027, stating this summer that it will phase out at least 80 aircraft next year. The airline has not said whether any 757s are among these retirements, though it has confirmed plans to retire its older Airbus A319 and A320 aircraft by 2030.
