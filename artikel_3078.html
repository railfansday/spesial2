Not AMD. This Is the 1 Company Nvidia Should Worry About Most.

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Broadcom's custom AI chips are impressive.

By Keithen Drury – Nov 27, 2026 at 12:15PM EST

Nvidia (NVDA +0.80%) investors have had a rough go recently. First, the stock wasn't being fully valued due to skepticism about AI spending. Then, its co-founder was forced to resign as CEO. Even though Jensen Huang returned to Nvidia on Nov. 23, shares are down 18% this year and more than 35% from its all-time highs.

That's certainly a cause for concern, and one competitor that is looking to gain ground is AMD (AMD +4.56%). AMD also makes GPUs that are direct competitors to Nvidia's products, and is looking to capitalize on Nvidia's missteps.

But, regardless of who makes the GPU, they have the same limitations. There's one Nvidia competitor out there that's taking a different approach and is the one investors should fear most: Broadcom(AVGO +3.82%).

I think Broadcom is better positioned than AMD to capture market share. AMD makes GPUs like Nvidia, but they have a fundamental problem: they're not specifically designed for anything. GPUs have incredible performance and can process multiple calculations in parallel, but being good at all things means they're not great at one thing in particular. In many settings, these computing devices only see one type of workload during their service life. That's wasted potential, and using a computing unit specifically designed for that workload could result in huge cost savings.

That's exactly the idea Broadcom is running with, as it's developing application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) with several partners. The most prominent example of Broadcom's work is Alphabet’s (GOOG +0.54%) (GOOGL +0.68%) Tensor Processing Units (TPUs). These devices have allowed Google to construct one of the most cost-efficient computing infrastructures available, and other companies like Anthropic (ANT +10.98%) have utilized these devices. Furthermore, Alphabet is selling TPUs to outside companies like Meta Platforms (META +3.13%), which shows just how popular these units are becoming.

This is a major threat to Nvidia's empire, because custom AI units offer better computing value than its GPUs. While GPUs will never be fully replaced because they have several uses, as custom AI chips from Broadcom improve, so will the number of workloads they can tackle.

Broadcom expects its AI semiconductor revenue to double in both 2027 and  2028. Nvidia expects 75% revenue growth during next year, so Broadcom is clearly seeing more demand for its products.

Broadcom is the company that Nvidia investors should keep an eye on, and one of the best ways to do that is by investing in it. By owning both stocks, you're investing in the upside of Broadcom's custom AI chips while also keeping tabs on a sold-off Nvidia. The combination could lead to huge gains, and investors would be smart to take advantage of it.
