Breakfast News: Stocks That Let You Sleep at Night

Tanggal: 2026-11-07

Motley Fool analysts share four steady stocks to hold through a market slump.

Some mornings, you open your portfolio and wish you hadn’t. The market falls, and your stocks fall with it.

At The Motley Fool, we hold great businesses through every slump. But some stocks make that a lot easier than others. We asked analysts from Team Rule Breakers and Team Hidden Gems to name the ones that do.

Each of these stocks has a history of moving less than the market. A calm chart only tells you so much, though, so our analysts dig into the business strengths behind each pick’s steadiness.

Costco’s (COST -1.13%) big advantage is in its business model. The warehouse giant buys items in such bulk that it can offer customers dirt-cheap prices. This prompts customers to sign up for membership — and keep coming back year after year, as we can see through the company’s 90% membership renewal rate in the U.S. and Canada, its biggest markets.

Meanwhile, Costco can offer customers these bargains because it doesn’t rely on sales of items in its warehouses to drive profit. Instead, the retail giant depends on something you pay before you even begin your shopping trip. I’m talking about membership fees.

In the fiscal year ended Aug. 30, membership fees totaled $5.9 billion on net income of $9.2 billion. Offering membership is high-margin for Costco because it involves little cost, making it a highly profitable part of the business. And the high renewal rates, at these levels over time, offer investors visibility year after year.

Costco could face challenges such as supply chain issues or pressure from rising inflation, but such potential headwinds are temporary and don’t upset the long-term story. Overall, thanks to Costco’s low prices, membership model, lengthy track record of earnings growth, and hefty special dividends paid every few years, this stock is one to hold with confidence through any market environment.

There are a few stocks in my portfolio that I feel are generally “safe” investments, almost certain to preserve my capital over multi-year periods. But my favorite is Realty Income (O -0.77%), a real estate investment trust, or REIT, that owns more than 15,500 net-leased properties in the U.S. and Europe.

The company has a 50+ year history of responsible capital allocation, creating and preserving shareholder value, and raising its dividend over time (116 consecutive quarterly dividend increases).

Realty Income is likely to underperform in periods of rising interest rates, but across multiple economic cycles, it has proven to be a great investment. In fact, since Realty Income went public in 1994, it has averaged a 13.5% annualized total return.

Mastercard (MA -0.49%) is a company I’ve personally owned for almost a decade now and I can honestly say I’ve never lost a wink of sleep over it thanks to itsmassive payments network that spans the globe.

Last quarter, the company saw 9% growth in transactions and 5% growth in cards. This contributed to currency-neutral growth of 12% in net revenue and 19% in adjusted diluted earnings per share from a year ago. Perhaps even more impressive was the company’s 8% increase in worldwide gross dollar volume, thanks in large part to continued growth in cross-border payments.

Mastercard and its competitors remain subject to ongoing global antitrust and legislative scrutiny on routing and interchange fees, which requires consideration. But that’s also been an ongoing narrative for as long as I can remember. Mastercard’s toll booth business model allows it to consistently repurchase shares (share count is down almost 11% over the last five years), and it pays a modest dividend that will continue to grow over time.

Under CEO Michael Miebach and CFO Ling Hai, Mastercard remains well-positioned to capitalize on the global cash-to-digital payment conversion. The company’s phenomenal margins and strong position in the market leave me excited for its future and plenty of good night’s sleep to come.

I think the healthcare giant Johnson & Johnson (JNJ +1.43%) is a perfect example of a stock for preserving capital. It’s a Dividend King that has increased its payout every year for 64 consecutive years, showing incredible resilience through every recession since the 1960s. That’s exactly the consistency I look for in capital preservation.

It’s also one of only two U.S. companies with an AAA credit rating, higher than that of the U.S. government itself. With a dividend yield around 2% and a conservative payout ratio, it generates massive cash to fund future growth.

It’s a sleep-well-at-night stock because people always need medicine and medical devices in any economy. Investors have rewarded that consistency with strong gains over the past year, and J&J has long been a stable compounder across market cycles.

These stocks probably won’t lead the pack when the market is racing ahead. That’s the trade-off. They earn their place when the market falls, because they make it easier to stay invested.

Keep in mind that a steady past doesn’t guarantee a steady future. These stocks can still drop, sometimes sharply. And a portfolio made up only of steady stocks may miss the growth that drives long-term returns. Most investors will want a mix of both.
