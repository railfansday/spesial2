There is something mildly hilarious about holding a modern SSD in your palm. At just 30 millimeters long, the Sandisk Optimus GX 7100M is roughly the size of a postage stamp — looking less like a serious piece of gaming hardware and more like a spare part that fell out of a watch.

And yet, after testing it over the past couple of months, it is easily a favorite of mine, and for you, the ultimate Swiss Army knife of Black Friday deals. Not only is it damn fast (more on that later), but it’s small enough to not just fit into your desktop or PS5, it can fit snugly into any gaming handheld too.

Save $88 on this postage-stamp-sized 2TB M.2 2230 SSD that packs blistering 7,250 MB/s PCIe 4.0 speeds and durable TLC flash to upgrade your handheld, PS5, or PC — plus a free month of Discord Nitro when you buy direct from SanDisk.

It’s easy to get hypnotized by big sequential read numbers on SSD boxes. This one easily saturates PCIe 4.0 at up to 7,250 MB/s read and 6,900 MB/s write for example.

But it’s when you’re actually sitting on the couch waiting for the massive open world of GTA 6 to load that you see the real heavy lifting. This comes down to two far less glamorous metrics: random reads and queue-depth-one (QD1) sequential reads.

The 7100M nails both of these effectively while not cutting corners on the actual memory chips inside. You see a lot of tiny options use cheaper flash chips, but with the 218-layer TLC flash in here, it’s faster, cooler, and far more durable.

Because the GX 7100M is a single-sided M.2 2230 drive, you’re not locked into a single ecosystem in your gear shuffle:

This is the drive’s natural habitat to fit into. Loading and running games is blistering fast (Forza Horizon 6 fired up and I was in-game in seconds).

Most people forget that the PS5’s internal SSD expansion bay natively supports smaller drives — just unscrew the little metal spacer and move it to the right spot. In my own testing, GTA 6 loaded in roughly 8-10 seconds (the same as it took on the internal storage).

If you’re upgrading a desktop gaming rig (check your motherboard has the 2230 mounting hole) or a laptop, this will do the trick here too. Sustained performance kills here with a Steam game transfer blazing by at 6.4GB/s without every gasping for air.

Of course, if you only ever plan to bolt a drive into a desktop tower and forget about it, you can obviously stick to a standard, full-length SSD.
