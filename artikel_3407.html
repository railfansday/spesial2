HBO’s Most Controversial Ending Is Still Better Than ‘Game of Thrones’

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Creating a classic TV series that stays at the top in quality season after season is a challenge. What seems to be even more difficult to pull off is the series finale. How does something so good, which means so much to its audience, craft a goodbye that fans enjoy? Sometimes a show is able to pull it off. Breaking Bad and Six Feet Under are two examples of last episode perfection. Then there are the ones that drop the ball. Seinfeld and Dexter continue to be mocked decades after their last episodes.

What they went through is nothing compared to the grief that Game of Thrones and The Sopranos received. These HBO series were the peak of television and pop culture during their run, only to crash in the final episode. Game of Thrones deserves the criticism, but it's a different story for The Sopranos. It's actually one of the best finales in TV history.

For eight seasons, Game of Thrones was the epitome of the water cooler show. Week after week, it had everyone talking on social media or at work the next day about the latest shocking twist. If you're a fan, there's a chance you remember where you were when the Red Wedding happened, or when Jon Snow (Kit Harington) came back from the dead. As long as it lasted, Game of Thrones had an end goal. This wasn't Lost, which seemed to just keep going with no true plan. For Game of Thrones, it all came down to who would be sitting on the Iron Throne when all was said and done.

The series finale of Game of Thrones failed spectacularly. It was starting to fall apart before this, with a rushed season turning Daenerys Targaryen (Emilia Clark) bad out of nowhere in a move that didn't feel earned. Her death in the last episode was cheapened by the writing, which had betrayed her. Then came the moment everyone was waiting for. At the very end, it was Bran Stark (Isaac Hempstead Wright) who ended up sitting on the Iron Throne. Fans saw it as a huge letdown, a move done to be unpredictable rather than making any sense.

Even outside of those two big plot points, Game of Thrones tore down its characters. There was the undoing of Jaime Lannister’s (Nikolaj Coster-Waldau) path to redemption by letting him die with Cersei Lannister (Lena Headey), making Tyrion Lannister (Peter Dinklage) look dumb when he had always been two steps ahead, and sending Jon Snow, the hero of the story, into exile. Throwing so much into one episode of Game of Thrones led to nonsensical chaos and went against what we had been shown.​​​​​​

The Game of Thrones series finale has nothing on the hate The Sopranos got when it went off the air in June 2007. Most of the episode works well enough, with Phil Leotardo (Frank Vincent) taken out, and the Soprano family looking like they're all going to make it out of this and live to see another day. However, just like Game of Thrones had a big question shadowing its run, so did The Sopranos. From day one, fans wondered if Tony Soprano (James Gandolfini) would die. Breaking Bad, which had a similar premise, was so successful with viewers by showing Walter White's (Bryan Cranston) actions costing him his life. Certainly, the same fate had to await a mob boss with so many enemies.

The final scene of The Sopranos is full of tension despite so little happening. The setting of Tony sitting in a diner, waiting for his family to join him, is intense because of how mundane it is. For this to be how it ends meant something shocking was coming. Everything has been leading up to this. The final minutes had the audience hooked. Every single time the diner door opens and another person walks in, we wonder if this is it. Are they here to kill our anti-hero? Then it happens, with nothing happening at all. As the diner door opens one more time and Tony looks up, Journey's "Don't Stop Believin'" stops and the screen goes to black.

Viewers hated the finale with a passion. After six seasons and 86 episodes, was this the best they could do? There were no answers in what felt like a cheap way to avoid having to make a choice about Tony's fate. However, it’s actually a brilliant finale, and one that’s earned. The finale rejected the predictable, either with Tony living or showing him going down in a hail of bullets. That last scene is unnerving, and rather than giving the audience what it wants, David Chase refuses to give any answers. All we get is what Tony is experiencing.

When it cuts to black, is life simply going on, or did Tony just get shot in the back of the head? That's for you to decide, although the latter is hinted at earlier when Bobby Baccalieri (Steve Schirripa) tells Tony that you probably don’t even hear it happen when you're shot dead. The Sopranos could have shown Tony die. But then what? By not giving a definitive resolution, it’s all anyone was talking about the next day, and we’re still talking about it two decades later. The not knowing sticks with us and allows fans to create their own conclusion. Love it or hate it, The Sopranos has one of the most memorable finales of all time, and it does so without rejecting everything that had come before.
