Inside Stoke City's turnaround under Mark Robins: How they cleansed 'toxic' dressing room, the medical guru who stopped their injury crisis, a transfer expert armed with Brighton's secrets - and why they won't fall away like last season

Tanggal: 2026-11-28

Published: 21:00 EST, 27 November 2026 | Updated: 21:00 EST, 27 November 2026

Stoke City have been here before. They know how it feels to be among the pace-setters, striding towards the place where they once belonged. They know, too, how easy it is to fall away.

At this stage last season, their first full campaign under Mark Robins, they sat second in the Championship. Would it be their year, a return to the top flight after seven years away, seven years in which they had finished in the bottom half of the Championship every season? It would not. They won three of their last 20 matches, none of their last six, and ended up 17th.

Victory for Stoke – who finished in the top half of the Premier League for three seasons in a row in the 2010s, remember – over Millwall on Wednesday night, their fifth on the bounce, sees them second again. Beat West Ham this weekend, who haven't won in six, and it's time to dream. Will this be their year?

Before a ball was kicked, Opta’s supercomputer had Stoke as the most likely team to be relegated. Robins, however, said things ‘felt different’. Speaking to those around the club this week, there’s a genuine belief they are.

Not that anyone would have thought it three months ago, when a 4-1 thumping at Wolves – a third straight defeat to start the season – left them bottom.

Club bosses wondered whether a change might be needed. The mood in the stands was starting to shift. There had been little appetite to sack Robins in the summer, but now the questions of whether they made the right call crossed a few minds.

Despite a dismal end to last season, and a poor start to this, Stoke stuck with boss Mark Robins - a decision that is paying off

Jack McGlynn leads the celebrations during Stoke's 2-1 victory over Millwall in midweek. They sit second in the Championship

After spending £30million on players over the summer, selling them the vision of what the club was building under Robins, it was felt sacking him before the window had even closed would be a dreadful look.

Stoke stuck by Robins, beat Norwich on deadline day in a gutsy backs-to-the-wall performance, and haven’t looked back since.

One thing that did change after the Wolves defeat, even if it wasn't the manager, was the formation. Sorba Thomas, the club’s leading scorer last season, was sold to Hull for £8million. Not only did it allow Robins to cash in on a player who made it clear he wanted to leave, but also to switch to a wing-back system that now gets the best out of the squad.

Robins and sporting director, Jon Walters, the former Stoke striker who felt the brunt of fan frustration following their collapse last term, have gone about building a club and culture that can finally succeed. The past few seasons have been spent shipping out plenty of rubbish and bringing in players with the right character and mentality.

Insiders talk about how ‘toxic’ the dressing room was just a couple of years ago, and how the standards set were ‘ridiculously low’.

A staggering injury crisis helped wreck their promotion hopes last season, so this summer the club revamped the entire medical department.

Damian Roden, the head of sports science during their Premier League days, returned as head of medical and performance. The club’s Premier League heroes of yesteryear, the likes of Ryan Shawcross and Robert Huth, have hailed Roden as one of the key factors in them finishing ninth three times on the spin under Mark Hughes. Roden’s periodisation model, which adjusts training volume and intensity to keep players at their freshest for match day, underpins Stoke’s weekly schedule.

He’s been joined in the medical bay by Matt Ratcliffe and Mark Sertori, who spent more than a decade at Manchester City and was recently head performance for Roma. So far, Stoke have only had a couple of muscle injuries.

John Coates, co-owner of Stoke (left), and sporting director Jon Walters have found a formula that is working as the club chase a return to the Premier League

George Hirst scores the second of two goals against Millwall. The son of Sheffield Wednesday legend David has been working with Robins, a former striker, on his finishing

The change in the Football League’s financial restrictions from the old-school Profit & Sustainability Rules, which capped losses over a rolling three-year period, to the new Squad Cost Ratio that allows clubs to spend a percentage of their revenue, has been a gamechanger for the Potters.

Suddenly, the shackles are off. Stoke have owners in the Coates family who put in as much money as they can. They recently opened a new £12m first-team building at their Clayton Wood training ground, which boasts a gym, swimming pool, hydrotherapy and cryotherapy suites and a tactics room for video analysis.

Now, they can spend on the squad too without worrying about being hamstrung by losses from previous years. They hope to bring in more players in January. The squad value has soared from around £30m a few years ago to more than £100m now.

Stoke are also finally starting to reap the rewards of their bespoke data-led recruitment model, following in the footsteps of Brighton and Brentford. Like those two clubs, Stoke’s owner John Coates – son of Peter, who founded bet365 – has vast experience in the gambling industry. Their head of recruitment Ian Torrance helped set up Southampton’s ‘Black Box’ scouting database and used data from Tony Bloom’s Jamestown Analytics during his time at Italian side Como.

Nearly all their signings this summer have hit the ground running. Ethan Galbraith, brought in from Swansea in a deal worth up to £10m, already looks like one of the best midfielders in the Championship.

Jack McGlynn, one of two players signed from the MLS alongside Luca Bombino, has been another revelation in midfield and already has five league goals. Justin Devenny brings Premier League experience from Crystal Palace.

Defender Luke Graham, snaffled from Dundee for £2m, has been a rock at the back and has earned his first Scotland call-up. Former captain and centre back Shawcross, one of Robins’ first-team coaches, has worked extensively with Graham, analysing video clips.

Robins, a former Manchester United front man, has been doing one-to-one work with striker George Hirst, who joined for an initial £6m from Ipswich. That’s paying off, too. Hirst scored the winner against Birmingham last weekend and both goals against Millwall in midweek.

Stoke paid compensation to hire Arsenal Under 21s coach Max Porter, who helped bring through Myles Lewis-Skelly, Ethan Nwaneri and Max Dowman with the Gunners. He is in charge of how Stoke move the ball in possession – and appears to be doing a fine job.

The big question, especially after last season’s tail-off, is whether they can sustain this form. Stoke are, on the face of it, overperforming their underlying numbers. Opta’s expected table, which calculates where a side should  be based on their expected goals (xG), has Stoke 16th.

The feeling inside the club, however, is that in games where Stoke took an early lead and looked to defend it, it was always likely their opponents would rack up shots and xG.

If you look at Stoke’s rolling xG average, too, Robins’ side are starting to consistently create more than their opponents. Even if they were getting lucky earlier in the season, they aren’t anymore.

Their first-half performance against fellow promotion chasers Birmingham was hailed by those inside the club as the best Stoke have played in years.

No goalkeeper has kept as many clean sheets as Viktor Johansson, one of the most reliable stoppers in the division

Insiders feel the squad now has the depth to deal with any injuries that do come their way. Midfielder Djibril Soumare came off against Birmingham and is expected to be out for a while but they have plenty of cover. Galbraith missed two games and Stoke won them both.

Fans, for the first time in years, are going into matches expecting to win. They have one of the meanest defences in the Championship. No goalkeeper has kept as many clean sheets as Viktor Johansson, one of the most reliable stoppers in the division.

Stoke’s realistic aim is to finish in the play-offs. They have 35 points so far and know the average to finish in the top eight is around 69. It would need near-relegation form not to achieve that from their last 29 games.

Stoke have been here before. This time it feels different.
