Marvel’s New 8-Part Sci-Fi Thriller Is Officially Streaming From Start to Finish

Tanggal: 2026-11-13

Hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

The funniest detail about Paul Bettany’s return as Vision is that, given the chance to start his life over, he gets a job at a pub in Edinburgh. It’s easy to see the appeal: the last people who got their hands on his body wanted to turn him into a weapon, and the pub gives him somewhere to go every day that reminds him of his wife. Of course, he also has to figure out what to do with memories of a wife and children he can remember loving without quite feeling like the person who loved them.

Then a bounty hunter named Paladin (Todd Stashwick) gets hold of Thomas Shepherd (Ruaridh Mollica), a teenager who might be the son Vision remembers raising. Suddenly, those memories are relevant to somebody besides Vision. Thomas has his own questions about the family he remembers, and there’s only so much either of them can learn about the other while somebody’s trying to capture them. The timing is also excellent for Ultron, who’s been waiting inside Vision’s head with plenty to say.

All eight episodes of Marvel Television’s VisionQuest are now streaming exclusively on Disney+. The finale, “Forever,” completes Terry Matalas’ series, which reunites Bettany with James Spader as Ultron. The two shared one of the quietest scenes in Avengers: Age of Ultron after the battle was over. Here, they get to keep talking long enough for Vision to start taking some of Ultron’s ideas seriously.

Four people, four completely different ideas of what it means to have somebody in your corner. Here is who picked up, what they would ask of you, and what you would get back.

You want the friend who does not leave. Steve is the one who turns up at the door without being asked, who remembers what you said you were worried about, and who has never once made you audition for his attention.

You want the friend who is genuinely exhausting and completely worth it. Tony will forget your birthday, then build you something at four in the morning that you did not know you needed, and hand it over as though it were nothing.

You want the friend who replies in four seconds and apologises for it. Peter is earnest in a way that is almost unfashionable, and he will take on far more than he can carry rather than admit that he is struggling.

You want the friend who insults your shoes, fixes your phone and then quietly rearranges your entire life for the better. Shuri does not do soft reassurance, and that is the reason you would believe her when she says you are fine.

Vision secretly kept a backup of Ultron, which is exactly the kind of decision an Avenger ought to have to explain to the rest of the team. A decade later, it gives Bettany and Spader a deeply interesting relationship to play. Ultron has spent some of that time getting into art and entertainment, and Spader can make his curiosity about people sound genuine even when there’s good reason to wonder where he’s going with it. Vision also has access to the likes of J.A.R.V.I.S. (James D’Arcy) and E.D.I.T.H. (Emily Hampshire), so of all the voices he could turn to, it says something that he keeps listening to the one he had to destroy.

Bettany found his way into Vision’s scenes with Thomas through his own experience as a dad. Speaking to Disney, he said, “My eldest son is not mine biologically, and yet, impossibly, he feels like he is.” There’s a lot of room for a Marvel series to get hung up on the mechanics of how Vision could have a son. Bettany’s answer gets to the part that makes their time together worth watching: Thomas can ask Vision a question about his past and hear the answer from someone who might remember him as a little boy. Whether he wants that man in his life now is a decision he gets to make for himself.

To see how the trilogy wrapped up, watch all eight episodes of VisionQuest now, exclusively on Disney+.

Rebooted after the events that restored his body, Vision has gone into hiding from the people who want to weaponize him. Now searching for purpose, he consults the artificial intelligence personalities embedded in his programming, but a bounty on his head forces him to go on the run with Thomas Shepherd, a mysterious boy who may be the reincarnation of his son.
