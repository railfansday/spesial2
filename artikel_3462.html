An Unexpected Arcade Classic Is Getting a Live-Action Movie From Controversial Studio

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Brought to you by Saudi Arabia’s government

In a film and TV landscape where it feels like every other video game is getting an adaptation, we can add another (unexpected) candidate to the pile. Variety just broke that the ‘90s shoot-‘em-up series Metal Slug is getting a live-action movie. As you might expect, given that Metal Slug publisher SNK is owned by the Saudi Arabian government's Electronic Gaming Development Company (EGDC), the film is being made by the Saudi-backed production studio The Arena.

The proposed movie will follow the characters Marco Rossi, Tarma Roving, Eri Kasamoto, and Fio Germi, who Variety describes as “four misfits and inseparable friends who become the army’s most unlikely squad — until General Morden’s tyrannical uprising forces them to become the improbable heroes standing between the world and an even greater evil lurking behind him.” The inclusion of these characters is unsurprising: Marco and Tarma are the protagonists of the original Metal Slug, while Eri and Fio first appeared in Metal Slug 2. The most recent game in the series was Metal Slug Tactics, a turn-based roguelike RPG that marked a big departure from Metal Slug’s typical run-and-gun gameplay. Another more traditional reboot is in the works.

The movie is being written by James Morosini, an actor and filmmaker who previously wrote, directed, and starred in the 2022 comedy I Love My Dad. Melissa Goodall and Sajan Algiri from The Arena are overseeing the adaptation.

Beyond Metal Slug, The Arena is currently working on several other adaptations of SNK properties. The studio is producing a Fatal Fury movie written by David S. Goyer (Man of Steel, Foundation, The Dark Knight trilogy) and a Samurai Shodown film, with Laeta Laogridis (Altered Carbon, Alita: Battle Angel, Shutter Island) handling the screenplay. The studio is also backing an Art of Fighting webtoon by digital creator Brandon Chen.

In 2022, the EGDC, owned by the Mohammed bin Salman Foundation, acquired a 96.18% stake in SNK. As for the studio producing the new Metal Slug film, The Arena is owned by MBC Group, a Saudi media conglomerate, and the Misk Foundation, also known as the Mohammed bin Salman Foundation.

The Saudi government’s purchase of Electronic Arts and backing of a film studio tie into broader efforts to invest in entertainment products, such as sports leagues, video games, movies, and more. Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund (PIF) recently acquired EA alongside Jared Kushner’s Affinity Partners and Silver Lake. The PIF also has stakes in companies such as Capcom, Nintendo, and Take-Two, as well as ownership in e-sports events like the fighting game tournament EVO.

As for why Saudi investments in the video game industry have proven controversial, these acquisitions have been criticized as a type of “sportswashing,” a method of sanitizing the Saudi government’s image on the world stage by associating itself with “fun” topics, like soccer and golf, instead of human rights abuses. The Saudi government has been accused of exploitation of migrant laborers, enforcement of anti-LGBTQ laws, and the killing of political opponents, such as the alleged murder of Washington Post journalist Jamal Khashoggi. Basically, the Saudi Arabian government would much rather you think about a movie adaptation of that nostalgic game series you used to love instead of these many accusations, all while you contribute to Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's war chest.
