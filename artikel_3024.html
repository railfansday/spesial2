Alibaba vs. Coupang: Which Asia Consumer Giant Is the Better Stock Buy for 2027?

Tanggal: 2026-11-14

Alibaba's profitability stands in stark contrast to Coupang's losses, but the valuation gap between these two regional giants may be the more telling story.

Deciding between Alibaba Group (BABA +0.02%) and Coupang (CPNG -0.65%) requires weighing mature profitability against rapid logistics expansion. Both companies dominate their respective regions, but they offer very different financial profiles for investors today.

Alibaba is a diversified technology conglomerate that powers much of China's digital economy through retail and cloud services. Coupang focuses on end-to-end logistics excellence in South Korea, leveraging its own delivery network to provide unmatched speed to millions of active customers.

Alibaba provides the essential digital infrastructure for merchants and retailers across China and several international markets. Its business model spans e-commerce platforms such as Taobao and Tmall, as well as a growing cloud services segment focused on artificial intelligence. While major customers were not disclosed in its recent filings, the company maintains a massive ecosystem of millions of sellers and brands.

In the 12 months ended June 30, 2026, revenue reached about $156.8 billion, representing growth of approximately 4.3% compared to the prior year. The company reported net income of roughly $11.0 billion during this period, despite the competitive pressures found among retail stocks in the region. This performance highlights the scale of its core operations in both domestic and international commerce.

As of its March 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio stands at approximately 1.3x. The current ratio measures a company's ability to cover its short-term debts with short-term assets, providing a look at its near-term liquidity. Its debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.2x, which measures total debt against shareholder equity, while free cash flow for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, was negative $7.6 billion.

Coupang has built a dominant presence in South Korea by focusing on a massive logistics network that offers rapid delivery services. The company expanded its reach into luxury goods by acquiring Farfetch (FTCHQ +0.00%), which serves customers across 190 countries. It recently strengthened its grocery segment through a partnership with Sartori Cheese to bring high-value dairy products to the Korean market.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached around $36.2 billion, an increase of roughly 7.4% year over year. However, the company reported a net loss of approximately $1.4 billion during this same period. This loss occurred even as Coupang expanded its active customer base and entered new international markets such as Taiwan.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio is roughly 1.0x, indicating its short-term assets match its short-term liabilities. The debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 1.0x, meaning its total debt is equal to its shareholder equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was about $522.0 million, though stock-based compensation represented roughly 26.8% of operating cash flow.

Alibaba faces significant competition in its home market from domestic rivals that are aggressively pricing their services. The company must also navigate a complex regulatory environment that has historically impacted large technology firms in China. Maintaining growth in its cloud and international divisions is essential as the domestic e-commerce market matures and consumption patterns shift.

Alibaba offers a much lower earnings multiple to investors, while Coupang offers a lower price-to-sales valuation to those focused on revenue growth.

Alibaba focuses on defending its cash-rich China e-commerce marketplaces, Taobao and Tmall, while funneling capital into higher-growth AI cloud and AI services. Alibaba is the public cloud leader in China and the owner of the Qwen open- and closed-source models. Yet its retail businesses are losing market share to Chinese rivals, which crimps the business's ability to fund money-losing AI model efforts. That means its AI cloud and compute services are the one impressive growth center for the business. It grew 45% year over year in that sector. One metric in its favor is that the current valuation of Alibaba is attractive relative to U.S. cloud and AI peers.

Alibaba is forecast to continue growing, but some one the Street are skeptical of the company's ability to win in the hyper-competitive Chinese market and to expand its fast-growing AI services business beyond the China-dominated region, especially given the capital investment needed for AI.

Coupang is similarly a dominant retail platform in its primary market of Korea, and it has made great inroads into Taiwan. But a fire at a distribution center and a data breach have made for a tough 2026 for Coupang, with an indication that some consumers who abandoned the platform have yet to return, creating a drag on results. Yet membership in its WOW program has recently reached all-time highs, suggesting the company could start to post improving results in the next few quarters.

Wall Street sees Coupang swinging to a net loss for fiscal 2026, but rebounding in 2027 to post much healthier net income and sales. Forward numbers tend to be speculative, but it's a good long-term sign.

So which stock is the better buy for 2027? Coupang is a fine business but appears richly valued by the market at the moment. Alibaba, meanwhile, is nearly value-stock priced. Even as it comes with its risks, BABA is the stock to buy now.
