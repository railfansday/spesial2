I didn't buy these munis; if I had the equivalent amount of cash I wouldn't buy them now, but ripping off the bandaid would incur a loss of nearly 4%*, and my total portfolio is not THAT big; around $2.8MM.
If you wouldn't buy them today for the price you can sell, why would you choose to keep them, especially when you can sell them for a tax loss?

Just because they were worth more several months ago doesn't mean they are worth that today or that people will think they are worth that next week or next month.

Just ask the people who bought NVDA a few months ago and now have a very nice tax loss harvesting opportunity.

I get not wanting to sell a highly appreciated asset because you don't want to pay a large tax bill all at once.

Not wanting to realize a loss because you hope the price will appreciate is something else. Some people will say both are ineffective behavior and they are probably right.

So, if you don't believe these bonds fit your portfolio needs sell them and use the capital loss to offset other gains in your portfolio and buy what matches what your AA and IPS say your portfolio needs.
I do get that, and I happily sold all her individual stocks for a nice tax loss, which I applied to some appreciated stuff that I didn't want. The ripping-off-of-bandaid approach is actually kind of fun.

However, it would take quite a while to offset a likely $80,000 loss* if I were to sell off the whole thing. I haven't got that much money or that much time left on this earth to utilize that. The low-hanging appreciated fruit has been pretty well picked, courtesy of the losses from the individual stock sales.

I do want to keep 20 or so for income purposes, at least until I get a feel for how things will work over the years. (They do have usefulness, within reason. Just not so dang many of them.) So I am looking for ways to evaluate what I have so that I can rank them into keepers (for the next five-ten years or so) and losers.

Any thoughts for evaluating these guys will be appreciated!
