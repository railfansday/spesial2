Why Draymond Green's NBA ‘loser' comments rubbed Baron Davis the wrong way

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

Why Draymond Green's NBA 'loser' comments rubbed Baron Davis the wrong way originally appeared on NBC Sports Bay Area

Another off-court Draymond Green statement is buzzing around the NBA, and a Warriors icon chipped in to give his perspective on the matter.

Baron Davis, the centerpiece of the 2006-07 Warriors "We Believe" team, was asked about Green calling Phoenix Suns commentator Eddie Johnson, among other critics, "losers" for debating Green's Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame case.

Speaking on the "Gil's Arena" podcast, Davis explained to hosts Josiah Johnson and Gilbert Arenas why those comments bothered him.

"I just don't like the loser thing, man," Davis said. "We've all played. It takes greatness to get into this league. It takes some hard work. Not everybody's gonna win. There's only one champion, so I just don't like the loser thing, because if Michael Jordan called us a loser, I can deal with that. If Kobe [Bryant] called me a loser, I can deal with that. But I think that we move the goalposts."

Davis went on to say that "everybody has their way of judging" players, emphasizing accolades or individual statistics, and gave props to both Green and Johnson, the 1989 Sixth Man of the Year.

While Davis differentiated between winners and NBA champions, he stated the conversation between the two "goes both ways."

"You learn to appreciate everybody because everybody is winners," Davis said. "They are champions, and so if you don't win a championship, that don't mean you can't talk to champions in a certain fashion. I don't agree with that."

The discussion began with a post by Johnson on Nov. 21 questioning whether Green's statistics were impressive enough to make the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame.

Green responded to Johnson's comments before Golden State's 88-87 win on Sunday and was asked about the comments after the game as well.

There is really no player who embodies the debate more than Green, whose Basketball Hall of Fame case is built on intangibles and generational defense despite a lack of elite offensive production.

Green, a nine-time All-Defensive team selection and the 2017 Defensive Player of the Year, never was the best player on the Warriors but played a vital role alongside superstar Steph Curry.

Despite declining at 36 years old, Green was the best playoff defender of his generation and an All-NBA Second Team selection at his peak.

The outspoken veteran's Olympic and college basketball resume at Michigan State strengthens his case for the Basketball Hall of Fame, being named the NABC National Player of the Year in 2012 and holding two Olympic gold medals.

The two Warriors legends might be cemented in franchise history for their impact. but this certainly appears to be a topic they don't agree on.
