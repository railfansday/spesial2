NBA Most Improved Player Power Rankings After Week 3

Tanggal: 2026-11-12

What a wild start to the 2026-27 NBA season. If you looked at the preseason expectations for the Most Improved Player award, you can throw that idea right in the bin, because reality turned out completely different. Most teams have only played about 10 to 12 games, so we are dealing with small sample sizes, but that is honestly what makes it so much fun.

The MIP award is always tricky to predict. Some guys make a massive jump by going from scoring 10 points to 20 points a night, while others completely change their style while helping their teams win actual games. Instead of the usual lottery picks taking normal steps forward, we are seeing bench players turn into stars, younger guards shoot the lights out, and veterans reinvent themselves entirely.

As we wrap up Week 3, the landscape is moving fast. An 82-game season is a long marathon, and injuries, fatigue, and scouting adjustments are coming. But right now, these 10 guys have grabbed our attention and completely flipped their season around. Let's dive right into our updated power rankings, starting from No. 10.

2025-26 Stats: 15.8 PPG, 6.9 RPG, 1.9 APG, 45.0 FG%, 36.3 3P%

2026-27 Stats: 17.0 PPG, 6.5 RPG, 2.4 APG, 50.4 FG%, 44.8 3P%

Jabari Smith Jr. isn't making the massive scoring jump that some of the other guys on this list are showing, but what he is doing with his shooting efficiency is incredible. Entering his fourth year in the league, the former No. 3 overall pick is finally unlocking the pure shooting potential that everyone knew he had.

Right now, Smith is putting up 17.0 points per game for a very solid Houston Rockets team. Bumping your average up by just 1.2 points from last year's 15.8 doesn't sound like a crazy MIP resume on paper. But look closer at how he is scoring. His field-goal percentage has jumped to 50.4%, and his three-point percentage is sitting at a scorching 44.8%.

For a stretch-four whose main job is to space the floor and defend, hitting nearly 45% of your threes changes everything for the Rockets' offense. He is splashing 2.7 triples a night compared to 2.3 last year, and get this: he is doing it while taking fewer total shots (down from 12.6 to 11.9 attempts). That is incredible efficiency. His true shooting percentage jumped from 57.0% all the way to 65.0%.

The only reason he is down at No. 10 is the raw numbers. Voters love big counting stats, and his rebounding has actually dipped a tiny bit to 6.5 boards. If he can push that scoring average past 20 points while keeping up this crazy shooting, he will climb these rankings fast.

2025-26 Stats: 15.0 PPG, 3.8 RPG, 3.7 APG, 44.7 FG%, 33.3 3P%

2026-27 Stats: 17.1 PPG, 5.9 RPG, 5.1 APG, 48.8 FG%, 42.4 3P%

Anthony Black has gotten better every single year since the Orlando Magic drafted him sixth overall in 2023, but the 23-year-old guard has officially taken a huge step forward to start this season. After flashing real promise last year by scoring 15.0 points per game, he is now elevating every single part of his game with starting guard Jalen Suggs sidelined.

Black is currently posting 17.1 points, 5.9 rebounds, 5.1 assists, and 2.0 steals per night while shooting nearly 49% from the field. If you remember his rookie year, where he barely scored 4.6 points a game, this growth is wild. He's doubled his scoring output while becoming a legitimate secondary playmaker and lockdown defensive guard.

The biggest game-changer for Orlando has been his outside shot. Last season, teams would gladly back off him because he only made 33.3% of his triples. This year? He is knocking down 42.4% of his threes on nearly five attempts a game. When a 6-foot-7 guard with great defensive instincts suddenly starts shooting like that, opponents are in trouble.

He has also beefed up his rebounding (up to 5.9 RPG) and passing (up to 5.1 APG). The only reason he is sitting at ninth is the small sample size: he has only played seven games so far due to minor injury tweaks. If he keeps playing like this for another month, he's going way up.

2025-26 Stats: 14.8 PPG, 4.2 RPG, 2.7 APG, 51.5 FG%, 41.2 3P%

2026-27 Stats: 17.0 PPG, 6.1 RPG, 3.6 APG, 51.3 FG%, 32.7 3P%

The Minnesota Timberwolves definitely suffered a big hit with Anthony Edward's injury, but the bright side is that it opened the gates for a scoring leap in Jaden McDaniels, who has always been a DPOY-candidate wing, but he's suddenly found an improved offensive game after last season's leap.

McDaniels is averaging 17.0 points, 6.1 rebounds, and 3.6 assists per game, which are all career highs. This continues a steady climb from 12.2 points a few years ago to 14.8 last season, and now flirting with 18 points. He's also grabbing nearly two more rebounds per night while taking on a bigger load on offense.

His touch inside the arc remains rock-solid, shooting 51.3% from the field despite handling more touches. However, there is one obvious issue holding him back right now: his jumper. After shooting a scorching 41.2% from deep last year, McDaniels has seen his three-pointer cool off down to 32.7% on higher volume (4.5 attempts per game).

His defense, length, and all-around impact keep him comfortably on this list, but he needs that outside shot to start falling again if he wants to crack the top five. If his jumper catches fire, his scoring will easily push past 20 PPG.

2025-26 Stats: 14.8 PPG, 3.4 RPG, 3.6 APG, 51.7 FG%, 43.9 3P%

2026-27 Stats: 18.1 PPG, 4.9 RPG, 4.0 APG, 52.1 FG%, 46.7 3P%

Right behind his teammate, Ayo Dosunmu lands at No. 7. Honestly, you could argue Dosunmu deserves an even higher spot just because of how ridiculously efficient he has been to start the season. Splitting time between Chicago and Minnesota last year, he was already a great role player, but he has unlocked a brand-new level this year.

The former second-round pick is putting up 18.1 points per game while shooting an incredible 52.1% from the field and an absurd 46.7% from beyond the arc. That is not a typo: he is hitting nearly half of his threes while taking over 13 shots a night, backed up by a nearly 90% clip from the free-throw line.

Dosunmu's journey from the 38th pick in 2021 to a nightly 18-point scorer shows what happens when you work your tail off. He has boosted his scoring by 3.3 points while grabbing nearly five boards (4.9 RPG) and dishing out 4.0 assists. He isn't just standing in the corner waiting for a pass; he attacks closeouts, pushes the pace, and makes smart reads.

Why isn't he higher up? Mostly because Dosunmu was already playing at a very high level toward the end of last season, shooting nearly 44% from deep. Going from great to elite shooting is awesome, but voters like to see huge jumps in overall role. Still, if he keeps hovering around 18 points with 50/40/90 splits, nobody will scrap him off.

2025-26 Stats: 12.2 PPG, 2.7 RPG, 2.1 APG, 45.6 FG%, 41.0 3P%

2026-27 Stats: 18.6 PPG, 1.8 RPG, 2.3 APG, 46.6 FG%, 45.7 3P%

If you told anyone a few years ago that Duncan Robinson would be getting Most Improved Player buzz at 32 years old, people would have laughed. We all know who Robinson is: one of the greatest movement shooters in NBA history, a legendary Miami Heat sniper who played huge roles in their Finals runs. But late-career breakouts like this are super rare.

Now playing for the Detroit Pistons, Robinson is having the best scoring season of his entire life. He is pouring in 18.6 points per night while launching 4.2 triples per game at a 45.7% clip. Compared to the 12.2 points he averaged last year, Robinson has added a massive 6.4 points to his average, one of the biggest scoring jumps in the entire league.

It's not just about getting more minutes, either. He is playing roughly 30.9 minutes compared to 27.4 last year, meaning this scoring surge comes from being a focal point in Detroit's offense. He has already dropped some masterclass games this year, including a 27-point night against the Philadelphia 76ers where he canned seven threes, plus 24 against Charlotte and 22 against Washington.

Will his age work against him in the voting? Probably, because voters usually prefer young guys expanding their games. But let's be real: averaging nearly 19 points on elite shooting in your eighth NBA season while spacing the floor for a top team is incredible. If Detroit stays competitive and he keeps draining four threes a night, he'll stay right in this conversation.

2025-26 Stats: 14.4 PPG, 10.8 RPG, 3.0 APG, 1.8 BPG, 70.3 FG% (5 games)

2026-27 Stats: 14.9 PPG, 10.8 RPG, 1.7 APG, 2.3 BPG, 69.1 FG%

Walker Kessler has found a brand-new home with the Los Angeles Lakers, and the fit is an absolute dream. The 25-year-old center has always been a premier rim protector, but playing under the bright lights of L.A. has pushed his profile way up. Kessler is currently averaging 14.9 points, 10.8 rebounds, and 2.3 blocks per game while shooting an insane 69.1% from the field.

To truly understand his season, you have to look past his injury-shortened 5-game stint with Utah last year and remember his breakout 2024-25 campaign, where he put up 11.1 points, 12.2 rebounds, and 2.4 blocks. He has essentially added about four points to his scoring baseline from that year while maintaining elite defense.

The former Jazz prospect has always had great instincts around the basket, making the All-Rookie First Team back in 2023. But playing over 30 minutes a night for the Lakers gives him a massive stage. He doesn't need anything drawn up for him; he feasts on put-backs, lob passes, and rolling hard to the rim, converting 6.3 of his 9.2 shots every night with Luka Doncic remaining the MVP favorite on his side.

Kessler sits at No. 5 because his statistical jump isn't quite as wild as some of the guards on this list, and his numbers are close to what we already knew he could do when healthy. Still, averaging a near double-double with elite shot-blocking on a No. 1-seeded team makes him a deserving top-five feature.

2025-26 Stats: 11.8 PPG, 3.4 RPG, 3.9 APG, 50.5 FG%, 34.3 3P%

2026-27 Stats: 18.0 PPG, 5.4 RPG, 4.4 APG, 51.0 FG%, 33.3 3P%

The sophomore leap is alive and well in San Antonio. Dylan Harper, the No. 2 overall pick in the 2025 NBA Draft, has hit the ground running in his second year and is proving he is one of the brightest young guards in the league.

After a solid rookie season where he put up 11.8 points in 22.6 minutes mostly off the bench, Harper has stepped straight into the starting lineup. He is now playing 31.4 minutes a night and rewarding the Spurs with 18.0 points, 5.4 rebounds, and 4.4 assists per game while shooting a rock-solid 51.0% from the floor.

That is a massive 6.2-point scoring jump, fueled by more shot attempts (from 9.5 to 13.5) without any dip in efficiency. In fact, his field-goal percentage ticked up slightly. Harper has also grabbed more boards (up to 5.4) and raised his free-throw shooting to 85.0%. For a 20-year-old, his cool on the court is way beyond his years.

If there is any hurdle in his MIP campaign, it's that he was already a high-pedigree lottery pick. Voters sometimes hesitate to give the award to guys who were expected to become stars anyway, especially after he finished fourth in Rookie of the Year voting last season, got to the NBA Finals, and was arguably the Spurs' second-best player there.

Even so, jumping to 18 points on 50%+ shooting as a 20-year-old starter is huge. If he pushes his stats closer to a 20/5/5 average, he'll be breathing down the necks of the top three candidates.

2025-26 Stats: 11.8 PPG, 3.3 RPG, 8.2 APG, 49.0 FG%, 39.5 3P%

2026-27 Stats: 16.5 PPG, 2.0 RPG, 8.5 APG, 49.6 FG%, 40.3 3P%

I think his PR is way lower than it should be, and Davion Mitchell deserves massive credit for the way he has completely reinvented his offensive game in Miami. Known mostly as a suffocating, defense-first point guard early in his career in Sacramento, Mitchell has blossomed into a complete floor general with the Heat, combining his passing with a brand-new scoring punch.

Through Week 3, Mitchell is averaging 16.5 points and 8.5 assists per game while shooting 49.6% from the field and a stellar 40.3% from beyond the arc. Last year, he was already dishing out 8.2 assists, but he only chipped in 11.8 points. This season, he has added nearly five points to his average while keeping his assist-to-turnover numbers at an elite rate.

The secret to his scoring jump? He is attacking the rim and drawing contact way more often. He doubled his free-throw attempts from 1.2 per game last year to 2.6 this season. On top of that, he is taking more threes (up to 5.7 attempts) while actually improving his accuracy to over 40%.

When he entered the league in 2021, people thought of him as a backup whose offense was a work in progress. Now, he is running the Heat offense like a veteran, committing just 2.6 turnovers per game while handing out 8.5 dimes. That kind of control combined with efficient 16.5-point scoring makes him a very dangerous top-three candidate, even if Miami keeps struggling to win games with Giannis in the mix.

2025-26 Stats: 13.6 PPG, 3.3 RPG, 3.6 APG, 48.5 FG%, 34.7 3P%

2026-27 Stats: 18.5 PPG, 3.6 RPG, 3.3 APG, 52.3 FG%, 26.5 3P%

If you love a great underdog story, Ajay Mitchell is your guy. Picked 38th overall in the 2024 NBA Draft by the Oklahoma City Thunder, Mitchell barely played as a rookie on a championship team. Fast forward two years, and the 24-year-old guard has carved out a huge role as one of the best bench-to-starter breakouts in the league.

After finishing fifth in Sixth Man of the Year voting last season with 13.6 points per game, Mitchell has taken another massive leap, currently pouring in 18.5 points per night for an OKC squad that's missing Jalen Williams, Chet Holmgren, and Cason Wallace.

What makes this so impressive is that he isn't just chucking shots on a bad team; he is getting buckets within the flow of a hyper-competitive Thunder offense, shooting an incredible 52.3% from the field and literally being SGA's sidekick to remain afloat in the West at 8-4 and the 7 seed.

He managed this 4.9-point scoring bump while playing only 1.5 more minutes per game than last year. That is pure efficiency. He is taking 14.1 shots (up from 10.4) and converting nearly 60% of his two-point attempts, and the Thunder definitely needed him to take that step forward.

Does he have a flaw? Yes, his outside shooting has cratered early on, dropping to 26.5% from deep after hitting nearly 35% last year. If he can straighten out that jumper and get it back above league average, his offensive ceiling is scary.

Even with the shooting slump from deep, an NBA champion who finished high in Sixth Man voting and is now pushing 19 points on 52% shooting represents one of the best developmental arcs in the league.

2025-26 Stats: 19.7 PPG, 2.8 RPG, 5.7 APG, 47.4 FG%, 42.0 3P% (15 games)

2026-27 Stats: 26.2 PPG, 3.5 RPG, 4.9 APG, 50.0 FG%, 36.6 3P%

Right now, there is simply no debate at the top of the ladder. Ty Jerome is playing the best basketball of his life, and his early-season explosion for the Memphis Grizzlies has been historic. After bouncing around the league as a backup and showing flashes in limited action last year (19.7 PPG in just 15 games), Jerome has exploded into a superstar offensive player, averaging an incredible 26.2 points per game.

Jerome's start to the season is video game-ish. On opening week, he dropped 45 points against the Utah Jazz, shooting 17-of-25 with five triples. Instead of cooling off, he followed it up with 28 against Golden State, 31 against Sacramento, and another masterclass 40-point performance against the New Orleans Pelicans last week (14-of-22 shooting). Two 40-point games in his first 10 appearances for a guy who had never averaged 20 points in a full season? Unbelievable.

Drafted 24th overall back in 2019, Jerome endured a long, grueling road filled with injuries and fluctuating bench roles before finally finding his footing in Cleveland and now Memphis. He is playing 30.7 minutes per night and taking 18.4 shots, carrying a massive offensive load for the Grizzlies.

While his three-point percentage dipped slightly from his scorching 42.0% last year down to 36.6%, his overall field-goal percentage actually rose to 50.0%. Plus, he is attacking the free-throw line, averaging 5.8 attempts per game and making 89.7% of them. His Player Efficiency Rating (PER) sits at an elite 24.4.

The main question mark is durability, given that his sample size last year was limited to just 15 games. Can he sustain this pace over an 82-game grind? We'll see. But early odds have him as the clear favorite, and for good reason. Jerome has made the biggest scoring leap of anyone in the league, transforming overnight into one of the most electric bucket-getters in the NBA. As long as he keeps putting up 25+ points a night, the award is his to lose.
