McIlroy’s Eighth Dubai Crown Proves His European Legacy Is Unshakable

Tanggal: 2026-11-15

Rory McIlroy won on Sunday and secured a legacy that now stands alongside the greats of the game. The 37-year-old Northern Irishman claimed his eighth Race to Dubai title, tying the all-time record set by Colin Montgomerie. This victory came after a nail-biting final round at the Jumeirah Golf Estates where the outcome hung in the balance until the very last holes. Per CBS Sports, the win marks his fifth straight season-long crown, a streak that shows his total command over the European circuit.

McIlroy entered the final round trailing Eugenio Chacarra, who held the top spot in the standings. The Spaniard had a slight lead, but McIlroy refused to let go. A birdie on the 16th hole, described as a season-defining moment, shifted the momentum back to the world number two. This shot from 130 yards landed just a foot from the pin, a move that ultimately sealed the deal. According to Golf Monthly, the points difference between the two players was a mere 0.1 at one stage, making every single stroke matter.

Even with the title in his grasp, McIlroy admitted the pressure was real. He noted that he did not fully understand how close the race was until he reached the 16th hole. "I don't really know what to say. … Winning eight Order of Merits was never really in my list of goals when I was growing up," McIlroy said. The humility in his words stands out, especially given the magnitude of the achievement. He remembered watching Montgomerie as a child and now finds himself right next to him in the history books.

While McIlroy took the season title, Tyrrell Hatton dominated the tournament itself. The Englishman finished at 15 under, taking a five-stroke victory over McIlroy. Hatton played with a calmness that contrasted with the tension of the Race to Dubai battle. "This is really special. I was really nervous today," Hatton said after his win. He credited his putter for saving him all week, noting how the back nine felt like a different day than he had feared.

The margin for error was non-existent for Chacarra, who finished in a tie for third. The young Spaniard fought hard but could not make the birdie needed on the final hole to overtake McIlroy. "It was a good fight. I just came out short," Chacarra said. He played well but acknowledged that the day required more than effort. The final numbers show McIlroy finished at 10 under par, while Chacarra closed at eight under. The prize money for the event was substantial, with Hatton taking home $3 million for his tournament victory.

This win solidifies McIlroy's status as the king of the DP World Tour. He has now won the Race to Dubai five times in a row, a feat that seems almost impossible to stop.
