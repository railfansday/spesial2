Brothers Sam and Andy Houghton take on sidecarcross racing

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

For Sam and Andy Houghton, competitive sidecarcross is much more than a motorsport. It is a shared passion, a test of teamwork, and a powerful expression of brotherhood. The two brothers compete together in a demanding form of motocross where one rider controls the motorcycle while the other moves dynamically inside the attached sidecar to maintain balance and navigate challenging corners. Every race brings intense adrenaline, powerful acceleration, and split-second decisions. Unlike traditional motorcycle racing, sidecarcross requires both competitors to work almost as one person. Their movements must be perfectly coordinated because even a small difference in timing can affect the bike's direction, stability, and performance.

Their journey into motorsport began when they were young. Sam started riding motorcycles at around four years old and began competitive racing when he was approximately ten. He quickly developed a passion for the sport, eventually winning his first championship with a local club at around eleven or twelve years of age. Over the following years, he progressed through different competitions and championships, building a promising racing career. However, in 2005, when he was 24, everything changed after a serious training accident. Sam went over the handlebars and landed heavily on his back, shattering his T7 vertebra and leaving him with a spinal cord injury that resulted in permanent mobility challenges.

Following the accident, Sam spent considerable time in hospital and several years away from competitive racing. Although his circumstances had changed dramatically, his passion for motorcycles never disappeared. The desire to return to the track remained strong, and eventually, he and his brother discovered sidecarcross as a way to get back into competitive motorsport. Rather than allowing his injury to end his racing journey, Sam found a different way to pursue the activity he loved.

One of the most interesting aspects of their partnership is the physical and technical responsibility each brother carries. Andy controls the motorcycle, while Sam works from the sidecar, constantly shifting his body weight from one side to another to help turn the machine and keep it balanced. There is no sitting comfortably during a race. The passenger must throw their weight across the sidecar, respond instantly to changing conditions, and anticipate the driver's movements. Both brothers need to understand each other's decisions without hesitation. Their relationship as siblings gives them an additional advantage because years of familiarity and trust help them develop an almost instinctive understanding of one another.

For Sam, the greatest reward comes from the feeling of being on the track, leaning into corners, and experiencing the motorcycle drifting sideways at speed. He particularly values the fact that, once the race begins, other competitors see him simply as another sidecar racer rather than defining him by his disability. His journey demonstrates that a serious injury does not necessarily mean abandoning a lifelong passion. With determination, teamwork, and the right opportunity, it is possible to discover entirely new ways of achieving old dreams. Together, Sam and Andy have turned brotherhood into a competitive advantage and proved that the strongest racing partnerships are built on trust, coordination, and a shared love of the sport.
