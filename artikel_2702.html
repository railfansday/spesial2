NFL Black Friday preview: Steelers cut Aaron Rodgers, turn to Mason Rudolph against hot Broncos

Tanggal: 2026-11-26

Black Friday represents a new dawn for the Pittsburgh Steelers.

The Steelers shockingly benched Aaron Rodgers late Tuesday night, and then released him Wednesday. Pittsburgh faces the 8-2 Denver Broncos at Acrisure Stadium on Friday. It will be Mason Rudolph starting in Rodgers' place for the 6-4 Steelers, who are fighting for their playoff lives after losing two straight since their bye week.

The shock was less because of how Rodgers and the Steelers' offense had played lately — not well — but more so for how the Steelers and head coach Mike McCarthy had backed Rodgers and seemingly endorsed him, as recently as Monday. That all changed when the team benched him, prompting Rodgers to go on ESPN and rip McCarthy while declaring he wouldn't return to the team.

Rodgers reportedly issued McCarthy an ultimatum over total control of play-calling and the head coach declined, and sent the future Hall of Famer on his way in stunning fashion.

Amazingly, it appears as if DK Metcalf will play Friday, less than a week after being ejected from a loss to the Philadelphia Eagles … and it wasn't Metcalf's first such incident. He's amid one of his worst seasons to date and now must match up with Broncos All-Pro CB Pat Surtain II and do so with a backup QB.

Rudolph has seen mop-up action in four games this season. He will make his first start since 2025 against Denver, a team he's never faced. The Broncos have 31 sacks this season, and the Steelers have allowed 31 sacks.

Denver's defense has been closer to good than great this season, but its offense has picked it up after a slow start to the year. In the past four games, the Broncos have scored 26 or more points in each after hitting that mark only once in the first six outings.

The Broncos are riding a six-game win streak and chasing the top playoff seed in the AFC. The Steelers haven't lost at Acrisure this season, but they've failed to hit the 20-point mark in three of their past four games.

The Steelers are in desperation mode. They're currently in the seventh playoff slot and have a 57% chance to make the postseason, according to Next Gen Stats, as things stand right now. That might sound pretty good, and no team currently outside the playoff field is over 50%.

A loss would drop the Steelers' chances to 50%. A win would push them to 72%. Pittsburgh's next four games are against teams still fighting for the postseason.

It's not as if Denver has nothing to play for. It is currently the No. 2 seed in the playoff slotting, just one game behind the Bills, whom the Broncos host in Week 16 on Christmas. The difference between the top seed (which comes with a bye) and the next spot down feels massive.

But the Broncos are near-certainties to make the playoffs, currently sitting at 99% chances to get in. A loss would drop them all the way down to 98%. Their schedule prior to and after the Bills game is soft. It would be tough to lose to a team making a QB change in a short week, but it wouldn't damage Denver's playoff hopes much at all.

Since coming off injured reserve four games ago, Coleman has seen his share of the Broncos' backfield duty increase with each game. On Sunday against the Raiders, the rookie scored three TDs and ran for 111 yards for his best outing of his short NFL career.

The Broncos likely want to see what more Coleman can do, especially with J.K. Dobbins suspended for this game. Perhaps not coincidentally, during Coleman's strong four-game span, Bo Nix and Denver's passing game have produced four straight games with 200-plus passing yards.

The Steelers rank third in rushing yards allowed and fourth in yards per carry allowed. They've had a stout rush defense all season, especially recently allowing only 221 rushing yards in their past four outings. It could be a tougher assignment, however, if Alex Highsmith (ankle) can't go, as expected. Mike McCarthy said it will be "tough for [Highsmith] to make it this week."

Is there a less impressive team with a great record than the Denver Broncos? Tied for the best mark against the spread entering Week 12 (8-2 ATS), Denver has won six straight games — and could've fairly easily lost four of them. However, its schedule sets up remarkably well for a run at the AFC's No. 1 seed if the Broncos can keep winning as a favorite. Denver is a 3.5-point road favorite in this one at Pittsburgh, and it's hard to find a lot of reasons to be excited about the Steelers going with backup QB Mason Rudolph. And while Bo Nix's Total QBR is only 48.2, Denver's defense is a much better unit than Pittsburgh's. The Steelers' playoff chances continue to dim with a loss here. — Ben Fawkes

Scoring expectations are muted (36.5 total) as the Broncos and Steelers meet for a Black Friday afternoon game. Here are three fantasy themes to unpack as you watch the game.

1. Can Mason Rudolph get Pittsburgh's passing game going? Aaron Rodgers has not played well for several weeks, but it was still a shocker when his benching was announced Tuesday night. Rudolph has always been a capable backup through his seven-year career, but his overall stats profile to a less-than-average starter. Still, sometimes there's a bounce when a slumping offense gets a new man in charge.

2. Is Jonah Coleman too good to be true? Coleman was a one-man wrecking crew in Week 11 (three touchdowns, 129 total yards), and with J.K. Dobbins suspended for this week, Coleman's floor is especially high. Coleman is the current favorite for Offensive Rookie of the Year, a notable development given how little he played in the first half of the year.

3. Is Jaylen Warren still a Circle of Trust guy? Warren was seen as one of fantasy's right answers through the first two months, but he's in a 16-43-0 rushing funk over the last two weeks, with zero touchdowns. Rodgers' slump played into this, but Warren needs a decent start because Rico Dowdle is going to see work, too. — Scott Pianowski
