The last 7 permanent Lincoln City managers ranked from worst to best

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

There arguably isn't a better run club in the EFL right now than Lincoln City.

The Imps were a National League side just a decade ago, and currently find themselves in the second tier for the first time since the 1960/61 campaign. That's been achieved thanks to sustainability off the pitch and patience on it.

In a footballing landscape where managers are axed off the back of one bad season, or a poor run of form, it's impressive that a list like this, detailing who has been the best manager (or managers) at Sincil Bank out of the last seven hires, stretches back all the way to 2013.

Since their promotion back to the EFL in 2017, only four managers were needed to get to where they are now. That's a good sign, but which of the last seven main dugout men have been the best?

Football League World has taken a look to find out.

Back in 2013, Lincoln weren't the remarkable football club that many know them to be. Instead, they were treading water in the National League, after being relegated from League Two a couple of years earlier.

The seventh-most recent Lincoln boss takes seventh spot, as Gary Simpson didn't do too much while in charge at Sincil Bank.

The former midfielder returned to Lincolnshire a decade after spending five months as caretaker boss during their time in the old Division Three (now League Two), with the club sitting two places and two points above the drop zone in the fifth tier.

He did enough to stave off relegation, but did need back-to-back wins in their final two games against Tamworth and Hyde United to make fully sure.

Simpson then oversaw a 14th-placed finish in the 2013/14 campaign before being placed on gardening leave and later sacked months into 2014/15.

Chris Moyses was the man to replace Gary Simpson, initially as caretaker when the latter was placed on gardening leave, and then permanently a month later after two wins and a draw in his first three league games.

Things started okay, with six wins in seven games to close out 2014, but the rest of the season was nothing more than mediocre, with the Imps finishing 15th.

He'd be given the 2015/16 campaign, and despite an okay start with Lincoln sitting around the top eight by the new year, it ended with another drab finish and a 13th-placed position, which was actually their best in the five years they'd been at the level.

Fast forward six years, and Lincoln were now pretty well-established in League One thanks to the good work of some people who we'll get to soon enough.

The Imps made the third-tier play-offs in 2021, but the following season finished 12 places and 25 points worse off in 17th. In came former Liverpool, Manchester City and Wolves left-sider Mark Kennedy, who had spent the last year as Lee Bowyer's assistant at Birmingham City.

He was given a four-year contract, which wouldn't have been seen as strange at the time, given his two predecessors lasted three seasons each, but Kennedy wasn't really able to push the club forward.

The Irishman oversaw a better season than the one previous in 2022/23, finishing 11th in League One and leading Lincoln to the fourth round of the EFL Cup, their best run in the competition in 55 years.

But despite only losing one of their first nine games in all competitions the following season, a run of poor results saw him sacked in October, a little over a year into that four-year deal.

It's hard to properly rank the current managerial duo at Lincoln, given they've only held the post for half a year, and they've been the men tasked with keeping the Imps competitive at their highest level for over 60 years.

So far, though, they're not doing a terrible job. The Imps started well, taking Middlesbrough all the way to the end on opening day in defeat, beating the likes of Bolton Wanderers and Bristol City away and destroying Burnley at Sincil Bank.

Their form has slipped, though, and now they're looking over their shoulders at the drop zone, which many critics expected them to do anyway.

The higher-ups at Sincil Bank have the faith that they can turn things around, and for now, they rank slap bang in the middle of this list.

Michael Appleton had big shoes to fill in the Lincoln dugout when he replaced Danny Cowley just weeks into the 2019/20 campaign, after the fan favourite departed for Huddersfield Town.

It was the Imps' first year at third tier-level since a brief campaign in the old Division Two in the 1998/99 season, and, as has become the case now in the Championship, many expected their backs to be against the wall.

Despite not winning in his first five games in all competitions, which included a 6-0 home defeat against Oxford United and a loss against Manchester United's kids in the EFL Trophy, he managed to keep the Imps competitive, and when the league was curtailed due to COVID, Lincoln were 16th on PPG.

Appleton's best season came the following year, though, when an Imps side bolstered by the likes of Morgan Rogers and Brennan Johnson alongside the more experienced heads of Jorge Grant and Anthony Scully led the club to a fifth-placed finish.

They beat Sunderland over two legs in the semi-final and took the lead against Blackpool at Wembley, but ended up losing the final 2-1 thanks to a Kenny Dougall double.

Unfortunately, they didn't recover from that hangover and finished 17th the following season. Appleton departed at the end of that term, after a real rollercoaster three years at the club.

The hiring of Michael Skubala raised some eyebrows at the time. The former England futsal coach and Leeds United Under-21s boss had never managed at senior level in the football pyramid, barring a three-game caretaker spell at Elland Road after the dismissal of Marcelo Bielsa.

However, Skubala's time at Sincil Bank was incredible. Replacing Mark Kennedy in November 2023, Lincoln became an established play-off contender under him, until they exploded into life last season and won the division with 103 points.

Back at the start, though, questions were being asked after a four-game losing run over the Christmas period in 2023. Skubala's side followed that up with a 16-game unbeaten run, which included consecutive wins by a 1-5, 6-0 and 5-0 scoreline to be right amongst the play-offs talk.

In the end, they gave themselves too much to do and finished seventh. A similar Christmas run the following year couldn't be saved, and they finished 11th.

Last season doesn't need to be dressed up, though. Lincoln were brilliant, especially in the second half of the campaign. They went 29 games unbeaten, a club record, and earned 12 more points than anyone else in 2026.

Unfortunately, Skubala opted not to take his Imps side into the second tier, instead leaving for Bristol City, a decision which isn't looking to be the right one.

There arguably isn't a better modern-day manager at Sincil Bank than Danny Cowley, though, who oversaw two promotions, an EFL Trophy win and a miraculous FA Cup run in just three years at the helm.

Let's head all the way back to 2016, when Lincoln had just parted ways with Chris Moyses after just under two years in charge. The Imps finished 13th, which backed up 15th, 14th, 16th and 17th-placed finishes in the National League.

In Cowley's first year in charge, Lincoln won the fifth tier, while also making it to the quarter-final of the FA Cup in one of the most memorable runs in recent history. The Imps beat Championship sides Ipswich Town and Brighton, as well as Premier League outfit Burnley, before losing to Arsenal.

Their first season back in the EFL for seven years saw them immediately finish in the play-offs, while also juggling a run to the final of the EFL Trophy, which they won, beating Shrewsbury Town to become the first fourth-tier side in nine years to lift the trophy.

Then, the following year they won League Two, making it three pieces of silverware in three years under Cowley and a return to the third tier for the first time since before the millennium.

Lincoln's rise up the EFL began with Cowley, and those who have been in the stands at Sincil Bank since their time in the National League still reminisce about how legendary his time at the club was.
