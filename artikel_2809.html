The Seahawks must put Drew Lock on the Russell Wilson plan

Tanggal: 2026-11-19

It's interesting that the Seattle Seahawks first acquired Drew Lock in the Russell Wilson trade on March 8, 2022. That deal sent Wilson from Seattle to the Denver Broncos, along with a 2022 fourth-round draft pick, in exchange for Lock, tight end Noah Fant, defensive lineman Shelby Harris, 2022 and 2023 first-round picks, 2022 and 2023 second-round picks, and a 2022 fifth-round pick, and it set the Seahawks up for future success in ways few could have predicted at the time. Seattle got Devon Witherspoon, Charles Cross, Boye Mafe, and Derick Hall out of those picks, while Lock didn't quite make the cut.

He was Geno Smith's backup in 2022 and 2023, signed with the New York Giants for the 2024 season, and signed a two-year, $5 million deal to come back to the Emerald City in 2025. Not much came of it in 2025, as Sam Darnold had enough on the ball to help the Seahawks to their second Lombardi Trophy, but things have obviously been different in 2026. Darnold was injured early in the season opener against the New England Patriots, and Lock finished that 13-10 win, and a 31-7 Week 2 win over the Arizona Cardinals, looking very much like the NFL's next reclamation quarterback. He completed 35 of 48 passes (72.9%) for 422 yards (8.8 yards per attempt), four touchdowns, no interceptions, a passer rating of 127.3 (the NFL's highest among quarterbacks who took at least 20% of their teams' snaps), and an EPA per pass of +0.43 (the NFL's fourth-best).

Still, it made sense that a (relatively) healthy Darnold returned to the field in Week 3 against the Washington Commanders. Dan Quinn's defense looked like an easy out, and the Lock story was more about the Seahawks having a top-tier backup than any sort of quarterback controversy. In that 33-31 upset loss, Darnold gave us the full Sam Darnold Experience, completing 31 of 45 passes for 379 yards, four touchdowns, two interceptions, a passer rating of 105.7, and an EPA per pass of +0.05.

From Week 4 through Week 8 as the starter, Darnold completed 95 of 164 passes (57.9%) for 1,086 yards (6.6 YPA), six touchdowns, seven interceptions, a passer rating of 72.4, and an EPA per pass of -0.18 — down there in the mid-thirties with Deshaun Watson, Cam Ward, Jalen Hurts, and Jacoby Brissett. Darnold's injuries were clearly affecting him, and this was especially true when he tried to throw outside the pocket, which was a big factor in the Seahawks' Super Bowl run. This season when throwing outside the pocket, Darnold has completed 13 of 35 passes for 162 yards, two touchdowns, one interception, a passer rating of 59.5, and an overall EPA of -12.14 — ninth-worst in the NFL.

Basically, because of his physical limitations, Darnold was put on a quick-game plan by offensive coordinator Brian Fleury — his average time to throw went from 3.14 seconds in Week 3 to 2.32 seconds in Week 7, and 2.67 seconds in Week 8.

I asked Darnold about this right after Seattle's 34-17 Week 8 loss to the Chicago Bears — a game he finished despite a quadriceps injury and a cracked rib.

"Yeah, I think for me, it's all about when I get the ball out quick," he said. "It's all about knowing where to go with the football. And I think, again, just being decisive, knowing when I need to move on in the progression and doing that. Talking about this game specifically, you gotta watch the tape, obviously, but I feel like there were opportunities to get the ball out, and I just didn't."

Fair enough, but when your passing game is that limited, it's a problem. Especially when your run game has been behind the sticks all season long.

So, Lock was back under center for Seattle's 17-3 Week 9 win over the Arizona Cardinals, and last Sunday's 21-13 win over the Las Vegas Raiders, and the work was similarly strong. In those two games, Lock completed 33 of 45 passes (73.3%) for 415 yards (9.2 YPA), three touchdowns, no interceptions, a passer rating of 123.8 (fourth-best in the NFL), and an EPA per pass of +0.22 (fifth-best).

By the way, Lock's EPA per pass of +0.33 is the NFL's highest among quarterbacks with at least 50 dropbacks. And he's the quarterback who allows the Seahawks to run their entire offense at this point in time. Whether Darnold's issues are tied to injuries and/or other things, he's not able to do that right now.

"Oh my gosh, yeah," Mike Macdonald said of Lock's two touchdown passes out of scrambles against the Raiders. "He's got that ability to extend plays and not put the ball in danger, which is cool. We turned it over twice, but those were really early in the play. Great plays, man. It was awesome."

"Yeah, we just talked about creating plays," Jaxon Smith-Njigba said after the Raiders game. "Drew's a creative player, great player, can move around, great athleticism. So, just sticking with him, knowing that his eyes are downfield trying to make a play. Just being there for him."

What Smith-Njigba said of Lock in the wake of Darnold's possible return revealed even more.

"Man — unbelievable player, great player. Not only how he played incredibly, but just what he does off the field and in the huddle and how he commands his troops. True professional, and [I] love sharing the field with him."

And how about an endorsement from Devon Witherspoon?

"Ah man, it's dope. See Drew come out there and do the things he does. We watch him in practice all the time. I call him the Locksmith, because it seems like he's just got all the damn coverages. To see Drew play the way he's played, given the situation he's in, you can't ask for anything better than that. He comes out here and he works every day. We see the looks that we give every day in practice, man.

"We've got all the confidence in the world, because we know he put in the work to do it."

By the way, Lock has done all this with injuries to his throwing elbow and his oblique muscles.

Okay. What do I mean when I say in the headline that the Seahawks need to put Lock on the Russell Wilson plan? Let's take a trip in the Wayback Machine.

I started covering the Seahawks with credentials in 2010, which means that I got to see the genesis of the Pete Carroll plan, and the creation of the Legion of Boom. I also got to see what happened in 2012 after the team signed Matt Flynn to a three-year, $19.5 million contract with $10 million guaranteed, and selected Russell Wilson with the 75th overall pick in the third round of that draft.

Wilson made his supremacy at the position evident from the start. At his rookie minicamp, he was making bang-on throws 40 yards downfield. As training camp turned into the preseason, I started hearing from veterans on the team that Wilson was the quarterback they trusted. He was the one they wanted to load the wagon behind. And that eventually happened, because Carroll understood the value of momentum, and that the best player must win the job, regardless of position.

Macdonald has said all the right things publicly about Darnold as his starter. But he's also smart enough to know that with his team outside the playoff picture right now at 6-4, and a bye week to get everyone on the same page, this would be the ideal time to make the right call.

And regardless of the reasons why, the right call is to give Drew Lock all the starter's reps. The difference in performance has been all the difference.
