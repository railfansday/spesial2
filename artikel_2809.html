Former ESPN analyst and NFL linebacker Tom Jackson dies at 75 years old

Tanggal: 2026-11-22

Longtime ESPN analyst and former NFL linebacker Tom Jackson has died. He was 75.

Jackson was a fixture on ESPN's NFL coverage after his playing career ended in 1986. He joined the network in 1987 and worked alongside Chris Berman for nearly 30 years until his retirement in 2016.

Berman eulogized his friend and colleague during ESPN's "Sunday NFL Countdown," expressing his shock after seeing Jackson weeks ago at the network's studios in Bristol, Connecticut, and talking to him last week.

"All of us are stunned. We've lost one of the best human beings ever to walk the planet," Berman said. "I was fortunate, as you know, to work side-by-side with him for 30 years on 'NFL Primetime' and 'NFL Gameday,' now 'Sunday NFL Countdown.'"

"Tom was so much more than an accomplished NFL analyst," he added. "There was nobody kinder, there was nobody more thoughtful, more loyal, more engaging than Tom Jackson."

Jackson died on Friday, according to Berman. His family informed ESPN of his death soon thereafter.

Jackson is known to generations of NFL fans for his work providing commentary on highlights for ESPN with Berman, long before pro football studio shows like "NFL Live" and the NFL Network became ubiquitous.

Watching clips of the day's games was never more electric than when the two detailed the action on screen with ESPN's iconic background music playing behind them during "NFL Primetime."

One of Jackson's signature bits was showing the week's hardest hits on Monday night, saying players got "Jacked Up!" Though that segment was eventually phased out as concern and consideration for head trauma and serious injury grew, Jackson conveyed the excitement and thrill that so many fans felt while watching the NFL.

Before going into broadcasting, Jackson was a linebacker for the Denver Broncos for 14 seasons. He played on two Super Bowl teams in 1977 and 1986, and ranks fourth in team history with 191 games played.

Denver's 1975 fourth-round pick out of Louisville, Jackson was named to three Pro Bowls and twice earned All-Pro honors. The Broncos inducted Jackson into their Ring of Fame in 1992.

At Louisville, Jackson played for fellow former ESPN analyst Lee Corso from 1970-72. The Cardinals also paid tribute to Jackson, who led the team in tackles in each of his three seasons. He was named the Missouri Valley Conference player of the year twice during his college career. Louisville honored his jersey No. 50 in 1999.

NFL commissioner Roger Goodell said Jackson "brought credibility, passion and humor to everything he did."

"The NFL lost a wonderful man with the passing of Tom Jackson," Goodell said in a statement. "Along with millions of fans, I will always remember him as a player and broadcaster, but also as a friend. He was an outstanding player whose toughness and leadership helped define an era of Broncos football. He brought credibility, passion and humor to everything he did, and generations of fans came to know him through his work at ESPN. Tom helped fans enjoy and better understand football, always bringing the perspective of someone who loved the game and the people who play it. More importantly, he earned the respect and friendship of players, coaches and everyone who had the privilege of knowing and working with him."
