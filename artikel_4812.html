Here’s what happened in crypto today

Tanggal: 2026-11-27

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, bug bounty platforms are increasingly turning to artificial intelligence to manage a surge in AI-assisted security reports, Spain moved to grant its financial intelligence unit the power to freeze crypto accounts without prior judicial authorization and Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin called for more verifiable computer chips as hardware wallet tampering threats become increasingly sophisticated.

Crypto bug bounty platforms have expanded their use of artificial intelligence to keep up with a growing mountain of AI-assisted security reports.

Crypto-focused HackenProof said in October that it was using AI to triage 70% of submissions fully autonomously, aiming to hit 85% by the end of 2026. It said it received an average of about 6,100 bug reports a month in the third quarter, 2.4 times the reports from December last year.

Rival Immunefi last week said submissions to its platform rose 136% year-over-year in the first nine months of 2026, as it introduced a tool that can flag duplicates before a researcher submits a report.

Bug bounty programs pay independent researchers to find and report potential security flaws. AI has helped researchers uncover more genuine vulnerabilities, but has also swelled the volume of invalid and duplicate submissions, making it harder for security teams to identify the threats that need urgent attention.

It’s like “bringing a sword to a gunfight,” Dmitry Tokarev, founder of crypto wallet provider Bron, told Cointelegraph, referring to security teams that haven’t already adopted AI to sift through reports.

A decree published on Nov. 25 would give Spain’s financial intelligence unit, Sepblac, the power to freeze crypto accounts and halt transactions without prior judicial authorization when it suspects money laundering or terrorist financing.

The measures are scheduled to take effect on Jan. 25, 2027. Under Spain’s Constitution, the Congress of Deputies must vote to ratify or repeal the decree within 30 days of its promulgation. If ratified, crypto companies operating in Spain would have to comply with suspension orders or face financial penalties.

According to the decree, Sepblac would be able to suspend the use of a digital asset account or a business relationship for up to five business days, while transactions in progress could be held for up to 10 business days. Although the initial suspension of an account or transaction would be at the discretion of Sepblac, it could be extended by a court at the request of public prosecutors.

Nikolai Timofejev, head of licensing and regulatory affairs at legal consulting firm Prifinance, told Cointelegraph that crypto firms should start preparing for the change before it takes effect. “My advice is to treat it like an operational workstream with a named person who receives Sepblac’s orders,” he said. “Non-compliance amounts to a very serious infringement attracting fines starting at €150,000.”

Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin on Thursday called for enhanced verifiability in computer chips, citing evolving security threats in the hardware wallet sector.

Buterin said in a blog post that most commercial open-source chips do not disclose enough detail to verify their physical construction. Publishing what a chip is programmed to do is different from providing a blueprint against which inspectors can check the hardware itself.

“This concern is not just theoretical,” Buterin said, adding that the crypto industry has seen attacks that involve inserting spy implants into hardware wallets before they get sold to consumers.

Buterin warned that future attacks could be harder to detect, with malicious functions built into replacement chips that resemble legitimate ones.

“This, you cannot detect with the naked eye,” he wrote.
