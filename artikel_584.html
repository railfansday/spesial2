Tom Cullen and Joe Locke’s Mystery Thriller ‘Black Church Bay’ Sells to Strand Releasing for North America (EXCLUSIVE)

Tanggal: 2026-11-23

Rhys Marc Jones’s “Black Church Bay,” a mystery thriller starring “Heartstopper” breakout Joe Locke and Tom Cullen (“Weekend”), has been bought by Strand Releasing for North America.

Jones’ feature debut, “Black Church Bay” follows a man whose world unravels after a secret relationship ends in tragedy. Set against the backdrop of Anglesey in North Wales, the thriller explores gay repression, identity and the lengths people will go to conceal their true selves. The deal was negotiated by Strand Releasing’s Jon Gerrans and The Bureau Sales’ Clémentine Hugot. Strand is planning a summer 2027 theatrical release.

“We’re thrilled to be working with The Bureau Sales on this innovative thriller that confronts complicated issues of gay repression and Cullen and Locke’s performances are so beautifully directed by Jones and create a real suspenseful thriller that is satisfying,” said Marcus Hu, co-president of Strand Releasing.

“Black Church Bay” world premiered at Zurich and went to play at Busan and the BFI London Film Festival.

“The film is the culmination of my thematic obsessions – a mystery thriller exploring notions of acceptance, authenticity and repression; how it is often our contradictions that define who we are and what we become,” said Jones.

“Black Church Bay” was shot last year on location in Anglesey and produced by Alex Polunin of Scotland-based Ossian International and Tristan Goligher of London-based The Bureau.

Polunin, who previously produced Jones’ short films “Pale Saint” and “Father of the Bride,” developed the film from its inception.

“One of the things that’s most distinctive and unique about this idea is the way in which it combines Rhys’s thematic and formal interests as a filmmaker – specifically in exploring the queer experience in such a nuanced way, but also through the genre engine of a thriller,” Polunin said.

Goligher, meanwhile, pointed to Jones’ ability to build and sustain suspense while exploring morally complex characters.

“It’s really rare to see short films where a filmmaker can create – and sustain – tension in the way that he did. This script does the same thing,“ Goligher said. “We’ve had a lot of very positive messaging around the gay experience in recent years but it’s also not everyone’s experience. Rhys wrote a very specific story about a very specific person and their experience, and I have no doubt that chimes with many people in the UK and the world.”

“But watching someone deal with a moral dilemma – trying to wrestle with the question of should they, and can they, do the right thing at their own cost – that’s also a very universal dilemma,“ added Goligher.

The film was developed with BBC Film, iFeatures and the Cannes Film Festival’s Cinéfondation Résidence. Financing came from BBC Film, Ffilm Cymru Wales, Screen Scotland and private backers including Los Angeles-based Manzini Films, U.K.-based Elephant Film and 1C6 Films, Kamila Serkebaeva and Brazil-based Daniel Staut.

Strand Releasing’s current slate includes Gregg Araki’s 4K restoration of “Mysterious Skin” and David Pablos’ “On the Road,” which represents Mexico in the Oscar race.
