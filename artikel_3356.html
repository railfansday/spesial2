On November 13, Johnny Depp made his comeback to mainstream leading roles with Ebenezer, a new take on Charles Dickens' frequently adapted A Christmas Carol. Depp hasn't headlined a major project since Pirates of the Caribbean: Dead Men Tell No Tales, which was released in 2017. Over the past decade, Depp became unhirable in mainstream Hollywood because of personal and legal troubles, but after he was acquitted in a libel case in the United Kingdom, studios started showing interest in him again. Next year, he'll star in Day Drinker, an action-thriller directed by Marc Webb.

At the lowest point in his career, Depp was fired from a major franchise project after having shot for a week. He was quickly replaced, and the movie in question ultimately ended up underperforming at the box office and bringing its franchise to a screeching halt. The film is currently available to stream in the United States on Peacock, but only for a little while longer. While Depp doesn't appear in it, the film remains etched in Hollywood infamy, along with Ridley Scott's All the Money in the World, as one of several projects that replaced a main cast member for similar reasons around the same time. Also in this group is Taika Waititi's Next Goal Wins, which saw Will Arnett replacing Armie Hammer.

Produced on a reported budget of $200 million, the film grossed around $407 million at the worldwide box office, making it the lowest-grossing installment of its wildly successful franchise. The studio originally planned to produce two more sequels, which were eventually canceled in the wake of the film's underperformance. The film was directed by David Yates and featured the likes of Eddie Redmayne and Jude Law in key roles. Mads Mikkelsen, on the other hand, stepped in to replace Depp.

By now you've probably guessed that we're talking about Fantastic Beasts: The Secrets of Dumbledore. The fantasy epic serves as the third installment of the Harry Potter spin-off series, beginning with Fantastic Beasts and Where to Find Them and continuing with Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald. Both films were helmed by Yates, who also directed the final four installments of the Harry Potter series.

Depp was cast in the role of the dreaded villain Grindelwald; he first appeared as the character at the end of the first Fantastic Beasts, in which Grindelwald was largely played by Colin Farrell. The Secrets of Dumbledore received mediocre reviews and is now sitting at a 46% score on Rotten Tomatoes, where the consensus reads, "Fantastic Beasts: The Secrets of Dumbledore avoids some of the pitfalls that plagued its predecessor, but lacks much of the magic that drew audiences into the Wizarding World many movies ago." You can watch the film, along with every installment of the Harry Potter saga, on Peacock until December 1. HBO's reboot series will premiere a few weeks later.
