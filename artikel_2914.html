The Brandon Aiyuk return is complicated by the money he owes the 49ers

Tanggal: 2026-11-06

On the surface, receiver Brandon Aiyuk’s effort to become a free agent should be easy. At a deeper level, the question is whether the 49ers will try to complicate the situation, in an effort to delay or to prevent Aiyuk from picking his next team with no trade compensation to the 49ers.

One potential motivation to frustrate his efforts is pride. If Aiyuk goes to another team and re-establishes himself as a high-level player, it will make the 49ers look even more foolish for giving him a massive contract at a time when they had a trade to the Steelers on the tee.

Another potential motivation is good, old-fashioned spite. Aiyuk constantly needled the team on social media this summer, culminating in a post that made fun of coach Kyle Shanahan’s pre-training camp car crash. The 49ers could be tempted to try to keep him from signing with a new team simply because they can — and because they feel no obligation or inclination to do him any favors.

The third, and perhaps best, motivation is money. Aiyuk arguably defaulted on his contract, making the team eligible to recover a sizable chunk of the money it paid him. A Thursday night tweet from Tom Pelissero of Netflix points to the effort to recoup bonus money as the potential basis for an effort to place a major roadblock in Aiyuk’s path to a new team.

Said Pelissero: “Veteran WR Brandon Aiyuk owes the 49ers roughly $13 million in bonus forfeiture — a potential complication in his efforts to secure his release by reporting to the facility Friday. The NFL Management Council and the NFL Players Association are both involved in attempting to resolve the situation. The sides could reach an agreement to pay back the money, or it could go to arbitration. But it’s complicated and may take time to resolve.”

The 49ers, in collaboration with the league, seem to be devising a way to avoid the otherwise simple application of the roster rules. One, Aiyuk files for reinstatement from the reserve/left squad list. Two, the league reinstates him. Three, the 49ers have to decide whether to put him on the 53-man roster (at a prorated base salary of $26.015 million) or release him.

Reinstatement from the reserve/left squad is supposed to be administrative. The NFL’s daily transaction report has yet to mention Aiyuk’s reinstatement.

It could be that the league (in a Brendan Sorsby-style move) is tapping the brakes on reinstatement as an effort to leverage a repayment agreement from Aiyuk. Should it be that way? No. Will that stop the NFL from doing whatever it chooses to do? Also no.

The mere fact that the Pelissero tweet links the two issues proves that the 49ers are at least considering, if not already implementing, a strategy that consists of not letting Aiyuk go until Aiyuk agrees to repay all or part of the $13 million that they believe he owes. And, if he doesn’t, he may not get the contractual freedom he seeks.

He’d have possible recourse under the Collective Bargaining Agreement. But that process would take time to resolve. And, through it all, the remaining weeks of the 2026 regular season will pass without Aiyuk able to join a new team.

Bottom line? The sooner he agrees to cough up enough cash to satisfy the 49ers, the sooner he’ll have a chance to play for a new team.
