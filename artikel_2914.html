Adobe Stock Is About 67% Below Its 2021 Record. Here's What I Think It Will Be Worth in 2030.

Tanggal: 2026-11-25

Even if the software giant's profit growth keeps cooling, a falling share count could do a lot of the work.

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 25, 2026 at 3:13AM EST

When Adobe (ADBE +3.62%) stock closed at its record of $688.37 on Nov. 19, 2021, the company was two weeks from ending a fiscal year that saw non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings of $12.48 per share. For this fiscal year, management's target is $24.45 to $24.50 per share.

That would almost double Adobe's per-share earnings from fiscal 2021. The stock, though, traded around $233 at midday on Nov. 24, about two-thirds below that record and off about a third in 2026.

At the record, investors paid around 55 times that fiscal year's adjusted earnings. Today they pay almost 10 times Adobe's adjusted earnings for its past four quarters.

My estimate is that Adobe stock will be worth about $400 at the end of 2030. From around $233, that would be a rise of about 72%, or roughly 14% a year over a bit more than four years. And it assumes slower profit growth than Adobe has been posting, with just a slight rebound in its valuation.

Adobe's profit growth has cooled. Its adjusted net income climbed 12% in fiscal 2024 and 8% in fiscal 2025. And this fiscal year, the quarterly growth rate has fallen from 12% in the fiscal first quarter to 8% in the fiscal third quarter of 2026 (the period ended Aug. 28, 2026). Earnings per share grew faster, though, up 15% in fiscal 2024 and 14% in fiscal 2025, and management is targeting around 17% growth for fiscal 2026. In other words, per-share earnings are growing faster than the profit behind them, and the gap has widened.

The difference is the share count. Adobe's diluted share count shrank from 450 million in fiscal 2024 to 427 million in fiscal 2025, and management expects around 400 million this year, down about 6%.

With the stock this low, every dollar Adobe spends on buybacks retires more shares. And as of Aug. 28, Adobe still had $24.55 billion left under a repurchase authorization that lasts through April 2030.

Adobe's fiscal year ends on the Friday nearest to Nov. 30, so fiscal 2030 should finish in late November 2030. Adobe released its fiscal 2025 results on Dec. 10, 2025, so fiscal 2030's results should be out before 2030 ends.

I start with the midpoint of this year's target, around $24.48 per share. From there, I assume adjusted net income rises 7% a year, a little slower than its pace in the fiscal third quarter. I also assume the share count drops 3% a year, less than half this year's rate, as buybacks retire fewer shares when the stock price is higher.

Together, that adds up to per-share earnings growth of around 10% a year, well under the 14% to 17% Adobe has reported or targeted for each of the past three fiscal years. It'd put fiscal 2030 adjusted earnings at about $36 per share. The first test of these assumptions should be on Wednesday, Dec. 9, when Adobe hosts its fiscal fourth-quarter earnings call and will probably give its first targets for fiscal 2027.

What investors will pay for those earnings is tougher to nail down.

At 8 times earnings, slightly under today's level, $36 per share would make the stock worth around $290. At 14 times earnings, still below the 17 times earnings or so it fetched at the end of 2025, it'd be worth just over $500. And at 11 times earnings, it would be worth around $400.

I think 11 times earnings is the most sensible of the three. It's just a bit over today's price-to-earnings ratio of almost 10. But if Adobe's per-share earnings keep rising around 10% a year until then, I'd expect investors to pay at least a bit more for them than they do now.

Of course, artificial intelligence tools from newer rivals might slow Adobe's growth more than I'm assuming. If adjusted net income stopped growing completely, buybacks alone would raise per-share earnings to only around $28 by fiscal 2030. At 8 times earnings, the stock would be near $220, close to where it trades now.

But Adobe's adjusted net income is still growing. At almost 10 times earnings, I'd consider buying the stock near $233. My $400 estimate doesn't need anything like the 2021 valuation to come back. It just needs those profits to keep climbing, even if more slowly than they have been.
