NFL Power Rankings entering Week 12: Is it time to start taking the Giants seriously?

Tanggal: 2026-11-24

NFC East: Cowboys | Giants | Eagles | Commanders NFC North: Bears | Lions | Packers | Vikings NFC South: Falcons | Panthers | Saints | Buccaneers NFC West: Cardinals | Rams | 49ers | Seahawks

AFC East: Bills | Dolphins | Patriots | Jets AFC North: Ravens | Bengals | Browns | Steelers AFC South: Texans | Colts | Jaguars | Titans AFC West: Broncos | Chiefs | Raiders | Chargers

Before Week 11, the New York Giants had a perception problem.

They hadn't beaten a team with a winning record. Their advanced metrics said they were a below-average team. Their point differential was +7, which was meager for a 5-3-1 team. They looked like a team lucky to be playing in the putrid NFC East.

Then on Sunday, the Giants got a win that can help legitimize their start. They beat a Jaguars team that was a major buyer at the trade deadline and looked like one of the NFL's elite teams. It was easily the Giants' biggest win this season, and for a team that has missed the playoffs eight of the past nine seasons, one of their biggest victories in many years. It might change what is being said about the Giants.

"We don't really worry about that, we don't really care what they think or what they say," Giants head coach John Harbaugh said after the game, via the team's transcripts. "It's not our concern how we're evaluated. It's our concern how we play."

Yet, they knew what was being said about them.

"They keep saying we can't beat these teams, we can't beat them. I don't know why they're saying that," edge rusher Abdul Carter said, according to Yahoo Sports' Jori Epstein. "But what are they going to say now?"

The Giants' upset win over the Jaguars changes things. It boosts their chances of making the playoffs as a division champion. The way the Giants' defense played, holding the Jaguars without a touchdown for the first three quarters, gives hope of a strong finish to the year after a first half of the season that lacked a quality win. The offense is still not great, ranking 22nd in DVOA. But J.J. McCarthy makes a few plays each game that also provide some optimism, Malik Nabers is one of the NFL's best receivers, Isaiah Likely is having a breakout season and Cam Skattebo generally provides at least a reasonable run game.

The Giants likely aren't going to challenge the 49ers or whoever wins the NFC North for a Super Bowl spot. But their start to the season has more weight behind it after beating a good team.

Here are the NFL power rankings after Week 11:

The 49ers have faced the No. 1 defense in DVOA (Seattle) and the No. 1 defense in EPA allowed per play (Minnesota). They scored 32 points against the Seahawks and 35 against the Vikings. They got a defensive touchdown in each game but it seems like no defense can hold down the 49ers this season.

While the offense has been fantastic, the 49ers wouldn't be the best team in football through Week 11 without their defense, which has excelled despite Nick Bosa not having a sack all season (he did have two quarterback hits on Sunday night). The 49ers have given up 17 or fewer points in six of their 10 games. They have given up fewer than 300 yards in six games too. Defensive coordinator Raheem Morris, who replaced Robert Saleh, is doing an excellent job.

The Bills have given up 37 points in four games since their bye. Of course, they have faced an incredibly fortunate run of backup quarterbacks, including Joe Fagnano, Carson Wentz, Cade Klubnik (who replaced an injured Geno Smith in the second half) and Kyle McCord. Still, they have not given up a touchdown in 43 straight drives, the longest streak during a season since the 2019 Patriots, according to the Bills' site. That makes the Thanksgiving primetime matchup against Patrick Mahomes and the Chiefs one that will help shape this NFL season. Is the Bills' defensive improvement real or because of facing inept quarterbacks?

The Bills' defense struggled early, allowing at least 29 points in five of Buffalo's first six games. The only team to not reach 29 points was the Chargers, who are currently 1-9. But the Bills felt like they weren't far off.

"When teams were scoring on us, I felt like we were playing good ball," Bills defensive end Greg Rousseau said, via the team's site. "We just had to clean up a couple things here and there. We were playing good ball. We weren't really finishing games at the end of the games."

The Bears haven't trailed in five straight games, and that streak brings back memories of the greatest team in franchise history. The last time the Bears went five games without trailing was 1985, when they went the final three games of the regular season and the first two playoff games without trailing, according to Larry Mayer of ChicagoBears.com. The Bears briefly trailed the Patriots 3-0 in Super Bowl XX before winning 46-10.

While this version of the Bears is much different than the famous 1985 team, their pass rush is starting to emerge since Jeffery Simmons came aboard in a trade. On Sunday, they sacked the Saints' Tyler Shough six times (three times by Simmons) and had 24 pressures, which is the most for the team since 2018, according to Next Gen Stats. The defensive improvement will be tested on Thanksgiving in a massive NFC North game against the Lions.

Very good teams are allowed to lose games, even ones in which they're favored. But the teams now ahead of them in the rankings haven't taken many losses, and now the Jaguars have three after a miserable performance in a defeat to the Giants on Sunday. Their chances to get the No. 1 seed in the AFC dropped to 1.6% after Sunday, according to DVOA.

The Jaguars can rightfully complain that a questionable call cost Parker Washington a touchdown. But the offensive issues for the first three quarters are what ultimately cost them the game. The Jaguars, whose offense had been very good before Sunday, had just one drive longer than 23 yards in the first three quarters and didn't score a touchdown. Jaguars head coach Liam Coen took responsibility, saying his plan "maybe had too many things in, moving parts."

"That's a direct reflection of me, and making sure that these guys are prepared well enough," Coen said, via the team's site. "We had been prepared in the majority of our football games, I felt like, and that did not show tonight, especially early on. I put that directly on me."

The Chiefs scored on all five of their first-half drives against the Cardinals, quickly putting away a poor and injured team. One of the stars of the game was tight end Travis Kelce, who is having a remarkable season at age 37. He has 586 yards and four touchdowns, two of which came Sunday against Arizona. His 58.6 yards per game is his most since 2023. There were only three instances of a tight end having more than 400 yards at age 37, and Kelce is already second on that list behind Tony Gonzalez and his 859 yards in 2013. Kelce is on pace for 996 yards.

There were conversations that Kelce could retire after each of the past two seasons, but he's still one of the most productive tight ends in the game. That helps a Chiefs offense that is fourth in EPA per play.

It was a typical Broncos win and not the blowout the 38-24 score indicates. They were outgained 370-351 by the Raiders. Las Vegas rallied to tie the game at 17 after falling behind 17-0 and had the ball at the two-minute warning, down seven points. A pick-6 by Ja'Quan McMillian put the game away in the Broncos' favor.

The best part of the win for the Broncos was perhaps finding an offensive identity. Rookie Jonah Coleman has taken over the backfield, and he had 111 yards and three touchdowns on 24 rushing attempts Sunday.

"It's not easy, but he's responded really well to his situation," quarterback Bo Nix said, according to the team's transcripts. "He's been given a great opportunity with RJ Harvey getting hurt (knee) and having a few weeks off. ... It was an impressive performance getting that many carries, running the ball hard."

The Bengals have leaned into being a one-dimensional offense, and it hurt them Monday in a road loss to the Commanders. Washington got consistent pressure on Joe Burrow, including on some well-timed blitzes, and that was partly because it didn't have to worry about Cincinnati's running game. The Bengals rushed 14 times for 31 yards and threw it 40 times. When the Bengals got into field-goal range in the final two minutes, they couldn't run for a first down to grind out the remainder of the clock. They passed on second and third downs and couldn't pick it up. That allowed the Commanders 46 seconds, which they used to drive for a game-winning field goal.

The Bengals should lean on Burrow, who is still tracking to win an MVP after 300 yards and two touchdowns on Monday night. But the lack of a running game, and even the lack of any attempt to try it, could be a big problem for the Bengals in the playoffs.

Detroit's first defensive test without Aidan Hutchinson went well. The Lions allowed just 274 yards and 21 points, and Tampa Bay got 65 yards and a touchdown on a meaningless drive at the end of a game when Detroit led by 17 points. The Lions had four sacks, which was a good sign. The Lions' defense has been much better since the first few weeks of the season, and Sunday's game was without Hutchinson and linebacker Jack Campbell.

The test Thursday will be tougher, with the Chicago Bears coming in with first place in the NFC North on the line. But if Detroit's defense can maintain this level, the Lions might be a Super Bowl contender. That could reveal itself on Thanksgiving.

"Our guys are competitive, man, and they don't want to be counted out," Lions head coach Dan Campbell said of the defense's performance Sunday, via the team's site. "And nobody cares. There are no excuses. And the only way to gain respect is just go about your business, man, and make plays and play together and prove your worth, man."

What we learned about the Vikings on Sunday night, mostly, was that the 49ers are on a much different tier. That's OK, because the 49ers are on a higher tier than all but a few teams.

The blitz was impervious to the 49ers' offense. Minnesota blitzed on 88.9% of San Francisco's dropbacks, and Brock Purdy was 19-of-23 passing for 231 yards and two touchdowns on those plays, according to Pro Football Focus.

"It's one of those things where I told our team, you know, when you play a good team like that and you don't play your best, you're going to get beat," Vikings head coach Kevin O'Connell said, according to the team's site.

That hadn't happened before to the Vikings' defense and is unlikely to repeat, but now Minnesota has to adjust after its worst defensive day of the season.

The NFC West race is probably over, with the 49ers leading it by three games, but Seattle has a shot to make it interesting. The Seahawks play at the 49ers in Week 12 and there's an opportunity to at least make the race interesting.

The big story will be Sam Darnold, whether he can return from injury and how well he plays. The Seahawks are not entertaining the idea of replacing him with Drew Lock. Seahawks head coach Mike Macdonald defended Darnold, pointing out that he has been injured for most of the season, starting with the first game.

"The beginning of the year for Sam has been a tough road," Macdonald said on Seattle Sports 710, via the team's site. "I can't wait for him to be fully healthy, probably for the first time — minimal times it's happened this year — and get a full week's worth of work and we'll rock and roll from there."

The dream of getting Puka Nacua back for a playoff run is dead, after Nacua had knee surgery that will keep him out the rest of the season. Davante Adams should return but the Rams' offense won't be what was expected before the season, when expectations were sky high.

Even making the playoffs will be difficult because of the Rams' challenging upcoming schedule. Their playoff odds are down to 38.1%, according to DVOA. The Rams practically need a win Wednesday over the Packers, who are one of their main competitors in a very tough NFC wild-card race.

Micah Parsons is set to return for the Packers after practicing in pads Sunday. It's major news for Green Bay to add one of the NFL's best players to the lineup. The Packers were 9-3-1 and leading in the second half at Denver when Parsons tore his ACL last season.

Wednesday at the Rams is a massive game for the Packers and their playoff hopes, and there will be the temptation to play Parsons more than he's ready for in his return. Packers head coach Matt LaFleur is aware of that issue.

"I never wanna place limitations on anybody, but he hasn't played football in a long time," LaFleur said, via Athlon Sports. "So, I think you've got to be conscious of that, and there's probably going to be plays that don't look like him, and there's going to be some plays that do."

Perhaps the biggest development for the Giants on Sunday was defensive end Abdul Carter picking up two sacks. Production had been surprisingly thin for the third overall pick of the 2025 NFL Draft. He had just six sacks in 26 games before Sunday, though his underlying metrics said that sacks were bound to come. Carter also had five quarterback hits.

Carter came in as an elite prospect and if he can start translating his pass rush success into sacks, that could be huge for the Giants. That happened Sunday as the defense played very well against a good Jaguars offense.

"As a defense, we showed what we really about," Carter told CBS after the game. "And for me, I'm the best in the world."

Seeing the Raiders' upset bid fall short was good news for the Browns. Their game against Las Vegas in Week 12, which got flexed to Sunday night, would have been much more interesting in the wild-card race had the Raiders been 5-6 instead of 4-7. It also wasn't bad for Cleveland to see the Steelers slip back to 6-4 with a bad loss at Philadelphia.

The Browns aren't a lock to make the playoffs but the path is very clear. They have two games left against the Bengals and one against the Giants, but the other four are against teams with losing records.

The biggest game in the NFC South this season comes in Tampa Bay next Monday night. The Panthers face the Buccaneers. Tampa Bay gets the season sweep and the tiebreaker with the win. The Falcons and Saints aren't completely out of the running, but Carolina and Tampa Bay look like the two best teams in a bad division.

An old problem popped up again, and it was the run defense. Baltimore had 157 rushing yards, averaging 7.1 yards per attempt. That's a bad weakness to have against the Buccaneers, who lead the NFL at 5 yards per carry.

"They had way too many yards today. Takeaways and stuff is cool, but we got to stop them in the run game," linebacker Tyrel Dodson told the team's site.

The Steelers were showing improvement but the last two weeks are alarming. They were destroyed by the Bengals, then didn't threaten an Eagles team that had lost seven in a row and was on its third quarterback this season. The lingering problem from the game is receiver DK Metcalf getting ejected after another unproductive game. The NFL reportedly won't suspend him, but that doesn't mean Pittsburgh can rely on a player who hasn't done much on the field and whose behavior is unreliable too.

There are also injury concerns at a key spot. Edge rusher Nick Herbig was inactive Sunday because of rib and hip injuries, then Alex Highsmith left the game against the Eagles early because of an ankle injury. The Steelers get the 8-2 Broncos on Friday and the Texans and Jaguars after that. Pittsburgh was once 6-2. It's possible it will be under .500 by mid-December.

David Montgomery could finally make a big impact for the Texans. In his return from injury, he rushed for 107 yards on 26 carries against the Colts. Indianapolis isn't a good team but it was still nice to see something positive from Houston's running game, even if it was in a blowout.

The Texans have been looking for consistency all season, but it's not too late for them to rally for a wild-card spot in a weak race. Their Week 13 game at the Steelers will be a big one for the Texans' playoff hopes. For now, Houston hopes a dominant win over Indianapolis is the start of a streak.

"It's huge," quarterback C.J. Stroud said, according to the team's site. "I think this is a start to something that we need to go on a run with."

Jayden Daniels is still capable of looking like a superstar at quarterback. When the Commanders looked like they had lost to the Bengals on a field goal with 46 seconds left, Daniels hit two passes to rookie Antonio Williams for a total of 64 yards, which set up a game-winning field goal as time expired. They were two great throws from Daniels and a great job by Williams to get open. Daniels finished with 289 yards, 122 of which went to Williams.

The Commanders remain two games behind the Giants in the NFC East. But Daniels and Williams kept the Commanders' season alive with two great plays in the final minute on Monday night.

Baker Mayfield's injuries have played a big part in his quiet season, but the Buccaneers still need more from the passing game. Mayfield's top game this season is 243 yards. He had 187 yards against a vulnerable Lions defense on Sunday. Mayfield has just five passing touchdowns in 175 attempts. His yards per attempt of 6.3 is the lowest of his career.

Mayfield was playing through a foot injury Sunday. The Buccaneers need more out of the passing game because they can't keep dropping games and stay in the NFC South race. A loss Monday night to the Panthers might ruin their playoff hopes.

"We're out of mulligans," Mayfield said, according to the team's site.

It's weird to say after giving up 30 points at home to the Titans, but the Cowboys' defense looked better after Mike Pettine took over play-calling duties from defensive coordinator Christian Parker. Almost all of the Titans' yards came when they were down double digits and trying to get back in the game. The Titans didn't score a touchdown in the first half.

"Very proud of those guys," Cowboys head coach Brian Schottenheimer said of the defense, according to the team's site. "I think the first half was outstanding, but I thought they did a great job of blocking out the noise and working. I was really excited for how they performed."

Fernando Mendoza played well, although his pick-6 in the fourth quarter with the game on the line was a rookie moment. Against a very good Broncos defense, Mendoza had 267 passing yards and three touchdowns. He also rushed for 22 yards. He played well despite being without superstar tight end Brock Bowers, who was out with an ankle injury. For a second NFL start, it was promising.

"He's a first-round pick, first overall for a reason," Broncos linebacker Alex Singleton said, via the team's transcripts. "He's going to be a good player."

The pick-6 just after the two-minute warning ended the Raiders' comeback hopes. There was still progress from Mendoza.

"I felt a lot more comfortable this week than last week," Mendoza said, via the team's site. "They're both great defenses, both top in the league. However, I just felt like I was able to settle in and was able to see the entire field better. I was able to see the entire football field."

Daniel Jones could remain an option for the Patriots after he was suddenly released by the Colts, though head coach Mike Vrabel wouldn't address that Monday. He said Tommy DeVito will start in Week 12.

"We're excited about that, good preseason, and Tommy's improved," Vrabel said.

DeVito struggled when he was forced into action against the Lions in Germany before the bye week. He gets to face the Chargers this weekend, which gives DeVito a chance to impress and keep the job. But it also wouldn't be surprising if Jones finishes the season for a Patriots team that is still in the playoff race.

Years from now, people will look back on the 2026 Ravens, see that Lamar Jackson missed most of the season and attribute Baltimore's poor record to that. And it won't be completely accurate. The defense, which is head coach Jesse Minter's specialty, has been miserable.

Over the Ravens' last five games they have allowed 35, 36, 33, 23 and 35 points. Baltimore lost all five of those games, and its losing streak is at six. Minter took the blame for the Panthers putting together a game-winning drive late in the fourth quarter, saying "It was just a poor job by me, and that's what it was." Players disagreed.

"I wouldn't necessarily agree with it, truthfully," linebacker Roquan Smith told the team's site. "I thought we were in some really good calls. It comes down to us doing our job. That has been the story all season."

The responsibility for a bad season still falls on Minter, who is in his first season as a head coach. Baltimore is 26th in the NFL in points allowed.

The Saints really don't do anything well. They're 21st in points scored per game and 22nd in yards gained, 23rd in points allowed and 27th in yards allowed. They're 16th in passing yards, 18th in passing yards allowed, 28th in rushing yards and 28th in rushing yards allowed. Even the passing yardage is not that great; Tyler Shough's yards per attempt of 6.2 is better than only four of the 32 qualified quarterbacks this season. Up and down their résumé, nothing stands out as a strength.

It has been a dreadful follow-up to a good second half to last season, which provided false hope for the Saints heading into this season. It looks like a multiple-year rebuild is still in the beginning stages. The good news is the Saints would have the eighth overall pick of the draft if the season ended now.

The problem with the Falcons isn't even their record because they play in the NFC South. But their upcoming schedule is at Vikings, vs. Lions and at Browns. Even one win in those three puts them at 4-9 with four to go. Atlanta will have to play a lot better to stay alive in the playoff hunt over the next three games.

The Falcons had a great two-game stretch against the Packers and Saints and also are the only team to beat the 49ers, so figuring out why they can't play that way most weeks has been tough.

"We got really good players, man," Falcons running back Bijan Robinson said, via the team's site. "Obviously, with the good players, you have to execute at a high level. We have to come together this week and tell ourselves, 'Is this how we want to end the season?'"

Sunday wasn't perfect for the Eagles, with just 214 yards on offense, but their defense is starting to play well. It allowed 255 yards in the 19-16 win over the Steelers, the second straight time the Eagles have held an opponent under 300 yards. Before that stretch, the Eagles hadn't held an opponent to fewer than 300 yards since Week 2, which was the last time Philadelphia had won a game before Sunday.

The Eagles will be tested against a very good Cowboys offense that is coming off a 51-point game against the Titans. But seeing the defense come through, with stellar linebacker play from Jeremiah Trotter Jr. and Jihaad Campbell, gives them hope for a happy Thanksgiving.

The Jets have little to play for, and a loss to the Chargers would have put them in much better draft position next April, but winning is good for any NFL locker room. Even if it's against the Chargers.

"The thing is, a win is a win and that's what we look for and that's the way you play this game," Jets head coach Aaron Glenn said, according to the team's site.

There have been some promising developments in the Jets' season, and probably none bigger than the play of edge rusher David Bailey, the second overall pick of the draft. Bailey had two more sacks, giving him 9.5 for the season. Jevon Kearse set the rookie record with 14.5 and Bailey is on pace to break that, though in a 17-game season. He has been undeniably impressive and a big piece for a Jets team that has three first-round picks next year.

Nick Rallis always seemed like an awkward fit as the Cardinals' defensive coordinator, considering he wasn't successful in three previous seasons under an old regime but was retained. First-year head coach Mike LaFleur fired him Monday after another disappointing performance. The Cardinals are 31st in expected points added allowed per play, ahead of only the Cowboys.

The Cardinals haven't been good on defense, but talent certainly seems to be lacking. Safety Budda Baker, the one star the defense has, leads the team with 3.5 sacks. That's a terrible sign. First-round picks up front, like Zaven Collins and Darius Robinson, haven't broken out. We're about to find out if that was because of a coaching problem.

The Colts cutting Daniel Jones less than 24 hours after his 11th straight start to begin the season was odd, even if it ensures they don't have to pay a 2027 injury guarantee. Jones was poor against the Texans, but he was poor all season. He's dead last among NFL starting quarterbacks in EPA per play this season. But the Colts didn't care before Week 11, starting Jones and seemingly never giving any serious consideration to benching him to at least get a look at Anthony Richardson Sr. Cutting a quarterback after starting him all season is a sign of a franchise that isn't making sound decisions.

At least the Colts now can get a look at Richardson, who showed some progress in the preseason. Richardson hasn't shown many signs that he can be an NFL starter, but it's not like the Colts are losing anything by taking another look.

The Dolphins haven't been dominated often. They were dominated Sunday, when they fell behind early and got crushed by a superior Bills team.

One positive point from Sunday was another big game from rookie linebacker Jacob Rodriguez, who had 13 tackles and an interception. He has 93 tackles, a pair of interceptions and a sack this season. Rodriguez was a tremendous college player but he fell to the second round of the NFL Draft. He has already shown that his production at Texas Tech was no fluke and gives the Dolphins one foundational piece in what is going to be a long rebuild.

There wasn't much to be happy with after Sunday's blowout loss. Cam Ward's final line didn't look terrible, but in the first half against a Cowboys defense that is worst in the NFL in many categories, Ward had 55 passing yards and the Titans didn't score a touchdown.

The defense was even worse, giving up 51 points. In Tennessee's last four games it has allowed 491, 510, 459 and 480 yards. The defense was supposed to be much better under head coach Robert Saleh, and it is one of the worst in the NFL. Tennessee has allowed more yards per game than any other team.

"These last couple of weeks, I'm not going to try and sugarcoat anything," Saleh said Monday, according to the team's site. "It's been dog s***."

Good thing the Chargers got that win in Week 10. Losing to the Jets on Sunday at home was a strong indication that Los Angeles might not win again.

At least there were a couple of decent performances on offense. Omarion Hampton continued to get hot late in the season with 89 rushing yards. Justin Herbert had a 119.3 passer rating. His best mark in a game this season before Sunday was 87.5. Unfortunately, Herbert landed in concussion protocol after the loss. Ladd McConkey has had an injury-filled and unproductive season but he had two touchdowns against the Jets. It's not much, especially in a loss. But it's not like there have been many other positives for the Chargers.
