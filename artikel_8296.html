Your main problem is that of most individual, non-professional investors: Refusing to sell at a loss.

No matter when you eventually sell, your saavy-Boglehead low-cost brokerage is NOT going to buy them. Some professional muni dealer is going to give you the haircut.

As another noted, you can hope for some full-price redemptions, especially if interest rates should happen to go down. (No one knows if that will happen. But if inflation forces interest rates up, redemptions become less likely. And your market prices would also go ... ... down.)

Are any of the bonds less safe credit risks?
On the contrary, I’ve been selling at a loss left right and center. I’ve got over $29k so far at Schwab alone from the last several weeks.

The point is that for now, I want to hang onto some of them for the income. My investments have two goals: financing LTC if and when needed and portfolio growth for my heirs. Both of these involve reinvesting of all possible distributions, meaning choosing to forgo income from those assets while they grow. So some tax-free income is good for the time being. Ten more will be sold in the new year. I’ll continue to sell after that at a slower pace as things settle down.

Thanks to Stinky and others, I changed my focus from what should I get rid of to what is worth keeping for now. This simple adjustment made the perplexing things simple.

(All bonds are/were A and above.)
