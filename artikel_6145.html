Delta Air Lines Teases Routes For 20 New A350-1000s: Where They Could Fly

SkyTeam Alliance partner Delta Air Lines is expecting 20 of Airbus's largest single-level widebody aircraft. The Airbus A350-1000 is a firm order with 20 options, and deliveries are expected to begin in 2027. With these planes set to help support the airline's long-haul ambitions, the airline has already hinted that the next decade could see the airline push further west.

In a report shared by Executive Traveller, Delta is gearing up for the 'decade of the Pacific', suggesting the airline will look to expand its reach to destinations in Asia and Oceania. Australia is likely to be the biggest winner here.

Delta is one of three of the US legacy carriers, all of which have growing networks to both Asia and Oceania. Delta itself has grown capacity in these regions by 15% year-on-year since 2022. The airline's Seoul-based Vice President for the Asia Pacific, Jeff Moomaw, noted that this could be just the beginning.

The majority of the airline's future expansion in the next decade is expected to be westward, and this could see the carrier expand to other destinations around the Pacific Rim. All of Delta's current flights to Australia operate from Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), where the airline is the largest carrier with more than 200 daily departures to 70 cities.

Seattle remains the airline's second-largest gateway on the US West Coast, with its northern geography providing better connectivity to Southeast Asian markets. A nonstop service from the US to Singapore remains a priority for Delta; however, it remains to be seen which aircraft type the airline will deploy on the route. Competitor United Airlines already serves Singapore nonstop from San Francisco (SFO), while Singapore Airlines maintains several services to the US from its hub at Singapore Changi (SIN).

Hong Kong has returned to the airline network from Los Angeles after close to eight years of suspension. This joined Taipei (TPE) and Shanghai (PVG), with Manila planning to begin in March. The carrier already maintains seasonal services to Auckland (AKL) and Brisbane (BNE), operating across the Northern Hemisphere winter season, where less demand is required for the airline's widebodies for flights to Europe. This sees most North American carriers redeploy aircraft to warmer temperatures south.

The majority of Delta's South Pacific network is concentrated at Los Angeles, and after the airport's multibillion-dollar redevelopment program, it makes clear sense for the airline to consider its network expansion from this airport. Services to Sydney (SYD) and Melbourne (MEL), which already receive the A350-900, could be upgraded to the larger A350-1000 once these come online.

SkyTeam ties and hub synergy hint at smoother connections and expanded long haul reach via Incheon gateways.

Delta can utilize the ultra-long-range capabilities and high passenger capacity to operate transpacific routes to Australia and Asia, where smaller planes could potentially not reach. Delta will likely use the first lot of the new plane for long-haul destinations that have continually provided consistent payloads that are otherwise reaching the limit of the A350-900.

The airline already plans to configure the new aircraft with its highest share of premium seats seen across its fleet, with next-generation Delta One suites and an expansion to its Premium Select seating. These include suites with sliding doors, longer lie-flat beds, and larger onboard entertainment screens, which should attract long-haul business or leisure demand.

With the delivery of the A350-1000 expected in 2027, this will become the largest aircraft in the Delta fleet since the Boeing 747, and while any new destination is yet to be confirmed, the airline has explained that as more deliveries arrive, it is highly likely that these will be seen across the Pacific.
