Festival In Focus: Qatar Ramps Up International Film & TV Ambitions As Local Talent Shines

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Qatar will be re-signaling its ambitions to become a major international film and TV player this week with the second edition of its revamped Doha Film Festival and accompanying Industry Days, with Joe Russo and Omar Sy among the guests at the latter.

The Doha Film Institute, which launched the festival under the Ajyal banner in 2013, and oversees the new version, has been steadily building an ecosystem supporting local filmmakers and indie world cinema talents since its creation in 2010.

The country’s Film Committee, which was created in 2022 under the auspices of Media City Qatar, is now turbo-charging efforts to connect the territory with mainstream film and TV players. At the inaugural Industry Days in 2025, the committee unveiled a 50% film and TV cash rebate; the shoot of a Barbara Broccoli-produced production of Othello starring David Oyelowo and Cynthia Erivo and partnerships with Neon, Company 3, Miramax, Department M and Sony. Watch on Deadline

Amid the buzzy atmosphere and networking chat at the event, no-one foresaw the U.S. and Israel’s military campaign against Iran’s Islamic Republic regime coming down the line at the end of February. The latter’s retaliatory bombardment of its Gulf neighbors would temporarily bring the region to a grinding halt.

Some nine months later, hostilities have abated and Qatar is at the heart of negotiations to bring about a truce, while life has almost returned to normal amid an uneasy calm across the Gulf states.

Film Committee Hassan Al-Thawadi, who previously oversaw the organization of the Qatar World Cup, remains resolute in his drive to make the territory a key film and TV player.

“The feedback around Industry Days was very encouraging. The first edition gave us an opportunity to introduce our vision for Qatar’s film industry, engage the region and hear directly from filmmakers, studios, producers and financiers,” he says.

“More importantly, after the event, we understood that our vision resonated with support for the ecosystem around financing, production, talent, infrastructure and access to the region we are trying to build,” he adds.

He acknowledges that the U.S.-Israel-Iran war has thrown up challenges but says the overall plan remains in place.

“We can’t escape the difficult period our region has experienced,” he says. “However, the fundamentals of what we are trying to build has not changed. We have a long-term strategy to attract international production, develop Arab talent and capability and to create a stronger platform for stories from our region to reach global audiences.”

Proof of this, is the second edition of three-day Industry Days which kicks off on Thursday, with a guest list bursting with heavy-hitters and the promise of as many announcements as the inaugural event.

“Last year was about introducing the Film Committee, our ambition to transform Qatar into a global hub for the film industry, and a new proposition for the region to create greater opportunities and international visibility for Arab cinemas. This year the emphasis is on progress and moving from ambition into activity,” says Al-Thawadi.

“The clearest evidence of progress this year is a portfolio of supported films with Paper Tiger, Artou and Furies reaching international festivals, and projects moving through creative development and production planning,” he continues.

“You will hear more about the Qatar Screen Production Incentive and how we are extending its regional reach, about projects and talent moving into their next stages, and about partnerships becoming more practical and production focused.

Among those due to attend are Squid Games creator Hwang Dong-hyuk, who will join a conversation on how the show conquered the world and Joe Russo, who is no stranger to the Gulf region.

Omar Sy will join a discussion on why actors are increasingly creating their own studios. Alongside his acting career, the Lupin star is a producer under the Carrousel Studios banner, his joint company with Thomas Benski who will also be town.

Regional talents joining the conversation include Ukraine-born, Germany-based Syrian Lebanese filmmaker Ameer Fakher Eldin (Yunan) who will join a conversation with Syrian star Taim Hasan and Kuwaiti journalist and media host Ammar Taqi on the challenge of bringing real lives onto the big screen.

In other panels, there will be a focus on Indonesia as Asia’s Next Screen Powerhouse, while the event venue is also hosting an exhibition going behind the scenes of the making of films including Jurassic Park and Forest Gump.

The Doha Film Festival program in the meantime bears testimony to the grassroots filmmaking culture which is slowly but steadily taking shape.

In a watershed moment for the local filmmaking scene, four locally produced features – spanning Jodari Meno by Jamal Al Khanji, A Very Ordinary Life by Muhammed Deniz, First to Fly by Rodrigo Vazquez Salessi and When the News Breaks You by Hamad Al Hajri – will world premiere in the Made in Qatar section.

In documentary Jodari Meno, Qatari director Al Khanji, a trained heart surgeon and avid spearfisherman, reflects on his life and family ties while documenting his worldwide quest to catch a world record dogtooth tuna.

Turkish director Deniz’s Qatari–Moroccan–Turkish feature film A Very Ordinary Life follows a retired Qatari pilot and a young Moroccan art teacher whose lives become intertwined through dreams and letters, unaware of a buried family secret.

Argentinian London-based journalist and director Vazquez Salessi’s documentary First to Fly follows two young gymnasts on a quest to become the first ever athletes to represent Qatar in the Rhythmic Gymnastics World Championships.

The debut feature documentary of Qatari filmmaker Al Hajri, When the News Breaks You explores the challenges facing journalists working in the Middle East.

Both Jodari Meno and When The News Breaks were made with the support of the Doha Film Institute over many years, with the directors also attending its workshops as well as its annual project and talent incubator Qumra.

“Attending events like Qumra and exposing the work to critics definitely shaped this work,” says Jodari Meno director Al Khanji.

“The film started as an adventure documentary with my goal to show all this footage I had shot around the world with these strange fish and crazy encounters with sharks,” he explains.

It slowly dawned him that there was also a more personal side to the story, but it took feedback from mentors at Qumra and the DFI to push him in that direction

“Someone said the underwater footage is beautiful, but you need to talk more about you,” he recalls. “It’s not something I would necessarily have done on my own.”

Al Khanji hopes films like his will bring a fresh perspective on the region.

“The more we have people telling human stories, the better the area will be understood,” he says.
