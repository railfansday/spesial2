Apple stock fell as rising memory costs sent its holiday margin forecast below expectations

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Free daily briefing on global business news.

Apple projected a December-quarter gross margin of 45% to 46%, below the 46.3% analysts expected, according to CNBC

David Paul Morris / Bloomberg via Getty Images

Apple $AAPL forecast a gross margin between 45% and 46% for its holiday-season December quarter, and it cited rising memory chip costs. Apple stock dropped roughly 3% in after-hours trading on Monday, having already lost nearly 2% over the normal trading day.

The company reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $114.5 billion on Monday, up 12% from a year earlier. Gross margin was 47.9%, including about 1 percentage point from tariff refunds. Diluted earnings per share were $2.10, up 14%.

The margin forecast falls short of the 46.3% that analysts were expecting on average, according to CNBC. Analysts had expected earnings per share of $1.99 and revenue of $113.39 billion.

"This includes primarily the impact of higher memory costs, as well as some costs related to the ramp of Siri AI," Chief Financial Officer Kevan Parekh said on the earnings call, according to CNBC.

CEO John Ternus said memory now costs the company more than it did in prior months of the year. "What we're seeing with memory pricing right now is unprecedented," Ternus said, according to CNBC. "Looking beyond the December quarter, we see the pricing for memory purchases continuing to increase."

Apple expects to have trouble supplying enough iPhones and Macs during the holiday quarter, mostly because of memory. Chief Operating Officer Sabih Khan said the company has less room to maneuver across its supplier network than usual. Khan said Apple's purchase obligations grew by $33 billion between the June and September quarters, but he declined to say whether Apple has signed long-term agreements with memory makers.

A drop in the December-quarter margin would be Apple's third straight decline, according to CNBC. The margin was 50.1% in the June quarter. If results land halfway through that range, it would mark the company's weakest gross margin since the final quarter of fiscal 2023.

Ternus became CEO on Sept. 1, according to CNBC.
