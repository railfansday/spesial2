Tanggal: 2026-11-14

OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Wallet to Let AI Agents Shop—Without Crypto

ChatGPT Wallet lets OpenAI’s dots complete purchases with tokenized card details, after users approve each transaction. The limited rollout targets U.S. ChatGPT Pro users; the wallet does not hold crypto or a cash balance. Coinbase and Circle offer a different route, using stablecoins to let agents pay for services with less human involvement.

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, giving its AI agents a way to purchase products using American Express, Mastercard, and Visa cards. The new system lets an agent finish online checkout without accessing the customer's actual card number, and it doesn't involve cryptocurrency.

The wallet works through dots, OpenAI's always-on AI assistants, which can browse websites and carry out tasks for users. A customer saves an eligible card, reviews the order and approves the payment using a security check such as a fingerprint or Face ID.

After approval, the card network creates a payment token containing substitute card details. The agent submits the token through the merchant’s existing checkout system, while the customer keeps applicable card rewards and dispute protections, according to OpenAI’s announcement.

“An agent making a purchase should strengthen, not bypass, the trust built into payments,” Sherri Haymond, Mastercard's global head of digital commercialization, said in the announcement.

OpenAI previously introduced Instant Checkout with Stripe in September 2025, allowing people to buy from participating merchants inside ChatGPT. Wallet takes a different approach: agents can navigate ordinary online stores and use existing checkout forms, without requiring each business to build a special integration.

OpenAI is also courting merchants. Just three days before the Wallet launch, it introduced business profiles that let companies update product information and connect with potential customers through ChatGPT.

Google is working on a competing approach. Its Universal Commerce Protocol, introduced in January, aims to connect shopping agents with retailers, payment providers and post-purchase services across Google products.

Coinbase launched Agentic Wallets in February, giving software the ability to spend USDC, trade tokens, and pay for online services within preset limits. Unlike ChatGPT Wallet, the Coinbase product is primarily developer infrastructure, not a consumer checkout feature.

“It's not an SDK, it's not a library—it's a purpose-built wallet to work with an agent as quickly as possible,” Coinbase engineering executive Erik Reppel told Decrypt at the launch.

Coinbase's x402 protocol lets software pay for data or computing services as part of a web request, often using the USDC stablecoin. That makes tiny transactions possible without requiring an agent to enter card details or obtain human approval each time.

Stablecoin issuer Circle is pushing in the same direction. Its Agent Stack, launched in May, includes stablecoin wallets and tools for payments as small as $0.000001.

“USDC is uniquely well-suited for the agentic economy because it is internet-native, programmable, and always available,” Circle product chief Nikhil Chandhok said in a company statement.

Actual agent-driven demand is harder to measure. In September, blockchain analytics firm TRM Labs estimated that agents accounted for only 0.6% to 7.5% of filtered x402 commerce by value, using models that cannot conclusively distinguish agents from scripts.

For consumers, ChatGPT Wallet offers familiar card payments rather than a digital asset balance. OpenAI's terms say merchants remain responsible for deliveries and returns, and users must generally contact their card issuers for billing disputes.

OpenAI plans to let users authorize future purchases within spending limits or specified categories, but has not announced when that feature will arrive. In the meantime, it warns that merchant websites may block shopping agents or flag otherwise legitimate Wallet purchases as fraud.
