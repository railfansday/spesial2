‘Peace through strength’: Albanese and Trump hail progress on Quad alliance

Tanggal: 2026-11-17

Leaders of India and Japan join for talks on military exercises, AI and rare earths as a counter to Chinese power

Donald Trump and Anthony Albanese have heralded plans for new joint Quad military exercises designed to push back on China’s expansionist ambitions, promising the grouping with India and Japan will guarantee “peace through strength”.

Albanese met Trump, India’s Nahrendra Modi and Japan’s Sanae Takaichi in Manila late on Tuesday in talks about enhanced cooperation on AI, critical minerals, infrastructure and maritime security.

“These nations represent some of the most important partnerships in the entire world,” the US president said. “They’re among the most successful nations in the world, and in certain ways among the most powerful nations of the world.

“Our countries are the four corners of a free, open, stable, and prosperous Indo-Pacific.”

The Australian prime minister praised Trump and said a new Quad business forum to be held in 2027 would build economic growth across the region.

Leaders in the group – established as China seeks to build its military might and exert influence across the region – say they will cooperate to maintain peace and stability with Beijing.

“When we work together for our common view in the Indo-Pacific of peace, security, stability, and prosperity, then the world will be a safer and more secure place, and the Quad has a critical role in achieving that,” Albanese told the summit.

The plan for new military exercises involving maritime patrol aircraft is likely to prompt strong criticism from China.

Albanese said Australia would deploy its P8-A Poseidon and P3-C Orion maritime patrol aircraft as part of the operation in early 2027.

Asked what he would tell Chinese leader Xi Jinping later this week about the plan, Albanese said military exercises were common.

“We want peace and security in the region. That’s the objective here. It’s not about any one country. It’s about our own sovereignty, but also about peace and security in the region,” he said

The Quad leaders want the group to present a multilateral coalition of resistance against Beijing, seeking to undermine its dominance, including in the South and East China seas.

Takaichi said the Quad would help shape the future of the region.

“We would like to realise a free and open Indo Pacific by strengthening resilience of countries in the region and promote their freedom to decide for themselves across a wide range of areas including maritime security, economic security, energy and critical and emerging technologies.”

Before the meeting, as the four leaders posed for a group photo, Trump praised Modi and Takaichi as “tough”, before gesturing to Albanese and saying: “We’re OK too.” Albanese laughed at the remark.

The first meeting of the Quad leaders since 2024 came as Albanese and Trump both prepare for setpiece engagement with China’s president later this week. Albanese will visit Xi in Shenzhen on Friday, a day after three-way talks between Trump, Xi and Russia’s Vladimir Putin.

Trump said he wanted talks with the North Korean leader, Kim Jong-un, but conceded he was not the host of the China summit and it was up to Xi to issue an invitation. The US president said he could have a one-on-one meeting with Putin “if it’s available”.

Quad military exercises would build on progress from the recently completed Malabar joint exercise near Japan, where sailors completed anti-submarine and anti-surface warfare drills.

Trump also talked up the Aukus nuclear deal with Australia. “We make the greatest submarines anywhere in the world, and we’re working with Australia and others on that,” he said.

Trump said the US was poised to catch up to China on rare earth production within 10 months, backed up by speedy progress from Australia, Japan and India.

He was accompanied by cabinet members Marco Rubio, Scott Bessent, Howard Lutnick and Jamieson Greer, as well as his new chief of staff, Stephen Miller.

Privately, officials believed the meeting alone showed the group was progressing. Australian officials welcomed the engagement of Trump in regional affairs as he enters the final two years of his presidency.

Trump took credit for reinvigorating the grouping, and said he placed a premium on relations with Albanese, Modi and Takaichi.

“When they have difficulty, when they want to tell me something, my phone is always open, and the Oval Office is always open to you, and you know that,” he said.

Albanese said before the meeting he would push Trump on ending the war in Iran, a conflict which has produced an unprecedented global energy shock and badly distorted conditions in the Australian economy.

It was Albanese’s first meeting with Takaichi since a weeks-long saga about comments he made on a podcast about her gift of melons presented to him during a visit to Australia in July.

Earlier, Albanese took part in the East Asia Summit where he called for regional partners to work together to boost AI protections. Trump, who calls the technology “superintelligence”, said it represented “the greatest opportunity for economic growth since the Industrial Revolution”.
