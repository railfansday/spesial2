Four separate flights from London, Copenhagen, Manchester and Israel affected by bomb threats – Europe live

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Warsaw-Modlin Airport gives the all-clear after concluding security sweeps on a LOT Polish Airlines flight from London

If you are joining us now, we are following reports about at least four planes affected by bomb threats across Europe this morning: two in Poland (from the UK 10:33, 10:58, 12:00 and Denmark 11:33, 12:59), one in Bulgaria (from Israel 12:50), and one in Spain (from the UK 12:44).

All four of them landed safely and are being checked by security services. It may be nothing, but is highly unusual (particularly after another alert yesterday 10:44), so we are keeping a close eye on that.

Separately, we are keeping an eye on Berlin where Ukraine’s president Volodymyr Zelenskyy is just arriving to meet German chancellor Friedrich Merz on his previously unannounced high-security trip to Berlin.

We should hear from them in the next hour or so.

Asked about his expectations for the Abu Dhabi talks, Zelenskyy says he hopes to see future security guarantees for Ukraine to be covered, alongside a “prosperity package” that would help with the reconstruction of Ukraine.

He also says he hopes to discuss a proposal on energy, Black Sea truce, which is “in the interest of not only Ukraine, but also Russia and various nations in Africa.”

He reiterates that “there can be no stable peace unless Europe is at the negotiating table.”

Merz for his part says that “it is crucial for Russia to finally engage in serious negotiations.”

Responding, Zelenskyy thanks Germany for its continued help, including on air defence, and says that while many people will be following the events in the US today’s midterm elections, “the role of Europe – and specifically Germany – remains crucial.”

He says he briefed Merz on the latest attacks on Ukraine.

He also confirms the upcoming Abu Dhabi talks on 10-11 November (11:21), which he says hopefully “will pave the way for a trilateral meeting at the level of heads of states and government.”

He also confirms further talks on Ukraine will be held in Brussels on Wednesday (12:41), as per the European Commission’s announcement earlier, and that the coalition of the willing will meet in Berlin on 19 November.

Germany’s Merz begins by saying Zelenskyy’s visit comes “at a critical moment” as Russian attacks on Ukraine continued with “undiminished – indeed, increasing – severity and ruthlessness.”

He says Ukraine “can continue to rely on Germany,” with more support from Berlin in the coming months, including on air defence, energy, and broader winter-related aid.

Merz also warns that the outcome of the war in Ukraine “will determine European security for a long time to come.”

He says Germany will be coordinating with France and the UK, and will host a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing in Berlin later this month.

The German chancellor also talks of growing concerns about the broader effects of the continuing war, including a potential “global food crisis” triggered by Russia’s continued attacks on trading routes in the Black Sea.

He says the upcoming Abu Dhabi talks are “the right step forward,” and Europe hopes to play its part.

In the meantime, Berlin and partners will “increase the pressure on Moscow and continue to support Ukraine,” he says.

Meanwhile, Germany’s Merz and Ukraine’s Zelenskyy are now briefing the media after their talks in Berlin.

Similarly, Warsaw-Modlin Airport has also given the all-clear after concluding security sweeps on a LOT Polish Airlines flight from London Heathrow following an earlier bomb alert (10:33).

“The bomb threat was called off … Following the completion of operations by the relevant services, Warsaw/Modlin Airport has returned to normal functioning.”

We have just got an update from Spain’s Guardia Civil on the Ryanair flight from Manchester to Murcia (12:44).

The authorities “ruled out the presence of explosives on the plane,” the Spanish government’s delegate in the region of Murcia, Francisco Lucas, confirmed.

“The International Airport of the Region of Murcia resumes its activity normally. Thanks to the emergency teams that have participated in the operation,” he said.

Over in Sweden, centrist leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist has just lost a vote on her proposed government – which is hardly surprising given that even she as very clear she had no chance of winning.

It’s been 51 days since the Swedish parliamentary election, and it’s the second failed attempt to form the government. Two more and we get a snap election. Gulp.

The next attempt is expected to be led by the interim centre-right prime minister, Ulf Kristersson, but his outgoing coalition is also unlikely to secure enough support in the parliament, further complicating the process.
